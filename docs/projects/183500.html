Project Summary

Driven and facilitated by community-based, women-led teams in Hebron and Bethlehem governates in previous project by DanChurchAid/Norwegian Church Aid’s (DCA/NCA) and the local organization, the East Jerusalem Young Men’s Christian Association (EJ-YMCA.) The overall project objective is to enhance vulnerable women´s economic coping capacity to overcome the impacts of occupation that was also increased by COVID-19 related to loss of income and well-being. The implementing partner EJ-YMCA will work closely with established Protection Groups to select and support 133 vulnerable women and their families (655 individual beneficiaries) through unrestricted, multipurpose cash grants that can help beneficiaries in any way they need.. While the entry point for identifying target women is their socioeconomic situation, the project’s impact encompasses women with different sensitive and vulnerable living circumstances, of different societal groups and different age groups, and their families including, but not limited to, women headed households, GBV survivors, women who lost their source of income in the pandemic, widows, women with disabilities and chronic disease, and elderly women.
The EJ-YMCA will work through a highly participatory approach centered around collaboration with established Protection Groups (PGs) in 15 communities. The project relies on a participatory approach in its design and implementation. Key elements of our project approach are:
A Survivor Community-Led Response (SCLR) method
The SCLR approach puts communities in the driver’s seat when identifying their own needs, deciding on priorities, planning, and implementing actions to respond to needs identified.
Results from previous projects showed that the SCLR approach is effective in enabling communities to identify and address protection and development needs; it contributes to resilience, strengthens cooperation between local community members, and enhances the ownership.
The proposed project will be work with the already established Protection Groups in the two governorates of Hebron and Bethlehem. A community Protection Group is an informal structure that consists of a group of women, young women, men, and young men from the community. PGs were formed through previous projects supported by DCA/NCA. The SCLR approach has equipped the PGs with the necessary skills and preparedness to facilitate, organize, and coordinate any efforts within the community. The proposed project will further empower the PGs as they will play a key role the implementation of the proposed project as they are the connection between communities, DCA/NCA and its partner EJ-YMCA.
The Protection Groups (PGs)
In this project each PG will nominate 3 women and they will act with the EJ-YMCA field teams to reach out to the vulnerable women within their communities. The PG will follow the principles of neutrality and impartiality will nominate three representative women members to map women’s needs in their communities for the vulnerable targeted women, based on their first-hand knowledge of the socioeconomic situation and vulnerability of women in their communities, develop the localized selection criteria, assist the EJ-YMCA project field team collect data, and in documenting the action/process.
Community sensitization With the project relying on nominated PG women, it seeks to promote women as active, capable, resilient participants in their communities. An important element of enhancing community sensitization to the important role of women, is to support the women themselves in making the choice to undertake and commit to responsibilities that go beyond the family sphere and are at community level. The project seeks to enhance their confidence in decision-making and highlight this within their communities. These discussions seek as well to add transparency, accountability, and responsiveness to the PG women’s role and enhance visibility in the communities.

1 - Needs

The project targets 15 communities in the southern governorates of the West Bank that DCA/NCA´s partner EJ-YMCA WDP already operates in. These communities are primarily located in Area C, where Israel retains nearly exclusive control, including over law enforcement, planning, and construction. The project also includes community members in Area B whose livelihoods are in Area C. In Area B, civil authority is under the Palestinian Authority, while security responsibilities are shared by both the Israeli and Palestinian authorities. Most of Area C has been allocated for the benefit of Israeli settlements or the Israeli military, at the expense of the wellbeing of the Palestinian communities. This impedes the development of adequate housing, infrastructure, and livelihoods in Palestinian communities. Structures built without permits are regularly served with demolition orders, creating chronic uncertainty and threat, and encouraging people to leave. Where the orders were implemented, they resulted in displacement and disruption of livelihoods, the entrenchment of poverty, and increased aid dependency. Israel further imposes restrictions on movement, which affect community members’ access to education, healthcare, financial institutions, and job opportunities. Surveys conducted by Birzeit University in 2019 report that the main sources of income are unstable and insecure, with less than one-quarter of households relying on the stability of a regular salary for their primary income. Episodes of physical and verbal assault, stone-throwing, damage to Palestinian property, and vandalizing cars, houses, and agricultural property added to routinely heavy-handed operations by the Israeli military and to the mental stress and anxiety of thousands of people and communities in Area C. This adds to the more common causes of stress, such as family issues or lack of financial security that is aggravated by the lockdown to curb the virus. The 15 communities that are targeted are deemed vulnerable as these communities experiences a lot of the everyday difficulties because of the Israeli practices and by settler attacks. An example of these communities is Bireen, as community that relays on livelihood is agriculture, they exposed continuous attacks by the Israeli forces and settlers, who attacked them constantly and blocks the road in front of their cattle during the grazing lands. Another example is Bet Mirsim, the village is subject to flying checkpoints. There is also a bypass road within the village. The Israeli Forces has confiscated 220 dunums of land. As a result of these activates in the village and surrounding it, there has been a great amount of pollution in water supply. The people have also experienced difficulties in accessing health centers or hospitals. Sometimes, farmers find it difficult to pick their fruit trees as the Israeli Forces have cut and destroyed the olive and almonds trees, and closed the agricultural roads in the village. Approximately eight families in the village have had their agricultural product damaged. The educational sector also suffers from the Israeli activities, students from the neighboring villages must walk about two kilometers to reach the school in the village. 11 harvesting wells have also been destroyed by the Israeli Forces. The Segregation Wall surrounds the village from three sides, the north, west and south. About 400 olive, almonds and forest trees were destroyed while constructing the Wall. Five houses and five establishments were also demolished to construct the wall. In 2020 as COVID-19 spread across the WB, communities in Area C faced higher risks and vulnerabilities as increase of settler violence towards Palestinians rose by 78% in March 2020 according to the UN. Furthermore, according to the World Bank , by June 2020, more than 121,000 Palestinian workers have lost their jobs. In the third quarter of 2020, the unemployment rate rose to 28.5 percent of 2020, up from 24.6 percent in the equivalent period of 2019, according to reports issued by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics. The outbreak of COVID-19 pandemic, has placed a heavy burden on Palestinians but particularly on women, adding to the long-lasting grievances of the Israeli occupation, as well as the patriarchal society that has been treating women as submissive partners. The impact of Covid-19 has affected the lives of women in several ways. The closure of schools has exacerbated the burden of unpaid care work on women and girls, who absorb the additional work of caring for children. Moreover, the outbreak has affected working women who were requested to work from home, since the majority of Palestinian households perceive childcare as the mother’s duty, which makes working from home conditions even more stressful. The crisis has gravely impacted women’s livelihood particularly those active in the informal sector where there is no work protection or income compensation. The virus outbreak has greatly amplified the socially imposed familial responsibilities and expected duties of women as mothers and caregivers. In addition, due to restrictions on movement, an increase in domestic violence has been documented as more people are confined to their homes which results in overcrowding and increased violence. According to an assessment by CARE and OCHA, 33% of female respondents indicated an increased risk of GBV during crisis. A survey by the UN also indicates that 53% of support service providers reported an increase in domestic violence during the pandemic. Women's access to justice has also been made more difficult due to irregular closure of courts, with many cases still pending and unresolved.

2 - Activities

The project will target 133 vulnerable women and their families from the West Bank in Hebron and Bethlehem governates. While the entry point for identifying target women is their socioeconomic situation, the project’s impact encompasses women with different sensitive and vulnerable living circumstances. The planned intervention will focus on 133 vulnerable women, of different societal groups and different age groups, and their families including, but not limited to, women headed households, GBV survivors, women who lost their source of income in the pandemic, widows, women with disabilities and chronic disease, and elderly women. The PG women will contextualize and expand upon the selection criteria in each community. The targeted women live in an estimated number of 15 rural communities in the Hebron and Bethlehem governorates (Area C, including community members in Area B whose livelihoods are in Area C). The proposed project will include the following activities: 1. Unconditional Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance: 133 women and their families (655 individuals in totals) over four months (one payment per month). each member of the family will receive an average of approximately 244 shekel per month (minimum of 162 shekel and maximum of 325 shekel), as unconditional/unrestricted multipurpose cash, based on needs, for four consecutive months. The choice and amount of cash grants was decided on after a rapid assessment conducted by the EJ-YMCA field staff through the Protection Groups, each within their own community.2. Each woman nominated by the PG will be given a one-time payment of approximately 325 Shekel (615 DKK) to cover basic costs like transportation and communication. The PG women will enhance the accountability of the project by posting general information about the activities on their already established Facebook group pages, while ensuring do no harm principles and reserving women dignity and privacy.3. The project will include a complementary component to promote the capacity of women in the Protection groups. Community sensitization With the project relying on nominated PG women, it seeks to promote women as active, capable, resilient participants in their communities. An important element of enhancing community sensitization to the important role of women, is to support the women themselves in making the choice to undertake and commit to responsibilities that go beyond the family sphere and are at community level. By providing this opportunity with this project, where PG women will lead consultations on needs with the community stakeholders and make decisions based on this for the implementation of the multi-purpose cash component. In this way, the project seeks to enhance their confidence in decision-making and highlight this within their communities. These discussions seek as well to add transparency, accountability, and responsiveness to the PG women’s role and enhance visibility in the communities. 4. In addition, DCA/NCA and YMCA-EJ field team will carry out the monitoring of the project, monthly post distribution monitoring survey and data collection by PG women and EJ-YMCA staff. In addition, a Baseline/ end line study will be carried out as well.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA): will address the multisectoral needs of the women and their families, bolster women’s social protection and provide a pathway for independence for women. The MPCA transfer value was developed based on HHs assessment and where it checked how much people spend on each category (food, health, education, etc) and then calculated the average starting from this. The amount is carried out based on size as families of 8 have different needs than a family of 4 and based on needs. When the beneficiaries were selected, they were informed that the amount might differ with other families as it is based on family size and needs that were assessed by the field team. The cash will be given through bank transfers, each woman goes and collects the money from the bank. This method was used for security and safety measures including safety for SEA as it will remove direct interaction between the field team and the beneficiaries. The women will go to the bank provide their name and ID and the teller at the bank will provide the money to them and they sign a document that they have received it. Each month post distribution monitoring survey will be carried out to assess how beneficiaries use the cash and address their satisfaction, where corrective measures can be taken accordingly. More than one modality can be considered regarding accessibility and safety of targeted women, i.e restriction of cash, or paper vouchers. For these cases, a steering committee consisting of DCA/NCA, and EJ-YMCA Cash Focal Point, will discuss case by case to decide on the best modality to provide cash to enable targeted women to utilize the cash assistance in the best way for them. With regards to people with disability, transportation will be provided to them to pick up that cash from the bank and to assist them in purchasing. PSEA messaging will be shared with beneficiaries during post distribution monitoring surveys through me.complaints@dca.dk

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

DCA/NCA will support partner to increase the capability of the complaints and feedback mechanism to inform decision-making, enhance accountability and transparency. This will be ensured through engaging beneficiaries in addressing the expected risks and challenges and empowering them to participate in the monitoring and evaluation process of the project. Beneficiaries will have access to both DCA/NCA and its partner complaint mechanism. DCA/NCA and its partner will consider the beneficiaries feedback and recommendation through direct contact and consultation with them. The complaint mechanism will provide the chance for all beneficiaries especially women and people with disabilities to express their concerns freely and provide their feedback on the delivery of project activities through web site, hotline, complaints boxes, directly to the field monitors, and directly to partner or DCA/NCA offices. The applied complaint mechanism considers specific standard operational procedures in case of gender sensitive complaints. The received complaints and feedback will be documented and analyzed by the program staff to feed into the decision-making process and inform possible corrections during implementation that ensures a better response to the needs of the vulnerable groups. Community engagement committees will be formed to build community confidence and networks and ensure efficient and continuous participation of the community in the implementation and monitoring processes and to inform decision making. The project will organize regular focus group discussions with the community members to collect feedback on the provided services. The collected information will be used to inform decision making by searching for possible areas of improvement and revisiting the implementation approaches and tools.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

DCA has operated in the project areas since the 1950´s, with an office since 2008. Today DCA has a joint office and programme strategy with Norwegian Church Aid (NCA) in Palestine. DCA/NCA have strong experience in responding to humanitarian crises, with proven capacities to provide responsive, efficient, and accountable services to the affected population. DCA/NCA also work to improve our humanitarian response and contingency planning capacity by applying innovative best practices. This has included testing of the SCLR approach (survivor and community led response), resilience approaches and promoting the know how in programming multi-sectoral interventions, together with local partners. This is to materialize localization, the grand bargain commitments and to promote successful practices of nexus. The fundamental premise of the approach is to respect and reserve the dignity of people in conflict and crisis situations. Since 2015, the EJ-YMCA WDP has been working in community led approaches and providing community cash grant schemes to help communities design and implement appropriate responses to the risks they face in their communities, including previous work with PG´s on individual/household cash grants in response to the COVID-19 crisis. The project design recognizes the rights and needs of beneficiaries in terms of safety, security, and dignity and proposes activities that foster the realization of these needs and rights. DCA/NCA and its partner use culturally responsive approach which involves respect for the cultural backgrounds of all right holders, particularly women and people with disability. DCA/NCA and partners apply Do No Harm (DNH) approach that is integrated in the project at all levels. Both partners are trained and aware of the approach which is part of the human rights-based approach adopted by DCA/NCA and YMCA-EJ WDP. In this project, consultation with the communities through the PG and the different stakeholders, different ages and backgrounds was carried out. We took into consideration all societal groups and how this project will impact them, and the PG, YMCA-EJ WDP and DCA/NCA will decide who is the most in need based on these. Confidentially of the most vulnerable is a key element and carried out across the project period. As an example, the cash will be given through bank transfers, where each woman goes and collects the money from the bank. In cases where there is a risk that women will not have the authority to use the cash as they anticipate, a steering committee consisting of DCA/NCA, EJ-YMCA Cash Focal Point, will discuss case by case to decide on the best modality to provide cash to enable targeted women to utilize the cash assistance in the best way for them. Cash grants, as well as their impact on women and families, will be monitored throughout the project. Post distribution monitoring survey will be carried each month to assess how beneficiaries use the cash and address their satisfaction, where corrective measures can be taken accordingly DCA/NCA and its partner also map out the different services that are given at the community to make sure that we are not duplicating the work that is being carried out by different organizations unless the families are still in dire need for assistance. In addition, as it is a multipurpose cash assistance the women can utilize it for their needs and not provide her with in-kind assistance that she maybe doesn’t necessarily need. In that form I am given her the agency for her needs. We preserve their dignity, confidentiality, we make sure that those people are the most vulnerable and the group that are most in dire need. Even we have criteria for those in need the PG contextualize it for the community to reach those who we are not aware of. DCA/NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certified agency (www.hqai.org) and works continuously to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance it provides to the affected population.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The response will be directly implemented by DCA/NCA and its local partner the YMCA-EJ WDP in the West Bank. DCA/NCA will conduct regular visits to the response areas, following up on the indicators and response plan, and assessing response implementation via discussions with the response team and rights holders and by visiting the response sites.DCA\NCA with its partner will ensure equal and impartial access to services as follow:- DCA\NCA access approach is consistent with the humanitarian core principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence, and humanity) in accordance with IHL. Partners apply: 1) Humanity in addressing the human suffering of most vulnerable women; 2) Neutrality so that humanitarian aid must not favour someone over the other; 3) Impartiality in providing services to vulnerable women solely based on need and without discrimination; and 4) Independence in advancing the autonomy of humanitarian project objectives from political, economic, or other objectives. The project provides multipurpose cash to vulnerable women and their family for them to utilize the cash assistance in the best way for them. While the entry point for identifying target women is their socioeconomic situation, the project’s impact encompasses women with different sensitive and vulnerable living circumstances. The planned intervention will focus on 133 vulnerable women, of different societal groups and different age groups, and their families including, but not limited to, women headed households, GBV survivors, women who lost their source of income in the pandemic, widows, women with disabilities and chronic disease, and elderly women. . As these communities have already an established Protection Groups these Protection Groups are aware of who in these communities are in need and if there is People with disabilities in the communities. The Women of the PG will create a list of the vulnerable women, this list is then shared with the field team of YMCA-EJ WDP who will validate the information as they have prior knowledge of the communities since they work with them as well in previous projects. In addition, the YMCA field team will meet with the village council to receive their list as well and to make sure that the information they have is accurate and that they are not missing anybody in the community those needs fits the criteria. The three lists are checked and if any of the lists has additional or is missing names then home visits are made to check the needs of the family and to see if they meet the criteria i.e are considered vulnerable women. If there are PWD then home visits is carried out as well to see what are the needs of the family. The beneficiaries are informed and that each one receives assistance based on their needs. The amount of cash is given based on the needs of the family, depending on the size, if they have an income and the women are informed that each woman is given based on their needs. Sensitive cases will be treated with the utmost confidentiality and alternative modality will be considered.
