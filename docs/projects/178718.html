Project Summary

Syrian Association for Children with Special Needs hopefully will be able to elevate the burden of seeking essential health care services over the shoulder of the Association beneficiaries through providing timely and easily access to integrated health care of primary, Secondary, trauma and MHPss services.
-The core objective of this project is to provide integrated health care services through establishing three health centers and running 3 mobile teams in Lattakia at Al Ramel Al Janoubi, Sainjwan and Qnainas aiming to scale up and improve access to mental health services at community and health facility levels through providing:
1- primary health care services:
-SACSN will be able through its affiliated 3 community wellbeing centers in Al Ramel Al Janoubi, Sainjwan and Qnainas. Each center composed of 3 clinics and a room for MHPss services.
-Medical consultations on Child health, RH, Acute and chronic diseases will be provided.
- ENT specialist, ophthalmologist and orthopedic will be operating on shift basis.
2-Secondary Health care services:
-SACSN will provide life-saving and life-sustaining health care services
-The referral cycle starts with a medical report developed by one of the physicians at the 3 community wellbeing centers (or from outside) describing the health condition and submitted to the referral officer along with all supported diagnosis investigations, who in turn conducts a thorough study to ensure that the surgery is the only proper medical solution for the case. Upon the approval of referral officer, the M& E officer conducts a socioeconomic study based on tailored form followed by a field visit to the beneficiaries home to verify his vulnerability degree and ensure the eligibility of the free-of charge service. Based on the approval of the project manager on the submitted documents, a referral letter is handed to the beneficiary to receive the needed service.
3- Trauma and rehabilitation services:
treatments from burns, medical glasses and hearing aids will be provided (The provision of assistive devices goes through similar referral cycle)
4- MHPss services:
-Under the supervision of three team leads, 15 trained social workers on WHO tools, guidelines and other evidence-based protocols will provide psychological interventions group sessions, raising awareness group sessions under the field of MHPSS/GBV and individual psychological interventions sessions
-The 15 social workers will be divided into three teams: 5 in Al Ramel Al Janoubi center, 5 in Sainjwan center and 5 in Qnainas center

4 - Needs

-Reference to WHO Health Resources Availability Mapping System (HeRAMs), there are one PHC center at each targeted location at Al Ramel Al Janoubi and Qnainas (maps attached) but the services in these health centers are limited and do not cover all health needs also to compound the situation, the limited operating hours of health clinics in public hospitals due to COVID-19 restrictions. Given the already density of the host communities and the burden of the increasing number of IDPs which makes the demand for integrated health care services of a persistent need , The targeted locations under this project proposal respectively are considered ones of the most slams areas where poverty , nascence, unemployment , displacement, lack of health centers available in these areas and lack of health awareness constitute all together unhealthy environment and full of stress and mental disorders.A multitude of non-contagious diseases also impact health for Al Ramel Al Janoubi, Sainjwan and Qnainas residents. Examples of prevalent non-infectious diseases include: cardiovascular disease, diabetes, chronic respiratory disease, neurological disorders, and mental illness. In Al Ramel Al Janoubi diarrhea is a significant health problem among children. Factors like poor sanitation, low literacy rates, and limited awareness make diarrhea and other dangerous diseases extremely prevalent and burdensome on the community-The scale, severity and complexity of humanitarian needs in Latakia remain extensive. Over 0.67 million people require assistance . The situation is further aggravated by the economic downturn, the COVID-19 pandemic and its socio-economic impacts, and the rising cost of basic food items.
