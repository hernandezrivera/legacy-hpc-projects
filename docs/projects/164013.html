Project Summary

SARD is committed to assist affected people most in need in North West
Syria, despite the unpredictability and the risks of working in areas with security threats.
The response plan with a budget of 4 200 000 USD will address the needs of A total of 42500 individual over 12 months, addressing life-saving and life-sustaining assistance.
42500 individual = 8050 men, 9750 women, 12325 boys, 12325 girls, these ratios were decided based on the IDP Sites Integrated Monitoring Matrix (ISIMM) 970K individual segregated (190k men (19%), 220k women (23%), 280k boys (29%), 280k girls (29%) in the link: https://unhcr-xborder-turkey.github.io/ISIMM/.
The action contributes to improve shelters and to provide life-sustaining assistance with NFI for selected most vulnerable persons including IDPs recently displaced or displaced multiple times.
The assessment conducted 2019/2020 show that NFI/hygiene items ranked as households’ main priorities for the next 3 to 6 months. NFI needs are diverse and include winterization kits, clothes, kitchen sets, bedding units and hygiene items. About 69% of the households shelter assistance as one of their main priority for the next 3 to 6 months.
Implemented with a protection lens, and addressing gender and disabled specific needs, the shelter response is aiming at providing housing options to primary emergency to life-sustaining situation, the activities ranging from emergency support in camps setting or temporary shelter solutions in open-field to life-saving upgrade of transitional shelter and life-sustaining house rehabilitation through self-help approach. The modality of intervention will be adapted to the urgency, the situation, the needs and the HLP situation of the beneficiaries. SARD is committed to promoting social cohesion : shelter programming is based community approach and include community project of common interest. The shelter activities will include :
-Distribution of tents and camps settings (including appropriate site planning)
-Emergency shelter option with distribution of kit or quick fixing in the sub-standard settings by SARD team
-Collective shelter upgrade and unfinished building repair through contractors under SARD technical supervision
-House rehabilitation through self-help – cash grant under SARD supervision
-Community projects through contractors.
As part of the HLP procedure, SARD will ensure free of charge security of tenure from 6 months in emergency temporary settings to 18 months in transitional/permanent shelter options.
Shelter response is integrated with basic shelter related NFI support, adapted to the needs of the assessed and eligible families. The modality of intervention will range from in-kind to cash/voucher intervention according to the market accessibility and availability, family capacity and needs. Door to door distribution is the preferred option to ensure specific needs and security is addressed to girls, women, elderly and people with special needs.

4 - Needs

Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute resulting in signiﬁcant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being and protection of affected populations. Overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in People in Need from 2019 by 20% to 5.65 million people. Of this, 3.8 million (69%) are in areas of sever needs or highly sever needs (SNFI HNIn Idleb, REACH ENT (Feb.2020) showed that shelter ranked as the 1st priority with newly-arrived IDPs required tents (92%), tarps (65%) and roofing material (24%). Inability to protect themselves against the winter weather, stagnant water and unhealthy conditions, lack of sanitation facilities (showers and latrines) and lack of lighting were cited as the main challenges faced by households in terms of shelter and living conditions/In addition, in SARD assessment Jan 2020, done on recent IDPs and most vulnerable families, 69% reported shelter assistance as one of their main priorities for the next 3 months with 63% unable or barely able to meet their current shelter needsThe NFI People in Need has declined by 22% from 2019 to 3.42 million people in 2020. However, this progress is fragile as many communities suffer from high prices, unpredictable inflation, and low wages, while populations routinely report need for items outside the core NFI packages. In addition, there is still ongoing displacement, with roughly one million people expected to be displaced in 2020.SARD conducted a rapid assessment with 1,562 newly displaced households affected by the recent hostilities in NWS. Findings show that food, NFI, hygiene items, shelter remained households’ top five priority needs, with 98% of households reporting NFI (98%) assistance as a priority for the next 3 months. Assessment done on most vulnerable communities show NFI was ranked by 87% of households as their main priorities for the next 3 months and that 73% were unable or barely able to meet their NFI needs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Shelter activities are monitored by SARD’s M&E team on a regular basis. SARD has a strong (CRM) that is advertised to local communities and offers multiple channels to beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike. Community tensions/crowd management: SARD has built strong relationships with local authorities over the years to ensure the buy-in of activities with local communities. SARD –whenever feasible, offers door-to-door distribution. When this is not possible, distribution sites are chosen in coordination with local authorities and offer gender-disaggregated lines. Distribution of relief is staggered to facilitate crowd management. To avoid the risk of inter-community tensions due to varied assistance packages, SARD follows the SNFI Cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content and ensures that distribution of kits is based on needs and not status. Bias or exclusion errors in selection: Selection criteria are transparent, and communities are informed of selection criteria through sensitisation sessions. Community members are encouraged to report any complaints – including on selection, to SARD’s CRM. Criteria are clearly explained to local authorities who are actively engaged in identifying and preparing beneficiaries list. PSEA: Staff were trained on protection principles, including on PSEA, child protection and GBV. In 2019, SARD has implemented its PSEA-Child Protection policy. All staff have been required to read and sign the new policy along with SARD’s Code of Conduct. PSEA focal points were also appointed. HLP: SARD staff abide by the HLP guidance document developed by the SNFI Cluster to reduce risks of beneficiary’s eviction due to lack of approval from property owner. HLP documentation are required prior any shelter activity and SARD conducts HLP awareness sessions with local communities. Extortion: SARD favours a voucher modality, whenever markets are accessible and functioning.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

*Assumptions:-Locations of project activities are secure and accessible.-Supplies, contractors, commodities and materials are accessible.-No Housing Land and Property (HLP) disputes.-No interfering from armed groups and no divert aid.-Women are equally included in the decision-making process.-IDPs population are not excluded and/or neglected.-Protection is mainstreamed throughout the response promoting meaningful access, safety, and dignity with "do not harm" as a key principle.*Risks and Mitigating measures •Insecurity conditions result in accessibility problems and exposure to risks to staff and beneficiaries.•Escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in target areas -Ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access.-Staff and volunteers from the local community are hired.-Staff trained on remote management.-Re-direction of programming into other accessible governorates.-Security of staff remains of utmost importance and work will be suspended in case of serious threats endangering the staff and the program.-Local Security Plan for operations inside Syria is established and shared among all staff. LSP is a document summarizes all Standard Operating Procedures as well as contingency plans. It gives field staff and SARD management team guidelines how to mitigate risks during the field operations inside Syria, and how to behave in a certain incident situation.-Risk assessments, risk matrix, situation scenarios, and mitigation measures are being reviewed quarterly a year and on ad-hoc bases.-The field team releases a monthly security report. It includes security & safety recommendations for the staff.-A monthly security map is produced; including locations of residence of staff, areas of operation, conflict areas and air strikes.-All routes of transportation vehicles of field staff are mapped, and the routes are modified in accordance with security reports.-SARD coordinates with OCHA Access Working Group (AWG)
