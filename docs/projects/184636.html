Project Summary

The high humanitarian needs in Somalia are driven by seasonal floods, severe drought conditions, acute water diarrhoea/ cholera outbreaks exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. According to 2022 HNO, The population in need of WASH services in 2022 is estimated at 1,619,415 IDPs and 4,818,944 vulnerable host communities. Access to basic water supply in Somalia is 37% in rural and 79 % in urban areas. The JMCNA assessment report 2021 approximates that 39% of the vulnerable people in Somalia do not have access to sustained, improved and safe water source for drinking and other domestic uses, while 25 per cent still walk for more than 15 minutes to collect water from a nearest water source. In addition, 16% of households do not have access to enough drinking water while 45% reported having no access to soap, while more than half of HHs reported lacking access to improved latrines and menstrual hygiene materials. CCCM cluster reports indicate that the majority of those most acutely affected are vulnerable groups; women, elderly, refugee returnees, minority groups and people living with disability (PLWD), putting further stressing affected families and limiting their ability to cope. In October 2021, Ministry of Water and Energy-Jubaland (Gedo Regional Office) and Regional WASH Cluster (Gedo Region) needs assessment report indicates that the drought situation in Gedo region is continually deteriorating. This has worsened in Doolow where estimated 5,244 household have no access to clean and adequate water Doolow due to displacements during recent inter clan conflicts in 17 riverine villages. The same scenario exists in Luuq District where more than 7, 120, HHs living in northern and south rural villages are also in need of basic WASH services including specific appeal for hygiene kits and sanitation tools. The inadequate access to water and sanitation services are particularly prevalent across the majority of congested IDP sites compared to host communities, which in turn leads to higher rates of acute diarrheal and other water-borne diseases, increasing the risk of malnutrition and disability. Further, aggravating the recurrent drought situation in Somalia, the Somalia Water and Land Information Management SWALIM reports that around 40% of existing water supply systems are non-functional mainly due to weak water supply management resulting to affected populations to depend on water trucking. For the year 2022, COOPI proposes to address the immediate Water, Sanitation and Hygiene needs of the severely affected people while sustaining ongoing response in Doolow, Luuq, Owdweyne, Baidoa and Banadir through activities aligned with the Humanitarian Programme Cycle for 2022. As part of the intervention, COOPI will purposefully target PLWD during identification of beneficiaries, design and build facilities to ensure accessibility to all, ensure Water Management Committees include at least one member with disability and that WASH campaigns are all inclusive.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The target areas experience chronic water scarcity and the affected communities routinely mobilise their resources to purchase water from mobile water tankers. From field experience and practice, COOPI proposes to provide pre-printed cash vouchers that will be distributed to the targeted beneficiaries, which will then be redeemed for water at convenience temporary water points with movable storage. Contracted local water commercial suppliers will replenish the water as required. The vendors will receive their payments per water utilized as the voucher system is self-regulating and reduce logistical workload by the field team. This design is well suited in insecure areas, can managed remotely, transparent, and empowers communities.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The use of the existing commercial vendors utilizes will ensure that local water tanker businesses are supported and strengthened. Exit from the intervention will also be easier, as the commercial water tankers are still available in the area for those who would like to continue purchasing water. In addition, only the targeted households that are most vulnerable will receive the vouchers thus eliminating blanket distributions and in case vouchers are misplaced, the field team will easily verify from the records. Finally, the voucher system will ensure the beneficiaries receive water at the current market prices without inflation brought about by contracting many water suppliers common in blanket targeting.
