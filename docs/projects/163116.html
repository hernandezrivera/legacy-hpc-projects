Project Summary

A total of 60% of Zimbabwe's population is considered food-insecure and living in a household that is unable to obtain enough food to meet basic needs. According to the July 2019 Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment report, nearly 5.5 million people, including 2.6 million children, in rural Zimbabwe are projected to need urgent humanitarian assistance during the peak hunger period January – March 2020.More than 72,200 children are likely to be affected by acute malnutrition in all 63 districts. Rising food insecurity is likely to reverse nutrition improvements noted in 2017. Inadequate access to safe water and sanitation has caused recurrent disease outbreaks. An estimated 780,000 people remain at risk of WASH-related disease outbreaks. Eleven districts are projected to have more than 70 per cent of their households having inadequate means to meet their food needs without resorting to severe livelihoods and consumption coping strategies. Throughout the 2019/2020 agricultural season, areas of greatest concern include Kariba, Binga, Gokwe North, Mbire, Buhera, Mudzi, and Chipinge with all of them already classified in IPC phase 3 and 4, hence the need for assistance in these areas. . There are rising protection risks, particularly for women and girls, due to economic stress and food insecurity. Girls are particularly vulnerable to family separation, early marriage, teenage pregnancy, domestic violence and extreme.
To alleviate food insecurity and reduce vulnerability of food insecure households through provision of food vouchers, promoting sustainable agricultural innovation and improved purchasing power, access to water , sanitation , hygiene (WASH) information and services and improved access to health information and services for the entire targeted districts but prioritizing those at most risk such as pregnant mothers, children under 5 and those hard to reach such as the disabled, elderly and marginalized religious or ethnic groups.
Approximately 5000 households (HH) receive food vouchers for 4 months, during peak hunger periods, 4000 small holders farmers (60% women) trained in Climate Smart Agriculture , Nutrition and post harvest management receive agricultural input starter packs (seed, fertilizer, tools. chemicals) as per agreed guidelines in the 2020/2021 cropping season.
To ensure that target communities have improved access to potable water and good health and hygiene information and services 1200 EHTs, Village health workers and volunteers will be trained in Participatory Health and Hygiene Education (PHHE) and how to properly refer cases, 80 village health clubs set up, 40000 HHs will be reached with health and hygiene messages. 40 water points will be rehabilitated serving 100000 people.
For improved health within the communities, 25 health centres will be provided with emergency medical commodities for Cholera and diarrhoeal disease outbreaks . These centres have a population coverage of 62, 500 people. In addition, 100 health staff will be trained in diarrhoeal diseases case management in at least 25 health facilities.
Building on already ongoing work on Disaster Risk Management, the project will facilitate health staff in the national epidemiology department to train and revitalise provincial and 4 district teams on rapid response to disease outbreaks and related emergencies
For increased availability and accessibility of multi-sectoral protection services for survivors of violence, including Gender-based violence (GBV), sexual exploitation and abuse, and separated and unaccompanied children, 1200 survivors will be identified and referred to multi-sectoral service providers on GBV and Child protection. The community-based psycho-social support approach will be used to ensure healthy mental well-being of victims and survivors. To enhance our inclusiveness, one of the faith-based consortium members will also use psycho-social support approach via the faith structures.
