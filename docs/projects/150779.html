Needs

The prolonged Israeli blockade of Gaza, the ongoing impacts of the Great March of Return demonstrations, and internal political divisions coincided with the crisis of the PA salaries cuts and the electricity shortage continue to have a devastating impact on the socioeconomic conditions for Palestine refugees, severely restricting the movement of people and goods and crippling the once vibrant local economy. The socioeconomic context continues to be characterized by widespread unemployment and stagnating low incomes, continuing to trap the majority of the population in the Gaza Strip, 70 per cent of whom are Palestine refugees, into poverty. According to Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics, the overall unemployment in Gaza reached 53.7% in the second quarter of 2018. Unemployment reached 56.0 per cent for refugees, compared to 49.3 per cent for non refugees. The moderate boosts to the economy generated by the post conflict reconstruction activities after 2014 have proved to be transitory and have not been enough to balance the salary cuts imposed on Palestinian Authority’s employees and the cuts in energy supply, as well as the protracted effects of the land and sea blockade which entered its twelveth year in 2018. These trends have had a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups, including women and young people. Unemployment is particularly high among women with shockingly high 78.3 per cent. In addition to lack of job opportunities, women face social barriers to employment in Gaza, particularly when this involves travel to a workplace away from home. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also particularly affected, with unemployment rate of 71.1 per cent, while young women face extremely high unemployment rate of 92.6 per cent in the second quarter of 2018. Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. Consistently high unemployment rates and deteriorating purchasing power have eroded many households’ capital and coping mechanisms, rendering the majority of the population of Gaza dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs and trapped about half of them into food insecurity. Persistently high levels of food insecurity in Gaza are not due to the unavailability of basic commodities in the local market, but rather to households’ lack of economic access to food, which, if unaddressed, would have lasting consequences on both the extent and severity of food insecurity. If to date hunger and undernourishment are not widespread in Gaza, it is because of the assistance provided by UNRWA and other humanitarian organizations. UNRWA projects that more than one million refugees in Gaza will require emergency food assistance in 2019, an estimated 98,000 of whom are chronic poor and will be assisted through the Agency’s core, human development programming. The increase in emergency needs is dramatic, almost 13 fold increase on the 80,000 refugees who required assistance at the start of 2000. UNRWA identifies refugees in need of assistance through a Poverty Assessment Survey (PAS), conducted by UNRWA social workers during home visits to each household.

Activities

UNRWA will provide food assistance to Palestine refugee families surveyed through the Agency specific poverty based targeting system, identified as living on less than $3.87 per person per day. UNRWA estimates that 1,009,990 refugees (208,303 families), including persons with disabilities and female headed households, will require emergency food assistance. Within this caseload, 620,310 refugees living on less than $1.74 per person per day will receive a food basket covering 1,675 kcal per person per day, equivalent to 80 per cent of the standard daily caloric requirement. 389,680 refugees living below the $3.87 poverty line will receive 902 kcal per person per day, equal to 43 per cent of the standard caloric requirement. Each eligible refugee family will receive a food basket every quarter which will include flour, rice, sugar, sunflower oil, whole milk, red lentils, chickpeas and canned fish. Quantities of each item are determined by the poverty status category and by the actual household size. Rations will be distributed at the 12 UNRWA distribution centres across the Gaza Strip. In 2019, UNRWA will continue to provide nutritionally improved food baskets, while also guaranteeing a fair, equal and consistent distribution. Community is engaged through effective consultation with refugee communities to ensure accountability. The UNRWA Monitoring and Evaluation team, in close coordination with the Relief and Social Services Programme (RSSP), will continue to regularly monitor the quality of the food distributed to beneficiaries, ensuring continued feedback from the community through focus group sessions and regular communication with beneficiaries. Targeting is based on a proxy means testing system, introduced in 2009 and adjusted in 2015 to better reflect the rapidly changing socioeconomic realities in Gaza. This methodology, based on data collected during systematic home visits conducted by trained UNRWA social workers, determines a household’s economic status through interviews during which information is collected on proxies such as housing conditions, asset ownership, education, employment or health status. Any family can obtain updated information on its eligibility status at the nearest Area Relief and Social Services Office. Families who are not satisfied with their eligibility classification can appeal if (i) their ration was reduced or interrupted (if the family is reclassified from abject poor to absolute or non poor, or from absolute poor to non poor) and (ii) they were found non poor after a new application or re application. This process ensures accountability and promotes transparency with the community. The food eligibility system takes into account the specific cases of wives from polygamous marriage, divorced women and separated or abandoned women (not officially divorced), both to guarantee that their poverty status is assessed fairly and that they are assisted with dignity. Particular attention is given by the social workers to the identification and assistance of “abandoned” women and their children who, besides social stigma, are also exposed to specific risk factors. Not being formally recognized as divorced, they do not receive any regular financial support from the husband and often have to rely on ad hoc and weak social networks. UNRWA will make sure these vulnerable families do not fall through the system and are able to be assessed to receive food assistance independently from the husband.
