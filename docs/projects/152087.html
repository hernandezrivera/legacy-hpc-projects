Project Summary

Since the beginning of 2015, the North-East of Nigeria has witnessed an increase in violence perpetrated by the Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG), causing a major humanitarian crisis. The intensification of attacks has resulted in prolonged insecurity, exacerbating the plight of vulnerable civilians and triggering waves of forced displacement as well as violation of the human rights, also resulting into forms of Gender Based Violence (GBV) and other protection concerns. More than 4,000 children have been abducted, killed, recruited or have been victims of violent attacks in the ongoing crisis. Such vulnerable girls and boys and their caregivers require socio-economic and psychosocial assistance to recover from their experiences and to reintegrate back into society. The conflict in north-east Nigeria has exposed threats to the mental health and well-being of children. More than 1.9 million girls, boys and caregivers require psychosocial support services due to the severe distress, hardship and displacement. In addition, adolescent boys and girls require basic life skills support to help them cope better with the threats and challenges occasioned by the conflict. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but it has also negatively affected the ability of parents to appropriately care for their children. At least 150,000 caregivers of vulnerable children, particularly female-headed households, foster and single caregivers with several children, will require psychosocial or socio-economic support to assist them to better cater for children in their care.
The proposed project aims to strengthen the existing protection and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) response to the humanitarian crisis that has developed in Nigeria and has directly affected three states in the North-East: Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. The project will adopt a community-based approach ensuring community engagement in the different phases of the program design and implementation. Ensuring active participation of communities (caregivers, children and adolescents), using appropriate communication approaches and strategies that emphasize and ensure communities’ needs are listened to, and help them to cope and address issues (unhealthy and unsafe practices). In order to provide direct psychosocial support and services to the affected populations especially children, adolescents and caregivers, IOM’s MHPSS mobile team members will continue to provide services in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States. The psychosocial activities will include lay counselling to provide basic emotional support to distressed children and caregivers, informal education to adolescents and adults, child protection sensitization and case identification, health talks, and recreational activities with a therapeutic aim targeting especially children and teenagers and informal education. Integrated small scale livelihood activities will be provided to adolescents and caregivers. The MHPSS teams will provide a range of services, throughout the pyramid of services, based on the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS and Child Protection (CP) in emergency settings. In addition, an IOM referral team will responsible for conducting preliminary assessments of potential mental health cases, referrals to the Federal Neuro Psychiatric Hospital in Maiduguri or a mental health facility in Yola, as well as additional referrals to the child protection and the education sectors. These teams will also support patients in following up their treatment and providing psycho-education to caregivers/families. In hard to reach areas, IOM will continue to support the deployment of psychiatric nurses (from the Federal Neuro Psychiatric Hospital of Maiduguri). Technical supervision and support will be provided through regular training and weekly supervision for psychosocial support mobile teams. Capacity building activities on MHPSS and CP will also be provided to humanitarian actors.

Needs

The crisis in North-East Nigeria remains one of the most severe in the world. In the three-worst‐affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, the insurgency has displaced 1.9 million people. Over 80 per cent of IDPs are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis, and over 60 per cent are living among host communities while other IDPs are living in formal and informal camps, collective settlement centres, and transitional centres and in host communities. The insurgency has led to a meltdown of socioeconomic and development structures, the destruction of health facilities, security, dysfunctional legal and justice systems amidst increasing safety and security concerns. Termed a protection crisis, human rights violations including GBV occurring within IDP camps and other settlements are widespread. Insecurity, triggered by the insurgency and military operations against NSAGs, has increased the number of displaced persons with an estimated 130,000 new arrivals in various locations since the end of October 2017. These movements present major humanitarian challenges to the already stretched resources in arrival locations. The ensuing insecurity continues to compromise safety, propagate a lack of access to essential services for the communities and hinder the capacity for humanitarians to provide much-needed life-saving support; hence the difficulty in humanitarian aid delivery. The protection and well-being of 2.6 million children and adolescents (1.4 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in the North-East remains seriously at risk mainly due to the complex and protracted conflict. At least 6,000 girls and boys in North East Nigeria are estimated to have been recruited, abducted or held by NSAGs; approximately 50% of these children are survivors of sexual and gender-based violence. 1,405 child-headed households also require enhanced support and access to basic services. These children are more vulnerable to abuse, violence and exploitation if not provided with appropriate and timely protection services that include family reunification and alternative care. The conflict in North-East Nigeria has exposed threats to the mental health and psychosocial well-being of children. More 1.9 million girls, boys and caregivers require psychosocial support services due to the severe distress, hardship and displacement. In addition, adolescent boys and girls require basic life skills support to help them cope better with the threats and challenges occasioned by the conflict. IOM MHPSS teams provide informal education at the safe spaces/resource centers and coordinate with child protection actors supporting formal education. Looking at the trend of release of children from administrative custody in the past two years , nearly 3,000 boys and girls are expected to be released in 2019 and will require socio-economic reintegration assistance. Sustained follow-up and community-based approaches to reintegration for children formerly associated with armed groups will be required to mitigate risks such as stigmatization and rejection within communities. More than 4,000 children have been abducted, killed, recruited or have been victims of violent attacks in the ongoing crisis. Such vulnerable girls and boys and their caregivers require socio-economic and psychosocial assistance to recover from their experiences and to reintegrate back into society. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but it has also negatively affected the ability of parents to appropriately care for their children At least 150,000 caregivers of vulnerable children, particularly female-headed households, foster and single caregivers with several children, will require psychosocial or socio-economic support to assist them to better cater for children in their care.

Activities

Activity 1: Support the deployment of 120 psychosocial support mobile team members, including to newly accessible areas and areas of return in Yobe, Borno and Adamawa state. Activity 2: Provide culturally appropriate structured recreational activities for children, adolescents and caregivers as well as informal education for adults and youths through psychosocial support mobile teams and facilitating access to formal education through referrals to education actors. Activities will be tailored according the age and gender categories. Activity 3: Provide focused psychosocial support services through lay counseling and support groups for children, adolescents and caregivers provided by trained psychosocial support mobile team members. The focused services will be tailored on an individual or problem based basis, taking into account age and gender categories. Activity 5: Support affected populations with increased access to specialized services and follow up for individuals suffering from mental disorders (through supporting referrals and treatment, through the deployment of governmental mental health care workers) Activity 6: Provide monthly training and weekly supervision for the psychosocial support mobile teams. Activity 7: Provide training on MHPSS concepts for relevant stakeholders and partners involved in the field of mental health and psychosocial support. Develop guidance notes on MHPSS and CP, referencing the already existing materials of the sector. Activity 8: Provision of re/integration assistance through vocational training and livelihoods activities integrated with MHPSS to adolescents and caregivers. Vocational training and livelihood activities will target vulnerable protection/ child protection beneficiaries. Adopting a do no harm approach, and to avoid a possible stigmatization of beneficiaries, livelihood beneficiaries will also include non-extremely vulnerable persons both the IDP and host community population. Activity 9: Mainstreaming MHPSS and child protection approach intervention (through trainings and technical guidance) targeting various sectors of the humanitarian response. Activity 10: Provision of an integrated Counter Trafficking in Crisis response. This will include awareness raising targeting conflict affected population, provision of direct victim assistance through National Agency for the Prosecution of Trafficking in Persons, capacity building of relevant actors. Activity 12: Streamlining existing referral mechanisms across units for better coordination, monitoring, and follow up for efficient referrals for child protection, GBV, health, and lifesaving concerns of the affected populations. This includes support to life saving and emergency health referrals.
