Project Summary

UNIDOR Child Protection team will continue to provide comprehensive protection response services and create a safe and protective environment for children who are at risk of or have experienced abuse, exploitation, neglect, and violence as a result of recurrent crisis and conflicts, assisting them in healing, recovering, and feeling safe again while collaborating with their caregivers and the local government and community-level prevention mechanisms in accordance with the Minimum Standards for CP and national
level SOPs, guidelines, and protocols. The UNIDOR CP team will reach 40,000 (12,000 Boys and men, 28,000 women and girls) people through an integrated response of static and mobile response approaches in the CP AOR priority locations (severity 2 and 3) of Rubkona county in Unity State. The proposed child protection responses are linked with the strategic objectives of the cluster and three domains of the CP AOR for HRP 2023-2024. Case management services will be provided to children who have concerns about their safety. FTR services, alternative care arrangements for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), and protection cash aid will also be provided to meet the basic needs of the children. The Mental Health and Psycho social Support (MHPSS) service will be provided for children and caregivers with psychological distress using a combination of approaches such as Safe Healing and Learning Space (SHLS) and schools for Social and Emotional Learning, Families Make the Difference Curriculum for parenting skills of caregivers, Supporting Adolescents and their Families in Emergencies Curriculum (SAFE) for life skills of adolescents, and UNIDOR’s Brighter Futures Model for malnourished under five years children with protection concerns. Finally, the community-based child protection (CBCPN) structures will be established/strengthened to the lower structure of the community, community outreach on CP awareness and COVID-19 prevention messages, and capacity building of the frontline staff and community volunteers. The response will be complementary to the existing UNIDOR’s GBV, general protection, nutrition, health, and livelihood programs to ensure comprehensive care. The existing health and nutrition facilities will serve as screening and awareness-raising spots and UNIDOR will establish CP help desks. UNIDOR’s GBV centers will also serve as a point of referral for rape survivor girls, and the livelihood section will support the cash-based intervention. In addition, the CP team will be part of the integrated mobile team in all locations where UNIDOR is operating.

1. Needs

UNIDOR has been Implementing Child protection interventions through responding to emergency needs of the most vulnerable and at risk children and their caregivers in Koch,Leer,Mayandit,Rubkokna counties. With an approximate number of 50,000 people in need of which our target reach was 20,000. among these 60% are girls,35% boys and 15% care givers. Triggers of needs for this year is basically the sudan crisis that happenned a few months ago and let to a huge displacement to south sudan in locations of Ruweng,Mayandit,Leer, Koch and Rubkona counties. According to the UNHCR dashboard many returnees are female-headed households with children. Children account for a large number of returnees and refugees arriving through Nyaruop Port in Eastern part of Koch County. According to Returnees monitoring report from Child Protection partners and RRC in mayandit, leer, Rubkona and Koch County, It is indicated by RRC director that around 120 HH with an approximate of 720 individuals arrived over the last two weeks but indicated that some new returnees are still on coming the number might raise depending on the influx of the returnees, Access roads to nearby towns are closed by rainy water and it takes about 2/3 days for returnees to arrive in towns like rubkuay, Koch and Leer town. Posing high risks on child protection concerns include unaccompanied children, emotional traumas, separation from families, high risks of child labour, exploitation, and engagement with armed groups, children’s limited access to food exposing them to negative copying mechanisms like wild fruits, water lilies, stealing, prostitution and to men as household heads food insecurity triggers trauma, abuse of drugs, low self-esteem and anxiety, Education and other basic services and exposure to gender based violence risks. According to Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) Survey Report in Mayandit County, Unity State, South Sudan. Being the middle of the lean season, the influx of returnees has put a lot of constrains on the already depleted food stock of the host community. Specific needs of these households during the crisis period include emergency food or cash assistance and support in building resilience against floods. UNIDOR seek to respond to child protection critical needs/ services in Rubkona county with emergency Mental health and psycho social support to returnees, IDP’s, and host communities and Ensure that vulnerable and at-risk girls, boys, men and women including children with disabilities have improved access to quality lifesaving child protection services. 2. Project activities

To ensure child protection services are delivered, UNIDOR will implement activities inline with the cluster strategies of Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. presence/missions to field and deep field locations. With the following activities: (i) Working with protection partners and communities to identify children with disabilities and at risk to provide individual specialized case management services and MHPSS that meet their unique needs, including cash-based assistance. (ii) Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. presence/missions to field and deep field locations, Working with protection partners and communities to identify and document UASC and provide them with family tracing and reunification services, (iii)Working with protection partners and communities of affected people and update child protection referral pathways, (iv) Working with protection partners and communities of affected people to provide children with disabilities and at risk with PSS services, especially in child safe. Working with protection partners, as well as authorities and existing community-based structures to organize and implement CPiE training for community leaders. This will lead to the implementation of the following specific activities; (i) provide Psycho-social Support (PSS) to survivors of GBV focused on healing, empowerment and recovery in Leer and Mayandit counties. UNIDOR will provide daily PSS activities at individual and group level at the child friendly spaces . (ii) Provide livelihood skills building activities will be carried out at the spaces to engage women and girls in productive activities and to support their psycho social well being. The proposed project will target women and girls in this activity.(ii) provide comprehensive and quality case management services to vulnerable conflict affected women, girls, boys and men in the targeted location. (iii)provide confidential and survivor-centred case management and psycho social support services across the project sites to GBV Survivors. Case management services will be guided by the inter-agency guidelines and tools. GBVIMS will be implemented to ensure safe and ethical data management. (iv) Support referral pathways for access for legal aid and access the nearest available centres and safe spaces that provide case management.(iii) UNIDOR will use the highly innovative SASA and EMAP approaches the proposed project will seek to implement social norms change programming to prevent CP/GBV through shifting attitudes and behaviors in project locations. The proposed approach SASA! is a community-based approach, international standard, and best practice for realizing positive social change to bring about a reduction in violence against women and girls. SASA! has four key phases: Start phase corresponds to “pre-contemplation”. Awareness phase corresponds to “contemplation”. Support phase corresponds to “preparation for action”. Action phase corresponds to “action” and “maintenance”. The proposed intervention will begin when the baseline and early ‘Start’ phase activities are underway. Community activists are identified women and men who will be ‘champions’ of SASA! and make a long-term commitment to facilitating activities. Community action groups are groups who integrate SASA! into their existing services or group activities (such as, health workers, faith-based leaders, local leaders, and local level peer groups – such as the protection groups described above). Activities part of the ‘Start’ phase will include training and capacity building of stakeholders in gender-power relations, facilitation skills, how to mentor others, making helpful referrals, using communication
