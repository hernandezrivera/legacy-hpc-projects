Project Summary

This Project is designed to respond to the existing food security situation caused by the prevailing drought conditions in Somalia. According to 2019 Post Gu results by FSNAU and FEWSNET up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2019. Also, an estimated one million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through mid-2020. The Somalia HNO 2019, shows that 4.2 million people require humanitarian assistance and protection and up to 2.6 million people remain internally displaced. The area that we are targeting, that is Lower Shabelle has experienced climatic shocks and so there is an increased severity of needs. This has resulted in 370,000 people in need and out of the number 137,000 are IDPs according to the Somalia HNO 2019. The project aims at preventing further deterioration of the affected population in this region and offer timely and appropriate assistance to households that lack food and other household needs. AWI will offer humanitarian interventions that meet the immediate needs and build resilience by starting with providing food Aid (Rice, Sugar, oil, and Pulses) through vouchers in IDP camps for two months as per the minimum expenditure basket. Due to lack of proper infrastructures and poor maintenance, reflection workshop with the irrigation committees will thus be conducted to work out modalities of restoration and maintenance of these infrastructures. Through cash for work activities, there will be a creation of temporary jobs to men and women just before the rainy seasons, providing 5USD daily rate to the laborers for two months which will increase their income. The infrastructures will contribute to the provision of water and also facilitate the transaction of businesses through feeder roads. Both the food assistance and the cash for work intervention will allow beneficiaries to free up some of their meager income and establish a quick economical boost which acts as a supplement to their household income helping them to afford appropriate food and establish an income for further investment in their livelihoods while reducing the level of vulnerability. Also, it stimulates the recovery of the local economy by creating short term work and supporting local businesses through the purchase of items (food and Non-food). Direct cash transfers also provide a flexible resource to affected households and empower individuals to spend money according to their needs and priorities. AWI will also provide households with agricultural inputs such as crop and vegetable seeds and land preparation to households before Deyr and GU rains and train farmers on improved farming techniques to give them knowledge and skills on how to improve crop production. Household metal silos provision and training on proper food storage will be done to farmers before harvesting to reduce post-harvest losses. The target beneficiaries are drawn from across different livelihoods who have been affected by the drought and need assistance to prevent further deterioration of their livelihood situation.

Project Activities

AWI will directly target 2,327 household (13,962 people) in need including IDPs. The following activities will be the core milestones in the project. 1. Provide food vouchers (Rice, Pulses, sugar and Oil) to 800 Households (4,800 beneficiaries) in IDP settlements with over 50% going to women headed households. To help them meet the immediate need 2. Provision and distribution of farming inputs, fertilizer, crop, legume and vegetable seeds providing 20 kgs per household, land preparation of 1 hectare per farmer to 440 HH (2, 640 beneficiaries) and provision of silos to 240 HH in Lower Shabelle. 200 Selected farmers will be trained on modern farming techniques and safe food storage and they will in turn train people in their respective villages. 3. Creation of temporary job opportunities for 407 Households (2442 beneficiaries) through rehabilitation of irrigation schemes (canals and water catchments) and feeder road rehabilitation.

Mitigation Measures

Transparency is key for the projects of AWI and this will greatly mitigate many of the risks that may affect beneficiaries. Specifically, the following measures need to be mentioned for each risk:2a. Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. To mitigate this AWI will hold a general meeting where project objectives, activities and selection criteria are explained to the community. The people and community representatives will have a phone number where AWI staff can be contacted in case of suspected exclusion with clear follow up procedures. This feedback mechanism will be shared with representatives of all minority groups and marginalized communities. 2b. Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls, and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes. To mitigate this separate meeting with Women will be held during the inception of the project to ensure the needs of these groups are included. During the assessment phase of AWI, these groups were also already consulted so their feedback is included in this proposal. 3d. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources. In order to do not harm and promote peaceful coexistence, all groups will be included in the projects. This also includes (a limited amount of) men and some people of majority groups, although their most vulnerable are considered the main target. 4b. Protection mainstreaming: Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distribution. AWI will work with protection actors on how to reduce the risks. Concrete the registration and distributions will be done in a transparent way where the entire community can be present which minimizes the risks. Community elders, imams and respected representatives of the community will also be present. 4d. Protection mainstreaming: Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms. AWI will install a community hotline, a phone number where all beneficiaries can give their complaints, compliments and other feedback. The custodian of the number is someone in AWI but outside the project. Strict procedures will ensure proper follow up for each concern and complaint.

Please provide further explanation

For agriculture and pastoral households that face recurring food shortages in lean seasons, assets (livestock, land and grain storages) play a vital role in their income and livelihoods. Households that are supported through these objectives in building up their assets over time through savings and other means of which AWI is aiming at will effectively improving their future prospects while increasing their resilience to weather-related and other shocks.To build resilience and ensure the sustainability of food insecurity in the target areas, the project intervention is designed to address the immediate needs of the communities targeted and work towards the long-term goal in improving their assets, capacity and reducing future risks/shocks. AWI will collaborate with the host communities and the IDPs to identify the needs in terms of resilience which are directly related to food security and livelihoods. AWI will have a capacity building to the target groups in good farming practices and income generation, both of which will support resilience in the community. Further AWI will work with the local authorities and community leaders to find durable solutions in capacity building, income generation activities, and community assets and even hand over of projects at the end.
