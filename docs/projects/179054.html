Project Summary

This project will support 250 farmer households with agricultural inputs and technical support for wheat crop planting. This support will improve bread production (the staple food for Syrian people) which has been negatively affected by the lack of wheat and high prices of production supplies.
The project will strengthen local production capacity and local markets, therefore, the self-reliance of the community to be stable against external conflicts. Also, the project will contribute to enhancing the quality of production as we will support farmers with high-quality seeds and all needed agricultural inputs (fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides, irrigation fuel), in addition to technical support through training and monitoring visits to farmers’ fields and providing guidance advice in order to have highest and best production.
This project will also support 400 vulnerable households including female-headed and child-headed households living in peri-urban and rural areas to produce their own nutritious food by distributing vaccinated chicken and chicken feed to 200 families and distributing sheep and fodder to 200 families, in addition to supporting them with the required knowledge and basic advice to achieve the best possible production of eggs, milk, and meat.
This will strengthen and re-establish productive small backyard chicken and sheep flocks to support local food production and to improve household nutrition, especially for children and pregnant women, and to open up additional sources of income for rural and
peri-urban women through small-scale marketing of eggs, milk, and meat.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO2021, the food security situation in Syria has steadily worsened over the past three years, the 56 percent increase in the number of food-insecure people from 7.9 million in 2019 to 12.4 million by the end of 2020 is unprecedented. Two out of three Syrians are in need of some form of food and agricultural assistance, with an additional 1.8 million at risk of falling into food insecurity. The number of severely food insecure people has more than doubled, from 0.6 million in 2019 to 1.27 million people in 2021 (six percent of the population). The high cost and limited availability of agricultural inputs, the degradation of livelihoods, the increased cost of living, and the annual wheat deficits are factors of increasing food insecurity. HNO 2021 also addressed that farmers also suffer from limited access to markets due to physical and economic constraints, limited access to finance, damaged infrastructure such as irrigation canals, limited access to relevant early warning information and the impact of COVID-19 on agriculture value chains, especially poultry. HNO 2021 showed that the Livestock breeders’ remain vulnerable since their animals are at risk of disease outbreaks; with limited pasture availability and high feed costs, many livestock farmers are forced to sell-off their animals,thus adversely affecting overall livestock production levels and compromising the long-term livelihoods of livestock keepers.
