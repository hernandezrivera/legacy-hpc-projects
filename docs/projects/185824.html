Project Summary

NRC will assist beneficiaries to access civil and legal documentation, through the provision of information, counselling and legal assistance. NRC will use mobile teams to ensure that beneficiaries in hard-to-reach areas can have access to these services. Where it is possible, especially in some localities of Far North, sensitization sessions will be improved through combination of mass media, and community-based approaches to deliver culturally adapted messages related to legal and civil documentation.
One of the main targets groups for these services will be children along with their parents who need birth certificates to sit public exams as well as people affected by displacement who face obstacles to access civil and identity documents to enjoy freedom of movement and access to other rights and services.
In addition to addressing the lack of awareness of the procedures to access documentation and providing legal assistance to deal with the judicial and administrative process of late registration of births, ICLA will address financial barriers impeding vulnerable populations from accessing civil and identity documentation. ICLA will advocate to reduce the cost of judicial late registration of births and facilitate free of charge access to the medical certificate required for late registration. After successfully advocating for the operationalisation of the administrative reconstitution procedure in the crisis-affected areas in Far North, NRC will continue with the support of BUNEC to advocate for the implementation of this procedure. ICLA will also contribute to the ongoing national exercise of revising laws and procedures related to identity and civil documentation. Most of the advocacy initiatives will be undertaken through the National Platform on civil documentation that NRC co-leads, as well as through the Far North. NRC will continue to strengthen the capacity of institutions, local authorities, as well as humanitarian actors (e.g., civil registration centres, courts, traditional leaders, etc.) by providing trainings and capacity building on LI, as well as by organising workshops to highlight challenges in accessing civil and identity documentation and to suggest practical solutions through an integrated plan of action. In order to follow up the actions (trainings, recommendation of workshop), joint supervision mission of officials in charge of LI will be made by BUNEC, MINDELVEL and NRC.
Together with relevant authorities and bodies, NRC will explore how to use technology and other innovative approaches to boost birth registration and other civil status aspects.
Access to civil documentation will increase the protection of vulnerable populations and their right to move freely, and the access and enjoyment of basic services, including but not limited to education.
The ICLA programme will benefit the following primary target groups are duty bearers: IDPs, refugees, returnees, vulnerable host communities, and other vulnerable populations facing difficulties in exercising their LI rights in displacement and return areas. Priority will be given to women, including widows, and displaced boys and girls in school without documents, as well as to people with disabilities and special needs, and persons facing challenges in realizing to obtain/recover civil or identity documents due to the conflict/crisis. Beneficiaries of other NRC Core Competencies who need assistance LI to effectively enjoy humanitarian assistance or other rights will also be considered as priorities.
The secondary target group will consist of duty bearers at national and sub-national levels includinglocal and traditional authorities, mayors, civil registration officers, government technical and administrative services, and the court. These are the main actors providing LI services and will be targeted for training but mostly for advocacy initiatives. Technical support will also be provided to civil status centres which are lacking the basic necessities

1 - Besoins / Needs

Following massive displacement in the crisis affected regions, the already weak civil status services have deteriorated. Some civil registries are no longer functional (destructed or civil servants fled) and others are not fully effective, especially in localities receiving IDPs and refugees as the pressure and demand have increased.During NRC’s assessments in the Far North Region conducted in 2020, IDPs and affected communities reported lack of birth certificates for themselves and their children as a challenge. In January 2021, according to the National Civil Status Registration Office (BUNEC), an estimated 400, 979 out of 950,574 pupils in the Far North do not have birth certificates. More than 35,671 (16024 girls (49,92%)) pupils in the last year of primary school will not be able to take national examinations during the academic year 2020/2021 due to lack of birth certificates. Moreover, according to the MSNA (Multi sectorial need assessment) conducted in July 2021 by the IOM, 65% of adults said they not possess national identity cards. The reasons mostly given by respondents to explain the low rate of possession of legal identity documents (birth certificates and ID card) in the region are mainly the lack of financial means due to reduced income; the increased distance to civil status centers as many have been closed due to the conflict so people have to go further: as well as the limited knowledge on procedures and conditions to obtain legal and civil documentation by refugees and IDPs. However, for the community as a whole, COVID has not brought about any change in this process.Information and counselling sessions on legal identity: Mobile ICLA teams will organize sensitization sessions on the birth registration procedure (including late birth registration) and the importance of the birth certificate. They will be deployed in pairs (male and female) in displacement and return areas to provide guidance on accessing the birth registration procedure (including late birth registration) and obtaining birth certificates.Legal assistance for the provision of birth certificates: NRC will organise mobile court hearings in order to establish birth certificates for children (and in particular girls) whose births have not been registered within the legal time limit of three months. This activity will bring together magistrates, clerks, civil registration officers and secretaries, and parents and children. The magistrates will provide a late registration judgment (jugement supplétif) which will then be transcribed into the relevant birth register by the civil registrar and a birth certificate issued. NRC will also address financial barriers impeding vulnerable populations from accessing civil and identity documentation.Capacity building on legal identity: NRC will strengthen the capacity of institutions, local authorities, as well as humanitarian actors (e.g., civil registration centers, courts, traditional leaders, etc.) by providing trainings and capacity building on LI, as well as by organizing workshops to highlight challenges in accessing civil and identity documentation and to suggest practical solutions through an integrated plan of action. Together with relevant authorities and bodies, NRC will explore how to use technology and other innovative approaches to boost birth registration and other civil status aspects. Access to civil documentation will increase the protection of vulnerable populations and their right to move freely, and the access and enjoyment of basic services, including but not limited to education.
