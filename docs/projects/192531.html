Project Summary

Children inside the Palestine camps and gatherings continue to live a life characterized by fear and violence. Boys and girls of all ages face high risks of violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect and their caregivers and communities have exhausted their resources and coping mechanisms to create a safe and protective environment for them. Since 2019 Lebanon has been battling with compounded crises - economic, financial and widespread decrease in the sense of psychosocial safety. The way that Palestinian refugees living in the country experience these multifaceted crises is particularly complex given their already marginalized status in the country, with children paying the highest price.
UNRWA, Save the Children and UNICEF undertook an extensive child safety assessment in March – June 2022, working with local organisations across 11 official Palestine refugee camps and 3 gatherings. The findings highlighted both the extreme crisis children of all nationalities are facing in the camps particularly around sexual abuse and gender-based violence, physical violence and the impact of drugs. Other findings included the lack of cohesion, coordination and collaboration between organisations, with the impact of individual interventions not reaching its potential due to a generalized lack of common objectives. The assessment included a detailed camp level stakeholder analysis and was an initial step towards bringing organisations together to analyse and plan collectively, identifying key ways forward across actors, and building some level of support and buy in from Palestinian leaders. The intervention proposed here is a direct response to that assessment and aims to continue to bring organisations together to work collectively for children’s protection.
The proposed intervention will address the complex needs of Palestinian refugee children in at least 11 Palestine camps across Lebanon. The project will respond to the immediate and long-term protection needs of children by strengthening the community-based capacity to respond and enhancing the resilience, safety and dignity of them, their caregivers and their communities. The proposed intervention has a particular emphasis on preventing and responding to child sexual abuse and gender-based violence through all its actions and will support the strengthening and expansion of gender sensitive community-based systems for child protection through improved community and family support.
The approach will include four pillars:
• Strengthening local level response and prevention of violence against children and gender-based violence through strengthening 10 CPNs
• The development of camp specific referral pathways agreed between all local actors, and capacity building on minimum standards for case management and safe identification and referral.
• Strengthening the capacity within the Palestinian National Security Forces (PNSF) to address child protection and GBV issues.
• Community based activities to support children’s mental health.
Target groups:
• Girls and boys, women and men including members of existing formal community-based structures, such as women’s committees, child protection networks and Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs), as well as local leaders and other key stakeholders identified in each area who play a key role in enhancing a protective environment for children.
• Specific support will be provided to the Palestinian National Security forces (PNSF) and the associated legal support unit to strengthen and increase access to justice for CP and GBV survivors.
• The project will rely on a close collaboration and common goal amongst local organisations, most of whom are members of existing Child Protection Networks (CPNs). These networks will be the key coordination element of the project at a local level, with coordination at a national level also enhanced through the project.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Pillar one: 10 existing CPNs will be revitalized or strengthened and at least one established. Each will develop actions to become more inclusive and effective, ensuring wider community representation. Each will be able to take action to prevent and respond to child protection and GBV issues at camp level, working with Lebanese and Palestinian authorities, national coordination system and with relevant organizations such as UNICEF, etc. We will provide technical capacity building for each CPN including establishment or review of TORs, best practices and sector standards, and referrals. Pillar two:Through participation and input of camp level actors, agreed referral pathways will be set up for safe identification and referral of child protection and GBV cases in each camp. This will include capacity building of local actors to agree parameters and to ensure that pathways are adapted and shared. Pillar three: Identifying CP/GBV focal points within the PNSF to strengthen their understanding and capacity to respond in a safe and dignified way to cases way to cases of CP and GBV. Pillar four:Volunteers in the camps as well as CPNs and other community-based structure will be mobilized in a coordinated way, across the life span of the project to create opportunities for socialization and recreation for children and adolescents. Activities like clownery, climbing, theater will be proposed and agreed in a consultative way with children based on their interest and age.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

As UN Agency, UNRWA is committed to a zero-tolerance approach to sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment and treats it as priority to prevent sexual misconduct and to support those affected. The PSEA Focal Point at UNRWA Field Office ensures that assistance and support is provided in a manner that is timely and victim-centered. Additionally, Lebanon Field Office has undertaken efforts to strengthen awareness and obligations around SEA in recent years through training to frontline staff, the establishment of a field-level PSEA Sub-Group in 2021 which has established an action plan of priority issues across each programme and department.In line with the Agency’s Disability Policy (2022) and Disability Inclusion Guidelines (2017) the Agency has taken important steps towards ensuring that Palestine refugees with disabilities have equal and meaningful access to its services on an equal basis. The project aims to address structural and entrenched gender inequalities.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

UNRWA considers accountability to affected populations to be a cornerstone of quality service provision to meet changing refugee needs and deliver more sustainable development outcomes. A number of formal and informal mechanisms, standards and practices are in place at the field and programme level to ensure the meaningful participation of the Palestine refugee population at various stages in the programming cycle. Some of the actions proposed as part of this intervention are the result of a mapping exercise conducted with children and adolescents in the camps as part of the above mentioned assessment which enabled them to identify risks they face and suggest solutions. A two way dialogue between UNRWA and refugees is already in place, including a feedback system and an hotline. FGDs and community consultations will also take place to ensure different means are offered to children and communities to expand as much as possible the capacity of adults and children to contribute.
