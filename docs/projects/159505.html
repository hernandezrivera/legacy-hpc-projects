Project Summary

NRC will ensure that a greater number of the most vulnerable conflict-affected people in hard-to-reach areas have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, will help reduce vulnerability and increase resilience; Strengthen the capacity of government and civil society legal service providers to serve an increased case load of the IDP and conflict-affected population in need of assistance;
Support economic and social well-being as a durable solution by providing IDPs with equal access to, and enjoyment of right to adequate standard of living without discrimination, including shelter, livelihoods and employment, effective and accessible mechanisms to restore housing, land and property rights, access to personal and other documentation and freedom of movement; Push for the adoption of legislation and practice promoting sustainability and protection in line with international standards.
NRC ICLA in Ukraine aims to support persons affected by the conflict in eastern Ukraine (CAP), broadly defined as individuals who have suffered harm, including physical or mental injury, emotional suffering, economic loss or substantial impairment of their human rights and fundamental freedoms as a direct result of the conflict in eastern Ukraine. CAP include: IDP (registered or not); people at risk of displacement and people who are unable to flee; returnees; host families or communities.
ICLA will endeavour to reach the most vulnerable within the above target groups, notably women/single headed households, especially with a large number of children, elderly (particularly living alone), people with disabilities or limited mobility, extremely poor and no-income families, people of pre-retirement age, people living in rural or remote areas. It is important to stress that this will include persons from GCA and NGCA, the latter being targeted through a range of modalities.
In addition to CAP, ICLA will target duty-bearer beneficiaries - central, regional and local legislative, executive and judicial authorities responsible for various aspects of the CAP issues, and other stakeholder beneficiaries - professional national associations/institutions (Bar Associations; Law Faculties Legal Aid Clinics, Secondary Free Legal Aid Centres) and nation-wide, long-standing NGOs, as well as local civil society organisations / initiatives / associations representing interests of conflict-affected people. Both of these target groups require sensitisation and capacity and institution building in order to cope with the displacement issues.

1 - Needs

On 14 April 2019, hostilities in eastern Ukraine entered their sixth year. The human toll of the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine is critical, with 5.2 million people affected by the crisis, of whom 3.5 million require humanitarian assistance and protection (800,000 people living along the contact line, 2.2 million in non-Government controlled areas-NGCA and 0.5 million in Government controlled areas-GCA). Civilians living in the vicinity of the 457-kilometers long contact line continue facing serious risks to their safety and disproportionate restrictions of the freedom of movement and access to basic necessities, including food, medical care and education. More than 40,000 objects of private property were damaged or destroyed due to shelling. The escalation of hostilities, withdrawal of public services from NGCA and linking access to pensions and social benefits with IDP status, restriction of freedom of movement and transfer of goods across the contact line, “nationalisation” of Ukrainian companies by de facto authorities, economic blockade of NGCA, failure to relocate a large number of State-owned and municipal enterprises and institutions and bodies of local self-government, as well as disruptions to industry transport and small and medium-size enterprise activity have led to decrease in economic activity, widespread job loss, problems of salary arrears, suspension of pensions and social benefits and record-high prices. This, in turn, has caused increased food insecurity levels, loss of access to services and livelihoods and the application of negative coping strategies from food reduction to depletion of savings to illegal or high-risk jobs for people living both in NGCA and in GCA. The Government of Ukraine (GoU) has registered 1.4 million internally displaced people (IDP), of which a half reside in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts, beyond the 20 km area along the contact line. In fact, only about 800,000 IDPs were living somewhat permanently on government-controlled territory at the end of 2019. Many IDPs continue to face bureaucratic impediments and discrimination due to various legislative acts and policies adopted since the beginning of the conflict such as the introduction of residence, identification and verification procedures, imposition of payment of pensions and social benefits through one State bank only - and solely on the basis of an IDP certificate. Internally displaced women are more vulnerable than men, often more financially dependent on other family members than men, more likely to be taking care of children (a 14.5 per cent gender gap), less active on the labour market and in starting their own businesses, and much more frequently faced with insufficient funds and forced to economize than internally displaced men. There are several recent legal developments that impact the situation with rights and freedoms of IDPs. Out of which, the recently established compensation mechanism is the most notable one. Ukrainian Government adopted Decree No. 623 on July 10, 2019, which provides for a one-time limited compensation procedure only for destroyed housing in the East of Ukraine. The main concerns about this mechanism are that the maximum amount of compensation is limited to UAH 300,000 (it is unclear why this amount was chosen and how it was calculated); the mechanism may be applied only to the territory under the control of the Government of Ukraine; the mechanism establishes compensation only for destroyed housing; the person must have legal ownership of the destroyed house; a new damage act must be drawn up for each destroyed house; the mechanism requires the adoption of additional by-laws. Hence, this compensation mechanism has not yet been put into practice and requires the adoption of secondary legislation as well as budgetary financing.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Legal aid (Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance) remains one of the key protection and durable solutions interventions needed for conflict affected people in eastern Ukraine. NRC ICLA Programme will continue to provide accurate, reliable and timely information to the affected population on the relevant legal framework, possibilities for legal assistance and access to basic services, rights and entitlements. In its turn, legal counselling entails a provision of basic, one-on-one assistance on a problem faced by the beneficiary and advice as to what is the best possible course of action to solve a particular problem, while legal assistance implies a degree of representation between NRC, and its partners, and the beneficiary of the intervention in resolution of a specific legal problem or problems. Legal assistance is not limited to in-court representation and involves any intervention by NRC on behalf of beneficiaries before authorities. • ICLA will continue to provide information, counselling and legal assistance in:o ICLA Legal Aid Centres (LACs); o During mobile (home) visits to the most vulnerable;o ICLA desks at local municipal, council or village premises; employment centres; collective and community centres, o Educational or cultural institutions, o Partner offices,o At the Entry/exit crossing points (EECPs) (Stanytsia Luhansk, Maiorsk, Marinka and Novotroitske EECPs) along the line of contact. • Information and counselling will additionally be provided remotely throughout Ukraine (GCA and NGCA) through: o Telephone hotlines & email o Multimedia including Radio, TV, social networks and/or Internet/websites. • Information provided through hard copy and electronic publications and group information sessions. • Legal representation and assistance before the courts and administrative authorities will be provided through partners. • In close coordination with NRC’s Advocacy team, ICLA will strengthen the use of research and advocacy resources to address systemic issues of concern for targeted CAP beneficiaries. • NRC will continue to lead the HLP Technical Working Group, and ensure that resources are used most effectively in coordinating the efforts of humanitarian actors with respect to HLP issues. ICLA will scale down directly implemented legal assistance service provision in communities where government and local civil society have the capacity, or can be given the capacity, to serve existing caseloads. In order to ensure community-based approach and enhance peaceful coexistence in the locations of the NRC ICLA interventions, the following actions will be taken: legal aid will be provided with prior consultations with hosting communities and local authorities to ensure all potential participants are informed beforehand and the most needed and suitable services will be provided; services will be provided to both IDPs and hosting communities representatives to avoid creating social tensions and ensure coexistence; legal aid services could be provided to resolve community-specific legal issues; beneficiaries feedback will be taken into account to improve further interventions. This project will establish links with past/present humanitarian responses (HDN). Furthermore, project activities will be aligned with/contribute to relevant GoU strategies in eastern Ukraine (e.g. IDP Integration Strategy/Action Plan, State Targeted Recovery/Peacebuilding Programme, etc.), as well as create synergies with activities of recovery/development actors. Accountability: NRC policy places a heavy emphasis on ‘rights based’ programming, in which accountability plays an important role. NRC will adhere to the Ukraine Accountability Framework as recommended by the STAIT as both HLP TWG lead and as a program implementer. NRC has a comprehensive Complaints and Feedback mechanism which is independent from programme operations.
