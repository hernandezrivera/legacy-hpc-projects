Project Summary

Maternal newborn and child health MNCH) are priority areas in the Palestinian health sector. The already challenged healthcare system in the oPt experience added strain from the COVID-19 outbreak since 2020 and frequent escalations. The reallocation of resources to respond to the emergencies es has undermined people’s access to quality essential MNCH health services. Maternal mortality ratio was increasing over the past years and showed a sharp increase in 2021 compared to previous years standing at 47.4 per 10000 livebirths, with an increase by 67.2% compared to 2020. COVID-19 infection was the leading cause of death. A study of MMR in 2020 showed that 78.4% of maternal deaths were deemed preventable, Neonatal mortality rate has not been improving over the past five years, represent 66% of infant deaths (MICS 2020) highlighting that the newborn period remains the period of highest risk for all infants. Main causes of neonatal deaths were respiratory and cardiovascular disorders and low birth weight and pre-term. In 2021, NMR for Palestine was 9.6 deaths per 1,000 live births. Moreover, child health care services at PHC and hospital level are under reported due to weak health information system and referral and feedback mechanisms between the two levels of care. In addition, special needs for children with congenital and metabolic disorders are limited special medication at levels of special medications and formulas.
The Health Cluster estimates that more than 60,000 pregnant and lactation women and more than 25,500 high risk newborns in addition to more than 80500 sick children including children with metabolic disorders are newborn are in need health interventions in 2023 to improve the quality of MNCH service. Ninety percent of them are in Gaza and ten percent in the West Bank.
To sustain the provision of quality MCH services at emergency and non emergency times, the Palestinian ministry of health will need the following
• 1)Improve the quality of care at time of birth through
• 1.1) capacity building of health care providers at obstetric hospitals on emergency obstetric care.
• 1.2) Sustain the implementation of early essential newborn care practices in Maternity hospitals in the Gaza and Expansion of EENC to the WB.
• 2) Strengthen neonatal health services in MoH neonatal units through
• 2.1) capacity building of neonatal staff to deal with very sick newborns, including premature and very low birth weight newborn in WB and Gaza.
• 2.2) Procurement of essential life -saving medications for NICUs in Gaza and WB in addition to special medications and baby formula for neonates and children with metabolic disorders as per needs assessment.
• 2.3) establishing baby friendly rooms in Nus, maternity hospitals and PHCs in Westbank and Gaza based on needs
• 3)improve quality of child health services at primary and secondary levels of care through
• 3.1) Capacity building of heath care providers in PHCs and hospitals on the updated IMCH clinical guidelines.
• 3.2) Upgrading the child registry based on the guidelines.
3.3) Conducting SBCC campaigns for caregivers in light of the newly developed child health guidelines WHO will support the Ministry of Health in strengthening the quality of MNCH services at all levels of care. WHO's project has been coordinated at various levels with all counterparts involved in the activities:
(1) Maternity interventions with MoH, UNFPA and UNICEF coordination mechanism

1 - Needs

According to the Health Cluster, more than 60,000 pregnant and lactation women and more than 20,500 high risk newborns in addition to more than 80500 sick children including those with metabolic disorders are in need health interventions in 2023 to improve the quality of MNCH service. 80% are in Gaza and 20% in the West Bank. The covid 19 pandemic and the recurrent escalations in the GS have strained the health system and allocated resources to respond to the Emergency. According to mapping assessment conducted by UNICEF in 2020, MNCH services in Gaza suffers from four major issues that require a budget of around 19 million euro to address the following:• Shortage of equipment, lack of spare parts, reduction in the supply of medicines and consumables, shortage of NCU and maternity beds• Shortage of human resources in terms of specialized professionals (neonatology) and specialist care nurses.•Lack of Special Care Nursery for babies who are born at or after 32 weeks and who weigh more than 1,500 grams who may have some health problems in WB.• Shortage of national standards and strengthening the health information system and reporting on quality of care and referral pathways.lack of access of women in menopausal and premenopausal age to specialized services including gynecological oncology or gynourology services MoH annual report 2021 showed increase in MMR in 2021 standing at 47.4 per 10000 livebirths, with an increase by 67.2% compared to 2020 where more than 70% of these Mortalities due to Covid 19 and hemorrhage. MoH maternal mortality report 2020 showed that more than 79% were deemed preventable ascertaining contributing factors, such as possible weakened primary health care, stock-outs of essential drugs and disposables, and lack of access to family planning services which have led to a serious deterioration in the availability and quality of health services in the Gaza Strip. The EENC clinical coaching approach coupled with regular assessments and action plan by hospital teams has significantly improved care applied during delivery and in the early post-partum period. Since 2017, the Ministry of Health (MoH) in Gaza has adopted Early Essential Newborn Care (EENC), a package of evidence-based interventions applied during delivery and in the immediate newborn period. Adoption of these practices, elimination of harmful practices and revising newborn NICU admission criteria will help to prevent and decrease many cases of sepsis, asphyxia and complications for preterm/LBW babies. EENC practices are now adopted at 16 maternity department run by MoH and Non-governmental organization hospitals. based on the last annual implementation review, EENC implementing hospitals are in need of policies and capacity building to minimize harmful practices and preventable morbidity and mortality. WHO aims at strengthening emergency obstetric care through multidisciplinary obstetric emergency simulation training an international approved approach that has strong evidence base in improving the competencies of health professionals in a safe and effective manner, allowing for the practice of not-so-frequent events without any risk to the patient. Newborn deaths currently represent 66% of infant deaths (MICS 2020) highlighting that the newborn period remains the period of highest risk for all infants. In 2021, NMR for Palestine was 9.6 deaths per 1,000 live births. Main causes of neonatal deaths were respiratory and cardiovascular disorders and low birth weight and pre-term. Care for premature, small and sick newborn which requires admission to neonatal units is challenged by lack of well-trained neonatologists and neonatal nurses in addition to shortages in essential lifesaving equipment, supplies, and medications. At WB and according to the latest PNIPH study, there are 38 neonatal units throughout oPt (27 in the West Bank, 3 in East Jerusalem, and 8 in the Gaza Strip). NICUs in WB are lacking private rooms for mothers to provide breast feeding for their newborns and facilities for breast milk expressions). Most of the practices inside the neonatal units depends on invasive procedure (ventilation and IV access) which raises the risk of acquired sepsis and eventually death. It is worth mentioning that due to the limited resources including shortage in health staff, the age of viability in Gaza strip stands at 26 weeks gestational age and 750 grams compared to 24 weeks in the West bank (2021). In addition to strengthening neonatal health services in MoH neonatal units through capacity building of neonatal staff to deal with very sick new-borns, including premature and very low birth weight newborns and provision of essential equipment, supplies and special medications for sick neonates and neonates with metabolic disorders. Moreover, neonate and child health care services at PHC and hospital level are under reported due to weak health information system and referral and feedback mechanisms between the two levels of centers that care. through its efforts to improve the quality of child health services at PHC level, WHO is working on supporting MoH with developing child health guidelines for PHC (IMCI) through a national committee. Capacity building of PHC staff on the newly developed guidelines and provision of supplies and medications as well as improving the reporting system, documentation and referrals between different level of care among the priority interventions.

2 - Activities

This project is fully aligned with the Health Cluster HNO matrix for 2023 and will focus on three objectives that will target strengthening the quality of health services across the life course through supporting access to healthcare and nutrition that prevent excessive maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality by ensuring availability of safe delivery, essential newborn care, safe spaces, and emergency obstetric services through supporting PA MoH and other health care providers including UNRWA and NGO health care providers. WHO's project has been coordinated at various levels with all counterparts involved in the activities: (1) Maternity interventions with MoH, UNFPA, and UNICEF coordination mechanism. (2) Child and newborn interventions with MoH, UNICEF, and MAP UK coordination mechanism. Moreover, WHO activities are an expansion of the current interventions implemented under the joint AFD project implemented by UNICEF, WHO, and UNFPA.WHO will support: 1) Improve the quality of care at the time of birth through:1.1) capacity building of health care providers at obstetric hospitals on emergency obstetric care. WHO will support introducing multidisciplinary obstetric emergency simulation training; an internationally approved approach that has a strong evidence base in improving the competencies of health professionals safely and effectively, allowing for the practice of not-so-frequent events without any risk to the patient.1.2) Sustain the implementation of early essential newborn care practices in Maternity hospitals in Gaza through procuring essential supplies and medication and equipment and upgrading the environmental standards in the delivery rooms. In addition, an annual implementation review will be conducted to assess the EENC practices and their impact on the quality of care.1.3) Expansion of EENC implementation to hospitals in WB. As per the updated national Every newborn action plan ENAP, WHO is planning to expand EENC implementation to hospitals in the west bank by conducting a baseline AIR, forming of EENC coaching team and start EENC onjob coaching for health care providers at three maternity hospitals in the WB. The intervention will entail also procurement of essential supplies and medication and upgrading the environmental standards based on the baseline AIR. 1.4)strengthen screening, early detection and management of women with gynecological and menopausal disorders through integration package of services in maternity services, updating the protocols and capacity building of health care providers.2) Strengthen neonatal health services in MoH neonatal units through2.1) capacity building of neonatal staff to deal with very sick newborns, including premature and very low birth weight newborn in WB focusing on neonatal life support and Bringing medical mission of neonatologist consultant and specialized neonatal nurse consultant to provide on-job training for doctors and nurses working in the 8 neonatal units at Gaza hospital including medical missions from WB to Gaza. 2.2) Procurement of essential life -saving medications for NICUs in Gaza and WB in addition to special medications and baby formula for neonates and children with metabolic disorders as per needs assessment.2.3) establishing baby friendly rooms in Nus, maternity hospitals and PHCs in Westbank and Gaza based on needs assessment through preparing and equipping private rooms/corners for mothers with careful attention to the environmental requirements of breastfeeding including a comfortable seat, breast pumping facilities, hand hygiene facilities and refrigerator for breast milk storage and ventilation. 3)improve quality of child health services at primary and secondary levels of care through 3.1) Capacity building of heath care providers in PHCs and hospitals on the updated IMCH clinical guidelines.3.2) Upgrading the child registry based on the guidelines. This intervention will include data extraction, improving reporting mechanisms, and development of periodic fact sheets and dashboards. This will facilitate producing policy briefs that can be used to support evidence-based decision making and linking the child registry with other relevant departments in the PHCs including the MCH eRegistry, laboratory, the pharmacy, the radiology department, and others. The project will target 9 PHC level 3 and 4 clinics who were included in the PHC quality improvement approach implemented by WHO.3.3) Conducting SBCC campaigns for caregivers in light of the newly developed child health guidelines to improve child health from age 0 to 5 years (1000 days) the activity will entail developing and implementing SBCC plan targeting caregiver to raise awareness on common child health illnesses and referral pathways.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

WHO is committed to ensuring a people-centered humanitarian response that is accountable to the affected population. For this project, the affected people will participate throughout the project, in the following ways: • A final list of procurement for supplies and equipment will be drafted and discussed at the joint WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA and MoH committees as well as the ENAP committee and endorsed by the MoH to ensure that there is no duplication and procurement responds to the most acute needs in MoH facilities • Where possible, WHO will continue to conduct regular visits/meetings to collect feedback, including using the complaint book, on the quality and availability of services from patients and health staff. WHO will consider the feedback and identify/implement remedy solutions collectively to improve the performance in consultation with affected people and counterparts. This includes any SEA complaints. WHO will continue to advocate through the Health cluster for strengthening the feedback mechanism for the entire response. * For advocacy, feedback will be used to strengthen processes and activities, while complaints will be fully investigated, with the involvement of impartial bodies where required. All complaints related to serious ethical concerns will be raised through WHO’s ethics mechanisms.For all the training activities, feedback will be used to adapt the training according to the needs of the direct beneficiaries, making sure that health personnel will be receiving training tailored to their needs. The EENC annual implementation assessment and report will include interviews and ensures patients' feedback will be submitted to policymakers for decisions and modification of policies and interventions. SBCC plans will be built based on the results of the quality improvement assessment conducted that entails interviews with care givers.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The centrality of protection is at the heart of WHO emergency response as mandated by the IASC. WHO works to ensure the prevention of harm to all persons it engages with through activities, including through upholding due processes of consent, the protection of confidentiality and the proper collection, communication, storage, dissemination and disposal of information on affected persons. WHO will also ensure that all interventions are context-appropriate and culturally sensitive. Affected people will be consulted on this where and when appropriate. The prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse has also been integrated across the WHO-supported projects through mandatory training of all staff on PSEA and seeking feedback from the affected people, which will be acted upon. WHO’s response team ensures that PSEA is integrated into the response from an early stage, to build the capacity of national PSEA focal points, and to ensure continuity of activities on the ground despite access constraints. Based on lessons learned from the independent report of the abuse perpetrated in the DRC, WHO oPt will organize a training session for staff, and has rolled out the inter-agency Code of Conduct in alignment with the national, and sub- national PSEA network and encouraged all staff and personnel to sign. WHO will participate in the GBV sub-cluster as well as the national and sub-national PSEA networks through a designated focal point. Currently, plans are under way to roll out more trainings, work on prevention, and to strengthen inter-agency feedback and reporting mechanisms, as well as case management and response services through linkages with GBV actors.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project activities will clearly focus on the provision of life-saving health interventions to the most vulnerable, including women, the elderly and people with disabilities (PWD) based on the latest available impartial assessments. PWD will be considered as one of the main targeted vulnerable groups in the proposed project. All indicators will be gender-age disaggregated where possible. WHO will continue to work to strengthen the empowerment of affected persons, including through the provision of platforms to promote patient voices as well as capacity building with health service providers to promote participatory mechanisms within health services design and delivery. WHO also works with the Ministry of Health and partners to strengthen monitoring and analysis of equity and barriers to health access, focused on vulnerable groups and protected characteristics
