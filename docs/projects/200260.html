Project Summary

The proposed intervention sets to alleviate the vulnerable status of migrants in Lebanon in response to the multi-faceted crisis in the country. Through this project, Caritas Lebanon (CL) is set to provide access and support the most vulnerable migrant workers and eliminate all protection risks, in order to decrease negative coping mechanisms. Particular focus will be provided to Persons with Special Needs (PWSN) from the migrants communities and migrants at the retention center (male and female).
Migrant domestic workers are not protected by the country's labor law, but instead work under the sponsorship system (Kafala). Many migrants, mostly women, report exploitative working conditions including long working hours, deprivation of rest days, denial of pay or imposed deductions, deprivation of food & proper accommodation, verbal & physical abuse, & denial of access to health care. Migrants are also under risk of trafficking, assault and deportation should they leave their employers’ home. Many migrants find themselves unable to cover their most basic needs in the absence of an effective social safety net in Lebanon.
Under the ERP 2023 Migrant chapter, and in response to the situation outlined above, CL aims to support a total of 550 unique beneficiaries, as per the below outcomes and activities:
Under Outcome 1, CL will provide 80 vulnerable migrants with awareness sessions on legal topics, community-based protection, access to services and/or MHPSS to migrant community groups (Activity 1.1). 150 right-holders will also be provided legal counseling, legal assistance and legal representation, 50 of which will be detained at the General Security Detention center (Activity 1.2). Moreover, 500 right-holders will be provided with protection case management and individual counselling to vulnerable migrants (including MHPSS) (Activity 1.3).
Under Outcome 2, “CL run two safe shelters providing shelter for vulnerable migrants in need”. CL will ensure that vulnerable migrants have improved access to multi-sectoral, dignified and lifesaving assistance through the provision of 500 one-time Emergency Cash Assistance to identified right-holders to help them meet some of their basic needs and eliminate risks of homelessness (Activity 2.3). Survivors of serious abuse will be also referred to CL safe shelters for a temporary stay. Residents in the shelter typically remain between three to twelve months before moving on, depending on the complexity of each case and the level of assistance they receive from their embassies or consulates.
Under Outcome 3, CL will collaborate with the International Organization for Migration (IOM) and other partners to support the voluntary humanitarian return of migrants, including PWSN migrants and survivors, to their countries of origin upon safe shelter departure and from the retention center. As a result, 20 migrants who want to return home will be given financial support to pay any fees they may incur while going, such as plane tickets, luggage, and COVID-19 testing (if necessary), among other things. For this intervention, priority will be provided to migrant women as most of the PWSN are women and are highly vulnerable to abuse, such as Gender-Based Violence, and exploitation, including human trafficking and forced labour. In addition to Male PWSN migrants who are victims of exploitation and with resources.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Caritas Lebanon prioritizes preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), promoting protection, gender, and social inclusion in all its projects. A comprehensive PSEA policy outlines its zero-tolerance approach, and staff and volunteers undergo background checks and regular PSEA training. Reporting mechanisms are safe, confidential, and accessible to all staff and beneficiaries, with investigations conducted promptly and impartially. Moreover, CL strictly follows the Do-No-Harm methodology and acknowledges that project beneficiaries may be among the most vulnerable groups, requiring special consideration in project design and monitoring, and therefore designs projects in a way that prioritizes their needs. Additionally, CL has a set of policies and procedures to mitigate operational risks and maintain ethical behaviour, including a Code of Conduct, Child Protection Policy, Safeguarding Statement of Commitment, Anti-Harassment Policy, and Anti-fraud and Corruption Policy.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Accountability of affected populations is ensured throughout all phases of project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The project design is based on continuous assessments at field level that analyze the changing needs of affected populations and the relevance of ongoing and potential activities. During implementation, CL team ensures that beneficiaries have the tools to be able to express their concerns and suggestions for improvement. CL has established comment boxes in its Centres for beneficiaries to provide feedback - positive, negative, and suggestive - on activities and services provided. Additionally, the Centre Manager is responsible for developing and encouraging a culture of accountability and feedback in the shelters through participatory approaches.
