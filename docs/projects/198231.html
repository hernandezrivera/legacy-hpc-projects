Project Summary

Children and young people with disabilities have been among the most excluded from all levels of education – a situation that is compounded during emergencies and protracted crises. In the conflict affected south west region of Cameroon, 78% of physically challenged and internally displaced youths have drop out of schools (PAID-WA 2020). Multiple complex barriers, including segregation, discrimination, financial barriers, stigma, and lack of trained teachers, inadequate learning materials, and inaccessible facilities prevent them from fully participating in education (PAID-WA 2021). Persons with disabilities in this conflict affected region face serious obstacles to meeting basic needs such as food, sanitation, and health care due to their impoverished state as a result of lack of employability skills to enable them generate income to be self-reliant (PAID-WA 2021). In the Rural areas of Fako Division, internally displaced and particularly physically challenged school dropout youths, aged 12 to 17, are 1.8 times more likely to be exploited and violated due to the fact that they are out of school and with no skills or low skills as a result of the crisis. (PAID-WA 2019) Starting and expanding a small business remains a serious challenge, especially for these disadvantaged youth. , Obstacles include the lack of access to information, skills, finance, land and property rights, pre-/post-start up orientation and business development support as well as administrative hurdles.
The Pan African Institute for Development seek to improve the welfare of physically challenged and internally displaced school dropout youth through:
1) The establishment of 30 community support safe spaces for Basic skills development of 300 youth (150 physically challenged and 150 internally displaced youths age 12-17) in 30 selected communities in the conflict affected Fako Division in the South West Region of Cameroon.
2) Capacity building of 300 school dropout youths (150 physically challenged and 150 internally displaced youths age 12-17) in income generating vocational skills in the conflict affected Fako Division.
3) Mentoring and incubating 300 school dropout youths (150 physically challenged and 150 internally displaced youths age 12-17) to develop business plan by experts in the conflict affected Fako Division
4) Provision of Startup capital to 300 school dropout youths (150 physically challenged and 150 internally displaced youths age 12-17) through a revolving startup scheme in the conflict affected Fako Division.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The conflict in the North West and South West Region of Cameroon that started in 2016 continues to demand humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations. The humanitarian situation in these regions is worsening particularly for drop out youths ( 12 to 17 years), especially young girls and boys that are physically challenged, and also face serious violation including family separation and gender based violence (GBV) due to fact that they are dependent (PAID-WA 2020). Children and young people with disabilities have been among the most excluded from all levels of education – a situation that is compounded during emergencies and protracted crises. In the conflict affected south west region of Cameroon, 78% of physically challenged and internally displaced youths have drop out of schools (PAID-WA 2020). Multiple complex barriers, including segregation, discrimination, financial barriers, stigma, and lack of trained teachers, inadequate learning materials, and inaccessible facilities prevent them from fully participating in education (PAID-WA 2021). Persons with disabilities in this conflict affected region face serious obstacles to meeting basic needs such as food, sanitation, and health care due to their impoverished state as a result of lack of employability skills to enable them generate income to be self-reliant (PAID-WA 2021)Internally Displaced drop out Youth, particularly, those that are physically challenged, aged 12 to 17 years in the rural areas of Fako division are 1.8 times more likely to be exploited and violated due to the fact that they are out of school and with no skills or low skills as a result of the crisis. (PAID-WA 2019) Starting and expanding a small business remains a serious challenge, especially for these disadvantaged youth. According to a need assessment conducted by PAID-WA in 2019, Obstacles include the lack of access to information, skills, finance, land and property rights, pre-/post-start up orientation and business development support as well as administrative hurdles. Even where public and NGO support services for self-employment exist, they are usually targeted to youth with a minimum of completed middle-school (e.g. Advance level and Diploma holders) or even university level education (e.g. PAID-WA 2018). School drop-outs and low skilled youth working the informal sector, on the other hand, currently have almost no opportunities to increase their productivity. The real solution on improving the welfare of these vulnerable group of persons , based on a survey conducted by PAID-WA in 2020, therefore lies with how well physically challenged and internally displaced School dropout youth in Fako Division of Buea Cameroon can be equipped with appropriate skills and competence to make them self-reliant. Therefore it is necessary to improve on the welfare of physically challenged and Internally displaced school dropout youths through Basic skills and Income Generating Vocational skills in 30 selected communities in the conflict effected Fako Division of of Cameroon
