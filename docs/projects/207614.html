Project Summary

This proposed project is designed to address the shelter and NFI vulnerability in the Rohingya makeshifts and the host community Area. IRB will assist the Rohingya community as well as the host community by providing durable and sustainable shelter materials and will build some shelters for EVI households. Optimum shelter locations in the camps will be selected in coordination with the CIC and shelter sector. Based on the gap analysis and subsequent discussions with the sector and other actors, IRB is planning to implement shelter and NFI support activities in camps 2E (Block-D), 2W (Block-A), Camp 3 (Block A & C), and 4 (Block B &D). Host community activities will be implemented in Ukhiya (All the 05 unions under Ukhiya Upazila) and the Whykong union under Teknaf Upazilla. To address the needs of vulnerable populations, IRB will continue providing shelter and NFI materials to refugee/ Rohingya households in Cox’s Bazar. Shelter support will include improved shelter designs with a focus on Disaster Risk and options for durable materials. IRB will support Transitional Shelter Assistance (TSA) to increase the shelter's durability and minimize the risk of shelter damage from extreme weather. IRB will organize training /orientation for the beneficiaries before distributing the TSA materials. Training will focus on elevating treated bamboo columns off the ground and proper metal footing installation. Training will emphasize DRR measures such as building household-level drainage, bracing corners, installing tie-down kits, and fire protection in cooking areas. As with shelter Care and Maintenance and distributions, IRB will provide Porter support and construction labor for verified HHs. In addition, skilled volunteers trained in TSA 1 will monitor households daily and provide technical advice to encourage beneficiaries to use the materials as intended. 2000 Transitional Shelter (TSA) will be built for the most vulnerable families of Rohingya camps. Among them, 667 TSA will be built in Camp 3, 667 TSA will be built in Camp 4, 333 TSA will be built in Camp 2E, and the rest of the 333 TSA will be built in Camp 2W.
IRB will provide Care and Maintenance materials to 2100 households in four camps (700 HHs in Camp 3, 700 HHs in Camp 4, 3500 HHs in Camp 2E, and 350 HHs in Camp 2W). Shelters not targeted for TSA 1 distributions will receive Care and Maintenance materials and will be jointly assessed by Shelter/NFI Sector, UNHCR, IRB staff, SMSD, and volunteers. Besides this, IRB will provide 50 Mid-term shelter assistants to the most vulnerable families of the respective four Camps. Households will be identified and verified by SMS and then technically assessed by IRB.
The project will build 50 permanent shelters for the most vulnerable families in the host community areas in Ukhia and Teknaf Upazila. Among the 50 HC shelters, 43 shelters will be constructed in Ukhia Upazila (Haldia Palong union-9, Ratna Palong union-9, Palong Khali-7, Raja Palong Union-9, Jaliapalong union-9) and 7 shelters will be constructed in Whykong Union under Teknaf Upazila. In coordination with UNHCR, NFI distribution will be completed in UNHCR camps according to the sector’s guidance, recommendations, and needs. IRB has a plan for NFI distribution among 3000 (1000 in camp-3, 1000 in camp-4, 500 in camp 2W, and 500 in camp2W) extremely vulnerable Rohingya families living in respective camps and another 1000 (500 HHs Ratna Palong Union and 500 HHs in Haldia Palong Union) NFI packages for the vulnerable host households in the host communities in Cox’s Bazar district. Among 4000 NFI packages, 1000 household solar light kits and 3000 household NFI packages (sector recommended items). NFI distribution will be coordinated with UNHCR. This proposed project is initially designed for JRP -2024, and continuity will depend on donor funds availability for 2024-2025.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The total number of Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar is around 931,574 individuals. A high majority of the Rohingya refugee population is concentrated in 33 extremely congested camps within Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’s Bazar district, Bangladesh. The FDMNs are fully dependent on the assistance provided by the humanitarian community and the government of Bangladesh. In the camps, shelters are exposed to cyclic household levels risk of floods, landslides, fire, and cyclones. Temporary materials such as bamboo and tarpaulin have a limited capacity to resist weather impacts and thus require regular repairs and replacement. The use of adequate material (treated bamboo, good quality tarpaulin) along with the design, site plan, and proper technical details for the materials connections, can improve the lifespan of materials if properly followed. Training for the beneficiaries on how to repair and maintain their shelters is also one of the essential elements of shelter assistance to ensure less dependency on humanitarian support. Following the gap analysis, Islamic Relief Bangladesh will assess the needs in the respective camps and deliver assistance in collaboration with the SCCCM sector and camp management. IRB will continue to work towards temporary shelter solutions to ensure protection and reduce fire risks and environmental impacts. IRB will implement three types of activities 1. New shelter construction, 2. Regular repair and maintenance support, and 3. Household-level site development in Camp 2E (Block-D), 2W (Block-A), Camp 3 (Block A & C), and 4 (Block B &D). Improved shelter activities will include transitional shelter, Mid-Term Shelter, Steel frame Shelter, and Shelter with TSA support. As different materials are being used for the different types of improved shelter, the quality also differs, and most of them still require regular repair and maintenance. After getting a gap analysis from the SCCCM sector, current gaps are found in the four camps (Camp 3, camp 4, Camp 2E, and Camp 2W). According to the S-CCCM sector report, 65% of the refugee households informed that they received shelter assistance last year, while 37% did not. Out of 33 camps, in 22 camps, the percentage of receiving shelter assistance is more than 50%. About 63% of shelters that were assessed had shelters standing on a plinth. 65% of shelters did not meet this standard for treated bamboo and visible signs of infestation, 20% met it partially, and only 15% of shelters were constructed with treated bamboo. In contrast, IRB has a provision to support with 100% treated Borak Bamboo. 40% of shelters met the minimum standard with all metal footings being concrete or metal. 49% of shelters did not meet the standard by having less than four footings in concrete or metal, and 11% of shelters met the standard partially with only four corner columns having concrete or metal footings. Considering the sustainability & durability of MTS and TSA shelters, IRB will provide metal footings for all MTS and TSA support in the respective 06 blocks under the proposed four camps. Household level Site Development work is also needed for Camp 03, camp 04, Camp 2E, and Camp 2W. While providing the Improved Shelter Materials, there is a need for Site Development in every household. “Shelters need to be improved” is considered as the GAP in the camp for household-level Site Development activities. These activities include slope protection, footpaths, and drains, among others.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Following the JRP and Sectoral Need Assessment, Islamic Relief Bangladesh will assess the needs of the target population for JRP 2024 and deliver assistance in collaboration with the Shelter/NFI sector, site management, and government. Islamic Relief Bangladesh (IRB) will continue to work toward temporary shelter solutions to ensure protection and reduce fire risks as well as impacts on the environment. To address the needs of vulnerable populations, IRB will continue providing shelter and NFI materials to refugee/ Rohingya households in Cox’s Bazar. Shelter support will include improved shelter designs with a focus on Disaster Risk and options for durable materials. The modalities chosen in this proposed project are developed and approved at the Shelter/NFI Sector level and ISCG. The geographical location of the target area has been selected in consulting with AoR lead agency UNHCR. The camps (Camp 2E, Camp 2W, Camp 3, and Camp 4) have been selected in consultation with the sector, UNHCR, and camp-level actors. An integrated approach will be followed to ensure inter-sectoral camp-based coordination and delivery services to FDMNs and the host community under Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila. This project is designed for 12 months, but we have funding commitments from Islamic Relief Worldwide up to December 2024. IRB has secured funding up to 2025 for the continuation of the activities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IRB’s shelter and NFI support project will ensure an impartial selection process to avoid involvement of other entities that may be susceptible to bias can be in turn cause community tensions, unequal distribution of resources, and aid diversion. This Proposed project will ensure complaint and feedback mechanism for right holders. The project will establish referral pathway to refer GBV, child protection concerns and cases of sexual exploitation and abuse. This project will ensure information about PSEA, including rules and code of conduct for humanitarian workers has to exhibit clearly in a variety of formats in distribution points. Distribution point staff and volunteers will be trained on PSEA referrals, the agency’s code of conduct. IRB shelter project will consider extremely vulnerable right holders (18-59 years old), consult HHs on specific changes required in their shelter to ensure accessibility. Include porter and construction labor support to extremely vulnerable FDMN HHs. IRB will arrange training on gender, GBV, women’s/human rights, and social exclusion, code of conduct and PSEA for all staff and volunteers of the shelter/NFI support project. IRB Shelter project will emphasize the importance of privacy and dignity during shelter construction, constructing new shelters or reinforcing existing shelters, consult individual households for a ventilation design that meets privacy needs, and include women and adolescents in the consultation.

3.3 - Age & Disability

This shelter and NFI support project will emphasize the needs of extremely vulnerable individuals of different ages such as adolescent girls, elderly people, and persons with disabilities during the implementation of SNFI activities including emergency and non-emergency shelters, and NFI distributions. As part of shelter and site planning, it is considered the specific risks faced by women, girls, and persons with disabilities. For example, during shelter kit distributions, the locations of distributions will be taken into consideration for the different access needs of the rights-holders. This proposed project will give attention to ensuring safe access paths for women, girls, and persons with disabilities. Inclusive shelter and settlement programming will be ensured for persons with disabilities, considering their community contributions and participation, as well as decision-making. In addition, women and girls accessibility to distribution points, as well as their mobility and access to other essential services will be ensured. Total 5% population of person with disabilities from different age group will be covered through this IRB shelter project. The camp area becomes dark after sunset due to the lack of lighting throughout the camps and women and girls may face GVB cases. Therefore, solar lamps are included considering their need for safely conduct activities at night.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The shelter project will prioritize and provide the Shelter/NFI assistance considering the right holder's shelter needs. Ensure accountable practice shares communications about distribution dates, locations, modality, selection criteria, and package materials with SMS and directly with the community through SNFI volunteers, tokens will be distributed directly by SNFI actors to ensure transparency and accountability to the affected population. Ensure missing or incomplete identification documents will not exclude individuals from accessing services. Consult the camp protection focal point for referral based on the existing referral pathway. Inform beneficiaries about the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM), how to use it, and where it is located in the camp. Shelter project staff will inform beneficiaries to register their complaints and feedback at the CFM box to streamline referrals, avoid duplication, and minimize delays in the distribution queue. This CFM will create opportunities for the beneficiaries/ stakeholders to come up with their concerns/complaints which will be considered by the project during/ before finalizing the selection of appropriate beneficiaries by maintaining an organizational complaint-resolving process. For effective CFM, a CFM box will be established at the Union Parishad complex, CIC office, Distribution point, camp-based market/main road for the refugee camp areas, and other important places for the host community. Besides, at the project office, it will use the local CFM register book. One cell number will also be given to each beneficiary and stakeholder and it will also be hung in different public places. Moreover, an orientation session will be provided to the selected beneficiaries on effective CFM.
