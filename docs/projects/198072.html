Project Summary

The overall objective of this project on Strengthening Community-based Protection monitoring in conflicts and Emergencies (SCOPE Project) under the framework of the UNHCR Project21 is intended to mitigate the impact of armed conflict in Cameroon by improving the protection environment for IDPs, refugees, returnees’ populations, and host communities through evidence-based prevention of and response to protection risks using relevant protection data collection and analysis tools to develop and deliver on improved protection outcomes.
Specifically, through this protection monitoring intervention, the implementing consortium partners and UNHCR intends to achieve the following protection outcomes:
• Strengthened protection by presence to mitigate threats and risks of protection/violations;
• Improved protection environment and reduction in related protection risks based on evidence generated through protection data collection and analysis to inform advocacy, resource mobilization and communication with all relevant stakeholders on the protection situation of persons of concern in Cameroon;
• Strengthened gender-based violence information management systems (GBVIMS) where operational and enhanced UNHCR database using various GBV data collection and analysis as well as other protection case monitoring tools;
• Improved protection mainstreaming by conducting ad hoc do-no-harm analyses prior to specific humanitarian interventions;
• Reinforced community-based protection systems including verifying the existence and efficiency of protection referral pathways and community-based complaints mechanisms (CBCM);
• Strengthened community protection framework including the development of community-based protection plans that facilitate the identification and the response to persons with special needs including separated and unaccompanied children, people with disabilities, elderly, GBV survivors, single women on the move and female/child heads of household, pregnant and lactating mothers, persons living with HIV and other chronic diseases, etc.;
• Enhanced capacity and increased awareness of relevant stakeholders on human rights and relevant international protection frameworks; etc.; and
• Improved protection monitoring, data collection and analysis on protection risk factors such as loss of civil documentation, incidents on housing, land and property (HLP), freedom of movement and risk of statelessness; etc.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon faces multifaceted humanitarian crises in the Lake Chad Basin driven by incessant attacks and forced displacement of communities by Boko Haram insurgents including refugees from Nigeria and IDPs in Cameroon. The affected population in the Far North due to this crisis is estimated at 642,706 individuals, including 377,877 internally displaced persons and 130,690 returnees . In addition there are estimated 90,000 flood- affected IDPs in the Far North and about 130,000 refugees from Nigeria who live in the North and Far North Regions, in both camp and out of camp locations.In the East Region, the instability in Central African Republic has also forcefully displaced thousands of men, women and children into the neighbouring East Region of Cameroon, most of who are need humanitarian protection and assistance.In the North West and South West Regions where a socio-political crises had degenerated into an armed conflict running into its sixth year with over a million people affected including 532,290 IDPs (August 2022 statistics) as well as 417,516 returnees, the protection environment has been marked by gross violations of human rights, sexual and gender-based violence and reported loss of civil documents, housing, land and property incidents. This humanitarian crises in the North West and South West Regions has resulted in an estimated 166,225 persons including 46,702 boys and 56,024 girls below 18 (making up 62% of the total IDP population) to be displaced into the neighbouring West and Littoral Regions .While Cameroon is signatory to several international and regional instruments including the 1951 Geneva Convention, the 1967 Protocol, the 1969 Organization of African Unity (OAU) Refugee Convention and the OAU Kampala Convention on the protection and assistance of internally displaced persons, most local authorities (duty bearers) are unaware of their responsibilities towards protecting and fulfilling the rights of the rights holders, in this case, refugees and internally displaced populations. At the community level, the protection environment is weak and social cohesion is threatened by tensions over diminishing local resources in host communities and increasing incidents of inter-communal crises.As a consortium, Rhema Care and Street Child Cameroon are bringing to the table unique synergy through value-added competencies and strengths including their community-rootedness and long-standing involvement in protection responses amongst the refugees, IDPs, returnees and host communities affected by the conflicts in the NWSW Regions and the Boko Haram crises in the Lake Chad Basin that have collectively left over 2,023,233 persons of concern in need including 490,322 refugees, 8,865 asylum seekers, 975,786 internally displaced persons (IDPs) and 548,206 returnees.Rhema Care has presence in five (5) of the six (6) Regions targeted by this proposed intervention, and already has cross-border experiences working with Cameroonian Refugees in Cross River, Benue, Taraba, Akwa Ibom states of Nigeria in partnership with UNHCR as well as Nigerian Refugees in Akwaya Subdivision of Manyu Division, South West Region. Rhema Care with support from UNICEF has executed social protection interventions in the Far North Regions including Logone & Chari, Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga divisions as well as the Moungo and Bamboutos divisions of the Littoral and West Regions respectively targeting IDPs, returnees and host communities.
