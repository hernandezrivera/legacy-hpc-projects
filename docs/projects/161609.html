Project Summary

This project is aimed at strengthening integrated primary health care services delivery in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. These states border Niger, Cameroon and Chad are expected to continue to receive increased numbers of returnees from hard to reach locations within Borno, Chad and Cameroun in addition to the over 1.8 million IDPs these states currently host as a result of on-going 10-year conflict. In Borno state, increased clashes over the past months between armed actors have essentially cut off once accessible locations and populations to humanitarian aid while significantly overburdening safer zones with increased congestion within the available IDP camps.
UNICEF, in partnership with the primary health care agencies, will support vulnerable groups through the provision of an integrated package of primary health care services to children under five years old, women and their families through IDP camp clinics and health facilities in host communities.
UNICEF will work with other NGO partners to support the Government to provide rapid response activities 19 LGAs with a protracted crisis context with high humanitarian needs while supporting Health System Strengthening (HSS) work in the remaining 18 stable LGAs.
UNICEF will focus on reaching people in need, including returnees, as well as supporting the three states (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) to implement interventions that will enable the transition from emergency phase to development in stable LGAs. In addition, UNICEF will partner with INGOs that are present on the ground and provide strategic technical assistance in building capacity for resilience at the community level. UNICEF will review existing supplies architecture and focus on the procurement and supply of essential medicines tailoring to the needs on the ground. UNICEF will leverage its comparative strengths to ensure the dovetailing of resources through convergence with other sections within UNICEF and other sectors at the state, LGAs and community levels for the attainment of sustainable and synergistic re-enforcing results.
The following objectives will be met through the project:
• Meet the immediate health needs of conflict-affected populations;
• Restore essential health services;
• Rehabilitate the health system; and
• Strengthen government led/operated health systems strengthening program

1 - Needs

In its tenth year, the armed conflict in Nigeria’s Northeast has significantly disrupted the health services delivery system in the three states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe and as a result, existing health facilities are overwhelmed. Specifically, the health care delivery system in Borno previously relied on a fragile network of LGA level health facilities comprising of General hospitals, PHCs, health posts and dispensaries; but because of the conflict approximately 497 of the 755 state health facilities (66 per cent) were destroyed, looted or damaged (Health Resource Availability Mapping System (HeRAMS), February 2018. Findings from IOM DTM data Round 28 of August 2019 and routine data collected by UNICEF show that health facilities are still constrained by inadequate staff and this is further compounded by maldistribution of key technical staff resulting in an inappropriate mix of skill as well as insuﬃcient supplies and equipment to meet the health needs of the population. In addition, the overcrowded living conditions in some IDP camps where there is limited access to safe drinking water and sanitation significantly increases the risks of preventable disease outbreaks. There is an active outbreak of Cholera affecting many communities in Adamawa state which is stretching available resources even further. Before the conflict, the northern states, especially Adamawa, Borno and Yobe have been characterized by extremely poor health indicators which were significantly worse than the national-level indicators. While the national maternal mortality ratio (MMR) was 576 per 100,000 live births, in the 3 states MMR was as high as 1,500 – 2,000 per 100,000 live births. Under-five mortality (U5MR) was also above the national average of 126/1,000 live births with over 160/1,000 in the three states (DHS 2013). These trends are reversing as a results of an improved high impact health interventions in the Northeastern states especially in Borno. U5MR in Borno stands at 86 per 1000 live births compared to a national of 132 deaths per 1000 live births (NDHS 2018). Sustaining this achievement require strengthening and scaling up on-going interventions especially maternal, newborn and child health services at PHC level and to also ensure an even and results-based distribution of trained health personnel. The results of the ongoing conflict have led to the significant displacement of populations and many trained health personnel have fled the area. This has left returnees, IDPs and refugees without quality health services, with those services provided being rendered by unqualified health personnel. Of the total number of health personnel available in Borno, only three per cent are doctors, eleven per cent nurses and four per cent midwives, with the majority of these concentrated in Maiduguri, the State capital. (WHO HeRAMS August 2018). Thus far, there remain the following emerging challenges and critical needs: 1. Inadequate number and distribution of health care workers. 2. lack of updated technical capacity to render quality high impact services by health personnel. 3. Absence of adequate accommodation for health workers. 4. Poor storage capacity and dilapidated warehouses, and weak logistics management system5. Insuﬃcient stocks of emergency drugs and supplies and 6. Absence of effective referral systems due to uncoordinated referral system, lack of ambulances and insecurity issues.

2 - Activities

To adequately address the gaps mentioned above in locations with high humanitarian needs, UNICEF, through our implementing partners will continue to ensure: 1. Procurement of essential drugs and supplies, 2. Improve on the existing logistics capacity to deliver essential commodities to end users at the last mile. 3. Improve the technical capacity of skilled health workers and support their deployment to locations where they are most needed.4. Focus its support to providing training and provision of technical assistance in supported health facilities and IDP camps, 5. Undertake limited rehabilitation of damaged health facilities and construction of semi-permanent health facilities in worst affected LGAs according to the Government priority of one PHC per ward strategy. 6. Partner with other health sector actors and participate in ensuring that a robust referral system is supported by improving pre-referral treatment in PHCs and the revitalization of ambulances to meet the demands of vulnerable populations. UNICEF will also work closely with the health sector to ensure adequate disease outbreak response activities are well coordinated. These activities shall be guided by SPHERE standards and the policies as directed by the states. They shall also be tailored to satisfy cultural sensitivities while preserving the tenets of human rights and fostering gender equality.
