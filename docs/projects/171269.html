Project Summary

The project aims at improving access of diversified food to vulnerable and food insecure households (HHs) and protection vulnerable households from further deterioration as a result of the continuous socio-economic conditions and the impact of the COVID19 crisis. The project aims to contribute to HRP’s Country Strategic Objective 3: “The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. The specific objectives of this project are to: (SO1) Improve food security and nutrition of vulnerable households (SO2) To protect livelihoods from further deterioration and depletion. The project aims to reduce the immediate food gap of 500 vulnerable households (HHs) (2750 individuals) by providing a bundle of food items through an e-voucher system for a period of 6 months to those HHs with no/or very limited access to food and raise awareness on nutrition and health in order to improve eating habits and consumption, particularly for women, children and children with specific disabilities/ nutrition needs. The project also aims at providing emergency protection interventions for 100 vulnerable livelihoods 100 HHs (550 individuals) (with a focus on GBV survivors and female headed households) who have an established livelihood activity and have been challenged by the increased humanitarian crisis and the consequences of the COVID19. This will be achieved through three activities; first by providing short term food vouchers for three months; secondly providing cash assistance to replenish livelihood assets and support their livelihood activities; and finally, by providing awareness sessions on how to protect and sustain their livelihood activities. The project is aligned with the Food Security Sector (FSS) specific objective 2 “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs”. The project is already adapted to COVID19 pandemic setting taking into account associated risks that will affect beneficiaries and staff in addition to all necessary precautionary measures.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Gaza continues to face multi-layered stressors and prolonged restriction policies imposed by Israel on the highly vulnerable and fragile enclave. The 13 years of blockade and the intermittent military escalations and restrictions imposed on farmers and fisherfolks in terms of movement, materials entry and access, in addition to the suspension of the PA’s coordination with Israeli authorities resulted in more than 3000 workers who used to work in Israel became unemployed solely due to this suspension. Unemployment has already reached 49% of the population in the second quarter of 2020 according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021. Moreover, the decrease in the labour force participation declined from 40% to 35% in April-June 2020, and for women participation in particular it stands at 15%. The already identified challenges in the social safety net in regards to accommodating and adapting to the increase in the need of support through the PNCT programme has resulted in having thousands of families on waiting lists or as potentially poor and deeply poor. According to the FSS HNO, the total people in poverty and under the sever need category are 1,076,279 people which is the majority of the Gaza population. As rates of food insecurity reported in 2018 were at least 68%, the impact of the current COVID-19 crisis will likely unfold similar or potentially worse risk to the already vulnerable situation. This also links with the overall need for humanitarian assistance for the Gaza Strip to all the basic humanitarian services is 1.57 million individuals as per the HNO 2021.In September 2020, Oxfam and MA’AN have carried out a rapid multi-sectoral assessment of the impact of COVID-19 on its current beneficiaries from different projects under WASH, Food Security and Livelihoods and Gender and Protection through a telephone survey. A total number of 102 HHs were interviewed included beneficiaries that have previously benefited from cash for livelihoods and cash protection, food voucher, wash repairs, livelihood in-kind support activities. Part of these 102 HHs are on Oxfam and MAAN’s waiting list to receive assistance under an ongoing HF project. The key findings were as follows: • 75% of HHs who had previously received food vouchers for two months through a 2020 HF ongoing project, and 80% of those who did not receive any assistance and were waiting to be assisted through the HF project with food vouchers reported that food was the most impacted need as a result of Covid19• 90% of HHs supported previously by Livelihood/IGAs but were not receiving any food assistance confirmed that food was their most impacted sector. • 72% of HHs supported by livelihood/IGAs support through previous projects reported that one of their preferred modalities for assistance was provision of food, 82% reported the need for unrestricted cash, and 90% reported the need for cash for livelihood inputs. Noting that they had the choice to select more than one modality in the survey. Amongst the 90% who selected cash for livelihood inputs as a preferred modality, 75% of them reported the need of food provision to enable them to protect their livelihood and not rely on selling livelihood assets or consuming their livelihood inputs in covering their basic needs.

2 - Activities

The project activities will be implemented jointly by Oxfam and MA’AN teams as per the following: Output 1: 500 Poor and food insecure households have access to diversified food items Activity 1.1 Beneficiaries selection: Coordination, nomination of potential beneficiaries and cross-checkingOxfam will contact the MoSD for nominating HHs with high food insecurity and poverty according to the Proxy means test formula (PMTF) this will include those on the waiting list and possibly those already receiving cash through the Palestinian National Cash Transfer Programme (PNCTP) but have food gaps. This will depend on the gaps identified by the MoSD at the time of the intervention. MA’AN will carry out a verification and vulnerability assessment survey via telephone given the activation of COVID19 SoPs to minimize contact with HHs. Based on the survey, scoring will be done for all nominated HHs and priority will be given for female headed HHs, and protection concerns reported within certain HHs. List of HHs will be cross-checked with FSS and FSS partners (such as IRPAL, CRS, Mercy Corps, and others working in parallel to this intervention) to avoid any duplication of the assistance. Activity 1.2: Contracting shops: Oxfam has already existing MoUs with 12 shops in the target areas. Selection will be reviewed based on localities of the selected beneficiaries. Activity 1.3: Selected households will receive food vouchers for a period of six months. A monthly top up will be provided each month that includes food items from three different shops (grocery, fruits, vegetables, and chicken). Output 2: 100 (HHs) Livelihoods (Income Generating Activities) are protected and recovered (benefiting 550 individuals)Activity 2.1: Oxfam and MA’AN will carry out an in-depth needs' assessment on a pool of 120 HHs who have previously benefited from IGA support projects with essentially one or more of the following criteria: vulnerable HHs under the MoSD’s PNCTP list, child protection families who were included in the case management system of MA’AN and Tamer Institute family centers, and GBV survivors who were supported through Women Health Center in the North of Gaza in 2019. Activity 2.2: Oxfam and MA’AN will finalize selection of beneficiaries based on a scoring system that will give priority to female headed HHs, GBV survivors, but will also include give weights in the scoring for household capacities and available resources (human, natural, social, physical, capitals etc.) Activity 2.3 Oxfam and MA’AN will provide three months food voucher for selected HHs as means to help protect and restore their livelihood. Activity 2.4 MA’AN will carry out a livelihood assessment to develop a plan for selected bnfs that will provide the basis for the cash assistance that will be restricted to the use of livelihood inputs and related activities. Activity 2.5 MA’AN will carry out will carry out a series of awareness raising on how to protect their livelihood activities and utilize their available resources in a an effective manner, and how to ensure and improve household harmony through gender awareness training.

3 - Indicators

Oxfam is committed to Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) including gender-sensitive M&E. Oxfam has a Common Approach to Monitoring and Social Accountability (CAMSA) which aligns with international good practices in monitoring, evaluation, and learning, promoting collaborative and effective, results-focused, and accountable MEL practice and Social Accountability. CAMSA is an approach based on minimum requirements for MEL and Social Accountability aligned to Oxfam’s program quality efforts and provides guidance, tools and methodologies including on gender analysis and the assessment of gender outcomes. Within this partnership, Oxfam will take the lead in monitoring and evaluating the project, ensuring that indicators are closely monitored, as well as gathering feedback from beneficiaries throughout the implementation period of the project, allowing Oxfam to amend activities where relevant and to address emerging beneficiaries’ needs. M&E will be conducted and assessed based on a participatory approach and involvement of MA’AN and local communities. Due to the COVID-19 crisis Oxfam’s monitoring activities will be adapted to using safe measures in its interaction with communities. Therefore, the monitoring activities that will be implemented in the project will include operational monitoring of progress in implementation against workplan and in compliance with project standards and procedures, satisfaction of beneficiaries post the distribution, and project impact monitoring that will be developed at the start of the project jointly by Oxfam and MA’AN.Indicators: Output 1: Indicator 1.1: # of beneficiaries receiving food vouchersBaseline: 0 Target 500 HHs * 5.5 = 2750 individualsOutput 2: Indicator 2.1 # of individuals benefiting from income generating activities (IGA)and/or supporting social Businesses enterprises.Baseline: 0 Target: 100 HHs (550 individuals) Output 2: Indicator 2.2 # of people reached by non-technical awareness sessions which includes (employability and entrepreneurship) skills Baseline: 0 Target: 100 individuals

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Oxfam’s Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) is an accountability mechanism that provides wider access for diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback, and minor or major complaints during project implementation. This system uses information communication technology (ICT) to receive feedback either online, through verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels such as helplines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and safeguarding team in-line with Oxfam’s confidentiality policies and reporting, thus will not be recorded by the system. Oxfam and MA’AN are currently adapting the project accountability and monitoring mechanisms to best address the needs of the people served in a changing environment due to the COVID-19 outbreak. Oxfam’s helpline will be primarily used for reporting any incidents of misconduct fraud, exploitation or abuse which will be automatically transferred to the designated teams. Two field supervisors will be handling inquiries and operational complaints from beneficiaries through making their numbers available to all beneficiaries, one in each area (one in Gaza City and another in the North) This mechanism will be available to help resolve issues in the field – such as prices, access to shops, changing contact information, and others issues. Beneficiaries will be provided with information at the beginning of the project on these mechanisms through SMS and through posters placed in the selected shops. the helpline will be the main feedback channel for the IGA activity beneficiaries. Complaint boxes, helpline and field supervisor phone numbers will be accessible for the recipients of the food voucher activity.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Oxfam premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential protection needs which would have put them at risk, had it not been available. Technical assessment will take into consideration the safety, dignity and confidentiality of the served population while also keeping the protection of data and humanitarian actors uncompromised. Project will also be adaptive to the ongoing risks of COVID19 with regards to beneficiaries' access to food, cash, and livelihood support services.Prior to the implementation of the activities, project partner staff will be invited to attend a training session by Oxfam’s Safe programming focal point who will deliver a training on safe programming principles that allows to identify project risks and explore mitigation measures. Also, Gender and Protection Officer will carry out a refresher training for the project team in Oxfam and MA’AN on the overall protection principles and on how to detect GBV, this should equip them with knowledge and tools to conduct risk analysis that identifies risks, risks level, likelihood and mitigation measures that will be taken into consideration in advance. All activities will be designed and implemented in close coordination and in full engagement and consultation with the targeted communities. Examples of how Oxfam will ensure that the “do no harm” approaches mainstreamed in the project are as follows: • COVID19 related SoPs are already prepared by Oxfam and MA’AN teams that highlight different scenarios of access of beneficiaries to the voucher shops, their exposure to potential gatherings, and access to continuously functioning feedback mechanism that is adapted to the specific scenarios. This also applies for accessing the cash that will be disbursed through the banks under COVID19 related precautionary measures. Oxfam and MA'AN have already tested the two related measures and SoPs in previous and currently implemented interventions.• Supporting women accessing food and cash by providing safe and appropriate locations, time and environment within project activities (I.e voucher shops safe access, banks to receive cash transfers). All activities will follow a quick risk assessment to take place that will identify any potential risks for women participating in the activity and identify means to support them within the project or beyond through a referral mechanism. This is particularly critical for addressing livelihood protection and support interventions for GBV survivors and more widely in targeting women as owners of their IGAs. Oxfam has already carried out such assessment in a previous project that resulted in learning and applying corrective measures during the implementation of the project. . With regards to PSEA, Oxfam has a designated PSEA team, including integrity and safeguarding focal points, and confidential councilors who are trained on PSEA and intimidation and bullying where they are responsible for providing capacity building to partners on PSEA and ensuring compliance of all field staff with the Oxfam policy on PSEA. In addition Oxfam PSEA focal point is trained as trainer of trainers by the UN PSEA Network which is expected to have a greater role in further enhancing awareness on the PSEA and on improving current mechanism and systems in place at the partner organizations.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Oxfam and MA’AN have strong previous engagement with communities targeted under this project. Access to project teams is already in place through the shops contracted in ongoing and previous projects, and with local CBOs where orientation meetings used to take place and communities are already familiar with the team and the mechanisms to reach out to Oxfam and to MA’AN. The project design has considered gender and protection principles by emphasizing on the participation of women as well as further responding to women and people with disabilities needs.This will be integrated through -Orientation sessions on the project activities with the project beneficiaries following rights-based approaches when engaging with communities.-Dissemination of the feedback mechanism under this project under Ma’an and Oxfam’s responsibility, -The use of accountability community engagement mechanisms: such as the dissemination of the beneficiary's selection criteria, clarity and orientation of the selection process, announcement to community of activities and channels and procedures to receive provided benefits etc.-Carrying out safe programming training to MA’AN's field and project staff that ensures continuous identification of risks and acting upon these risks along the project cycle.- Selection of IGAs beneficiaries will be carried out through a committee that consists of MA'AN and Oxfam technical teams to assess applications and do joint decision making on the most qualified beneficiaries
