Project Summary

As the region of NWS enters its 11th year of conflict, humanitarian actors and agencies recognize the necessity to integrate more durable modalities in order to transition from an unsustainable mechanism of purely emergency assistance. While capacity building is already at the core of NGO1A’s preexisting activities, NGO1A and its sub partner will aim to further empower local communities and markets and narrow the unemployment rate by improving access to livelihood opportunities and increasing the resiliency of vulnerable, conflict-affected men, women, youth, children, and PWD through the provision of short-term skills building trainings and startup kits and through offering a 20-day temporary job opportunity, both skilled and unskilled, through a Cash for Work (CFW) intervention.

4 - Needs

2.8 million IDPs in Northwest Syria (NWS) are in need of basic services and livelihood assistance, as needs have been exacerbated by economic decline, protracted conflict, growing food insecurity, and the continued COVID-19 pandemic (UNHCR, 2021). Additionally, ERL is now identified as one of the top needs due to price increases and loss of income. According to our organization’s November 2021 Multi-sectoral Program Post Evaluation under other donor funding (“Scaling Up Flexible Health, Mental Health and Support to Conflict Affected Syrians”), key recommendations for intervention included: highlighting importance to livelihood opportunities and coping strategies for individuals, particularly considering the impact of economic problems and other contextual stressors, addressing the lack of livelihood projects and vocational training workshops targeting women which will further present exposure to protection risks, reduce dependency level and increase the wellbeing of survivors, strengthening the internal referral system with livelihood services or considering the establishment of new ones. Thus, there is a need to engage families with protection issues and children who are idle, while ensuring equal representation across genders and PWDs, in meaningful economic empowerment activities that will enhance purchasing power of families and stimulate local economic growth which will reduce stresses triggered by lack of economic opportunities and stimulate the local market.

5 - Implementation of activities

Livelihoods Restoration: These activities will be integrated with our organization’s existing protection activities - targeting the most vulnerable with safe and sustainable livelihood opportunities and critical protection assistance to increase their income, reduce the risk of GBV and CP, and reduce the risk of adopting negative coping mechanisms. By complementing skills building training with startup kits, beneficiaries will be able to start their own businesses/income generating activities like sewing, knitting, hairdressing, pastry, drinking water purification, agro-food processing including pickles, jams, table olive, and dairy production through small grants and have access to productive assets. Temporary Employment: Through our organization’s sub partner, AFH, this project will offer 660 individuals a 20-day temporary job opportunity, both skilled and unskilled, through a Cash for Work (CFW) intervention where beneficiaries will be linked with the agro-dominant value chains activities. CVA: The project will coordinate with other actors providing CVA, local leaders in the target locations including area-based CWGs, and targeted communities to identify gaps in service provision that can be addressed through CfW interventions, verify list of beneficiaries to avoid duplication/double targeting, and harmonize cash delivery approaches like daily wage rates, working hours per day, timing, project duration, and cash delivery mechanism to ensure our intervention does no harm.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

• Our organization has prepared a safeguarding action plan to promote and make sure Safeguarding measures are in place. • A safeguarding policy is being distributed for all staff to read and sign acknowledge • Our organization delivers MAST training which includes SEA training for new hires and an annual refresher for all staff. • Our organization has safeguarding focal points in the fields for the staff to revert to in case of any concern, question, or complaint. • Our organization has safeguarding posters placed in all of our facilities along with the several complaint hotlines that staff or beneficiaries can use. • Our Protection Team delivers training sessions for all staff for the safeguarding and the referral pathways. • Our Protection Team conducts a safeguarding risk assessment for all facilities • our organization established the CBRFM, taking into consideration community awareness • Safeguarding measures are implemented in the recruitment processes to make sure suitable candidates are hired.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R: Lack of technical expertise and untimely delivery of materials can limit the effectiveness of CFW / M: A) Limit need for technical expertise by keeping project design simple and focused on projects that require minimal expert oversight B) Provide pre-work training/orientation to workers C) Plan early procurement, storage, delivery of material supplies; R: Participants may not have employment after the end of the program or may have difficulties transitioning as CFW activities phase out / M: A) Consider designing CFW with a gradual phase-out, such that participants work fewer hours per week towards the end of the project and have time to adapt and seek work elsewhere B) Integrate CFW with livelihood or production support programs C) Time the program to coincide with seasons when labor opportunities are few and to phase out as normal or seasonal labor opportunities increase (e.g., at harvest time) R: CFW may cause disruption to local labor markets / M: A) Do not set wage rates above the average level B) Limit the number of days that people can work with the project. C) Do not implement programs at times when they could compete with normal labor opportunities D) Conduct market assessment before and after project; R: More IDPs than residents meet the vulnerability criteria causing social tension / M: A) Communicate clearly how the CFW benefits the whole community, even if there are more IDP participants.
