Project Summary

Food for the Hungry (FH) in partnership with Medical Teams International (MTI) as the ‘Joint Rohingya Response Program (JRRP)’ aims to directly improve the health status of 58,636 individuals, of which 27,185 males and 29,451 females are Rohingya refugees in Camps 5, 7, and 12 of Kutupalong Extension Camp and 2,000 are from the nearby host community, Palong Khali. 53% (30,936) of the targeted beneficiaries are <18yrs. In 2021, the JRRP will provide quality comprehensive primary health care services including community outreach health promotion and preventative interventions. Services shall be provided through two primary health care centers (PHC #048, PHC #115), 1 health post (#051) and 1 Severe Acute Respiratory Infection Isolation and Treatment Center (SARI-ITC HP#050). JRRP will provide surveillance, case identification, and referral following the defined pathway. Sexual/reproductive health services shall include basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care, referrals, ANC/PNC, and Family Planning impacting over 14,159 women of reproductive age, 2831 pregnant and lactating women, and 11,372 adolescents. Child Health, Immunization, Mental Health and Non-Communicable disease management shall be fully integrated. Disaster risk reduction, gender and protection concerns shall be mainstreamed and emergency preparedness plans developed for cyclones/outbreaks. A network of 90 male and female Community Health Workers(CHWs), 12 TBAs and 20 Community Psychosocial Workers (CPWs) shall be equipped to cover 18,966 households in Camps 5,7 and 12. Monitoring mechanisms will include supervision visits, data analysis and beneficiary feedback (complaints boxes, community meetings). FH/MTI works with UNHCR as an institutional donor who has committed 80%. Therefore FH seeks to raise 20% match funding to support operations, human resource costs and field activities not covered by the UN-funding to ensure timely delivery of quality services. FH/MTI also plans to continue increasing reproductive services uptake at community level and the scope and coverage of emergency preparedness and response.

1 - Identified Needs

"Approximately 1.36 million Women, girls, men and boys need humanitarian assistance in Cox's Bazar district of which more than 889,000 are Rohingya refugees whose needs are critical. The refugees continue to face numerous health risks that increase morbidity and mortality and these risks are higher given the fluid COVID-19 situation. In addition to COVID-19, outbreaks of diphtheria, measles and acute watery diarrhea continue to stretch camp health systems. The camps are located in natural disaster prone areas meaning the effects of cyclones and monsoons each year worsen the already severe vulnerability. While Expanded Program on Immunizations (EPI) activities are in place, complete coverage was low in 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic situation and there was a dropout rate of more than 66%, meaning herd immunity levels have not been reached. Strong follow up and mobilization systems for vaccination are needed in 2021 to ensure complete vaccination coverage. Improved coordination & regular monitoring is needed to reduce missed opportunities.Malnutrition rates remain high among children under 5 with GAM rates of between 10-14.9% and stunting rates of >30%, while the prevalence of anemia among non-pregnant women aged 15-49 was >20% (ACF survey 2019). Over 66% of mothers did not initiate breastfeeding within 1 hour of delivery and only 51% of infants under 6 months are exclusively breastfed (ISCG, 2020).According to the MSNA 2020 report, 45% of the population seek self-treatment from local clinics and pharmacies from inadequately trained staff. 35% of the population have borrowed money to finance their health care. In 2020, the clinic utilization rate in FHMTI facilities fell to less than 2 consultations per person per year. This was due to misconceptions about COVID-19 that affected service uptake, especially at the beginning of the epidemic. Treatment for non-communicable disease (NCDs), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services and other specialized services (eye-care, geriatric care, oral healthcare and services for people with disabilities and palliative needs) remains limited.Over 14,000 Women of Reproductive Age (WRA) need SRH services of which 3000 are Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) (UNFPA calculator). Access to SRH services has improved, evidenced by an increase in skilled deliveries from under 45% to almost 60% across the settlement; however, this is still lower than global targets (CHWG data). Over 6% of all deliveries are to girls aged under 18 years, increasing the risks of pregnancy related complications including death. CEMONC centers in camps are not open 24/7 given specialists’ rotation and lack of blood products needs to avert maternal deaths. This leaves mothers with options of referral to cox’s bazar that is approximately 2.5 hours by vehicle.Existing needs were exacerbated by the Covid -19 outbreak, with to date 5,304 confirmed cases across Cox’s Bazar District. Among the refugees, children aged 0-10 account for 36% of confirmed cases, and women/girls for 52%. Even though 83% of refugees in the recent survey reported wearing cloth masks all the time they are outside their homes, observations from humanitarian actors suggest otherwise, and some women indicated in the survey that they felt that a face veil/scarf was sufficient. This highlights the need for ongoing community engagement to achieve behavior change and avoid community complacency, until a longer term solution such as vaccination is achieved."

2 - Activities or outputs

Facility-Based Primary Health Care Package; Health Care Centers will provide the minimum services as defined by the sector essential services package for Rohingya refugees. Up to 111200 (F-57824, M-53376, U5-22240 & 5+ - 88960) consultations are targeted for 2021. Adequate qualified male and female clinical and support staff shall be retained and/or recruited to provide curative and preventative services ensuring strong IPC systems and practices to optimize active and sentinel surveillance through the Early Warning, Alert and Response System (EWARS). Appropriate PPE will always be provided to clinic staff. Quality improvement activities including monitoring visits will be carried out by a Technical Advisor. Clinicians will receive mhGAP HIG training or refresher training to ensure mental health issues are identified and treated. Essential mhGAP medications will be procured and supplied. Psychologists and counsellors at each supported PHC will provide PFA, basic counselling for individuals, families and groups and psychotherapy. Referral to secondary care and other actors will be provided whenever indicated.Expanded Program on Immunization: Provide vaccines at the approved, fixed sites (PHC #048 and PHC #115) (and will advocate for approval in HP #051) to ensure that all mothers and children born & consulted in these facilities receive the needed vaccines. Participate in special immunization activities with Upazila’s Health Complex and WHO, advocate for outreach EPI activities to increase immunization coverage and reduce dropout rates. The complete schedule of EPI will be provided as guided by the MoHSexual reproductive health services: Provide comprehensive BEmONC services including PMTCT following WHO/GoB and Sphere standards supported by qualified staff. Provide referrals to CEmONC Centers for complex cases. Adolescent reproductive health will be prioritized to reduce numbers of teenage pregnancies and their associated complications. Increase family planning uptake through increasing awareness and provision of contraceptives for women of reproductive age ensuring that family planning supplies are consistently available at supported PHCs. In addition to 15 midwives committed by UNHCR, 10 additional midwives and a Midwife Supervisor shall be recruited and trained in order to offer quality services to mothers.Program operations support: Medicines, medical and nonmedical supplies that are not received through the UNHCR supply chain will be procured in accordance with FH/MTI and donor Procurement Guidelines. The medical supplies shall be stored in well managed stores with temperature and humidity control, appropriate cold chain control, and rodent free environments where feasible to minimize loss. Improved warehouse management software will be operationalised. This will ensure that impact population get quality services at JRRP. Rent and maintenance of health center premises, Office premises and hostels for SARI ITC staff shall be covered. Contribution to senior program management staff costs especially quality assurance staff and expatriate staff offering strategic program leadership.Community-based Activities: Recruit 16 traditional birth attendants and train them in safe motherhood promotion and referral to BEMONC centers. Provide referral assistance through porters from community to health centers especially in remote and hard to reach areas such as Camp 7.Mainstreaming Disaster Risk Reduction: FHMTI shall actively participate in emergency preparedness and response mechanisms to natural disasters like cyclones, monsoons and epidemics like measles, cholera, COVID-19. This will be done by having mobile medical teams that are trained and ready to respond and prepositioning contingency response stocks.
