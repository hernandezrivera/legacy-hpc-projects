Project Summary

Somalia remains a challenging context revealing quite protection challenges, the ongoing multi- layered conflicts and insecurity continue to heavily influence the situation throughout Somalia, impacting on access to affected populations and causing displacement. The situation is complex with wide variety of causes of displacement, armed groups and protection violations. Somalia exists in a state of legal pluralism where customary law (Xeer), religious law (Sharia) and secular law operate. Among the three, Xeer is the dominant system to govern societal relations and serious crimes and there is weak enforcement of secular law, and most areas in south central, formal courts and government structures exist only in urban centres. The divisions between social groups, coupled with competition over scarce resources, fuel conflict as well as systemic discrimination and marginalization of groups across the country. Exclusion from receiving humanitarian assistance due to status, abilities or minority clan affiliation is a protection concern.
Moreover the violation of HLP rights is commonplace across Somalia and the protection thereof remains largely inadequate. However, limitation in HLP capacity is not unique to government; it transcends state institutions and encompasses humanitarian organizations.The challenges and protection risks engendered by such limitations are overwhelming with far-reaching consequences, including recurring displacements, destruction of HLP assets, competition over resources resulting to tensions between host communities and displaced populations, land conflicts which is a key risk in most return areas , widespread tenure insecurity, and forced evictions occurring at alarming rate in predominantly urban areas. From January 2020 to October 2021, 294,305 individuals were forcefully evicted across the country , underpinned largely by insecure land tenure, and in most cases, the incidents resulted in land grabbing and multiple counter ownership claims. A new National IDP Policy, National Eviction Guidelines, National Interim Land Protocol and a National Durable Solutions Strategy have been approved. Although implementation and enforcement remain a challenge, these policy initiatives are important steps toward addressing HLP problems across the country, including forced evictions and access to justice.
SOHRA, through this application aims to respond to HLP needs and to address the acute protection needs of individuals facing HLP violations including forced evictions, and severe distress. This includes providing wide range of HLP activities that are aimed at ensuring respect for the rights of all individuals, regardless of their age, gender or social, ethnic, national, religious or other background. Housing, Land and Property interventions shall include: eviction monitoring, reporting, response and advocacy, information, counselling and legal assistance services for HLP cases.
