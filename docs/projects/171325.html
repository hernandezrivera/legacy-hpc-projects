Project Summary

There are approximately 2,400 built IDP sites located mostly on privately owned land, hosting around 2.6 million displaced people in Somalia. IDPs are exposed to poor living conditions and they are subjected to constant displacements through forceful evictions by landlord and floods, the sites are also overcrowded, are inaccessible, and lack proper planning to allow for efficient emergency services. According to HLP AOR, from January to August 2020, a total of 105,755 displaced people were evicted mostly (92%) from Banadir and Bay regions in Somalia.
SYPD plans to strengthen the effectiveness of multi-sectoral interventions at the site level by establishing CCCM coordination mechanisms that focus on attaining durable solutions with the full participation of all stakeholders.
The most vulnerable to the challenges faced by IDPs have been identified as women who head 60% of IDP households, households headed by girls, minority clans, and persons living with disabilities or old people who have no source of income. Factors contributing to their vulnerabilities have been identified as inadequate shelter conditions, lack of long-term security of tenure, and limited employment opportunities. These factors affect their ability to secure durable, safe, accessible, and decent shelter and settlements which exposes them to gender-based violence increased spread of diseases especially COVID-19, and flooding due to poor drainage. Additionally, a person’s living with disabilities and old people are hindered in their access to information and physical access to humanitarian assistance.
Displaced people in informal settlements are in great need of CCCM services to ensure access to quality basic services, ensure dignified and safe living conditions and enhance the accountability of humanitarian actors to affected populations through community participation and consistent engagement until durable solutions are achieved.
The project targets vulnerable Internally Displaced Populations (IDPs) particularly women and persons living with disabilities who live in IDP sites that lack dignified living conditions, sufficient access to basic services, land tenure and adequate resources to sustain their populations. The project aims to use a collaborative and inclusive approach towards contributing to Strategic Objective 2: Sustain the lives of 4 million people requiring humanitarian assistance, including 2.4 million non-IDPs, 1.6 million IDPs, and people with disability across 74 districts, by ensuring safe, equitable and dignified access to livelihoods and essential services by the end of 2021. Needs of these activities are informed by the Detailed Site Assessment (DSA) of the CCCM cluster which identified that existing IDP camps coordination and management mechanisms are largely informal, have limited accountability, and little adherence to minimum standards, with most sites being managed by landlords or gatekeepers.
The project-specific objectives include:
1: Strengthen safe access to multi-sectorial services at the site level through improved site management and coordination
2: Improve living conditions of displaced people through site development, care and maintenance, and decongestion initiatives
3: Strengthen community self-management and access to information for displaced populations.

Project Activities

NEEDS ANALYSIS: Conflict, drought, insecurity, and floods are reported to be the main drivers for displaced people in Somalia where an estimated 2.6 million people have been displaced and have settled in sub-standard IDP sites in urban and peri-urban areas across the country. Majority of the displaced individuals are usually women and children who are forced to live in informal settlements where living conditions are precarious and lack access to basic needs. Most IDPs live in hazardous conditions and are exposed to numerous protection risks such as unlawful evictions, overcrowded and unsanitary environments with limited access to basic services, increased risk to Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and tensions with the host communities.There are over 2,400 IDP sites in Somalia where 84% of the sites are informal settlements located on privately owned land and 74% of these are in urban areas. Most of the IDP sites located on privately owned land are managed by landlords or gatekeepers and have informal coordination and management mechanisms. As a result of these informal mechanisms, the camps have limited accountability and little or no adherence to minimum standards. Cases of evictions are widespread, increasing the stress levels on displaced families and limiting their ability to integrate into social structures. PROJECT ACTIVITIES: SYPD proposed project activities will target vulnerable Internally Displaced Populations (IDPs) particularly women and persons living with disabilities living in IDP sites that lack dignified living conditions, sufficient access to basic services, land tenure and adequate resources to sustain their populations. The project will aim to support the target groups through:#Establishing roving site management teams to ensure coordination with community governance structures. In coordination and collaboration with other CCCM cluster implementing partners, roving site management teams will be established to act as oversight for community governance structures. # Identifying existing governance structures and focal points for sites. A mapping exercise will be conducted of all existing and newly established IDP campsite governance structures. Gaps and capacity needs within the structures will be identified and this will help inform the capacity building exercises for the structures. #Providing capacity building to existing governance structures/focal points. Following the identification of gaps and capacity needs, camp level structures will be strengthened where they exist and established where necessary, through capacity building exercises tailored to the identified gaps. SYPD will support the governance structures through the provision of community spaces for management committees and ensure the operation of timely and responsive feedback and complaint mechanisms. # Providing trainings on site maintenance and site planning to camp committees. Camp coordination and management will be strengthened in targeted locations through the provision of trainings to camp committees on maintenance and planning of camp activities. The trainings will build the capacity of camp committees and will enhance continuous upkeep of sites in a bid to minimise protection risks and ensure safety for displaced people, particularly women, girls and people with disabilities. # Supporting community-led site maintenance activities to ensure upkeep of sites (cash for work, site maintenance committees, distribution of tools). # Implement emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites (flood mitigation, fire prevention). Site improvement projects include street lighting, site layout and management, drainage systems, firebreaks, hand washing points.

Mitigation Measures

PREVENTING RISK OF EXPOSURE TO VIOLENCE DURING REGISTRATION/DISTRIBUTION – SYPD will ensure that the location and access to routes to registration and distribution points are safe. SYPD will identify areas in and around camps that could pose potential threats and monitor them regularly at different times of the day, these might include areas around showers, pit latrines, child-friendly spaces, schools and routes to schools, health facilities and water collection points. SYPD will ensure that adequate sources of lighting such as solar lights are located in areas leading to distribution points and common areas. SYPD will also ensure that safety and security measures are in place, where they will be aware of existing friction between different groups inside the camps. Care will be taken to ensure that conflicting groups are not placed together during registration/distribution activities. SYPD will also ensure to consult the host community, where applicable to identify any assistance needed and will consider providing the host community access to camp services and assistance if need be to avoid or reduce community tensions between camp and local/host communities. PREVENTING RISKS OF EXCLUSION BASED ON GENDER, AGE AND VULNERABILITY - SYPD will ensure that beneficiaries safely access basic services without causing harm by promoting meaningful access, accountability and participation of all gender (men, women, boys and girls) in the service provision. In particular, SYPD will promote the participation of beneficiaries by establishing and strengthening project committees and building their capacity through training on gender mainstreaming and resources support. The beneficiary committees will be involved in the identification of vulnerable groups and monitoring the project implementation and feedback/complaint mechanisms. SYPD will safeguard against protection risks against women and girls by ensuring that all services such as items distributions are carried out during the day, in locations close to IDPs residences and clearly guarded with crowd control mechanisms to avoid theft, intimidation and gender-based violence. Where men and women are all involved in the collection of items, SYPD will keep separate lines for women and men. Care will also be taken to ensure children are not exposed to hard work or exploitation by having to carry heavy items. Each child-headed household will be assigned a guardian during the collection of supplies to avoid exclusion and abuse.PREVENTING THE RISK OF LACK OF ACCESS TO COMPLAINT AND FEEDBACK MECHANISMS - SYPD will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedbacks mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with an aim of improving programming, understanding community perceptions and assist in detecting misconduct. This will be one through setting up a toll-free number which beneficiaries can call to report a complaint or share feedback anonymously. A feedback box will also be set up in locations that afford beneficiaries privacy to anonymously share their feedback/complaints. SYPD will respond to all feedback and complaints regardless of whether corrective measures can be put in place or not. SYPD will also arrange for awareness-raising sessions so that people know how the mechanism works. Joint feedback mechanisms with other sectors will also be considered to reduce confusion.

Please provide further explanation

The building of resilience of vulnerable individuals, households and communities in Somalia to future shocks remains of paramount importance to tackle the key risks and address the underlying causes of vulnerability for IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in terms of sustainable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services. With the increasing recognition of governments and local authorities at the target locations, we believe the protection environment is sufficiently safe/stable to enable durable solutions. Our project will coordinate with government institutions and both Federal and State levels as well as local authorities in order to promote durable solutions, housing, and land tenure systems for IDPs.Our initiative is linked to the Somali National Development Plan on Social Protection and Infrastructural Development through the improvement of resilience and improvement in land tenure system, hence making it more sustainable. The facilities built by the project such as streetlights, drainage systems etc, will be subject to warranty to ensure sustainability. Besides, the management of the facilities will be handed over to local authorities and community level camp management structures with proven interests in social wellbeing of the society for a longer-term. The community feedback and complaint mechanisms which will be established at the commencement of the project will have their views incorporated in the refining of the project design; this gives the target community the biggest say in the implementation of the agreed interventions. SYPD will work towards strengthening self-reliance of displaced people, both refugees and IDPs, by ensuring a more active engagement community structures, development actors, government ministry of water, local authorities, private owners and other donors such as the Somalia Stability Fund and the World bank. SYPD will work with the community leaders, relevant ministry at the local and national level, and local government authorities to find durable solutions including development of legally binding agreements giving rights to the target communities to use the facilities constructed by the project, ensuring secure land tenure system, capacity building and income generation activities and hand over mechanisms for community-based projects. During the project implementation, SYPD will mainstream capacity building initiatives for community water infrastructure management systems in order to create a strong management structure and eventual hand over mechanisms.
