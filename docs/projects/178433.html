Project Summary

The conflict in Syria has caused extensive damage to water networks and sewerage infrastructure, resulting in restricted access to potable water and affecting domestic and personal hygiene, which in turn has increased the risk of disease outbreaks. Providing adequate environmental health services is even more critical during the COVID-19 pandemic to avoid any additional public health hazard.
In 2021, UNRWA will continue providing essential WASH services to Palestine refugees. WASH items will be distributed to Palestine refugees affected by emergencies, as well as Palestine refugees spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas and those facing access constraints, to help safeguard their personal and domestic hygiene especially in the context of COVID-19.
The Agency will also conduct minor repair and maintenance of infrastructure systems including water, sewerage and surface drainage systems, in addition to collecting and managing solid waste and controlling rodents and insects in accessible camps. Building on 2020 COVID-19 prevention measures, UNRWA will ensure that all sanitation labourers continue to be equipped with PPE. Moreover, UNRWA will continue to conduct additional disinfection campaigns, sterilization activities and sanitization of garbage collection points to ensure that Palestine refugees living in the camps can apply basic hygiene methods to prevent the further spread of COVID-19.

4 - Needs

The living conditions of Palestine refugees in Syria are particularly concerning in terms of WASH services. Yarmouk, Dera’a, and Ein El-Tal (Aleppo) camps, previously home to more than 30 per cent of the Palestine refugee population in Syria, have been almost completely destroyed, including water and sanitation infrastructure. More than 400 vulnerable Palestine refugee families have already gone back to Yarmouk despite the lack of basic infrastructure and WASH services . According to an assessment conducted by UNRWA in July 2020, close to 60 per cent of the Palestine refugees are not living in their own houses and living elsewhere in overcrowded conditions. 7 per cent said there is no availability of a healthy and appropriate toilet in the house. Over 40 per cent of Palestine refugee households confirmed mobile tanks to be the source of drinking water, and 70 per cent households said the source of water for cleaning is the residence faucet . A total of 15 per cent of Palestine refugee households confirmed that vulnerable individuals in the family (including persons with disabilities, children, women, older persons) encountered challenges due to COVID-19, lack of soap and other hygiene items being one of the biggest challenges .

5 - Implementation of activities

UNRWA will continue providing essential WASH services to Palestine refugees. Hygiene kits (HK) will be distributed to Palestine refugees affected by emergencies, as well as those spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas and those facing access constraints, to help safeguard their personal and domestic hygiene especially in the context of COVID-19. HKs are distributed as part of the non-food items package provided to the targeted families (which consists of blankets, mattresses, mats, packages, and tarpaulins).Each HK contains hygiene and sanitary items that families need in emergencies, in addition to other items designed for women and girls. Each HK includes sanitary napkins, soap, toothbrushes, towels etc. These elements aim to help maintain the personal and domestic hygiene of the refugees, including the dignity of women and girls, and increase their mobility during emergencies. The Agency will also conduct minor repair and maintenance of infrastructure systems including water, sewerage and surface drainage systems, in addition to collecting and managing solid waste and controlling rodents and insects in accessible camps. Building on 2020 COVID-19 prevention measures, UNRWA will ensure that all sanitation labourers continue to be equipped with PPE. Moreover, UNRWA will continue to conduct additional disinfection campaigns, sterilization activities and sanitization of garbage collection points to ensure that Palestine refugees living in the camps can apply basic hygiene methods to prevent the further spread of COVID-19. Sanitation works is conducted by UNRWA in ten Palestine refugee camps: Jaramana, Sbeineh, Jaramana, Khan Eshieh, Khan Dannoun, Homs, Hama, Latakia, Neirab, and Dera’a.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK 1.Beneﬁciaries face security/safety risks when travelling to and from WASH services. • Likelihood: Low, Impact: High• Mitigating measures:- UNRWA prioritizes maintaining and restoring WASH services in camps, in order to minimize unnecessary movements of beneﬁciaries. - UNRWA prioritizes awareness raising and training to staff, community members, including children, on reducing exposure to risks related to ERW. - UNRWA conducts regular protection audits to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and optimize its compliance to the protection principles. RISK 2. Timing and process of distribution (hygiene kits) put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. • Likelihood: High, Impact: High • Mitigation measures: Safe and secure distribution points are selected. DSS staff are present to ensure the process is going smoothly. Spacious sites are selected and the distribution is done over several days to avoid crowdedness/frustrations. Separate queues for men and women are created. Female staff are present on site to address female beneﬁciaries’ needs. Staff take into consideration the needs of elderly beneﬁciaries, and persons with disabilities.

Risk: SEA during distribution of WASH items • Likelihood: medium • Impact: high • Mitigation Measures: 1. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. 5. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 6. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure conﬁdentiality and set up accessible and eﬃcient complaint mechanisms. 7. UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is responded to.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. RISK 1: UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. The RBM approach to programming ensures evidence-based decision-making and accountability. Risks are also mitigated by ensuring a sound and solid monitoring of implementation. RISK 2: UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. RISK 3: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization materials will be made available at offices and distribution centres.
