Project Summary

This project will seek to build on gains made in ADA projects supported by CARITAS GERMANY in 2017 to 2019, meeting the needs of approximately 18,000 conflict-affected individuals (indirect beneficiaries) and 3000 Households (direct beneficiaries) through Food Security and Livelihoods response. Food assistance distribution was active through April, 2018 to March 2019. Agricultural inputs and fishing kits and capacity building is active from April to December 2019. Therefore, this FSL Programming is aimed at enhancing emergency food production through complementary cropping, vegetable gardening, fishing and capacity building through agronomic best practices to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs and diversify livelihoods through fish production and to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks that affects beneficiaries. Nutrition training sessions and food demonstrations will also be provided and incorporated into the capacity training tool in order to help mitigate the underlying causes of poor or under nutrition, and complement the current FSL activities.

1 - Needs

According to the August 2019 Famine Early Warning Systems Networks IPC analysis, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes remain pervasive in South Sudan, but the proportion of the population experiencing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes during the May-August lean season slightly declined from 59% in 2018 to 54% in 2019. An estimated 6.35 million people faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes at the July-August peak of the lean season in the presence of humanitarian assistance. Food security is expected to seasonally improve in several areas with the availability of the main harvest through January, but Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes or worse will likely remain prevalent especially in Ayod county and there is greatest risk for the affected peoples to slide back to emergency (IPC phase 4) without consistent FSL response to the affected communities. A risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) will likely continue to persist in areas already classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse including Ayod county. Humanitarian food assistance continues to prevent more extreme outcomes in parts of Greater Upper Nile, especially in Nasir county. However, despite lower conflict events and improvements in humanitarian access, the number of beneficiaries reached with humanitarian food assistance has declined since May . Assistance continues to remain below the estimated need and is slightly lower than the same time last year, yet this is affected more, with the continuous floods and returnees from Ethiopia. Food security improvements through January are expected to be facilitated by the decline in conflict events and driven by similar to slightly better crop production and fishing activity compared to 2018. The decline in conflict has facilitated some improvements in trade flows and household movement, which is permitting greater engagement in livelihoods activities such as fishing, cropping, vegetable gardening and capacity building, access to markets, and seasonal declines in food prices. In areas where households lost crops to flooding especially Ayod and Nasir counties or did not plant and where households still face limited access to markets or food assistance, Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes are expected to endure, as will a risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5). Although food security has slightly improved, high levels of acute malnutrition have endured. Nutrition data collected during the 24th round of Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Systems (FSNMS R24) classified global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence in Ayod and Nasir counties as ’Serious’ (GAM WHZ 10.0-14.9%) or ’Critical’ (GAM WHZ 15.0-29.9%), which are indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) in Ayod county and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) in Nasir county. Critical levels of GAM continue to be observed in Ayod and Nasir counties, among the local population is mainly driven by low food factors, including high morbidity, poor access to nutritious food such as vegetables. Besides, conflict events have occurred in recent months in Duk county, disrupting seasonal farming activities, displacing and exacerbating peoples situation to worse and causing resource constraints in Ayod, to the already limited services. Markets in state capitals and in some rural areas are beginning to recover. Based on a rapid assessment of Juba markets conducted by FEWS NET in late July and early August, relative improvements in security have served to open major trade routes and this is increasing both domestic and regional commodity flows, exceptions of Ayod and Nasir areas where flooding has hampered in and out flow of goods and services, hence affecting people livelihoods and food consumption levels. Ross Director for Nasir county early Sept 2019 reported 15,901 returnees in Mandeang, 14,836 in Jigmir, 8,615 in Makak and 14,269 in Maker Payams bio-metrically registered. An Inter-Agency assessment by ADA, INTERSOS, RAO on July 2019 reported 1437HHs affected IDPs and returnees in Ayod County.

2 - Activities

I. With support of local authorities, ADA will do identification, registration and verification of beneficiaries, in close coordination and collaboration with the local authorities, community committees, enumerators and other partners on the ground. It will use the standardized FSL cluster tools and methodologies. During selection and registration, high priority will be given to GAM and the most vulnerable members of the IDPs, Returnees and Host Community, in consideration of the most elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers, people with special needs or physical impairments, persons with no sources of livelihoods. Verification process will be accompanied by issuing of tokens. II. ADA will facilitate formation of community committees and establish a clear and strong relationship with the community through continuous community communication and engagement (CCE) throughout the project cycle, by soliciting and sharing feedback from different stakeholders in the community. The elected committees will 1) help to disseminate information on access issues relating to distribution points amongst the different communities 2) support with identification of the most vulnerable persons 3) identify GBV and protection cases and refer for further help III. ADA will distribute Vegetable and Crops seeds and tools to 3000 HHs and Fishing Kits to 1000HHs from the FAO/WFP core pipeline agricultural inputs (Vegetables Seeds: Eggplants, Okra and Tomatoes; Field Crops seeds: Maize, Cow peas and Sorghum. Tools: molado and Fishing gears (hooks and twines). A distribution plan will be prepared stating the distribution timelines and locations and share this with both the cluster and donor. IV. Agriculture skills trainers will identify 80 lead farmers and 40 fisher folks among the targeted beneficiaries, train them on nursery bed establishment & management, pests and diseases control, and post-harvest handling and fishing techniques and preservation measures. Lead farmers/fisher folks will be identified among the beneficiaries and will be given additional technical and leadership skills training to ensure sustainability and ongoing coaching of the community/beneficiaries beyond the project life time. V. ADA will conduct awareness raising on the signs, dangers and risks of early child marriage and gender base violence identification, mitigation, prevention and highlight risk behaviour examples at the family level to the beneficiaries. The awareness will build knowledge of communities to dialogue on family issues such as drunkenness. Awareness on sharing of family roles by both male and female counterparts shall be provided. a monthly 30-60 minutes session on GBV/protection awareness raising will be provided to the beneficiaries by the trainers VI. ADA will pass key messages of peace and the importance of co-existence among the IDPs, returnees and host community to restore hope from the effects of conflict to building trust and confidence, and acceptance of different ethnic groups or communities to share common community resources such as boreholes, schools, health centers, roads and markets among others. A monthly 30-60 minutes session on peace messages will be provided to the beneficiaries by the trainers VII. A Post-Distribution Monitoring will be conducted within 2-3 weeks after distribution of the agricultural inputs, in accordance to FSL Cluster standards and will capture beneficiaries’ feedback on current living status, level of satisfaction on the inputs/service received, quality of the inputs and services received, and if the inputs distributed responded to their priority needs. The samples/respondents for the monitoring will be randomly selected.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, beneficiaries rights will be legally recognized, respected and promoted through this project design in accordance to the United Nations Deceleration on Human Rights, international human rights treaties and commission. The human rights deceleration requires that humanitarian aid projects that intends to address crisis, emergency or catastrophes incidences be designed to allow maximum participation, fair and equal treatment, access to projects for all, to all affected persons regardless of their color, race, religion, affiliation, sexual orientation. This project is designed to allow equal participation and engagement done through consultation of the affected persons to ensure interventions respond to solve the immediate and priority needs of the affected people. The beneficiaries have the legal right to report tribalism, sexual abuse and exploitation, mismanagement of project funds including diversion of funds for personal gain by Agency staffs to the donors, cluster and the government for action and accountability. This can be done through established complaint reporting and feedback mechanism.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project target to reach to 3000 households of most vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and host community in Ayod and Nasir counties. The Caseload allocation or breakdown is 2000 households will be enrolled in Nasir and 1000 households in Ayod, which is inline with the priority needs analysis of the affected populations. Caseload allocation per beneficiaries category; 7200 households will be IDPs, 8100 households will be Returnees and 2700 households will be the most vulnerable Host Community, regarded to as the project beneficiaries. The project intended benefits include capacity building on agronomic best practices including crop rotation, mulching, mix cropping, thinning, establishment of nursery beds, pests and diseases control and management, harvesting and post harvest handling, fish catching techniques and best preservation methods. Besides, the beneficiaries will have access to agricultural inputs and fishing kits for their household consumption or for planing, which in returns will generate food and incomes to enable them engage in investment activities to improve their well being.The project is designed to allow for maximum participation and engagement of all affected people regardless of their being. A prior information will be passed to the community on potential date for project sensitization and mobilization on the project start, objectives, selection criteria and target beneficiaries, separation of responsibility, project expectation or outcomes and exit strategy to ensure all vulnerable households and authorities receives the information about the project coming into their community. The project beneficiaries registration, verification and distribution dates must be made open and announced publicly to all members of the community, to ensure inclusivity and to promote transparency and accountability to the affected persons. The project will connect beneficiaries with other people to share and exchange information, experiences and learning.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, ADA jointly work with the government mainly RRC/ROSS through project coordination and joint missions which are planned collectively and aligned to the government priority plans. Prior to project inception or entry, project meeting for implementation will be conducted with the local authorities in the project areas, both at the state, County, payam or boma levels as safety audits measures for projects links with government plans. The communities in question were consulted prior to the project design phase through needs assessment and existing community feedback mechanism and the interests, needs, aspirations and future goals were included into the programming and implementation plan. This project complements the many contributions of the national government, Jonglei and Upper Nile States and FSL cluster Food Security and Livelihoods plans and objectives on the communities needs in Nasir and Ayod Counties. It recognizes and will endeavour to work with existing network of local community structures; utilizing the support of local leadership and other partners such as ADA, INTERSOS, CMD, ADRA, NH, CRS, ALSI, RMF and CAO in Ayod and Nasir areas as well as the beneficiaries to ensure that only the most vulnerable populations receive the project inputs and skills. During the beneficiary selection stage, ADA will consult all partners with ongoing related projects in the counties, regarding them as the primary channel for sharing and crosschecking of information on targeted beneficiaries and needs for this project. ADA is statically located, hence will participate in all FSL coordination meetings to update on activities, challenges, achievements, lessons learned and best practices to leverage coordination synergies and improve programming with other stakeholders on ground, and also contribute to state and county level contingency plan.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, this project is aligned to the national plan or/framework for returnees, IDPs and Refugees reintegration and it is consistent across clusters in the country. The humanitarian relief programming and coordination is regulated by the Relief and Rehabilitation commission at the national, state and county levels in conjunction with OCHA support. The FSL cluster and OCHA does the alignment and coordination with support of cluster state focal points to ensure that project goals, objectives and activities are in line with national objectives and that the implementation criteria does not deviate from planned cluster methodology. The project developed its goal and objective from the overall cluster general objectives which were developed in consultation and participation of national ministry of agriculture, animal and fisheries resources priorities for 2020. The project aligned these to its organizational strategic plans and priorities for Ayod and Nasir counties. The FSL cluster consulted the refugees and returnees lead Agency -UNHCR to ensure the cluster priorities are well aligned to the national returns/refugees framework.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the security situation of Nasir and Ayod counties is currently stable as a result of reduced armed conflicts and the revitalized signed peace agreement between the warring parties, paving opportunities and an enabling environment for affected populations participation and engagement in productive activities including crop production, petty trade, market exchange, business exchange or transaction, social networks and safety nets development for support and flow of foreign aid into the country to boost the local economy. Local authorities are part and passion of the project design and will be the primary entry points for safety and security guarantee and clearance on safety issues for both humanitarian organizations and beneficiaries in this project. The project design team contacted and consulted the authorities about the security situation and the safety of the beneficiary before the HRP proposal, highlighted above on the needs analysis and reported very calm at the moment. They will be briefing the project team from time to time during the implementation phases to ensure consistent safety measures are guaranteed to the humanitarian assets and are secured from damage and that beneficiaries lives are well protected from physical harm. This project has considered protection measures against exploitation and abuses of vulnerable beneficiaries including rape and sexual abuse through use of power and possession of assets. Sensitization sessions will be provided during the project inception and implementation on the risk factors leading to sexual exploitation and abuses and mitigation measures and reporting channels for immediate support, action and assistance. Project employees are well trained on GBV and child protection measures to ensure safety audits for beneficiaries and holistic programming for smooth integration across the project activities.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, ADA is implementing a three-year project renewed annually, advocating towards FSL resilience approaches and ending emergency live saving responses with support from CARITAS GERMANY. Therefore, ADA will continue to monitor the project and statically support beneficiaries ongoing practices with coaching and mentor-ship especially of lead farmers to be able to provide continuous guidance to beneficiaries in their respective communities as needed after the project period ends. Other sector gaps that may be identified will be reported through different cluster forums with the capacity to respond to the specific needs. The one year period, in addition to ADA’s experience in Ayod and Nasir Counties and involvement of other partners, will help the team to identify and map other service providers, ensuring that all the gaps identified are reported to specific partners for further intervention as needed. In addition, ADA will apply its funding strategy to solicit for additional donor resources for these locations to ensure that vulnerable people that may be left out in this FSL complementary and resilience farming response will be covered with none HRP funding.The FSL capacity skills training, demonstration plots and establishment and inputs distribution will be entirely community based, helping the beneficiaries including the youth to carry along the skills gained from this experience to promote self –reliance and resilience approaches. This will increase food production and consumption to reduce hunger and poverty levels in Ayod and Nasir Counties. As stated above, ADA will maintain its presence in the two counties and will be able to monitor the use of the project inputs and tools and provide further guidance if improper use is reported by partners or community.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ADA will conduct intensive mobilization and sensitization to reach out information to all intended communities to ensure members of the most vulnerable community are included in consultations and/or humanitarian services/assistance. Use various channels of communication including through churches, markets, schools, hospitals, local authorities to ensure all members of the community in all project locations receives or hears the information. The project will establish a community committee comprising of all categories of affected persons including the disable, lactating and breastfeeding mothers, elderly, youths, women and girls to ensure the most vulnerable beneficiaries are identified, selected and registered into the project to access the intended assistance. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas would require discussions on land tenure system with local authorities and sensitization of the affected community to ensure affected persons land rights are not violated or neglected and they have land ownership or title deed rights and have access to land for farming and settlement. ADA project team with support from HLP Agency such as NRC will engage and advocate through the local authorities for beneficiaries rights access to land for settlement and farming or rearing animals. Hold consultations with the displaced women and girls to thoroughly assess their priority needs and provide timely resources and mental support to reduce their vulnerability to exploitation and abuse by those with power or in possession of assets. Besides, ADA will provide better channel of communication on access to information about food, protection/GBV survival supplies, medical services, water, education and shelter.

Feedback from vulnerable women and girls can be accessed through establishment of complaint and feedback reporting desk managed by all category of affected persons including female staff to mitigate stereotype or/ fear of reporting gender sensitive issues that matters to women and girls, elderly to ensure vulnerable elderly persons are included, male to ensure most vulnerable male persons are included, disable to ensue persons with disability are included. Use of local staffs/enumerators and language is a preferred channel of communication to enable affected communities voice out their issues without fear, thus helping the project to better serve them.
