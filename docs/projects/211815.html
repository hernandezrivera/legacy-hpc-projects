Project Summary

With 16.7M people in need, the humanitarian crisis in Syria showcases the demand of sustained intervention in 2024. 15.3M people are in need of protection, 7.2M children and education personnel require emergency education services (HNO 2024). 2.45M children aged 5–17 years, are out of school 40% in Rural Damascus and 38% in Aleppo), and over a million are at risk of dropping out (UNICEF). Efforts in education and protection are paramount, catering to the urgent needs of children and adolescents, particularly young girls, and children with disabilities.
AVSI’s integrated approach aims to safeguard Education and promote Protection, enhancing access to quality services, and nurturing self-resilience and self-esteem. The project will be implemented in Rural Damascus and Rural Aleppo, expanding the reach of previous results, and impacting sustainability in the subdistrict of Harasta and Jabel Saman. In line with the Education SO2, the project will enhance quality education and access thereto, intervening in four schools (all primary schools, one including secondary level) through NFE, art therapy sessions, and the provision of incentives and educational material. To strengthen technical capacities and prevent protection risks (Protection SO2), teachers and parents will participate in training sessions addressing bullying, PSA, violence, and positive parenting. Moreover, the project will deliver rehabilitated and furnished spaces in the schools, and leverage access to services therein (Education SO1).
To provide adequate response, AVSI’s applies population consultations, investment in monitoring resources, and feedback mechanisms. AVSI will identify (or create) safe spaces for the implementation of activities to provide individuals with a sense of security, hospitality, attention, and care. One protection risk lies on the violence that people, particularly women, experience moving from one place to another. In response to this, AVSI conducts an attentive selection of quality services and transportation support. Regular monitoring will boost timely prevention measures against protection threats and ensure “Do no Harm”. In addition, community engagement prevents the risk of exacerbating disagreement and strengthens the sense of ownership, hindering discrimination among beneficiaries.
AVSI’s PSEAH strategy reinforces the commitment to safeguarding personnel, beneficiaries, and communities contributing to the HRP SO2 and the creation of a secure environment. AVSI's PSEAH policy, Child Safeguarding Policy, and Code of Conduct follow IASC guidelines and DAC recommendations. The measures in place include rigorous staff screening, comprehensive awareness training (last one in January 2024), risk assessment and a transparent reporting mechanism. Thus, community engagement remains pivotal, empowering people to report misconduct and reinforcing a zero-tolerance approach. The identification of beneficiaries, particularly women, girls, and children, will be guided by the analysis of needs, exposure to protection risks, and individual or family vulnerability.
AVSI can count on the solid relationship with local authorities, enhancing access to the implementing areas and facilitating clearances. Political and social situation will be regularly monitored to adapt strategies and readjust activities. Socio-cultural barriers will be addressed through cultural-sensitive training to staff to engage within diverse communities. In parallel, logistical challenges are overcome through adaptive planning, regular risk assessments, and transparent communication. Exchange rate volatility and irregular in-country cash are actual risks. Measures such as expedited procurement schemes and payment procedures helps the organization in minimizing delays and inconveniences. Moreover, the risk of fraud or corruption is mitigated through internal procedures (DIGE 8/2020) that set high standards of accountability, transparency and regulatory compliance.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project has been designed based on the achievements of previous interventions. AVSI acknowledges the importance of boosting the Education-Protection nexus to enhance the impact, both in the short and the long term, of the intervention on the target population. The targeted schools in Rural Damascus include primary school and kindergarten, counting a total of 2183 children (1222 boys, 961 girls) and 80 teachers. At the same time, the institute in Jabel Saman is a primary school, and the one in Almadina Alsinayea adds a secondary school, together they count a total of 1741 children (837 boys, 904 girls) and 48 teachers. The selection of the most vulnerable children is determined by the criteria provided by the clusters, the technical working groups, and following the guidelines of the MoE. Those attending the remedial classes will be selected upon the result of pre-tests to evaluate educational gaps. While those attending catch-up courses will be first reached through a BTL campaign. The rehabilitation works and the provision of equipment will mainly focus, but not exclusively, on the library and the science laboratory of the schools. The aim is to promote culture, through the organization of book clubs, and improve the spaces where children can discover themselves, experiment and collaborate with one another. In this sense, the beneficiaries will be participating in activities in the newly rehabilitated spaces, and the new books and tools will remain at the disposal of the school in the long term. The incentives provided to teachers and students will cover transportation for the most in need, school supplies, and consumables, in both formal and non-formal education settings. At the same time, children in-school will benefit from the overall services and material offered to the school. PSS activities will be performed at the individual and group level, so to enhance the focus on specific cases and sharing, particularly with children. Students, in and out of school, caregivers, and teachers will benefit from PSS assistance. The identification of PIN of protection is based on AVSI manuals and procedures, and conducted by social workers in tight relation with the community. In addition, following 2023 intervention in At All, AVSI will support 700 students enrolled in 9th and 12th grade in national exams, to cover food and transportation expenses. All the activities provided by teachers, will be performed after completion of specific training, enabling them to acquire new skills and share knowledge. Constant monitoring, and follow-up meetings will allow AVSI to verify the success of the activities and promptly intervene in case of needed adjustment. AVSI's international experts and Thematic Focal Points will provide technical support during the entire project cycle. *The budget presents the costs of a one-year intervention.
