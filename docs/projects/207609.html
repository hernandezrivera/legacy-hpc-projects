Project Summary

The proposed health programme is aligned with the JRP 2024 objectives and will provide health care services in 11 Rohingya camps through 5 Primary Healthcare Centers (PHC) and 2 Health Post (HP) in camp 1E, 1W, 8E 13, 9, and 16; community health in camp 1E (2), 1W, 7, 8E, 9, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 22 ensuring its footsteps in 11 camps. This project will be dedicated for the Rohingya population only.
These health facilities will provide primary healthcare services following the guidelines of Minimum Package of Essential Health Services (MPEHS) that encloses Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR), Maternal, Neonatal, and Child Health (MNCH); and Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health (ASRH) with a special focus on differently abled, women, girls and the elderly people. In addition, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) will be offered.
BRAC health facilities has been providing outpatient medical consultation for all age and genders, immunization for children and pregnant women, ANC, NVD, PNC, family planning services for eligible couples, adolescent counselling on reproductive health, and engaging male in SRHR related awareness sessions prevention and treatment of non-communicable diseases, physiotherapy, referral services, and routine laboratory investigations and medicine dispensation for all the age and gender groups. In addition, there is 24/7 ambulance services to facilitate the referral of the pregnant mother and other emergency cases to the secondary and tertiary health facilities.
Special focus is given to providing services and making health facilities inclusive for persons with disabilities considering all genders and providing assistive devices as per their needs.
The health programme also focuses on improving the health-seeking behaviour and health practices of the Rohingya refugees. The project train and engage community health workers to conduct awareness sessions with women, men, and adolescent girls and boys in Rohingya community, including basic antenatal, postnatal cares and family planning services. BRAC has engaged community health workers to conduct regular household visits and refer eligible children and women to health facilities for immunization, ANC and family planning services. They also refer pregnant women to avail facility-based delivery through reducing widely practiced home delivery.
The health programme of BRAC focuses to strengthen SRHR among the Rohingya refugees by promoting gender equality and gender mainstreaming activities. The awareness-raising efforts are targeting key actors to be sensitive on gender rights to influence the decision-making process of SRHR to strengthen such efforts. BRAC ensures an inclusive response to reduce the gap induced by stigma and oppression and provides survivor-centred health management training and support to its health workers and practitioners to ensure optimal clinical management of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) survivors, ensuring confidentiality and privacy for referral and screening.
As the facility for psychosocial support among the refugees is still low, BRAC will provide these services ensuring the inclusion of children, adolescents, and caregivers under the health sector. BRAC aims to strengthen MHPSS including individual level PSS services and increase access of the Rohingya community integrating with the health services. In addition, to health and WASH integration, BRAC will prioritize integrated health and nutrition services in 2024. The nutrition services will include malnutrition screening, growth monitoring, counselling, referral and necessary health care.
BRAC has an updated and simulated contingency plan to respond to any health-related emergencies to ensure immediate response with uninterrupted services and to increase further capacity during disaster and any disease outbreaks.
BRAC will ensure optimum infection prevention, medical waste management and control, embedded in the programme.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In Rohingya camps, women and girls comprise 52% of the Rohingya population, while children under 18 years are 52% of the total population. The women and girls of the Rohingya community are more vulnerable due to culture and gender norms which resulted a high rate of SGBV (mainly intimate partner violence), rape and child abuse cases which needs regular medical attention.The 2023 Joint-Multi Sector Needs Assessment highlighted that 10% households are too far away to access any healthcare facility and 5% refugee individuals with disabilities experienced challenges in accessing healthcare. Recent health service data (2023) from the Health sector, reveals that despite high SBA delivery rate (82%), MMR in the Rohingya community is still awfully high (300 per 100,000). In the same community, U5 death rate was 23 per 1,000 live births (2022). Hence, curbing maternal and U5 death with focus on neonatal death has become a priority of the health sector. A survey conducted by BRAC in November 2022 at Rohingya camps of Cox’s Bazar reflected that only 15.50% women were having an awareness on H&N and SRH while 31.73% of total men respondents were found aware of the same. From the survey, it was found that 33.75% of the women could take part in decision-making regarding health and nutrition, intimate relations, contraceptive use, and reproductive health care. Besides, 42.4% of the eligible couples claimed that they used contraceptive methods. However, the majority of the men respondents were found not using any contraceptive methods. ANC and PNC coverage are low as 24.15 % of pregnant women had received at least four (4) ANC, whereas 13.53% mothers had received four (4) PNC visit after the child delivery. though the health sector data (2023) shows that 82% of mothers had their delivery by SBA. To reduce maternal and perinatal mortality, social factors need to be addressed along with the medical causes. Community surveillance and response can contribute identifying social factors that may contribute to these deaths and help to take programmatic actions accordingly. Community Health Workers (CHW) along with other community level healthcare providers like midwife, TBAs can be involved in this mechanism. Over the last five years, the health needs of the Rohingya refugees have changed from emergency to regular and long-term needs. Multiple efforts have been made to alter health-seeking behaviour and utilisation of Primary Health Care facilities. Yet, women and adolescent girls have limited access to these services due to gender inequalities prevailing in harmful cultural practices. The other factor is their disenfranchisement and little participation in socio-economic activities, thus limiting women and other genders in exercising their sexual and reproductive health rights and the ability to make key decisions regarding important issues that directly affect their lives.Antenatal care services and delivering their babies at their shelters, increasing the risk of maternal mortality and morbidity. The major reasons behind the home deliveries include cultural norms, the influence of traditional birth attendants, lack of caregivers/attendants to take care of children when the mother is admitted to the clinic for delivery and poor access to transport in the camp, especially at night. The above gap shows that a lack of gender equality and poor male engagement are closely linked to poor access to SRHR services among the Rohingya community. There is a need to continue community mobilisation at the camp level to share health information, education and information sharing support health-related preventative measures, and also seeking feedback from the community. In the camps, preventive measures and response to any outbreak or health hazard need careful planning and coordination, which is vital for improving the health situation.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The health project has been working to ensure that all Rohingya refugees can have equitable access to all basic health services in an efficient and effective way, with maintaining transparency and accountability. Rationalization process ensures that common strategies, systems, protocols, and tools are used across all the camps leading towards a standardized and cost-effective approach to providing equitable services to refugees and a minimum standard of service delivery by different partners through jointly coordinated approaches between agencies and key stakeholders.Through ISCG, the health sector has already initiated the Rationalization process. To comply with the Rationalization process BRAC has been adopting an integration approach so that health services can be delivered through multisectoral camp-based activities. BRAC has already received the allocation of camp through rationalization process where BRAC health sector will be working in designated 11 camps. In this regard, BRAC health service will be integrated with Nutrition, MHPSS, WASH and Protection programme. Referring to the rationalization principle-3 holistic approach will be taken to ensure all healthcare related services to all age, gender and diversified people.BRAC has a solid fundraising team to work for funding opportunities, and as a result BRAC has confirmed funds from the non-UN donors which will be utilized to continue the services. The project considers all of its experiences (gathered through internal coordination with different sectors) in the humanitarian context as an asset to continue improved health services for the Rohingya community in a better and cost-effective way.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

BRAC health project ensures to provide the services to all age, gender and diversified community people to ensure collaborative participation in capacity building and maintain age and sex disaggregated data for reporting. BRAC has been working closely with the protection sector in a synergic way where the health sector is providing survivor centered services to GBV survivors to mitigate the risk of rape, grievous violence of affected women and girls during the gender based violence incidents. BRAC ensures all the health facilities have necessary alignments with the protection sector services according to the protection guidelines. BRAC health facilities are committed to offering comprehensive support for survivors of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), prioritizing their safety, dignity, and overall well-being. To achieve this,need based services and counseling are being provided to survivors through dedicated GBV corners established within the health facilities where ensured structured Psychosocial Support (PSS) through case management. At BRAC health facilities, survivors undergo a primary medical checkup to assess their immediate needs. Depending on the medical requirements identified, appropriate referrals are made to higher health facilities to ensure survivors receive the most relevant and effective care following the medical protocol. Additionally, if survivors require mediation support, BRAC GBV facilities make referrals to legal actors while prioritizing the safety and confidentiality of the survivors throughout the process. This integrated approach ensures that survivors not only receive essential medical care but also have access to legal support along with basic needs, all within a framework that safeguards their well-being and dignity.BRAC is concerned regarding the access of persons with disabilities at the health facilities and the facilities are designed in a disabled- friendly manner to ensure easy accessibility.

3.3 - Age & Disability

During the project designing phase, as well as the monitoring of the project implementation phase, BRAC ensures incorporations of gender, age, and disability elements. Furthermore, our health staff, including community health workers and volunteers, are trained and aware of the child safeguarding policy. If any such claim is found in our health programme, the PSEA committee will investigate, mitigate and take disciplinary action(s) according to the procedure. BRAC’s Health sector acknowledges diversity through disability inclusion while selecting volunteers and community workers. BRAC prioritises the individuals with disabilities along with the women and other genders and supports these groups to enhance their capacity and engagement with the communities. BRAC’s PHC provides physiotherapy to victims of abuse, accidents and life-changing illnesses. It also offers assistive devices to its patients along with the persons with disabilities.The physiotherapists assess the types of disability in camp areas using the Washington Group Questionnaire on Disability and afterwards provide appropriate support. In collaboration with the Protection sector, health staff are participating in the capacity building initiatives to develop their knowledge and skills on disability inclusion. Moreover, the health sector has GRT (Gender Response Team) and will be engaged in the initiative to include gender-diverse people in the Rohingya community. Disability inclusion approach will be reflected through all the activities and initiatives of BRAC in 2024. The activities and practices of the Health sector, ensure that the services provided to the disabled people are accessible to the Rohingya community, so that disabled people are treated as equals and get dignity, and that the diversities are welcomed.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) is an integral component of a rights-based approach to programming related gender response. It supports multiple rights, including the right to dignity, the right to protection, and the right to be heard and participate in decision-making. To improve the service quality of the health facilities, BRAC has already introduced Exit-Interview with the patients to know their satisfaction level and to seek their suggestions to improve the service quality. Every month, certain Exit-Interviews are taken among the randomly selected patients of the Rohingya community to know their satisfaction and dissatisfaction level; they are also asked to provide suggestions so that the service quality can be improved. Their suggestions are considered to make necessary modification of the health services.Using CRFM (Complaint Response Feedback Mechanism), BRAC will deal with recommendations, suggestions, complaints and feedback on the services offered by the health facilities. BRAC has installed a Feedback Box in each health facility to get service seekers’ complaints and subsequently take initiatives and link them with the relevant sectors so that they can access necessary support, as and when needed. Moreover, a 24-hour dedicated hotline number is kept operational in PHCs to ensure access of the participants to any emergency healthcare services.

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

BRAC is a member of the Child Safeguarding and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Network and has stringent safeguarding policies for adults and children. In addition, a dedicated PSEA committee is formed in BRAC, having a focal person to deal with all cases, including child safeguarding issues. All the staff including service providers and Community Health Workers, involved with the Rohingya community are oriented on PSEA to ensure a congenial atmosphere at the workplace avoiding all kinds of sexual exploitation. If any case of sexual exploitation is noticed or reported in our health programme, the PSEA committee will investigate, mitigate and take disciplinary action(s) according to the procedure. BRAC shows zero-tolerance against sexual exploitation and abuse. BRAC, within its policy emphasized on PSEA mainstreaming. As a result, inside and outside of the health facilities in the Rohingya camps, the health programme is committed to ensure PSEA mainstreaming.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC HCMP has an EOC (Emergency Operation Centre) and ERT (Emergency Response Team) structure with representatives from all sectors to respond in an integrated way in an emergency. During environmental emergencies and man-made or natural hazards, the Health and DRR sectors jointly work in the affected areas by conducting primary assessments and forming temporary primary health posts; providing emergency medical support; and through referral support, both sectors ensure their other basic supports.As a regular activity, health message dissemination through volunteers on natural disasters, personal hygiene, fire extinguisher, medicine, and advocating for handwashing or use of hand sanitizer are conducted in the camp areas during a disaster.DRR staff provides basic Disaster Risk Management and Emergency Response training to our health staff, including community volunteers, to respond effectively to an emergency crisis. The DRR sector will also provide first aid training to the health staff and volunteers as a part of their skill development.
