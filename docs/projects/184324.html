1. - Projects activities

Project ActivitiesNeeds: Since 1991, the security situation in Somalia has remained volatile through armed conflicts occasioned by clan hostilities and lack of functional government. The emergence of terror groups has worsened the already fragile operating environment of the NGO community. Somalia remains among the most dangerous countries in the world for aid workers. For the last three decades, the country has consistently remained among the top five in absolute number of critical incidents (killings, kidnappings and severe injuries) and, in aggregate in the last two decades, it is the number one in ratio of incidents per number of aid workers deployed. In order to improve the safety of their staff, NGOs need to enhance their threats awareness, vulnerabilities awareness, context information and understanding, risk mitigation measures, impact mitigation measures, capacity for crisis management, individual safety and organizational safety. The safety of the NGO staff is fundamental to ensure that they efficiently and effectively deliver on their mandate and remains accountable to the vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in Somalia. Timely information sharing on security incidents, threats and risks increases awareness on safety actions that can be taken by the staff of the NGOs operating in areas affected by hostilities. Activities: 1. Information sharing and analysis INSO provides a number of services to inform on and analyze the changing context and the threats involved through; a. Reports (Flash reports, weekly incident list, biweeklies, quarterlies, advisories, special reports, area briefs): These unique reports compile the key security incidents of a given period for the whole country, analyze the data and provide critical advice to NGOs. b. Maps (areas of influence, access, geographic, town maps): They are highly valued by NGOs, the areas of influence maps help understand which armed actors are present in a given area. Similarly, INSO access maps are a reference for the whole humanitarian community to synthesize the difficulties in accessing an area, staying there and delivering. c. Briefings (biweekly and ad hoc): The biweekly security briefings to NGOs provide a forum to openly discuss about the key events and security threats in all the regions. However, the ad hoc briefings, upon request of NGOs, help in the planning of operations, understand the threats specific to the location and pro􀀃le of the agency, and tailor their security management to their needs. 2. Training: a. The Hostile Environment Individual Safety Training (HEIST) provides the trainees aid workers to be deployed in Somalia the tools to deal with specific situations that they may confront in the 􀀃eld. b. The Country Directors Training improves the security management of the country Director's organization, tailored to the Somali context. 3. Support to Crisis Management In case of critical incident (e.g. abduction, medical evaluation) INSO supports the NGOs on the management of the crisis, especially on the first 72 hours by setting up the crisis team, acting as expert adviser, helping in data gathering and connecting with critical stakeholders.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

INSO is not working directly with Beneﬁciaries in the ﬁeld but is providing services to NGOs as such this section is not relevant to our activities' INSO have put in place measures to guaranty the confidentiality of information received. Sources of information are never disclosed to donors or other NGOs. Every INSO 􀀃eld staff have his own sources of information that he is not obliged to disclose to INSO management. INSO ensure that information is impartial and neutral and engage with a constant dialogue with authorities and various stakeholder to make sure that INSO mandate is understood, and no external pressure is exerted against INSO staff. INSO having staff based in all states it mitigates the risk of interruption of activities in case of political pressure exerted at a particular location, as INSO can continue to operate from other sub offices INSO has no control on the beneficiary’s inclusion, as NGOs are managing directly the list of their staff that are receiving INSO product through the Partner portal. Inclusion of person with disability depends entirely on NGOs who are bene􀀃ting of INSO services. Having said that, for meeting and training INSO does not discriminate and people with disability are welcome to attend and the trainer will make sure that special needs are taken care off. INSO also record in a confidential manner in its data base the incidents of rape or SGBV, these incidents are recorded without names, only for statistical purpose but disaggregated by region. Statistical data (not individual data) can be use for example by OCHA for advocacy.
