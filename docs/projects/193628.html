Project Summary

Our proposed plan is to provide education for the most vulnerable school dropouts. our target is to improve and provide support for the quality of education and provide a safe environment for them. We want to contribute to the improvement of the mental well-being and resilience of children in Gaza strip. project target is to support 950 vulnerable children through providing remedial education to school going vulnerable along with provide education supplies for the target children. In addition, provide 100 school’s staff capacity building include psycho social first aid (PFA) and awareness training about psycho social. The selection criteria for students to be enrolled in the project will ensure that vulnerable students are prioritized, this will include gender balanced selection (500 girls/450 boys), inclusion of students with disabilities. In addition, it will provide logistic support for disabled targets who will have full access to ensure equal opportunity of education despite special needs.

1 - Needs

Due to the situation in Gaza where an ongoing blockade, political divides, wars (last one 2022), socio- economic crisis gave rise to increase in the number of vulnerable school children whereby some are injured physically, some are having mental illnesses, financial problems and finally they leave schools at an early age, especially in the area of North Gaza according to 2022 Multi Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), 5.7% of school-aged children reportedly dropped out of school during the current school year (2021-2022), with 5.3% of all school-aged boys and 2.7% of all school-aged girls reportedly dropping out, with needing to catch-up for learning after closure due to COVID 19 and other reasons. (281 children) that dropped out of school during the current school year (2021 - 2022) by most commonly reported reasons for dropping out of school: Girls: Cannot afford costs 56.5%, Lack of interest 19.1%, Disability-specific needs not met 17.9% Child labour 13.7% Boys: Child labour 67.7% Cannot afford costs 66.2% Lack of interest 49.6%, Disability-specific needs not met 21.9%. more than 70% children have sleeping problems and about all the children have depression, hypertension and feel aloof. the Israeli attacks along with siege has affected their daily life as well as attitude towards each other. power cuts also have affected their way of learning and performing whereby they lack concentration and skill achievements. children feel disappointment, annoyed and hostile due to power and water supply cuts. family heads have expressed concern about their children due to all these factors which piles up their daily problems. According to 2022 Multi Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) access to education has been affected whereby we see increase in dropouts. Services of UNRWA also decreased in a very large manner which has affected the children as well as their caretakers who are employed by the agency. addition of shifts has also added to their woes. children are being put under an unending pressure of being resilient thus risking their innocence and future. 2022 Multi Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA): 7.1% of households with school-aged children not attending school for many reasons including: Cannot afford to pay school related expenses 35.6%., Lack of interest (of children) in education 28.0% Illness 16.5% Children working to support household 9.4%, School cannot accommodate child with disability 7.6%. while e 9.1% of households not planning to enroll school-aged children, % of households by most frequently reported reasons for non-enrolment*: Cannot afford school related expenses 15.2% Child not interested in school 14.4% School cannot accommodate child with disability 9.5% Child needs to support family at home 8.0%. According to MSNA 10% of teachers only trained on PSS, which is a very small percent regarding to children who need PSS. So we have a very high number of school dropouts due to reasons above and serious financial reasons and to get them back into the school we need to remove barriers by promoting collective efforts for their better future. We will collect data from MoEHE and conduct verification through field surveys for proper results and delivery of services (to avoid duplication/conflict of sorts). We will coordinate with concerned clusters as well as CBOs to work together. this will strengthen the network locally and provide better quality of work on field.

2 - Activities

Main activities: Selection the target beneficiaries according to MSNA 2022 and with coordination with MoEHE -provide logistic support (educational supplies) to vulnerable school children with special needs (350 girls and 250 boys) - Train 100 school’s staff (40% Males & 60% Females) for the importance of PSS to increase their awareness about it, - Train 100 school’s staff (45 Males & 55 Females)) for the Psychological social first aid. -provide remedial courses to vulnerable children school students (400 girls and 200 boys) in the age group of 13 to 16 yrs whose education outcomes have been affected by COVID 19, poor electricity and financial problems & the May 2021 and 2022 escalation. HCCD will work in coordination with Education cluster, its members, Ministry of Higher Education to reach and effectiveness as well as local CBOS.

Every component of the proposed project is to solve a problem which leads to decrease the dropout children’s school.
