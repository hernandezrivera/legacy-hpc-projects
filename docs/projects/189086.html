Project Summary

A total of 133,200 people (about 85% of the population) in Mayom county are in need of humanitarian assistance following a triple shock of floods, intercommunal conflicts and the COVID-19 pandemic that displaced people, destroyed their coping mechanisms and killed their resilience to survive. Infact 15,000 are classified as catastrophic while 23,000 others are in severe need (HNO 2020).
Access to health services is poor especially for safe motherhood services. The existing health facilities in Mayom county have limited capacity to provide comprehensive safe motherhood services, for example there are no comprehensive emergency care services in Mayom County. Referral pathways to Bentiu
hospital are blocked due to floods and on a few occasions have had to evacuate mothers by air to Juba for an emergency caesarean section.
The main goal of the proposed project is to increase access to maternal and child health services by vulnerable women and children in Mayom County. The capacity of Mayom PHCC will be built to provide
Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonetal Care (CEmONC) services as part of an integrated package of quality safe motherhood and child health services to 2,695 mothers and 2,858 children under 5 in one year. Inter-sectoral coordination and partnerships will be strengthened within the counties for integrated safe motherhood services. Community participation will also be mobilised for social change, communication and social accountability and create awareness on GBV and girl child education.
The approach:
The intervention focuses on a systems approach to strengthen capacity of Mayom PHCC to provide CEmONC as part of integrated safe motherhood and child health services through recruitment and retention of staff, including medical doctors in Mayom PHCC, installation of necessary equipment and rehabilitation of operation theatre space. CORDAID already supports primary health care services in Mayom county through a network of eight health services under HPF project, and referral pathways will be strengthened from the PHCUs to Mayom PHCC. Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases will be carried among children under one year in the three counties, and there will be strengthened disease surveillance and outbreak detection and response capacity. Scale up of safe motherhood trainings for health workers, Boma health workers and local community group leaders will be enhanced. A community survey using quantitative and qualitative methods will be carried out to assess the status of safe motherhood and child health services before implementation of the intervention and at end term to assess progress.
Based on the evidence from the survey and subsequent routine data as well as feedback from beneficiaries, decision makers at the CHD, SMoH and at the health cluster levels will be engaged to address inequities and inequalities in safe motherhood and child health service access. Safe motherhood and child health information management will be enhanced to facilitate effective inter-sectoral approach with stakeholders. The data collected will be disaggregated by age, gender and disability status.
Community led platforms will be built to mobilise community participation through monthly community meetings that will identify safe motherhood and child health priorities and identify local remedies. These meetings will generate action plans that will be discussed in quarterly interface meetings with the health workers, CHD, SMoH and community representatives to discuss needs, priorities and strategies. These dialogues will catalyse social change, and empower communities to demand for social accountability. The Boma Health Initiative structures will be used to strengthen community- health facility linkages for safe motherhood and child health services.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian caseload in South Sudan remains high due to multiple drivers. About two thirds of the country’s population is in need of humanitarian assistance (51% females, 49% males) and this high caseload is likely to persist due to the persistent inter-communal violence, access constraints and operational challenges. The COVID 19 pandemic and flooding have added to public health challenges that have hindered women's and children's access to healthcare services with negative impacts on their livelihoods.In Mayom County, there are 133,200 people in humanitarian need and 23,000 of these are in extreme need and 94,000 are in severe need (HNO 2022). There is a continued interplay of inequality in women's health and rights, gender inequality and economic inequality that has rendered women incapable of participating meaningfully and contributing to household livelihoods. The result is a poor access to lifesaving maternal and child health services that even got compounded by the persistent floods that has locked communities away from health facilities, and away from Bentiu state hospital, the only referral facility that can provide CEmONC services in the whole region. Cordaid currently supports eight health facilities through HPF funding including two PHCCs and six PHCUs but none of these facilities is equipped and staffed well enough to provide CEmONC services to the needy populations in Mayom County. In some occasions, humanitarian partners have had to airlift pregnant mothers with obstructed labour to Juba. The quality of services in the facilities is not yet responsive to the needs of the vulnerable communities and does not effectively detect and respond to disease outbreaks among children, and lacks inter-sectoral coordination, long-term planning, information sharing and workforce capacity. A national cross -sectional survey among 156 randomly selected health facilities in 10 States using a two-stage lot quality assurance sampling design showed that only 12% facilities were categorized as acceptable for adequate utilization by the population for sick -child consultations, and only 3% for having infection control supplies available (Berendes et al, 2019). As a result of these access barriers to adequate SRHR services, South Sudan continues to present very poor maternal and child health indicators: antenatal care attendance is very low at only 39% for fourth visits and only 14.7% of deliveries are managed by skilled birth attendants.( OCHA 2020) There is a high teenage pregnancy rates at 30% among girls aged 15-19 years, modern contraceptive prevalence rate is only 5% while the unmet need was at 29.7% in 2020.(Wall et al, 2020) Gender inequality and gender-based violence are widespread and perpetuated by several factors including socio-cultural norms and armed groups presence in communities.(Murphy M, 2020).This project will support the state Ministry of Health (SMOH) to provide life-saving emergency maternal and child health services to vulnerable mothers in Mayom county. It will provide accessible, equitable, integrated, quality maternal and child health services for all, near where the communities live with efficient referral linkages within the county. The specific objectives for the project are to:1. Strengthen the health system within Mayom county to improve access and utilization of lifesaving maternal anc child health services through a network of eight supported health facilities in Mayom County2. Strengthen multi-sectoral coordination and partnerships for lifesaving Maternal and child health services in Mayom County.3. Enhance community engagement frameworks for social change, social Accountability, Behavior change and communication through empowered women and youth groups, traditional community leaders and the BHI structures.

2 - Project activities

Output 1. Strengthened health system to improve access to and utilization of lifesaving maternal and child health services.Activities- Strengthen the capacity of Mayom PHCC to provide CEmONC services and act as a second level referral centre to the seven other primary health care centres in Mayom county- Recruit and retain skilled staff for CEmONC services in Mayom health facility and provide mentorship to those in seven other health facilities to provide maternal and child health services.- Through the Health cluster pipeline, mobilise medical supplies and consumables for maternal and child health services in eight health facilities in three counties- Maintain cold chains in selected health facilities to improve coverage for vaccinations- Strengthen community health systems through BHI structures to provide Maternal and child health services and community -health facility linkages - Create awareness about GBV in the three counties - Increase collaboration with the County Health Department (CHD) and the state MoH to strengthen quality maternal and child health data to provide reliable data feed into DHIS 2. Output 2: Strengthened multi-sectoral coordination and partnerships for lifesaving maternal and child health services in Mayom County. This will enhance SRHR information management with partners and CHD so as to effectively communicate a multi-sectoral approach for maternal and child health. The decision makers in different government sectors at county and state levels as well as partners at the health cluster will be engaged to address inequities and inequalities in maternal and child health access using the generated data. The activities for this output will include:- Quarterly participatory policy and budget analysis- Participatory public humanitarian assistance tracking/ input tracking within the county for maternal and child health especially for the eight supported health facilities- Participatory maternal and child healthcare service performance monitoring, evaluation, and quality improvement Output 3. Enhanced community engagement frameworks for social change, social accountability, behavior change and communication for maternal and child health services in Guit, Mayon and Rubkona counties. The efforts of the needy communities will be drawn towards collective engagement with health workers, the county health department authorities and partners in the provision of public services. Youth and women group leadership will be empowered to organise community forums that will scrutinize their maternal and child health priorities, their capabilities, access to services and the quality of services offered. It will also provide opportunity to dialogue on the existing harmful traditional practices within the communities such as child marriages that affect maternal and child health. The activities will include:- Strengthen sexuality and gender education including menstrual hygiene by trained women and youth leaders and BHWs to promote retention of young girls in School- Organize monthly communities meetings through the women and youth leaders to set community priorities- Organise quarterly dialogue meetings between health workers, community leaders with representation from different population groups (gender/age groups and disability status) and the CHD authorities to discuss community MCH priorities, access challenges, quality of services, harmful traditional practices and policy views.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will contribute towards implementation of the gaps identified and prioritized in the community action plan. The focus will be to improve the quality of life saving RMCAH services and improving client satisfaction. This is likely to result in improved quality of care in the healthy facility through improving the WASH infrastructure in the facilities, targeted on job training and mentorship of healthcare workers focusing on improving their responsiveness, replacement of missing essential equipment, improving supply chain of essential medicines and supplies, supporting the health information systems at the primary healthcare levels, and improving the management, leadership and oversight of the health facilities by encouraging the community participation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

It is acknowledged in this proposed project that a diversity of needy people including those living with disability (physical, psychosocial, intellectual, or sensory) come with functional limitations and other vulnerabilities. The effects of the interaction between these individual impairments and the social and material environment are usually severe for women and children. There are additional barriers that they may face due to inaccessible and disempowering environments and the discriminatory attitudes of society and individuals.This project will purpose to apply a gender lens to all aspects to enrich the impact, using an intersectional approach at the state and county levels to look at gender, disability, and sexuality in the RMCAH data. This will clarify on the different forms of inequality and strategies to address discrimination and promote inclusion of all persons with disability. People living with disability will be called to monthly community meetings where such data is been discussed while their representatives will also be invited to the interface meetings with the health workers and the government officials.There will also be effort to remove barriers to reporting crimes and/or cases of discrimination within the health facilities. This project will attempt to create awareness on the pathways for accessing justice in case of any discrimination within the health facilities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will use CORDAID PSEA policy to manage and protect both staff and beneficiaries against acts of sexual exploitation and abuse. Staff will be trained and retrained on the policy. A safe and accessible community-based reporting mechanisms will be established, and the investigation channels will be discussed with the community including how to obtain assistance and the possible disciplinary measures will also be explained. The project will adapt PSEA Network information campaigns with linked information, education and communication (IEC) materials. The information campaigns will target both county health departments, partners and project staff on one hand and the community members on the other hand. All IEC materials, as a part of the campaign, will be translated into the local language and be distributed widely.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

A community survey will be conducted to establish the current state of access to MCH services and the quality of care in the nearest public health facility for that community will be assessed. The results of these survey will be compared with the standards set in the government policy documents.Local government leaders will be organized together with community leaders and leaders of community groups such as women and youth groups who will meet monthly to discuss progressive against the baseline survey results. This community level meetings will create awareness on MCH related care, risk factors, ANC, postnatal care, institutional deliveries, and access to child health care services including immunizations. Using various participatory methods, the community will identify the priority health concerns, develops an action plan suggesting improvements that require no or only modest additional resources. This action plan will be shared with the health facility staff and the state ministry of health officials in an interface meetings.The interface meeting involving representatives from the community and the health facility staff attending together with the state ministry of health officials particularly the director general, director for primary health care, director for reproductive and the director for monitoring and planning. In this meeting, the participants will discuss and agree on the community action plan and come up with an action plan that outlines the top priority concerns for that month, what needs to be done, and how, when and by whom. The implementation of this community contract will be tracked in the subsequent monthly meetings, to discuss how to sustain or improve progress or why no progress was made.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The practice of early marriages and the social support it receives among certain communities poses a risk to the project. The drivers of the vulnerability in these communities pushes some families to arrange early marriages for their girls as a survival strategy by families suffering from recurrent conflicts, flooding and growing economic challenges.(5) It has also been noted certain societies see early marriage as in the best interest of the girls and their families. It is perceived to be protective against promiscuity and preserves family honor. There is also a high risk of persistent lack of awareness on the availability of GBV services and how to access them.The women and youth groups whose leaders will be in the monthly community meetings together with the Boma Health Workers will provide a service in wide-ranging needs of women and girls including HIV/STD, community organization, dissemination of information, and behavior change interventions. They will also take part in peace building and promotion of women’s socioeconomic participation through small business enterprise trainings.
