Project Summary


This project targets IDPs, community groups and enterprises founded in Idleb and Aleppo governorate as Al-Bab, Azaz, Afrin sub-districts in Aleppo and Idleb, MaraatTamasrin, and Dana sub-districts in Idleb . The project’s main objective is to strengthening the resilience of 8000 people (M:4200, W: 3800) including 200 PWDs against exacerbating living conditions related to both conflicts and floods in where economic deterioration and its effects such as income loss, reduced purchasing power are severely experienced. Through the provision of livelihood opportunities consisting of integrated interventions, the project aims to support ERL efforts and community revitalization; It will contribute to rehabilitation and restoration of disrupted livelihoods, providing skills, income opportunities through three main interventions.
By pursuing the main objective referred above, the project aims to achieve the following outcomes:
1. Local small and home-based enterprises and related activities are built up with financial, in-kind supports and restorations targeting 2000.
2. Access to given term livelihoods opportunities involving CFW for 1500 workers both in restoration works (including 10 parks & 150 classrooms in schools) and lighting for roads.
3. Support livelihood& empowerment embedded Local small businesses and entrepreneurs targeting 550 persons.
4. Community 30 initiatives with focusing on elderly.
5. Solid management including distribution of bins, and plastic grinder (500 in total) and solid waste removable.
MWL engage with target communities throughout the project design, implementation, and monitoring, to ensure that all interventions are meaningful, relevant, and community-driven. Beneficiaries (both IDPs and host communities) are engaged at each step in the intervention and information is shared freely and openly through direct consultations with a variety of actors, including men, women, youth, local producers and traders through both qualitative interviews.
Both IDPs and host communities members in particular HHs headed by a child, a female member and/or PWD, will be prioritized as beneficiaries for CFW. Enterprises of which facilities-activities are affected by floods in medium and high level will be prioritized for financial and in-kind supports. Both to ensure all potential beneficiaries have equal and non-discrimination access to the interventions of the project. MWL will follow Do No Harm approach through coordination meetings with related stakeholders such local councils, Local market owners, enterprises before submitting the proposal, identifying the locations with access issues that are being targeted under this proposal. In addition to, cash working group to follows the Cash for Work (CfW) Minimum Standard guidance (final draft version) to ensure the harmonize and do-no harm within implementing the CFW activity.
Furthermore, a regular channel of communication with ERL, SCHF will be established for information sharing on developments and possible emerging issues to market stability or market distortion / markets volatility. Also, an articulated coordination with Shelter, and WASH clusters will ensure the promotion of access to livelihoods activities that would contribute to the supply of health, shelter and WASH related material/services including through start-up/support to businesses and related employment opportunities.

3 - Needs

Based on HNO 2023, One of the top Unmet Needs is 45% for livelihood. Life is increasingly unaffordable, with the erosion of savings, and price increases for basic commodities that have almost doubled, driving needs severity and new displacement. The crippled economy, which is characterized by high inflation, currency depreciation and increases in the prices of commodities, remains one of the biggest drivers of need. It drives more people towards poverty, makes them more reliant on humanitarian assistance, increases resort to harmful coping mechanisms and increases the cost of response. It is creating a situation in which some population movements in Syria are triggered by the search for better access to basic services and livelihood opportunities. The economic decline creates additional obstacles for people, especially the most vulnerable and marginalised, to access services. Basic services and other critical infrastructure are on the brink of collapse. Widespread and frequent power outages mean more than half of Syrians need regularly to rely on candles to light their homes. For IDPs in camps, the main factors determining vulnerability and severity of need are their displacement status, limited livelihood opportunities in camps, and the poor shelter and overcrowded conditions they live in that trigger protection concerns, especially for women and girls.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will address the needs through an interconnected and integrated set of activities, as below:- Singing MOUs with the local councils and camp managers.-Conduct comprehensive labor market assessment to identify the needs, demands, and gaps of careers, and vocations to avoid the increased demand and potential impact on market- Announcement about the project.- Prepare the centers to be equipped for the training.- Contract with trainers to provide entrepreneurship trainings and follow-up with in-kind or financial support to the enterprises- Provide CFW with the values adopted by the cluster as skilled and non-skilled workers who worked in the rehabilitation of essential socio-economic infrastructures and services. This activity will target both IDPs and host communities to ensure promoting the social cohesion.- Conduct waste management activities including distribution of bins.- Rehabilitate community and public infrastructures, destroyed markets, mills, slaughterhouses as a means to help recover the local economy/businesses which also paves way for sustainable supply of livelihood opportunities for local communities. - lighting roads in camps.- Provide for technical/entrepreneurial capacity-building and small grants to businesses, both startups and existing ones, to help them initiate and restore their livelihoods. - Conduct related community-based activities and initiatives targeting elderly in camps and communities.
