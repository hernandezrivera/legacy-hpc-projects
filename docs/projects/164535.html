Project Summary

The proposed action seeks to protect and enhance food security and livelihoods of and protect conflict-affected highly vulnerable IDPs and host communities in North West Syria (NWS). This will be achieved through an integrated programme of assistance including repeat mixed-modality life-sustaining food assistance (monthly assistance to the most vulnerable households) and emergency life-saving food security assistance in addition to livelihoods support. The complementary package of livelihoods support will increase household resilience through the promotion of entrepreneurship and the development of small businesses and to improve agricultural production and increase the ability of targeted households to meet food needs.
The proposed action seeks to target an estimated total of 108,510 beneficiaries, of which 58,192 are female and 50,318 are male. Further breakdown of beneficiaries as follows:
-Females (0-4) = 9,311
-Males (0-4) = 8,051

-Females (18-59) = 28,222

-Females (>59) = 1,978
-Males (>59) = 1,711
With reference to livelihoods, funding is sought to support the Olive Value Chain (OVC) to improve the livelihoods of 1,500 households / 9,000 conflict affected people in Idleb and northern Aleppo. A comprehensive program of assistance is planned to benefit 750 small-scale olive farmers and create income generation opportunities for an additional 750 acutely vulnerable IDP and host families through Cash for Work (CfW). The project will support self-reliance, protect and build productive assets and increase access to inclusive livelihoods. Proposed activities are complemented by our existing Food Security programming in Idleb and northern Aleppo which provides repeat, in-kind food assistance to the most vulnerable households, and by our ongoing Emergency Response (ER) interventions which will continue to provide timely, life-saving assistance to new arrivals. Funding is also sought to promote entrepreneurship and Small Business Development in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. The NGO proposes to support entrepreneurship and the growth of small businesses through working with stakeholders in the business community and the relevant local authorities / LCs to promote an environment conducive to and supportive of economic development and entrepreneurship. The project will promote entrepreneurship and the development of 500 small businesses through provision of micro-grants, peer-to-peer networks and entrepreneur support packages that facilitate business start-ups and investment.

4 - Needs

The protracted & increasingly complex Syrian conflict continues to result in high levels of humanitarian need while demanding increased focus on actions to support greater self-reliance and resilience of affected people.The 2019 HNO reports that an est. 65 % of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies with drivers of food insecurity reported as prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods (LLHs) and reduced production capacity. These factors limit financial access to food, raise food prices and cause inflation which in turn contributes to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of LLHs assets of the most vulnerable. The Idleb Crisis has compounded an already desperate situation with humanitarian need reaching unprecedented levels. HNAP Mobility and Needs Monitoring overview for Dec2019 reports 196,067 newly displaced IDPs in Dec2019. Acute need exists to increase access to food through repeat FS assistance to the most vulnerable while also creating income generation opportunities to increase economic access to food and other needs. The 2019 HNO ests. that 600,000 farming HHs are suffering from the lack of agric inputs to support their own production. Olive production in NWS has historically been one of the main agric and economic activities in the region, with the govs. of Idleb and Aleppo representing the largest producers of olive in the country. The conflict resulted in the sudden halt of gov support for olive production in opposition-held areas from 2011, impacting negatively on production and on the communities who have traditionally depended on the olive value chain for their LLHs. As a direct result farmers have resorted to highly harmful and irreversible coping strategies, including cutting productive trees for firewood in winter months. The result is lessened ability of smaller, more vulnerable olive farming HHs to meet basic needs & damage / destruction on productive assets.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggreg of info, participation, access & an understanding of how different needs will be met.To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable pops in the face of conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries (BNFs) will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cycle wherever possible, including, needs & resource identification, design, implementation & M&E.This project will work with female & male community leaders & members to ensure that the FS distributions are equitable & gender sensitive, eg having separate lines for male & female BNFs at distribution points & ensuring female staff are present whenever possible. Our PDM also shows that female-headed HHs are more at risk of engaging in harmful coping strategies to meet basic needs. In selecting the OVC for agric LLHs, this project seeks to increase inclusive access to income opps through CfW activities in which women traditionally engage.We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity & non-discrimination so that all potential BNFs will benefit from inclusive services to the greatest extent possible.We will engage with LCs, RCs & other actors to mitigate risks faced by women & children, in particular & to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights. To mitigate protection risks, we work closely with LCs & other admin bodies to ensure open & accountable feedback mechanisms for BNFs & non-BNFs including face-to-face interviews & a direct Whatsapp #. We require all staff to sign the org’s code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report abuse by staff or BNFs, including misuse of assets or funds.We have strict protocols against any sexual relations with minors or BNFs of any age. Additionally, we will ensure that assessments and BNF selection will assess women’s & girls’ unique needs in addition to other vulnerable groups.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Consideration of the highly volatile situation within Syria has been built into program design. Real-time analysis of the security situation will continue to allow for rapidadjustments to planned activities as necessary. This can include temporary suspension of field activities when our field teams employ various techniques to maintain communication and support to project stakeholders and beneficiaries. Distributions will be placed on temporary hold during periods of heightened risk to staff and beneficiaries. Door-to-door distributions, or phased distribution times to reduce the risks of large numbers of people gathering will also be used. Framework Agreements are in place with food kit and RTE suppliers who have proven reliable and able to consistently deliver, mitigating against the risk of supply chain breaks. Should breaks be experienced due to border closures or other, unanticipated events, we have the ability to immediately transition from in-kind assistance to cash or conditional food vouchers to ensure targeted beneficiaries can maintain access to food. We are continually updating its contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in NWS, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Risks beyond our control could include: shifts in administrative powers; heightened insecurity restricting access; targeting of aid organisations or staff. Funding is secured for FS operations in Afrin district, Aleppo. This will allow us to respond to need in these areas should significant incoming displacement be experienced as a result of further, possible conflict in Idleb and western Aleppo. Securing this access also allows us to re-programme planned food assistance to areas in northern Aleppo should security prevent ongoing operations in Idleb or western Aleppo.
