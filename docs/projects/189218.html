Project Summary

The proposed project is designed to respond to the most essential need for child protection responses,children with and without disabilities. Based on GCAF needs assessment conducted in January 2022 in Tambura,Ezo and Nagero, it becomes clear that children with and without disabilities are of need of child protection activities.They need to enjoy a safe environment within their homes, schools and the whole surroundings. The project tackles the problems faced by the children that threatens their protection and mental-wellbeing. Children protection and psychosocial support is of significance to mitigate the effects of child violations in family surrounding and in schools.
This project aims to provide timely and appropriate integrated response in the field of child protection through responses in areas of needs for IDPs,returness and host communities to increase resilience and coping mechanisms including risk reduction.GCAF will ensure that vulnerable children in Tambura,Nagero and Ezo are protected in their communities and receive support when they experience harm. GCAF will build on previous and ongoing child protection programming in the locations. The intervention will target both IDPs and returnees through both joint, integrated as well as targeted specialised services to prevent and respond to violence against displaced communities to achieve the following outcomes: Outcome 1: Women, girls, boys and men are free from violence in their communities (prevention); Outcome 2: Women, girls, boys and men are protected from the consequences of violence (response); and Outcome 3: Women, girls, boys and men access rapid and equitable emergency relief services (emergency response).Children who were exposed to severe harmful act will receive legal counselling and representation. Children will learn to have a voice and express and report child protection issue that they encounter. Parents
will be targeted to help them cope with life burdens and turn improve their parenting skills which will create a safe environment for children at their homes. Service providers, teachers, and community members will be sensitized on child protection issues such as
child labor, schools’ dropout, and abuse so that they integrate the child protection mechanisms to mitigate the risks that children face in the community.
The project will be coordinated with other partners on the ground like DRC world vision and CIDO
Due to current COVID19 situation, the protection and psychosocial support will be implemented in remote and blended modalities that involves including technological
solutions to make sure that most children have access to protection intervention.Families will be reached through the field team taking all needed precautionary measures. The 2020- 2021 Covid-19 aggression has caused children with and without disabilities to suffer from severe post-traumatic disorders which makes it important to have structured psycho-socials support program for children with and without disabilities
Targeting criteria will include: newly displaced individuals/households; children affected by armed conflict or associated with armed groups; unaccompanied and separated children; children at risk of or who have experienced abuse, neglect and exploitation; children at risk of early or forced marriage; female- and child-headed households; households with elderly individuals or individuals with disability, chronic illness or reduced coping mechanisms and children with disabilities.

1 - Needs

Exposure to violence in the short and long term negatively impacts on the mental, emotional, behavioral, social and physical well-being of children and unless they receive counselling these effects can become worse over time.Child protection needs in South Sudan have been compounded by the continuing COVID-19 pandemic, as well as an increase in conflict-related violence .Children and adolescents in South Sudan continue to be exposed to a wide range of protection risks including, risks of family separation, recruitment,and use of children by armed forces and groups, psychosocial distress, sexual violence, neglect, abuse and exploitation. The COVID-19 outbreak protracted situation, community displacements, flooding and ongoing conflict have exacerbated this situation; hindering adequate, safe access and full utilization of lifesaving humanitarian services in South Sudan. The Child Protection Area of Responsibilityy (CPAOR) Plan to reach 930 K children,adolescents, and caregivers across 65 counties with highest needs and 9 counties with high severity particularly Kapoeta South, Bor South,Fangak, Pibor, Rubkona, Akobo, Tambura, Ezo and Twic Eas with life- saving Child Protection (CP) services.GCAF will scale up critical CP intervention to address and respond to the increasing protection risks facing children and adolescents in these three locations to meet the need of 4800 vulnerable people. There will be an expansion of the comprehensive case management services for children and adolescents identified with critical protection risks including family's tracing and reunification services (FTR),reintegration for Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG), and support to childrensurvivors of GBV.GCAF will form part of both state and nationalprotection coordination groups through technical working groups and the utilization of the CPIMS+ will contribute and improve the quality of case management services. Most of the resources are concentrated around Juba,leaving local governments with very limited capacity options (in terms of human and financial resources), leaving them virtually unable to ensure safety, security, access to health, education, and other services, exposing vulnerable populations to increased protection risks. Constant exposure of girls and boys, including those with disabilities,to violence, exploitation, neglect, and abuse such as family separation, mental health and psychosocial distress, violence, maiming, or death takes a toll on children’s wellbeing and disrupts the environments in which children develop.We can observe a significant rise of reported cases of child abuse comparing the data from 2021 and 2022.Children are still forcibly recruited into armed forces/groups.The ongoing fighting and the resulting displacement continue to separate families ,attacks,destruction and occupation of health structures and schools continue unabated,while lootings and destruction of aid facilities are wide spread.The recent GCAF protection assessment done in Tambura in January 2022 indicate increased cases of rape or a ttempted rape of women and a dolescent girls.In the Payam there are no child protection actors to provide protection and reponse activities putting more risks to the victims.Communties are not valuing the dignified comfort of women and girls but rather view them as source of income which increases the levels of abuses happening to IDPs and returnees.GCAF will collect information on protection risks and threats and the protection officers will carry out indepth protection analysis while supporting the static teams across the counties.Hence the specific locations with detailed descriptions of needs will be identified at alater stage and these analyses will shape the responses accordingly in all interventions-including other sectors

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Our staffs will recognise the need for formal feedback mechanisms to help them manage the scale ofhumanitarian response. They will provide suggestion boxes, a dedicated phonenumber and an email address to receive feedback, and a ‘help desk’ will bestaffed during distributions of food and other items so that people could givefeedback and get answers to questions about the project. Recipients providedfeedback and if they left their contact information, they received a responsewithin two weeks. When staff received repeated or surprising feedbackrelated to problems with the project, they investigated whether programmingchanges were necessary and, if so, what they should be.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Participation of all age and gender groups will be emphasized so that all beneficiaries can voice their concerns and express their preferences during implementation. Women and girls will be consulted to ensure they feel safe when using the established GBV services at their preferred places. The project will consider several approaches to address the issue of women's involvement by mobilizing elderly women, which is more accepted, identifying trained women from the community already engaged in the previous emergency response project and in the needs assessments carried out by GCAF. The project will work with individuals with disability of the target communities to get acceptance for women's and girls’ participation in the committees and trainings. Individuals who are illiterate will be equally and meaningfully involved in decision making related to emergency GBV program design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation GCAF will establish complaint mechanism to enable women, youth, elders, disabled and minorities to raise their concerns. GCAF will always employ feedback mechanisms, from the start of assessment to the end of PDMs, to ensure all people, especially marginalized groups, have an active role in the way GBV partners carry out their assistance and improve response appropriateness.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

GCAF enhances Protection partners’ initiatives through types of GBV activities distributed; sharing GBV and HLP referral information; designing projects with the community to address important needs; training the community on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) GCAF will continue to work with Protection partners through agreed Standard operating Procedures to avoid any overlap.1. Sexual exploitation and sexual abuse perpetrated against beneficiaries constitute serious misconduct and are therefore grounds for disciplinary action. .2 Sexual activity with persons under the age of 18 is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally Mistaken belief in the age of a person is not considered a defense. 3 Exchange of money, employment, goods, services or any type of assistance for sexual favours is prohibited. 4 Sexual relationships between staff and aid beneficiaries are strongly discouraged, as they are based on an inherently unequal power relationship and undermine the credibility and integrity of work. 5 Where a staff member develops concerns or suspicions with regard to possible sexual exploitation or sexual abuse by a fellow worker, whether the individual worker or for another aid organization, he or she must report such concerns through the line-management channels, or should this be the case, to the programme’s PSEA focal point. In concrete terms, this means reporting concerns or suspicions, including rumours, in good faith, without attempting to investigate the matter personally in order to obtain proof or write a report. Reprisals against any staff member having reported sexual exploitation or abuse is prohibited. 6 Girl Child Africa Foundation undertakes to ensure that any allegations remain confidential and that an impartial internal investigation is carried out that will not, however, replace investigations which may be conducted by the competent local authorities. If reported information concerns a partner of GCAF or any other aid organization, the Field Programme Director/ Head of Programmes will be required to take the appropriate measures. 7. All GCAF staff members, and managers in particular, whatever their level, are expected to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Preventing a culture of complacency and impunity demands continuous effort. Managers, amongst others, must be familiar with and enforce this policy. They must be proactive in overseeing their teams. Staff responsible for project implementation, follow-up and evaluation and for fund-raising must be particularly mindful of risks of sexual exploitation or sexual abuse, in accordance with activities and context. Risk reduction strategy must be a matter of constant concern. 8.GCAF undertakes to provide assistance to any individual who files a complaint with the organization, and to ensure specific assistance and support for the victims of sexual exploitation or sexual abuse perpetrated by one of its staff members.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

It will provide a channel for community and beneficiaries to easily raise questions, suggestions and concerns about aid activities, and have agreed protocols for action to be taken in response. In this way community members can ‘hold an organization to account’ for their actions, and ensure their answerability for how resources are used in their community.Transparency is the foundation of accountability and a pre-requisite to functioning BFMs. For communities to provide relevant feedback, they first need to know what the main services oractivities are that they are being asked to give feedback about, who is being targeted, and what commitments or standards the responsible agencies can be held to account to. Community members also need to know that it is their right to provide feedback, how they can provide feedback, and what will happen to their feedback.This helps to redress the power imbalance that often exist between aid actors and recipient communities. Information is power, and so too is the means to bring about change.GCAF will spend at least two weeks in the field with communities for every intervention which allows them to engage the communities in a way that improves their protection and understanding of pressing issues in areas like SEA and HLP, among other benefits. GCAF will always employ feedback mechanisms, from the start of assessment to the end of PDMs, to ensure all people, especially marginalized groups, have an active role in the way child protection staffs carry out their assistance and improve response appropriateness
