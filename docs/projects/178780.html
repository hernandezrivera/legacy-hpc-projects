Project Summary

The rationale of this area-based housing project is derived from experience gained from previous pilots and shelter projects, and in light of Shelter Sector’s evaluation and interventions, as well as challenges emerged during the last years, concluding that
• Restoration of services and infrastructure, simultaneous to rehabilitation of living space, is a key factor in reviving the affected neighbourhoods and improving stability,
• The return, and the subsequent rehabilitation of damaged properties, are tied to demanding procedures and steps to be taken by returning families including the rehabilitation permission, approvals of relevant authorities, civil and tenure documentation, as well as technical matters to restore their damaged homes including damage assessments and classifications, technical specifications, costs, technical designs, business management and so on.
• Community (and municipality) involvement in project identiﬁcation and planning processes is a prominent element in emergency assistance, as it serves to mitigate possible tensions arising from perceived or real discrimination in selection processes. It is however essential to manage expectations of beneﬁciary populations, thus, to boost positive impact of outcomes of delivered assistance.
Objectives:
Through this area-based housing approach outlined herein, UN-Habitat aims to boost sustainable access to housing, through rehabilitation and restoration of community infrastructure (roads, street lighting and debris removal) and support returning and affected families with housing-related services and facilitations (housing support units and support cadastral services) for later rehabilitation phase in Rural Damascus, Daraa, Deir-Ezzor, Hama, Homs and Aleppo.
The holistic notion to serve entire conﬂict-affected, vulnerable communities through improvement of essential infrastructure and housing -related services, under the objective to revive neighborhoods, is at the core of this project.
Project Components:
In alignment with the municipalities’ recovery plans, the project targets to:
• Light repair and maintenance of roads (mainly the Asphalt layer) in residential areas
• Rehabilitation of sidewalks (including tiling, and replacement of damaged curbstones)
• Debris removal
• Provision and installation of solar street lighting
• Support the formation of Housing Support Units which is a virtual entity comprising representatives from municipality, local community, cadastral department, and technical experts. This unit aims to mobilize collective efforts of housing stakeholders to assist the returnees in housing-related services at community level including obtaining rehabilitation permits, conduct rapid damage assessment of houses/buildings, obtaining/restoring tenure documents, and all kinds of other technical services related to housing rehabilitation work. Supporting the restoration of cadastral services will be part of this activity wherever needed.

4 - Needs

After 10 years of crisis, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain deep and far-reaching. An estimation of 5.88 M people is in need for shelter assistance (4% increased from 2020), and 3.89 M (66%) are in areas of severity 3 or higher (HNO SYRIA 2021). The shelter response requirement goes beyond humanitarian response and the shelter sector members will not be able to meet all the needs.Furthermore, The population continues to look for safety in parts of the country where the level of violence has been reduced, and 448,000 individuals have returned to their areas or to safe areas close to their homes in 2020 (HNO 2021). While this trend is only a fraction of the total displacement figures (6.7 M), but it reflects the increasing need for more stable infrastructure to move toward more sustainable solutions for those able to return. Simultaneously, a variety of ownership issues, e.g. destroyed premises of institutions, damaged, lost or incomplete records, etc., present further challenges to the situation, especially that rehabilitation of damaged houses is only pursued following conﬁrmation of relevant tenure documents.Besides the criteria in HRP, the selection of targeted locations is based on the following criteria •Being in line with the Shelter Sector and HRP 2021 strategic priorities.•Being priority locations identiﬁed in the ﬁeld assessments and consultants with local experts and municipalities through UN-Habitat program of local planning. •Complementarity with ongoing projects of UNH resources and donor commitments •High numbers of PIN, vulnerable and expected returnees.*High complementarity with area-based interventions in WASH, Protection, and ERL.

5 - Implementation of activities

Implementation will adopt the following sequence: • Update the Municipality’s action plan identifying the priorities and areas of intervention using an area-based approach aimed to target speciﬁc areas, through an integrated participatory approach, where communities’ representatives, international organizations, ministries and public departments, are jointly identifying priorities in a manner that considers the mutuality between the identiﬁed issues.* Support the formation of Housing Support Units within target locations.* Conduct a detailed assessment for physical activities (roads, sidewalks, debris removal, and street lighting) to update the Municipality action plan reflecting current and immediate needs.• Tendering and contracting with private contractors and companies.• Implementation in full compliance with the standards and guidelines endorsed by Shelter Sectors.• Completion and handover to the relevant stakeholders.Through the project implementation, and in coordination with Municipalities, the local community will have a signiﬁcant supervisory role in terms of projects’ selection and implementation.In addition to reporting to the FTS, all activities and indicators will be reported to the sector through regular 4Ws mechanisms. UNH has developed monitoring mechanism including site visits reports, progress reports, inspection tools and ﬁnancial tracking tools. All these tools will be utilized during the implementation to enhance the monitoring process. The Municipalities will be monitoring the implementation through the technical committees that shall be formed to supervise the work and issue the oﬃcial handover reports, also the implementing partners are required to provide regular progress reports.
