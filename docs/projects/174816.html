Project Summary

Fierce fighting in Njikwa left a trail of destruction and pains in the community and in the hearts of the people with all social services shutdown especially in central town of Njikwa Administrative area, Ngwo and Oshie villages. The Water systems in these three communities were highly vandalized with untold damages ranging from breaking of pipes, stand taps and catchments putting the population vulnerable to sicknesses like Typhoid, malaria and other dirty water associated diseases. There are 23 water collection points including 10 standard catchments in Njikwa Sub Division in the 9 official villages.
About 20 000 people have limited or no access to safe drinking water, sanitation, or hygiene services. As of July 2020, typhoid and malaria affected all the 9 villages in the sub division with almost 80% of the population having traces of malaria or typhoid. A good number of cases of cholera noticed at local levels. Between June 2018 and July 2020 over 300 people died in Njikwa from what the local people called "high fever" - Typoid and Malaria; Many children died of high running stomach - "diarrhea" as community health volunteers reported, many people complained of belly bite. No medical report was available to attest to the cause of the deaths since the health services were not functional to determine the actual cause. The toileting system of the people had collapsed as many people had ran into the bushes, you could see open defecation at most back yards and roadsides.
The Comprehensive WASH Support to Conflict Affected Populations in Njikwa (CWS) project intends to improves access to safe and adequate water supply and basic sanitation infrastructure for 4000 households with a population of 20 000 people, 9 health facilities, 34 community learning spaces. 23 water collection points and 700 taps/80 wash basins will be cleaned and rehabilitated. 20 community latrines will be built in community public places as samples to encourage the households to construct theirs in like manners under the supervision of the community WASH committees. The project builds resiliency through complementary hygiene practices to maximize and enhance health-seeking behaviors among the most vulnerable communities.
CWS supports and improves sustainable environmental health conditions of vulnerable communities in Njikwa by improving water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) infrastructure and service delivery. The project addresses the key challenges to long-term sustainability by enabling (a) behavior change, (b) community ownership, (c) local capacity to manage infrastructure, and (d) sensitize and provides the communities with Covid 19 preventive measures and gadgets.
Community Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Committees (C-WASH-C) will be created in each community. This is a component of our community emergency response team programme as a water management board, sanitation and hygiene issues in the communities of Njikwa council areas. This project aims to train 100 Hygiene and sanitary inspectors who will also serve as first aiders in the 9 pilot villages as zones of Njikwa council area. 23 Community based water maintenance personnel with be trained on basic water repairs and maintenance and provided with basic plumbing tool kits. They shall organize regular clean up campaigns, do repair works on broken pipes, drinking points (spring), inspect homes and the latrines, do disinfection services in latrines and slums clearances. 3 community volunteers shall be specially trained to do chlorination of the water systems for the sub division on regular basis.
The project will also target 3000 vulnerable girls and women for the sensitization on good practices on menstrual hygiene and to provide and train them on the use of reusable sanitary dignity kits.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Problem Statement Fierce fighting in Njikwa left a trail of destruction and pains in the community and in the hearts of the people with all social services shutdown especially in central town of Njikwa Administrative area, Ngwo and Oshie villages. The Water systems in these three communities were highly vandalized with untold damages ranging from breaking of pipes, stand taps and catchments putting the population vulnerable to sicknesses like Typhoid, malaria and other dirty water associated diseases. There are 23 water collection points including 10 standard catchments in Njikwa Sub Division in the 9 official villages which have all broken down and water not chlorinated for the past four years. The fighting has caused significant damage to essential infrastructure, including water and sanitation systems and health facilities. Access to basic services has been drastically reduced against a backdrop of increasing rates of food insecurity, malnutrition, the reemergence of Typhoid, Malaria, Cholera, and near complete economic collapse. About 20 000 people have limited or no access to safe drinking water, sanitation, or hygiene services. As of July 2020, typhoid and malaria affected all the 9 villages in the sub division with almost 80% of the population having traces of malaria or typhoid. A good number cases of cholera were seen at local levels. Between June 2018 and July 2020 over 300 people have died in Njikwa from what the local people called "high fever" - Typoid and Malaria; Many children died of high running stomach - "diarrhea" as community health volunteers reported, many people complained of belly bite. No medical report was available to attest to the cause of the dead of these people since the health services were not functional to determine the actual cause. Though "high fever - Typhoid and Malaria" took the central stage for the deaths in Njikwa, many others died in the bushes, some at home and others in the emptied health posts because of in hygienic handling. Yet others died of malnutrition, eating dirty food. Babies and mothers have died at delivery , others have been beaten by insects and reptiles in the bushes and at home because of the dirty environment. People will not cleanup compounds for the fear of exposures to factions. Water catchments have been abandoned with broken pipes. All regular technicians fled and no one to clean or repair them. In other places water points are not cleared or drained. No knowledge of filtering water before drinking. No chlorinated water for over three years. Typhoid and malarial very prevalent. The communities use mostly pit latrines of which many have been abandoned and have collapsed, leaving the people using back yard bushes thus more exposures to sicknesses. Over 3000 thousand people fled their homes in Njikwa that have been invaded by bushes. Latrines infested by reptiles and mosquito nets are not available. Malaria is very prevalent, The Mbororo community in Njikwa is highly affected and threatened for their purported involvement in the crisis supporting the government that caused much pains and loses for the people. As a consequence trust was lost and the Mbororos are being chased away and have suffered a great lost of their livelihood -cattle and sheep and some have been killed in the course of the crisis. Most of them live in forest and will not come out even when they are sick. They are in need of clothes, blankets, pullovers, basic CRIs and even Shelter Kits. Given these varying problems in the sub division, we hope that our intervention will bring some relief to the traumatizing population until peace subsequently returns in the country . Women have resorted to the old traditional methods of managing menstrual issues. Some use pieces of clothes inn turns while others use the traditional leaves and back of trees. These are usually not treated and thus expose the women to more diseases and infections. Sanitary Pads are very expensive in local markets.
