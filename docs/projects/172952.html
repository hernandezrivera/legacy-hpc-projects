Project Summary

The project will be implemented by CAFOD in partnership with national NGO Sustainable Action Group (SAG). The project will provide critical agricultural inputs (seeds and tools) through vouchers to 1400 farmers in 7 communities of Al Fasher Rural to mitigate the socio-economic impacts of COVID-19 and natural disasters such as floods. The project will also support livelihood diversification and home-based food production for approximately 1700 HHs. This will include prioritization of animal treatments, pastures improvements, specifically targeting pastoralists, agro-pastoralists whose movements are restricted, and support livelihood diversification through livestock fairs for the distribution of small stock. The project will target the most vulnerable populations to food insecurity, addressing constraints in inadequate access, availability and consumption of diversified nutritious food, and limited and unstable livelihoods and productive capacities. Emergency food assistance through the distribution of half ration food basket vouchers to 350 HHs for 3 months will ensure that basic needs are met for girls, women, boys and men at risk of hunger and malnutrition during the peak hunger period. Strategy of selection of individual beneficiaries of the project will be based on the level the needs in terms of food security, socio-economic services and women headed household. A participatory community wealth ranking tool will be used for the selection and targeting of the beneficiaries. Community consultations through community forums, and the leadership of the villages will be involved in leading the processes of identification of the HHs. Project partner staff and community leaders will be trained in Save, Accessible, Dignified and Inclusive (SADI) program to ensure the selection is inclusive and the project implementation cycle captures cross-cutting issues. A Community complaint, reporting and feedback mechanisms shall be set up/ established at the start of the project to ensure that the concerns of project beneficiaries (both direct and indirect beneficiaries are addressed.

1 - Needs

2020 HNO reports that an estimated 7.1 million people in Sudan face Crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) and need urgent action to save lives. This represents a 22 percent increase from 5.8M compared to the same period last year (IPC analysis 2019). Around one million of the 7.2 million food insecure people are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) and around 6 million people are facing crisis levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3). An additional 17 million people are estimated to be under stress (IPC Phase 2), and any additional shocks like floods, inflation and poverty can push them to severe levels of hunger.More than 9.6 million Sudanese, almost a quarter of the entire population, are now severely food insecure. The figures, the highest ever recorded in the history of the IPC analysis in Sudan, represents an increase of 65 percent if compared with the same period (June to September) last year. This includes about 2.2 million people facing emergency acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4). The situation is especially concerning in some states like North Kordofan, with an increase of 335 percent, or Gazera, with an increase of over 200 percent. Conflict related displacements, significant macroeconomic challenges, resulting in high inflation rates, exacerbated by COVID-19 prevention measures are the main causes of the increasing hunger in Sudan. North Darfur State hosts Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) Refugees, and Returnees. The FSL Cluster estimates that approximately 141 266 individuals need emergency support in Al Fashir which represent approximately 25% of the population which was analysed. In Kutum locality, the FSL Cluster estimates that approximately 8977 individuals need emergency support in 2021, representing approximately 34% of the analysed populations. The two localities are also hosting IDPs and returnees who are highly vulnerable segments of the population. Moreover, the deteriorating economic situation has resulted in shortage of fuel and spiralling prices of stable food. This has been compounded by the Covid-19 which resulted in the loss of livelihoods opportunities, restricted movements, loss of employment and limited capacity of the national government to provide basic services. The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods, daily labour opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population. Movement restrictions has impacted farmers’ access to markets for critical inputs, pastoralists’ access to water and traditional grazing areas and households’ access to income from informal farm labour. Many pastoralists are likely to continue facing difficulty meeting their minimum food needs as a result of very high staple food prices, reduced access to labour, lower than normal terms of trade and seasonally low access to animal products during the lean season. The vulnerable and poor whose income and livelihoods heavily depend on a daily wage, especially people living in urban and peri-urban areas, were affected by COVID-19 containment measures first and then with floods, thus at high risk of food insecurity. Women stand out as highly impacted by the dire situation due to their restricted access to factors and means of food production, processing and consumption.The 2021 HNO identifies 6.9 million people in need of food and livelihoods assistance, an increase from 6.2 million people in 2020.The Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Sector will target 5.3 million people, an increase from 4.6 million people in 2020
