Project Summary

to reduce the vulnerability of children and young people in Mozambique to climate-related risks. UNICEF aims to achieve this by integrating child-centered Disaster Risk Reduction (CCDRR) into development and humanitarian efforts. The ultimate objective is to strengthen individual and community resilience in the face of increasing climate-related disasters. Access to quality health services is crucial to preventing child deaths from tropical diseases and improving their health in crisis and post-humanitarian crisis contexts. MENTOR’s work in this area focuses on three key areas:
• Support to community health workers;
• Training and supervision of promotors, supervisors, and coordinators.
• Supply chain support.
• Health Supervision structure logistical support from provincial to district level

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

A mentor carries out daily, hands-on work with local authorities to respond as quickly as possible to real needs, whether it be with the provincial health department or education. The goal is to provide, whenever possible, a coordinated and holistic response.MENTOR will continue its collaboration with the DPS and SPS (Health) DPE (Education) of Cabo Delgado to enhance the training and supervision of Community Health Agents. These agents serve as the initial and often sole responders to the health needs of the hardest-to-reach populations, particularly children. MENTOR will further leverage its expertise in distributing materials and water to health units, schools, resettlement camps, and host communities. Additionally, the organization will conduct hygiene promotion campaigns and implement malnutrition control and treatment programs in these locations. This comprehensive approach aims to enhance the overall response and quality of health services in close proximity to the most vulnerable populations, ultimately bolstering the capacity for swift responses to outbreaks and diseases. Recognizing the dynamic nature of challenges in different provinces, MENTOR remains adaptable and capable of responding rapidly to various situations. Whether it involves transitioning from Sofala, in the aftermath of a cyclone, to Cabo Delgado, amidst armed conflict, MENTOR will continue to exhibit impressive results and demonstrate robust logistical capacities as needed.MENTOR will continue to prioritize a multi-sectoral, community-based response across various sectors, including health, nutrition, WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene), and SBCC (Social and Behavior Change Communication). Collaborating with the DPS and SPS, MENTOR will extend its efforts beyond health centers and schools. The organization will focus on training health technicians to diagnose and treat illnesses in children, as well as contribute to the construction and reconstruction of water access infrastructures like boreholes and rainwater harvesting systems. Additionally, MENTOR will be involved in the development and reconstruction of sanitation infrastructures, such as latrines.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Integrated vector control management using a range of tools and strategies to tackle vector-borne diseases. Each tool plays a role within the integrated vector management framework, addressing different stages of the vector life cycle and targeting various points of disease transmission. These includes: • Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS); • Larval Source Management (LSM); • Social Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC);• Entomological Surveillance involves monitoring mosquito populations;• Long-Lasting Insecticide-treated Nets (LLINs); Water, sanitation and hygiene is integral to our work tackling vector-borne and neglected tropical diseases by promotion and creation secure practises and spaces addressing the faecal-oral route of transmission and reducing breeding sites for multiple disease vectors, healthier environments are created to help prevent the spread of diseases. To improve access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene practices we focus on:• Infrastructure development - to improve water supply systems, build or repair sanitation facilities, and establish safe and sustainable hygiene infrastructure;• Urban WASH - In urban areas, addressing water supply and storage alongside sanitation and waste management; • Tippy Taps - are simple, low-cost handwashing devices that promote proper hand hygiene, especially in settings with limited resources.• Community-led total sanitation empowers and engages communities to take ownership of their sanitation and hygiene solutions. • Waste management is essential for maintaining a clean and healthy environment and to reduce mosquito breeding sites. Cholera Outbreaks control – as experts in Rapid Response, MENTOR is currently considered by the Cabo Delgado Health Services to be one of the partner organisations for rapid and effective responses to cholera outbreaks. In less than 48 hours, Mentor is able to activate logistical support and transport people and materials to respond to immediate needs - which is crucial when children are involved, and a rapid response is highly recommended and necessary. Monitoring and evaluation organizational skills enable MENTOR to assess the effectiveness of interventions, track progress towards programme goals, and make informed decisions to improve health outcomes. We work closely with local health authorities and partners to develop and improve disease surveillance systems.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

An initial risk assessment will be conducted in order to identify the potential risk that the programme may encounter or cause. Follow-up assessments and meetings with representative beneficiaries will occur throughout the programme to understand if life and condition has changed in the population and if any kind of harm is being caused. MENTOR coordinators will be trained to monitor these risks and identify potential harms. The findings of the risk assessment will be used to design specific action plans to mitigate the risks identified. All MENTOR staff will be trained on PSEA and they will be committed to respect the Code of Conduct. Specifically, for the different activities the practical points that we will implement are: - Implementation of a green line available to denounce any kind of harm. - Implementation of suggestion boxes across the sites of implementation.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

MENTOR will include women and elderly people within the disaggregation of the data collection indicators. This will ensure that specific groups are reached. MENTOR will include in the trainings of staff and short-term activists PSEA and points about elderly population and people living with disabilities. When implementing the activities, the activists and MENTOR staff will need to map and target people that may encounter difficulties in accessing assistance. Supervision of the activities will also focus on this matter. In all activities proposed, MENTOR will look for gender parity across all human resources - if they are hired by MENTOR. MENTOR will also work together with representatives of different groups of the communities including women groups and community leaders. These meetings will serve to communicate and collect feedback from the activities at the planning phase as well as in the evaluation phase in line with accountability of affected population.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People living with disabilities may have social and physical barriers to access assistance. In the different activities of these project, the community leaders, health technicians and all WASH FIT group members will be trained to map individuals with difficult access to assistance and will be encouraged to give attention to this. Durind on job visits MENTOR will monitor the activities in order to include mapping of people living with disabilities.
