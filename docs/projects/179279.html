Project Summary

GC implements integrated protection services to prevent, mitigate, and respond to harm,
exploitation, and abuse for the most vulnerable boys, girls, men and women in northern
Syria in Idleb governorates. Under this program, we will manage 13 safe
spaces and 16 mobile teams. Safe spaces, in the form of Community Centers (CCs),
Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) and Women/Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) will be service
centers used to provide consistent services to communities. Mobile teams will support
the static facilities to provide services to those not able to access the safe spaces, raise
awareness in the wider community, quickly respond to new movements and emergencies,
and conduct assessments. Mobile teams will also be ready to respond and provide
emergency response services to IDP displacements and any influx of IDPs. The objective
of the project is to enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond
to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria, by promoting
international law, IHL, HRL and through quality principled assistance.

4 - Needs

With the Syrian conflict in its ninth year, over 13 million people remain in need ofhumanitarian assistance. In 44% of the assessed communities, respondents reportedpresence of at least one of the assessed seven protection issues as very commonlyoccurring and in 52% of the communities respondents have reported the presence of five ormore out of the assessed seven protection issues (HNO 2020). Our largest concentrationof protection services are in Dana sub-district of Idleb, which is severity level 6, and as ofIn September 2019 there were over 700,000 people in need and 1 million across HaremDistrict, which does not include the hundreds of thousands that have arrived since andcontinue to arrive with the ongoing changes in control in Idleb. The narrowing space forthose fleeing violence is closing on Harem District and those arriving are largelyvulnerable, women-headed households and those who were not able to leave before. Witha lack of shelter available to host these new IDPs, protection risks will increase, such asfor women lacking privacy and safety within shelters hosting multiple families, toincrease tensions within and between families leading to increase child protection risks,and inadequate services such as for those with disabilities and the elderly.

5 - Implementation of activities

GC has been implementing specialized and non-specialized protection services in northern Syria since early 2016. We are currently operational in Idleb governorate and will continue working in these locations throughout 2021. We directly implement comprehensive protection services, covering CP, GBV, and Community-Based Protection through static facilities and mobile teams. We support vulnerable IDPs and host community members through services in and out of camps.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risks: For awareness raising: expectations of response/services in locations where services may be limited/non-existent Measures: Develop clear communication to manage expectations and receive feedback on the quality and usefulness of the information being provided. Member agencies to use referral pathways where possible. Risks: GBV service providers safety and security because of their role and PTS dealing with violence. Privacy concerns on confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services. Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging. Persons with disability have difficult accessing GBV services. Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girls mobility. Measures: Self care strategies as part of programmes and organization HR policy. GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. Targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as safe entry point. CP Risks: Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles. Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and caregivers) through the provision of specialised child protection services if not adequately trained/ services are of low quality. Risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys and their families if staff are not appropriately trained or if client data is not managed appropriately. Measures: Engage with diverse members of community.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The border crossings between Turkey and Syria will remain open and accessible for humanitarian goods and staff, without extended closures, and/or alternate border points are opened and accessible. If the border closes for humanitarian goods or commercial traffic and our ability to receive essential items or funds, we will consider other, less accessible means of delivery. GC staff continue to have consistent access to target locations while maintaining acceptable levels of independence, impartiality, and neutrality. We will ensure this by continuing to utilize our community engagement team to liaise with the relevant camp stakeholders, and continue to reinforce that we are a humanitarian agency that abides by the humanitarian principles of independence, neutrality, and impartiality. Should our level of access change significantly or impact our ability to work within our humanitarian principles, we will look to identify alternative locations or solutions. Active violence does not directly impact our areas of operation for extended period of time; Violence in the targeted areas remains at a level which allows GC to implement the program, with both armed and unarmed parties to the conflict allowing access to vulnerable HHs for provision of services. We will regularly assess the risk to our beneficiaries and staff of violence in our project locations. If the risk becomes unacceptable we will suspend activities temporarily and close permanently if needed, according to ethical closeout procedures and in communication with the Cluster. Camp managers, Local Councils, relief committees, and community stakeholders will collaborate with GC, and facilitate access for delivery of humanitarian assistance.
