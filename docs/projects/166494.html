Project Summary

This project aims to focus on IDP groups most vulnerable to COVID19 infections in camp/HC settings through strategies that will efficiently mitigate the spread of the infection while providing much needed shelter assistance simultaneously. It proposes interventions in Dikwa, Monguno, Damasak and Mubi. For a most effective delivery, NRC will be providing a mix of in-kind shelter construction/NFI in-kind provisions and shelter kit distributions in accordance with the NRC’s program response strategy.

1 - Needs

Emergency and makeshift shelters are the shelter options for the majority of IDPs in host communities and IDP settlements. There are 3.5 million people in the north-east region in need of shelter and NFI support. Shelters constructed in camps have been predominantly temporary and are emergency shelters not meant for long-term use. Outside of camps, semi-permanent shelters used by displaced people are dilapidated. 60% of IDPs live in host communities; 90 % of them are hosted by family members, while 10 % have their own accommodation. Of the total 1,6 million returnees, 421,000 have various shelter and NFI needs. Returnees living in return areas with a high number of returnees and living in partially burnt houses or in self-made/makeshift shelters are the most vulnerable. Among the returnees, 72 per cent are women, girls and boys (17 per cent, 31 per cent and 24 per cent respectively). 823,000 individuals (254,800 girls; 210,200 boys; 177,600 women; 141,100 men; 21,000 elderly women; and 18,200 elderly men) from inaccessible areas have been prioritized for shelter and NFI support, in addition to almost one million people (275,900 girls; 227,700 boys; 192,300 women; 152,800 men; 22,800 elderly women; and 29,900 elderly men) remaining in their areas of origin who will need shelter assistance and two per cent who will need NFIs. People with specific needs (single parents, female-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children, single elderly, physically disabled, and children forced into labour) include women (20 per cent), children (with 23 per cent under five), and elderly people (3 per cent) as they are more prone to domestic violence, abuse, harassment and exploitation 40% of IDPs live in one of the more than 250 camps or settlements (schools or government buildings used as temporary shelter), the majority of which were created spontaneously. Many of the camps, both formal and informal, have been established in areas without proper site planning resulting in congestion, fire outbreaks, and flooding risks. In most sites (60-70 per cent), shelters need to be urgently upgraded or replenished, as a result of the short lifespan of shelters and continuous fluid population movements. In all types of camp settings, most shelters are occupied by women-headed households (HH) or single mothers. With the continuous influx of new arrivals, especially in Borno State, mainly triggered by military operations, reception centres are overstretched. Congestion is a major protection concern for women and girls, and also negatively affects the elderly and people with disabilities. The shelter and NFIs needs among returnees are also urgent. Almost one-third of all returnees are currently living in either damaged or self-made shelters. Returnees living in return areas with a high number of returnees and living in partially burnt houses or in self-made/makeshift shelters are the most vulnerable. According to assessment reports, after food, NFIs remain the second most reported need of IDPs, both in camps or settlements, and host communities, due to the lack of prepositioned NFIs in key receiving areas given the short lifespan of shelter-related NFIs. The three main NFIs requested by the affected populations are mosquito nets, mats and kitchen sets.

2 - Activities

The NRC response strategy will provide as a critical factor, technical training for local carpenters and beneficiaries to build their capacities in basic construction techniques, which can be employed in simple construction without any elaborate third party assistance. Training in habitability measures and awareness for the improvement and maintaining the healthier living environment will also be provided along with the technical construction training. This will help reduce social interactions among all stakeholders during construction which will in turn also help reduce spread of the C19 infections. To start activities, NRC will conduct a needs survey to urgently determine the most vulnerable groups on ground especially ones in living in makeshifts or in reception centres. This will inform which individuals will be targeted for the assistance. As a follow-up, NRC will ensure close collaboration with the traditional leaders, the affected group representation and other stakeholders (on ground humanitarian actors including the CCCM agency) to establish the Beneficiary Selection Committee (BSC) through which NRC will select and register beneficiaries for shelter assistance. NRC will be emphasizing the project objectives and beneficiary selection criteria to these same stakeholders. In order to strengthen the project impact, NRC will coordinate the response with other partners through the shelter sector to provide rapid and timely delivery, and avoid gaps and duplication of efforts. While needs assessments and selection processes are ongoing, NRC will seek access to land with the assistance of the NRC’s HLP team and the shelter sector in the proposed locations of intervention. This will aid timely delivery of the project especially in consideration for the COVID19 response and the coming rainy season that will be in place in the next 2 quarters of the year. Once the beneficiary selection is completed NRC will commence training of beneficiaries and selected local technicians in essential construction methodologies. Habitability measures training will also be conducted and will focus more on hygiene practices around the shelters and fire mitigation practices. This will be done through the use of IEC materials pamphlets and FGD’s where possible. FGD’s will be done with a maximum number of 10 people/per session with emphasis on social distancing and any other C19 mitigation strategies that need to be employed. Where this is not possible, NRC will try to seek out remote facilitation through e-platforms and speakers to better adapt the activities `without creating any health risks for the beneficiaries or the NRC team. Monitoring will be done by the NRC shelter technical team to ensure quality delivery of the shelter construction. NRC will further determine in which locations/scenarios to provide constructed shelters and kit distributions respectively through assessment of the situations in each of the proposed locations at the proposed time of shelter activities. Depending on the nature of access at the time and any other constraints also, NRC will consider remote monitoring of activities, distributions and CFM’s in accordance with NRC/Sectors strategy guidelines. There will also be distribution of NFI’s to recipients of the shelter assistance and possibly other beneficiaries in the HC depending on available provisions. This will ensure acceptability in the community and reduce the risk of friction between the beneficiaries and the HC. NFI’s will be provided after hand over of completed shelters or simultaneously with the distribution of shelter kits, depending on which scenario is adopted in the location of intervention. NFI in kind distributions will be done through vendors that will be predetermined by the NRC logistics team. They will make available the NFI items in predetermined locations that will be easily accessible to beneficiaries for collection and will not create the risk of overcrowding during distributions.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In the provision of shelter assistance to beneficiaries, the project proposal will be shared with the beneficiaries to give them opportunity to share any ideas, requests and comments on the proposed shelter assistance which will be considered and possibly integrated into the final package to be delivered to the beneficiaries. This will further enhance and better tailor the project design intended for the target beneficiaries. While providing a much needed shelter assistance to targeted beneficiaries, a CFM help desk will be provided for a wide range of feedback concerning quality of shelter provisions, selection process and any other issues that may come up for monitoring. In addition, NRC will be conducting PDMs and PCM to evaluate the status and quality of shelter/ NFI provisions during the project.
