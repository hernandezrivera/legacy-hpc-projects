Project Summary

Following the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus (HDPN) approach that IOM strongly supports and actively promotes in Cameroon, and building on the programmes and activities implemented in the recent years, IOM plans comprehensive emergency interventions for 2025 focusing especially on coordination, shelter, mental health support (health), psychosocial support (protection) and WASH interventions. IOM’s multisectoral response aims at contributing to reinforce life-saving assistance and to improve well-being of displaced and the most vulnerable populations affected by the crisis in the Far North region of Cameroon. Critical data on displacement situation, alerts as well as humanitarian needs following our DTM methodology will be provided to the humanitarian community in the frame of the Coordination Sector. IOM will also distribute shelter and NFI kits using cash-modality solutions when possible (bearing in mind that lack of civil documentation poses a huge challenge) and help the construction for most vulnerable people. In the WASH response, IOM will drill and rehabilitate boreholes, construct water points, latrines and other infrastructures and conduct hygiene sensitization and promotion activities combined with distribution of WASH kits. Finally, IOM multisectoral assistance will also include mental health activities including direct MHPSS assistance using PMTs, reinforcing referral mechanism and capacity building of health actors (Health sector), while also directly providing psychosocial support to populations impacted by crisis by reinforcing safety and social network as well as strengthening community capacity to provide psychosocial support to victims of GBVs (Protection sector).
Through activities, direct feedback from beneficiaries and community members will be gained via IPs and also directly by IOM staff during fi eld visits and visits of the M&E/AAP independent team in accordance with the wider IOM AAP emergencies workplan. AAP focal points will be assigned in Buea and Yaounde respectively, with clear SOPs to be in place to capture complaints from beneficiaries and to take any appropriate action. Members of IP will be trained on topics such as communication in high-stress environments. Targeting of beneficiaries will be done according to a vulnerability criteria including persons living with disabilities assessed through RRM household level assessments conducted by IOM. Also, IOM will integrate measures to ensure gender-specific needs when conducting needs assessment and to mitigate risks of GBV in all activities (e.g., including through training of IPs on GBV core concepts, guiding principles as well as on how to respond to disclosures of GBV). IOM will also ensure the effective participation of these communities in decision-making processes and in establishing community-based mechanisms.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Since 2013, ongoing hostilities and violence in the Far North Region continue to trigger lower magnitude displacements and community shocks due to sporadic attacks by armed groups designated as terrorist organizations (AGDTO) such as Boko Haram and the Islamic State West Africa Province. Attacks are not only limited to clashes with Government security forces but are carried out on nearby communities, with as a result significant number of displaced persons uprooted as well as returnees. Furthermore, intercommunity violence between different ethnic groups over lack of resources and competing livelihoods is occurring more periodically, most recently in the Logone Birni arrondissement. Insecurity in this region is exacerbating the already limited access of displaced populations to essential social services as looting and destruction of goods and public infrastructure are becoming more recurrent, further harming community cohesion and leading to higher inter-community tensions. Currently, all humanitarian response actors are struggling to meet the large humanitarian needs due to a low level of humanitarian funding and an overall low visibility. Low funding has meant that there is a greater need for the reinforcement of a Rapid Response Mechanism which requires reliable and quickly available data on alerts. IOM’s last multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted in the Far-North region (August/September 2024) showed a variety of needs relative to individual sectors. Shelter/NFI was identified as the second priority need with situation still remains worrying for the households surveyed. This can be explained by the increase in mobile populations over the last 6 months, and by the fact that many mobile households live in makeshift shelters: (25% of refugees outside camps, 19% of IDPs and 11% of returnees). For the health sector, 59% of women give birth in a health facility which is still a long way from the target of 100% of women giving birth in a health facility, assisted by qualified personnel. As outlined in the protection sector response plan, reduce GBV risks, reduce exposure of children to arm aspects of war and improve protection and respect of fundamental rights for all the persons affected by crisis are the main issues to be addressed. In particular, there is a very limited capacity in the health centers in terms of mental health, and IOM has been providing capacity building to staff and specific drugs to these centers. For education, a large majority of the communities consulted recognize education as a right for every child and agree that it is important for every child to have access to quality educational services. This trend remains constant, regardless of the status of the stakeholders involved. The three main sources of household food are daily labor (72% for IDPs, 65% for Returnees and 75% for Refugees out of camp), Household agricultural product income (42% for IDPs, 59% for Returnees and 47% for Refugees out of camp), Income from their own business or trade (32% for IDPs, 44% for Returnees and 33% for Refugees out of camp). According to the OCHA flood information note from September 19, 2024, at least 365,060 people have been affected by flooding in five departments of the Far North: Logone and Chari, Mayo-Danay, Diamaré, Mayo-Tsanaga, and Mayo-Kani. Mayo-Danay and Logone et Chari are the hardest hit, with 193,892 and 146,224 people affected, respectively. The floods resulted in at least 30 deaths, the destruction of over 56,084 houses, flooding of 82,509 hectares of arable land (54,094 in Mayo-Danay and 27,653 in Logone and Chari), the loss of 5,278 livestock, and damage to around 262 educational structures. Affected communities have significant needs in various sectors, including nutrition, shelter, non-food items (NFI), WASH, education, health, and protection.
