Project Summary

UNICEF will improve the access of adults, adolescents and child survivors to quality services through, i) capacity development, mentorship and coaching of service providers, ii) strengthening institutional systems to provide multisectoral services, iii) maintaining 21 safe spaces for women and girls in camps and host community to provide services and referrals, iv) engagement of community to address negative social norms, v) Mitigating GBV risks in sectors and through safety audits, vi) strengthening Child and adolescent survivors initiative and vi) advocacy for equitable access to justice and social services.
70,000 women, men, boys and girls including persons with disabilities, will be reached with GBV response, risk mitigations and prevention interventions, targeting 80% from refugees and 20% host population to enhance social cohesion.
Of which, 10,000 girls, boys and women will access Multi-sectoral GBV services (Case Management, Psychosocial support, referral for medical, legal and safety) in survivor-centred approach and in the best interest of the child, while 8,000 women and girls will be engaged in structured skills activities to mitigate GBV risks and to build their resilience and 20,000 women and girls will receive dignity kits to uphold their dignity and reduce GBV risks.
Community outreach mobilizers, volunteers and community groups will play a crucial role in reaching 45,000 women, men, boys, girls, religious and community leaders, Manjis and other stakeholders with messages on COVID-19, GBV prevention and risk mitigation, using EMAP, SASA and other strategies to address negative social norms, and mitigate GBV risks and other forms of violence among children and women.
UNICEF will preposition front liners and supplies for emergency preparedness and response during natural disasters while strengthening engagement with WASH, Nutrition, CP and Education sectors to further mitigate GBV risks in programming.
UNICEF will continue to enhance accountability to the affected population by strengthening PSEA and feedback mechanisms.

1 - Identified Needs

As of September 2020, UNHCR estimated 861,545 Rohingya refugees to be living in the camps in Cox’s Bazar district. Of these, women and girls make up to 52% while children constitute to 51% and 1% are persons with disabilities. Due to the prevailing living conditions and fragile protective environment, women and girls in the camps continue to be exposed to violence including; Gender-Based Violence (GBV), trafficking, child marriage, forced prostitution, sexual exploitation and abuse (JRP 2020). Some studies have also shown Rohingya men and boys to be victims of different forms of sexual violence and multifaceted impact of violence (WRC 2018). Rohingya refugees continue to rely heavily on humanitarian assistance for their basic needs, increasing vulnerabilities and risks especially among children, unaccompanied and separated children, female-headed households, elderly and persons with disabilities. In addition, issues such as insufficient food rations, lack of livelihood and inadequate/unsafe WASH facilities and protection risks such as kidnapping, child marriage, sexual harassment, threats by local people/armed groups/manjis and restrictions in movements to access services were reported (J-MSNA July-Aug 2020) heightening exposure to violence for the vulnerable groups. Adolescent girls in the camps, have limited access and information on adolescent sexual and reproductive health which increases risks of child marriages with its consequences (UNICEF and WRC ASRH assessment 2018). In the host community, an estimated 472,002 individuals need humanitarian assistance due to overwhelmed public services (J-MSNA July-Aug. 2020). Due to deep-rooted patriarchal social norms, violation of human rights is rampant, resulting in violence mainly GBV among women and girls. In 2015, 73% of married women in Bangladesh were found to have experienced some forms of violence perpetrated by their current husbands, while access to justice remains a concern due to cost and prolonged waiting ( VAW-Survey 2015). Natural disasters such as Monsoon and Cyclones in the region, increase population vulnerabilities and amidst COVID 19 pandemic, safety and security risks among women and girls escalated with 43% of the host community population feeling unsafe, outside their homes while 42% Refugees feeling unsafe inside their homes (Rapid Gender Analysis, 2020). These risks exacerbate pre-existing vulnerabilities and exposure to sexual exploitation, child marriage, trafficking and GBV especially among children, female-headed households, women and girls in camps and host community. COVID 19 measures to control the spread of the virus, resulted in economic stress, reduced services, movement restrictions, disruptions in protection and empowerment activities hindering women and girls’ access to gender-specific services. 43% women in camps and 40% in the host community, reported lack of menstrual hygiene supplies and limited access to SRH services (Rapid Gender Analysis,2020). During COVID 19 pandemic, incidents of Intermate Partner Violence (IPV) and child marriages highlighted (In the Shadows of the Pandemic, 2020). GBVIMS, August 2020 report, showed IPV (84.4%), as the highest of all the reported GBV incidents compared to 72% before COVID19 pandemic (GBViMS Q1 report,2020). Although IPV directly affects survivors, it also indirectly impacts the wellbeing of children and adolescents witnessing the violence. Reports on child marriage through GBViMS are low however, other sources confirm child marriage to be happening (GBVSS May 2020). Gaps in access to quality Multi-Sectoral services, caring for child and adolescent Survivors, remain. These are attributed to i) inadequate capacity among actors, ii) cultural barriers, iii) limited knowledge on available services and vi) gaps in systems (GBVSS 2020). UNICEF will strengthen; Multi-Sectoral responses, integration of GBV prevention, risk mitigations across sectors, PSEA, advocate for systems strengthening.

Strengthen community engagement targeting 45,000 men, boys, women, girls, community and religious leaders to increase awareness on GBV prevention, risk mitigations, response services and facilitate safe/confidential referrals and disseminate messages on COVID-19 in the camps and host communities8. Pre-position 20,000 Dignity kits and strengthen the capacity of implementing partners for emergency GBV responses during natural disasters9. Strengthen integration of IASC -GBViE risk mitigation measures in sectors (CP, Health, WASH, C4D, protection, Shelter/NFIs, Education & Nutrition) for safe programming 10. Adolescent Sexual & Reproductive Health information integrated in GBV programming to increase ASRH knowledge among adolescent girls and facilitate referral for services 11. Strengthen PSEA mechanisms for UNICEF and implementing Partners to prevent, report and respond to SEA12. Ensure accountability for the affected population, by strengthening feedback and complaints mechanisms such as UNICEF Information Feedback Centres (IFCs) and community-based groups in camps and host community, to receive and send feedback to and from the affected populations on their concerns and services and actively engage women, men, boys, girls and persons with disabilities and diversities, in assessment, decision making and influence on program implementation.13. UNICEF will continue to ensure emergency preparedness, response and disaster risk reduction (DRR) by prepositioning dignity kits and solar lamps, in coordination with GBV SS, PWG and CP, preposition frontline workers in PERU and work with WASH, Health, Nutrition and other sectors to create awareness on the risks, mitigate GBV risks and respond to the needs of women, men, boys and girls including PWD, affected by disasters such as monsoon and cyclones14. Promote positive transformation of social and gender norms through proven community GBV prevention strategies such as SASA and EMAP approaches among 7,000 community members (men, women, boys, girls, religious leaders, Majis and other gatekeepers)
