Project Summary

Olive Branch Organisation throughout this intervention is going to address the education needs and bridge the gaps of the education sector according to the strategic objectives of both the HRP 2022. The project’s activities are aiming to improve the access of 65,594 children 3 -17 years old (33,971 G, 30,243 B),~ 2% 1380 CwDs (630 G+750 B),and 2,758 education actors(1,718 W, 1,040 M), in Idleb governorate, includes 15 sub-districts to quality inclusive education and learning opportunities with the safe and protective environment by providing a package of formal and non-formal education services, the response prioritizes vulnerable children, adolescents/youth, girls and boys, and CwDs, in 15 sub-district located within the high-level of severity according to 2022 HNO PiN Severity:
(5) Benesh,Maretmesrin,Salqin,Ariha,Ehsem
(4) Aljanudiyeh,Jisr Alshoghur,Dana,Muhambel,Idlib,Taftanaz,Harim,Armanaz,Sarmin
(3) KaferTakharim
with strong coordination with authorities, Local councils, and IDLIB Education of Directorate.
OB will be focusing on this intervention to bring OOSC back to formal education and support children who under risk of school dropout through variety of integrated activities ensuring that children have safe access to quality learning opportunities in 138 formal schools, with establishing 15 TLSs to provide non-formal education of ECE activities for children age group 3 -5 years to address the numeracy literacy basics of ECE as a pre-school.
Objective 1: Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children
OB will conduct a package of education services starting with Back-to-school companies in the 15 selected areas targeting 18,000 (8,000 M, 10,000 F) children/youth with their parents/caregivers to raise their awareness of the education importance, Establish of 15 ECE TLSs, Provide minor repair based on the each school’s damage assessment in 138 schools and 15 TLSs (2,145 classrooms) including maintenance for the wash facilities gender-based, Provide school furniture including safety equipment in 138 schools and 15TLSs, provide running costs (drinkable water - cleaning materials, buffet materials..) to 138 schools and 15 TLSs, provide heating fuel in winter to 138 schools and 15 TLSs, Provide School bags includes stationery to 65,594 children designed based on age group’s needs (3-5 & 6-17 age), Provide formal education (SIG curriculum) 1–12 grades to 56,594 children (29,971 Girls, 25,243 Boys + 1380 CWD) 6-17 years in the 138 selected primary and secondary schools, provide PSS sessions to promote children wellbeing, provide to 138 schools 828 kits materials of education, PSS, and recreational, in addition to Provide 75 ECE kits materials to 15 TLSs, provide BLN-ECE that encompasses literacy and numeracy education, physical, social and emotional care, intellectual stimulation to the 9000 children (4000 B, 5000 G) 3 -5 years in the established TLSs
Objective 2: Enhanced quality of formal and non-formal education for children
OB will pay incentives to 2,758 education actors (1,718 W, 1,040 M) in the 138 schools and 15 TLSs, provide professional development/trainings to Teachers and supporting staff on TICC, child-centered, gender-sensitivity, inclusive education, CoC, PSS, referral mechanisms, Integration of Children with disabilities in Education, INEE. In addition, to train the schools' protection supervisors to improve project quality and compliance with CP and humanitarian principles, establish 138 PTAs (1104 members 552 w, 552 m) that the main role of the schools’ PTAs is to build strong working relationships among parents, teachers, and schools, in support of students’ improvement and wellbeing
MEAL Team will develop methodology and tools including relevant indicators to track progress, design and conduct registration, FGDs, PDMs, satisfaction surveys, field monitoring visits, collecting complaints and feedback to be addressed

4 - Needs

OB’s M&E team conducted a rapid assessment in the selected 15 areas in IDLIB Governorate, and based on triangulation analysis of the collected data with HNO and report of EoD in Idlib 2022, the main findings are:HNO draft 2021 (‘’An estimated 6.9 million people (97% children) need humanitarian education assistance. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of OOSC, and children who are at risk of dropping out’’), 138 of the functional school need support, %86 of 138 functional schools partial rehabilitation, %14 of 138 functional schools need total rehabilitation, %68 of 138 functional schools need school furniture including safety equipment, all educational staff in the 138 schools need incentive support and running cost including heating fuel, %87 of schools’ staff need capacity building training, 138 schools need recreational materials, there are 56,594 children~2% CwD affected in the 138 assessed schools, 95% of schools’ children need textbooks in formal education, 138 schools need support with teaching materials, 90% of the young children 3-5 age are unlikely to attend early childhood education due to lack of ECE centers (95% of school have no ECE), While early learning is not compulsory, due to its ability to enable children’s readiness for school and enhance life-long learning, it is a priority

5 - Implementation of activities

OB throughout 2022-2023 will provide partial/totally repair works in 138 schools including wash facilities based on safety, gender and CwDs inclusivity, provide school furniture based on the assessed need of each school, provide capacity training for the schools' staff and protection supervisors on a package of professional topics related to the INEE,CP, and EIE, conduct BTL campaigns in 15 areas by distribute Flyers/Brochures about education importance to encourage parents to bring their children back to schools, 138 schools’ PTAs will be established, the formal tutors (SIG curriculum) will start in Sep 2022 for 56,594 children 6-17 years in the 138 selected schools with provide PSS and recreational sessions until June 2023, pay incentives for 2593 staff in 138 schools, provide running costs including heating fuel during winter months, provide COVID-19 awareness and prevention materials, purchase and distribute school bags includes stationery for 65,594 children, in other hand 15 TLSs will be established in the selected locations to provide NFE ECE for 9000 children 3-5 years, with providing child safe environment including furniture and educational and recreational materials, ECE sessions will be started in Jul 2022 to Dec 2023 with providing running monthly costs including incentives for 165 TLSs’ staff, MEAL team will follow up the progress of the activities to ensure all achievements are in place with quality according to the work planned activities and log-frame matrix

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1 Violence against education facilities and providers Mitigate1 OB’s Safety and security department prepared contingency plan actions with evacuation plan with conducting regular drills for the children and staff, providing safety equipment (fire extinguishers and first aid kits), all attacks will be reported through MRM and cluster mechanisms Risk2 Violence, physical punishment, or bullying in schools Mitigate 2 OB keen to train all staff of schools and TLSs on COC to ensure the commitment, taking serious actions for misconduct cases, teachers and school personnel will be trained on prevention and reporting of type violenceRisk3: Students are not sufficiently protected from adults or older students in learning spaces Mitigate3 OB has Child Safeguarding policies and systems in place and this is regularly monitored and corrective action is ta when needed, make sure that all students and staff are aware of the feedback and complaint mechanismsRisk4: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) by aid workersMitigate 4 OB has PSEA policies in place, to cover standards of behavior for the OB’s staff and representatives, to address risks at the organizational level and the project level Risk5: Insufficiently safe learning facilities Mitigate1 OB will ensure the learning spaces safe and protected, Sufficient water and sanitation facilities will be maintained. WASH facilities will be sex-segregated and accessible to CWD, facilities will be kept clean, Covid-19 prevention measures


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: organisation’s Staff, volunteers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving.Mitigation and Prevention measures:1. all staff, volunteers and associated personnel sign the OB’s code of conduct which includes PSEA issues. 2. all staff, volunteers and associates receive induction and (where relevant) annual refresher training on PSEA and staff members are aware of their obligation to report SEA/misconduct.3. HR and relevant hiring bodies check employment history during interview and questions related to PSEA asked during interview,4. criminal records check is conducted where possible and new employees sign contracts which include PSEA and the organisation code of conduct, 5. complaints mechanisms developed to receive and respond to reports of SEA - CSG with train all OB's staff, employees, volunteers, and contractors on these mechanisms and tools to make sure they are aware how and when to use it.6. The awareness-raising sessions on PSEA will be conducting for children by classroom teachers or schools' management. In case any violates the CoC/CSG; OB will follow the investigation procedures in order to take the appropriate disciplinary actions based on OB’s CSG - PSEA policy.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Learning facilities near the M4 road mostly could be attacked by bombing or airstrike, Mitigation: OB safety and security field team will conduct trainings, awareness-raising, and drills appropriate for the age of the involved students in addition to the staff. Topics include self-care, first aid, mine risk, and unexploded ordinance awareness, fire drills, evacuation drills, and stay-and-shelter drills. COVID-19 pandemic:Mitigation: OB has developed policies to be followed due to COVID-19 including prevention measurements, conducting hygiene awareness, providing learning facilities with cleaning materials, dividing students into shifts in case the school is not able to apply the social distance between the students, in addition to following the recommendation of WoS re-opening schools.Re-Closure of learning facilities:Mitigation: Transform the physical learning to distance learning throughout OB learning platform, train the education staff on the distance learning modalities methods, and approaches.
