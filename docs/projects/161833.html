Project Summary

In line with NRC's WASH strategy for North East Nigeria aligned to the sector, the project's overall object aims at reducing the conflict affected population exposure to water and sanitation related illnesses by providing access to emergency WASH. The project will target Dikwa, Monguno, Jere, Mmagumeri, Nganzai and Konduga LGAs of Borno State and Mubi North in Adamawa. The project will have an emergency component, that will not be linked to any specific location but will be implemented within the two states (Borno and Adamawa).
WASH activities that will be implemented under this project will include construction or rehabilitation of boreholes, water points and temporary water storage facilities to ensure affected communities have access to safe water for drinking and addressing hygiene needs; construction of latrines, both shared household and communal latrines to curb the practice of open defecation; establishment of bathing shelters to compliment hygiene promotion initiatives that include provision of hygiene-related items , including the use of cash or vouchers for fostering good hygiene practices in order to mitigate the risk of waterborne disease outbreaks. The project will include a component of emergency response to epidemics such as cholera, flooding and any other climate induced or natural disasters. NRC will also seek to improve the efforts of coordinating the over all WASH sector response in the North East Nigeria.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in the most three affected states (Borno, Adamawa, Yobe) in Northeast Nigeria, remains precarious. The conflict provoked by Boko Haram resulted in widespread displacements, including movements across the border to seek for refuge in the neighbouring countries. . Since the start of the conflict in 2009, more than 27,000 people have been killed, thousands of women and girls abducted, and children recruited into organized armed groups. The 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) reports a total of 1.8 million people displaced internally. 7.1 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, 800,000 people living in inaccessible areas, 1.6 million people having returned to the original areas and 80% of the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) being women and children. To compound the situation further, over 60% of the total displaced persons reside within the host communities, making targeting difficult in order to reach out with assistance. Borno State hosts a significant number of IDPs, around 80% from the total displaced reside within the areas considered to be safe. Majority of these people resides either in camps that are overly congested (40%) or within towns such as Maiduguri and other garrison towns. With over 75% of the pre-conflict WASH infrastructure estimated to have been destroyed, the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) anticipates that approximately 3.6 million people (IDPs, returnees, and host communities) will be in need of WASH services. Displacement- affected populations in the three States of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe continue to live in deplorable WASH conditions. Due to continued movements into specific locations guarded by the military, congestion and unavailability of land for construction are two main challenges affecting proper WASH service delivery. Lack of proper WASH service has a negative impact of public health, household economies, and personal dignity. In conflict and displacement, girls/women are exposed to risks of sexual violence and harassment at water points and latrine facilities. Communities range from having no latrines to one latrine for many people. During safety audits carried out as part of the assessment, the audits noted no lighting in latrines and lack of gender segregation. Without accessible latrines, women and girls use the edges of the camps in the evenings in hopes of privacy, increasing their vulnerability to potential harm and abuse. Communities also noted tensions over water sources. Based on the situation in newly accessible areas, it is anticipated that the WASH needs in inaccessible areas will be very high. Cholera outbreaks have been reported year after year, since 2009. A large number of people in the Northeast still access water from open sources which are a known risk factor for Cholera epidemics, especially in north-eastern Nigeria. Nigeria has a high prevalence of Cholera and in the last 10 years, multiple large-scale outbreaks have been experienced in the North. Areas bordering the Lake Chad are particularly prone to these outbreaks. In 2019, a number of cases were reported from Adamawa state, and a few other unconfirmed suspected cases in Borno State. As at the end of September 2019, a total of 780 cases, with 4 deaths were reported from the cholera outbreak in Yola North, Yola South and Girei as at the time of writing this strategy, the outbreak is yet to be contained. Low WASH coverage coupled with the practice of open defection heightens the risk of waterborne disease and worsens malnutrition. This is further exacerbated by a lack of knowledge and poor hygiene and sanitation practices. Lack of accompanying hygiene supplies, due to constraints related to access to market and finances to purchase supplies by the target populations has also contributed to slow behavior change to adopt good hygiene practices.

2 - Activities

For this project, NRC will adopt a phased response, from emergency to a conventional sector response as outlined below;Emergency response: Activities that will be conducted under this phase will include pre-positioning essential WASH supplies/emergency package; emergency water supply through water trucking when other alternative water supply options are unavailable; setting up emergency sanitation facilities; and the distribution of essential hygiene items such as soap, detergent, and water treatment tablets, mass hygiene sensitization. quick fix of existing facilities, including desludging of full latrines in areas where there is no space to construct new emergency latrines to provide for access to sanitation will be included under emergency response phase Sector Response: Water supply- Activities includes new construction or rehabilitation of water points (boreholes), operation and maintenance of existing water points, water point chlorination, water quality monitoring, creation and training of community water management committees expanding the existing water systems and potential partnership with private water vendors to supply water in areas where access is difficult. Sanitation: NRC will aim at providing user-friendly and culturally acceptable latrines and bathrooms that are gender-segregated to prevent and mitigate the risk of potential GBV-related incidents, including harassment against women and girls. NRC has noted an issue of operation and maintenance for communal latrines, therefore, the aim will be to deliver family-shared latrines so as to reduce maintenance costs. All facilities constructed will be equipped with a cleaning kit and community mobilization will be done to ensure proper use and maintenance. Environmental sanitation activities will adopt more of a community-driven approach where the target populations will be provided with tools required for cleaning such as shovels, rakes, wheelbarrows, etc. Community mobilization on how to collect solid waste and dispose of it appropriately will be done by hygiene promoters, engaged 5 days a week. NRC will also organize quarterly clean up campaigns. Hygiene Promotion: Hygiene promotion will adopt contextualized approaches that address the main public health risks and promote positive hygiene practices. Contextualized Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials, as agreed with the hygiene promotion technical working group, addressing the key risks will be developed and adopted to reinforce the messages. NRC in 2020 will pilot the use of WaSH EM tools to stimulate behavior change for hand washing. Other approaches that will be adopted will include door to door visits, Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation amongst other approaches. Specific sessions will also be done on menstrual hygiene, especially for women and adolescent girls. One common risky practice noted in the camps and other settlements is open defecation, due to shortage/ lack of proper maintenance of sanitation facilities. NRC will pilot the use of Community Led Total Sanitation to trigger communities to make use of the locally available materials to construct their own latrines as well as using these facilities properly. As a result of the multiple risky hygiene behaviours to be targeted, at least 3 key messages at nay given time.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

NRC will establish a complaint and feedback mechanism in order to cater for the needs of the most vulnerable households. Inclusion of the less vulnerable will start from the beneficiary selection stage. The following process will follow;NRC will form a Beneficiary Selection Committee (BSC). The beneficiary selection committee will constitute both genders, person representing people living with disabilities, youth representatives and person representing minority groups. The BSC will have a clear terms of reference and will contribute to refining the selection criteria. NRC will moderate the discussions.Beneficiary selection shall ensure that the needs for the most vulnerable are met. Once the selection is done, the BSC will also be charged with the responsibility of ensuring that the target beneficiaries have their entitlements.In order to allow for beneficiaries to receive appropriate feedback to their complaints, NRC will establish a complaint and feedback mechanism. A hot line, through which the complaints will be channelled will be put in place and shared with staff. There will also be a Complaint desk during distributions. All the complaints shall be addressed on a timely manner.NRC will also ensure that the project meet the needs for the different groups within the target population. This will be done through holding separate focus group discussions with men, women, girls, boys, elderly, disabled and other minority groups, focusing on what their priority and needs are when it comes to the implementation of this project.NRC will hold periodic meetings with the BSC and will also ensure that the main meeting, giving feedback on the output of the project is done before handing over the facilities to the communities.
