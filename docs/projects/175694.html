Project Summary

South Sudan remain hard to reach areas for humanitarian partners and implementation of humanitarian services, the signing of the revitalized peace agreement in September, 2018 on the conflicts resolution of South Sudan which has last for three (03) years, and its implementation has not reduced the sufferings and the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people and this is couple with lack of durable peace which has affected the entire country and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development.
According assessment conducted by REACH coverage of Jonglei state in April-September 2020 on the situation overview of the humanitarian the presence of internally displaced persons (IDPs) remained stable this quarter (60% in March). This is probably related to the continuation of protracted inter communal clashes. Food security deteriorated further during the reporting period, with just 49% of assessed settlements reporting adequate access to food in March (68% in December 2019). This is likely a consequence of severe flooding which appears to have brought forward the onset of the lean season from March to January. Since early July 2020, flooding, caused by atypical rainfall, runoff from surrounding highlands and the overflow of major rivers, caused mass displacement, particularly from low lying and riverine areas in the counties of Fangak, Ayod, Duk, Twic East, and Bor South. According to FGD participants, as much as 60-70% of the population in Duk13 and Twic East14 counties, and almost all of the population in Jalle and Baidit payams15 (Bor South county), had displaced to Bor town, and area hosted in to four (04) (Pabial/ Bor stadium, Tara-Agok, Malual Agar-Baar and Kondaai also numbers of displaced population moved to Mangalla or Mingkaman temporary created in to IDP sites, at the time of data collection. Many of those who did not displace to the south were vulnerable households that did not have plastic sheeting to carry food or goods for the journey and/or household members with specific needs and reduced mobility, such as children and elderly. These populations were largely displaced to areas along the Jonglei Canal, such as Panyagor (Twic East County), and Duk Padiet, Puktap, and Panaru (Duk County). With decreased access to food following a year’s worth of shocks and livelihood disruptions. the protection situation in some parts of counties in Jonglei, Upper and Central Equatoria appeared to have worsened in March-December, 2020, likely due to an increase in inter communal violence and floods affected area. Also the report has indicate that water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) needs remained high and may be further exacerbated by COVID-19 as reported barriers to accessing clean water sources and functional healthcare services pose challenges to both the widespread implementation of World Health Organization (WHO) 4 hand washing practices and the accessibility of healthcare services. Access to reliable shelter seemingly varied by population group: 24% of assessed settlements reported the main shelter type for IDPs to be less solid structures, such as rakoobas and these is both host and IDP sites.
Melut IRNA reported dated from 12-13/January 2021 by the team of humanitarian partners indicated that, the current security situation and life challenges experienced by IDPs are raising serious protection concerns and human rights violations such as restriction of movement, access to basic services and economic, social and cultural rights. Situation of IDPs is further worsened by the hiking market prices and basic goods which is not affordable by most of the IDPs, the IDPs complain about lack of basic services to some elderlies died because of hunger according to IDP leaders. This forces IDPs to undertake negative coping mechanisms with increased cases of child labor in the markets, reduction of food ration, and increase in crime/theft cases, SGBV risks.

1 - Needs

The joined bio-metric registration conducted November, 2021 (IOM/WFP) in Bor South indicated that Households of 7,720 with 32,015 individuals are continue to shelter in Bor South from five IDPs sites and these are (Tara-Agok=13,843 individuals, Bor Stadium=3,251 individuals, Kondaai=12,695 individuals, Malual Aguarbaar=2,226 individuals and including the former Bor PoC with total population of 2,017 individuals), the affected population in Bor South still without much support from the agencies to meet the most needed live saving needs such us food, NFI, health/nutrition and protection and general humanitarian coordination and services monitoring. In addition, due to inter-communal violence in Jonglei, The current security situation is life challenges experienced by the IDPs which is rising serious protection concerns and human rights violations such as restriction of movement, access to basic services and economic, social and cultural rights, floods still life threatening in some parts of the state especial areas along the river Nile such as Jalle, , Pakeer, Twic East and Duk where most of Bor IDP originated and this situation of floods could not allow the returns of the IDPs, therefore a significant figures of IDPs are living with host communities, straining both communities’ ability to cope. Sources (Humanitarian needs overview South Sudan, issued January 2021) In order to meet their needs, some displaced families opt for negative coping mechanisms, including child labor and early marriage. Early marriage among IDP children is more prevalent than in recent years. The COVID-19 pandemic influenced households’ resilience in positive and negative terms. On the one hand, people’s access to basic services, especially health and safe water, improved from last year following the investments made to respond to and prevent COVID19. On the other hand, COVID-19 related restrictions led to a significant reduction in people’s participation in social groups and affected their access to essential services such as community-based protection, and capacity and resilience building activities which previously had a positive contribution to households’ resilience. in conclusion the above outline needs are the most challenging and are hindering both humanitarian partners and beneficiaries participation to provide the most needed assistance, in turns affecting the most vulnerable displaced community in Bor South, require immediate humanitarian intervention through assistance to avert the continue suffering of the population of IDP in Bor South.
