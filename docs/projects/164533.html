Project Summary

The fund will be utilized to providing the irreplaceable food items needed by distributing the food vouchers, which will contribute to supply these items for the IDPs and host in the targeted locations in Idlep and Aleppo Governorates.
Silk Road will contribute $ 1,100,000 $ over a ten-month period, which will enable food assistance through monthly cash vouchers to extremely vulnerable urban households in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates.
Some 4500 households (about 27,000 individuals), consisting mainly of poor IDPs women and households headed by the women, will benefit from the project. Each monthly voucher is worth US$ 50 and can be exchanged for food items in selected local shops. The implementation plan is based on the distribution of monthly vouchers to beneficiary families so that each family will benefit from four consecutive months of vouchers program, in other words, 2250 families will benefit for four months and then we will target a new 2250 families in the next four months.
For the 4500 targeted families, the vouchers mean guaranteed access to food every month. Beyond that, the project also gives them the chance to choose which foods will best meet their families’ needs.
For local sellers, the vouchers are creating hundreds of new customers, most of whom previously had difficulties affording to buy food in their shops.
Silk Road has developed a strong plan for the implementation, monitoring and evaluation of this program will start by pre-assessment of the target population aims to collect a quantitative data focused on project indicators (measuring all project indicators prior to intervention) and that will tack a place in the first month of the implementation.
In the next phase, Silk Road will select the beneficiaries of the program and the participating sellers as well also we will do verification of the selected beneficiaries. We will use clear and logical selection criteria in partnership and cooperation with the community (Through focus groups discussion that will be held) in order to ensure that all families understand why some were selected and why some other families were not eligible. We will use a separate team to verify the extent to beneficiaries meet criteria to ensure transparency and objectivity in selection.
The priority will be given for:
1. Newly IDPs families.
2. Women-headed households.
3. Families with large size above six members and do not have a breadwinner.
4. Families with low income.
Criteria for selecting Sellers:
1. Seller's ability to re-supply goods
2. The diversity of goods in the market
3. The accessibility of beneficiaries to the market
4. Number of days and hours working
5. The reputation of the seller in the region
6. Fees or commissions payable by the Organization if any
7. Cooperation with the organization and the seller's willingness to accept the terms and conditions of the payments
Also, within the first month of the project, we will conduct the sealers capacity assessment include the frequency of re-supply of goods focusing on the goods we plan to distribute, the availability of a warehouse close to the shop, notes by the team on the seller's skills in dealing with customers as well as the number of customers who came during the evaluation period, reputation and seller's interest in participating in the program and finally the financial capacity of the seller. Furthermore, we will update the assessment of prices, the current situation of the local market and the most requested goods by local population before identifying sellers.
In this project, we will use unconditional vouchers (Food for a fixed monetary value (value-based vouchers) in the selected shop) that allow sufficient flexibility for the beneficiaries, where they can buy whatever they need from the list of the items included in the voucher according to the voucher amount.

4 - Needs

An estimated 7.9 million Syrian people are thought to be food Insecure with an additional 1.9 million at risk of food insecurity - Physical and Mental Wellbeing. According to need assessment conducted by Silk Road, we found that the most affected groups are children, people with disability and women, while the percentage of children is 27%, PWD is 20% Elderly 21%, Women 20% and Men 13% as shown in the figure below.The same assessment proofed that IDP’s remain with a high and urgent need to a food security support, IDP’s to this area came from Homs, Hama, Aleppo and Idleb. The chances to reach basic food items are through reaching the market and buying it directly but lack of economic resources remains a problem to get all basic food items for IDP’s families in general and for Women and Boys House headed due to the very limited source of income and suitable work and fund opportunities. The most items needed for displaced people in the assessed areas were; 32% bread,28% dairy products, 25% vegetables, 10% cereals, 4% meat and 1% fruits.The main challenges face’s IDP’s to reach basic food requirementsLack of economic resources and work choices.High averages of basic food items prices.Lack of work opportunities that suit the women house headed families which make this category of IDP’s with the lowest chances of funding and most needy.Lack of external cash support to reach basic food items and source of decent living.According to the market assessment conducted by SRO team, the main factors that limit local sellers' ability to increase their supply of goods are: • High prices of the items from the main sources.• Security Situation in the area in these days which leads to movement a large numberof populations from the community.• High USD exchange rate in Syria.• Lack of demand for goods due to the high prices and Unemployment. • transportation /importation of goods, 60% come from Turkey while 40% from inside Syria.
