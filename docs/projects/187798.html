Project Summary

Over the years, the prevalence of GBV cases in NES has been high as a consequence of the conflict and the COVID-19 effects. The displacement and the harmful gender norms and stringent patriarchal societies that enable GBV have played a greater role in escalating effects to women and girls. The risk of gender-based violence for women and girls has increased dramatically due to the armed conflict and displacement, the subsequent breakdown of law and the erosion of social structures and norms, as well as limited access to basic formal and informal protection mechanisms. According to an overview of Gender-Based Violence in Syria (Advocacy Brief / 2022), reports of sexual harassment, early/forced marriage, and domestic violence (including intimate partner violence and other forms of family violence against women and girls) continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home. In addition, the changes in traditional gender roles and responsibilities within the families, due to the displacement and the current living conditions, resulted in an increased level of stress and tensions within the families leading to an increase of GBV episodes. Additionally, the conflict and multiple displacements, which contribute to poverty, desperation, and unfamiliarity with legal systems, are significant factors increasing the vulnerability of women and girls. Women are more vulnerable to violence and adolescents’ risk early and forced marriage; in addition to a growing number of children who lose one or both parents due to random shelling and displacement. These have a long-term negative impact on women, children, and their families’ psychosocial wellbeing. Accordingly, from the total number of beneficiaries the organization targets, 70% are women and girls, who are provided with GBV services. GBV services will include the provision of GBV case management, provided within exclusive women safe spaces within or adjacent to health facilities. The NGO will coordinate and strengthen referral pathways with health, mental health, and other partners to provide comprehensive services to survivors. The NGO will also include 30% males in Engaging Men in Accountable Practices (EMAP) and deliver structured PSS activities for women and girls to allow survivors to identify pathways to raise their confidence, relieve stress, build up their social network and develop positive coping mechanisms. The organization will also offer MH services to the population affected by different mental health conditions. MHPSS activities will provide a wide range of services that will prevent, assess, and treat MNS disorders and promote psychosocial resiliency for the affected population in NES. The services will include mhGAP consultations, which will be linked with comprehensive case management services, psychoeducation, community outreach services, mhGAP detection and referral, capacity building, and structured group support. There will also be upscaling evidence-based interventions through training of trainers and providers, who will implement to beneficiaries to reduce mild and moderate mental conditions. The project will integrate MH services into primary healthcare facilities with the intention of better accessibility of services to all community members regardless of age, gender or impairment and other vulnerable population.
The organization also includes 21% of PWD in the total target. Services will be tailored to meet beneficiaries’ needs depending on the type of disability, while online sessions will be conducted, and the mobile outreach team will conduct activities targeting the beneficiaries’ communities and providing referrals when necessary. Data will also be disaggregated to further capture people with disabilities. We will also work closely with Handicap International to refer people for services that we do not provide.

4 - Needs

The Voices from Syria 2022, and assessment findings of Humanitarian Needs Overview, shows women and girls across NES were subjected to physical violence, psychological and emotional violence, sexual violence, social violence, forced and early marriage, systemic denial of economic resources and education, movement restrictions, and exploitation at work. Also, women and girls who face multiple and intersecting forms of marginalization due to their gender, age, disability, and displacement status face compounded GBV risks and additional barriers when accessing support and services. Despite increasing needs for GBV risk mitigation and response services, NES is currently facing decreased donor funding. More structured community-level GBV prevention mechanisms and other community-based risk mitigation measures are needed. Due to the Syrian conflict, people have experienced major known risk factors for poor mental health, including exposure to traumatic events, forced displacement and ongoing stressors such as unemployment, impoverishment, social dislocation, and loss of social support. The data show that nearly half of all Syrian children show symptoms of post-traumatic stress disorder, and a quarter face intellectual and developmental challenges. Children become highly susceptible to depression, anxiety, and other forms of mental illness in the short term when living in conflict zones. These challenges often act as barriers to physical health, education, and economic stability.

5 - Implementation of activities

Different GBV and PSS services will be provided through an integrated model in Raqqa, Deir ez Zoor, Al Hassakeh, and Aleppo governorates. GBV services will include case management for adults, caring for child survivors of sexual abuse, and safe referrals to other multi-sectoral service providers. The WGSS will engage women and girls in recreational activities. Adolescent girl's services will be provided based on Iraq UNICEF toolkit. GBV Prevention engages men in accountable practices (EMAP). The program will regularly update service maps for referral pathways. GBV risk mitigation activities include joint safety audits, FGDS and other safety mapping activities. MHPSS services will be integrated into primary healthcare through mhGAP consultations, as trained mhGAP doctors will assess, diagnose, and treat patients with different MNS conditions. The patients will also be offered sessions on prevention measures to avoid deterioration of MNS conditions. Beneficiaries will be provided with psychoeducation sessions on several topics. Peer groups will offer therapeutic healing groups. Community-based outreach activities will be facilitated through community sessions to respond to community needs. Various training will be conducted for health, mental health, and GBV staff. Also, Participating in different Cluster meetings. MEAL team carry out routine monitoring activities such as GBV facility check list, beneficiary satisfaction survey, client satisfaction survey, training monitoring

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Women and girls do not attend centers because of family constraints or social gender norms, and unmarried girls do not want to access SRH services. The program will conduct community outreach activities to engage with the local population and ensure understanding and acceptance of the services provided. The WGSS will be staffed exclusively by women, making it easier for women and girls to attend the centers without any reservations. Reporting GBV incidents will be encouraged by building an atmosphere of trust and safety in the centers by delivering regular women and girl-focused activities and integrating GBV services in the WGSS. The NES context has a high degree of poverty that may make clients resort to commercial sex, sex for food, and other related abuses to security needs. In addition, the risk of violence/harm toward staff during PSS sessions-certain clients may have mental disturbances that can lead to staff abuse. This can happen due to a lack of income to most mental patients and thus demand financial support from the case managers, who are not in a position to help. To mitigate the situation, MHPSS staff will receive PSEA training and know-how and when to report suspected exploitation cases. MHPSS team will employ an equal or similar number of male and female staff. Staff will work in pairs in the community. Each MHPSS staff member will have a mobile phone with them also and check-in with their team leader regularly.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Beneficiaries seeking special treatment to access services: Beneficiaries will be told that the NGO does not charge for services and any engagement. -Dual relationship between helpers and clients: The clients may become emotionally entangled to the client thus making a window for exploitation. To mitigate this all staff shall be trained on PSEA and disciplinary measures will be employed in case of any violation. MEAL team has set up Community Based Feedback Mechanism (CBFRM) in all project implementation locations and seeks views of the affected population to provide us with their feedback on our interventions and establish community trust. MEAL team regularly conducts community consultations with affected people to know what their preference way is to raise their feedback/complaints through interviews. The MEAL team has different safe, accessible, and available channels such as Hotline number or text, WhatsApp, Complaint's box, Feedback Email and face to face with MEAL team on the ground.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

*Risk of Border closure due to weather or political, military or security situation, the risk is mitigated through have the flexibility of intended departure dates and further travel from the region, market survey for domestic procurement and look at longer-term program needs to consider larger deliveries and higher storage to take opportunities to travel when in an ad hoc/ sporadic situation. *Risk of worsening COVID 19 cases, the risk is mitigated through placing a remote implementation approach shall be adopted to ensure that MH clients are appropriately supported in cases where the facilities are suspended.*Risk of Government of Syria takeover or embedding in the health facilities supported by NGO, the risk is mitigated through Emplace red lines and closely follow the GOS actions and discus its implications with the health committee. The NGO will turn to the donor and seek approval regarding the continuation/adaptation of the project. *Risk of security disruption escalation of internal conflicts, forcing cessation of service delivery the risk is mitigated through, the risk is mitigated by placing a contingency such disruptions, where MH beneficiaries will be provided some additional stock of medication if deemed safe by the psychiatrist or mhGAP Doctor. Change of military/political control of geographic locations where the NGO implements programs; the risks a mitigated through maintaining strong lines of communication with global and regional security.
