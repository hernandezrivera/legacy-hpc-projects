Project Summary

UNH a dual mandated organization that provides development & emergency response. Based on our experience in emergency response, developed this project following consultative meetings with the respective state MOH, CHDs, local authority & affected population including elderly & PWD. UNH has operations presence in Jonglei, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile States & Greater Pibor Administrative Area providing essential health services.
UNH’s HNRP for 2025 targets Pibor, Pochalla, Renk, Tonj North & Twic targeting returnees, IDPs & vulnerable host communities. These areas are mainly inhabited by pastoralists, prone to conflicts from cattle raiding, land disputes, interclan/revenge attacks resulting to insecurity, displacement, inaccessibility & humanitarian crisis. Moreso areas bordering Sudan, Renk, Twic are receiving a lot of people from Sudan due to Sudan crisis since April 2023, Greater Pibor also receiving returnees from Ethiopia.
Health needs of Pibor, Pochalla, Renk, Tonj North & Twic enormous, complicated with contracted economy, conflicts, inaccessibility, lack of water quality control, IPC measures, food insecurity, flood, threat of disease outbreaks, limited health facility functionality, stockouts of essential medicines. Cholera outbreak already declared in Renk, risk of transportation to other counties high due to the movement of returnees. 2024 DHIS2 analysis for GPAA, Twic, Tonj North & Renk shows malaria is the top cause of morbidity followed by ARI & AWD. Health needs of the beneficiaries include BeMONC, EPI, treatments for malaria, ARIs, AWD, MHPSS, GBV, services for PwD, positive SBC messages.
Severe food insecurity coupled with Sudan crisis, subnational violences, contracted economy, effect of disease outbreaks, weak healthcare system will drive malnutrition rate & health indicators in these counties will deteriorate if no emergency health services supported.
In 2025, UNH will provide emergency and lifesaving health services through PHCCs/PHCUs, mobile clinics to the most vulnerable population. Will implement following emergency and lifesaving activities which are in line with health cluster specific objectives & will contribute to the HNRP objectives.
• Implement emergency preparedness & contingency planning for epidemic prone diseases including Cholera, Mpox
• Provide OPD consultations for major cause of morbidity/mortality
• Conduct mobile EPI services.
• Provide BeMONC services
• Provide MHPSS/CMR services.
• Provide services for PWD
• Provide emergency kits to the health facilities.
• Conduct community consultative meetings
• Support regular meetings with CHDs
• Conduct meetings with humanitarian, development & peace partners.
• Conduct quarterly community health events at each health facility for SBC
• Conduct quarterly suportive supervisions.
• Participate in Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) that will be conducted in UNH operational areas in 2025.
• Strengthen IPC in our health facilities
• Provide management for SAM children with medical complication.
• Support Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care.
Direct project beneficiaries 100,000 beneficiaries (9,928 M U5; 8,307 F U5; 19,474 M 6-17 years; 18,519 F 6-17 years; 16,930 M 18-59 years; 19,139 F 18-59 years; 3,342 M >60 years; 4,361 F > 60 years). Out this, 15,00 are PWD (1,490 M<5; 1,246 F<5; 2,922 M 6-17years; 2,778 F 6-17yaers; 2,539 M 18-59 years, 2,870 F 18-59 years; 501 M>60 years, 654 F> 60 years).
To implement those activities, UNH needs 1,500,000 USD in 2025, aligned with health cluster’s strategy on cost per beneficiaries. Will work with other health, Nutrition/WASH/FSL/Protection partners to enhance synergies, will engage CHD & development health partners in each county to take over at the end of the project to ensure sustainability.

1. Needs

November 2024 Health Cluster PIN estimated 5,657,327 people in South Sudan/Abyei will need humanitarian health services in 2025, population targeted for response 3,089,736. UNH will response in Pibor, Pochalla, Renk, Tonj North & Twic targeting 17,387, 7,246, 30,250, 16,536 & 28,581 respectively mainly IDPs, returnees, vulnerable host communities. Inhabitants of targeted counties mainly pastoralists, prone to conflicts due cattle raiding, land disputes, revenge attack leading to insecurity, displacement, inaccessibility, humanitarian crisis. HSTP supports only 14 HFs in GPAA out of 36, 22 in Twic out of 35, 14 in Tonj North out of 18, 11 in Renk out of 25. Non supported HFs are nonfunctional, some supported operate at minimum capacity due low level of healthcare professional, poor infrastructure leading to significant gaps in health service delivery especially in hard-to-reach areas. These counties have been devastated by flood & subnational violence for past 3 consecutive years. The counties have significant number of returnees due to Sudan crisis, number expected to increase in 2025, in Renk number expected to be 176,607 from 20,396, Twic to 13,026 from 7,242. This will exert more pressure on the limited existing health services increasing the vulnerabilities of the returnees, IDPS, most vulnerable host population especially children, women, PWD, elderly. Cholera outbreak declared in Renk, risk of transportation to other counties very high due to the movement of the returnees. DHIS2 analysis, GPAA malaria accounts for 45.9%, ARI 16.8%, AWD 9.8%; Twic malaria 53.1%, ARI 21.3%, AWD 6.3%, Tonj North, malaria 53.8%, ARI 23.2%, AWD 9.5%; Renk malaria 51.7%, ARI 29.6%, AWD 7.5%. IPC published on November 11, 2023 puts Pibor, Renk, Twic in IPC4 (Emergency) & Tonj North/Pochalla in IPC 3 for period April to July 2024, returnees in IPC5 (Catastrophe) hence all these counties have small proportion of population in IPC 5.HMIS exist in all counties, reporting progressively improving, malaria, AWD & ARI remain the major cause of morbidity & mortality. IDSR week 43, 2024, Twic was at 57% of completeness of reporting. Health needs of Pibor, Pochalla, Renk, Tonj North, Twic are enormous, complicated with contracted economy, conflicts, inaccessibility, lack of water quality control, IPC measures, food insecurity, flood, threat of disease outbreaks, limited health facility functionality, stockouts of essential medicines. Health needs of the beneficiaries include BeMONC, EPI, treatments for malaria, ARIs, AWD, MHPSS, GBV, services for PwD, positive SBC messages.Severe food insecurity will have more impact on children, PLW. Poor maternal nutrition is associated with adverse pregnancy/birth outcomes, food insecurity, violence, erratic population displacement, inadequate public health services increase the risk of disease outbreaks among undernourished population, are major cause of excess morbidity/mortality.UNH will provide emergency Healthcare targeting 100,000 beneficiaries (9,928 M U5; 8,307 F U5; 19,474 M 6-17 years; 18,519 F 6-17 years; 16,930 M 18-59 years; 19,139 F 18-59 years; 3,342 M >60 years; 4,361 F > 60 years). Out this, 15,00 are PWD (1,490 M<5; 1,246 F<5; 2,922 M 6-17years; 2,778 F 6-17yaers; 2,539 M 18-59 years, 2,870 F 18-59 years; 501 M>60 years, 654 F> 60 years).

2. Project activities

UNH has aligned its emergency health activities to MOH Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services and Health cluster humanitarian health services, proposed to implement the following activities in 2025;• Implement emergency preparedness and contingency planning for epidemic prone diseases, disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, alerts reporting and verification within 24 hours, outbreak investigations and case management for selected diseases including Cholera, Mpox, measles.• Provide OPD consultations focusing on major cause of morbidity, mortality and epidemic prone diseases, malaria, ARIs, AWD, measles, ABO.• Provide routine Immunization at mobile/static facilities against vaccine preventable diseases, strengthen capacity of health workers on EPI through training, support reactive measles vaccination of boys/girls between 6 months – 15 years.• Build the capacity of health workers on BeMONC and provide BeMONC services in our health facilities to enhance deliveries assisted by skilled birth attendants. • Provide life-saving essential quality health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through static health facilities & mobile clinics & build the capacity of health workers on provision of lifesaving essential health care.• Integrate & provide MHPSS services. • Integrate & provide services for SGBV including CMR, enhance referral to safe houses where necessary. • Provide services for people with disability, rehabilitation services disaggregated by age and sex.• Coordinate with core pipeline managers to get emergency kits (WHO Interagency emergency kits, UNFPA RH Commodities including Rape kits; cold chain equipment including vaccines from UNICEF) to support our health facilities.• Conduct community consultative meetings to identify needs of affected populations & strengthen feedback mechanism at the health facilities to ensure beneficiaries provide feedback on the project.• Scaleup human-centred design for demand generation for health services. Will conduct focus group discussions with health workers, community/youth/women/religious leaders, chiefs, care givers, influencers & patients to derive context specific human centred design solutions to increase uptake of services such as immunization, MNCH, nutrition, WASH, GBV. The derived messages will be integrated into routine positive Social and Behavioural Change (SBC) messages. • Support & strengthen regular meetings with the County Health Department.• Conduct meetings with humanitarian, development & peace partners.• Conduct quarterly community health events at each health facility for behavioural change communication.• Conduct community sensitization & disseminate IEC materials include those for Mpox, Cholera etcs.• Provide technical & operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervisions.• Provide community health services using BHI & strengthen the linkage with facility-based services. • Participate in Rapid Response Mechanism that will be conducted in UNH operational areas in 2025. • Strengthen IPC in our health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities.• Participate in the training on Rationale use of essential health care commodities.• Conduct MUAC screening for children under 5 & PLW to identify MAM and SAM cases and refer to TSFP and OTP centres respectively. • Provide management for SAM children with medical complication in our PHCC and Hospitals.• Support the Surveillance System for Attacks (SSA) on Health Care and provide regular report on the attacks. • Support and submit timely, accurate and complete reports through EWARS, IDSR and 5Ws. • Coordinate the health response with Health Clusters, MoH at all levels, CHDs, partners including UN agencies, national & international NGOs to enhance synergies.
