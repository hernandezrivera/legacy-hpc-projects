Project Summary

In Somalia an estimated 6.6 1 million people, about 42 per cent of the Somali population are in need of emergency life-saving WASH assistance of which 4.2 million will need safe drinking water, 3.2 million sanitation services, and 5.2 million hygiene supplies. 16 per cent of these are females, 64% children and the elderly for 4 per cent. This presents an increase of 43 per cent from 2021, in which 4.6 million people were estimated to be in need of WASH services across Somalia. Severe drought condition, seasonal flood, diseases outbreak and conflict related displacement are contributing to the increase in the number of people in need. The population in need of WASH services in 2022 includes approximately 1,619,415 IDPs and 4,818,944 vulnerable host communities. About 6.1 million people are in the Severe category” who lack access to safe water, proper sanitation services and are highly vulnerable to Acute Water Diarrheal (AWD) and cholera while around 379,757 people (2 percent) are in “Extreme condition as they access water directly from rivers and surface bonds, practice open defecation and lack access to soap and other essential hygiene materials and are susceptible to communicable diseases. Among other people in need are 965,754 (15 per cent) of persons with disabilities and 254,465 (4 per cent) of elderly persons who face the risk of exclusion from assistance due to the barriers they face when accessing WASH services. According to WASH Cluster disability inclusion assessment 40 per cent of females and 75 per cent of males3 with disability never collected water for their household due to distance and in-accessibility of water points and rely on neighbors and children to collect water for them.
Significant pasture degradation and water shortages have been reported in many areas of Jubaland particularly Afmadow and Kismayo due to two consecutive seasons of below average rainfall. Crops are failing, substantial livestock mortality is occurring, raising food prices. Currently, water shortages are alarming in the two district affecting a high number of already vulnerable people, many of whom are IDPs. Pastoralist families across the districts are moving in search of water for their animals, particularly in the areas bordering Kenya.
Water prices have increased in most settlements in the district. A quick market spot check conducted on key water points in Afmadow towns by WASDA team revealed that the average price of a 20- litter jerry can of water in the main water points in these towns was 8,000 Somali Shillings, an equivalent to USD 0.3.
According to OCHA’s most recent report on the drought conditions in Afmadow district (25th October 2021), the majority of districts in Jubaland state are facing severe drought con
It is against this background that WASDA is proposing to provide life-saving WASH assistance to drought and conflict affected communities in Afmadow and Kismayo districts Lower Juba Somalia. The specific activities to be undertaken under this intervention are emergency water trucking to settlements with severe water crisis, construction/rehabilitation strategic water sources (6 hand-dug-shallow wells and 2 two boreholes), formation and training of WMCs on O&M, financial management and internal governance, water quality control and monitoring, fuel subsidy. To improve access to sanitation services, the project will construct 200 gender and disability sensitive latrines with hand-washing station for communities and public institutions. The project will also deliver hygiene gender sanitize hygiene kits to 1,000 vulnerable households affected by drought and carry out hygiene promotion campaigns in the target districts of Kismayo and Afmadow. The hygiene promotion campaigns will be carried through CHVs who will be trained to conduct hygiene awareness.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1: Emergency water trucking to 12 settlements with severe water crisis – WASDA will provide emergency water trucking to 1,000 households in 12 settlements under Afmadow and Kismayo districts affected by affected by drought as a contingency to respond to immediate water needs. The targeted households will receive 7.5 liters of water per day person for their water needs as per the WASH cluster and SPHERE standard recommendations. Activity 1.2: Construction and rehabilitation strategic water sources (6 hand-dug-shallow wells and 2 two boreholes) - through this project, WASDA will rehabilitation 6 hand-dug shallow wells and two boreholes in the target districts. The rehabilitation of the shallow wells will involve deepening, removal of debris, installation culverts, construction of well aprons, installation of hand pump and fencing with chain link and metal posts. On the other hand, the borehole rehabilitation work will mainly involve construction of concrete elevated tanks, construction/rehabilitation of water troughs and water kiosks, replacement and extension of pipeline systems. Activity 1.3: Formation and training of WMCs on O&M, financial management and internal governance – WASDA will form and train water management committees comprising of a total of 5 (2 women and 3 men) in the 8 water points targeted for rehabilitation under this proposed project. The training will involve O&M, internal governance, financial management and record keeping, sanitation and hygiene, conflict resolution, tariff setting as well as communication skills. Activity 1.4: Water quality control and monitoring – Throughout the project period, WASDA will increase access to safe and clean drinking water through water treatment at the source and point use by providing water treatment chemicals and community sensitization on water treatment. Activity 1.5: Fuel subsidy – WASDA will also provide fuel subsidies to 2 of the strategically located and highly populated boreholes targeted for rehabilitation in the target district to cushion drought-stricken communities and their livestock from the high cost of water. Each of the target boreholes will receive 48 liters of fuel per day for a period of three months. WASDA will sign memorandum of understanding with the WMCs on the use and management of the fuel subsidy. Water will be provided to the pastoralist households at subsidized rates. Activity 1.6: Construction of 200 gender and disability sensitive shared latrines with hand-washing station - To provide adequate and safe access to sanitation facilities, the project twill construct 200 gender and disability sensitive ventilated improved pit latrines with hand washing stations for IDPs and vulnerable host communities in the target districts. A total of 800 vulnerable households will benefit from the latrines. Activity 1.7: Provision of gender sensitive hygiene kits to 500 vulnerable households – WASDA will procure and distribute hygiene kits to 500 IDPs and vulnerable host communities in the target districts. The content of the hygiene kits will include re-usable sanitary pads, pants, non-collapsible jerrycans, bathing soaps, laundry soaps, aqua tabs and face masks. Activity 1.8: Hygiene promotion campaigns – WASDA will conduct training for 20 community hygiene volunteers to carry out hygiene promotion and sanitization awareness campaigns through public address system and household visit in the target districts of Kismayo and Afmadow. WASDA will also visit schools and madrasas to sensitize learners on best hygiene and sanitation practices and COVID 19 prevention awareness. The hygiene promotion will be carried in all the sites where the emergency water trucking and rehabilitation of water sources and sanitation services are provided.
