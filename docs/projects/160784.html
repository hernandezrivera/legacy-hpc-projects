Project Summary

This provision of sustainable WASH services and GBV mitigation to vulnerable Returnees, IDPs and Host Communities in Ikotos and Kapoeta South Counties of Eastern Equatoria State in South Sudan. The project duration will be one year from January to December 2020. The project will serve 6,500 Households, 3,500 in Ikotos County and 2,500 in Kapoeta South County. The project will adopt a year-round programming with main season activities involving technical and practical support for mainstreaming with WASH cluster in target locations, risk mitigation activities including GBV safety audits and GBV mainstreaming in WASH, Women’s leadership & confidence building training especially for those participating in water management committees and being trained as pump mechanics, community mobilization and capacity building of community structures and other GBV partners to prevent and respond to GBV victims, strengthening referral pathways including conducting workshops with partners to support the GBV sub-cluster, provision of GBV Case Management and individual psycho-social support, strengthening of clinical management of rape (CMR) services, distribution of dignity kits, provision of psycho-social support focusing on community-based and non-specialized support through women and girl’s existing groups, provision of livelihoods for GBV survivors and those at risk, awareness raising and campaigns on GBV consequences, available services and risks, organizing GBV prevention dialogues and activities to change harmful social norms causing GBV. The project will have three main components, GBV mainstreaming in WASH, GBV prevention and response. Cross-cutting issues including disability, social inclusion, environmental protection, accountability to affected populations (AAP), protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), gender balance among staff, staff welfare and respectful working environment will also be addressed. Collaboration and integration will be key pillars of this project. Working in collaboration with other stakeholders will reduce duplication. Integration with other sectors including nutrition, child protection and Food security and livelihoods is meant to ensure greater impact at household level. The project also aims at ensuring gender parity by providing both men and women an equal opportunity to participate in project activities. Right from inception, community involvement will be pivotal to project implementation to ensure transparency, accountability but also project sustainability through community localization approach.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan remains one of the largest and most volatile in the world, with a complex reality, rapidly changing vulnerabilities, and serious economic problems. Since the outbreak of the civil conflict in South Sudan in December 2013, an estimated total of 1.9 million people have been forced to become IDPs, 2.3 million as refugees to neighboring countries, 202,776 seeking shelter within the UN Compound 2.1 million acutely malnourished women and children. The majority of these war affected persons are vulnerable children, women, elderly and youth. Ikotos County was one of the most conflict-affected county where out 101,982 population over 75% of the population left and become refugees in the neighboring Country of Uganda. However, after the signing of the R-ARCSS Agreement in September, 2018 and return of relative in the Country, an estimated total number of 10,166 people in Ikotos, Torit and Magwi have returned from Uganda and Kenya to their place of origin yet it is expected that after the formation of the new R-TGoNU by November 12th, 2019, there will be massive returnees, IDPs returnees and refugees movement from neighbouring countries into South SudanInsecurity and political intimidation have been a major driver of humanitarian services provision disruption in Ikotos and Kapoeta South. Front-line locations have been too insecure to allow humanitarian operations. There has been an outright restriction by government to access areas considered as opposition sites and all network operations were shut down. Road Ambushes, killing and robbery were rampant causing fear to humanitarian agencies to access the targeted locations. Such areas have therefore not been served hence exacerbating the provision of WASH services and WASH related GBV issues and other basic needs to most returnees and host communities. Hygiene and sanitation situation are so desperate in these areas. Most people still practice open defecation which is the source of disease infections to a number of household members affected by relevant health issues. Many of this has greater gender biases where women and girls are mostly affected because they are forced to defecate on open spaces and also travel long distance to fetch water from water points. In Ikotos and Kapoeta South, conflict also manifests as cattle raids between clans. The civilian population has been constantly on the move in search for safer grounds, and livelihoods have been severely disrupted. Persistent cattle raids have reduced the number of livestock and denied children and women access to milk and dietary which is a staple source of protein in the counties. In Ikotos, 24,451 households representing 25% while in Kapoeta South 55,787 households representing 61% have access to an improved water source only. It is expected in Ikotos 40% and Kapoeta South 60% of the population will be either in crisis, emergency, or catastrophic cholera situation since these counties fall within WASH Cluster Cholera hot-spots classification as the returning population keep on increasing in the project locations as long as peace and stability continue to hold.This proposed project aims at increasing access to safe and sustainable WASH services, delivery of livelihood assets and opportunities for GBV survivors and psycho-social support to the victims of GBV in the targeted locations.Therefore, there is a need to provide WASH services to the returnees, IDPs and host communities both to [1] enable them meet their basic needs immediately, [2] enable them recover their livelihood or start new ones and restore their livelihoods, while supporting key community led GBV prevention mechanisms processes to return to full functionality and live a meaningful life while also addressing cross cutting issues in this project.

2 - Activities

The proposed activities will involve both input support and training. It will be utilizing a strong community-based approach to enable target population of vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement, and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses through targeted programming to support coping capacities and livelihoods in prioritized geographical areas. The following activities that will be implemented include;• Rehabilitation of 10 boreholes in areas of water supply gaps after GBV risk analysis with women and girls.• Construction of 30 Water points (faucets).• Laying/extension of 100 miles of water supply pipelines.• Train 100 pump mechanics.• Distribution of water treatment products and soaps.• Distribution of Hygiene kits, sanitary pads, and water storage container and followed by a PDM exercise.• Organize 6 training of trainers workshops on Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) for local authority officials and community volunteers for 400 participants.• Train 300 WASH Committees on Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS)• Train 300 water system management committee.• Train 550 community Hygiene promoters.• Conduct environmental sanitation trainings for 213 participants.• Construction of 1500households latrines, 100 Communal latrines, and 20 institutional latrines considering gender-sensitive and design to reduce safety risks in consultation with women and girls. • Construction and provision of hand washing facilities• Conduct 35 awareness raising and behavior change on key hygiene behaviors• Organize 45 awareness sessions combine with distribution of hygiene materials, including risk reduction for possible GBV and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse for 215 beneficiaries.• Train 315 beneficiaries on understanding and capacity to prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV cases.• Monitoring and reporting of project activities.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

With two years of static presence in these communities and multiple existing programs in the target locations, RIPDO is familiar with the protection risks in the population and regularly analyzes and tailors its programming. Existing programming addresses the needs of those with protection concerns. RIPDO has established two layers of community engagement in the project proposed locations. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community level volunteers working on health and nutrition. The volunteers will be involved in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-based committees, which are comprised of members of both genders, elderly, youth, people with disabilities, and of minority groups where relevant. The community level engagement is materialized through conducting regular meeting with the volunteers and community-based committees. RIPDO sector staff and area coordinators regularly conduct meeting at community level and discuss progresses, challenges, future plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks through an AAP community approach. Feedback is discussed with the management team in Juba and used to adapt the project to meet the changing needs. RIPDO also interacts with specific protection actors concerning their findings in the target locations to make sure programming addresses the needs of all community members. Protection will be mainstreamed across this project to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse and exploitation faced by the target population. Consultation with local authorities and line ministries as well as other stakeholders will determine the locations of project implementation, taking into consideration safe spaces that are accessible to the beneficiaries. RIPDO will take a firm, zero tolerance stance towards any staff or volunteers taking advantage of beneficiaries vulnerability.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Right from inception, project will engage the local community and leadership in project activities. the project will establish local project management committees at Bomas level. These committees will be involved right from identification of beneficiaries to final evaluation. This committee is also expected to receive all complaints and forward them to the project team. At Payams level, beneficiaries will be sensitized to report all complaints involving project team or project management committee to Payam administrator who will in turn relay to the project team. At the county level, the South Sudan Relief and Rehabilitation Commission Coordinator will be the focal person to receive complaints and forward to project team and protection cluster where such issues are not handled at project level. At the project level, the protection officer and protection assistants will be required to document any complaints and address them together with project team or relay them to the senior management where senior management involvement is required.
