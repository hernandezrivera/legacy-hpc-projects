Project Summary

This initiative aims to bolster the resilience of communities adversely affected by ensuring their access to essential services via a comprehensive, integrated approach to multisectoral activities in select areas of Aleppo Governorate - Azaz and Idlib Districts, focusing on IDPs, returnees, and other individuals impacted by conflict and at risk of eviction post-earthquake. These activities target zones directly affected by the earthquake and ongoing conflicts.
The core goal is to enhance the resilience and meet the urgent needs of these vulnerable populations through a variety of measures, including infrastructure rehabilitation, cash support through Cash for Work (CFW), expansion of businesses, and provision of startup grants.
MWL will undertake Emergency Response and Livelihood (ERL) interventions in the most critical and affected communities. An initial field Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) was carried out by MWL, emphasizing areas in dire need of immediate intervention. This proposal is grounded in a meticulous needs assessment.
The selected sites were thoroughly inspected by the field team, alongside relevant stakeholders and authorities, to gather essential data for planning and executing the proposed activities, including detailed Bills of Quantities (BoQs) for rehabilitation efforts.
MWL has ensured access to these locations via its field offices, benefiting from its existing presence and operations in these sub-districts, and maintains a cooperative agreement with Turkish authorities, allowing for the expansion of proposed activities into ongoing projects.
The timeline for project completion is set at 14 months, encompassing:
Rehabilitation of key infrastructure, including 3 schools, linked with 40 short-term employment opportunities for highly vulnerable households.
A CFW scheme, injecting cash into the local economy through 40 beneficiaries over 3 months while facilitating rehabilitation.
Support for 80 workshop owners with grants to boost their capacity, including hosting 160 beneficiaries for 1-month vocational training, followed by a 1-month apprenticeship and a 4-month job placement phase.
Startup grants for 20 standout participants from the training scheme, demonstrating exceptional skill and potential.
Formation of community committees at targeted locations to foster engagement and oversee project initiatives, promoting social cohesion.
Challenges may arise, such as low participation or potential harm to specific groups, including women and persons with disabilities (PWDs). Mitigation strategies involve community leader engagement, tailored messaging, and role-specific staffing. Concerns over beneficiary selection criteria and risks like sexual exploitation are addressed through local team collaboration and coordinated efforts to prevent service duplication. Additional measures include staff training, adherence to conduct codes, and establishing complaint mechanisms. Assumptions of ongoing workshop owner involvement and market stability are critical. Efforts to counteract trainee dropout and PWD exclusion incorporate targeted training, transportation support, clear agreements, and accessible facilities. Protection policy violations are countered with comprehensive training, awareness campaigns, and feedback mechanisms. Community tension is managed through strategic engagement, while exclusion issues are tackled with gender-sensitive approaches and inclusive practices, specifically targeting PWDs through prioritization, monitoring, referrals, and enhancing staff capabilities. The project commits to upholding protection principles through rigorous policy enforcement, training, and awareness initiatives.
