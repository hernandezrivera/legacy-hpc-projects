Project Summary

The project aims to address the widening gap between the number of people with specific needs and the deteriorating health care services in Libya after years of conflict and economic crisis. In order to avoid a likely decline in the physical and mental health state of the most vulnerable people, HI’s intervention seeks to respond to the most urgent needs identified at individual level, while at the same time improving the quality of and access to local-level services. The project will target people with physical and functional limitations due to injuries/disabilities and their caregivers within the conflict-affected population, IDP settings and areas of return. The project will also target children and adults in acute need of psychosocial support and mental health services.

1 - Needs

In 2019, the rapidly evolving conflict, exacerbated by increasing numbers of migrants and internally displaced people, has resulted in mounting health and protection needs across Libya. With 301,407 IDPs, of whom 94% displaced due to the deterioration of the security situation, and 447,025 persons returned to their areas of origin, the capacity of service providers to serve the population is hindered in Libya. The WHO estimates the number of persons with disability globally as up to 15% of the population, which represents around 1,000,000 persons in Libya today according to population figures. After years of conflict in Libya, conflict-affected and injured persons may have now developed severe physical and functional complications as consequences of a lack of physiotherapy at hospital level during the early stages of the recovery. Psychological distress or mental health needs are also growing bigger with the ongoing conflict. Analysis of HI beneficiaries between January and August 2019 shows the following data: 20% of households have more than 6 people living under the same roof; 5% of persons with disabilities have no caregiver at home; 6% of households have more than one person with disability/injury receiving assistance from HI, 93,5% of HI beneficiaries is in need of and receives PSS support; 55% of HI beneficiaries reported health as highest in terms of barriers to access; 53% of households report having physically inaccessible shelters and 59% report having physically inaccessible surroundings to their shelters.Severe shortages of health staff, medicines, supplies and equipment have been compounded by years of under-investment in the health system and a lack of transparency and accountability. Between 22 and 25% of public health care facilities are closed. Of those that remain open, 26% offer no services at all, and only 6% of 78 functioning hospitals offer all essential services. The health sector has identified 3,970,842 people in need of health assistance for 2020. With this bleak and deteriorating background, the overall needs of persons with disabilities/injuries in Libya can be summarized as the following: - Lack of access to services: persons with disabilities or injuries are often not only physically unable to access services, but also often report barriers such as fear for safety and financial constraints; there is also fear of stigma related to psychosocial distress and mental health disorders. - Lack of availability of services: specialized services for persons with disabilities/injuries are increasingly unavailable as well as established psychological or psychiatric services for persons with severe mental illness or psychological distress. Public health services are suffering severe shortage of trained staff and long waiting lists, whilst the cost of private services is in itself prohibitive. - Lack of information about available services for persons with disabilities and injuries: Even despite the limited services available, there is also lack of understanding and knowledge about the specific needs of persons with disabilities and how to access them. Caregivers and family members in particular struggle with the physical and emotional burden of caring for the individual, lacking the time, energy and ability to seek out information about what assistance is available to them.In Libya's conservative society women and girls are often difficult to reach to assess needs and provide services, as in the most vulnerable households they are mostly confined at home to care for the family and house. Caregivers of male/female elderly, children and mentally/physically disabled are mostly women; and as per HI's data 17% of households are female-headed. This makes women more prone to suffering psychosocial distress due to this disproportionate burden and in need of specialized support to better help those under their care, particularly in case of mental/physical disability.

2 - Activities

- Identification and assessment of the most vulnerable persons with functional limitations in affected communities;-Identification and assessment of the most vulnerable persons with psychological distress or mental health disorders;- Direct provision of physical and functional rehabilitation, PSS services and guidance to people with functional limitations due to injuries or disabilities through home visits or at a fixed point;- Direct provision of guidance, advices and PSS services to the caregivers of persons with injuries or disabilities to improve care and coping capacities;- Direct provision of assistive & mobility devices and guidance on their use to people with functional limitations due to injuries or disabilities and their caregivers;-Direct provision of MHPSS services to persons with psychological distress or mental health disorders: psychoeducation on MH disorders and low intensity psychological interventions;- External referrals of people with functional limitations due to injuries and disabilities and/or with psychological distress or mental health disorders, to facilitate access to basic needs and other services and to ensure continuity in the quality of care following the intervention;- Provision of basic rehabilitation equipment to supported health facilities;- Provision of training to health care providers on early rehabilitation and inclusion of persons with functional limitations due to injury or disability;-Provision of Awareness and psychoeducation on psychosocial distress, on most common MH disorders and on referral mechanisms;- Provision of awareness sessions for community focal points including IDP leaders and local & international NGO staff on the identification, inclusion and referral of people with functional limitations and psychological distress;-Training of staff (from PHCs, local and international NGOs, CBOs) on psychological first aid (PFA), psychosocial distress and low intensity psychological interventions (PM+).

4 - Protection risk analysis

- Breach of law and human rights violations: Sexual abuse and exploitation of beneficiaries, children's rights violations, corruption/bribery;-Conflict related violence against civilians: deteriorating security situation preventing HI outreach teams from accessing the intervention areas or beneficiaries from reaching HI’s fixed points;- Relations with local stakeholders impeding access: current support and/or cooperation with authorities ceases, including the Commissions of Civil Society, Ministry of Social Affairs and Ministry of Health;

5 - Mitigation measures

- Breach of law and human rights violations: institutional policies on anti-fraud and corruption, child protection, and protection of beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse are deployed in Libya, and are shared with new staff, partners, and suppliers. Moreover, all HI staff have signed and follow HI Code of Conduct, which prevents HI Staff and beneficiaries from protection risks.- Conflict related violence against civilians: continuously coordinate with other agencies and the HAT to exchange information on security conditions and agree on cooperation in case of emergencies; closely adhere to the organization's procedures to ensure programme staff and assets are safe; regularly conduct risk analysis based on the updated Safety and Security Management Plan; maintain positive relationships with community focal points and local authorities to ensure acceptance and reduce possibility of security threats against HI staff.- Relations with local stakeholders: continue engaging line ministries, local governments and other stakeholders in all stages of the programme, together with the INGO Forum and donors, as appropriate, and in line with International Humanitarian Law; maintain constant positive dialogue with local authorities through HI staff to ensure acceptance of planned activities; maintain indirect support to services by accepting referrals from the Ministries of Health and Social Affairs supported facilities, as well as referring to their services; seek approval from the relevant authorities prior to conducting activities in new locations.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

HI ensures accountability towards beneficiaries, having in place a hotline and Facebook page to receive potential complaints and feedback. This mechanism follows specific communication channels according to the degree of gravity of the feedback, triggering a series of immediate corrective measures.In terms of rehabilitation services and psychosocial support, the beneficiaries are fully involved in the action, starting with their consent to benefit from the project's activities. Following an initial assessment to better understand the medical history, functional limitations and psychosocial issues, the physiotherapist and the psychosocial worker suggest and build with the beneficiary a common treatment plan. The beneficiaries are also systematically asked to give feedback on the quality of the intervention and the improvement of their access to other services.
