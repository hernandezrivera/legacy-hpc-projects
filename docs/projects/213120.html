Project Summary

The project aims to deliver sustained lifesaving food assistance to the 7,500 most
chronically food-insecure households. It will focus primarily on underserved areas,
informal IDP settlements in multiple governorates with severely food-insecure PiN and
higher prevalence of chronic malnutrition, while also increasing the provision of
assistance to conflict-affected areas along the Euphrates River in Deir-ez-Zor
Governorate. This will be done in coordination with FSL WG and other food security
partners in NES in line with the MFA targeting guidelines (2024) and WoS Food and
Agriculture Sector Strategy. The project will consist primarily of two main modalities of
food assistance dry food rations and food vouchers and a small stock of RTEs (1,000)
will also be pre-positioned in case of any new displacements or emergencies to meet the immediate food needs of the affected communities.
The team recognizes that aid can have unforeseen consequences and prioritizes a ‘do no harm’ approach to mitigate and prevent possible negative impacts. the NGO informs its programming through a needs-based approach, looking specifically at the differences between sex- and age-disaggregated data to inform the analysis of vulnerability factors. and will ensure safe programming throughout the project cycle to avoid risks linked to activity implementation through the below strategies:
In terms of community level protection risks, the intervention takes into consideration potential tensions among host communities and IDPs. In fact, to minimize the risk of exacerbating existing tensions and in line with ‘Do No Harm’ approaches, and will target the most vulnerable families from nearby host communities for food assistance interventions, when applicable. This would be driven by need and gaps in existing NGO response to neighboring communities.
Vulnerability and needs assessment at onset of intervention to understand how the crisis has changed and affected the needs, risks, and opportunities of vulnerable groups. Assessments will be designed to capture information on age, gender, and disabilities enabling the tailoring of the intervention according to the different needs. the team will conduct distribution based on assessment findings rolled put at the project design and implementation stage, prioritizing those most vulnerable such as the elderly, Persons with Disabilities, and women-headed households. NGO does not rely on lists from local councils or camp managers, but instead does direct household assessments.
The team will conduct Protection Mainstreaming (ProM) assessments (also referred to as Protection Risk Analysis) to identify the level of ProM principles incorporation and adherence. This will ensure NGO’s ability to implement corrective measures and identify possible risks, to maintain and improve programmatic quality service delivery. The ProM assessment will provide the basis for NGO to promote safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability, participation and empowerment through informed program design.
NGO’s Complain and Response Mechanism (CRM) is implemented to ensure meaningful access and channels of communication. NGO uses complaint boxes, hotlines, emails, and a WhatsApp number, providing beneficiaries with safe and accessible means to share feedback and complaints. The CRM will be complemented through NGO's distribution monitoring and post-distribution monitoring activities to ensure the quality and accountability of the intervention. Accessibility and awareness of the CRM are ensured also through the provision of NGO CRM details printed on every food voucher, and at the time of distribution, the beneficiaries are also informed that they can directly provide feedback or complaints on NGO’s programs through the channels mentioned in the voucher. To reduce community tension, families who are not selected for assistance or new arrivals are allowed to request re-assessment.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis; The NGO will ensure gender-sensitive programming by strengthening the targeting, delivery and monitoring of interventions that take into consideration the differential needs of women, men, girls, and boys. Assessments and monitoring surveys will collect data disaggregated by gender and age, promoting gender and age analysis and participation of all groups. Special focus will be placed on female-headed households, PLW, PWDs, and nutrition-smart targeting to ensure inclusive, adequate, and commensurate responses to their specific needs. Based on the analysis of the FSS sector of the numerous risks and potential threats linked to the implementation of the sector strategy, the NGO similarly identifies two key protection risks around distributions themselves and the possibility of causing tension between IDP and host communities. Mitigation measures are spelled out below:- To mitigate the security/protection risks for all beneficiaries during distribution, they will be carried out early in the day to ensure beneficiaries can come and go with daylight.- Women and men have separate lines at distribution points that are staffed by male and female field workers. - In camp settings, multiple distribution points are established to help keep the number of people at one point to a minimum and to reduce waiting times. For out-of-camp distributions, the duration between assessment and distribution is kept to a minimum to avoid large distributions and to help deliver assistance to beneficiaries as soon as possible. - Voucher distributions take place by mixed-gender teams at the household level. Whenever possible appropriate staff from the area will carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive informational networks, and good community acceptance. - The NGO also holds consultations between host and IDP communities PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategyThe project prioritises the importance of PSEA by explicitly addressing the issue in its Programme Participant Protection Policy and staff Code of Conduct, which together define the project's expectations of staff and partners, their responsibilities as well as a mandatory duty to report in cases of potential abuse. These policies are backed up by a Community Response Mechanism open to and accessible to staff, beneficiaries, and the public to provide multiple means of reporting. In the specific case of Syria, the project is committed to supporting and engaging with the current Inter-Agency Standing Committee PSEA Expansion Plan for the Whole of Syria - to date, three staff have been trained as internal focal points for PSEA and are involved in rolling out PSEA training and awareness sessions for the rest of the project's staff.
