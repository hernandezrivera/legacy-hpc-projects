Project Summary

Since the eruption of armed violence mid-April, WASH assistance was highlighted as a priority need in particular in states that have received significant IDPs and other affected populations that have settled in informal sites and in camps. This also includes populations of local host communities, where water supply, sanitation systems and the general hygiene environment are at risk of collapse due to the onset of the rainy season, ongoing conflict, and the increased burden placed on WASH systems by the newly arrived IDPs. Acted's RNA findings are in line with IOM-DTM's situation reports, highlighting that displaced households are currently seeking shelter with relatives, temporary shelter or in IDP gathering sites prepared by the local government in Al Jazirah, Gedaref, Sennar Blue Nile and White Nile.
The present project aims at scaling up Acted's pre-conflict, multisectoral operations to respond to the new emergency WASH related needs of conflict-affected populations including IDPs, refugees, and local host communities in White Nile, Blue Nile, Al Jazirah, Sennar, Gedaref and North Kordofan.

1 - Needs

Acted's RNA indicate that affected people reaching Gedaref and Al Jazirah are increasingly moving towards urban areas incl. collective sites and informal settlements, while in White Nile and Blue Nile they tend to move towards rural or existing larger camp settings (raising the issue of overcrowding and insufficient capacity for basic services in existing camps). The RNA also indicates that (at the time of surveying) the government had set up 3 collective centes in Gedaref and 5 in Al Jazirah (each hosting a few hundreds individual but lacking service provision). Only Sudanese nationals were permitted to stay in formal sites, with non-Sudanese facing secondary displacement in camps in Kassala, White Nile and Gedaref. WASH assistance was highlighted as a priority need in particular in Blue Nile and White Nile states, for instance as the former has seen a large influx of returnees from South Sudan, overcrowding the five existing returnee camps in Ulu. The existing WASH environment in these camps is already poor, with a lack of adequate sanitation facilities and poor water supply. With the onset of the rainy season and the regularity of open defecation in these areas, the likelihood of cholera or acute watery diarrhea outbreaks is high. In the IDP sites of Gedaref and Al Jazirah, ACTED's key informants indicated that the water supply was insufficient and sewage trucks were required multiple times a month to prevent overflowing of latrines. These findings go in line with IOM-DTM situation report, highlighting that displaced households are currently seeking shelter with relatives and most in host sites, temporary shelter or in IDP gathering sites prepared by the local government in Jezirah, Gedaref, Sennar Blue Nile and White Nile. It should be highlighted that the needs of the conflict-affected populations are multisectoral in nature - reason why the present WASH project is to be implemented jointly with emergency interventions in the S-NFI, FSL and protection sectors. In addition, special consideration must be given to ensuring persons with special needs, as well as populations with vulnerabilities predating the current crisis, are covered.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Across all areas of intervention, ACTED is pairing its WASH activities with a mobile CCCM approach, targeting sites of displacement for multisectoral assistance. In coordination with local authorities, other humanitarian responders, and in accordance with Acted’s assessments and SPHERE standards, Acted will identify pressing needs and provide family water kits to 830 vulnerable households as well as individual hygiene kits to 3322 vulnerable individuals in displacement sites. This will ensure that displaced individuals and households have access to clean drinkable water and personal hygiene items. At the same time, ACTED will ensure adequate water supply for 2,000 individuals through water trucking to locations hosting displaced persons that lack safe access to water points. To improve the sanitation at sites of displacement, ACTED will construct 55 handwashing stations and latrine blocks, as well as 33 communal emergency shower blocks. With these activities, ACTED aims to provide emergency WASH services to IDP hosting sites serving an estimated 12,000 individuals.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Waste management, including that generated by the crisis, can be a problem because it can increase risks to public health - this is especially the case for the construction activities under this action. The life cycle of materials distributed must therefore be taken into account. In addition, the use of water trucking is not sustainable, but cannot be avoided in the current context given the profile of those targeted by the ACTED intervention. The initial design of the project shall encourage future recycling or reuse, and the selection of materials could be diversified to minimise reliance on a single source. When developing and implementing the project, Acted will consider the following environmental mitigation measures:- Encouraging local procurement to limit the environmental impacts associated with the supply chain. Engaging suppliers or manufacturers with sustainable or environmental certifications would, for example, help to reduce the environmental footprint of operations.- Make a strategic selection of items for distribution, to reduce resource consumption and waste production.- Consider the usefulness of items distributed after the crisis, in particular the possibility of supplying multifunctional items.Finally, as part of on-site monitoring activities, the MEAL unit will also pay particular attention to the environmental impacts of ACTED's activities under this project. Indeed, in the framework of all its projects (including emergency responses), Acted measures its carbon footprint and defines measures to reduce it.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention envisages specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups and encourages gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedding the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach.Preparation Phase:- Before the intervention, ACTED will assess the security context and protection risks, and continue monitoring the situation throughout the project’s lifespan.- The whole community will be sensitised on the project’s scope and activities and informed on selection criteria, which were designed to address protection risks and cover sectoral needs of most vulnerable (e.g. single women, PLWs, children, the elderly, PwDs).Program Implementation Phase:- ACTED mainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during community sensitization and trainings.- waiting time and safety during traveling will be addressed through adhoc protection measures.- First aid kits and protective wear are available on distribution sites. Accountability:- ACTED has an independent MEAL unit with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM, through which beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Beside its staff deployed in the field daily, ACTED will also conduct regular spot checks. ACTED will evaluate if a complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the proposed intervention if needed, and inform future projects’ design. Human resources:- ACTED has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. ACTED also complies with The Code of Conduct (CoC) of The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and UNHCR’s CoC. Acted’s CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of the Program Manager. Any incident would be immediately reported to coordination through a standardized communication pathway and actions would be taken by the appropriate levels of coordination.- For the intervention, ACTED will set up a gender-balanced implementation team to ensure a safer environment, improve protection mainstreaming and favour inclusion mechanisms. In all activities, ACTED will promote an equal gender participation.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, Acted will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly through its CRM. Acted has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations. Recruitment:- Acted prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening. People employed by Acted already have obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies.Administration and employee cycle:- Signature of Code of Conduct for staff;- Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected- Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination.- Training program related to safeguarding issues.- Acted has an in-house training related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at HQ for 7000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. ii) Specific training on SEA & Child Protection in intervention countries. Acted Complaints and response mechanisms.- Acted will establish Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage CRM permanently installed in Area Office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. CRM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between Acted and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks. Complaint’s investigation & disciplinary measures -Acted has capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator (Acted SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; Acted Child protection policy, Articles 20 to 22). Acted must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. Acted also endeavours to protect victims of prohibited acts (Acted SEA policy, article 23). Acted has psychologists available to support individuals who would have been impacted as a result of a failure to comply with Acted's safeguarding policies. The HQ HR department will assess allegations to design an investigation plan and select an investigation team. Depending on investigation conclusions, different measures can be taken including disciplinary actions or reporting to prosecuting authority.
