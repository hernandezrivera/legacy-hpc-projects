Project Summary

UNHCR will implement comprehensive nutrition programmes in 19 integrated nutrition centers across 14 camps and two stabilization centers in partnership with health and nutrition partners. UNHCR strategic vision is to provide services following public health approach through integration of health nutrition services to foster access and continuum of care for beneficaries as per UNHCR public health strategy and Nutrition sector guidance. Nutrition services will be strengthened through more collaboration with other sectors, such as Health/Mental health and pyschocial support, education protection and linkages with food security.
Nutrition programmes comprising of treatment through community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) programmes and preventive programmes such as blanket supplementary feeding programmes, infant and young child feeding programmes and community management of mothers at risks and infants 0-6 months (CMAMI) will be provided for all children under-five years, pregnant and lactating women, and other vulnerable groups in the population. Nutrition programmes for the adolescent girls at the community level will be prioritized as well. About 400 community nutrition volunteers (CNV’S) will be recruited to support the community outreach activities screening, health education and follow up of beneficiaries enrolled in different programmes. To ensure quality of the programmes continuous capacity building will be provided to nutrition staff and volunteers.
New arrivals living in the transit centre and families in the quarantine centers will be supported through provision of meals and nutrition screening and treatment on site. UNHCR and partners will ensure compliance of the COVID-19 prevention and transmission through provision of adequate PPE as per WHO guidelines and strict adherence to government of Bangladesh (GOB) regulations.
The Nutrition status of refugees will be monitored using the UNHCR standard expanded nutrition surveys (SENS) in additional to other assessments planned by the nutrition partners. Age and gender mainstreaming will be observed in the whole project cycle with more focus on persons with special needs including disability, the vulnerable population, and more engagement with the community. Mechanism to monitor the partners accountability to the affected population will be put in place in line with the UNHCR standards and other activities that have been agreed upon with the Nutrition sector.

1 - Identified Needs

Malnutrition among children continues be a concern in the camps and may have worsened due the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic which can attributed to changes humanitarian assistance and especially food security and nutrition service delivery modality in the refugee camps. The prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) among children 6-59 months in the newly established Kutupalong and Teknaf camps slightly increased from and 11.3% to 13.7% as per the 2021 nutrition survey depicting a serious state of malnutrition in the camps as per the WHO classifications of acute malnutrition (GAM 11-14%). Chronic malnutrition rates are still above the WHO thresh hold of >30% and anaemia prevalence remained high with rates>70% for children 6–23-month standard (<40%) as per the 2021 Nutrition survey. Infant and young child feeding (IYCF) indicators are still poor with exclusive breastfeeding of 63% and access to minimum acceptable diet of 44% and complementary feeding of less than 50% across all the camps. Based on the risk factors of the nutritional causal analysis (NCA) Integration of Nutrition services with the other sectors such as health, mental health (MPHSS), food security, WASH, protection and CBI and livelihoods will be prioritized to ensure collective response in addressing the high levels of malnutrition in the refugee camps. The progress of implementation in 2021 was been extremely affected by the COVID-19 pandemic restrictions employed by the government to reduce the spread of Corona virus. This led reduction of staff working in the nutrition centers, modification of nutrition protocols and the suspension of majority of the community-based programmes. Monsoon period also had an impact on access to nutrition services due to flash floods and reduced community outreach activities. The nutritional needs of older adults and other vulnerable categories such as children above five years, medical cases and other specific needs will be considered and supported as per the sector guidelines. Continued capacity building of the nutrition staff will be strengthened to ensure implementation of the nutrition services as per the protocols to ensure good quality of care and overall outcomes and the overall nutrition wellbeing of the beneficiaries. In 2022 UNHCR and partners monitor the programmes and make relevant adjustments to ensure implementation of good quality programmes and accountability to the affected population is maintained. More focus on implementation of nutrition sensitive programmes in the nutrition centers m community engagement and scaling up of the mother lead MUAC concept to ensure its sustainability will be done in collaboration with other relevant sectors. UNHCR is supporting the GOB in strengthening localization strategy through engagement of two national NGO and is working towards building their capacity to implement comprehensive nutrition programmes. A total estimated number 118,134 beneficaries (Children under five years are 71,421, adolescent girls 30,339 and 16,374 pregnant and lactating women) will be supported in the UNHCR funded nutrition programmes as per the sectors needs analysis.

2 - Activities or outputs

Community based screening, detection and referral of acutely malnourished children aged 6-59 months to the nutrition centre. Inpatient management of children under five severe acute malnutrition with medical complications in the stabilization centre. Implementation of outpatient therapeutic feeding program (OTP) and Targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) for the treatment of severe and moderate acutely malnourished children respectively. Implementation of the Blanket supplementary feeding program for the Children 6-59 months -and Pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Implementation of infant and young child feeding programmes and community management of mothers at risk and infants 0-6 months (CMAMI). Implementation of adolescent nutrition programmes IFA supplementation and counselling for girls 10 -19 years. Nutrition support to the new arrivals in the transit centre and the quarantine centres for the families affected by COVID-19. Conduct the annual nutrition survey based on UNHCR SENS guidelines to strengthen effective nutrition emergency response planning, implementation, monitoring and come up with recommendations to support nutrition programming. Roll out of the UNHCR IYCF -e framework to ensure intergration of IYCF with other sectors; ECCD services under the nutrition programme.
