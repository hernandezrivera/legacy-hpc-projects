Project Summary

In 2021, FAO will improve food security, nutritional status and incomes among vulnerable, conflict- and pandemic-affected households in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states in Northeast Nigeria. The now more than decade-long insurgency has decimated agricultural livelihoods upon which more than 80 percent of the population relies as a primary source of household income. The COVID-19 pandemic has further compounded this already highly vulnerable humanitarian context, generating interconnected vulnerabilities that negatively affect availability and access to food, agriculture inputs, finance, farming land, extension services, markets and the fundamental viability of agricultural livelihoods.
Support to crop production and the rebuilding of a robust crop value chain have been identified by FAO as key to the restoration of livelihoods and to improving the food and nutrition security of vulnerable populations in the BAY states. FAO will support vulnerable households with critical inputs for rainy and dry season cultivation as well as for micro-/backyard gardening and seed production.
Prior to the insurgency, close to 80 percent of households in northeast Nigeria reared livestock and/or poultry as a vital source of food and household income. The conflict has caused the wide-scale loss of these assets either through looting, property destruction, disease, or abandonment when fleeing attacks by armed insurgents. In addition to being a source of income generation through breeding for sale, livestock and poultry restocking increases the availability of meat, milk and eggs at the household level. FAO will increase support to livestock and poultry production in 2021 in support of affected households.
Prior to the crisis, the fisheries sector had significant economic importance and fish from the northeast were supplied to the rest of Nigeria. With increased land and areas retaken by the military, the return of IDPs to their communities of origin is ongoing, thereby enabling some to access fishing grounds and resume their traditional livelihood. However, the lack of quality inputs remains a limiting factor for the majority of fishers who continue to rely on humanitarian assistance to meet their food and livelihood needs. By leveraging FAO’s extensive experience and capacity, conflict-affected fisherfolk households will be supported to resume their livelihoods and meet their food and nutritional needs.
The ongoing crisis is also termed a ‘protection crisis’ with vulnerable social groups – particularly children, women and the elderly – disproportionately affected by the impact of the ongoing conflict and the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and are at heightened risk of adopting negative coping strategies such as child labour, borrowing, street begging, basic needs deprivation, transactional sex, early and forced marriage as their families seek to reduce the number of mouths to feed in their homes, and collection of fuelwood in insecure areas, which remains a high-risk livelihood. FAO will support vulnerable households by way of fuel-efficient stoves to reduce exposure to potential protection risks, and stove production and briquette production centres for income generation.
Access to credit is a particular challenge for vulnerable groups in Northeast Nigeria, particularly women. Through the establishment of Group Savings and Loans Associations (GSLA), in 2021 FAO will extend its GSLA programme and will identify vulnerable groups to enhance their access to credit and financial services as an important social safety net.
FAO is the co-lead of the Food Security Sector (FSS) which plays a critical role in coordinating food security programming in the BAY states. Food security data collection and analysis are also key and upon which food security actors base their intervention. In 2021, FAO will continue to support and host the Food Security Sector to facilitate the required coordination and analyses required to reach people most in need.

1 - Needs

More than a decade into the conflict and the humanitarian crisis in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states in Northeast Nigeria remains one of the most severe in the world today with the number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in Africa surpassed only by the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and Somalia. As of August 2020, over 2.1 million persons remain displaced across the BAY states with women and children making up 81 percent of the overall crisis-affected population and 87 percent of the new displacements. The ongoing conflict has generated interconnected vulnerabilities that negatively affect availability and access to food, agriculture inputs, finance, farming land, extension services, markets as well as agricultural and alternative livelihood options. The situation remains critical as many cannot meet their food needs, especially in areas where large-scale displacements have taken place. The impact of the conflict is compounded further by climate variability that negatively affects production systems resulting in poor agricultural productivity with direct negative impacts on livelihoods and food securityThe outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic further compounded an already highly fragile humanitarian context, negatively impacting agricultural livelihood activities across both the region and the country. The effects of the pandemic soon became evident, particularly in relation to food supply chains and interstate movements of agricultural produce, including both food commodities and animal feed. Given that the majority of the population of the BAY states relies on agriculture for household income, the COVID-19 pandemic has further weakened access to critical agricultural inputs for food production and income-generation and, as a result, has exacerbated food insecurity and malnutrition. The impact of the ongoing insurgency and COVID-19 pandemic has been borne more heavily by already vulnerable communities (IDPs, returnees and host communities), specifically those in fragile conflict-affected areas in Northeast Nigeria that are already affected by significant food insecurity and malnutrition. Since these communities disproportionately rely on agriculture as their primary source of livelihoods, further erosion of their resilience to socioeconomic shocks are intensifying vulnerabilities. These factors combined have resulted in alarming rates of food insecurity and malnutrition across the BAY states. According to the Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis conducted in October 2020, over 3.4 million people in the BAY states are currently facing critical food insecurity and crisis (CH Phases 3-4), a figure that is projected to rise to over 5.1 million in the 2021 lean season (June – August 2021) in the absence of sustained humanitarian support. The prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) is also a cause for grave concern with stressed to emergency malnutrition thresholds in Yobe (14.7 percent) and Borno (14.5 percent) states. In terms of food security and livelihoods, the ongoing insurgency and COVID-19 pandemic have not fundamentally altered the nature of the needs of vulnerable, conflict-affected populations in Northeast Nigeria but have undoubtedly extended and intensified the scale, scope and urgency of these needs, particularly among already highly vulnerable groups, further worsening an already dire situation. The ongoing crisis is also termed a ‘protection crisis’, with vulnerable social groups – particularly children, women and the elderly – disproportionately affected by the impact of the ongoing conflict and pandemic. Given that an estimated 80 percent of the population of Northeast Nigeria depends on the agriculture sector for household income, it remains evident that humanitarian support towards the restoration and protection of agricultural livelihoods is key to preventing a further deterioration in food insecurity and nutrition outcomes as well as to mitigating exposure to protection risks.

2 - Activities

Support to Food Production - Support to land preparation including distribution of rainy season and dry season kits (seeds and fertilizer) for crop production as well as essential inputs for backyard vegetable gardening to restore/protect agricultural livelihoods and promote household nutrition. Rainy season crop beneficiaries will also benefit from a Fresh Food Voucher to prevent premature consumption of food stocks during the lean season. - Training of existing seed producers on improved production techniques, good crop production management practices, post-harvest handling, storage and record keeping, including establishment of community-based seed out-growers to promote the production of improved quality and climate-resilient seeds through seed multiplication- Creation of market linkages between seed producers and buyers (agricultural support projects, farmer cooperatives, government)Support to Livestock-based Livelihoods including animal restocking, value chain development and veterinary support. The support will specifically entail:- Animal restocking targeting women and youth with goat and poultry kits for income generation and improved nutrition. Targeted beneficiaries will also benefit from FAO’s ‘cash+’ approach through which they will receive a cash transfer aimed at protecting the livestock assets from premature consumption or sale, and from market linkages. - Vaccination campaign to control animal diseases aimed at protecting livestock-based livelihoods, including biosecurity/biocontainment training for disease control, food safety and animal welfare to key associations/groups involved in livestock production/processing.- Building the capacity of state veterinary services and Community-based Animal Health Workers (CBAHW) to provide animal health services to project beneficiaries and other vulnerable livestock owners. Support to Fisheries - Traditional fishing households that have lost their livelihoods owing to the conflict around the Lake Chad basin will be supported through: - Provision of fish farm starter kits (tanks, fingerlings and feed) for aquaculture production, mainly targeting male youth, including a cash transfer for protection of assets.- Establishment and/or scale-up of private/community-based fingerling production enterprises in close collaboration with the Federal College of Freshwater Fisheries Technology, Baga. - Establishment of local fish feed manufacturing cooperatives through local products assessment and capacity building programmes. - Promotion of good hygiene and handling practices through capacity building activities and provision of handling goods and tools to enhance the quality of fish products at market, thereby increasing sale potential. - Provision of fish processing kits (drying, smoking) and trainings to women’s groups to maximize the utilization of fish catch, limit production losses and increase the shelf life of fish products. Protection- Establishment/upgrading of fuel-efficient stove production centres and briquette production centres for income generation.- Distribution of fuel-efficient stoves to households facing heightened protection and health risks; mitigate poor energy access and adoption of negative coping strategies.Increasing Access to CreditTo enhance access to credit among vulnerable groups – particularly women – Group Savings and Loans Associations (GLSAs) will be established to facilitate access to financial services through group-mobilized funds. This will promote upscaling and resilience-building by enabling beneficiaries to procure productive assets and develop micro-businesses with the loans and productive resources generated. Food Security Coordination, Data Collection and Analysis- Critical support to the functioning of the Food Security Sector for coordination of food security activities in the BAY states- Critical support to food security data collection and analysis to enable evidence-based programming by food security actors.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

FAO will undertake a broad scale protection risk analysis to understand the risks of specific groups across agricultural value chains in the northeast and is well placed to design projects which consider the specific risks facing at-risk groups. FAO has field experts including female field assistants and staff who speak the common local languages in the BAY states and are frequently deployed during trainings, distributions, monitoring and planning missions. To greater understand the protection risks faced by HHs, FAO will undertake a protection risk assessment prior to the implementation of the activity. The Organization will also reinforce training to staff on GBV, PSEA, AAP and CwC. By adopting a CBA, ensuring beneficiary participation in decision-making, and involving local and traditional authorities, the project will promote accountability, capacity building & empowerment. Five percent of the budget will be allocated for the recruitment of a protection expert, training of stakeholders communication and advocacy.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

FAO will ensure that feedback mechanisms such as toll free numbers, complaint boxes, complaint desks are available throughout the life cycle of the project and that vulnerable community members have access to these mechanisms. The organization will also ensure that the views and experiences of the most vulnerable are integrated in post-activity monitoring and reporting. FAO will engage a wide cross section of stakeholders to identify the most-vulnerable and those least likely to utilize feedback mechanisms . These individuals will be identified and reached in person where possible and their specific requirements and needs will be taken into account in project design, implementation and appraisal.
