Project Summary

Somalia is a country whose public health system has significantly underperformed for many years because of longstanding conflict and instability and is further overburdened by the impact of climate shocks such as drought and flooding. Somalia has the world’s highest child mortality rate. The risk of childhood mortality is highest between birth and one month, accounting for approximately 45 percent of death among children under age five. Nearly 54 percent of health facilities do not provide an ‘Expanded Programme on Immunization’ services; driving coverage below 50 per cent, leading to a rapid accumulation of children susceptible to vaccine-preventable illnesses and exposing communities to the risk of communicable diseases. Surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2020 indicate the persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization, and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices.
The 2020 HRP estimates that overall 5.2 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance (compared to 4.2 million in 2019).
ADA through this interventions in the stated localities (Gedo, Galgaduud, Middle Juba and Lower Juba region of Somalia) will work towards Strengthen and improve access to essential lifesaving health services aimed at reducing avoidable morbidity and mortality; reducing of maternal and child morbidity and mortality among,
; strengthen emergency preparedness and response capacity at all levels in order to mitigate and respond to communicable disease outbreaks in an efficient, coordinated, and timely for crisis-affected and host populations in Gedo, Galgadud and Lower Juba region of Somalia.
The project targets 48,700 (24,836 female, 23,864 male) beneficiaries from both host and IDP communities with healthcare services, out of which 2,435 beneficiaries are people with disabilities. This project will directly contribute to the Health cluster objectives of reducing excess morbidity and mortality due to disease outbreak driven by climatic shocks and conflict, contribute to improvement in the physical and mental well-being of the population affected by conflict and displacement, improve case management and referral services for survivors of sexual or gender-based violence and improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential health services.
The project’s intended impact is to: Contribute towards improved health, particularly for women of childbearing age and children under-five, by providing health care services at the 8 targeted facilities. 3 mobile and 5 static.
As a way of working towards the provision of Integrated life-saving primary health care services, we will improve access to health care systems by providing health interventions to the residence within the target population to the nearest proximity. Health care services through consultation and management of childhood illnesses with an aim of reducing maternal and neonatal mortality will be given focus. ANC and BEmONC with referrals of the CEmONC services will be provided to the pregnant mothers.
Staff and the community will be equipped with adequate information to manage emergencies and outbreaks that may arise from the dynamic natural and conflict-related concerns. Reports shall be shared with the inline MOH and the cluster including proper coordination so as to respond to emergencies. ADA staff will be equipped with training on ANC and BEmONC, IMCI, Emergency response preparedness, and CHWs equipped with skills in community management and targeting. The community dialogue will be key in adherence to the AAP framework.
ADA will ensure that services are provided in a safe and protective environment adhering to do no harm standards. Mainstreaming of cross-cutting issues like protection, accountability to the targeted population as well as avoidance of discrimination of the less fortunate.

Project Activities

Improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for crisis-affected. aimed at reducing avoidable morbidity and mortality through 5 static health facilities and 3 outreach teams. Activity 1.1 ADA will set 3 mobile teams to deliver clinic curative services and implement through 5 existing health facilities. Activity 1.2 The setup health and clinic facilities will undertake 48,700 (24,836 female and 23,864 male) at the health facilities in an integrated approach will be able to provide lifesaving health care services including, treatment of childhood illnesses, immunization, Ante Natal Care (ANC), post-natal care (PNC), Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) with referrals of the complicated cases to respective referral hospitals. Activity 2.1 Training of 20 health workers (10 men and 10 women) and 28 community outreaches on BEMONC. Activity 2.2 Training of 20 health workers (10 men 10 women) and 28 community outreaches (14 men 14 women) on integrated management of childhood illness.Activity 2.3. training of 20 community health workers and 28 volunteers on the promotion of hygiene, management, and prevention of AWD/cholera cases. Activity 3. ADA will undertake public health promotion activities to at least 38,960 persons (19090- men, 19,870 women) community members through health sessions at the facility and community level.

Mitigation Measures

Volatile risks in Somalia context with service delivery has been very fragile. ADA will focus on the humanitarian response and provision of protection services in areas that have been inaccessible thus forcing IDPs to move to find assistance in accessible, less conflict prone areas. ADA will engage further in conflict affected areas through various actions including but not limited to engaging the various gatekeepers and the local authorities and the IMAMs Our mitigation strategies will include Community engagement of all groups of people especially marginalized groups to ensure a joint transparent process in the response mobilization phase. strengthening of gender inclusive community-based protection mechanisms. Information campaigns will be boosted to ensure impartial and timely access to GBV protection services, especially in situations of displacement. Protection monitoring will be used for both referrals and evidence-based advocacy, especially with authorities responsible for the implementation of durable solutions for displacement affected communities. ADA will build trust in the efficiency of our Complain and feedback mechanism to identify any situation of exclusion, and to condition implementation to be an equal and ensure direct access to all communities. We will ensure that complaints are followed up immediately and corrective measures are implemented on time. In site planning by provision of solar lights around water and sanitation facilities will reduce risk. ADA will work with communities in order to increase access to the disabled persons and ensure that persons with disability will receive necessary intervention without discrimination and stigma.The team will be trained on PSEA
