Project Summary

NRC will provide and improve access to protective, quality education in Tripoli and Benghazi, reaching approximately 5800 vulnerable and out-of-school children and adolescents (Libyan and non-Libyan) with non-formal education classes and PSS, improvements to the school environment, material distribution and will train and support 318 non-formal and formal teachers.

Needs

• Based on the latest DTM and multi-sectoral MSNA, 15% of Libyan children do not attend school regularly, citing security, displacement and issues of quality as barriers. Disabled children are less likely to be attending as there is not a conductive environment to meet their needs.• Physical learning environments have suffered from the violence. The conflict has damaged vital education infrastructure, 489 schools had been affected by the crisis of which 40 have been fully damaged, 423 partially damaged, and 26 are accommodating Internally Displaced People (IDPs). • In Benghazi, 100% of mahallas report not to have access to education for refeugee/migrant children. In Tripoli, 79% report the same. Migrant/refugee children face insurmountable issues in regards to language, legal documentation, discrimination and pathways to access accredited education. • According to an unpubished UNICEF study regarding violence against children, 66.87% of children report being subject to physical violence perpetrated by teachers in formal schools, with ethnicity and gender cited as correlating factors. Many children are reported to have been subject to sexual and emotional abuse. • The Libyan conflict has caused children to suffer from psychosocial distress or trauma and the school system is not equipped to address such issues. Teachers are not trained in child-centered pedagogy, psychosocial support, child protection or disciplinary methods alternative to violence. Referral pathways to access health, child protection, or GBV services are non-existent. • Non-formal learning opportunities are not available for non-Libyan children and adolescents to access education or training. Non-Arabic speaking young people have virtually no access to education (especially in French or English). Adolescents and youth have little access to training opportunities, or to continue their secondary or tertiary education. • Legal pathways have not been defined for non-Libyan children to enter Libyan schools or access accredited education. Additionally there are few resources for students to receive support with obtaining legal documentation to meet the Libyan requirements. • Little data exists regarding out of school Libyan and non-Libyan children, including their locations and obstacles.

Activities

• Urgent, child-safe rehabilitation of educational infrastructure, including improvements to WASH facilities (priorities based on health risks)• Distribution of teaching and learning materials that encourage child-centered pedagogy • Provision of teacher training to formal and non-formal teachers on child protection, improved teaching methods and psychosocial support • Provision of non-formal education classes including psychosocial support to out of school, at-risk and non-Libyan children. This activity includes payment of non-formal education teachers who are not on government payroll, Libyan and non-Libyan• Identification of pathways and support for legal documentation for non-Libyans to access formal or accredited education • Establishment or strengthening of referral pathways for Health, Child Protection and GBV services • Re-establishment or strengthening of Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) in vulnerable schools • Provision of training to non-teaching education personnel, including NNGOs and local education authorities, on key topics of Education in Emergencies

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

Since NRC will be working in diverse communities with refugees and migrants, FGDs will be held regularly as part of the outreach components to identify the barriers to access, including the safety of the girls, boys, women and men as they travel to the learning centers. Non-formal teachers will be recruited from non-Libyan communities so as they are represented within the centers and can help NRC tailor the interventions to the specific needs of children and adolescents. Regular monitoring with the parents through parental engagement and PTAs will discuss issues of access and safety. Through the Adolescent Girl Clubs, NRC will also understand how specifically adolescent girls are able to access (or not) and we will use peer-to-peer engagement techniques in outreach.
