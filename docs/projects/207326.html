Project Summary

This project started in January 2021. As part of this intervention, AAH provides technical and organizational support to the health centers of the two health districts regularly affected by shocks in order to better equip them to respond to health / nutrition emergencies and to address psychological distress. Health workers and community volunteers participated in trainings on health / nutrition / mental health and care practices so they can better address the different needs and facilitate referral to activities provided by AAH and other actors in the region. AAH sets up mobile clinics (MC) to provide emergency assistance in nutrition / health / mental health for affected and isolated vulnerable populations in the Mayo Sava (MS) & Mayo Tsanaga (MT) divisions. The package of services and care is delivered free of charge. It targets children under five (U5), pregnant and lactating women (PLW), but also disabled people and the elderly among the newly displaced. With regard to the MHCP (mental health and care practices) component, the activities targets children over six and adults with a specific focus on PLW to ensure they follow appropriate care practices and to limit the impact of traumatic stress on pregnancies and infants.
Project Targets: 59 374 individuals are targeted. This include Adults and children U5 and above, PLW including IDPs / Refugee, CHWs: including IDPs / Refugee, Health staff and health committee's members, community leaders including IDPs / Refugees, Persons With disabilities (PLD) including IDPs / Refugees, the elderly : 60 and > including IDPs / Refugee, Host populations.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

During the second quarter of 2023, the security environment remained volatile in the Far North region affected by the Lake Chad Basin conflict. Violence, often characterised by incursions by non-state armed groups (NSAGs) and kidnappings of civilians, continues to force thousands of people to move to safer areas. Some 54 civilians were abducted between April and June 2023. Violence and insecurity displaced almost 30,000 people between January and August 2023. Humanitarian partners have carried out several assessments of the needs of displaced populations and host communities, which have led to urgent response activities. However, many needs remain unmet, depriving thousands of people of health care, including mental health and psychosocial support, nutritional assistance, education and protection. (Cameroon: Situation report 2 October 2023-OCHA).The rains have also frequently caused roads to fall into disrepair, affecting access to certain areas. The lack of ressources, linked to underfunding, remains a major challenge for the humanitarian response in the region. The constant change of location weakens people's resilience at individual, family and community level, and limits their access to adequate primary healthcare. Women and children are particularly vulnerable and exposed to greater violence, including abuse and exploitation.All these events increase the need for mental health and psychosocial support. ACF will complement the ongoing RRM project with MHPSS, health and nutrition activities. The approach will also enable a wider coverage and genuine lifesaving interventions. This approach was developed during the implementation of the current GFFO funded project and through the deployment of emergency integrated MHPSS and Health &Nutrition activities along with the RRM.
