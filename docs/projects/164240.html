Project Summary

The project designed to provide integrated services for W&G to meet all their protection needs (psychological and physical) in four Safe Spaces for W&G and their community centers managed by WSA in KafrLucin, KafrTakhrim, Binnish, and Jarablus.
Which are:
GBV
• Promoting the existing community protection mechanisms through upgrading provided services
• Investing in the positive impact achieved through the previous activities in the centers, which made the LC more accepting and supporting GBV activities.
• Upgrading the services and activities that WSA is conducting (PSS+GBV CM+ GBV referrals+PSEA+CBCM+Case fund+Cash-based response+ PFA for new IDPs).
• Increasing meaningful access for all vulnerable W&G, throughout the following:
- Provide quality GBV services (prevention & response) to most vulnerable, at-risk, and survivors’ W&G.
response activities including:
(Focused PSS for GBV survivors, GBV Case management, including a responsive case fund, by qualiﬁed caseworkers (trained by IRC and UNFPA), And Referral to other services.
Prevention services including:
Empowerment and awareness activities for women and adolescent girls (Reproductive Health awareness, Life Skills sessions, RE, community messages).
- Outreach activities will include conduct parenting skills and GBV awareness and information sessions.
MA:
• W&G with disabilities including survivors of explosive hazards incidents will be reached by specialized services and humanitarian assistance which includes, PSS, referrals, Case funds, Cash-based assistance, capacity building, vocational training for the survivors and their families.
• WSA Field staff will receive refreshment training about the special needs of the PWDs, and best practices to work with them.
• Provide awareness sessions to community leaders regarding the rights and protection of PWDs.
• Conduct community-based survey and in-depth needs assessment for PWDs Currently.
- Providing RE sessions in the communities and for the non-mine action specialized humanitarian worker, community RE focal points networks.
• Provide specialized and inclusive services and humanitarian assistance to W&GwD including survivors of explosive hazards incident.
• Reduce the risk and the impact of ERWs by providing RE sessions in the communities and for the non-mine action specialized humanitarian worker, including establishing a community focal points networks in the three locations targeting mainly W&G.
All these will be for the sake of reaching the most vulnerable, neglected and marginalized categories of beneﬁciaries (Survivors with disabilities-Widows-vulnerable adolescent girls) and giving more access for mothers with multiple young children. Which means more accountability for vulnerable W&G beneﬁciaries.
GP:
Cash assistance
Protection monitoring
CP:
Enhance the CFS for children of the women beneﬁciaries in each center (with PSS + basic non-formal education + Child daycare activities) (depending upon the ages).
Emergency Response:
All of the activities are designed flexibly to be transformed into emergency response intervention by decreasing the indoor activities and enhance the outreach activities, and the WSA team has got deep experience in responding to emergencies.
• Emergency response to new IDPs arrivals (PFA, receiving and orienting activities, Protection monitoring, and GBV case management).
• distribution of IEC material, PSS, Capacity Building for local initiatives and associations.
• Identifying the most vulnerable cases and providing case funds and cash assistance.
in case of large escalation in one of the planned location WSA have planes to move to more stable locations like Afrin, Azaz, Albab, and Telabyad some of these areas are already assessed by WSA team and the needs are already high, and some of them are under assessment. WSA has good experience in responding to emergency situations and has developed procedures for the response, evacuation, and beneficiaries and staff tracking.

4 - Needs

For the civilian population in Syria, hostilities, insecurity, and protection risks continue to be the key driversof the crisis, leading to large-scale displacement and massive humanitarian needs, particularly for people inthe north-west and north according to HNO 2020. From 1 December 2019 to 2 February 2020, over half a million people displaced in northwest Syria. Some 200,000 of these individuals displaced over one week from 26 January to 2 February. Since late November, airstrikes and shelling gradually increased.In a needs assessment conducted in Jul 2019 by WSA MEAL field staff, 46 KIs were interviewed from the local authority, NGO staff (protection background), teachers or school administrators and medical staff. The methodology of the assessment was developed by WSA. Three of MEAL field female staff who have good capacity in protection monitoring conducted the assessment. The questionnaires were done in the communities where WSA has WGSS. When discussing the coping mechanisms in the last three weeks, results show the following: 13% said that restriction of movement for women and girls is always, and 15% said it is more common now. Besides, 24% said that child marriage is also more common and 39% said it is common now. Access to the aid services 9% said that they never benefited from such service, while just 11% answered that they have access to the centers. As asking about the humanitarian assistance in their area, 2% were not able to answer, 4% answered negatively, and only 11% said that they benefit regularly and easily from the available assistance in the area, also, 67% of answers show that some of the community members received assistance like basic need, health, PSS, Woman protection and WASH. When asked directly whether girls are affected, or at risk, by violence, exploitation or neglect, 9% answered positively that most of the girls are at risk, while 41% answered that some of the girls are affected by the mention protection risks.

Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also leads to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks are divided into security and program risks Security risks: A- Shelling / It is one of the major threats in Syria, especially with the escalation of military conﬂict in northern Syria in this period. The shelling can be direct or indirect . S/ M measures get prepared for all the scenarios and prepare the security and emergency plans to be implemented as necessary. B- Unforeseen Changes in the Conﬂict / This risk including that staff of an organization cannot safely access locations, staff gets besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. S/ M measures: 1- Continue to gather information from a range of sources. 2- Develop contingency and evacuation plans to work in an alternative location. 3- Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space. new regulation for working in the operation location, S/M liaise with all the authority and the stakeholders D- The intervention of Armed groups in humanitarian action/ Its common risk, especially extremist groups, to obstruct humanitarian action (blackmail) Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- The organization's obligation not to provide any information about the donor to any military faction interventions and other related information. 2- WSA policy is clear, do not to make a concession. 3- Coordination with civilian local authorities E- Flocculation of Dollar rate and issues of money transfer, Mitigation: 1- WSA is using OWNDA &QUICK BOOKS tools of cash monitoring and follow-up. 2- Sending money to Syria by PTT (Turkish government postal and telegraph organization)
