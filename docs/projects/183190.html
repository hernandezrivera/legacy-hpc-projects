Project Summary

Relief International (RI) will increase access to basic needs of vulnerable Lebanese and migrant communities in Beirut, Mount Lebanon, and North regions of Lebanon through provision of Muti Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA). With a focused cash response, RI aims to improve the overall wellbeing and status of households (HHs) in the targeted areas and with the security of cash inputs to meet their array of needs. RI has extensive experience implementing MPCA programs in Lebanon, particularly over the last five years, providing unconditional MPCA of $175 to 980 HHs (880 Syrians and 100 Lebanese) in North Lebanon (T5 and Akkar), Bekaa (Central, West and North), and BML for ten months.
Due to the continually worsening economic situation in Lebanon, Lebanese and migrants HHs across the country have been pushed into severe economic difficulty. With limited financial capabilities, HHs are unable to seek or obtain essential services and needs (medical, foods, and daily requirements). With major fuel shortages curbing movement and limited employment prospects, HHs are in desperate need of support to provide them with emergency needs. According to the World Bank, GDP declined by 20.3% alone in 2020, and economists at Bloomberg reported a 400% increase in food prices. This is also echoed in IOM’s recent Needs and Vulnerability Assessment of Migrants in Lebanon (May 2021), stating that of 589 migrants, 50% were unable to meet their food needs, and of 529 respondents 50% reported being unemployed. Clearly, the prolonged, economic deterioration, social and political tensions, and major shortages continue to undermine food security, work prospects and access to basic needs and services of Lebanese and migrants across Lebanon.
Aligned to the Food Security cluster strategic objectives, RI will address the basic needs of 5,715 individuals from 1,500 HHs (1,215 Lebanese HHs (4,860 individuals) and 285 migrant HHs (855 individuals)) in Beirut, Mount Lebanon, and North (500 HHs per region). RI will provide $23 monthly assistance in accordance with the SMEB rate for all MPCA beneficiary HHs currently being used by all partners in accordance with the Basic Assistance Sector. RI will use the same proven successful delivery mechanism utilized in its current Cash For Education (CFE) program providing monthly transfers through BOB Finance and notifying beneficiaries upon every loading through an SMS alert, with extensive follow-up. For the identified beneficiaries, RI will conduct a validation/verification process and screen HHs’ bio/meta data. RI will also provide training to all beneficiaries receiving MPCA assistance to ensure their understanding of the system, their rights, and services provided. Since 2014, RI has implemented as a central component of all cash programming more than 40 distribution and validation events such as beneficiary training, project sensitization, cash transfers, and cash assistance payment modalities. As such, RI has ready developed training materials to meet the understanding of the varied targeted population. RI will conduct four surveys with a random stratified sample created on district level from the target beneficiary population: 1) A baseline survey (quantitative questionnaire) prior to the assistance if the caseload is new to RI ii) An exit monitoring survey which will be done during the distribution sessions iii) A PDM survey after two weeks from the first loading, and iv) An OM assessment at the end of the assistance cycle. RI actively participates in Basic Assistance and Food Security working groups at national and field level. RI is co chair of the Basic Assistance Working Group and sits on the Basic Assistance Core Group.

RI’s teams will increase access to basic needs and services, through the provision of focused MPCA. This intervention will help decrease issues stemming from the extremely dire economic situation and unemployment.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Key project activities related to 1.3: number of individuals provided with basic assistance through cash will include:- Set targeting criteria for identification of beneficiaries- Validation of selected beneficiaries- Training sessions regarding RI’s project objectives, the purpose of the cash assistance, how to receive the cash assistance, how to best protect themselves, COVID-19 precautions measures, safeguarding reporting, and how to use RI’s complaints and feedback mechanism.- Monthly execution of cash assistance- Monitoring activities (post-distributions, outcome monitoring)

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

RI will abide by key humanitarian principles to maintain integrated protection, gender and social inclusion and ensure prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). With engagement of communities at design phase, and monitoring of implementation, RI will provide services to all, regardless of age, gender, and nationality. Essential gender and protection activities across the project will protect all vulnerable, eligible groups, such as sharing key messages on protection principles by posters, flyers, pamphlets and field staff; RI’s hotline for feedback and reporting will be visible in all target areas to report any incidents/suspected cases of violation and abuse; and feedback mechanisms will be available in languages, formats and media appropriate and accessible to all groups. Through capacity building trainings, RI and partner staff will be trained on and accountable to uphold PSEA policies and practices. In addition, RI has signed the inter-agency SOPS on PSEA.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

RI will adhere to the Interagency Standing Committee (IASC) five commitments: 1) Leadership/Governance through RI global Humanitarian Accountability guidelines that guarantee Feedback and Complaints Response mechanism (FCRM), enforce protection measures for staff and beneficiaries, and ensure a gender balance; 2) Transparency and Information Sharing through awareness sessions about types of services provided and available mechanisms for feedback in appropriate languages and accessible media to beneficiaries; 3) Participation of Beneficiaries at all levels of project cycle from different age groups, nationalities and gender; 4) Design, Monitoring and Evaluation by tracking performance Indicators, conducting data reflection meetings and evaluations including a requirement to involve affected communities; 5) Feedback and Complaints collected from all beneficiaries to ensure queries are responded on time, meeting concerns of affected populations in need of humanitarian assistance.
