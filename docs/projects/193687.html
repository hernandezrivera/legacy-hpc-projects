Project Summary

The proposed action intends to respond to the humanitarian needs in the educational sector and support the most vulnerable populations affected by conflicts and the protracted humanitarian crisis in Gaza Strip through the provision of quality and equitable services for the most affected students, thus, strengthening their abilities to cope with the changes in education modalities which consequently change to respond to the humanitarian crises. The proposed project will respond to the Strategic Objective 1, 2 and 3 it aims at enhancing the educational environment in Gaza strip by achieving two main outcomes: 1. Enhanced access to distance learning in governmental schools, 2. Improved psychological status of the most affected students in targeted schools. The first outcome will focus on improving the distance learning system (E-services Portal) of the MOE and the abilities of the students and teachers in addition to upgrading ICT infrastructures in the targeted 5 schools. Furthermore, the project will contribute to enhance the access to distance learning among the most vulnerable children by the Provision of 100 iPads and help them to keep Intouch with the upgraded educational system. The second outcome will work at improving the psychological status of the most affected students in targeted schools by targeting 500 students in summer camps activities. 100 from each one of the 5 targeted schools will benefit from both STEM and Recreational activities which will help them to improve their mental and psychological status. Furthermore, five schools will benefit from recreational activities.
The project will implement in coordination with MOE & CBOs within the period from 01/01/2023 to 31/12/2023 (one year project). With a total budget $ 266,340.

1 - Needs

The need for enhancing the learning module in Gaza schools is highly needed and this project came to responds to national and international assessments for the educational needs in Palestine and especially in Gaza strip. And to define these specific needs accurately the triangulation Mechanism was conducted. Where focus groups with MOE departments were held to define the expressed needs and normative needs. Furthermore, there was a significant figure and cross cutting issues led to focus on our mentioned interventions as mentioned below:1. According to MSNA interactive dashboard 2022 the households needs support in education sector where the findings show that: the percentage of households reporting a need for catch-up learning due to school closures due to COVID-19 or other reasons, by household refugee status 81.2 % from the refugee households and 76.7% from non-refugee households.2. The prolonged school closures due to COVID 19, escalations in Gaza and West Bank and teachers strikes have caused a huge learning loss issue for students. MSNA states that around 70% of children are need of catch-up programs.3. 9.3% of Gaza households reported having a child with a disability, and perceived additional challenges for children with a disability (mental and physical) in accessing educational services were reported by 93.5% of households.4. According to educational list needs for 2022 – 2023 HNO, there is a general lack of school-based psychosocial support for children in Gaza who face regular attacks and harassment to, from and during school, and still dealing with psychological distress as a consequence of the recurrent hostilities, based on the Sectoral PiN Aggregation Model for the education cluster around 57,000 HHs in Gaza reported that at least one child is showing signs of psychosocial distress, moreover, around 84,000 HHs with school-aged children in Gaza, Area C, Hebron H2 and East Jerusalem reported that psychosocial support is not available at the school for children if needed In addition to PSS activities, children studying in these schools in particular lack opportunities to participate in extracurricular activities.5. SDG 4: Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all.6. There are still concerns that online learning may have been a sub-optimal substitute for face-to-face instruction, especially so in the absence of universal access to infrastructure (hardware and software) and lack of adequate preparation among teachers and students for the unique demands that online teaching learning pose.7. Information and Communications Technology (ICT) can impact student learning when teachers are digitally literate and understand how to integrate it into curriculum.According to the above-mentioned points, the detailed needs for the project intervention are:• Enhancing teacher capacities in the fields of digital resources for pedagogical practice and promoting teaching practices adapted to this context is key to ensure that ICT is leveraged effectively.• Developing strong attitudes towards learning is crucial in supporting students using information and communications technology (ICT) effectively and making the most of new technologies for learning. Positive attitudes towards learning, self-regulation and intrinsic motivation to learn play an important role in improving performance at school in general, but may be especially important should online learning continue.• Strengthening the engagement between schools and parents in order to improve information and guidance to parents on effective practices for supporting their children’s learning. • Maintain the mental and psychological health of the students and teachers by implementing recreational days inside the targeted schools.• Upgrading the portal of electronic services system in the MOE in order to facilitate the effective communication between students, teachers and HHs.• Enhancing the accessibility of the most vulnerable students to distance learning by providing tablets.• Improving the psychosocial wellbeing for the children in need (Most vulnerable children who affected by Pandemic or escalations) and in the targeted schools by implementing extracurricular activities through summer holiday. These activities include psychological support activities, STEM activities and recreational activities.• Improving the ICT environment in the targeted schools by providing the minimum acceptable infrastructure for ICT.

2 - Activities

• Teachers’ professional development activities: that aim to increase their ability to use ICT for formative learning assessments, individualized instruction, accessing online resources, and for fostering student interaction and collaboration.• Provide school with the minimum acceptable infrastructure for ICT, including stable and affordable internet connectivity and security measures such as filters and site blockers.• Provide the most vulnerable students with tablets to facilitate their access to internet and benefited from distance learning services and cash their peers in the educational journey.• Implement recreational days inside the targeted schools where every school will benefit from one recreational day.• Enhance the e-services portal of the Ministry of Education website to include digital communication platform that would strengthen engagement between schools and parents in order to improve information and guidance to parents on effective practices for supporting their children’s distance learning.• Conduct extracurricular activities through summer holiday. These activities are STEM activities and recreational activities.
