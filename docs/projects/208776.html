Project Summary

Nkafamiya Rescue Mission (NRM) child protection project will focus on strengthening access to quality preventive and responsive child protection services that address the needs, rights and dignity of children and their caregivers in the conflict affected states of Borno & Adamawa and Yobe (BAY). The emergency response will prioritize the most vulnerable including at-risk children and their families. NRM’s efforts to address child protection and gender-based violence in emergencies is part of its over-all strategy on prevention and response to all forms of violence against children (VAC).
Results will be achieved by providing integrated child protection case management services for children including family tracing and reunification for UASC and delivering quality and sustainable socio-economic reintegration services to children formerly associated with armed groups as well as other vulnerable children and their families. In addition, conflict-affected children will have access to community-based mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) including structured recreational activities and adolescents’ life skills education. The project will also contribute to strengthening capacity of service delivery to GBV survivors. Child and adult survivors of conflict-related sexual violence and GBV will in addition have access to comprehensive minimum lifesaving services including medical, psychosocial, legal and safety
Using a nexus-approach, this project will also support the efforts of peaceful coexistence, transitional justice, reinforcing existing local and national capacities including a focus on climate change mitigation where possible.

1 - Needs

The armed conflict in north-east Nigeria can be characterized as a protection crisis. Armed groups and security forces both continue to commit crimes under international humanitarian law and international human rights law and have a severe impact on the protection of children. In 2021, the United Nations verified 250 grave violations against 208 children (92 girls) in north-east Nigeria. Most violations of recruitment and use by Boko Haram affiliated or splinter groups, the Islamic State West Africa Province (ISWAP). The insurgency in Northeast of Nigeria took an unprecedented turn since July 2021 leading to large number of individuals, including fighters, exiting armed groups and presenting themselves to security authorities, largely in Borno State. Sustaining this momentum of exits presents challenges to effective process of handling and treatment of the individuals led by government authorities and ensuring their reintegration into communities amidst an ongoing conflict, an internal displacement situation and significant intergenerational grievances stemming from the longstanding conflict. Forced displacement and returns impose high risk on separated and unaccompanied children. Boys and adolescent male risk forcible recruitment by armed groups, or suspicion of association with armed groups by authorities. Civilians continue to suffer death and injury from explosive ordnance, including the use of explosive weapons in populated areas. Protection needs are formidable with women and girls under the threat of violence, abduction and rape. Gender-based violence (GBV) including sexual violence and forced and child marriages continue to be reported and are attributed to the conflict, insecurity and poor living conditions in IDP camps and informal settlements. In 2021, about 2,500 cases of GBV survivors were supported through case management in the north-east. The risk of SGBV in conflict contexts including north-east Nigeria, is exacerbated by several factors. These include the breakdown of family and community support systems; the presence of armed forces or combatants in displaced settlements; weak legal systems and law enforcement; abuse of power by individuals in positions of authority; laws and practices that reinforce gender discrimination; inadequate access to shelter, food, water and fuel; and inadequate access to livelihood opportunities. Two per cent (88 out of the 4,388 cases) of reported and documented incidents of GBV were perpetrated against survivors with a disability.

2 - Activities

Key activities: 1. Family tracing and reunification for unaccompanied and separated children and referral of vulnerable children to access inter-sectoral and other specialized services. 2. Children deprived of parental care, or whose parental care is assessed as inadequate, are provided with timely, reliable and safe alternative care arrangements with prioritization of family-based solutions 3. Identification, assessment, train and support to foster parents including supervision during the provision of foster care (kinship care). 4. Provide confidential and dignified professional case management services for GBV survivors 5. Strengthen existing or establish functional community-based complaint mechanisms for SEA reporting and survivor assistance. 6. Provision of centre-based interim care services for girls, boys and young people formerly associated with armed groups 7. Provide specialized psychosocial support services to children, adolescents, and caregivers affected by mental-health distress 8. Deliberately target girls, boys and adults living with disabilities with integrated support services 9. Provide structured life skills education to girls, boys and caregivers at risk 10. Provide mental health psychosocial support services to children, adolescents, and caregivers through community structures and open spaces 11. Deliver structured training sessions for parents on positive discipline methods to address behavioral problems in children and adolescents 12. Support the registration of all under-5 and issue birth certificates 13. Raise awareness at community level on child protection with specific focus on grave violation against children, unexploded remnant of war (UXO), GBV and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) within a given community 14. Train community-based child protection committee members on their roles and key thematic areas of child protection 15. Train child protection partners, social welfare staff on child protection and related areas

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NRM will collaborate with other agencies, communities, and stakeholders to develop a more holistic environment of protection for vulnerable groups, displaced populations, and children at risk. The proposed Programme is focused on mainstreaming protection. NRM will deploy the principle of “Do no harm” and employ appropriate measures to avoid exposing people to further harm as a result of project actions and ensure that the environment and way in which assistance is provided do not expose people to further hazards, violence or human rights abuses or violations. NRM will manage information in a sensitive manner so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized, NRM will ensure that the affected population have access to humanitarian assistance against any form of direct or indirect discrimination. NRM will engage with the target population at all stages, and the participation of all in the design and targeting of interventions, in particular vulnerable and marginalized groups, women, girls, boys, men, people with disabilities, old age, women headed HH, child headed HH and pregnant, lactating and mothers married girl children. All implementing partner staff, will undergo gender-based violence (GBV) and protection from sexual exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training before they begin working in the program. This is to both help prevent GBV and PSEA but also to spot potential warning signs of gender exploitation and abuse in targeted communities.
