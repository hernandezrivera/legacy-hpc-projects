Project Summary

The project is designed to improve protection services for vulnerable GBV victims, IDPs camps in Nugal, Mudug and Bari Regions in Puntland that enable and strengthen protection of affected populations through protection monitoring, reporting, coordination, and advocacy, address acute protection needs stemming from violence, coercion, and abuse and mitigate risks, uphold the rights, dignity, and well-being of individuals affected by protection violations, prevent further abuse, and strengthen resilience, and create a protection conducive environment.
The crisis affects individuals of all ages and genders, as well as from diverse social backgrounds, in different ways and protection needs may evolve over time. Somalia’s societal landscape is highly granular, numerous social groups clans and ethnic groups and divisions between those groups remain one of the dominant characteristics of society. Livelihood strategies vary, and some of these groups have comparatively less social capital, resulting in reduced clan-based protection and potentially less (financial) support during crises. Negative coping strategies to adverse circumstances may emerge and are aggravated by displacement and food insecurity: according to a recent assessment 81 per cent of children interviewed had noticed a change in their parents’ behaviour since the start of the drought. Harmful coping strategies include family separation, early marriage, child labour, and other forms of abuse, while distress exposes women to physical (intimate partner) violence. Boys and girls including adolescents, children with disabilities in IDPs and host communities, facing life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress have access to well-coordinated and disability, gender-sensitive quality child protection services. This will be achieved through strengthening community-based child protection structures to raise awareness, prevent, respond and refer cases of violence against children, providing case management support and referral services to children with protection concerns including SGBV.
In general, women and girls are frequently victims of GBV within IDP communities, including rape and sexual assault, raising grave concerns about the safety and security in IDP sites. Families with members with specific needs (i.e. serious health conditions, disability) are at heightened risk in adverse circumstances and might require specialized life-saving assistance, as well as interventions to mitigate further exposure to rights violations such as extortion, taxation, and abuse while the affected individual may suffer from violence neglect. Populations in drought affected areas controlled by non-state armed actors, especially men and male youth from pastoralist communities, are reported to experience hampered access to areas where assistance is provided. Ensuing family separation results in elevated protection risk exposure for women and children, also due to the risk of exploitation and abuse by gatekeepers. The complex nature of protection violations and needs, as well as insecurity and a lack of access to affected populations, inhibits comprehensive data gathering with sufficient detail on protection violations and needs across Somalia. Innovative approaches to protection monitoring and data analysis, at individual and community level, remain a priority, ensuring protection needs and concerns are identified and enabling targeted protection service delivery to and evidence-based as advocacy on behalf of those who are most affected and vulnerable in Nugal, Mudug and Bari regions. SKN has been working delivering GBV projects in Puntland for a number of years with MoWDAFA and have observed that gender based violence is a indeed a social threat scenario. The project will target 9204 persons (1534 households) including women and girls victims, survivors and persons with disabilities from IDPs, and poor host communities.

Mitigation Measures

In order to mitigate the above identified risks Stichting Kaalo Nederland will carry out the following measures (most of which address more than one risk);  Empowering girls and boys - Children play a key role in protecting themselves and each other, therefore Stichting Kaalo Nederland will empower girls and boys to protect themselves according to their specific and unique needs. This will be achieved through participatory methods to facilitate discuss protection issues and plan their own actions to improve their situation and influence adults around them. Community mobilization: Like IDPs, refugees, vulnerable women and girls and vulnerable communities are often the first line of protection for children, but also a source of harm, so we always engage and mobilize community actors in our child protection and GBV programs. Community mobilization is a capacity building process through which local individuals, groups or organizations identify needs, plan, carry out and evaluate activities on a participatory and sustained basis, so as to improve the protection of children, vulnerable girls and women from IDPs. In community mobilization, we support women’s groups, establish community child protection, and empowering of girls and women focal points or groups or committees, facilitate community dialogues and conversations around child protection issues, support child rights clubs, youth groups and facilitate mass media campaigns such as radio programs and messaging.  Stichting Kaalo Nederland will prepare a beneficiary selection criterion in close collaboration with local leaders and community representatives to ensure transparency and selection of beneficiaries.  Stichting Kaalo Nederland will apply effective data protection measures to ensure that beneficiary data is kept confidential and safe from unintended use.  All SKN staff is trained in, GBV, Child Protection and Sexual harassment. Stichting Kaalo Nederland will be facilitated GBV survivors to seek assistance in a confidential and dignified manner that does not expose them to further stress.
