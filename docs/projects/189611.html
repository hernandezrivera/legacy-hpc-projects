Project Summary

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022, about 6.6 million people has inadequate or lack of access to safe water, and sanitation in South Sudan. Insecurity, displacement, natural disasters such as heavy flooding for the last three years have compounded the impact of low access to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services in the country. The complex operating environment combined with accessibility challenges indicates that in-kind provision of lifesaving WASH supplies continues to be one of the most significant modality for emergency responses in the country.
Since 2014, IOM has been successfully managing a portion of the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) Core Pipeline, which is an essential support to the WASH Cluster in providing critical services to Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) partners.
Under the HRP 2022, IOM will maintain its commitment support the WASH Cluster Pipeline through the provision of key WASH supplies to benefit a total of 1.2 million people in need. Procured supplies will be transported for prepositioning in strategic logistic hubs throughout the country, namely Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Rumbek, and Wau to ensure efficiency by contributing to the reduction of response time through moving the supplies closer to final distribution sites. The Pipeline system optimizes economies of scale, reduction of cost, harmonized quality of items, non-duplication and supply chain efficiencies. The distribution will be prioritized to directly benefit vulnerable people affected by displacement, conflict, natural disasters (such as the heavy seasonal rains and flooding), disease outbreak, malnutrition, famine/food insecurity, and GBV risks.

1 - Needs

People in South Sudan continue to face bleak humanitarian conditions, despite its continued pursuit of peace and stability. The compounded effect of the complex protracted humanitarian crisis; intermittent sub-national, endemic violence; displacement; inadequate government investment to infrastructures; recurrent long and heavy seasonal rains; have left an estimated 6.1 million people in South Sudan negatively impacted by the lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices. The limited access to safe water as well as basic hygiene materials and practices, contribute to the spread of diseases (e.g. Hepatitis E, cholera, COVID-10) and contribute to famine and malnutrition that remains a threat to a country that is already facing inadequate health facilities to address health concerns. According to the 2022 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview, the situation in the next three to six months is projected to worsen, driven by the impact of heavy flooding across multiple states with some counties in the Upper Nile State still under water despite the dry season. As one of the leading pipeline agencies in South Sudan, IOM manages a portion of the country’s WASH pipeline and supports the WASH Cluster in providing partner organizations with timely access to life-saving materials for their emergency responses. Depending on the local context and severity of needs, the WASH Cluster and pipeline teams will maintain a context-specific approach to responses in prioritized locations. The WASH supplies provided to partners’ responses are determined through assessments of the needs of target communities and will include prioritized key lifesaving items. The most vulnerable and underserved populations will be targeted, specifically, those displaced (including protracted and new displacements, inside and outside of IDP sites), at risk of GBV, malnutrition, famine/food insecurity, and those affected by environmental disasters. IOM's extensive experience in pipeline management has built its capacity and expertise to meet partner needs in a complex environment characterized by major logistical and accessibility challenges, including surges of insecurity and a lack of basic infrastructure. South Sudan has a very poor road network with less than 100 kilometers of paved road. Most markets, especially in hard-to-reach areas have collapsed as a result of the protracted conflict, and the affected population cannot access the most basic of needs. About 70 percent of the country's road network becomes inaccessible during the rainy season, which has increasingly lengthened from May to December for the past three years. Due to the magnitude of flooding in Unity State in 2021, one of the major logistics hubs in Bentiu, is still inaccessible more than halfway through the dry season, which is having an impact on prepositioning for the incoming rainy season, which is forecasted to come earlier in 2022. In general, airstrips within South Sudan are of poor quality, with only a few airstrips used continuously in the wet and dry seasons. During the dry season, insecurity along the main supply routes is a significant challenge restricting movement and increasing transportation costs due to a high-risk operational environment for transporters. Transportation by road where/when possible remains the most cost-effective means of delivery. The preposition of supplies in key-hubs locations across the country is important as part of WASH Cluster's crucial effort to ensure swift access to emergency relief items in a coordinated approach.

2 - Project activities

IOM core pipeline will provide critical WASH supplies to implementing partners so that the most vulnerable and underserved have improved access to safe water; sanitation and hygiene relief supplies in a timely manner. The targeting will prioritize the most vulnerable groups specifically, those displaced (including protracted and new displacements) by conflict and natural disasters (such as flooding), at risk of GBV, disease outbreak, and/or those facing nutritional crisis, famine and food insecurity. Women and adolescent girls of menstrual age in school and/or community will receive crucial support, such as menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits, to cater to their specific sanitary needs with dignity. Vulnerable children affected by moderate and severe acute malnutrition (MAM/SAM) will be prioritized to receive the WASH NFI and hygiene kits designed for household usage. Critical WASH supplies, such as soap and household water treatment, that will help to curb transmission of infectious diseases will be included as part of WASH NFI supplies to be distributed for beneficiaries. The following activities are proposed: - Procure emergency WASH supplies for the humanitarian response following assessed needs, targeting 1.2M individuals- Administer and process partners' approved request for distribution- Coordinate with the WASH cluster, partners, Logistics cluster, sub-national coordinators, and all other stakeholders relevant for pipeline operations- Management of the WASH Cluster pipeline request system, and participation in WASH Cluster Core Pipeline technical working group to provide additional assistance and support to the cluster- Transport procured WASH pipeline supplies to six IOM logistic hubs outside Juba (Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek) and other possible partner warehouses across the country for prepositioning of stocks- Appropriate storage and management of the WASH emergency supplies at IOM warehouses in Juba, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek, and Bentiu- Regular monitoring of WASH core pipeline supply delivery, supplies quality control, storage management, and supplies utilization- Conduct post-distribution monitoring of select WASH partner response to gather beneficiary feedback on the quality of the items distributed, assess the appropriateness and effectiveness of the response, and assess mainstreaming of cross-cutting issues- Provide capacity-building for Pipeline partners - Undertake pipeline survey with partners for the efficiency, appropriateness, and coverage of the pipeline service- Actively participate in operations working group, technical working groups, cluster meetings, and any other forum or gathering within the WASH and inter-cluster operations to ensure a coordinated approach in all WASH activities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

- IOM Pipeline activities mainly deal with partner agencies and their focal points in WASH program and logistics and has only limited interaction with beneficiaries during post-distribution monitoring activities. In any humanitarian emergency, the partner agencies conduct an inclusive consultation process with the community leaders as well as the representatives of all groups within the community to assess needs of the affected population. Planning of the response starts here wherein the location of distributions centres and areas of assistance and services are discussed and agreed upon so that not only community leaders decide on important matters. Access and security of beneficiaries are the most important factors that are considered. Community members also take ownership of the process and tasks such as off-loading, guarding during the distribution, enumeration, and such are given to qualified members of the community. During actual response, security is also monitored by the response team. PDM surveys have questions that are directly related to safety of beneficiaries during and after distribution and results are taken into consideration so that the principle of “Do No Harm” is continuously ensured across all context of humanitarian response. - All WASH pipeline approved requests from partners go through a rigorous review process by the WASH cluster and its respective subnational coordinators, through which assessment reports and response/project documents are required for approval. The review considers Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and gauges how partner organizations target beneficiaries with priority given to the most vulnerable segments of the population. The WASH cluster also requires partners to undertake rapid need assessment (RNA) before a response and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) after a WASH response. There are recommended tools and templates to conduct these activities that categorically incorporates questions on inclusion, participation, and protection in order to measure effectiveness of the response in addressing the needs of the affected population. Findings and recommendations are given to ensure that issues are raised and addressed for future response.- IOM has in place a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IOM staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IOM Contractors. Extensive training and workshop on protection against SEA has been rolled out and all IOM Core Pipeline staff has undergone at least one training in the past year. IOM Core Pipeline also requires its WASH partners to sign a memorandum of understanding in order to promote and strengthen accountability. This agreement includes clauses on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse that partners are also obligated to strictly enforce since they directly interact and deal with beneficiaries.
