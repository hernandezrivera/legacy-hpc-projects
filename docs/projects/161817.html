Project Summary

To improve access to economic opportunities for vulnerable conflict- and displacement-affected populations. In order to ensure that the DRC programme response is relevant and appropriate it will be required to understand and address the needs of the different primary groups who are displaced and otherwise conflict-affected: - IDPs in host communities and camps - Returnees (from internal displacement or voluntary/forced repatriation from mainly Cameroon) - Host Communities (hosting IDPs or receiving returnees). DRC strives to address tailored economic recovery assistance needs of people affected by conflict and displacement.

1 - Needs

The crisis in Northern Nigeria have created an array of humanitarian needs that are predominantly protection in nature. The security and operating environment is fluid and fast changing. For those most at risk, immediate and often lifesaving assistance continues to be an absolute priority – particularly for those affected by military operations and/or attacks by Non State Armed Groups (NSAGs). Addressing the ever-growing needs of the affected civilian population (both IDPs and hosting communities) requires flexible resources and rapid response approaches. The ability to respond to numerous contingencies also necessitates a flexible, multi-sectorial approach to effectively respond to sudden, emerging, and evolving needs of affected communities. Now in its tenth year, the conflict in NE Nigeria continues as one of the largest and widespread humanitarian crises, negatively impacting the lives of millions of people affected by the ongoing hostilities, particularly women and children. Deep rooted issues of inequality, political exclusions and social marginalization are key factors fueling the conflict. An estimated 7.1 million (compared to 7.7 million last year) remain in need of humanitarian assistance in the states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) in 2019; out of these 7.1 million, 2.3 million are girls, 1.9 million boys, 1.6 million women and 1.3 million men. While the number of people requiring assistance has reduced over the year, primarily due to efforts of the humanitarian sector coupled with different assistance programs offered by the Government, a great number of people (more than 800,000 people) continue to live in areas controlled by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) and/or hard to reach areas in the North East, where access remains limited. The conflict has generated large population movements with current displacement figure standing at 1.9 million individuals (IOM DTM Round 26). DRC has conducted a broad scope of assessments that provide evidence of the nuances of interlinked need across multiple sectors. This action seeks to address some of these critical gaps through a holistic and protection-led approach. Up to 2019, DRC identified new trends in displacement linked to the evolution of the conflict, military operations, as well as the possibility to travel more easily in military convoys for the civilian population. In Western and Southern Borno State, as well as in Northern Adamawa State, three "corridors of displacement" with a trend in mobility of population, based on previous commercial main axes. All corridors are based on pre-existing populations staying as close to their area of origin as possible. The first corridor starts in Jere, along the Muna Corridor, and moves toward Mafa, Dikwa and ultimately Ngala. The second corridor encompasses Konduga, Bama and Banki, as well as Pulka and Gwoza. The third corridor follows Gwoza down to Madagali, Michika and Mubi. DRC's analysis showed that movement of displaced persons - whether returning to their areas of origin, trying to find a different level of humanitarian assistance or access to livelihoods - will remain the same in 2020, making mobility of assistance and consistency of presence and services along the corridors one of the main added value of the DRC programme.

2 - Activities

Activity 2.2.1: Small business management training for SME beneficiaries: A total of 1600 households (representing 11200 individuals - women and youth) will be supported with small business management trainings and business grants to improve their skills and increase their access to safer livelihood options for income opportunities and future recovery. Beneficiaries will engage in the viable small businesses identified by the 2017 DRC Livelihood Market Assessment for Adamawa and Borno States. Activity 2.2.2: Business planning: Business Management Trainings will support with disseminating information on financial planning that clearly justifies businesses having different start-up capital requirements. Beneficiaries will design business proposals that will describe business operational and financial plans. Activity 2.2.3: Business grants disbursement to beneficiaries valued at approximately 300 USD: The DRC Livelihood Market Assessment (LHMA) identfied marketable IGAs that are appropriate to the local needs of beneficiaries and their communities and where there are market gaps that can be exploited in Borno State. Existing viable income generating activities identified for Borno included: tailoring, groundnut oil extraction, petty trade, selling of fish, water melon, meat, dry pepper, groundnut, bed sheets and curtains, beans, bean cakes and goats. Beneficiaries will receive business grants in tranches. Activity 2.2.4: Follow-up and business advisory services: DRC will provide business advisory and technical support to the beneficiaries operating businesses (cash flow, cost benefit analysis and performance ratios such as gross profit ratio, return on equity and return on investments). DRC will also support beneficiaries to ensure that basic record keeping is in place. Activity 2.2.5 1500 household members (representing 10,500 individuals) will work 13 days per month for 12 months and shall be remunerated N 18,200 for unskilled work and N 22,750 for skilled work. Households will work to rehabilitate and protect community assets and receive cash as payment. DRC will work with relevant State Government line ministries to ensure technical standards are met.
