Project Summary

Given the current Syrian context, there is a need to integrate support to legal awareness and legal counselling, GBV prevention and response and mine-risk education into the humanitarian response as part of the protection cluster in order to address immediate and longer-term needs to rebuild communities. In this regard, UNDP will contribute towards the following outcomes and activities:
(1) Promotion of Social Cohesion through utilising UNDP Peace Agents
a. Use the platform of agents of change established by UNDP’s social cohesion team. The team of volunteers trained on community mobilisation, social engagement and community level dialogues will work with the target communities in order to harness harmony, sense of co-existence and respect for the rights of others in order to promote social cohesion among host communities, IDPs and returnees. These will support the following interventions from (as moving from being passive beneficiaries to active partners.
(2) Increased capacities to address GBV through prevention and responsive action at the community level within safe environments
a. Preventive measures and activities:
1. Conduct outreach and GBV awareness raising activities for the general public (GBV defined, COVID-19 impact of GBV, rights and legal consultation, survivor response, etc.)
2. Support women networking as part of peer-to-peer support – whereby women network members are the entry point to their communities, and start meaning participation (digital bloggers to amplify the voices of women during the COVID-19 crisis and community participation) in women’s movement and leadership in preventing violence. These women are provided skills enhancement to lead designing, developing and implementing their own community-based initiatives.
3. Men and boys Engagement through dialogue and awareness sessions to be aligned to eliminate violence against women and girls.
4. Conduct Campaign – create the demand for behavioural change.
b. Responsive measures and activities
1. Support to victims of GBV related response services (MHPSS Mental health psychosocial support – holistic support which covers psychiatrist, psychology family counselling and sociological support who is responsible for adopting the referral system, documentation and legal consultation and referral) for survivors of GBV.
2. Coordinating and working with local municipalities and concerned directorates to prevent and respond to violence and discrimination against women and girls.
(3) Increased knowledge of rights and access to justice at the community level
a. Provide Legal Awareness (know your rights, channels to access justice, women’s rights, children’s rights, civil documentation and HLP) for the general public.
b. Support Legal Aid clinics to address the many social-legal issues faced by the general public.
(4) Increased knowledge of mine-risk safety at the community level
a. Provide mine risk education sessions for the general public.
b. Widely disseminate Mine-risk materials.
UNDP will target 13 locations in need of humanitarian support while targeting 40,000 individual beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 HNO, there are 13.1 million people in need of GBV interventions (51% women). The pandemic and armed conflict have added to Syrian women’s vulnerabilities and posed serious challenges to their protection. lack of awareness about their rights & limited capacity of the duty bearers have further complicated the situation leaving women without medical, psycho-social support and protection and a means for legal redress.1 in 10 households is worried about threats of sexual exploitation and abuse at the community level. GBV continues to be predominantly directed against women and girls with or without disabilities, with access to psychological, health and legal support services severely limited.Conflict-related displacement has resulted in disputes over housing, land and property (HLP). Access to Justice through legal aid is required to support the population in managing the multiple legal & social issues, including HLP. There remains a need for access & support to legal awareness & legal aid in order for Syrian people have an enhanced understanding of their rights and how to access justice through different systems and services available to them. Approximately 10.3 million people are reported to be at risk of increasing the likelihood of civilians being injured or killed and leading to the contamination of HLP and vital infrastructure. 1 in 4 victims of explosive hazards are children (40% killed and 60% injured). The presence of explosive hazards also poses severe danger to voluntary returns and movements of IDPs. 70% of HNO survey respondents reported to not have had Explosive Ordinance Risk Education. The population needs to be made aware of how to manage such risks until the hazards are removed.

5 - Implementation of activities

Role of UNDP Country Office: UNDP Country Office programme unit is responsible for project design and implementation and will provide technical guidance and support to the area office and project team. The programme team will be responsible for project oversight to ensure that the project is implemented as per the project design and in accordance with the allotted budget. The Country Office operations team will be responsible for providing support on operational issues including procurement, human resources management and financial management of the project. The project will be implemented under community safety and access to justice thematic area operating under the Social Cohesion and Local Development portfolio. Role of the Area Office: UNDP area offices will supervise implementation of the project activities. The area office manager will directly oversee the implementation. A dedicated project team, managed by a project manager, will be responsible for implementation of the project activities. The project team will work in close coordination with the country office programme and operations teams in order to implement the project effectively and efficiently. Monitoring and Evaluation: UNDP utilises a three-layer approach to Evaluation (M&E). The first layer is conducted by the project M&E officer responsible for data collection, analysis and reporting. The area office manager will also conduct project quality assurance. The second layer of M&E is conducted by the country office programme team via field missions, review the data collected, progress reports. The third layer of the M&E is conducted by the third-party monitoring team.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mine risk education: - Without widespread Mine Risk Education, people will remain at risk of serious injury or death. With limited resources the project will still risk not reaching those most in need. In order to mitigate this, the project will endeavor to build onto all possible networks to spread messaging and disseminate life-saving information. The project will also seek opportunities to provide widespread public information on the risks of mines / UXOs.Women victims of GBV: -The women victims of GBV seeking legal assistance and pursuing formal justice process for security of their rights could be at risk of facing retaliation from the perpetrators. To mitigate this, cases of GBV will be treated with utmost confidentiality and sessions will be conducted in private. In addition, cases that need further support from the formal justice system, women may be deterred from follow-through due to costs associated with legal support or due to the high transportation costs. the project will seek support of other networked services who can provide in kind support to help facilitate access.Legal Awareness and Legal Aid.-Some groups could be at risk of facing retaliation from the opposite parties for seeking legal support. In order to mitigate this, Legal aid sessions will be handled with utmost confidentiality. Individuals will also be asked about the risks associated with their cases in order to be advised appropriately on how to take action to mitigate such risks.-Increased Legal Awareness may increase demand for justice raising expectations of the public on an strained system. To mitigate for this the project will advise on efficient pathways to access justice in legal counselling sessions & in Legal awareness sessions

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security and implementation: The security situation remains unpredictable. The risk of security deterioration expectedly remains true and may challenge implementation of the project activities. UNDP will constantly undertake security risk analysis and take timely actions vis a vis security situation in order to ensure timely delivery of the project outputs with least exposure of the project team and beneficiaries to security threats.Government Support: It is assumed that the authorities at the local level will support implementation of the project activities as per the plan. In order for the assumption to materialise, UNDP will closely coordinate project activities with the local authorities, and they will be kept informed about the project activities. In the case that the Government or local councils are not willing to provide approvals and space for implementation, UNDP will need to adjust the focus to advocate for the needs of the people, therefore requiring more time to follow through with full scale project implementation.Support of Communities: It is assumed that the community representatives, volunteers, and the existing women’s networks will support implementation of the project activities. Should the communities resist, UNDP will be required to continue dialogue and advocacy efforts with key decision makers.Exchange rate and unstable markets: Unstable prices of equipment and resources needed by the project and/ or different prices between suppliers. In order to mitigate for this risk, UNDP will contract more than one supplier to provide options to choose the most suitable offer.
