Project Summary

The proposed action falls under the 2018-2020 HRP strategic objective 2 “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”. Speciﬁcally, the project will support and enhance the coping capacity of vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns. The action will provide emergency assistance to acutely vulnerable households living in substandard shelters in Middle Area governorate (Gaza Strip). The action ensures their access to adequate and safe shelters and strengthens their capacity to cope with future shocks through a community-based approach. In total, 430 households (2,451 beneﬁciaries at households’ level) will be assisted, through the urgent rehabilitation of 130 shelters and the provision of 300 emergency winter kits to beneﬁciaries for whom a full upgrade is not possible, thus contributing to the Country Strategic Objective 3 “the ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. The project will provide shelter adaptation and adequateness with special needs for the permanently disabled people affected by the Great March of Return and other people with disabilities. 5 Community-based Protection Committees (35 individuals), strengthened through a training on technical maintenance, SPHERE standards and protection principles, will ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level and guarantee a better compliance and respect of protection standards.The project design endorses all of the Shelter Cluster recommendations, in particular by providing a tailored combination of modalities to maximize impact on beneﬁciaries while fostering ownership and resilience through a demanding community based self- implementation approach.

1 - Needs

Initial ﬁgures of the Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2020 show that 300,000 people are in need of humanitarian assistance in the shelter sector in oPt. Out of them, 99% show high severity needs. The shelter survey on substandard housing conditions in Gaza, conducted in March 2018 by the Shelter cluster, showed than 92% of the Gaza strip housing units possessed some kind of shelter vulnerability. The inability of these HHs to cover their basic shelter needs reflects acute vulnerability factors, such as abject poverty, disability, chronic illness, killed or disabled breadwinner, and occurs at higher rate among female-, elderly- or children-headed HHs. The capacity of these HHs to cope with their shelter needs has been drastically decreased because of the current economic deterioration resulting from the blockade and exacerbated by the recent Palestinian Authority’s measures in Gaza. Living in substandard shelters, coupled with lack of safe and dignified living conditions, worsens these HHs’ latent vulnerabilities, undermines their capacity to face future shocks and heightened GBV risks. It exposes them to additional threats, in particular harsh weather, adverse health impact and domestic accidents. These risks affect disproportionately the most vulnerable groups such as PWDs, permanently disabled people, the elderly and pregnant women, who are more prone to health conditions. Additionally, hardship case HHs are compelled to resort to negative coping strategies, such as moving to host families in overcrowded shelters, putting women and children at increased risk of domestic violence and further decreasing their resilience. ACTED and MAAN rely on several sources in order to identify the 430 HHs to be targeted by the proposed intervention. First, ACTED and MAAN conducted a Shelter needs assessment published in May 2019. Through this assessment, partners identified 143 HHs in Middle Area who are in need for emergency shelter interventions; 53% of the assessed HHs are categorized as High and Moderate Shelter vulnerability. Some of these HHs will be covered by partners' project funded under the 2nd allocation of the OCHA HF, while the rest will be targeted by this action. Also, late 2018 ACTED and MA’AN conducted a Multi-sectorial Need Assessment in Wadi al Salqa area targeting 198 HHs, 45% of the assessed households showed substandard shelter between moderate to severe vulnerability. Out of the 198 assessed HHs, 150 are currently being targeted by a WASH intervention implemented by ACTED in partnership with CESVI. These 150 HHs will be considered to be targeted by a complementary shelter rehabilitation under the proposed project to ensure that all the identified needs are covered. Moreover, the Ministry of Public Works and Housing is leading an assessment of substandard housing units in Gaza. Preliminary results show 5,306 HHs in need of shelter rehabilitation in Middle Area governorate and high needs in terms of shelter rehabilitation. In addition, MAAN identiﬁed further 38 HHs in need on shelter rehabilitation through a list provided by the Ministry of Social Development. Finally, in September 2019, ACTED conducted a rapid assessment targeting individuals affected by the Great March of Return demonstrations to understand how sustained injuries affected individuals, their households and their shelter conditions. The permanently disability conditions of these individuals create additional high needs in term of house adaption to their speciﬁc needs. Indeed, most houses inhabited by this group are not accommodated for people with disabilities, and without the capacity to adapt their houses these individuals risk being secluded in their houses, with very limited access to WASH and other facilities inside the house. As 14% (31 HHs) of these permanently disabled live in Middle Area, ACTED and MAAN will prioritize these households and shelter adaptation for PWDs in the proposed intervention.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 – Vulnerability and technical assessments (identification and selection of most vulnerable households) Vulnerability and technical assessments will be conducted based on households pre-identified as vulnerable by recent ACTED and MA’AN assessments targeting Middle Area in Gaza and local authorities, in order to evaluate current conditions, detailed needs, and vulnerabilities. Beneficiaries will be actively engaged in the need assessment to ensure they can express their concerns and most pressing needs. At least one male and one female per household will be interviewed to ensure gender specific needs and concerns are taken into account. Criteria of vulnerability will include: basic NFI needs, damage repairs/renovation needs, exposure to harsh weather, adequateness with special needs (permanently disabled people affected by the Great March of Return and other PWDs), exposure to domestic violence/GBV cases, safety risk. A detailed Bill of Quantities (BoQ) pricing all the necessary shelter interventions and a work plan for the self upgrade works will be established for beneficiary HHs. Based on this assessment phase, ACTED will select beneficiaries for both activities of emergency rehabilitation and winterization of vulnerable households. Activity 2 – Upgrade of substandard 130 vulnerable households in Middle Area Technical guidance will be provided to the 130 beneficiary households to build their technical capacity to conduct repair works in their own shelter. Households will receive conditional cash for repair based on BoQs developed in Activity 1. This cash assistance will be conditional to the works implementation plan based on the household’s BoQs, which will be monitored through 4 visits conducted by MA’AN Field engineers to each household. These engineers will review progress made by beneficiaries and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel subsequent instalments. Those visits will also be used to identify any technical issues beneficiaries could be facing and solve them on the spot through complementary training and guidance by ACTED and MAAN. Moreover, field engineers will also control that the self-implementation results in safe and quality shelter solutions, compliant with SPHERE and Shelter Cluster minimum standards. Tailored remedial action, complementary guidance and/or special assistance will be provided to any household who would fail to reach those standards. Households who would not be able to perform the repair themselves will be assisted through direct repairs rather than cash assistance. Activity 3 – Provision of winterization assistance 300 households will be provided with winterization kits to ensure their safety during winter, reduce their exposure to protection risks, thus increasing preparedness to harsh weather conditions and resilience capacities. ACTED and MA’AN will prepare 300 kits to be distributed in the Middle Area communities (Al Bureij Camp, Al Maghazi Camp, Al Musaddar, An Nuseirat Camp, Az Zawayda, Deir al Balah, Deir al Balah Camp, Wadi as Salqa). These kits, prepared in line with Shelter and protection standards, will include – but not limited to- tarpaulins, plastic sheets, mattresses, blankets. Activity 4 – Promotion of community engagement to protection principles and activities, through the Community- Based Protection Committees (CBPCs) - 5 Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPCs),trained under former projects in the targeted areas, will be strengthened through training on technical maintenance, SPHERE standards, and protection principles. These committees are involved throughout the whole project to participate to all activities, ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level and guarantee a better compliance and respect of protection standards, especially for the most vulnerable groups. Women are particularly encouraged to join the CBPC as active members.

3 - Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects and anticipate and mitigate any challenge. In addition to the standard Shelter Cluster indicators, ACTED and its partners will measure the following indicators: # of substandard shelters rehabilitated (Target: 130; Means of verification: Financial instalments, technical report, post-rehabilitation monitoring); % of assisted children/women/PWDs/elderly reporting less concerns about safety and security at the rehabilitated shelter - 80 target (Target 60%; Means of Verification: baseline and end-line assessment); # of winterization NFI kits provided to vulnerable HHs (Target: 300; Means of verification: BLs, Financial instalments, PDM); % of HHs reporting that the NFI kit is appropriate to mitigate the risk they could be exposed by natural hazards (Target: 80; Means of verification: satisfaction surveys with representative sample of the HHs receive NFI kits); # of Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPCs) strengthened through a refresher capacity-building training scale-up their ability to provide services to their communities (Target: 35; Means of Verification: Terms of Reference (ToRs), Training report); % of female members of Community-Based Protection Committees (Target: 40%; Means of Verification: CBWCs ToR and list of members, Training attendance sheet); % of CBPCs members who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the refresher capacity-building training (Target: 80; Means of Verification: Pre-Training Assessment (PTA), Post Training Evaluation (PTE)). The ACTED Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU), independent from the Program Department, will be in charge of measuring project outcomes and progress indicators through a variety of approaches and methodology. In particular, the AMEU will conduct a baseline and endline assessment to measure progress against pre-identified indicators, as well as beneficiary satisfaction, and draw lessons learned from the project. Those activities will enable gathering and reporting detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneficiaries and to measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups (female-headed households, permanently disabled- headed households). Satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), both managed by the AMEU, will enable to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) complying with HAP standards and ACTED’s SoPs. Following the completion of the project, the committees will be responsible for monitoring, identifying and reporting any technical or protection issues in regard to rehabilitation of land and assets to ACTED as well as the relevant local service and maintenance providers, to ensure the sustainability of the project’s outputs and outcomes. In addition, ACTED Protection Oﬃcer, with the support of the Committees, will be in charge of monitoring the dissemination of protection principles as well as their concrete application during the implementation process. In particular, ACTED Protection Officer and the committees will regularly visit all beneficiaries to ensure that protection concerns are being respected and addressed through the works. Were any lack to be identified, the Protection Officer will offer guidance and complementary training to beneficiaries.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through ACTED’s Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM), implemented in all ACTED countries of intervention, including oPT. The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. All beneficiaries of the action will be explained their right to provide complaints in confidentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance, and will provided with small pamphlets summarizing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. It is worth mentioning that CRM relies on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, field paper forms, compliant tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanisms, as well as mandatory timely feedback (within 15 days) to the complainants. This CRM will enable ACTED to identify and solve any issues reported by beneficiaries andnon-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanisms features several channels, including a dedicate hotline and frequent visits by ACTED Protection Oﬃcer to ensure that feedback is received from beneciaries and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. Engagement and acceptance of community members is key component of the design, implementation and monitoring of ACTED projects. Beneficiaries’ satisfaction is continuously monitored by ACTED’s AME Unit, that regularly visits and interviews beneficiaries regarding ongoing activities. The AMEU will report any issues brought to its attention. ACTED has been implementing projects in the targeted governorates since 2010 and benefit thus from a high level of acceptance in local Gazan communities. The project will be implemented in compliance with ACTED security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented if needed.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). ACTED analyzes protection risks, anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. Strong Monitoring and Evaluation and continuous communications with stakeholders, including community leaders and local authorities (with whom ACTED works with as well under other projects) as well as beneficiaries, are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict. A community-based protection approach, established by ACTED under previous projects, will be followed throughout the whole project to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level and to guarantee a better compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups (female headed HHs, disabled, elderly). The CBPCs will be trained through a continuum of capacity building through monitoring visits and guidance by ACTED’s Protection Oﬃcer. The training will cover topics such as: 1) the minimum protection standards and principles to the right to adequate standard of living including safe access to basic shelter and housing (SPHERE and IASC – Guiding principle 18); 2) the objectives and implementation details of the project; 3) their role and responsibilities, notably regarding the identiﬁcation of protection case issues ; 4) monitoring of the most at risk cases, follow-up of the reparation works to match protective criterion and Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM); 5) reporting and alert on cases to be referred for other humanitarian support.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize its effects on beneficiaries, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have different needs and assistance will be adapted to beneficiaries’ needs, i.e. permanently disabled people and women. ACTED’s Protection Officer will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages, solid family and community support networks, accountability through accessible Complaint Response Mechanisms, adequate referral to other organization for individual protection assistance. Protection and privacy-related items will be prioritized for the rehabilitation works and NFI provision under the cash-base assistance. Particularly, these items will be taken into account for family members who are particularly exposed to safety and security concerns, i.e. children, elder people, people with disabilities, people with chronic hill. House adaption for people with disabilities will be also prioritized, in order to include individuals who are permanently disable due to the Great March of Return as they are identiﬁed as one of the most vulnerable group in the Gaza Strip. As 60% of the households reported that women do not feel safe in their houses, items related to women and girls’ safety and privacy will be also prioritized. During the vulnerability and technical assessment at least a man and a woman for each household will be interviewed to guarantee that gender-speciﬁc needs are taken properly into consideration. A stronger engagement and ownership at community level will vouch for a better compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups (female headed households, disabled, elderly, etc.). ACTED Protection Oﬃcer will support the Community-based Protection Committees by providing training and conducting monitoring visits to the household. Based on the work of the protection committees, he will link project’s beneﬁciaries in need with the existing Referral Mechanism developed and coordinated with relevant stakeholders in the Gaza Strip, when required. The CBPSc follow a clear schedule of visits and reporting mechanism to the Protection Oﬃcer, through paper-based forms that are compiled by the Protection Officer into a visit follow-up database.Finally, protection and privacy items will be taken into account in the rehabilation works for family members who are particularly exposed to safety and security concerns, i.e.children, elder people, people with disabilities, people with chronic hill.
