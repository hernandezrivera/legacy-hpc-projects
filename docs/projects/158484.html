Project Summary

During the early recovery phase which will be initiated in August, WFP aims at assisting a total of 100,000 beneficiaries of which 56,100 people through light Food Assistance for Assets (FFA) schemes and General Food distributions for the most vulnerable groups who are unable to engage in FFA schemes, representing 20% of the overall number of people in need. WFP plans at implementing these early recovery interventions in Cabo Delgado (Ibo, Quissanga and Macomia) and Nampula (Memba and Erati) provinces. An additional 43,900 conflict-affected Internally Displaced Groups will also continue to receive recovery support.
As part of the light FFA approach, food assistance is conditionally provided in exchange of labor for assets rehabilitation and constrution. In this phase WFP will be supporting low-risk, low-cost interventions that requires low technology, and the use of simple, well-defined and light work norms, without compromising the quality of the assets to be created. The assets include soil disalting and cleaning, acess roads rehabilititation, rehabilitation of water points for human and livestock production, improving the household rehabilitation of access roads, rehabilitation of water sources to improve the availability of water for people and livestock, as well as the rehabilitation and construction of houses, latrines or schools. In return to their participation in FFA schemes, households will receive nutritious food basket that will meet 75 percent of their food needs.
In Cyclone Kenneth affected areas, early recovery-oriented FFA/FFT activities in a post-disaster context supporting communities to re-establish their livelihoods. Following the relief phase of the response, recovery activities are key in paving the way to resilience strengthening of communities who have lost their productive assets, community and household assets and are already surfering of food insecurity and undernutrition.
Through the FFA programme, participant households receive a nutritious food basket per month to meet 75 percent of their food needs in exchange of labor for disaster risk reduction and asset creation interventions, thus contributing to post-shock rehabilitation. The most appropriate transfer modality will be determined based on the context and needs, as informed by market assessments and financial institutional capacity analyses. Food, vouchers, and cash could be explored, with the latter requiring governance clearance and support.
