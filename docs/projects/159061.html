Project Summary

Ongoing conflicts resulted in severe negative impacts on people in South/West Kordofan and East/North Darfur casualties and trauma among civilian population. Child, women and young females have been at risk and live in very destructive environment, possible incidents of GBV, poor health services, and many other issues affecting their life. There is a lack of protection services and referral system to address Sexual & Domestic Violence, Family tracing & reunification in the targeted locations.
Newly displaced IDPs are often overlooked during humanitarian interventions, as they are not mainstreamed into programming and face obstacles in accessing support that has not been tailored to them. There is an increase in the number of reported different forms of GBV such as domestic violence, FGM and early marriage of young girls. Vulnerable individuals and families are affected the most, in destitute situation and lack protection and assistance support including for basic needs and community safety nets.
The project ensures equal treatment and participation of men, women, girls and boys, older and persons with disabilities, regardless of their age, gender, ethnic, social, religious or other background in the activities. Furthermore, it will reinforce community-based protection committees (CBPCs), deployed social workers through mobilization of a network of volunteers from the targeted communities to ensure sustainability of protection activities and identification of the most critical protection concerns and prevention of violence, abuse and exploitation.
Under the project, an assessment exercise will be conducted to identify existing structures, their composition (gender, participation of groups at risk of exclusion), areas of work and impact; implement quick impact projects to serve host communities and IDPs; strengthen capacity of CBPNs and social workers to improve the identification of PSNs, including survivors of SGBV and to ensure that direct material support to vulnerable people covers specific needs and referral cost. Such support can include health insurance, mobility equipment (crutches) for elderly or people with disabilities, thereby, strengthening referral pathways to relevant service providers.
Through the construction/rehabilitation of new community centers and provision of equipment members of community-based protection committees’ will be able to meet, discuss, identify the protection concerns and decide on solutions. The results of these meetings will be discussed with CBPCs and representatives of the concerned communities on quarterly basis to respond to main concerns.
Furthermore, awareness raising campaigns will be held for of the CBPNs and community members on protection, GBV and PSS including during the 16 days of activism on the Elimination of VAW with other partners. Women will be provided with PSS services and SGBV survivors referred to service providers through a well-defined referral system. Besides, vulnerable women and girls will be assisted through recreational activities, skills trainings based on the market survey. AMVO will held capacity building training to health personnel and service providers from targeted localities to support protection of IDPs such as definition of protection, guiding principles on internal displacement, GBV and PSS in Sudan. The project will support capacity building of local MoSA employees, as well as locality and ministries at state level.

1 - Needs

The evolving socio‐political situation in Sudan has resulted in an increase in protection risks in many parts of the country, including areas where the protection and security context was previously stable or had improved.These risks are exacerbated by the deepening economic crisis, which has increased exposure to abuse and exploitation among the most vulnerable, as well as increased inter‐communal tensions. Armed militias are harassing farmers, IDPs and returnees, preventing them from accessing their land during the planting season.Tensions within IDP populations are also growing, resulting notably in bursts of violence in IDP camps between groups with opposing political views and affiliations.The influx of displaced persons and refugees in South Kordofan (Abu Jubaiha, El Abassiya, Dilling, Rashad, Kadugli, Talodi), West Kordofan State (Lagawa and Keilak), East Darfur State (Abu Jabra, Adila, El Dein, Assalaya, Bahr El Arab, El Ferdous), North Darfur State (El Fashir, Millit and Tawilla) puts communities under stress, and may trigger tension/ conflicts. Mechanisms and capacities contributing to conflict resolution and peaceful coexistence, as well as advocacy with authorities in displacement and return areas, need support. Breakdown in family and community structures leaves vulnerable people without adequate support to address their essential needs. Community safety nets are severely weakened, and social institutions are usually too stretched to provide adequate protection services to the affected population. According to SUDAN HUMANITARIAN NEEDS OVERVIEW 2019, inter-communal conflict was one of the principal drivers of protection needs. Areas in Darfur saw an increase of tensions caused by land occupation and conflict between farmers and herders during the harvest season, which further exacerbated the protection environment. There are 1.8 million people suffering or potentially at risk of Gender based violence (GBV). Women and girls continue to suffer disproportionately from GBV, poverty and violations of basic rights due to insecurity, low economic status and lack of livelihood opportunities, lack of community awareness on women’s rights due to cultural and societal norms. About 55 percent of displaced people are women and girls, with 27 per cent of those women below the age of 18. Lack of awareness among communities makes it challenging to ensure prevention of GBV and to ensure access of survivors to the relevant multi-sectoral services. Vulnerable women and girls in areas of conflict need support to prevent risks of GBV, in particular to reduce their exposure. The IDPs are mixed within the host community. The main consequences of this conflict include disruption of basic services, exposure of women, girls, men and boys to further insecurity and violence, disruption of economic and agricultural activities added additional burden placed on host families. Almanar has a well-established main office in Khartoum state and 4 sub- offices in South Kordofan, west Kordofan, east Darfur and north darfur state with qualified and committed staff recruited in each office and good relation with the government and communities. Almanar has a successful experience in implementing protection programs in mentioned states with UNHCR, EU, SHF, UNFPA and other donors through working with well - trained protection community based committees / social workers &LGAs: awareness raising sessions on Human rights, women’s rights, child rights, citizenship, peace building & conflict resolution, cross cutting issues: (GBV, HIV/AIDS, environment), legal aid, protection materials support, PSS to GBV survivors and recreational and basic vocational skills as well as provision of protection support to the prisoners. Also Almanar is working in building the capacity of government authorities, community leaders and CSOs in the geographical areas where the proposed intervention target

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated through the entire project management cycle and integrated in the assessments to ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. Key environmental concerns will be built into the project in order to reduce deforestation in Sudan. Almanar will attempt to switch away from fire bricks to alternative constructive methods such as stabilized soil blocks (SSB) or concrete blocks (CSB) depending on the prerequisites of the region. Environmental mainstreaming will be included during monitoring and evaluation of the project outcomes of the response to ensure that the effects sought was indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time in order to adjust activities accordingly. As the main element of the project, the principle of do no harm is fulfilled. Furthermore, to help identify best practices and lessons learnt that offer valuable insight for making improvements in future aid efforts. Almanar will work with local NGOs, CSOs and communities as well as with the national and state governmental authorities, since Community consultation and participation is a key part of environmental management. Throughout the project cycle gender will be considered to access to, and management of natural resources to receive consultation from both men and women.

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

One of the most significant factors, which is increasingly recognized as an integral component of UN programming, is the gender equality and protection of girls and women. Inequalities between the sexes typically reflect, and can increase, vulnerabilities of young girls, teens, may face different protection risks, have different needs and choices, and possess different skills, knowledge and coping strategies. The project activities, will aim to ensure community participation through the project cycle, capacity and vulnerability analysis, reduce the level of exploitation to women and promote protection of girls in communities and families with the support of legal and traditional protection systems. The project will use and advocate for Minimum Standards of Protection package amongst the targeted communities throughout the project implementation process. This project through the referral pathway will ensure that women, girls and boys victims of S/GBV, elderly, disables and their families and guardians are fully aware of available services around them. Almanar will ensure that the service locations are safe and accessible to all without discrimination, and ensure that the beneficiaries receiving services in dignity and confidentiality.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability will be ensured across the activities in this project through establishment of complaints and response mechanisms at project level aimed at ensuring that the voices of the affected populations will be heard. The existing community committees and community leaders will be actively engaged at all levels of the project including management, monitoring and evaluation. Complaints handling mechanisms will be established to ensure that complaints are comprehensively analyzed and responded to with the beneficiaries getting feedback on a monthly basis. These committees are expected to be gender and ability sensitive with the inclusion of both males, females, elderly and people with disabilities to ensure that all voices, including those of the marginalized are heard, equality and equity are realized with regards to access to services that will be provided by the project. Project staff will ensure adequate consultation with the communities to ensure that existing complaints handling mechanisms are incorporated into the complaints and response mechanisms to be established by the project. Almanar staff is trained and signed the sexual exploitation and abuse policy and committed to it with close monitoring by the supervisors.
