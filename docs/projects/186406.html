Project Summary

Employing a three-tiered approach CHAD aims to support a holistic response targeting children, caregivers/families, and community structures including local authority child protection actors at all levels and civil society partners operating in conflict-affected communities. To achieve this, CHAD has aligned its response to the northeast child protection sub-sector goal of strengthening locally-appropriate child protection prevention and response for children at risk, promoting positive norms and behaviours, strengthening internal capacity for quality service delivery, evidence gathering and data management.
Overall, 14,500 children, caregivers, and child protection staff are expected to benefit through this project.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in North-East Nigeria has entered its eleventh year and poses the biggest humanitarian challenge in the country. An estimated 2,045,461 million people (1,791,557 million children and 253,904 caregivers) need child protection services; this includes an estimated 22,394 children and 3,174 caregivers living with disabilities. In 2022, the northeast Child Protection Sub-Sector will target 756,139 children (404,287 girls, 351,852 boys) and 333,005 caregivers (285,580 women, 248,406 men), bringing the total number of persons targeted with child protection services to 1, 290, 090 people. Women and girls are the most affected by the armed conflict. They are often kidnapped and married by force, sexually abused, killed and maimed. Those whose partners are taken away become household heads with limited economic means to maintain their households. The on-going conflict creates a challenging environment for effective community support systems to address child protection issues especially in the context of widespread lack of local government/civilian administrators/authorities. Community support systems rarely link with state authorities on child protection. Recent closure of camps in Maiduguri even against public outcry will mean that child protection actors need to invest heavily into strengthening more sustainable community-based initiatives to protect children. Innovative approaches will be required to achieve this prupose. Assessment information from DTM Round 37 estimates that there are 8771 internally displaced children in Dikwa alone. Of this [51%] are girls, while [49%] are boys. Internally displaced Women and Men constitute [23%] and [19%] of the entire IDP population respectively. This scenario is very typical of deep field locations where women and children are left to bear the longer-term consequences of emergencies. Though Protection actors exist in most IDP locations, host communities often lack sustained presence by child protection actors nor Psychosocial support actors on ground. CHAD will seek to expand its operational presence within host communities including informal camp settlements. Attacks in April by non-state armed groups on humanitarian operations in Dikwa and Damasak seriously affected humanitarian assets and personnel, causing the temporary suspension of operations in those areas in order to safely relocate humanitarian staff. Ongoing conflict and insecurity coupled with rising food insecurity renders children and their families vulnerable to protection risks including family separation either from attacks or poor living conditions, gender-based violence, and threat of life. In the context of constantly depleting livelihoods and basic resources and services children are left vulnerable to child labor, absence from school, exploitation and abduction especially for adolescent girls and recruitment by armed groups affecting boys. Negative coping mechanisms are still on the rise as families seek leaner family structures to better manage hardship caused by the conflict and accompanying loss of livelihood. Girls are easily handed out to adults seeking to marry young girls as one way of coping with challenges brought about by the conflict and COVID-19. Child participation has been higlighted as a priority for the child protection sub-sector for better quality and accountable responses. CHAD will deploy innovative participatory processes in partnership with other sectors and international organizations across the programme cycle including needs definition, planning and design, & monitoring and evaluation processes to increase the effectiveness of our responses to conflict-affected children. Effective Psychosocial approaches in emergency responses requires a multi-sectoral approach to address the holistic needs of children and women. CHAD will join efforts to strengthen referrals and partnerships within a multi-sectoral approach to effectively address the holistic needs of children.

2 - Activities

For a coherent and coordinated responses all activities will be aligned with the northeast Child Protection strategic objectives for 2022:Access to Prevention and Response Services:- Support Family tracing and reunification of separated and unaccompanied children.- Place children affected by protection risks into appropriate community based alternative care.- Provide Community Based reintegration support to children formally associated with armed groups- Provide children with disabilities with appropriate needs-based child protection services, care, and support.- Register children under 5 years and support the issue of birth certificates- Referral of vulnerable children to access inter-sectoral and other specialized services.- Provide life skills education for adolescent girls and boys- Support age appropriate structured recreational and creative activities for children in Child Friendly Spaces.- Provide children at risk with specialized care and support- Provide psychosocial services/ positive parenting lessons for caregivers of vulnerable children Positive Norms and Behaviours:- Establishment and support to Community Based Child Protection Response- Development and implementation of Participatory Needs and Response Approaches - Child Protection Awareness Raising - Establishment and support to Child Rights Clubs Strengthening Capacity for Quality Prevention and Response Services:- Training of CHAD workers on the Child Protection Minimum Standards- Training of CHAD workers on the INSPIRE Strategy Framework- Support to access specialized training on needs assessment, monitoring and evaluating child protection response, development of strategic plans, and procurement and logistics, financial controls etc.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

CHAD will adopt the nexus approach in the design and implementation of this project, moving away from the traditional focus on life-saving interventions to more long-term and rights-based approach to delivering and managing child protection interventions. Priority areas within the nexus approach will include - Birth registration: with a focus on strengthening collaborations with local authority agencies to increase access to registration systems for vulnerable children in hard-to-reach communities. - The Child Rights Act: with a focus on driving widespread awareness, implementation and enforcement of laws to address harmful practices and norms- Systems strengthening approach to prevention and response service delivery. Conceptual understanding of the nexus approach is still very weak. In 2022 CHAD will invest efforts in strengthening knowledge and practice around the nexus approach.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CHAD will ensure principled humanitarian actions in the course of delivery. Protection principles will be promoted. Child safeguarding measures including child friendly feedback and complaint mechanisms will be developed to ensure appropriate responses. Inclusive participation of local communities will inform all stages of project cycle including planning, implementation and monitoring. All CHAD staff are expected to adhere to code of conducts developed to protect project beneficiaries from sexual abuse and exploitation. Training on PSEA will aim to raise awareness amongst CHAD staff, local communities and project beneficiaries on relevant measures to prevent and respond to incidents of sexual abuse and exploitation.
