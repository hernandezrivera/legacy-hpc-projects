Project Summary

AL-Birr Association is one of the oldest working NGOs' in Syria it's established since 1956 with a main vision of fighting poverty, nescience, disease.
At the beginning of the crisis in Syria 2011, Al-Birr concentrated their efforts to help affected people in Homs City and it's rural and started providing a lot of services in cooperation with different UN Agencies.
The project aims at helping affected people in Homs in eastern and northern rural of the city and providing them with services as follows:
1-Case Management in the field of child protection and psychological support and also GBV.
2-Awareness sessions in topics related to GBV, Child protection.
3-Soft PSS and social recreational activities.
4-providing in-kind assistance
5-services to elderly people.
Al-Birr is well-known in all of the areas in Homs since the association have branches and medical centers all over Homs city and it's rural, also al-Birr has a great bond with the local community and it has a good reputation.
Al-Birr has been known for its experience in implementing such projects since 2012 the services provided by Al-Birr was always appreciated by beneficiaries and they've always appreciated the quality services they received through al-Birr projects.
Al-Birr Has a very experienced and highly trained staff related to protection services. Al-Birr qualified staff are working in the field of protection since 2012 and received a professional training to deal with protection issues ( Child protection – GBV – PSS )
Al-Birr large geographical coverage in Homs and across Syria makes it one of the oldest Known NGOs in Syria and its bonds with the local community facilitate the service providing- mechanisms.

4 - Needs

The scale, severity and complexity of needs across Syria as it's entering the ninth year of the crisis, remain overwhelming. Some 13.2 million people in Syria are in need for protection services (6.7 million of them are women and 4.2 million are children), including internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities and More than 800,000 thousand people in Homs are in need for protection services(HNO).IDP returning movement are increasing significantly, and the process of return adds an additional layer of needs to be addressed.The displacement crisis has led to a steady erosion of coping mechanisms of individuals and communities. Concerted efforts are still needed to focus on durable solutions in areas of displacement or elsewhere.The project aims to achieve protection goals for the benefit of IDPs and returnees as well as the host communities to reinforce the resilience of the populations.According to the findings of the available assessments, the protection environment in Syria remains characterized by the occurrence of several critical protection issues, with varying frequency of occurrence across locations and population groups, as well as some constant patterns. In 48 per cent of assessed communities the occurrence of at least four protection issues was reported by the consulted population (HNO).Gender-based violence continues to be pervasive in the lives of women and girls both inside and outside their homes, with adolescent girls bearing the brunt of the crisis. Gender-based violence also continues to be pervasive in the lives in the lives of older people at family and community level, as well as at institutional level.Multiple and complex child protection risks continue to shape the lives of girls and boys throughout Syria. Continuous displacement, exposure to violence, deepening poverty and the persistent challenges in access and availability to services are taking a huge toll on children. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Risk of discrimination since some of activities are provided to specific people (children at risk, survivor women and girls or at risk). Likelihood: medium, impact: highMitigating measures: 1. Enhancing communication with different people in the area through different ways (advertisement, etc). 2. Communicating with key persons to clarify the target people of activities2. Risk of violence toward women if they involve in activities, especially that the community in rural area is conservative. Likelihood: high, impact: high.Mitigating measures: 1. Holding sessions with men to clarify the goals of the activities. 2. Communicating with key persons to help in reaching message to people. 3. Holding FGD with men and women to choose the best way to implement activities based on "do no harm" for beneficiaries.
