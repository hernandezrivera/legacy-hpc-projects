Project Summary

The Home Upgrading for Vulnerable Households Headed by Women in the Gaza Strip is a twelve months project aims at supporting vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns to meet their basic needs and enhance their coping capacity. The project comes in line with HRP strategic objectives 2,3, and in accordance with the Shelter and NFIs Cluster Objective 2.The project will target 200 women-headed households living in substandard housing conditions whom are at risk of exposure to extreme weather and protection concerns identified by SIF annual assessment and updated of the data base of orphans sponsored households of in August 2018. SIF also, conducted a rapid shelter assessment with focus on summarization and winterization elements on 2nd September 2019 targeting a sample of 62 HHs identified by the annual assessment as living in poor shelter condition. The assessment found a need for urgent shelter upgrading intervention in 2020. These households are at risk of exposure to extreme weather conditions and living below shelter minimum standards.
The project has been designed in accordance with the SIF extensive experience in the field and in close consultation with beneficiaries as well as the shelter cluster to support families with the needed urgent summerization rehabilitation works such as replacing or rehabilitating of roofs made of metal or asbestos sheets, fixing or replacing windows and doors for bathrooms and bedrooms to maintain privacy, closing holes in the houses walls for safety and weather protection, maintaining basic WASH facilities and electrical network and installing renewable power supply system to improve coping capacity of the families during the summer season and electricity cuts. Based on actual need and priority, each HH will receive an individualized rehabilitation plan corresponding to the specific needs of their shelter and beneficiary preferences in order to make it comply with summerization minimum standards. SIF project through rehabilitating of metal roofs and broken windows and doors as well as installing emergency power energy will contribute to improve the Gender-Age and GBV related concerns as well as protection at the household’s level by increasing the privacy and designate more space for specific gender and age group and children.
The project will apply contracting approach as the contractor will furnish all skilled workers, materials, equipment, and tools to complete the work in compliance with the required quality and in timely manner

1 - Needs

In August 2017, SIF launched an annual assessment for 2542 widowed women-headed households (WWHHHs) registered in the SIF Orphan Sponsorship Program, focusing on shelter assessment that aimed to determine housing conditions in substandard shelter condition. The initial assessment done by four SIF social workers revealed that 33%(838) of them live in crowded,lack of privacy as well as leakage of roofs and inadequate of basic furniture which contribute to GBV,GenderAge and protection concerns. Between the 1st and 15th September 2017, SIF conducted a second shelter assessment aimed to identify priority needs for house rehabilitation according to the minimum standard among a random sample of 91 out of the 838 WHHHs identified in the initial assessment.The sample included a proportion of households representing the WHHHs from all Gaza governorates. The needs assessment revealed that there is an urgent need to rehabilitate houses to adapt to harsh weather conditions. 59% of assessed families have tin roofs while 69% reported that their roofs require maintenance. 31% don’t have a room to protect them from harsh weather. The majority 69% has a ceiling that leaks in winter and allows and heat in summer. The corresponding number for walls is 55% and 74% for windows of the houses. Nearly 80% of HHs didn’t have a source of light in the house. 97% of HHs live in housing units without an emergency electricity system in case of electricity cuts. SIF assessment was validated by the findings of the Inter-Agency Shelter Survey on substandard housing conditions in Gaza that published by Shelter Cluster in June 2018. The survey revealed that 43% of households in Gaza lack protection from rain water leaking through windows, and 35% suffer from water leaking from roof whilst 30 % of the households had a risk of children falling from height due to the lack of proper protection at stair shaft, balconies, and roof. These conditions will require minimum shelter interventions for protection of the vulnerable families exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns. Over 23,300 households have more than half of core minimum standards missing, raising a number of humanitarian health and protection concerns (including gender-related protection concerns) as a result of overcrowding, lack of privacy or security, unsafe conditions, and exposure to weather extremes. This figure includes 2,800 female, divorced or widowed headed households. On 2nd September, 2019, SIF carried out a rapid needs assessment to WHHHs identified in SIF database(Kobo) as needing urgent rehabilitation ahead of the winter season to verify the need, taking a sample of 62 out of 247 HHs by three social workers and site engineer. The sample includes 15 HHs in North Gaza, 17 HHs in Gaza city, 15 HHs in Deir Al Balah, and 15 HHs from Khanyounis. The assessment has found that 95 % of surveyed HHs suffer from leaking roofs that need urgent rehabilitation, 79% of HHs contains openings in the walls not closed with cement materials. In 71% of the surveyed HHs the kitchen and bathroom roof is not sealed allow sun light, heat, and rain, in 79 % of the HHs windows are not sealed from heat, rain, and wind. Also, 21% overcrowded, 51 % of HHs the bedrooms and bathrooms doors are not lockable and thus do not allow privacy and safety, 83% of stair railing, handrails for balconies, and windows protection are in bad condition, 81% of HHs surveyed reported that family members exposed to colds frequently during the winter. 63% do not have access to store water for domestic use and drinking. 40% the kitchens do not provide a healthy space to prepare and store food and cooking and 63% their basic furniture in a bad condition. SIF has assisted 310 WHHHs with upgrading out of the already 838 identified WHHHs and expected to assist another 110WHHHs under 2nd Allocation in 2019 which leaves 50% in need in addition to newly sponsored HHs since last assessment which will be identified in the assessment in 2020

2 - Activities

SIF will hire a project manager and two site engineers (one is woman and the other is man) to supervise the project implementation through contracting approach. SIF will achieve the project’s results by delivering the following activities 1 Conduct Beneficiaries Selection SIF will conduct a shelter vulnerability assessment on the list of 700 households pre-identified by the Orphan Project team whom in poor housing condition to reassess the HHS using the vulnerability and housing survey questionnaire developed by the shelter cluster focusing on summerization items. The shelter team will carry out technical assessment visit to each household using a combination of two criteria and rankings as follows:1- Shelter vulnerability assessment tool based on the technical assessment of the housing conditions following the shelter minimum standard.2- The Household Vulnerability (HHV), based on the presence of certain socio-demographic and socioeconomic factors in the household characteristics. The data from the assessment will be entered and analyzed in a scoring system. The analysis will result in a ranking per household which will present the most 200 eligible households. Upon finalizing the list of beneficiaries for the 200 eligible households, the project team and the Lawyer will develop legal and technical support awareness sessions for all the beneficiaries. The awareness sessions will give more legal details about the conditions and clauses of the assistance agreement between SIF and each beneficiary and clarify to the beneficiaries their and SIF roles and obligations in this project. An agreement will be signed with each beneficiary individually in the presence of SIF’s Lawyer. At the same session SIF will sensitize the beneficiaries about SIF complaint and feedback mechanism to keep continuous communication with them and respond immediately to their concerns. 2 Carry out Technical Assessment and HH Baseline (re-verification assessment)The site engineers will verify the 200 households’ shelter vulnerability resulted from SIF initial assessment. Overall technical assessment of each case will be determined by the Shelter Vulnerability (SV) based on the technical assessment of the housing conditions following Shelter Summerization Minimum Standard. The rehabilitation works may include several components of the house that subject to the summerization components as following:1. Cover roofs with plastic sheet. 2. Rehabilitate HHs WASH facilities.3. Fix install new doors and windows.4. Supply renewable power supply systems and maintenance of electrical network. 3 Carry out upgrading/rehabilitation components for 200 HHSAfter developing the bill of quantities and hire qualified contract(s), SIF will consult with the beneficiary to ensure their readiness to start the rehabilitation work in their houses. The project team will supervise the work on daily bases to ensure the adherence to the standard specification. The contractor will furnish all materials and workforce to achieve the upgrading/rehabilitation works including repair or replace the doors and windows, supply and maintain the existing electrify network, and install renewable energy system. and maintain the WASH facilities according to previously agreed plan.

MEAL field visits’ reports will be shared with the shelter Project Manager and follow up plan will be jointly set to ensure prompt feedback and learning. -MEAL Manager will conduct focus groups and individual interviews with sample of beneficiaries to collect their feedback and assess their satisfaction. -End line survey is planned using shelter assessment tool in representative sample of HHs that received the intervention to assess results against indicator targets and to measure project success as well as to discuss with beneficiaries their perceptions and experiences in terms of protection from harsh wither, privacy, safety, and dignity. Upon completion of the project, SIF will conduct an internal evaluation and learning workshop including all project team and participates from finance and logistic departments. The results of the workshop will be used for improving future shelter interventions

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

SIF takes into account the Core Humanitarian Standard to build inclusive beneficiaries’ communication, participation, and feedback mechanisms over the project cycle including sharing information, listening carefully to targeted beneficiaries and involving them in decision-making to contribute to effective intervention and improve the quality of the delivered services. Beneficiaries’ feedback will support a real-time adaptation of the project to precisely fit their specific needs. It will be used to hold the project team and the contractor accountable and to inform upward accountability to senior management. Each beneficiary will decide on the priority need of rehabilitation by drafting individual rehabilitation plan that will be agreed on and reviewed by the beneficiary and will inform making the decision on the estimated cost of the work. For instance, the beneficiaries’ opinion will be taken into account to identify the room they would like the ceiling to be rehabilitated, the volume of the water tank, and the type of WASH facilities. The daily feedback through interaction with site engineers will be valued and used to develop trust and improve the rehabilitation works on an ongoing basis. A formal feedback will be sought through focus group discussions or interviews with women and endline survey by MEAL manager. SIF has an effective complaints and response system that involves direct communication flow among beneficiaries. Two complaint and feedback boxes exist in SIF office as management team meets bi-weekly to sort out the complaints and refer them to the responsible manager to respond within the response duration. A complaint and feedback template is enclosed with the beneficiary agreement. The beneficiaries will be oriented to the complaint and feedback mechanism from the beginning at the time of singing the agreement in order to increase thier sensitization of the process of giving feedback in, build confidence ,and overcome fears about giving feedback

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project is realized based on a needs assessment that has been carried out with participation of the beneficiaries and will continue including them in the technical re-assessment. Through focus groups and individual interview, SIF will discuss with beneficiaries the priority need of summer works including: identifying the rooms they would like the ceiling to be rehabilitated in order to prevent the heat, ensure privacy and decrease the overcrowding, rehabilitate the doors, windows, and install locks for bedrooms, toilets and bathrooms to ensure safety, and dignity, rehabilitate the WASH facilities to sustain personal hygiene, increase capacity of drinking water tank to sustain storing water along the time in summer, maintain the electricity network to improve safety and lighting, install renewable energy system and train the beneficiaries on the efficient operation so as to prevent improper use. The beneficiary dignity will be maintained during the rehabilitation of ceiling by providing rent fees to beneficiaries for seeking temporary shelter. The beneficiaries will participate in the implementation of the upgrading works to ensure that upgrading works meet their needs and they will be able to input how the rehabilitation works can further address their protection needs. SIF will oversee the contractor’s works to ensure there is no discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, exploitation or any other harm done to beneficiaries, and it holds the contractor accountable. SIF will ensure compliance by all parties to its protection Code of Conduct. SIF mainstreams the Abusive Behavioral Procedure among all project stakeholders including the beneficiaries as anyone can submit a real, clear, and accurate description of the facts of abuse or misconduct incident by writing directly to stop@secours-islamigue.org. The Abuse Behavioral Procedure documents will be an essential part of the contract document with the beneficiaries. At the time of signing the agreement with the contractor, SIF will conduct an orientation session for the contractor and workforce on the code of conduct and will emphasis on respecting the culture sensitivity of working in the households headed by widowed women. SIF’s protection unit’s team (Protection Manager and Counselor will carefully monitor the protection concern by building the capacity of the shelter project’s team, targeted beneficiaries, and the contractor team by enhancing their understanding of protection aspects, and provide an avenue to address any protection concerns such as GBV, child abuse and or child labor through monitoring visits and mentorship. In case any GBV case arises during the project implementation, SIF Counselor will refer any case(s) through relevant referral services. Summer works will take into account any special needs according to Gender -Age and any potential disability. In case during project implementation some cases of disability emerged, the project will address their needs such as handrails and ramps. SIF applies the Core Humanitarian Standard from the beginning of the project design. At the end of the project SIF completes CHS self-assessment tool and the final score of the assessment is used to further improve the accountability and protection mainstreaming. SIF will disseminate all lessons learned and good practices in protection mainstreaming within the project among other programs in SIF and with shelter cluster. A complaints mechanism is in place that is used in previous SIF shelter projects. In realization with the cultural and social challenges specific to the targeted beneficiaries with the emphasis of doing no harm approach, field staff will consist of men and women to ensure that women beneficiary are comfortable communicating with SIF women employees SIF complies with REGULATION (EU) 2016/679 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL of April 27th 2016 on the protection of individuals’ data. SIF uses a dignifying visibility and reporting system

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

SIF is already working with the women-headed households as part of its sponsorship program and hence has a good relationship with and access to the beneficiaries, facilitating acceptance of the project. The fact that SIF works with a pool of socially vulnerable HHs ensures that the basic summerization intervention are targeting one of the most disadvantaged groups in society. SIF will apply the Shelter cluster standardized criteria for vulnerability of the different thresholds including proportion of children under 5, type of housing unit, , availability and condition of household assets, household dependency ratio (adjusting for disability and chronic illness), and designated hardship (employment) as well as number of children /youth in school and university, number of people with disability. This will enable all the women headed households identified by SIF annual assessment in substandard housing condition an equal opportunity to participate in the project application. The principles of impartiality, neutrality, independence and humanity are realized in beneficiary selection as a scoring system is used to prioritize the beneficiaries solely based on their humanitarian state. SIF will apply contracting approach to enable the targeted women headed household access to the project services as they do not have the capacity to manage shelter rehabilitation through self help approach.Likelihood of acceptance is further increased by the close involvement of beneficiaries throughout the project cycle. Physical access to the communities where summerization works take place are good, with main roads paved and all targeted beneficiaries are in accessible areas in the four governorates in Gaza Strip , accordingly no need for prior coordination with any officials implement the intervention
