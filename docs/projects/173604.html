Project Summary

IOM aims to facilitate the immediate, adequate and equitable delivery of services to the displaced population in camp and camp-like settings through camp coordination and camp management (CCCM) services, information management mechanisms, and sector coordination of partners and stakeholders.
CCCM activities will continue to provide site facilitation support and site level coordination in formal and informal camps in the following Borno locations: MMC, Bama (including Banki), Dikwa, Gwoza (including Pulka,) Ngala, Monguno, Mafa, Jere, Kala Balge (Rann), and Damboa; and in the following Adamawa locations: Yola North, Yola South, Girie and Song.
The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) will continue to provide mobility tracking in the six conflict-affected northeastern Nigerian states (,Adamawa, Bauchi, Borno, Gombe, Taraba and Yobe). with the Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT) deployed mainly in Adamawa, Borno, and when necessary, in Yobe due to fluidity of population movement. Biometric Registration will remain a priority in selected camps to assist partners for verification (WFP) and support other organizations and their distributions. Finally, detailed assessment surveys for return locations will continue in selected areas. DTM will strive to strengthen regional collaborations in place with emerging needs as well.
As Sector Co-Lead, IOM with UNHCR, is accountable for the overall CCCM, Shelter and non-food items (NFI) responses in North-East Nigeria. The sector will continue to ensure access to multi-sectoral assistance in camps and camp-like settings, advocating and identifying durable solutions for the displaced families and affected communities; and building national capacity through CCCM trainings for national authorities and local partners.

1 - Needs

Insecurity, triggered by the conflict between Nigerian forces and Non-State armed groups (NSAGs), has further resulted in a massive displacement crisis in Northeast Nigeria bringing the total number of displaced persons to just over 2 million. Population movements still present major humanitarian challenges to the already stretched resources. While camp management structures have been established under the lead of NEMA/SEMA in the main sites hosting IDPs, gaps in terms of camp management staff presence and multi sectorial response remain in 2021. The latest DTM assessments have accounted for 2,118,500 displaced population and 1,714,682 returnees. The last three months, an average of 700 new arrivals have been recorded per month. Congestion is still an issue in Borno, i.e., at least 1,000 individuals above camp capacity/ camp, and which fall severely below the globally acceptable Sphere standards. The spontaneous nature of sites coupled with existing gaps in humanitarian assistance make individuals in displacement sites extremely vulnerable to a multitude of risks including, but not limited to, flooding, wind, disease outbreak (including COVID-19) etc. Due to seasonal patterns, lack of access to potable water and sanitation infrastructure, and vulnerabilities as a result of continuous displacement due to military operations and low access to essential healthcare, north-east Nigeria remains highly endemic for to diarrheal and other diseases New influxes of IDPs into overcrowded and under-serviced camps and settlements exacerbate therisk of disease outbreaks. Widespread displacement, new waves of displacement, or secondary displacement and steadily increasing number of returnees have also highlighted the need to reinforce the data assessment mechanism through DTM, and to expand the ﬂexibility of interventions conducted in camps, host communities, in areas of return and in areas expected to have humanitarian access within the next few months. Since 2014, when detailed widespread data collection begun, over 60% of IDPs have been displaced more than once conﬁrming the continued, protracted and precarious plight of the displaced. Thus, continuous monitoring and analyses of the displacement situation is essential. At the same time, since August of 2015, more than 1.6 million persons have returned to their places of habitual residence, requiring the need for detailed assessments on the conditions of the locations of return, identiﬁcation of livelihood opportunities as well as ways of both rebuilding and improving infrastructure across these locations. Moreover, as additional work continues to improve conditions of affected population in the crisis across the board, detailed information on needs of the most vulnerable and deprived is needed in real time. Given that the security situation continues to be extremely ﬂuid there is need for round-the-clock monitoring of new displacements or arrivals and capturing of their needs for adequate humanitarian action. Timely and accurate information is needed not only for planning and response but also for avoiding duplication of efforts by different humanitarian actions, making better use of limited resources.

2 - Activities

IOM will continue to provide and enhance its site facilitation capacities in formal and informal camp and camp-like settings to ensure that IDPs have access to basic services and have improved living conditions. This will be achieved through continuous deployment of,mobile camp management teams. IOM CCCM operations will be guided by the CCCM Camp Management Standards and agreed humanitarian standards in north-east Nigeria ensuring community participation in all aspects of camp life. IOM CCCM will continue the provision of the following services: • Coordination and information management• Communication with Communities: • Site improvement and care and maintenance of infrastructures • Community participation • Protection mainstreamingIOM will continue to support the IDPs living outside the camps through establishment of community centers, mobile teams, and establishment of community structures. IOM will also support the decongestion strategy of the Sector in Pulka and Dikwa through stie preparation, development and community mobilization, in aim of providing adequately spaced shelters mitigating further risks related to safety and health. IOM CCCM will continue the management of reception centers for new arrivals in Bama, Banki, Dikwa, Ngala, and Gwoza.. CCCM Capacity Development programs will continue for government counterparts, IDP communities, national authorities, and key stakeholders and partners. IOM through its role as Sector Coordinator will improve support for field sector/operation coordination in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe through coordination of sector partners and continued support to coordination mechanisms set up for deep field locations and hard to reach areas. IOM’s DTM Unitwill continue its comprehensive and periodic assessments in over 2,400 locations to bring to light movements caused by range of factors including communal clashes, escalating crime and natural disasters in north-east Nigeria. DTM will provide better understanding of the numbers and needs of the affected population as well as an analysis of sector-wide needs.The growing numbers and needs of returnees are similarly captured, analyzed and widely shared with humanitarian partners. Specifically, DTM will ensure improved information management mechanisms for displaced populations in north-east Nigeria to facilitate planning, gap analysis and rapid response• DTM: track and monitor displacement, including gender and age disaggregation, service delivery, and key needs and gaps across communities.• Biometric registration of IDPs and affected population in the new humanitarian operational areas through registering of new caseloads as well as update of previously registered caseloads • Reinforcement of monitoring and first alert mechanisms for rapid referral in close coordination with sector partners and Government counterparts through rapid paper registrations in order to share data within 24 hours of displacement.• Use IOM distribution application to track assistance provided to beneficiaries over time to prevent duplications of efforts by organizations and better enhance assistance provision.• Continued surveys on returnees and case by case analysis to identify the return conditions in areas of origin and further advice government and developmental partners on areas of focus• Provision of data and sharing with respective local authorities• IOM has established a Stability Index, which utilizes a variety of statistical techniques to assess perceptions of community stability in return areas to enable partners to better develop strategies and plan resources and operations in vulnerable areas.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM mitigates protection risks and minimize GBV and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) risks in camps and camp-like settings through enhanced community participation and consultations with communities such as identification of risks and hazards with representatives of the communities to plan site improvement works and install safety measures; inclusion of women and girls in decision-making fora in camps; training of entry points areas on PSEA; training of camp management staff and community volunteers on GBV and PSEA; and working with Protection, GBV, Child Protection and Health actors in sites ensuring referral pathways are functional and active. IOM’s commitment to preventing and mitigating risks of GBV in emergencies including in camps and camp-like settings is captured in its Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in Crises (GBViC Framework). IOM’s CCCM response includes the outlined risk mitigation measures in this Framework and works with various Protection and GBV actors in planning and implementation. As part of regular CCCM activities in camps, the camp management teams will raise awareness on GBV and PSEA, reporting mechanisms and referral pathways. DTM will continue collecting protection data on vulnerability and needs of IDPs, Gender-Based Violence risks referrals and access to health facilities. In the frame of the PRM global initiative, the DTM data collectors equipped with training in Protection Enhanced DTM, will concentrate their attention on emphasizing, identifying and properly following the channels set about in referring Protection related cases. The team will refer all cases that fall into the category and refer them to specialized Protection agencies. DTM team in close coordination with IOM's Psychosocial Support (PSS) teams will refer all cases they may come across for referrals. Continuous engagement with other Protection actors especially Child Protection Sector where all unidentified minors captured during assessments will be referred to them immediately. Through its biometric registration and verification activities, profiles of each IDP or affected person will be created to include where possible, a small component on soft protection monitoring in newly accessible areas, promoting PSEA efforts and enlarge advocacy in order to provide further information to the Protection SWG. IOM, as an active member of the PSEA task force, aims to contribute in the development the following: 1) IEC materials on PSEA targeting community and humanitarian actors; 2) Advocacy tools; 3) Curriculum on PSEA; and 4) Community Based Complaints Mechanisms (CBCM). IOM has received funding at its HQ level to provide assistance in developing the CBCM specifically tailored to the North East Nigerian context, as well as to organize a training of trainers.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population through all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons With Specific Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision making process. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the different methods such as information desks, complaints and feedback desks, suggestion boxes, audio recorders, and various community meetings. IOM has already established these mechanisms in all its managed sites across Adamawa and Borno, functioning as a two-way communication between the community and the agency. These are also shared with other agencies accountable for their respective sectors.
