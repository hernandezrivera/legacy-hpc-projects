Project Summary

The project aims to seamlessly integrate CP, GP, and GBV protection services across six targeted sites over two years (2024-2025), catering to the needs of 17,750 BNFs. Key initiatives include:
- Establishment of two protection help desks within new camps slated for construction in Azaz and Al Dana. These desks will serve as vital conduits for vulnerable groups— men, women, boys, and girls, and PWDs—facilitating easy access to essential services and fostering peace within the designated areas. Outreach efforts will extend to 1500 BNFs (766 W, 734 M).
-The project will provide awareness-raising activities in general on the mechanism of complaints, its importance, and how to deal with cases of exposure to risk and offer parenting programs and general awareness-raising to Reduce household violence and its negative impact on children. Additionally, raising awareness Mitigates risks of GBV,CP, GP-related, medical, and specialized PSS services according to the referral pathway mechanism provided by GBV SC that is based on an understanding of people’s needs Through Conduct a human-centered design HCD, where sessions will be provided to 5,000 beneficiaries.
- Protection staff in Binaa will develop GBV, CP, and general practices services, map referral pathways conduct psychological, medical, and protection referrals for 500 beneficiaries, and coordinate with partners who provide these services on the ground.
-As part of our comprehensive project initiative, we prioritize the well-being and mental health of those affected by various adversities. Through tailored Psychosocial Support (PSS) sessions for 2000 beneficiaries.
- Establishment of four child-friendly spaces across targeted communities to address the recreational, educational, and emotional needs of 600 children. These safe havens, guided by trained facilitators, offer opportunities for play, learning, and social interaction.
- Furthermore, we prioritize safeguarding the housing, land, and property rights of 3750 IDPs. This includes providing legal assistance and conducting community awareness campaigns. By prioritizing HLP safeguarding, we empower IDPs to rebuild their lives in safe and dignified living environments.
- Enhance strategies to empower women and girls, focusing on the most at-risk groups (adolescent girls, divorced and widowed, W&Gs with disabilities, older women, and families supported by women) to prevent GBV and to change negative attitudes by empowering women through small-scale projects, training initiatives, and financial grants for families supported by women for 100 women and providing dignity kits for 1500 Female.
- Provision of cash assistance for protection to 150 families at risk of protection issues, notably those led by women, families with persons with disabilities (PWDs), and those employing child labor, aimed at mitigating these risks.
- Our project focuses on promoting community integration and peacebuilding, particularly between minority groups and society, as well as between displaced populations and their host communities. Through targeted outreach sessions and initiatives, this includes facilitating dialogue forums, reconciliation workshops, and livelihood support initiatives to reduce tensions and promote social cohesion for 300 individuals.
- Conduct focused training sessions for 150 key individuals, including educators, and local councils, equipping them with the knowledge and skills to effectively raise awareness among Beneficiary members Through Conducting a human-centered design HCD.
Finally, BINAA M&E team will implement mechanisms of M&E during the implementation of the project activity, such as collecting data using different tools, implementing PDM, PSEA, and CPM questionnaires, Ensuring that safety requirements are applied, Ensuring the beneficiaries' awareness of the feedback and complaint mechanism process., analyzing data to track engagement, gaining knowledge, reporting incidents, and use data to improve project effectiveness.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Based on (HNO) 2024, Syria faces significant humanitarian challenges, with 16.7 M people (8.4F, 8.3M) requiring assistance, including 5.5 M IDPs. The situation worsened following a series of earthquakes in northern Syria on February 6, 2023, and escalated tensions in NS. Key findings from Binaa FGDs in evaluated camps underscored various critical issues, including unaccompanied or separated children, caregivers experiencing psychosocial problems, and the urgent need to empower women responsible for family support.The project aims to the following activities:Establishment of 2 protection help desks serving 1500 BNFs.Conducting awareness sessions for 5000 IDPs.Providing referrals for psychiatric, medical, and protection services to 500 IDPs.Delivering individual PSS and group activities for 2000 BNFs.Safeguarding HLP documents for 3750 individuals.Distributing dignity kits to1500 females.Offering CVA to 150 induvial and families.Conducting awareness sessions for 150 key ind (local councils, camp managers, etc.) to disseminate information on evacuation mechanisms during emergencies.Implementing initiatives for community integration and peacebuilding, engaging 300 individuals in joint cultural and social activities.Empowering 100 women through small-scale projects, training, and cash grants.Establishing child-friendly spaces equipped with recreational services in four targeted locations.Regarding child protection activities:Safe spaces: Establishing protection centers and child-friendly spaces for vulnerable children.Awareness raising: Educating children and caregivers to prevent domestic violence and child labor.Referrals: Providing psychological, medical, and psychosocial support to children at risk.CVA: Supporting families with children to reduce child labor.Training sessions: Empowering community members on child protection in emergencies and reuniting separated children.We will also conduct a monthly CPM.The most vulnerable groups of children: Prioritized displaced children, separated children, child laborers, and children WDs.Risk assessments highlight potential challenges:GP risk: Risk related to the location of service provision, Binaa ensures site selection through agreement with the community and conducting safety audit operations.GBV risk: Women and girls may face harassment and abuse, when travelling to/from distribution points, Binaa will ensure to Train staff on Do No Harm, and PSEACP risk: risk of doing harm with unintended consequences to girls, and boys through the provision of specialized child protection services, Binaa will ensure training packages are in line CP minimum standards and consider remote managed context.Assumption: Secure, accessible project locations with available resources, yet risk of access loss due to armed groups or natural disasters. Mitigation: Continuous security monitoring, development of exit scenarios, and coordination with stakeholders to prevent overlap
