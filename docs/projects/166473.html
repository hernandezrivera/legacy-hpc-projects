Basic Info

Project Name

Provision of life-saving assistance through strengthening of the preventive and curative nutritional services for under-five children in Borno,Adamawa and Yobe States in the context of COVID-19 pandemic.

Start Date

26/05/2020

End Date

25/12/2020

Project Summary

This project will focus on strengthening the capacity of the state and partner organizations in the management of severe acute malnutrition across the BAY states in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic. It will assist the healthcare workers prevent and control the spread of COVID-19 infection as well as provide effective management of existing cases severe acute malnutrition and COVID-19. This will be achieved via the conduct of an integrated T.O.T on the management of severe acute malnutrition in with COVID-19 in accordance to the latest WHO-approved recommended protocols. Also component of infection control and prevention will be fully integrated into the training module. WHO will also preposition essential medicines and medical equipment for the smooth running of the Stabilization Centres to ensure uninterrupted supply of commodities to the implementing partners. These supplies includes life saving drugs and equipment for the management of critical patients e.g oxygen concentrators and patient monitors. It is also aimed at mitigating/interrupting the morbidity and mortality of COVID-19 through safe nutrition preventive interventions like implementation of the mother MUAC screening as soon as possible, and ICCM approaches in the 113 hard-to -reach communities in the BAY states with dissemination of messages and materials to promote safe breastfeeding and other IYCF practices in the COVID-9 context.

1 - Needs

On 11th March 2020, WHO declared the COVID-19 PHEIC event as a pandemic due to the number of cases of COVID-19 outside China increasing in 13-fold, and the number of affected countries tripling. There are now more than 3.5 Million cases in 202 countries, areas and territories, with over 248,000 deaths. Many more patients are still fighting for their lives in hospitals. The Africa continent has had the effects of the outbreak. All the index cases in the Africa region were imported from the affected countries in Europe andAsia. As at May 3, 2020, there have been 29,438 confirmed cases in Africa with 1,068 deaths. Nigeria has also recorded a total of 2,558 confirmed cases with 87 fatalities. There are already 125 confirmed cases across the BAY states with 12 deads. The pandemic is adding to the burden of endemic infectious diseases prevalent in North-east Nigeria, such as cholera, measles, and malaria. Pre-existing poor hygiene practices, poor coverage in water and sanitation services and overcrowded living conditions especially in IDP camps also augment the incidence and spread of contamination by the virus. In an already degraded context of weak health systems, expanded food insecurity, massive population displacement, limited access to WASH infrastructures, and restricted humanitarian access, the COVID-19 pandemic is a major threat to the nutritional status of young children and mothers. Potential impact may include:• Closure of all land borders leading to a disruption of food supply that, in turn, will increase food prices and exacerbate food insecurity;• Compromised quality of care for acute malnutrition treatment if hospitals and health staff are overwhelmed leading to increased mortality;• Suspension of IYCF and mother support groups, cooking demonstrations, mass MUAC screening, and MNP distributions due to prevention measures that do not permit gathering of people;• Reduced coverage of curative and preventative health and nutrition interventions due to restricted movements and decreased geographical access;• Suspension of nutrition programs due to national directives or measures, or complete unavailability of health system and/or health workers.New evidenced based guidelines are emerging and their is need to adapt these protocols aquick as possible to the unique context of the north-east considering the degree of spread and the risk involved. This will mitigate the morbidity and mortality that the pandemic might impose on the already deteriorating nutritional status of the population especially women and children.

2 - Activities

The main thrust of WHO in emergencies is to strengthen, through information dissemination and training; the national, sub-national and international capacities of partners to meet the nutritional needs of populations in emergencies. This project will focus mainly on supporting government and partners in-patient management of SAM in the context of COVID-19 pandemic according to the latest guidelines and provision of effective technical support through the production and dissemination of scientifically validated and up-to date guidelines,criteria and methodologies relating to nutrition and COVID-19. SC staff from MoH and partner iNGOs will participate and benefit from each of the training. The training will be Training of Trainers(T.O.T) adapted to respect the IPC recommendation of physical distancing. 3 T.O.Ts will be conducted across the BAY states with remote support provided for its step-down at the treatment facilities.IPC protocol will be fully integrated in these training. • Provide lifesaving drugs and equipment for treatment of SAM children with medical complications: More than 4000 severely acute malnourished children with medical complications will be provided treatment with lifesaving drugs through the provision of 75 additional inpatient kits to the stabilization centresacross the 3 states. The kits consist of basic and supplementary drugs as well as include some equipment and additional supplies that will include Oxygen concentrators and PPEs for the SCs. • WHO will conduct regular supportive supervision field visits to at least bi weekly to nutrition stabilization centres. SC staff will be provided on the job training ongaps identified in their knowledge and skills. WHO will also conduct virtual coaching sessions with SC staff in the field to provide specialized care(by pediatricians) through mobile video calls to sit with them in detail and support and demonstrate addressing the gaps identified. .WHO will also develop adapted nutrition/covid-19 job aids/IEC material for the SCs, some of them will bepasted on the walls as important reference material for the staff. • Improved collaboration with health sector partners including SMoH, Hospital management board, nutrition sector and partner organizations will be maintained to review needs, assess progress and devise a strategy for strengthening integrated strategies in the management of malnutrition in Covid-19 context.Provide integrated nutrition services (MUAC screening, IYCF ) to children in Hard-to-reach areas of Borno ,Yobe and Adamawa State during 6 months through the implementation of Mother MUAC screening(training of mothers and provision of MUAC tapes) and support prompt referral for treatment by WHO's mobile teams ,provision of IYCF counseling to caregivers of children under 2 years while ensuring recommended physical distancing.
