Project Summary

It is estimated that there are 2.6 million internally displaced persons in Somalia. Successive years of drought exacerbated by a prolonged conflict has resulted in an enduring humanitarian crisis that demands urgent life-saving interventions. But at the same time, the protracted nature of this crisis requires a strategy that sees beyond the immediate needs and incorporates durable solutions throughout the response. With this project IOM will provide camp management and camp coordination services to men, women, boys and girls in displacement sites in the districts of Baidoa, Kismayu and Doolow to ensure that the most urgent needs of the IDPs are met, while also working towards the achievement of durable solutions for displaced families.
Latest estimations of displacement conditions in Somalia indicate that around 1.8 million people have settled in almost 2,000 IDP sites, most of which are informal, small and scattered settlements in urban or peri-urban areas. Living in private land often means that IDPs are at constant risk of eviction, which further stresses their already vulnerable situation. Protracted conflict-driven displacement aggravated by natural disasters, limited livelihood opportunities, limited availability of public services and limited access to durable solutions all contribute to the continued increase in the number and vulnerability of IDPs.
Baidoa hosts the largest number of IDPs after Mogadishu with more than 362,000 displaced persons living in 435 sites. Recently government authorities have allocated public land for IDPs which has enabled IOM and other humanitarian/development partners to relocate families at risk of eviction, linking traditional CCCM activities with an approach tailored towards the achievement of durable solutions. As more public land becomes available to IDPs, IOM plans to support more families to relocate. However, for the foreseeable future the majority of families will remain in makeshift sites under the constant threat of forced eviction.
In Kismayu, 59,200 IDPs are hosted in 133 sites. These sites are privately owned and most IDPs have to pay to live there; however, these agreements do not prevent families from the risk of being evicted. Most of these IDP sites are overcrowded and lack basic infrastructure; living conditions are poor with overstretched services and shelters often made of locally available materials such as plastic and worn-out clothes.
Doolow is currently hosting around 50,700 IDPs in two makeshift sites located in public land: Kabasa and Qansaxley. The high population density in these camps and the subsequent concentration of service provision makes service monitoring and coordination key aspects of CCCM related needs. In addition, community mobilization and the enactment of representative governance structures are needed to facilitate dialogue between service providers and affected populations, and to enhance accountability towards them.

Project Activities

IOM will continue CCCM activities in Baidoa, Dollow and Kismayo. IOM will coordinate the delivery of mulitsectoral services in sites by representing CCCM in relevant coordination forums; conducting site veriﬁcations on a quarterly basis; mapping the services available in sites; monitoring service delivery in sites. IOM will improve living conditions of displaced people by supporting community led site maintenance activities to ensure upkeep of sites; conducting safety audits to identify areas of high protection risk ; implementing emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites and developing new land for the relocation of displaced people. IOM will strengthen community self-management and access to information for displaced populations by supporting diverse and inclusive governance structures; providing capacity building to existing governance structures on CCCM and related principles; providing community spaces for management committees; and operating timely and responsive feedback and complaint mechanisms.

Mitigation Measures

Lense 2: Exclusion and marginalizationIOM will ensure diverse and inclusive governance structures are present in sites that represent all subsets of populations. IOM will ensure 2 way feedback mechanisms for populations to voice their concerns on services and site governance.IOM will provide information to service providers on service availability and access in IDP sites to ensure non biased targeting of IDP sites by partners.IOM will encourage the participation of women through activities specifically geared towards reducing barriers to women’s participation. Where no governance structure is presence IOM will support communities to come together and agree on the type of representation they would require that allows the participation of all groups in decision makingLense 3: Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and servicesIOM will conduct safety audits of sites in collaboration with the appropriate partners to identify safety risks and mitigate GBV.IOM will ensure the training all community workers in GBV referrals.IOM will coordinate with GBV partners to ensure that GBV referral pathways are operational and known to community members in IDP sites. Lense 4: Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areasIOM will work with HLP partners to ensure that all IOM managed sites have their eviction status mapped. IOM will work with local authorities to mitigate evictions of displaced populationsIOM will ensure the land tenure of all public land designated for relocation of displaced populations is documented legally.
