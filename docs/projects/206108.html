Project Summary

This proposed project will address emergency shelter and lifesaving non-food items needs of vulnerable populations affected by conflict, flooding or in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) and IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) acute food insecurity both displaced and in secondary displacement. The project will target 53000 individuals (Men 5300, Women 10,070, Boys 13,250, Girls 24,380) in situations of emergency or transition both newly displaced, returnees, protracted IDPs and host communities, in Cueibet – 14,200, Yirol West 10,600, Abyei Administration Area – 11,200, Aweil East – 8800, and Twic – 8200 individuals. Those to be targeted are most vulnerable populations in need (after an assessment). The project will reach 15% of the target population (7950 individuals) as People with Special Needs. 80% of the target population will receive Gift In-Kind assistance because there are no functional markets. This assistance which will be prepositioned from the Cluster Core Pipeline. After an assessment has been done, and an analysis report submitted through the SFP to the Cluster at the National level, a pipeline request will be submitted. After approval, items will be transported to respective CCOSS storage facilities and subsequent distributions. 20% of the target population in Abyei, Aweil East, Yirol West and Twic will receive Sectoral Cash. This is because these locations have functional markets, making cash and voucher assistance a feasible and the quickest modality to provide urgent assistance. A market assessment will be conducted, a report submitted through the SFP to the Cluster for approval. Beneficiaries will undergo biometrics registration. CCOSS will coordinate will other cluster partners in respective locations for effective response.
Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) will ensure that there is proper coordination especially in locations where there is more than one implementing partner. County cluster meetings will be held weekly to ensure proper coordination, avoid double-counting, and as well reduce duplication of efforts. Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) is a key member of other clusters working within the same locality as well as a State Co-lead for Lakes and has a strong static presence in Abyei, Aweil East, Twic and Yirol West.
Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) will use humanitarian hubs for coordination meetings and other related humanitarian activities. Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) will be working with other partners who are operating in the same areas or locations on the issues affecting vulnerable populations in hard to reached areas through mobile response in collaboration with other partners on the ground. Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) will also work with other partners on area-based approaches through the open forums during cluster meetings where partners would highlight activities being implemented and locations of operation to avoid duplication activities. This would be achieved through coordination of the cluster and support of CCCM, coordinating the activities of partners to avoid overlaps of the same activities in one location.
General Operation & other direct costs include costs associated with providing direct management & other support functions at the Main office in Juba. This includes management staff salaries, communication, stationery, rents, etc. The Indirect project costs include bank charges, staff recruitment & servicing costs, auditing and consultancy costs.

1. Needs

Since the outbreak of fighting in Sudan on 15 April 2023, the influx of people fleeing the country has increased exponentially. Based on the IOM/UNHCR tracker, as at November 22, 2023, the number of arrivals registered in Northern Bahr el Ghazal is 11,297 individuals, Abyei – 1551 individuals, Twic 6802 Individuals and in lakes 424 Individuals. This could potentially exacerbate the protection risks and vulnerabilities of the population of the northern States but also returnees arriving from Sudan and increase food insecurity, negative coping mechanisms and acute humanitarian needs. According to the South Sudan Humanitarian crisis report, “South Sudan is facing its worst flooding in decades. Four consecutive years of record-breaking rains and floods covered two-thirds of the country and left people without food or land to cultivate. The devasting flooding damaged shelters and schools, destroyed crops and household goods, reduced access to safe water and hindered humanitarian access” According to UNOCHA, in June 2023, “these recurring floods have worsened an already dire situation, leaving people without food and viable land for cultivation. The same states affected by severe flooding are now receiving refugees and returnees from Sudan, further straining the capacity to respond” According to the ISNA (2023), an estimated 8.5 million people will require humanitarian assistance in 2024. Of these, 2.1m will require urgent shelter and non-food items support, with half the affected population representing IDPs. Shocks related to conflict and insecurity have likely driven multi-sectoral needs across Lakes, Abyei, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states. The effects of these high levels of conflicts have seemingly led to displacement on an intra- and inter-county level, and driving a deterioration of shelter conditions for already food insecure IDP populations and host communities. Moreover, there are indications of particularly severe levels of food insecurity and vulnerability to further shocks in Aweil East, Twic, Cueibet and Yirol West Counties which are Emergency (IPC Phase 4), October 2023 – March 2024, with food insecurity conditions projected to persist at the same level until July 2024, primarily related to low levels of harvested food stocks, displacement and decreased livelihood access and diversity. This compounded further by flooding in Yirol West further makes the population more susceptible. Territorial conflict in the Abyei Administrative Area (AAA) continues to cause displacement of people from (Twic) Warrap into Abyei Administration Area. The displaced communities are in dire urgent humanitarian support, with most, if not all of their emergency coping mechanisms exhausted due to repeated shocks. Those to be targeted include women and children because they remain especially vulnerable due to their roles in society and the protection risks inherent in being without shelter or social support networks. A small number of host community members do not have sufficient emergency shelter or non-food items (ES/NFIs) as they have used their already limited resources to provide for the displaced. CCOSS will use participatory approaches to identify the needs of women, men, boys and girls. In this proposed project, barriers to participation of women and other vulnerable groups will be discussed and taken into account in both assessment and implementation stages. Gender and age considerations will be included in needs assessments prior to implementation. CCOSS will also train staff, community leaders and community members on AAP methodology. This will ensure that AAP/CCE will be incorporated in all the stages of the project cycle.

2. Project activities

1) Engage the community in a way that stimulates active participation and promote accountability to the affected populations. This will be through focus group discussions; setting up complaint and feedback mechanism (feedback desks in the community) prior to project implementation; formation of committees. This will enhance the community’s dignity while increasing their ability to respond to new shock and build on existing skills. 2) Provide assistance with in-kind emergency shelter to 3640 displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Cueibet, Abyei, Aweil East, Yirol West and Twic. 3) Provide assistance with in kind light emergency shelter to 4900 displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Cueibet, Abyei, Aweil East, Yirol West and Twic. 4) Provide assistance with in-kind standard lifesaving NFIs to 3640 displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Cueibet, Abyei, Aweil East, Yirol West and Twic. 5) Provide assistance with in-kind light NFI kits to 4900 displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Cueibet, Abyei, Aweil East, Yirol West and Twic. 6) Provide assistance with in-kind flood kits to 15700 displaced populations displaced by floods in Cueibet, Abyei, Aweil East, Yirol West and Twic. 7) Provide assistance with in-kind individual Shelter/NFIs to 5800 displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Cueibet, Abyei, Aweil East, Yirol West and Twic.8) Provide assistance with essential emergency shelter upgrades to 8000 highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non-displaced in Cueibet, Abyei, Aweil East, Yirol West and Twic. 9) Provide assistance with essential NFI replacements to 7200 highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non-displaced in Cueibet, Abyei, Aweil East, Yirol West and Twic. 10) Provision of Sectoral cash assistance to 7760 returnees, food insecure, floods or conflict displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Abyei, Aweil East, Yirol West & Twic. The needs will be identified after conducting a market assessment and cash assistance feasibility studies. This activity will promote transition to durable solutions for the affected populations protect displaced persons from harsh weather conditions and protection risks as well as enhancing access to other basic services through construction of durable housing solutions that contribute to resilience and withstand future shocks11) Monitoring of quality service provision – PDM will be conducted two weeks after the distribution is finalized. The format for the PDM will include conducting focus group discussions, random sampling for interviews. This activity will ensure fast, lifesaving response to the right people, enhances ability to use S/NFIs for supporting results bases health, protection, livelihood and hygiene needs and reducing overlap to reach the truly vulnerable. 12) Mainstream HLP AAP/CCE and conflict sensitive approaches throughout all stages of S/NFI response through conducting conflict analysis, sensitizing local chiefs on women rights especially about land, avoiding manipulation of distribution of S/NFIs by remaining neutral and practicing “do-no-harm” principle. This activity will reduce harm to communities and build resilience by strengthening existing systems and community dynamics. 13) Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response – this will be done through conducting gender safety audits, ensuring that distributions are done in an open space, during the day, and close to the camps. This will ensure gender and protection risks are addressed directly, mitigating the chances of exacerbated vulnerabilities
