Project Summary

IRC through its Women Protection and EmpowermenIRC through its Women Protection and Empowerment interventions will support at risk women and girls through provision of quality and timely lifesaving GBV Response and Prevention services in Juba, Aweil, Leer, Mayandit, Rubkona, Maban, Rotriak, Koch, Rumbek, and Panijiyar. At risk women and girls including GBV survivors will receive response services following interagency GBV case management guidelines focusing on survivor centered approach to support the healing process. IRC will provide capacity building trainings and supportive supervision to response team for provision of quality services to at risk women and girls. The interventions will strengthen the coordination among GBV service providers to improve referral mechanism during GBV case management processes. Multisectoral referral pathways will be developed and updated on regular basis. IRC will not only lead the coordination with other service providers during case management but will also ensure that the client is satisfied with the services. Regular case conferences will be conducted with relevant GBV actors involved in case management to improve the quality of the service provision maintaining confidentiality. The data will be managed and protected through GBVIMS and GBVIMS+. IRC will also share the trends and analysis of GBV cases with relevant agencies that will guide the service provision process. IRC will establish women and girls safe spaces for healing, recovery and empowerment of at risk women and girls. The activities at the safe spaces will be guided by WGSS toolkit, tailored curriculums including Women Rise and Girls Shine for PSS and life skill-based education for women and girls. All the interventions at safe spaces will be women and girls centered. The safe spaces will serve as an entry point for GBV survivors to get GBV case management services through trained case workers. To prevent GBV, IRC will engage community through multiple outreach activities including awareness raising campaigns, capacity building of community leaders and women support networks and behavioral change interventions. The behavior change interventions will include implementation of EMAP and SASA! Together curriculums. The prevention interventions will also strengthen the community protection mechanism to ensure safe referrals of GBV cases. For Risk mitigation, IRC will conduct regular safety audits engaging women and girls at the community and safe space levels. IRC will develop an action plan on the basis of safety audit findings to mitigate the identified GBV risks by women and girls including distribution of dignity kits and menstrual hygiene and management sessions. IRC will make conscious efforts to include all marginalized groups including persons with disabilities in prevention and response interventions. Activities will be conducted at an accessible place and suitable time for persons specially women and girls with disabilities. IRC will make information accessible for persons with disabilities through door to door visits and in local easy to understand languages. IRC will also link persons with disabilities with the service providers through referral pathways. Special measures will be taken to make women and girls safe spaces accessible for wheel chair users. Similarly, IRC will also ensure to allocate space for children following CP guidelines so that lactating mothers can also actively participate in the interventions. IRC will also foster an inclusive culture by raising awareness, challenging stereotypes, and promoting positive attitudes towards persons with disabilities within communities and society at large.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces a multitude of humanitarian risks, encompassing natural disasters, conflicts, and pandemics. Within this challenging context, preexisting high levels of gender-based violence (GBV) persist due to entrenched structural gender inequalities, a lack of law enforcement, and harmful gender norms. These factors leave women and girls vulnerable to various forms of abuse. The onset of any emergency situation exacerbates these existing gender disparities and discrimination, resulting in an upsurge of Gender-Based Violence.During the recent crisis in Sudan, thousands of refugees and returnees, including women and girls, fled the conflict and entered South Sudan via different entry points. Multiple assessments conducted by UN agencies have revealed that people, particularly women and girls, are at a heightened risk of physical, psychological, and sexual violence on their journey to South Sudan, within transit centers, and in IDP and refugee camps. The situation has been further compounded by limited access to available humanitarian services, primarily due to extensive flooding rendering many villages inaccessible.Incidents of intimate partner violence and rape have seen an increase for various reasons. Shifting traditional gender roles have placed additional burdens on women, who are now compelled to assume the role of breadwinners for their families, thereby exposing themselves to greater risks of GBV. According to the information provided by GBV AoR, the 2023 GBV Information Management System indicates that 44% of GBV incidents occurred within the context of intimate partner violence. In a protracted conflict setting, conflict-related sexual violence has also surged.GBV poses a substantial concern within the community, as evidenced by a national prevalence study on violence against women and men conducted in 2022, which revealed that 58% of women and girls experienced at least one form of GBV over the past 12 months. Moreover, the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that 2.7 million people, primarily women and girls, are at risk of GBV. Women and girls with disabilities are even more vulnerable and are at risk of GBV because of a number of factors including lack of access to information and services, cultural barriers, dependency and lack of opportunities. Persons with disabilities often face various vulnerabilities due to social, physical and attitudinal barrier in society. These vulnerabilities significantly impact their quality of life and access to fundamental rights and opportunities. In addition to the lack of accessibility to services and opportunities, persons with disabilities frequently encounter discrimination and stigma leading to social inclusion and unequal treatment in various aspects of life including education and social interactions. The conflicts and natural disasters are further compounding the situation by disrupting support networks and services.

2. Project activities

IRC will provide GBV response services and prevention intervention targeting the crisis affected populations especially at risk women and girls in Koch, Panyijiar, Aweil, Maban, Juba, Leer, Mayendit, Rotriak and Rubkona counties. GBV response: Through the provision of life saving GBV case management services guided by the survivor centered approach, IRC will ensure the service provision will be in coordination with other service providers in line with the multisector approach which comprise of medical, legal, safety and psychosocial support. All incidents reported will be captured in the GBVIMS upon the consent of each survivor and the team will ensure satisfaction of GBV survivors. IRC will continuously engage women and girls and the community at large to identify barriers in accessing the GBV services and collaboratively work together to address them through the existing community-based protection structures. Additionally,IRC will quarterly reviews and updating of the referral pathways as well as continuous feedback sessions to ensure the pathways are functional across the proposed locations. The women and girls’ safe spaces will serve as avenues of empowerment for women and girls with a major focus on provision of contextualized psychosocial support interventions which seek to support in the healing, recovery and building resilience of women and girls. The Women and Girls Safe Space toolkit will remain instrumental in providing guidance on the operationalization of the safe spaces especially in ensuring accountability as well as encouraging the women-girls led processes and ownership. Tailored curriculums for the engagement of women and girls such as Girl Shine for the adolescent girls and their care givers as well as Women Rise a psychosocial support tool kits for the women will ensure each age group has appropriate intervention. GBV prevention IRC will engage communities through SASA! Together and EMAP as the primary GBV prevention approaches. The two approaches seek to provoke discussions on Power Imbalance as the root cause of violence while analyzing negative social norms, beliefs, behavior, and practices that contribute to violence against women and girls. Through EMAP IRC, the trained community EMAP facilitators (male and female) will lead the community engagement activities as well as facilitation of the EMAP sessions. SASA! Together activities will be collaboratively implemented through the community SASA activist who will be trained at the project inception to ensure community ownership and sustainability of the engagement even after the project phases out. Additional IRC seeks to build the capacity of communities to respond and prevent GBV through training and community led dialogues which will equip them with knowledge and skills. GBV risk mitigation measure IRC will work closely with women and community led structures to carry out GBV safety audits where action points to address the identified risks and concerns will be tasked to the relevant actors. Findings from the safety audits will inform other interventions such as training on Fuel Efficient Stoves for the women as well as production of reusable sanitary materials with the locally available raw materials. In coordination with the GBV SC IRC will distribute dignity kits to the women and girls of reproductive age at risk of GBV.
