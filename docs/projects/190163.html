Project Summary

This project will enhance access to equitable, safe, and inclusive education for 16,667 (50% girls, 4% CWDs) emergency-affected school aged boys’ and girls’ in Axum, Adwa, Adigrat, Wukro, Mekelle, and Kuha town of Tigray region. The project will be implemented by three organization in a consortium approach with Imagine 1day, Plan International, and World Vision. These organizations have ample years of experience implementing education and other emergency programs in Ethiopia. In line with the draft 2021 Ethiopia Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), the project mainly aimed to increases access to equitable, safe, inclusive and quality education through non-formal learning opportunities using accelerated learning programs. It will also support education service delivery systems and partners by strengthening their institutional and technical capacities and building resilience of children. communities, parents, and existing education structures through capacity building, coordination and information management support.
To meet these objectives and ensure that children can exercise their right to education and are protected from risks, the consortium members prioritized the provision of safe and protective learning spaces for children affected by the conflict to helping children continue learning and enhance the resilience of communities by supporting learning during this crisis in Tigray region. The consortium partners will capitalize on their collective best experiences in their effort to deliver effective and sustainable program for IDP and host communities in targeted areas. The consortium partners agreed on the geographic share based on their operational area. Imagine1 day will implement the project in Axum and Adwa. Plan International will implement in Mekelle, Kuha, and Wukro. World Vision will implement in Adigrat Plan International is the lead applicant. Imagine1 day will reach 6,667 age 5-14 children and World Psion and Plan International will each impact 5,000 children.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The project works with relevant stakeholders starting from drafting phase to the following project implementation monitoring and evaluation stages.Local and international organizations actively participate in the project design and implementation through consortia modality and other informed about the project through joint monitoring, programme review meetings and reports. The consortia members , Imagine1day and World Vision are currently implementing education, protection, WASH, and other programs in different parts of Tigray region. Plan international is also in the process to start implementing in the region. All agencies will ensure complimentary between their actions currently implemented in the targeted zones increasing coverage and impact of those interventions. This project will complement the agencies broader interventions to strengthen access to equitable, safe, and inclusive quality education in Axum, Adwa, Adigrat, Wukro, and Mekelle IDP and host communities. World Vision, Plan International, and Imagine1day actively participate in cluster and various coordination meetings at the national and regional level. At national level, the consortium members will continue attending and sharing information in the Education clusters meetings, as well as submitting timely 5Ws to the clusters coordinators. The cluster members will continue to support the Joint Education Needs Assessment (JENA) when initiated by the education cluster. Tigray Region Education Bureau, Finance, Planning and Economic Development Bureau, BOLSA, and local NGOs, as well as the respective offices at Zonal and Woreda levels will be continuously engaged. Each agency will keep engaging with local authorities, from the needs assessment throughout the all project cycle. This engagement include the signature of the Technical Agreements and MoUs, holding regular meetings with the Woreda offices of concern, organizing joint planning sessions, reporting, monitoring missions and exchange of lessons learned. By considering carefully the COVID-19 situation and relevant SoPs for activities implementation, a wide range of activities will be implemented jointly with the Regional Education Bureau, engaging specific expertise at Zone and Woreda levels. Children Safeguarding Working with a pre-primary and primary school population, the scariest risk is physical or sexual violence towards children. This can happen in the classroom (e.g., in the form of corporal punishment or inappropriate touching), at school (e.g., with bullying or sexual aggression), on the way to school (e.g., abduction and traffic accidents), and at home (e.g., as family-based violence towards the child or another family member). Another risk for children is shaming, whether overt, as in name-calling, or hidden, as in exclusion from learning or in the social life of the school. All these risks are usually most acute for girls, CWD, and other children with some “label” of marginalization. Also needing safeguarding may be teachers, especially those who come from outside of the community, and even more especially women teachers. They, too, may be subjected to shaming, bullying, or greater violence, including sexual. The project will enlist all actors, from students to school and community leaders, in recognizing these and other risks and in identifying, taking, and monitoring actions to safeguard against all of these.
