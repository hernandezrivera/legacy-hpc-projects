Project Summary

AMAN (Association for Mass Advancement Network) has developed the project with objectives to support the government, operational sectors, and humanitarian actors in ensuring information provision for the targeted communities through community-centric communication approaches on key humanitarian concerns; improve accountability to affected people, following minimum standards for the referral and response to community feedback, and through increased use of collective data analysis; and strengthen collaboration, advocacy, and technical support across different sectors and stakeholders to mainstream principles of community engagement and participation. Indicators for the assignments are; CwC_Obj_03_IN1: The number of agencies, sectors, and platforms that received technical assistance, services, and tools developed by members of the CwC Working Group. Number of humanitarian staff and volunteers from various sectors and stakeholders who received community engagement and accountability training and/or orientations, broken down by gender, age, and disability; Percentage of Rohingya refugee and host community households reporting that they were consulted and that aid providers listened to their concerns, broken down by gender, age, and disability.
The project's proposed location is Camp 14, 15 & 16. A total of 10 community promoters will be assigned to the project as its cross-cutting activities, along with health, WASH, shelter, FSS, and other project activities, will be covered during 2022. The total budget for the project is US$ 12,948 to reach 3,000 individual beneficiaries for one year of the project period. The proposed project will be implementing the following sector and sub-sector policies and guidance.
Major activities will be: training for each of the operational sectors on CE & AAP (for 10 CP); Provide technical guidance on CE/AAP to operational actors. Printable IEC (Poster, Festoon, Banner etc. (for 150 groups and 10 CP)"; Operate Information Hubs (04 Hubs)"; Mainstreaming Community Engagement/AAP"; Community Leader Groups (150 group with 5 members); Community Group Session (10 CP @ 15 group with 20 members); Community Mtg/Workshop (10 workshop with 15 members in each workshop).
Group members will be chosen based on need, and people with disabilities will be included in groups based on an independent assessment to ensure a "Do No Harm" approach. Group leaders will be selected through a group approach and segregation of responsibilities will be provided.
Gender responsive and people with disabilities with elderly people’s inclusion will be considered when and where applicable. The program's systematic process Monitoring using participatory methods is what AMAN will do next year to deliver the project and to ensure beneficiaries' inclusion in the implementation. Collect, analyze, and use disaggregated data and analysis on gender, age, and diversity and equally consult with women, girls, and marginalized groups, including during assessments and in the overall response monitoring.
Coordination, social cohesion, protection mainstreaming, age and disability, and government coordination will continue for effective implementation and protection of rights for the targeted people. Staff will receive training and orientation on community engagement and accountability for disseminating and managing CwC activities. AMAN has skilled manpower to run the assigned project. AMAN has the necessary policies and guidelines that will be followed through on the project.

1 - Identified Needs

An estimated 9,80,000 new arrivals joined over 3,10,000 existing Rohingya people who had previous contacts. Together with people in the host communities, this has led more than 1.2 million people in need of support. Community engagement and feedback is more challenging for the most vulnerable individuals, such as older persons, women and girls, PWD, illiterate persons, and those who can communicate only in Rohingya, given their reduced access to public spaces, smaller social networks and possible inability to complete a written complaint if required. This not only impacts their communication with humanitarian staff and other authorities, but their willingness and ability to provide feedback. The Rohingya expressed distrust and a lack of open communication between themselves and humanitarian agencies and government authorities. They also stressed their desire to play an active part in decision making that affects their daily lives. Qualitative data collected during the COVID-19 response through the COVID-19 Explained series found that the impact of refugees not receiving adequate feedback from health facilities about when and whether problems they raised could be resolved led to frustration and dissatisfaction and negatively affected the overall perception of humanitarian actors, with refugees questioning the reliability and trustworthiness of the response. JMSNA 2021 key finding that, 74% of households reported that they were unable to access enough clear information on the types of assistance available to them in the 6 months prior to data collection, 9% of households reported having faced challenges when providing feedback or complaints in the 6 months prior to data collection. Households reported having been able to access enough clear information (Rohingya camps) during Cyclones 97%, Covid19 100%. 57% of households reportedly having received aid reporting having been consulted and feeling that aid provider took their opinion in to account, 9%of households reportedly having received assistance, reported having faced challenges when providing feedback or complaints,AMAN working with all types of communities in the camp according to integrating DRR, Environment, Social Cohesion & Covid-19 issues. The Rohingya people face many other problems for exchanging their views. They can't have identified what to do in emergency situation. They feel hesitation for making any decision because of lack of confidence. The Root level Rohingya people feel discrimination from Majhi in services. They need proper awareness in necessary steps. Sometimes women can't express their views, we will ensure them environment for expressing their speech. in the same way, sometimes the disable persons don't get proper services as they can't come close to the communities, we will provide them services according to their needs. There is also needs adequate information provision for the targeted communities through community-centric communication approaches on key humanitarian concerns; needs Improve accountability to affected people and needs to strengthen collaboration, advocacy and technical support across different sectors and stakeholders to mainstream principles of community engagement and participation. According to their problems, AMAN has taken initiatives for improving communication with communities. There have also needs to run information hub at camps level which initiatives will be taken by AMAN.
