Project Summary

Gender inequality gaps in South Sudan continue to widen as Gender Based Violence cases increase. As a result of COVID 19, GBV cases have been escalated including teenage pregnancies and will need the response of different actors and partners to address. This project will tackle unequal power relations which a root cause to GBV in Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria, Lakes State, Pibor in Jonglei state and Mundri East in Western Equatoria which are areas that Plan International operates in by working men, women, boys and girls to transform their communities by actively participating in project activities.
These will be done through our gender transformative programming and influencing and Community engagement approach that addresses the root cause of power imbalance. Plan mainstreams GBV, safeguarding and PSEA in all its programs by mitigating risk to the people that we work with and ensuring that they feel safe while working with us. Interventions shall be tailored to have the involvement and participation of men, women, girls and boys in diversity including persons with disabilities.
The project will involve key actors in GBV response and prevention through taking leadership of the Protection Cluster or GBV sub Cluster at the state and county level. Currently, Plan International leads the protection Cluster in Nimule and Co-chair the protection Cluster in Yei. Also, in the rest of the states Plan International has been an active member in the cluster meetings and activities as a member. Plan will build on this to support the initiative of the cluster in coordinating GBV prevention and response interventions.
Plan International will create, engage and maintain linkages/ relationships with the key stakeholders and partners, such as Government including Ministry of Gender, police, judiciary including the ABC Court (A- the grassroots traditional court system at the Boma level headed by the Boma Chief; B- middle traditional court headed by the Payam Head chief; and C- Last Traditional Court system headed by the Paramount chief at County level), religious institutions, cultural and community leaders, etc.
While building on the projects like Action Research on Violence against Women and Girls; Youth Voices in Eastern Equatoria, Integrated response to conflict-affected at-risk boys, girls, women and men in Central Equatoria State, South Sudan, Improving the quality and access in provision of life-saving integrated Health, Protection, Education and Nutrition services for vulnerable children and targeted communities in Pibor county, Jonglei State and Humanitarian protection measure for children and young people affected by South Sudan Crisis(GFFO) in Lakes state, among other Protection/ GBV projects that Plan International implements.
The project will be implemented in collaboration with the State Ministry of Gender, GBV cluster/partners, community leaders and women and youth groups at different levels. Plan International will ensure that these life-saving interventions eg PSS and referrals are provided to critically needed GBV survivors including vulnerable groups and other special cases such as Persons with disabilities. Cases with compounding medical conditions will be supported through CVA and referred for clinical management.
The project will mobilize communities to form drama groups that will will be trained and supported to conduct awareness raising on GBV in communities through radio, community mobilization and sensitization, participating in advocacy events such as 16 days of activism, etc. Youth will be mobilized into groups/ clubs as Champions of Change(CoC) plays a pivotal role in peer to peer mobilization of young people to challenge negative and discriminatory social norms that contribute to GBV.
COVID-19 mitigation measures will especially those affecting public gatherings will be strengthened with COVID SOPs and items to effect COVID.

1 - Needs

Because of the power imbalances/ unequal power relations between men and women, boys and girls due to the harmful gender norms that increase economic vulnerability and inequality . With the unequal positions, women and girls are prone to impunity and violence such as sexual exploitation and abuse, early and forced marriage, denial or rights, etc. Women and girls continue to face discrimination in different spheres of their lives and attempts to challenge some of the social norms are met with resistance and violence pelted against them.The harmful social norms reinforce negative masculinity among men and boys that need to be addressed through directly involving them. The project will mobilize boys to change the harmful gender norms using the Champions of Change model. Their capacity will be built to challenge the negative masculinity that contribute to SGBV.The culture of silence over SGBV issues in families and communities has contributed greatly to human right abuses especially SGBV. Community dialogues are necessary for communities to appreciate the rights of girls and women and promote them so that they can live a dignified life.The number of GBV cases have continued to spike as a result of COVID 19 and different measures have to be put in place to combat. This was escalated by the lock down where children were not in school and a number of girls have dropped out of school due to teenage pregnancies, some resulting from sexual abuse and exploitation.According to an assessment conducted by the protection cluster in Rumbek in January 2022, it was established that there is increased incidences of GBVs in the three areas of Citcok, Tiaptiap and Pagoor payams. Such cases include early/forced marriages as people marry off the girls to feed the other family members; rape, etc. In addition, the recent floods affected about 250 households in Citcok Payam and displaced over 5,000 people displaced putting the girls and women at risk of GBV. According Lainya Center Rapid Assessment Report, February 2022, it was established that the rape cases of women and girls were reported during the assessment, most of the survivors did not receive the necessary services.Communities underscore GBV such as rape, domestic violence, sexual exploitation, sexual harassment against women and girls and consider it is seen as a ‘private’ matter to be dealt with by the community. It is based on this that we need to engage communities to understand the impact of GBV in the lives of women, girls and the whole community such mental health issues, suicide, physical harm, unwanted pregnancies, fistula, etc.Persons with disability usually bear the brunt of discrimination and violence because of the negative attitude that communities have towards them. They have limited access to GBV messages and services since their needs are not prioritized. Diversity is a key for Plan International interventions and always conducts a risk assessment to reduce risk to all children, young people and all beneficiaries including persons with disability prior to conducting activities. Mitigation measures are put in place so that the needs of Because women’s participation to community decision making is limited, they do not have a voice to challenge GBV. Through women and girls being supported to work in groups and clubs, their capacity will be built to articulate these GBV issues and voices amplified to the duty bearers.Also, the poor implementation of laws and policies has made communities lose trust in institutions that provide justice to GBV survivors. It is important to closely work with government and other community leaders in implementing the laws and police thus prioritizing capacity building for law enforcing institutions and personnels, line ministries, community leaders, etc. so as to strengthen the justice process for GBV survivors.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The Cash Voucher Assistance shall be provided to survivors of GBV to aid them access medical treatment in case of critical cases. Also, it will be provided to adolescent girls to buy their basic needs including sanitary towels or materials to make sanitary towels.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

One of the key issues that limit women and girls from accessing GBV services is related to medical bills, transport cost, etc. Women and girls usually find it challenging to procure sanitary towels because they are costly or not easily accessible in markets yet its a critical component to enable them participate in the project. Due to the insecurity and conflicts, girls and young women are susceptible to sexual exploitation because they lack basic needs.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback shall always be provided to beneficiaries on their complains and concerns raised after being addressed.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We shall consult with them on the appropriate feedback mechanism they would feel comfortable with and we put them in place. We shall have feedback meetings with communities; safe calls, suggestion boxes and establishment of help desks.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Training of all staff and associates on PSEA and a taskforce will be formed. The role of the PSEA taskforce include training community structures and awareness raising on PSEA. Our safeguarding and PSEA approach requires mainstreaming, therefore all our interventions including awareness raising.We shall work with communities to conduct a safeguarding risk assessment for all activities involving children and young people and put in place measures to mitigate these risks. A focus group discussion is done.We also work through community structures(CoC) who always trained on safeguarding and PSEA including the reporting procedure. As community safeguarding/ PSEA focal points they will raise awareness on PSEA as they carry on their dutiesAs a minimum standard, all offices are expected to have complaint mechanisms with clear messages and information on its use.The project shall establish help desks in the communities that will be used to receive concerns of safeguarding and PSEA. Also, banners and posters containing PSEA information including the reporting procedures and contacts shall be printed and displayed in public places.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will organise community meetings and engagements to agree on the appropriate feedback and complaint mechanisms in place. As Plan International, we always use suggestion boxes, safe calls, help desk, community feedback meetings. Through our M&E systems, complaints and feedback are compiled on a weekly basis(except PSEA and safeguarding on daily basis) and shared with the responsible persons to be addressed. Once they have been addressed, feedback is provided to the concerned person/ community. However, we continue to monitor our projects and interventions to ensure beneficiaries feel safe and satisfied with our interventions.
