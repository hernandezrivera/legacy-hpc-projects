Project Summary

In Borno State, displacement has also led to a sharp increase in the number of children forced to join insurgents and an increase in incidents of gender-based violence. Access to education for IDPs and other youths remains extremely poor due to a lack of structures (including learning spaces), lack of teaching and learning materials, and insufficient numbers of teachers. The proposed intervention is targeting 6000 conflict affected children to have access to safe inclusive gender transformation education services and aid delivered will protect all segments of the target population and will not put some at risk, especially girls. GHIV Africa ensure that gender will be significantly entrenched in this project, to guarantee a protective environments for girls, boys and CWD.
Furthermore, providing remote education activities through Radio lessons aligned basic and post literacy accelerated curriculum will increase access to education and continuity of education for vulnerable girls and boys including children with disability. Similarly, through family learning club approach, community learning will provide remedial learning opportunities to reduce barriers to education for at-risk children and their families.
Equally important is supporting the learning including training of teachers, learning facilitators, provision of learning and teaching materials as well as the wellbeing of students and teachers to ensure that affected children and youth are safe from abuse, exploitation, neglect, and violence; and are psychologically, emotionally, socially, cognitively, and physically safe.
The schools in North-East Nigeria need supportive school environments that can help students feel safe and protected to promote greater well-being and foster a sense of hope for the future and help build their resilience. Thus, providing pedagogical and psychosocial support skills which teachers can use to establish positive learning environments, such as creating predictability through classroom routines, co-creating rules, using positive discipline, meaningful classroom tasks, positive social relationship, inclusion, and effective referral systems for children to have access to other supports is very important.
GHIV Africa will support school-based management committees (SBMCs) through training and capacity building, with a special focus on improving participation in school management and the myriad key issues in protection, MHPSS, risk reduction and child safeguarding that have been identified as being prevalent in the communities and school.
Additionally, constructing and renovating classrooms and learning spaces, as well as provision of furniture and teaching and learning materials for alternative and formal education settings will respond to the very substantial challenges facing access to education posed by the limited available infrastructure.
, GHIV Africa proposed Education intervention in Bama,(Banki), ,Jere,, Ngala LGA of Borno State and in Yusufari, and Gujba of Yobe State . Access to appropriate education for Persons with Special Needs is the main thrust of inclusive education. Making appropriate education available to children and youth with Special Needs is one sure way of achieving access for inclusive education programmes.
Therefore, GHIV Africa shall – Provide concrete innovative interventions to displacement affected children with disabilities to improve their independence, dignity, and protection by providing them with basic education and life skills.

1 - Needs

According to the Education sector’s analysis on Education in the HNO 2022 for Northeast Nigeria, about 1.18 million children need access to basic quality education. Drawing from assessments which supported the development of the education sector strategy (Multi Year Strategic plan- MYRP) for the Northeast Nigeria 2021-2023. key findings reveal that poverty has been a major hindrance to education access. The assessment further reveals that dilapidated classrooms and low teacher to pupil’s ratio has reduced access to basic education. Also, lack of sufficient learning materials has adversely affected the quality of learning at school The Joint Education Needs Assessment (JENA, 2021). A summary of key findings, especially as they relate to priority needs identified for NHF Funding, is as follows:• 30% of schools were unable to enroll some children in the last school year 2020-2021. Of those enrolled only 21% attend school every day and around 25% come just around a quarter of the time.• Girls marginally outnumber boys at the early childhood school level through to JSS• Across the 3 states, one in four schools was sharing infrastructure, typically with schools from conflict-affected communities which had moved to safer or more accessible locations. This was particularly common in Bama, Dikwa, and Gwoza. The average number of students per classroom in BAY states is 124, very high, considering also that just 60% of teachers have some form of qualification and 14% are volunteers.For the innovation part of the proposal ( Disability); According to IOM 2021, Approximately 27% of vulnerable displaced people in Northeast Nigeria have a disability, a number that has increased by the insurgency. Persons with disabilities living in camps are disproportionately affected, often being excluded from activities and interventions. With a lack of wheelchairs or other mobility aids, and with some people having lost their caregivers, persons with disabilities face further barriers particularly in regard to mobility to places of distribution and the physical implications a distribution brings. Also, according finding from JENA, 2022, Having disabilities is a clear barrier to education and according to the KII feedback from the EiE NWG possible consequences to CWD out of school can lead to being exposed to begging and abuse. .Schools across northeast Nigeria are not typically adapted for children with disabilities with just 6% having adapted buildings for physical access and 5% ensuring transportation, where the major difficulty reported, is walking (mobility) in 40% of schools According to EiEWG Accountability to affected Population ( AAP ) in Education strategy and from the AAPIE SWOT Analysis conducted, the weakness identified was the lack of Specific strategy response for Accountability to Affected Population in Education, with particular note to Child Participation & Inclusion (AAPiE) not being in place. More so, from the mufti-sectoral needs assessment conducted by GHIV Africa in 2020, PWDs pointed out that, they have not been deliberately targeted as a group for any intervention in recent time which is why this innovation is an integral part of this interventionTherefore, GHIV Africa will be responding to the above needs hindering access to inclusive education in selected LGAsBorno and Yobe State

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GHIV Africa as a member of Network of Borno State women led organization (BONWOC) and also gender focal point of EiE, will ensure gender equality is fully integrated throughout the project, starting from targeted inclusive implementation processes to staff recruitment and the participation of both men and women in project structures and evaluation processes. Women and men will be given equal chances during project staff recruitment, with women highly encouraged to apply for positions. In addition, because of their important role in successful community mobilization, the project will deploy as many female workers as possible. While all program staff are accountable for project gender equality and safe programming goals, a Gender and psychosocial support Officer will work closely with staff members to ensure gender and child protection standards integration into interventions. Activity 1.3.13; Provide gender responsive and safeguarding social, & behavioral change communication activities at learning centres. Training/orienting all project staff, partners and stakeholders including project beneficiaries on the gender-related barriers, child protection and building their accountability on safeguarding standards to address issues and concerns on risk of harm will also take place. In collaboration with the government, awareness/capacity building of teachers, community volunteer teachers, SBMC, and CEC participating in the project will be raised around the effects of gender inequality, power dynamics harmful traditional practices that have a direct impact or hinder on access to education, especially girls and children with disabilities, as well as the differential experiences and effects of child protection and safeguarding issues on girls, boys and CWD through training. Activities proposed are specifically designed to better meet the needs of children of school age, disability status and gender groups. GHIV Africa follows the principle of “Do No Harm” by anchoring it on the connectors and dividers practices which will be considered in all of its activities, which requires the organization to take specific steps and measures to protect and reduce the risk of harm from all intervention processes and activities , such as participatory beneficiary selection, involvement of community members throughout the implementation, informing them about the intervention’s aims and activities, informed consent collection, conducting a risk assessment prior to engagement , as well as ensuring the existence of a functioning and responsive feedback and complaint mechanism at community level that allows timely action to address issues of concern that may cause or put beneficiaries at risk of harm, reputation risk or create conflict in the beneficiary communities. Moreover, identifying gender-differentiated opportunities and constraints for improving access to education during the design phase of a project often leads to better interventions. In line with this, and building on our ongoing experience, sensitization on gender, child protection and safeguarding concerns is included in any training and awareness raising activity, especially during the inception workshop. Other sensitization measures include the mandatory on boarding signature of the Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct by GHIV Africa staffs and associates, and the gender responsive safeguarding risk assessments conducted prior to engagement to mitigate all concerns of risk of harm.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GHIV Africa is committed to the principles of participation and downward accountability. GHIV Africa is focal point for AAP and child safeguarding for EiE in North-east Nigeria. GHIV Africa will uphold its accountability to the project beneficiaries by ensuring that they understand the procedures, decision-making processes and financial flows of the projects. GHIV Africa will sign MOU with the concerned government partner organizations (SUBEB, SAME)) and facilitate a project launching workshop with the participation of local authority, community and beneficiary representatives for them to better understand project objectives, procedures as well as their roles and responsibilities in the course of project implementation. During execution of project activities, coordination with other humanitarian actors will be emphasized to extend the number of services and to strengthen collaboration. GHIV Africa will be maintaining transparency and our participatory approach that will help to ensure decisions that affect the community are made in a socially responsible way and that groups, are not excluded from the benefits of activities. The project will be established complaints and feedback mechanism that utilizes different types of mechanisms to solicit feedback and complaints from different groups (including child-friendly mechanisms) will be utilized and strengthened for this project. Child friendly Mechanism will be done through listening to children, young people, persons with disabilities and communities using simple language and illustrations to communicate key messages about the feedback mechanism. Complaints or feedback and U- report are invited through the Community Volunteer teachers who will register the complaints/ feedback at the wards and community level. Also, direct feedback will be received through hotline and suggestion boxes in the communities. GHIV Africa is notified of every complaint and feedback, follows up and then closes the feedback loop.
