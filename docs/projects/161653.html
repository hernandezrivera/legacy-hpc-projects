Project Summary

CHAD aims to support over 18,000 vulnerable girls and boys and 2000 vulnerable caregivers of vulnerable boys and girls at risk of conflict related protection risks with the hope that their wellbeing is greatly improved while contributing towards strengthening their resilience. Case management services will be provided for boys and girls at risk of or exposed to exploitation, violence and neglect. Psychosocial support programming targeting vulnerable children and caregivers will be implemented including recreational and structured social activities targeting younger children, and a life skills education initiative targeting adolescents primarily. Children deprived of parental care or children living under abusive caregivers will benefit from family tracing and reunification services or alternative care.
Strengthening protective environments for boys and girls by building the capacity and knowledge base of caregivers will reflect an important part of CHAD's approach towards sustainable impact for children's well-being, development and protection against violence and abuse.
CHAD will continue to implement its Localization commitments with active and on-going contribution towards strengthening the role of the Borno State Case Management Taskforce which aims to improve the quality of case management targeting conflict-affected boys and girls.

1 - Needs

Child Protection:An estimated 1.8 million children and adolescents (0.96million girls and 0.84million boys) in north-east Nigeria are in need of child protection and specialized support services. Boys and girls who constitute over 63% of those in need of humanitarian assistance are at high risk of neglect, exploitation and violence. A look at the Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS) dashboard for Quarter 2 of 2019 revealed a total number of 2,519 conflict-affected children in the north-east reported to be at risk of at least one form of child protection concerns or more. A high proportion of these children are either unaccompanied or separated. Reunification rates by child protection partners remain very low due to limited resources or funding related challenges. Mental health and psychosocial support programming remains a critical component of the north-east humanitarian response by Child Protection partners. Life-skills education targeting adolescents and boys and girls is very much needed to support their resilience while dealing with the attendant hardship of being a witness to wanton destruction of life and properties and abrupt disruption of social support networks. Age- and gender-specific recreational, creative and specialized mental health support for children severely impacted by the crisis will be required to improve the general wellbeing and resilience of boys and girls.The ability of an estimated 110,700 parents and caregivers to care appropriately for children has been negatively impacted, putting children and families at risk of negative coping mechanisms including child marriage and dropping out of school. The situation of caregivers especially amongst internally displaced persons remain a concern with limited resources available to cater for their family and pursue livelihood opportunities to ensure availability of basic needs for their children. Analysis from the Child (CPSS Guidance note 2020)Overall response related to socio-economic reintegration of victims of grave rights violations have been very low with only 13% of target for socio-economic reintegration reached so far. There’s generally a very limited number of child protection organizations with a comprehensive programming on the socio-economic reintegration. Low technical capacity especially amongst local organizations have hampered meaningful progress in this area. In 2020 CHAD will aim to leverage on technical capacity building initiatives to foster programming in this area especially for CAFAAGs.

2 - Activities

Child Protection:1. Identification, assessment and service provision for boys and girls at risk of child protection concerns including case management for children at risk of violence, exploitation and neglect.2. Provision of alternative care and routine follow-ups for children deprived of parental care or children living under abusive caregivers.3. Family tracing and re-unification for unaccompanied and separated children4. Capacity building for socio-economic reintegration of survivors of grave rights violations including children formerly associated with armed groups.5. Community-based economic/livelihood reintegration support for girls, boys and young people formerly associated with armed groups and other vulnerable children and youth within the community.6. Community-based social reintegration support for girls, boys and young people formerly associated with armed groups and other vulnerable children and youth within the community7. Support to girls, boys and young people formerly associated with armed groups and other vulnerable children and youth within the community to access education.8. Educate children and community members on the grave violations against children with a focus on preventative actions.9. Structured and age-specific creative, recreational and social activities for children including adolescents.10. Mobile and age-specific creative, recreational and social activities for children including adolescents11. Life skills education for adolescent boys and girls.12. Positive parenting lessons for caregivers of vulnerable children or vulnerable caregivers.13. Raise awareness at community level on child protection with specific focus on relevant child protection issues within a given community
