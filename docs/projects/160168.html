Project Summary

Unreliable access to a temporary and/or inadequate WASH facility remain an important feature of the humanitarian landscape in Somalia. More than half of Somalis lack of access to adequate sanitation, with critical gaps in Lower Shabelle, Lower Juba, Gedo and Bakool and in most IDP settlements, resulting in widespread open defecation. Hand washing with soap is practiced by very few because of the limited availability of soap and functional handwashing points. In drought affected regions, water scarcity and inadequate quality are leading causes of displacement and conflict, particularly in Puntland and Somaliland, where women and children must often travel over 30 minutes to reach drinking water points. The heavy Gu rainfall observed in 2018 resulted in water supply infrastructure damage in more than 25 districts, particularly in southern and central Somalia. Most such infrastructure is yet to be repaired. As a consequence of recent disasters, population displacements and the chronic lack of investment in quality WASH infrastructure, Somalia continues to suffer from AWD/Cholera and risk factors for outbreaks persist in several hotspots, including main urban and sub-urban crowded settings.
Access to a sufficient number of quality WASH services in IDP settlements and host communities must be enhanced, both in crowded urban contexts and rural areas. Environmentally and functionally sustainable longer-term WASH solutions are required in historical drought and AWD/cholera hotspots, when feasible, to progressively improve existing water supply and sanitation systems. WASH needs in institutions such as schools and health care facilities must be addressed in emergency and chronically under-served locations. Water related disease outbreaks and sudden-onset disasters continue to require emergency temporary responses. A stronger protection lens must be applied when programming WASH responses, to reduce risk of violence against women, children and other vulnerable groups. Approximately 2.9 million people require urgent WASH support. IDP communities, host populations in rural areas at risk of drought and those living in districts prone to disasters and outbreaks of AWD/cholera are the most vulnerable. While most urban and sub-urban populations often have access to a sufficient quantity of water, it is often unsafe due to cross contamination between water and sanitation systems. Safety of people, particularly women and children to access latrines and water points remains a serious concern and a serious risk for exposure to violence.
Direct Aid an active WASH cluster in Gedo and Banadir Regions and having presence in Hiiraan,Lower Shabbelle,Banadir and Puntland deliverying free humanitrain services with its own fund is planning to respond WASH needs in Gedo and Banadir Regions the year 2020 in order to respond WASH critical needs in those areas.

Project Activities

Activity 1: Construction/Rehabilitation and protection of 10 wells, in order improve access for 2500 women, 1600 men, 3700 girls and 1200 boys. To improve the quality of water,DA will chlorinate and monitor residual chlorine levels (0.2mg/l -0.5 mg/l) of water at the rehabilitated water points. DA will also provide emergency household water treatment chemicals/or devices (including bucket chlorination).• Activity 2: Construction/Rehabilitation of 3 water Kiosks in 3 IDP camps in Tuula Aamiin, Camp Ajuuraan and Bacaadweyn IDP camps in Belethawa districts to improve access to safe water. Where absent,DA is proposing installation of running water at 3 target health facilities and 3 schools by connecting the nearest strategic borehole. This activity will target 30,000 beneficairies including boys,girls,men and women• Activity 3: Improve environmental sanitation through construction of 10 solid waste disposal sites in selected IDP settlements and other environmental cleaning and management activities including drainage and/or removal of stagnant black/grey water ponds in Mogadishu.• Activity 4: Improve sanitation coverage through construction of 100 (30 Belethawa and 70 Mogaishu) gender-segregated communal latrines. The latrines will be designed to allow future desludging access, ready accessibility and hand washing facilities. DA will consult all anticipated users, particularly vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, the disabled and elderly), on siting, design, & appropriateness of the latrines to ensure the facilities allow for rapid, private, safe (well-lit and lockable) and secure access for all users. • Activity 5: Rehabilitation of 50 sex disaggregated schools (20 Belethawa and 30 Mogadishu as per SPHERE and WASH cluster standards.• Activity 6: Establishment, training and ongoing supportive supervision of Solid Waste Management Volunteers (50 male & 10 females) 20 for each IDP camp including host community members. (Targeted IDP camps, are Tuula-Aamiin,Camp ajuran and Bacaadweyn IDP CHPs, WASH committees and waste management volunteers will coordinate with local authorities to lead community members in clean-up campaigns aimed at addressing any accumulated solid waste or stagnant water that serve as a breeding ground for disease vectors.• Activity 7: Establishment, training and ongoing supportive supervision of 20 gender balanced WASH committees (10 male & 10 female• Activity 8: Conduct hygiene promotion for 9,000 beneficiaries through house to house visits (average 8 -10 households per week/per CHP), IEC materials, FM radio messaging and during participatory community outreach activities,• Activity 9: Provision of health/education to improve WASH in institutions, including DA operated cholera treatment centers, nutrition facilities and primary health care facilities. • Activity 10: Distribution of 1500 Hygiene kits with accompanying messaging on.DA will regularly assess needs for hygiene supplies (aqua tabs, 20L water storage containers, soap, & menstrual hygiene items) among the target population; and address these through distribution of items that will help ensure men, women, and children have the materials to act on the hygiene education.
