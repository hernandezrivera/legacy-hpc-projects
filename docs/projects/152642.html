Project Summary

This project will support the delivery of preventative and therapeutic nutrition services for 6,391 boys and 5,899 girls 3,550 PLWs in Old fangak, Wangchot, Chotbora, Nyatuat, Dhonor, Tock, Wanglel (Fangak); Amiel, Pajut, Dongchak and Pagaak (Duk); Mayen, Pajariei, Wutliet (Islands of Bor South) during 2019. These will be supported through fixed sites, and outreach models is a robust referrals chain with other sector players, interventions will be complemented by preparedness capacity to scale-up in the event of the likely case-load up surges in 2019 hunger gap and or due to returnees, and additional internal displacement in Duk (due to cattle raid attacks) and impacts of flooding including Cholera especially in the Islands of Bor South.
This intervention is based on the existing Hold the Child presence in Bor (Education), Duk (Health interventions) and Fangak (Nutrition and Health interventions), Consistence coordination through the cluster and existing Agreements with Cluster core supply pipelines (WFP and UNICEF).

Needs

The impact of the protracted national crisis in the areas of Jonglei can not be over stated; Fangak county (Old state system) hosts 26,200 IDP (OCHA sept 2018 reports), while Duk county hosts 37,689 and Bor south 32,527. According to the recent Humanitarian needs analysis, about 82,971 (23,387 male & 22,996 female), 65,341 (18,442 male & 18,149 female), and 78,229 (22,079 male & 21,729 female) in Fangak, Duk and Bor South respectively some of the highest in Jonglei state. The recent IPC analysis (Sept 2018) indicate worrying classification phase with IPC phase 4 (emergency) for Fangak, IPC 3 (Crisis) for Duk and likely to worsen if no humanitarian support step-up, while Bor South falls in IPC 3 (Crisis). Where as Duk presents with a GAM rate of 9.4 (Nutrition cluster. Sept 2018), Fangak shows a GAM rate of 16.1 (ACF May 2018 SMART report) way above the WHO threshold values, these report further indicated Crude death rates for U5s at 0.5 in Duk and 0.7 in Fangak out of 10,000/day. The above trends are attributed to: [1] Limited resources to provide routine health care i.e. staffing and intermitted supplies movements etc., RRHP 2018 reports (Jonglei and UpperNile states) a shortfall in development funding which might even decline in 2019 there predisposing more children (boy and girls) to illnesses that undermine their nutrition status. [2] Access constraints to in; Fangak water streams and Bor Islands are cut off by water weeds, while Duk is cut by seasonal roads from Panyagoor and Bor town; these affect free trade and access to goods and services and as a result people go without food (basic nutrients), health care etc. if they can afford the exorbitant market prices. This is evidenced by the limited coverage of services; No fixed feeding centres and health care services in Islands of Jalle, Baidit and Kolngang which account for 47,743 people with 10,025 children U5s (Local authorities), Delayed deliveries of Nutrition supplies to Toch in Old fangak; Scarcity of food products in the markets of Fangak and Duk which exacerbate the food security situation. [3] Displacements due to intermittent insecurities in Duk due to Murle attacks and raids. [4] Seasonal floods characterised with poor harvest and tendencies for water borne illnesses; Fangak, Duk and Bor South South communities are among the Cholera prone counties, Fangak presented with 7 cases in week 39 this year (Weekly 39 cholera report WHO). [5] Poor hygiene and sanitation practices coupled with low treatment seeking behaviour characteristic of ignorance. The trends are expected to rise during 2019 with the anticipated returnees from Unity state, UpperNile, Ayod and Juba PoC to Fangak, or transit through Fangak and Bor South Islands; Returnees from East Africa to Bor South and Duk. These will further overstretch the limited nutrition services where available and more predispose boys and girls to Malnutrition AWD and Respiratory infections and associated morbidities if no interventions are done particularly for areas including Amiel, Pajut, Dongchak and Pagaak (Duk county); Mayen, Pajariei, Wutliet (Islands of Bor South) and if the running nutrition services in Old fangak, Toch, Wangchot and Chotbora among other Hold the Child run feeding centres are not support through 2019. Hold the Child program data indicate a differential vulnerability between boys and girls i.e. June 2018 MAM reports 1,034 admissions with 48% as girls from the 7 supported sites in Fangak.

Activities

• Provide training for health and nutrition care providers on management of acute malnutrition and MIYCN based on the updated CMAM and MIYCN guidelines.• Provide services for the treatment of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) for boys and girls 6-59 months based on the CMAM guidelines and Sphere standards; supplementary feeding centres will be through both static and outreach/mobile sites based on the local operational context, this will have linkages to BSFP and FLS. • Provide services for the treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) for boys and girls 6-59 months based on the CMAM guidelines and Sphere standards; therapeutic feeding centres will be through both static and outreach/mobile sites based on the local operational context, this will too include referrals for TSPF for MAM treatment. • Conduct regular rapid assessments with MUAC across the target Vulnerable communities through static and outreach mechanisms• Provide of quality MIYCN and micronutrient supplementation services in nutrition sites and community level• Ensure functionality hand washing installations and availability of drinking water at all supported feeding sites so as to promote optimal hygiene and sanitary practices.• Conduct health education on hygiene and health promotion sessions at all feeding sites. • Provide testing and treatment of malaria to SAM children aged 0/6-59 months and women identified with uncomplicated malaria attending Nutrition services, and refer of cases of severe malaria for further care.• Integrate Child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM at feeding sites with play materials for children as adults go through the routine heath education etc. Hold the Child will work along with CFS to strengthen active screening for malnutrition • Conduct safety audit/assessment at nutrition sites to identify and address women and children’s protection/safety needs.• Establish kitchen garden at nutrition sites for demonstration.• Conduct Post Harvest nutrition SMART survey in Fangak county• Train partners and County health department staff on NIS and utilization of nutrition information for early warning and decision making at State level• Support training on data collection and supervision of FSNMS

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Through avenues including; project inception sessions with local leaders and stakeholders (including male and females), Key messaging IEC materials, awareness sessions during outreaches, community nutrition workers/volunteers and local gatherings like churches, and health education sessions.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages provided seek to (1) Paint a clear understanding of the project scope and service to set the right expectations in cognisance of the local contextual dynamics, (2) Convey key messages on behavioural changes on hygiene & sanitation, child feeding, immunisation, ANC etc. This therefore makes our messages contextual with tailored cluster generic phrases.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Our rights based programming recognises Sexual exploitation and abuse as critical issue across the country. In conformity with our relevant policy, this project will integrate recommended measures to Provide optimal awareness about this evil in our workstations and feedings sites; and ensure compliance to PSEA reporting channels while providing referrals for victims.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

This project is designed with inputs from prior consultations through the ongoing project activities. Such a spirit will be maintained into the new project cycle 2019, and will include measures like: Consultations during project inception sessions, Courtesy calls to local authorities and County health departments and State Ministry of Health offices, Focus group discussion with beneficiaries (including male and females), Donor agencies interactions, Cluster coordination and technical working group session, and Hold the Child’s internal consultations and reflection sessions.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Like our ongoing projects, this project will integrate feedback and complaint mechanisms including: suggestion box (at the bases), one on one, focus group discussions (including male and females) among projects sites and catchments.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through the use of community-based structures; the targeted communities (women, children, and other vulnerable and marginalised populations) will be engaged in assessments, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of project activities.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project design is based identified needs among target populations attributed to the national crisis, and lifesaving interventions designed to meet the immediate needs of the people.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project will seek to operationalise feeding centers that are closed or otherwise be closed due to lack of qualified staff, supplies and equipment; through fixed or outreach sites/posts. This action will extend access opportunities to such vulnerable boys and girls and pregnant lactating women that would otherwise be left out. Furthermore the project will advocate for support to rapid responses to fresh IDPs that would otherwise be cut off in hard to reach areas. All nutrition services will be free to all people in the catchment areas supported under this project.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project design will address inequalities in limited ways with particular focus on equitable access to basic services for vulnerable boys and girls and pregnant lactating women within the catchment areas regardless of there ethnicity, disability and creed. Men and Youths and Elderly will too be involved in MIYCN initiatives

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All project interventions are set to provide services for all as hinted above, and thus in ways contribute to addressing inequalities among the targeted communities.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Project interventions are to provide critical nutrition services to address the identified needs for vulnerable populations among the target areas. Operations will involve beneficiaries taking services from supported feeding sites and data captured in based on these interactions. Project data will capture beneficiaries accessing a feeding sites from a given catchments and reports will seek to analysis the extents of reach Vs targeted beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project includes activities such as safety audit/assessment at nutrition sites to identify and address women and children’s protection/safety needs

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Our rights based programing recognises the dignity of beneficiaries to be the essence of our intervention among this project targeted areas. And to this end, the project design carries integrated accountability measure to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their aspirations during the project cycle. The project focus groups and some consultations sessions will deliberately engage different social grouping to provide meaningful engagements on matters that within the project scope that have different impact on their well-being; to inform project design refinement, referrals and future programming.
