Project Summary

According to HNO 2020, an estimated 709,000 new displacements were reported from January to December 2019, mostly to urban areas, where displaced people move to private IDP sites with poor living conditions and little access to basic services. There are over 2,000 recorded IDP sites across Somalia. Approximately 85 percent of the sites are informal settlements on private land and about 74 percent of them are in urban areas according to the Detailed Site Assessment (DSA). According to the DSA, over 70 percent of IDPs in informal sites lack access to information about their rights and available services. Displaced communities, especially new arrivals, people with disabilities and women need access to information and representation in the community governance structure to make informed decisions about how to receive services and potential solutions to displacement. This year (2020), Somalia has seen serious flooding which have forced hundreds of thousands of people to flee their homes amid risks of COVID-19. According to UNHCR more than 150,000 Somalis have been forced to flee their homes since late June, including some 23,000 in the last week of July, due to flash and riverine flooding in the Southern regions of Somalia. As of 19 May (OCHA), ongoing flash and riverine flooding in Somalia has affected about 919,000 people, of whom 412,000 have been displaced and 24 killed, in 29 districts.
The displaced people in informal sites in Banadir, Lower Shebelle, Lower Jubba, and Gedo Region are in greatest need of CCCM services to improve the living conditions and protection of IDPs at the site level. The target areas have been through successive years of drought and conflict, leaving the population vulnerable to shocks and inhibiting a sustainable return to places of origin. The displaced population in these areas are living in precarious conditions with their basic needs not met due to inconsistent service provision and exclusion with all existing coordination and management mechanisms at site level being informal, with most sites managed by landlords and gatekeepers limiting accountability and adherence to minimum standards. While ensuring equitable access to services and assistance of all persons in need, NoFYL will ensure to enhance the accountability of affected populations through community participation and consistent engagement with a focus on moving toward attaining durable solutions.
NoFYL intends to scale up its CCCM program to contribute towards strengthening camp coordination and camp management to improve the living conditions and protection of IDPs at the site level by ensuring equitable access to services and assistance of all persons in need. All the project activities will be carried out to maximize community participation and enhance the accountability of humanitarian actors to the affected population. NoFYL will coordinate with relevant responders and mobilized towards providing sectorial assistance to those identified as very vulnerable at the site level. Together with the established site governance, NoFYL will carry out service monitoring activities aimed at highlighting service gaps, and ensuring that minimum standards are being adhered to at the site-level. To ensure that sites are safe and hygiene conditions are ensured NoFYL will provide training on-site maintenance and site planning to camp committees to strengthen their capacity, Support community-led site maintenance activities to ensure the upkeep of sites through cash for work activities, distribution of tools to site maintenance committees. The project will also support maintaining inter-sector complaints feedback mechanisms (CFMs) at the IDP site-level.
The project will be implemented in 4 regions, including Banaadir, Gedo region covering Beletxaawo and Ceelwaaq districts, and Lower Juba covering Kismayo. The project will target 60 sites in total targeting an estimated IDP population of 64,000 to be verified through the detailed site assessments.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1.1: Establish/update service mapping of partners operating in sites - Mapping exercise will be conducted to find out the existing resources/infrastructures in the targeted settlements to identify gaps and inform responses, which will be shared with CCCM cluster and will be used by different humanitarian partners for data collection and their response analysis. This will enhance the strategic thinking of all stakeholders in terms of needs and gaps in a particular settlement. Through these mapping exercises, it will be easy for all stakeholders to plan their activities related to a particular settlement. Activity 1.1.2: Maintain CCCM coordination structures in collaboration with appropriate government counterparts, and conduct coordination meetings during the entire project period, which will include partners working in the same area. The purpose of these coordination meetings is to avoid any duplication, sharing information, and referral mechanisms. Activity 1.1.3: Site Verification exercise covering all sites in the target locations will be conducted with support from other humanitarian partners and the local authorities to provide multi-sector information on access and availability to basic services to ascertain gaps in service provision and monitor services being provided, the verification will be conducted by NoFYL's multi-functional mobile teams, this will help to produce detailed information on the population of each site, its perimeter, access and services available in sites. NoFYL will use this data and contribute to its dissemination to inform humanitarian response at each level. The site verification will also inform the selection process of the target camps.Activity 1.1.4: NoFYL will support the involvement of the community through the local structures, and the representation of IDP leaders and vulnerable groups in Site activities. NoFYL will work to establish effective and representative Site governance and for the promotion of IDPs’ participation in decision-making and the daily life of the Site, including the development of Site rules. NoFYL believes that well-functioning Site environments depend upon the direct and indirect participation of the IDP in addition to the humanitarian partners and other stakeholders. In addition to gender representation, minority groups, people with special needs will be assured equal representation. Community leaders and representatives that are selected will be given clear functions and written, agreed-upon roles and responsibilities that will be utilized as a possible entry point for other humanitarian partner’s activities that require community consultation. Activity 1.1.5: Settlement planning training will be conducted for the existing governance structures and focal points for sites this will target to equip them with skills that will facilitate participatory management and maintenance of public spaces. Activity 1.1.6: Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms are operational within target locations- With a continued commitment to enhancing accountability to the site population, NoFYL will establish CFM structures in the target communities– whereby site residents can easily access the opportunity to raise concerns or complaints related to any sector within the site. Complaints are collected and registered by trained staff at the NoFYL information desks. The importance is placed on confidentiality particularly in the instance of complaints arising with potential Protection concerns. 1.1.7: Support community-led site maintenance activities to ensure the upkeep of sites through cash for work activities, site maintenance led by the site maintenance committees in the target locations, the conditional cash for work, beneficiaries who will support in site maintenance through digging drainage systems, pit-holes for garbage collection, bush clearing and clear pathways.

Mitigation Measures

1- Preventing risks of beneficiaries not being informed. Develop and widely disseminate the referral networks and ensuring service mapping are regularly conducted and referral networks updated. Enhanced community outreach and awareness campaigns around access and availability of services. Continuously assess and closely monitor the evolvement of the security and accessibility situation in the target areas, and have the flexibility to quickly adapt the implementation methodology as needed in order to guarantee the achievement of the project objective and the most effective impact. In this regard, NoFYL will maintain regular information flow and constantly consult with the cluster leads and donor. On the staff skills continuously train, coach and follow up the frontline workers to ensure timely and quality services is provided to beneficiaries. 2- Preventing risks of exclusion based on disability, gender and age. The proposed intervention has been designed in a way to enable beneficiaries to fully access and enjoy their rights, safety and dignity, while at the same time avoiding harm. All project activities will be carried out in accordance with AGDM principles: equal participation of women, girls, boys and men will be integral part of the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the overall project. NoFYL will ensure that key cross-cutting issues are mainstreamed into the proposed intervention including age, gender, diversity, environment and human rights. Gender will remain a key focus, guaranteeing consultation with female beneficiaries when undertaking protection assessments through NoFYL female staff accessing them and identifying their needs. 3- Preventing risks of community not using available complaints and feedback mechanisms. Ensure all beneficiaries are informed of the available complaints and feedback channels. Ensure CFM mechanisms are easy to use and accessible. Ensure complaints and feedback provided are acted upon and feedback provided to the community.

Please provide further explanation

Sustainability of the project will be ensured through community ownership of the project. For all activities reaching the communities, IDP community leaders and host community leaders, including women and youth leaders will be consulted in order to adapt the activity to the context and ensured community participation, Where possible community members will be encourage to volunteer and participate in the project. Collaboration with both host community and IDPs will ensure the project adhere to do no harm principles. Using community participation leverage will ensure a longer term impact of the project. Establishment of community structures and its capacity building aims at ensuring the community focal persons have capacity to continue support referrals from the communities to other service providers beyond the project durations, established service mapping will also provide a long term network between the communities and service providers thus ensuring sustainability.
