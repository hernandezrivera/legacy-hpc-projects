Project Summary


Gender based Violence (GBV) remains a key protection concern in Somalia, predominantly affecting Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) (GBV strategy report )According to the GBV Information Management System (IMS) trend analysis, from January to June 2020) 4,424 GBV cases reported, with 60% (2,654) occurring in south central Somalia.Out of this, 33% (1,460) cases were incidences of rape. Out of the GBV cases reported, 78% (3,451) of affected were IDPs. The situation has been made worse by the Covid 19 whereby parents and children have been forced to stay at home due to restricted movements and schools closure. There have been increased GBV cases including, rape, FGM and early marriages.
IDPs vulnerability increases the likelihood of them experiencing GBV. Somalia is a challenging environment for the protection and especially for people living with disability. Support of GBV survivors due to cultural sensitivity, fear of stigmatization and reprisals
. Hence, access to service providers is limited for GBV survivors. People with disability are the most vulnerable and are at risk of exposure to GBV. GBV remains significantly under reported and undocumented. A legitimate concern is the lack of prosecution of the GBV perpetrators which has contributed to a climate of impunity. This is due a weakened rule of law and lack of legislation. The main protection risks to GBV survivors are: limited access to health services, lack of information and insecurity in the camps
The proposed project is aimed at strengthening and improving protection activities in Kismayo, Baidoa and Afmadow Districts. The proposed project aims at one provide timely GBV awareness, effective and quality response. The project will also aim at strengthening case management and referral services of GBV and unaccompanied and separated children. This includes providing training and mentoring to the GBV and CP staffs of WRRS. The project will conduct community safety audits in the target areas of Afmadow, Kismayo and Baidoa, Complaint and feedback mechanism will be established in the three locations whereby beneficiaries will be able to access, even those living with disabilities.
The project provides immediate assistance to vulnerable populations. GBV survivors will receive case management and psycho-social support (PSS), including emergency care, referral to medical teams trained in case management of rape (CMR), tailored material assistance, basic emotional support, and follow up on the services will be provided. Capacity building with formal and informal authorities will be strengthened and improved. Further, the intervention provides training and technical support for health workers engaged in CMR, as well as for additional health and protection staff, in order to equip participants with the skills to provide compassionate, confidential, and competent clinical care to survivors of GBV. Persons with disabilities will be prioritized, and especially for children living in the streets with disabilities. The project will also provide material support to the victims of GBV, GBV survivors will be provided with dignity kits, street solar lights will be installed in the most congested IDP sites to enhance protection of women and girls.
The proposed project also aims to protect child rights and safety, prevent critical deterioration of their physical and mental health and promote their short and longer-term well being. The activities to achieve the above includes: Recreational activities/Counseling and medical child support, case workers capacity building, material support to enhance provision and access to essential services for child survivors, Prevention and Response services for unaccompanied and Separated Children(IDTR ). awareness and community engagement, supporting coordination, CP assessment and Monitoring of CP situation in settlements.

Project Activities

1. Case management and psycho social support for 250 GBV survivors (women and girls) provided by 2 GBV case workers (specifically focusing on child sexual abuse. Technical support sessions on case management and PSS (for 25 persons including GBV and CP WG members). Provided by GBV prevention & response officer and case workers.2. To support case management process and the most vulnerable GBV survivors and CP cases tailored material assistance to 153 GBV survivors ( women and girls), dignity kits for 123 rape and sexual assault survivors and vulnerable IDPs and referral support (transportation costs to health centers for 100 survivors) will be provided.3. Case workers will provide accompaniment and transportation support for survivors to access health facilities. The activity aims at providing continued technical support in-line with WHO CMR guidelines and provide orientation on GBV guiding principles and referral for health facility and outreach staff for 20 front-line service providers (including CHWs & CNWs).4. Community-led GBV awareness campaign to improve access to response services will be supported by the community mobilization officer. This will include initial identification and training on GBV core concepts, safe referral, and basic emotional support and community mobilization methods for 24 community volunteer focal points in each target location. The awareness campaign will utilize community awareness resources developed by the cluster on referral and service availability (inter-active GBV mainstreaming curriculum components and GBV/CP IEC flip books) targeting also community education committees (CECs). At least one awareness sessions per month will be supported, in addition to general peer-to-peer awareness. 5. Community Mobilization Coordinator will conduct 24 awareness sessions (1285 persons) for clan elders, imams, community leaders, community police (madani), CEC and CHW, formal and non-formal authorities (50% women and 50% men) on CP basics, GBV causes and consequences, services available, importance of timely reporting of cases, GBV guiding principles and referral (in at least 50% of target villages, depending on the security situation). Out of the people targeted 10% will be people living with disabilities.6. Education facilities are a key site for identification of vulnerable children requiring psycho social support. Therefore, 25 teachers (12 men and 13 female) children living with disabilities will be prioritized will be trained on GBV awareness, and psycho social support for children and safe and compassionate referral. In order to strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors to mainstream GBV and CP in emergency response based on the updated IASC guidelines (particularly from Health, WASH and Education clusters) WRRS will conduct 1 training in potential displacement area.7. Identification of GBV survivors including those living with disabilities to benefit from dignity kits distributed8. Conduct Community safety audits in the project target location9, Establishment of community feedback and complaint mechanism that will target all the community members including those living with disabilities.10.Recreational activities in camps/ psycho social support. The children will as well receive counseling therapy from the counselor to overcome trauma and adopt to child friendly environment. 300 Children to be benefit from the counseling services.11.Training of Case Workers on IDTR, case management for GBV, clinical management of rape. WRRS in coordination with the Child Protection sub cluster to conduct Case workers training on IDTR, case management for GBV, clinical management of rape targeting 20 (T=20) persons. 12.Setting up of CP Desks in Camps. Child attendants will be standby for children by guiding and advising the children on what to do to ensure their safety and rights. The children in camps will be engaged in recreational activities.13.Material support such as food for child survivors

Mitigation Measures

Women will be given priority and especially those from the marginalized clans. Persons living with disability will also be included in the project activities as in many instances they have been left out. The elderly will also be part of the key decision making of this project.Somalia has over the years reported cases of violence during distribution of aid/relief to mitigate this risk WRRS will ensure that the distribution sites for NFIs and ESKs are located in a well secured area and that security is provided to avert any incidences of Violence or GBV within the areas of distribution. The location will be selected carefully to ensure that beneficiaries are not exposed to any form of insecurity. The distribution centers will also be located near the IDP and Host community sites where there is an open space and that the security is not a threat to any beneficiary.The organization is well versed with the risks associated with SEA and therefore will work closely with all the stakeholders to ensure that the beneficiaries are not exposed to any form of sexual exploitation and especially during the delivery of project activities. Awareness raising on how to report the incidences of SEA will be done to all the organization stakeholders that is the staff, contractors and other stakeholders involved with the activities of the organization. A reporting mechanism for PSEA will be established to look into any issues related to Sexual exploitation during and even after the end of the project.WRRS will work with other partners in the area of operation where necessary to ensure that there is a proper referral of beneficiaries to services and especially the victims of Gender based violence and reported cases of child abuse.WRRS will ensure that all groups of beneficiaries are involved from the onset of projects, normally in Somalia people from marginalized clans and women have been left out in key decision making process and mostly they are not consulted. This is not going to happen for this project as WRRS will ensure that all the minority and marginalized persons will be involved in major decision making of the project. WRRS will establish a complaint and feedback mechanism that will be accessible to all the beneficiaries in the project target area. WRRS will create awareness on the same to ensure that all the beneficiaries are well informed of the availability and use of the complaint feedback mechanism. A hot line will be established that will be accessible for all, a help desk will also be put in place for beneficiaries who have no access to a mobile phone can access the help desk. Confidentiality will be maintained in the CFM for beneficiary’s protection in instances of sensitive matters.WRRS will work closely with the land owners and the local government in instances where there will be land disputes as Somalia is well known of this kind of disputes. The organization will work with the authorities and more especially in areas where construction is anticipated to secure land tenure.In areas where WRRS work cases of explosives are rare although measures will be put in place to prevent the risk of explosive hazards in the target area.
