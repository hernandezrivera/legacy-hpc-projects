Project Summary

In order to address the identified needs, AN plans an intervention aiming at providing assistance to medical institutions of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts located in close proximity to the line of contact. The intervention will aim at improving water-related and sanitary conditions of the abovementioned institutions. This will be achieved through: Ensuring immediate and sustainable access to sufficient safe water, and minimal levels of sanitation provision, for conflict-affected people.
• creating safe access to adequate water
• creating a sewage system in medical institutions such as First Aid Points (FAPs/ambulatories);
• rehabilitation of toilets in public institutions (FAPs/ambulatories and hospitals);
• rehabilitating the existing sewage systems in medical institutions (FAPs/ambulatories);
• cleaning of cesspits in public institutions;
• installation of septic tanks;

1 - Needs

Access to adequate water, an internationally recognized human right, is a precondition for the adequate standard of living and for the right to the highest attainable standard of health. Despite that, in the fifth year of the ongoing conflict, access to safe water remains a challenge, both in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts of Ukraine. This can be put down to numerous factors:• the ageing of water installations which require repairs and which have been further damaged due to the conduct of hostilities; • decreased functionality of essential water pumps due to power shortages caused by the shelling of electrical systems; • access issues, either due to the presence of armed groups, landmines or unexploded ordnances coupled with the poor condition of the roads which hampers residents from accessing wells or from receiving water supply by trucks; • access issues impeding workers from conducting repair works or from receiving spare parts needed for the repair of the water installations; • frequent water cuts which have contributed to the worsening of the situation. Similarly, to the situation related to the access of water, the sanitary situation in the region demands proper assistance. Sewage networks and wastewater treatment facilities (often 70 years old), are breaking down, which creates a risk of water contamination. Close to the LoC (within 5 km from the frontline) 56% of people have a toilet connected to a sewer network, among which 18% have a septic tank and 32% have an outside toilet (this figure rises up to 69% in rural areas). Lack of safe access to adequate water and poor sanitary conditions can be also observed on the institutional level. Schools and medical institutions (FAPs, ambulatories and hospitals) located close to the LoC are of particular concern.Water-related problems often create risk of spreading water and sanitation-related diseases. Little if any reliable data on medical institutions located close to the contact line collected by other international agencies is currently available, which is especially true for FAPs and ambulatories. They seem not to be the primary focus of INGOs and other international agencies. Knowing about the needs, AN has conducted a preliminary needs assessment focused on FAPs and ambulatories which aimed at assessing basic needs of such facilities in terms of WASH and Shelter. The collected data covered the institutions located in two raions of Luhansk oblast: Novaydarskyi and Stanychno-Luhanskyi as well as two located in Donetsk oblast: Volnovakhskyi and Kostiantynivskyi.FAPs in Kostiantynivskyi raion have better conditions (access to water, sewage system and buildings) in comparison to FAPs in other three analyzed raions. Particularly, FAPs of other three raions have limited access to water supply system or do not have centralized or local sewage systems. For instance, analysis of 53 FAPs (Stanychno-Luhanskyi, Novaydarskyi and Volnovakhskyi rayons) shows that 39.4% of them (21) do not have any access to water or have very limited access. 13.2% FAPs (7) do not have toilets (even outdoor toilets), which causes the situation in which FAPs’ staff have to go home or to neighbour-households to use toilets. 49.1% of the analyzed FAPs (26) have only outside toilets. In total in all three raions, 64.2% FAP (34) use outside toilets – which means that in more than half of FAPs there are very primitive unlined pits constructed under toilets, which might be sources of hazards for local population and/or environment. For all four raions, 34.8% of FAPs (24) do not have access to water and 14.5% of FAPs (10) do not have neither indoor nor outdoor toilets. 43.5% of FAPs (30) have only outdoor toilets and, in total, over half of them (55.1%; 38 FAPs) use outdoor toilets. About 29% of FAPs have protected sludge storage in addition to 55.1% of FAPs which use unlined pits / soakaways. The preliminary analysis identified that some sewage holes were constructed too close to FAP buildings.
