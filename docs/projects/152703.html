Project Summary

The overall objective of the proposed project is to provide timely, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services to 48,500 people affected by conflict and drought in Yei, Terekeka, Kapoeta east and Tonj east counties. The project is expected to provide Water, sanitation and hygiene temporal and sustainable services that will address the vital needs and public health and protection issues using AAP community approach to the targeted vulnerable women, children, men, elderly and disabled people affected by drought and conflict. The intervention will further enhance community resilience by addressing the main drivers of vulnerability in the targeted areas of Yei, Kapoeta east, Terekeka and Tonj east.
The project will seek to improve and increase timely access to equitable safe water and sanitation and hygiene services through construction of new water points (boreholes ) , rehabilitation of existing water and sanitation facilities in both the community and health facility set ups . 17 new boreholes are expected to be drilled, 50 existing but defunct boreholes are expected to be rehabilitated and restore clean water supply to the targeted communities. The project is expected to target the most vulnerable households with WASH NFIs these include water storage and collection containers , washing basins , washing soaps to 3,000 Households, while 3,000 girls of adolescent age and women of child bearing age will benefit from women menstrual management kits , this is to improve the health and protect the dignity of women .
The project is expected to enhance behavioral change towards better hygiene practices, different groups will be targeted with hygiene specific message, schools, nutrition and health centers will be targeted to increase their knowledge on hygiene. The project is expected to build resilience of the affected people, selected members from the community will be trained as members of water management committee with 40% women represented, and capacity of selected members of the affected people will be enhanced to carry out hygiene promotion activities and campaigns in the targeted areas. The project is expected to build and strengthen the capacity of local community members as village hand pump mechanics with specific roles of repairing and maintaining the community water supply systems in Yei, Kapoeta east, Tonj east and Terekeka.
The intervention is expected to reinforce protection of the various groups affected by the conflict during service delivery by so doing, IRSS will conduct protection risk analysis and the intervention will be based on understanding the local context in terms of socio-cultural dynamics to mitigate all risks to various groups (women , girls, and children) of the affected group. Awareness on GBV and sexual exploitation will be conducted to raise awareness and inform the affected community of all forms of GBV and sexual exploitation practices and action that might affect the people and be able to mitigate.
The project will ultimately contribute to saving, improvement, protection and recognition of dignity of the affected people and ultimately reduce their vulnerability and enhance their resilience.

Needs

Across the country, a total of 5.7 million South Sudanese people will require water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) support in 2019, an increase from 2018. The needs are closely related to the conflict, internal displacement and the economic downturn that is now in its fifth year, which have all put a further strain on already limited WASH services. 90 per cent of the population do not have access to improved sanitation or are not accustomed to using basic sanitation facility and therefore practice open defecation. Only 13 per cent of the population has access to WASH NFIs, such as jerry cans, soap and mosquito nets. High malnutrition rates are also linked to poor WASH conditions, as exemplified in the diarrhoea-malnutrition cycle prevalent in vulnerable populations. Women and girls face increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water and using communal latrines, and access to menstrual hygiene products, and appropriate and dignified washing locations remains their key need.Islamic relief carried out assessments in the target counties of Yei, Kapoeta east, terekeka and Tonj East. In Yei, 42 boreholes were not functional due to a lack of spare parts and trained pump mechanics in the five Bomas of Jordan, Sobe, Mahad, Jansuk and Jigomoni (IRSS, Aug 2018). However, the situation is expected to be much worse in the surrounding payams due to limited access as a result of the conflict. Public latrines are only available in institutions such as churches, schools, and health facilities, with most in need of rehabilitation as some lack doors, locks, and the slabs have large cracks. This was of serious concern, particularly to women, as they stated that they found them very shameful to use. Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) is a challenge, as women have limited access to funds for sanitary pads and resort to clothes. In Kapoeta east, communal fighting mainly over pasture and water in July 2018 among the cattle keepers resulted in loss of lives and displacement of about 3,000 vulnerable people into Kasongor. The drought situation in most of the county has led to increased level of migration in search of water and hence heightened conflict among neighbouring communities. IR assessment findings also identified gaps in the WASH services in the three target counties of Kapeata east, Tonj east and Terekeka. In Tonj east up to 58 % of all the boreholes were non-functional, while 52% of boreholes were not functional in Terekeka and 75% in Jie payam of Kapoeta east were also non-functional. The main problem being lack of capacity and spare parts to maintain the boreholes by the local authorities and communities. This increases the burden of water collection and transport for women and girls, which also leads to protection concerns such as rape and sexual exploitation & abuse (SEA).The assessments also indicates very low access to sanitation services, between 3%-10% of the population in Tonj east, Terekeka and Jie have access to good sanitation at house hold levels while the rest 70% practices open defecation. Parts of Tonj east in Makuac boma, Terekeka in Tombek boma and Kapoeta east in Napotpot boma have been prone to Cholera outbreaks which is an indication of the poor sanitation and hygiene conditions. IRSS assessment findings indicated between 60%-90% of the people in Kapoeta east , Tonj east and Terekeka do not practice hand washing and do not have hand washing facilities in their homes, while 48% of people in Yei town said they consistently observe the key hand washing times.

Activities

The IRSS WASH response will be aimed at provision of timely, equitable access to safe water , sanitation and hygiene to 48,500 people affected by conflict – IDPs, returnees and host communities in the targeted areas. The interventions will ensure:(a) WASH temporary and sustainable services provided addresses the key and vital needs , public health and protection issues through an AAP community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross –sectoral evaluation and analysis to ensure that the different groups of the affected population have access to safe water supply. This implies IRW will undertake broader consultation with the various groups of the affected population – women , men , girls and boys . Ensure that water supply facilities are safe at to access at all levels. Ensuring that sanitation facilities and services are likewise safely accessible by all groups. IRW will conduct risk analysis to ensure that the various groups are equally positively affected by the activities.(b) Ensure specific activities for GBV mitigation within the target areas will be based on protection assessment conducted by IRW female staff with women, girls and children. This will guide on putting the WASH infrastructures in sites considered by women ,girls and children as secure and agreed upon by the respective groups . Boreholes and sanitation facilities will be located after a GBV risk analysis is conducted to ensure that all water points and sanitation facilities provide easy and safe access and use of the facilities.Based on the needs identified, IRW will undertake the following activities: - Drilling of 17 boreholes completed with Indian MKII/III hand pumps (Kapoeta east 5 , Terekeka 4 , Tonj east 5 , and Yei 3 ) health , nutrition centers and schools will also be targeted . - Rehabilitation of 50 defunct boreholes to full functionality ( Kapoeta east 17 , Terekeka 10 , Tonj east 17 , and Yei 6 ) health , nutrition centers and schools will also be targeted. - Training of 190 members into water management committee for effective management of the water facilities with 30% women. ( Kapoeta 60 , Terekeka 50 , Tonj east 60 , Yei 20 . - Training of 31 community members as hand pump mechanics with 20% women ( Kapoeta 10 , Terekeka 07 , Tonj east 10 and yei 4 ) - Training of 31 community hygiene promoters ( Kapoeta east 10, Terekeka 7 , Tonj east 10 and Yei 4 ) - Supply and distribute WASH NFIs ( plastic jerry cans , plastic basins , washing soaps ) to 3,000 most vulnerable house holds – I kit/ HH ( Kapoeta east 1,000 , Terekeka 500 , Tonj east 1,000 and Yei 500 ) - Supply and distribute women 3,000 dignity kits to vulnerable girls of adolescent age and women of child bearing age (Kapoeta east 1,000, Terekeka 500 , Tonj east 1,000 and Yei 500 ) . - Conduct 10 sessions hygiene promotion ( campaigns in public places , schools , households , health facilities , nutrition centers , focusing on public health problems , cholera and other diarrheal diseases ) - Conduct 8 sessions on WASH based awareness on GBV mitigation .- Conduct WASH KAP survey in Tonj east and Terekeka counties - Rehabilitation of 8 blocks existing VIP latrines in schools, health and nutrition centers complete with hand washing facilities , to improve the sanitation condition of the targeted facilities and as a modality of mainstreaming WASH and GBV . (Kapoeta east 02 , Terekeka 02 , Tonj east 02 , Yei 02 )- Conduct GBV risk analysis prior to construction and rehabilitation of boreholes and latrines. - Strengthen access to gender sensitive feedback and complaints mechanisms in the project sites - Formation and training of accountability committees - Training of CHPs, WASH committee members on detecting indicators of violence and ask the right questions; - Staff capacity building on gender, protection analysis and PSEA.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

IRSS staff at the field office openly interact with the community during meetings, and it is in this meetings whereby a series of issues are discussed. At the commencement of the project, the project team clearly explain to the affected population the objectives of the project, the duration, the target villages, the number of the affected people the project intend to serve and how the response will be, their roles and contribution in the project, entitlements etc. IRSS will display project related beneficiary entitlements, activities through visibility boards using pictures/illustrations and in local languages for all community members to understand the purpose. IRSS will also recruit community mobilizers who act as translators, interpreters and who live in the community as a community resource person to make communication flow as smoothly as possible. Finally, continuous meetings with the community members will be undertaken so that they will be informed and sensitized on the project, their roles, entitlements and their contribution. Accountability committee that will have been formed with members selected from all sections of the target beneficiaries (male, female, elderly, disabled etc) also have the responsibility of explaining the entitlement to the rest of the beneficiaries and community members.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All the messages related to health and hygiene promotion are made contextualized or tailored to the local needs especially needs of women, girls, boys and men are considered and focused. Banners and other printing materials are also made in local language so that targeted communities (men, women, boys and girls) understand it easily. For example, Hygiene promotion activities will involve women and men; and activities will be timed to fit around women and men’s other work schedules. CHPs will be sensitised on referral pathways so as to be able to refer to appropriate services and actors when they encounter protection issues and also trained on the capacity to detect indicators of violence and ask the right questions.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IRSS will integrate proactive measures to minimize SEA risks, through staff capacity development and codes of conduct. All new IR staff complete a mandatory induction which includes the Code of Conduct (reviewed and signed), and where a session (1:1 or group) is provided by both Protection and Gender staff. IR also has policies on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), child protection and a Safeguarding focal point to address any allegations of sexual exploitation or abuse and all staff are trained on these policies. In addition, programme staff participate in training sessions on protection and gender (general principles of gender mainstreaming and safe programming and specific themes). The project also considers the protection from SEA through the placement of feedback mechanisms; the following of WASH Sphere standards, such as locks in latrines; siting of WASH infrastructure based on consultation with women, elderly, disabled etc; the training of CHPs, particularly on being able to detect indicators of violence and ask the right questions; the training of project staff on gender equality and protection; undertaking GBV risk analysis on water, sanitation and WASH NFI distributions to make sure that no-harm is done; undertaking monthly monitoring of access to WASH services for women, girls, and vulnerable populations such as elderly and people with disabilities and the collection of monitoring data that is sex and age disaggregated.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Diverse community members and the local authorities took part in the assessment and prioritization of needs. IRSS assessment team were provided with information on the needs, the existing gaps and type of services and support required by the community. The information received was put back to the project proposal for finalization.During the assessment, focus group discussions and KIIs were conducted with different community groups including separate discussions with women, adolescent girls and disabled people. Consultations were also done with IDPs, returnees and host communities so that all segments of the community were able to give their perspective on their needs.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

IRSS has established a feedback and Complaints mechanisms and reporting procedures at the field offices where all the beneficiaries are given the leeway to communicate their grievances directly to the office. A complaints and feedback mechanism focal point (CFM focal point) is available in each of the field offices with a dedicated hotline phone number where network is available. During the project implementation, accountability committees will be formed on community defined vulnerability criteria and whose members will include individuals from the WASH committees and CHPs. The committee will be trained on the purpose of the complaints and feedback mechanisms as well as how to collect the information from the community and report through the established system. Visibility boards will also be installed at the project sites with the hotline telephone number put on it and the name of the CFM focal point. The CFM focal person therefore records grievances for each complainant in the complaints/feedback form while greatly considering the confidentially of the complainant and share with the management team at the country Office for appropriate decision to be taken. IR responses to this complaints/feedbacks are always clear, accurate, balanced, simple, fair and easy to understand. All the issues raised in the complaint will be addressed.In summary, below are the mechanisms IR South Sudan is employing in tracking complaints/feedbacks• In person, i.e. verbal feedback or complaint to any staff member of IRSS especially the CFM focal person. This is really beneficial as people with low literacy levels are also given a platform to express themselves and make recommendations on how IRSS can further improve on the project.• By phone/Mobile (call or text SMS). Focal person is assigned to collect and record feedback and complaints • Via a ‘suggestion/ complain box/visibility boards erected at the project sites

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A project monitoring framework will be developed based on the approved logical framework approach and targets in consultation with different community stakeholders. The project monitoring framework will have indicators to track the progress and capture information on gathering evidence to inform results achieved against the project targets. The proposed monitoring system will provide an ‘early warning’ to issues that might risk the project completion and allow for timely appropriate action to be taken if the project is not implemented according to the approved target plan. The project monitoring will be conducted on weekly and monthly basis by WASH officers supported by the MEAL staff. A joint monitoring and supervision will also be conducted on quarterly basis with the participation of government departments and local community representatives. Monthly monitoring reports will be produced in order to document the project progress and share with management in Juba.Moreover, at field level, IRSS will involve WASH committees and CHPs to participate in the monitoring and follow up of the project implementation at site levels. Due to insecurity and access challenges, involvement of the local committees in the implementation will be very significant and help IR to have representatives from the local communities that participate in the project implementation as well as monitoring. Having such strong role from the target communities will not only help to better monitor the progress towards project objectives but it will also help to build local ownership of the project services and infrastructures in the long term. This will lead to gradual take over by the local community and authorities after the project ends and IR exits.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

During implementation a GBV risk analysis will be done prior to construction and rehabilitation of boreholes and latrines in order to ensure that girls, boys, women and men (including older people and those with disabilities) have access to appropriate and safe WASH services;The project will also ensure girls, boys, women, and men (including older people and those with disabilities) have access to gender sensitive feedback and complaint mechanisms so that corrective actions can address their specific protection and assistance.Monitoring &Evaluation will include consultations with communities particularly women and girls, disabled and elderly regarding their satisfaction, access and perceptions of safety in relation to WASH interventions.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IR’s analysis shows that women and girls have been disproportionately affected by this crisis, have an important role in providing for their families amid these hard times and yet lack decision making power at both the HH and community levels. Women and girls are particularly affected by a lack of adequate, safe and appropriate WASH. IR will address these needs through borehole construction and rehabilitation so as increase access to safe and clean water, gender-sensitive latrine rehabilitation to mitigate SGBV, hygiene promotion, the distribution of gender-appropriate hygiene kits, and SGBV referrals, ensuring meaningful participation of particularly women and girls. Participation of community members, especially women and girls, is a key component of this project, ensuring a gender-sensitive and culturally appropriate project approach. This approach will be accomplished through a variety of aspects, including consultations with all members of community, including women, men, girls, and boys, throughout the project cycle; the placement of feedback mechanisms; the following of WASH Sphere standards, such as locks in latrines; the training of CHPs, particularly on being able to detect indicators of violence and ask the right questions; the training of project staff on gender equality and PSEA; the inclusion of gender analysis findings in the project intervention; and the collection of monitoring data that is sex and age disaggregated.
