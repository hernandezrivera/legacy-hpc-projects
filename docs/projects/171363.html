Project Summary

Nutrition situation in target areas remain fragile with unstable state following presence of aggravating factors that trigger high levels of acute malnutrition.
Afmadow and Kismayo in Lower Juba are areas with persistent high acute malnutrition rates. Both areas have IDP and urban poor as vulnerable populations whose state are affected by multiple aggravating factors . In the last season these factors have included: disease outbreaks, especially measles and AWD cholera, acute household food insecurity which was heightened by COVID 19 prevention measures among IDP and urban poor and displacement mostly through floods and military offensive in neighboring districts contributing to migration into these districts. In Afmadow acute malnutrition state is stressed.GAM among Kismayo IDP and urban is 12.2 and 11.4 receptively. SAM rates however remain high at 2.6 and 3.2 respectively. Acute malnutrition among PLW in Kismayo is stressed (FSNAU<2020).
Baidoa IDP remain some the most vulnerable in Somalia (Reach IDP profile 2019). IDP
numbers continue to increase due to a number of factors including conflict, insecurity,
flooding and drought among others. In January 2020, the first influx of IDP fled from
Tooswayne to Outskirts of Baidoa town following clashes with Al Shabaab community
leaders on forced taxation and child recruitment. As a result, belongings of the
community members were looted including grain storage and business centers, forcing
the people to flee empty handed. In June 2020, armed conflict broke out
again in Toosweyne village causing communities to flee to IDP camps. More IDPs continue to arrive at camps straining resources and increasing vulnerability to acute malnutrition. By end July 2020, an estimated number of 4,500 households have moved to IDP to find shelter, security, healthcare and food .These IDP reported high household food insecurity, poor access to nutrition services with high SAM rate, poor integration with health and high risk to COVID 19 infection (OCHA led Multisector assessment report,September 2020).
WRRS is currently implementing integrated health and nutrition interventions in Afmadow and Kismayo that it intends to sustain in 2021. In all locations both mobile and fixed
services will be offered. Mobile teams will cover rural areas where services hardly reach
while fixed facilities will cover the town areas and IDP sites where the population can
easily access services. CHW will also be employed to ensure the population is encouraged to use these services and that some services are offered at village/community level.
In Baidoa, WRRS is implementing a camp management project covering most new IDP. Having identified the huge nutrition gap in camps, WRRS plans to scale up and include integrated nutrition and health interventions in the camps where it has operational presence.
In all location Food security, WASH and protection activities will also be integrated.All sites will offer treatment of acute malnutrition (MAM and SAM) and prevention services for both children under 5 and PLW.
Project will also include : identification and referral of protection victims, prevention of COVID
19 and hygiene promotion with distribution of hygiene kits and risk communication.
Proposed interventions will also include all gender groups, marginalized groups and the
disabled who are often left out or given unequal treatment. Disabled will participate at all
levels of the project cycle. Staff will be trained on disability inclusion as to to eliminate
discrimination . APP and protection will also be mainstreamed. All data will be
dis aggregated according to gender and disability.The project will adhere to set
government policy and guidelines and any other MoH protocol, while observing
international health guidelines.
Coordination will be ensured with health actors and those from other sectors.

Project Activities

Activity 1: Identify acutely malnourished children(Boys and girls) and PLWs through screening; at both the health facility and at community level Activity 2: Treat moderate acute malnutrition cases (Boys, girls and PLW);this shall be through fixed and mobile sites. Activity 3: Treat severe acute malnutrition with and without complication among children under 5 (Boys and girls);this shall be through fixed and mobile sites. Activity 4 : Provide MCHN package for pregnant and lactating women who deliver at health facilities. Activity 5: Provide BSFP package for families with vulnerable children under 3 years from the community. Activity 6: Micro nutrient supplementation for Pregnant and lactating women Activity 7:Provide Vitamin A to children 1-5 years ( boys and girls ) at the health facility, mobile sites and during national campaigns. Activity 8:Food based and non‑food based preventive actions including nutrition sensitive activities for communities (Men , women, boys and girls) Activity 9: Nutrition health and hygiene promotion with distribution of hygiene kits to the population(Men , women, boys and girls) Activity 10: IYCF/IYCF- E promotion and support for pregnant and lactating women, willing spouses and other close relatives (Men and women) including support for feeding the sick children (Boys and girls) Activity 11: Training of nutrition workers (Men and women) in revised IMAM and IYCF/IYCF-E Activity 12 : Train community nutrition workers in community mobilization and health education

Implementation of activities have been through locals and working very closely with government entities. These has been the organisations approach given that once the emergency subsides and the NGO has to hand over to the government and community; there shall be a smooth transition.Once the environment is safe/stable and everything has been handed over, there shall be no mobile sites in operation and all activities shall shift to the health facilities; that shall be run by the Ministry of Health.Working closet with the government and aligning the project with its initiatives shall ensure that even after the Projects are over, there shall be continuation; and beneficiaries shall get the services which shall also be legally recognised by the government.The project does use existing structures within the community, networks already established within the community (CHWs) hence this will guarantee a smooth transition once the area is stable. The project having been in support of government initiatives, it shall be more of a continuation of activities with nothing new.
