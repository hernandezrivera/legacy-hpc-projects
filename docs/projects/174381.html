Project Summary


The project is aimed at strengthening the 4 of the 6 key pillars of the health system in Governance and Leadership, Health care service delivery, Human Resource for Health, Health information Systems in the States of Borno Yobe and Adamawa. It would seek to produce and adapt key policy and strategic documents within the BAY states and support the states to coordinate the implementation of these strategic documents through the state structures while ensuring strong partners collaboration.
WHO in Partnership with the State Ministries of Health in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa would work Collaboratively with all the other partners within to strengthen the Nexus between the Humanitarian and development programs and also promote the principles of aid effectiveness. The 3 health training institutions within Borno Yobe and Adamawa would be sustainably strengthened to improve the quality and the mix of Human resource for Health Production which is critical catalyst in achieving Universal Health Coverage. The Health institution would be supported to achieve the recommended Tutors/students ratio, the Curricula of the training institutions would be reviewed in line with the standards of the accreditation Bodies and relevant equipment provided for the practical demonstration room, science laboratories, classroom while the colleges would also be supported to equip and operationalize the e-libraries. Tutors would be supported to attend the mandatory Continuing medical education and in-service trainings to improve their capacities to deliver quality training packages to the institutions. The zenith of the implementation would be the tracking of the institutions preparedness to achieve and retain their accreditation status.
WHO would also work with the SMoH in the 3 states to strengthen the Human Resource for Health Units to improve the implementation, monitoring and review of progress made with the implementation of the Human Resource Strategic Plans in the BAY states through regular review meetings with the HRH focal points of the State and LGAs with quarterly feedback provided to the government and stakeholders for action.
WHO would partner with the SMOH in Borno state to conduct Work load indicator of Staffing needs (WISN) assessment surveys to guide the redistribution of staff based on needs in order to address the distribution of health workers in the state which is currently skewed with more than 70% of the population residing in the Urban Centers leaving out the rural areas with a dearth of qualifies Health workers.
WHO would further work with the states of Borno, Yobe and Adamawa to conduct training needs assessments for PHC workers whose TOR has been expanded as a result of the task shifting and task sharing policies (TSTS) of the state to prioritize the areas that would address the maternal and child morbidities and Mortalities within the states. Following which there would be trainings of the health workers based on the documented gaps and prioritization. Supportive supervision and quality of care assessments would be conducted in the Health facilities to further improve the quality of healthcare services within the state.
The estimated target population for the intervention would be 6,577 comprising estimated 6,000 students from the Health training institutions in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa, 500 health workers, 77 HRH focal points from states and 77 LGAs. The indirect beneficiaries would comprise 783,00 people distributed as 250,560 IDPs, 156,600 Returnees and 375,840 Host community in BAY States. The interventions would be delivered through very strong engagements with the existing capacities within the Ministry of Health while further technical assistance would be procured. WHO would mainstream gender into the interventions to ensure equitable distribution of interventions for men, women, Boys, Girls including the disabled, the elderly and most vulnerable population. Accountability would be to the affected population through feedback

1 - Needs

The health indices in the North East is historically among the poorest globally with key indicators like maternal mortality ratio and under five mortality rates consistently far worse than the national average. Empirical evidence estimates maternal Mortality ratio at about 1600 per 100,000 live births which is arguably among the worst in the country. MICS 2016 estimates that only 53.4% of women receive antenatal care (ANC) from a skilled provider, while 34.2% complete 4 visits of ANC. Those delivered by a skilled provider are 29.3 %, and much fewer (17%) deliver in health facilities. Access to other sexual and reproductive health services remains low in the 3 states. There is also a wide Human resource for Health gaps in State with only 38.4 skilled Health workers per 100,000 which is a far cry from the recommended 445 skilled health workers per 100,000 recommendation. The picture has further been complicated by the activities of the insurgents which has limited access to health services with many health facilities not able to operate 24hour health services. To address this alarming poor indices, the Borno State Ministry of Health is working with the Federal ministry of Health and Health sector Partners to prioritize maternal and child health interventions including addressing some of the Human Resource for Health skill gaps by adapting and promoting the task shifting and task sharing policy to empower other cadres of Health workers to acquire the right skills to support the maternal and Child health programs. Despite these measures there are still wide capacity gaps in the implementation of Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) in primary health care facilities hence the need to progressively address these gaps through the SMoH and partner agency structures Health Resources and Service Availability Monitoring assessment report presents baseline data from 809 assessed health facilities across 27 LGAs in Borno State. Only 41% of the health facilities in those LGAs are fully functional, while up to 48% of the facilities are not functioning (including fully damaged ones) and 11% partially functioning. Additionally, 64% of the assessed facilities were either fully (31%) or partially (33%) damaged with only 34% of the facilities not damaged. Non-functionality of the health facilities resulted from damage to infrastructures (60%) and lack of access due to security constraints (32%). From the fully and partially functioning facilities, just over 25% had operational in-patient services, 20% had trauma services, and 25% had the necessary stocks of essential medicines and supplies.

2 - Activities

Sustainably strengthen the capacities for production, retention and distribution of quality Human resources for Health in the BAY State. WHO would support the state to strengthen the capacity of the health training institutions to produce quality healthcare workers in line with the needs of the state. Accreditation needs assessment would be conducted in 3 colleges of Health technologies in Borno, Yobe and adamawa and the gaps would be used to guide the support to these institutions. The organization would support 15 tutors of the colleges on the Mandatory continuing Education to build their capacities and meet the accreditation requirements. Similarly, 9 tutors would be supported, and 4 lecturers would be supported to undertake in service trainings within accredited Units and departments. This would help to bridge the existing gaps in tutor /students ratio in the institutions.Furthermore, WHO would support the equipping of 4 departments each for the 3 Health training institutions in Borno Yobe and Adamawa with medical equipments for the practical demonstration rooms, science laboratory equipment, classroom furniture for 3,000 students and also provide a well-equipped electronic Libraries in the Colleges of the three states. Who would also support the training institutions to review their curriculum in line with the prescriptions of the regulatory Bodies. To ensure gender mainstreaming and equity in the enrollment of the students in the institutions, a committee would be established and inaugurated to oversee the admission processes and to review adherence to Gender sensitive recruitment and distribution. The 20-man committee in which state would meet quarterly to review Gender sensitive process both in admission, production, recruitment and deployment. To address capacity gaps in Human Resource for Health Planning in the state, WHO would facilitate the conduct of regular review meetings with HRH focal persons and assistants for the 15 MDAs Human Resource for Health Units and the HRH focal points of the 67 LGAs in the BAY states. This meeting would be used to review and address HRH related issues and validate the monthly data for the routing update of the Health workforce registry. The HRH Units of the SMoH would be supported to produce bi annual bulletins. The heads of Health facilities in 500 selected health facilities would be trained also on leadership and HR management. -Sustainably strengthen capacities of Health workers to deliver comprehensive healthcare services in line with the minimum service package in BAY States A comprehensive approach to healthcare service delivery would be deployed to enhance quality in the delivery of Healthcare services across the states. This would focus on the Key elements of quality of care and address the implementation of the Minimum Service Package of Health services already developed in the 3 States. WHO would support the development of tools and checklists that would assess the implementation and compliance to the Minimum standards for Primary Healthcare services and use same to conduct routine supportive supervision and Monitoring Visits to the Health facilities where implementing partners are supporting Healthcare services. The findings from such supportive supervision would be analyzed to guide the Health sector in mapping service delivery and compliance to minimum standards across the states. To ensure availability of adequate capacities to address the implementation of minimum service Package for Primary healthcare which already have existing task shifting and Task Sharing Policies that are designed to address the gaps with the numbers and mix of core health workers like doctors, Nurses and midwives would seek to train other cadres of health staff to perform some important roles in the drive to reduce the high rate of maternal and under 5 mortalities . A capacity needs assessment would be conducted across the BAY states and the findings would be validated and shared.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

WHO is committed to promoting Accountability to the affected population, for the project, this will be Direct involvement in the monitoring and evaluation o ensure compliance to our commitment to the affected population. The proposed M&E framework will include feedback and accountability mechanisms to take into account feedback from the beneficiaries in the monitoring of the intervention and achieved mainly in the following three areas;• WHO intervention will take into account the diversity of the community and ensure the beneficiaries are clearly disaggregated by social markers (age, sex and disability) throughout the lifecycle of the project. WHO will periodically solicit opinions and thoughts of beneficiaries (including of the vulnerable groups and those with special needs- including people living with disability) through community engagement sessions. Feedbacks from the community will serve to guide the project implementation.• WHO will give updates of the progress of health interventions and discuss way forward with stakeholders through key informant interviews and focused group discussions (FGDs)• Through established network of LGA facilitators and availability of Toll-Free lines, the beneficiaries will be able to give feedback on the quality of service delivered and how well the activities have addressed their needs.• WHO will also work with the State Ministries of Health and the community health workers on the perception and concerns of the beneficiaries about the activities. This feedback will be used to modify services and activities implementation to the vulnerable population.
