Project Summary

WFP is implementing a nutrition response frame with stronger focus towards prevention of malnutrition. The programme focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with focus on the first 100days of life. The programme targets all children aged 6-23 months including those children suffering from moderate acute malnutrition-MAM) as well as for children 24-59 months with MAM in the most food insecure departments presenting highest GAM rates in the Far North, North, Adamawa, and East region, while in the North west and south west region the programme targets children aged 6-23 months and pregnant and lactating women and girls in food insecure household. The programme activities include:
1.Screening for malnutrition and referral of severe cases for treatment;
2.Promotion of infant and young child feeding practices
3.Distribution of specialized nutritious food to children aged 6-23months and Pregnant and lactating women and girls(PLWGs)
4.Social and behavioral change communication in favor of good nutrition and health practices
5.Promotion of the use of the blanket feeding platform or provision of complementary services including immunization, micro-nutrient supplementation, deworming, malaria and other childhood illnesses prevention, family planning etc

Besoins / Needs

The results of the latest SMART nutrition survey conducted in November 2019 by UNICEF and the government of Cameroon classified the Far North, East and North regions in a poor nutrition situation with global acute malnutrition rates of 5.2percent, 5.5percent and 6.2percent respectively. Stunting remains a public health concern in Cameroon. Stunting remains a public health problem in all 4 regions with prevalence exceeding the WHO serious threshold of 30%. The Far North has the highest prevalence at 38.2%. Regarding health situation, low immunization coverage, limited access to essential health care services (ratio between the population and the number of doctors remains far below the norm) and essential drugs increased risks of epidemics.The prevalence of food insecurity is 11% in the Far North region (CFSVA February 2018, including 1% in severe food insecurity. The proportion of households whose food consumption is poor remained relatively high in the North (9%). In this context, indicator related to infant and young child feeding practices are among the poorest in the country, especially for the Far North region with exclusive breastfeeding rate of 30.8%, a timely introduction rate for complementary foods of 33.3% and a low dietary diversity (dietary diversity score of 19.7% and minimum acceptable dietary intake of 32%). Indicators related to early initiation of breastfeeding and diet diversity follow the same trends thus highlighting the needs to knowledge and support around infant and young child feeding. MUAC Screening integrated into the emergency food security assessment (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 revealed a proxy global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates of 4.51percent and 7.4Percent for the NW and SW regions respectively. Furthermore, the proportion of children who meet their minimum acceptable diet remains extremely low at less than 30% percent (MICS 2014). The drivers of malnutrition deteriorated in 2019 and are expected to remain in the same trend in 2020. Reduced food production, reduced prices in rural areas, increased prices in urban areas and decline in food consumption as a coping strategy adopted in most households have resulted to substantial deterioration of food insecurity and household livelihoods in 2019. This has exposed IDPs and poor host populations to poor living conditions and acute food insecurity Crisis (IPC 3).It is estimated that more than 300,000 children and Pregnant and lactating women and girls in in emergency need of nutrition services across all 6 regionsWFP’s programme focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with focus on the first 100days of life. The programme targets all children aged 6-23 months MAM children 24-59 months in the most food insecure departments presenting highest GAM rates in the Far North, North, Adamawa, East region, while in the North west and south west region the programme targets children aged 6-23 months and pregnant and lactating women and girls in food insecure household. The programme activities include:1.Screening for malnutrition and referral of severe cases for treatment, Social and behavioral change communication in favor of appropriate infant and young child feeding practices and other Family practices, distribution of specialized nutritious food to children aged 6-23months and Pregnant and lactating women and girls(PLWGs) and promotion of the use of the blanket feeding platform for provision of complementary services including immunization, micro-nutrient supplementation, deworming, malaria and other childhood illnesses prevention, family planning etcWFP in 2020 intends to target about 94,000 beneficiaries across all 6 regions (Far North 31,000, North,10775, East, 13,915, Adamawa,9256 North west,24150 and South West,400 with the package of malnutrition prevention activities.
