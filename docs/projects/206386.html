Project Summary

The project addresses the return of 337,844 individuals to conflict-affected areas in Metuge, Quissanga, Palma, and Mdp, focusing on urgent shelter needs and enhancing community resilience. Key activities include emergency shelter provision, early recovery solutions, capacity-building in resilient construction, and revitalization of community disaster preparedness committees.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The situation of return in Quissanga, Palma, and Mdp underscores the reintegration of over 337,844 individuals into an area ravaged by the impact of armed conflict. The returning population confronts substantial challenges in rebuilding their lives, primarily due to the dilapidated infrastructure and services, the limited presence of humanitarian actors, and the diminished capacity of local authorities. Additionally, Mozambique is a country with a high incidence of natural disasters, particularly cyclones, during the rainy seasons. This, coupled with the previously mentioned context, heightens the vulnerability of populations and increases the risk of their resources being depleted and affected by these phenomena annually. In this context, shelter emerges as one of the most pressing needs. Accordingly, the project aims to expedite the recovery of the returned and displaced population while simultaneously enhancing resilience and capacity-building within communities in Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Prepardness (DRP) concepts and techniques. To achieve this, the project outlines four main activities: (1) Provision of emergency shelter solutions and retrofitting of existing structures for 2,500 families; (2) Provision of early recovery shelter solutions for 1600 families; (3) Building capacity in resilient construction techniques and basic Building Back Better (BBB) principles at the household level for more than 4,500 individuals; (4) Revitalization of 3 community disaster preparedness and response committees.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The shelter situation in Metuge, Quissanga, MdP and Palma demands a tailored approach considering diverse factors such as community location, return dates, and access to construction materials. Ayuda en Acción, in collaboration with SDPI and INGD, will conduct prior community-specific assessments and implementation for each project activity. 1. Provision of Emergency Shelter Solutions and RetrofittingTargeting 2500 households (HH) and 12,500 individuals, the project involves constructing emergency shelters for those without alternatives and providing materials for repairing deteriorating shelters. AeA's field team will supervise and assist in technical repairs, utilizing old tarpaulins for rainwater harvesting. 2. Provision of Early Recovery Shelter SolutionsWith an estimated 1150 HH and 7750 individuals benefiting, early recovery solutions will be built in selected communities after technical assessments. Focus groups will ensure the design meets specific needs, aiding communities in early recovery and resilience building. 3. Building Capacity in Resilient Construction TechniquesApproximately 990 HH and 990 individuals will receive training in resilient construction techniques and Building Back Better (BBB) principles. The initiative focuses on creating easily replicable construction models and empowering families to undertake improvements independently. 4. Revitalization of Community Disaster Risk Preparedness and Response CommitteesDirectly benefiting 3 communities, 900 HH and 4,500 individuals, the project aims to revitalize Disaster Risk Reduction committees. Collaborating with SDPI and INGD, it includes four stages over six months: committee identification, risk mapping, training, and equipment. Committees will also access Community Development Loans for income generation. This intervention responds to the urgent shelter needs, facilitates early recovery, and builds resilience within the returning population, aligning with broader community development and disaster preparedness goals.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

AeA ensures that, during the design phase of its projects, information is gathered on the specific needs of each gender and various vulnerable groups. To achieve this, AeA conducts a socio-spatial analysis to identify the requirements of each demographic (considering age, ethnicity, gender, disability, etc.). This involves exploratory walks, interviews with key informants, focus groups, and direct observation to pinpoint the challenges and risks, including access to public spaces and services, associated with each specific group.AeA then analyzes this data to target groups where it can have the greatest impact, actively coordinating with relevant stakeholders to avoid duplication of efforts. Subsequently, the activities are customized to address the actual needs observed in the field. AeA also monitors these activities, placing a strong emphasis on using a gender-aware lens throughout the process. In addition, protection mainstreaming is an integral part of AeA’s design strategy, this alongside the participatory methods that AeA use to collect information help greatly to identify the risks posed to each demographic. Staff working in the field are aware of the do no harm, a principle which assists in making the most suitable decisions for the populations in question. We prioritise preserving safety and dignity in the design of any installation, infrastructure or the planning of any event. Ensuring that there is equitable access for all and encouraging participation in order to empower those we are helping.A multi-sector risk analysis for each component of the project will be made to ensure that all risks are considered and mitigated. As an example early marriages (the families force minors to get married in order to create another HH and receive extra plot and shelter) are a frequent practice on the sites. In these cases a previous analisis will be carried out and provide extra shelter support for those large families and/or having children under this risk. In addition AeA will work alongside CCCM to locate street lights where necessary and assure the proper secure contitions to access shelters.In addition AeA will closely coordinate with protection actors in the field in order to attend refearals about GVB or other protection cases with needs of shelters, adapting the design to these individuals/families to assure the security and confort. In addition, a risk assessment and safety walks will be carried out prio to the intervention in order to identify the safe areas and potential and current risk in the community for the different population groups in order to adapt the design and location of the shelters to each context, preventing protection risks.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project looks not only in assisting the most vulnerable population but also on including women and youth on training, construction and cash for work programmes. Women participate mainly in mudding activities besides the technical trainings on resilient construction methods and in the establishment of site Maintenance Committees and DRR committees supported by INGD ( Comites Locais de Gestao de Risco de Desastre) that will include at least 50% of women in each group. In order to complete successfully these activities, the schedule and dates of the trainings will be consulted and agreed with the different commitees to facilitate the attendance of all groups (specially women and youths).

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Dedicated committees for Persons with Disabilities (PwD) and their families will be established to articulate specific needs and identify gaps in the response process. This initiative takes into account various types of disabilities, ensuring the implementation of accessible service structures. This approach aims to enhance overall participation and provide adequate physical access to essential services. Furthermore, throughout the project implementation, households with PwD will be prioritized for assistance in terms of shelter and non-food items (NFI), receiving additional support for the construction or rehabilitation of their shelters. Considerations such as functional diversity and other special needs will guide the adaptation of shelters. Measures may include enlarging doorways for wheelchair access, ensuring direct entry without obstacles or steps, and implementing other necessary adjustments to enhance the comfort and living conditions of shelter inhabitants. In the context of Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) committees, specific training will be provided with a focus on addressing the needs of PwD. This training will incorporate elements that highlight the unique requirements of individuals with disabilities, preparing the committee to respond effectively in the event of a natural disaster. The implementation of this training approach is currently underway by INGD within the DRR committee training program.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Prior to the initiation of development projects, a comprehensive diagnosis of the territory, inclusive of an environmental risk assessment tailored to our actions, is conducted by AeA. The guiding principles of our initiatives revolve around risk management, adaptation, and mitigation. To delineate the scope of our work, we emphasize specific actions such as territorial planning, analysis of land ownership or use, and sector-specific environmental risk assessments (agriculture, fishing, services, etc.). Environmental mitigation measures will be implemented by AeA through the replanting of identified areas near beneficiary populations with bamboo, especially in riparian zones. Simultaneously, community training sessions will be organized on bamboo planting, care, and proper harvesting, accompanied by the establishment of natural resource management committees. These training sessions will be carried out in collaboration with IIAM (Instituto de Investigação Agrária de Moçambique). Additionally, livelihood components, such as bamboo processing systems for crafts or charcoal production, will be integrated to foster community-level interest in sustainable natural resource management. Furthermore, an analysis will be undertaken by AeA to identify specific areas for the extraction of earth and mud for shelter construction. This activity aims to mitigate the risks of erosion, floods, and other associated hazards that could impact the shelters in the future. This program will encompass a holistic intervention in Quissanga, MdP, and Palma, incorporating water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), housing, land, and property (HLP), and livelihood components. This integrated approach allows for resource optimization and a more impactful project footprint.
