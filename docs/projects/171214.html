Project Summary

The project seeks to strengthen case management through direct service provision to children at risk and survivors of gender-based violence (GBV). Additionally, the project focus will be on issues that increase risks to children such as when they are unaccompanied or separated children (UASC), children without proper care who are children at risk of being recruited by armed forces or armed groups (CAAFAG). The interventions aim at direct services like family tracing, reintegration into the community for children formerly associated with armed groups (including psychosocial first aid- PFA and Psychosocial support to both children, caregivers in addition to victim support and referral services for where a holistic approach is necessary. The project will include capacity building by providing training and mentoring to CP and GBV staff from INTERSOS as well as implementing partners (IPs). CPiE aims to provide immediate lifesaving assistance to vulnerable populations, prevention, and response to GBV.
Project beneficiaries will receive comprehensive case management and psycho-social support (PSS) – including emergency care, referral to INTERSOS medical teams trained on clinical management of rape (CMR). Additionally, access tailored material assistance, basic emotional support, and follow up on all services (both directly provided and those received during/after referrals).
Capacity building of duty bearers including formal and informal authorities to strengthen and improve identification. Considering current approaches, the emphasis is on scaling up prevention and response services to protection-related risk for children and survivors of GBV. For children who are separated, prioritizing restoring the family links/family tracing efforts) who are the primary duty-bearers if in their best interest. Commitment to participate in strengthening service mapping to facilitate appropriate referrals and through coordination and maintaining links necessary in service delivery. Comprehensive services to survivors as well as children at risk (UASC, CAAFAG, and/or experiencing any form of abuse including those neglected), to ensure the protection of children from recruitment or use by armed forces or armed groups and even use of girls (gender-based violence (GBV)).
The project will further strengthen protection monitoring in areas of settlement of returnees and IDPs through community-based protection monitors (or advocates) working with CSOs to ensure that quality evidence-based reports about protection issues and human rights violations in a systematic collection of protection-related data to inform better response and advocacy (including information feeding to the monitoring of grave violations against children (MRM) when applicable.
People with disabilities will be given change to participate in all aspects of the project e.g. including them among protection monitors and prioritizing them in the event that they are directly affected by GBV or related issues clinical management of rape.

Project Activities

(1). Providing Survivor-centered multi-sectorial support (including provision or referral to Medical/legal/PSS services and but not limited to safety relocation) through comprehensive case management provided by trained caseworkers, relevant service providers as well as Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) according to beneficiaries needs(2). Provision of structured MHPSS activities including individual and group counseling focus on children in need, caregivers, and families (including positive parenting and in-kind of support) in order to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their own children. Supporting tailored material support to at-risk children (including but not limited to UASC or CAAFAG) and survivors GBV as well as provision of dignity kits (to at-risk women and girls) including solar lanterns to enhance safety and protection.(3). Strengthening community-based protection mechanisms to prevent and mitigate risks faced by boys, and girls (as well as women/survivors of GBV or those at high risk of abuse/exploitation) through multi-layered, community-based psychosocial support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks by building a protective environment at family and community levels. Equip traditional duty bearers as well as medical personnel, and humanitarian staff with relevant information and approaches on child protection in emergencies including assessing and management of child-friendly environment or space; support to health facilities equipped to provide clinical care of sexual assault survivors as well as nutrition to at-risk children under the care of vulnerable women.(5). Facilitate awareness-raising initiatives, direct advocacy, and public outreach campaigns on Child risks and GBV prevention (and mitigation) targeting community-wide on awareness sessions including on recruitment/CAAFAG (use of girls), SGBV, harmful practice, family separation, MRE, MHPSS, and Children’s rights(6). Strengthen child safeguarding measures including PSEA and code of conduct to ensure that all interventions are safe for children including support to staff trained as a PSEA and child safeguarding focal point linkages with community focal point on implementation of safe reporting and action relating to PSEA issues. (7). Coordinate with respective AoRs (and clusters) on safety audits/walks/mapping exercise to identify safety and protection issues for children in camps and host communities that allow children’s participation in identifying risks they are exposed to, and then participate again on coming up with mitigation strategies owned by them (children), the community members, service providers, and humanitarian actors.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash is efficient and cost-effective where beneficiaries' individual costs such as survivors' cost access to the hospital (during referrals), can be expedited irrespective of the time to ensure access within 24hours (which might be life-saving depending on the need and how quick they are able to access a medical facility). This is also applicable for family reunification transport support, which is facilitated to the beneficiary one-way (and with caregiver / accompanying person as applicable) rather than the costly nature of commissioning of a third party which may increase risk (to the beneficiary) and raise expectations (from the community members) if a supplier is engaged by the organization. Cash also strengthens the perception of resilience as the beneficiary tasks themselves with how best to manage the provided resource.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The management will ensure that staff previously trained and with experience on CVA undertakes the responsibility to plan, engage, and linking mobile notification of transfer to the feedback loop system. In consideration to community-based targeting is which is mechanisms that involve delegating to the community (a group of representatives, community leaders, CBOs) the decision about who should receive assistance, within a defined criteria (vulnerability). The process identity existing with the positive coping mechanisms and community-based protection mechanisms. As part and parcel in the process of prevention and identification and beneficiaries of capacity building rights approach; the infrastructure in-country includes 4 financial institutions (Dahabshiil, Amal Bank, Premier Bank, Salaamah Bank) which all link to EVC (which is the most accessible to the vulnerable beneficiary and is hosted on a mobile platform and the most common transaction process in-country.

The key mitigation measures to reduce movement include; (1). Strengthening coordination with non-protection service providers (present in less accessible areas).(2). Deploy mobile teams to respond to in less accessible areas(3). Conduct needs assessment and advocate for resources to those in need (where they are).(4). Build the capacity of the local community members on the rights approach and link to community-based mechanisms to foster resilience With the risk that members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. INTERSOS mitigation measure include;(1). Working with community-based groups and focal point, INTERSOS links to multiple sites ensuring equal opportunities for all clan and sub-clans as per family tracing networks (2). Special focus to support vulnerable caregivers (such as the elderly), beneficiaries with disabilities (including those with mobility issues) INTERSOS will ensure non-discrimination principle and remain neutral per humanitarian principle.(3). Needs assessments conducted will also take consideration to ensure the participation of different demographic from, age, gender, clan, location, or social status in the community focusing on risks for all and protective environment for all (4). Where applicable jointly with all stakeholders consider special arrangements for persons who have difficulty/needFor the riks in terms of barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms, the mitigation measures would include; (1). Creating awareness to beneficiaries on the existing contact numbers; sites for suggestion boxes and staff trained to handle and document all forms of feedback. (2). Ensuring that the community is aware of the responses to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures put in place per feedback/complaint received. (3).
