Project Summary

The overarching objective of this project is to provide child protection services to 35,000 of the most vulnerable conflict-affected populations in Darfur, West Kordofan, and Khartoum States, including IDPs, resident communities, and returnees. The project aims to achieve child protection activities by providing legal assistance, Case Management and referral services, psychosocial support, community base structure strengthening, advocacy and awareness-raising on human rights and protection principles, protection assessment and monitoring, and capacity building for service providers.

1 - Needs

According to the MSNA, three-quarters of children (40 percent of girls) reported difficulty accessing quality child protection response services such as clinical care, psychosocial services, social workers, legal services, community-based child protection structures, and referral systems. Approximately 1 million IDPs (51 percent of whom are females) are vulnerable children from host communities. Children with disabilities, who account for 15% of the child population, continue to face physical and social barriers to accessing essential services, as they're more vulnerable to violence, exploitation, and abuse. The project aims to reach ten communities in Central Darfur (Gharb Jabal Marrah and Shamal Jabal Marrah), Khartoum (Khartoum & Sharg An Neel), North Darfur (Kutum & Melit), South Darfur (Ed Al Fursan & Rehaid Albirdi), and West Kordufan (Al Idia and Ghubaish) More than 450,000 children require CP assistance, with a total target of around 360,000. The project is intended to assist 36,000 children in these locations with a high severity rank, and all of these locations, with the exception of Khartoum, have IDPs and returnees, making those young girls and boys extremely vulnerable.
