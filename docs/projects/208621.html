Project Summary

The initiative by Mon Club International targets 10,000 individuals affected in Maiha LGA, Mubi North LGA of Adamawa state, Fune LGA and Tarmuwa LGA of Yobe state, and Ngoshe ward of Gwoza LGA, Borno state. Its primary focus is on addressing the critical needs of IDPs, host communities, and returnees in these areas. Specifically, the intervention aims to improve breastfeeding practices, enhance knowledge regarding optimal infant and young child feeding practices among caregivers and children aged 6-23 months.
Mon Club will utilize its experienced staff and consultants to provide specialized expertise and technical support. Leveraging its existing presence in these areas, the organization plans to concentrate efforts on locations with the highest needs, including areas often deemed inaccessible by humanitarian groups. Collaboration with other stakeholders is prioritized to prevent duplications and ensure a comprehensive approach.
The project will target all affected individuals, including IDPs and returnees, with a focus on vulnerable groups such as pregnant and lactating women, women-headed households, the elderly, people with disabilities, and those with chronic illnesses. Gender sensitivity will be maintained, with female community hygiene promoters predominantly recruited from the targeted locations.
Attention will be given to the specific needs of children, persons with disabilities, and proper disposal of menstrual waste. The initiative also aims to involve community leaders in activities to raise awareness about protection, particularly aimed at improving the welfare of both girls and boys. Furthermore, measures to reunite separated children and prevent gender-based violence among IDPs and surrounding communities will be implemented, with a commitment to reporting and providing support for all GBV cases.

1 - Needs

The project aims to address critical needs related to malnutrition among children in Northeast Nigeria, an area facing a triple burden of high undernutrition levels, prolonged insecurity, and the challenges posed by the 2023 floods. Malnutrition in children increases the risk of mortality from diseases like diarrhea, pneumonia, and measles, while poor nutrition during early childhood can lead to irreversible stunted growth, impacting cognitive abilities and hindering school and work performance. Mon Club International's initiative specifically targets undernutrition by focusing on improving breastfeeding practices and enhancing awareness of optimal infant and young child feeding practices among caregivers and children aged 0-23 months in Maiha LGA, Mubi North LGA of Adamawa state, Fune LGA and Tarmuwa LGA of Yobe state, and Ngoshe ward of Gwoza LGA, Borno state. This necessitates specialized expertise and technical support, which Mon Club, with its experienced staff and consultants already on the ground, is well-equipped to provide. Leveraging its existing presence, the project aims to provide a responsive intervention to these affected communities.

2 - Activities

Mon Club's chosen activities and targets are meticulously designed to address critical needs in the identified areas. We are prioritizing mass MUAC screenings for children aged 6-59 months, aiming to swiftly identify severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases. Our Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) will provide essential treatment for children aged 0-59 months without complications, while Nutrition Stabilization Centers (SCs) will cater to cases with medical complexities, ensuring comprehensive care. Additionally, we are committed to promoting proper Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices, offering counseling to primary caregivers of infants aged 0-23 months and pregnant women. We will provide crucial micronutrient tablets to pregnant and lactating mothers, along with distributing MNP to enrich family foods for children aged 6-23 months, preventing micronutrient deficiencies where there is no BSFP implementation. Moreover, our initiatives include Vitamin A supplementation, deworming, and prophylaxis for children aged 6-59 months, ensuring a holistic approach to their health needs. Capacity building activities for SPHCDA/Health workers in Nutrition in Emergencies interventions will strengthen local responses to nutritional crises. Also, the formation and execution of IYCF through mother-to-mother support groups in the designated areas will foster community engagement and sustain long-term positive health outcomes.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

When targeting households, gender issues will be implicitly tackled, as the household entity is often shared by men and women. The project will be so designed to include both males and females as beneficiaries. The project takes into consideration gender differences by coming up with designed interventions which are specific to female beneficiaries. The project will create synergies between both males and females through an approach which provides the opportunity to both genders to interact and participate jointly in project activities. The project is all inclusive as volunteers provide community outreach services which can be easily accessed by people with disabilities. As part of project monitoring, data will be gathered and segregated by both gender and disability type. Mon Club will also work closely with the Nutrition sector at LGA and state levels. We will also coordinate with all organizations that work in the Nutrition sector to be sure we avoid duplication of functions and apply best practices in implementation. Lastly, we will ensure that we carry out community engagement sessions with the implementation communities so that we can have their buy-in and also ensure that they are a major stakeholder in all aspects of our project. This will help to strengthen sustainability and resilience. Mon Club will implement and hold itself accountable to her Minimum Standards for Gender Equality and Women’s Rights in Emergencies, safeguarding of children and vulnerable adult’s policy, and Do No Harm policy.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In all the locations, Mon Club will conduct community mobilization and sensitization exercises prior to and throughout the implementation period to ensure that beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project. Field staff will explain the key components and the approach, as well as soliciting regular and ongoing feedback on the implementation. Project Management Committees (PMCs) will be formed, trained and empowered in-line with best practices. Active FCM desks will be set up to receive and respond to community members.
