Project Summary

Medicair's plan proposes a response, that will provide lifesaving nutrition service to flood, and conflict affected children and women in Akobo, Uror, Aweil East, Aweil North, and Rubkona Counties. The aim of this is to reduce suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition and wasting, using CMAM and MICYN approaches, and will be coordinated, collaborated and integrated into the ongoing health, WASH, GBV and FSL activities in the above locations including SMOH and CHDs. Medicair will prioritize life-saving treatment services targeting children U5 years, PBW as the most vulnerable groups. Prevention activities that address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition will be enhanced. Additionally, the response will integrate nutrition with other clusters and employ innovative approaches to address key drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, sub-optional childcare and feeding practices, morbidity, and lack of safe water and sanitation.
The objectives of the proposed project: CO 1) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2025. CO 2) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW in prioritized locations by the end of 2025, CO 3) To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2025, CO 4) To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2025.
Medicair will strengthen services and scale up the quality and coverage for community-based management of malnutrition (CMAM) by extending services to hard-to-reach areas and underserved communities. Medicair will support 8 static OTP/TSFP sites integrated into health facilities across the targeted counties. The project will target 10,250 SAM and MAM (U5 and PBWs) as direct beneficiaries. 5,364 children will receive Vitamin A supplements and 4,508 deworming tablets. 3,600 caregivers of children will be reached through Maternal and Infant Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) programs in outpatient. This project will establish 4 new outreach units in these counties to provide outreach nutrition services to communities with limited access to services due to displacement in Rubkona, Aweil East, Aweil North, Uror and Akobo Counties. A gender-targeted and nutrition-sensitive service delivery approach will be used, and nutrition services will be integrated with WASH, health, gender and FSL programming. Community awareness and feedback mechanisms will enhance Accountability to affected populations.

1. Needs

South Sudan is currently faced with myriads of challenges including insecurity, flooding, disease outbreaks like cholera, and high influx of refugees. According to IPC Sept 2024 - Jul 2025, in the second projection period of April to July 2025, the reports show it is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people 57% will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in phase 4, and 63,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). An estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and Upper Nile, UnityAccording to FSNMS Round 30 and SMART surveys' findings, the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, with 2.1 million children aged 6 – 59 months at risk of suffering from acute malnutrition and 1.1 million PBW at risk of acute malnutrition in 2025 and will need urgent treatment with Rubkona, Uror and Aweil East are listed as counties prone to annual flooding and currently are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, while Rubkona remains the county of concern in Unity state given the protracted vulnerability of most households through to July 2025. Aweil North is classified as IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical) and where the second projection shows deterioration. Commodity flows will improve but purchasing power will likely remain low due to lack of income opportunities. With Akobo County remaining classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. The recent FSNMS report shows Uror GAM rate of 23.7% were reported to have been ill due to diarrhoea, fever, cough, pneumonia, malaria among other diseases.30.2 % of HHs are reported to have poor food consumption and household dietary diversity score falls at 22.5% of HHs experiencing severe hunger. Between March 18 and22, 2024, a SMART survey was conducted in Aweil North shows a GAM rate of 26.1%. FEWS NET Oct 2024 show Rubkona of Unity has high levels of displacement from current severe flooding and from prior conflicts and/or flooding. According to OCHA, as of October 18, 2024, about 36,000 people in Rubkona were affected by floods, though reports suggest these figures could be an underestimation. Although conflict has been relatively low since mid-June 2024, tension remains high in Rubkona due to the influx of returnees from Sudan. High disease burden is observed in all the counties (FSNMS 2024) with Malaria prevalence mostly as high as 40% and above and highest at 84 % in Uror County in Jonglei State. Diarrhea reported prevalence of above 30% in majority of the hotspot counties with IPC Phase 3. Low immunization has the potential to aggravate disease and negate the impact of nutrition interventions provided thus resulting in recurrent acute malnutrition despite improved food security with Uror indicating 45%. FEWS NET Oct 2024- May 2025, indicates Aweil East and North which hosts large numbers of South Sudanese returnees and Sudanese refugees, and where conflict and flooding are interfering with livelihoods, trade, and market functionality. Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) is present among returnee households in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, as well as conflict-displaced households who lost their livestock and missed the cultivation period due to persistent conflict. Medicair currently supports 2 static nutrition sites in Rubkona County under SSHF and despite the improved quality of nutrition care and robust coverage of CMAM services, a very high concern of the nutritional situation is attributed to food insecurity, sub-optimal feeding

2. Project activities

The overall program plans to support the below emergency nutrition activities covering nutrition specific interventions, nutrition-sensitive interventions and strengthening the enabling environment for nutrition in line with the cluster objectives,CO1; Medicair will: i) provide nutrition counselling to primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months who received Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition counselling ii) Conduct cooking demonstration sessions at both the facility and community levels iii) Support Vit A supplementation to girls and boys aged 6-59 months during semester 1 & 2 iv) and Support deworming using albendazole for children aged 12-59 months in semester 1 &2, vi) support the formation of MtMSG and Father Support Groups CO2: Medicair will i) Provide nutrition screening for acute malnutrition to boys and girls aged 6-59 months including PBWs through CNVs in the community and Nutrition Workers at the nutrition site ii) Admit and provide SAM treatment to children aged 6-59 months who are identified as severely malnutrition iii) admit U5 children and with MAM for treatment at health and nutrition sites iv) Provide treatment to pregnant and breastfeeding women who are identified as having acute malnutrition vi) Conduct programmatic visits to nutrition sites and reports submitted by members nutrition manager/coordinator from County Office vi) provide on job training and mentorship to nutrition staff at the health facility. CO3: Medicair will: i) Conduct MYICN Barrier Analysis ii) Disseminate quarterly nutrition data dashboards at state level iii) Support reporting to NIS and 5Ws. CO4: Medicair will i) Distribute LLITN to girls and boys admitted with acute malnutrition in nutrition sites to prevent malaria ii) Distribute LLITN to PLW admitted with acute malnutrition in nutrition sites for malaria prevention iii) We will provide WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites, sensitize households on the correct use of aqua tabs through the CNVs, sensitize communities in the priority counties with hygiene and sanitation messages, iv) Integrate nutrition activities with other clusters like WASH, Health, FSL and GBV.
