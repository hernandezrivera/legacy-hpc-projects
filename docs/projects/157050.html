Project Summary

International Medical Corps (IMC) is proposing the implementation of an integrated health and nutrition project in targeted health facilities in Central and West Jabal Mara Localities of Central Darfur and in Biliel locality of South Darfur serving an estimated catchment population of 450,000 affected by conflict and disaster, including internally displaced persons and host populations. International Medical Corps will provide support to health facilities, the health facilities will provide the following primary health and reproductive health services: outpatient consultations, EPI, disease surveillance, reproductive health services (antenatal care (ANC), postnatal care (PNC), family planning and delivery care) and integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI). Health education will also be a component of the project. This project will support referrals of patients to the next level of health care. Furthermore, IMC will continue to strengthen basic obstetric services in targeted clinics, as these are the only options for women that require reproductive health services in the camps. IMC will provide support Ministry of Health in epidemic disease surveillance, outbreak response and immunization campaigns. During this proposed project, IMC is also planning to conduct minor rehabilitation on targeted clinics to ensure quality, patient safety, infection prevention and continuity of services. IMC also intends to continue to provide short-term and on the job training to health facility staff. Drugs and medical supplies will also be procured and delivered to the clinics.International Medical Corps’ integrated nutrition intervention will focus on CMAM. The components of CMAM include community outreach and active case finding, defaulter tracing, and mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) screening of children aged 6-59 months, and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW). Children aged 6-59 months will be screened for malnutrition at the health facility. Nutrition workers/CHWs will collaborate with PHC staff to ensure coordination of care and referrals to health services and/or nutrition services. By fully integrating the nutrition treatment into the primary healthcare response, International Medical Corps provides a comprehensive package of services to beneficiaries.
This project will treat children aged 6-59 months and PLWs with MAM at 16 SFP sites. Children with MAM will be enrolled and receive a medical and nutritional check-up,
In addition to the activities proposed above, International Medical Corps will provide life-saving services to eligible pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children (6-59 months of age) with moderate acute malnutrition through SFPs programs at health facilities. The health facilities will also provide treatment for children (6-59 months) with severe acute malnutrition (SAM). All targeted health facilities will have OTPs and SFPs. Those cases with severe acute malnutrition and medical complication will be referred to stabilization centers. International Medical Corps will support also SCs at targeted locations. Nutrition education and counseling will be provided for caretakers of children under-two years of age as a component of infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices. Selected training will be provided for health facility staff on CMAM, IYCF and Monitoring and reporting. Community screenings, defaulter tracing and necessary follow-up will also be part of the intervention. International Medical Corps has the required operational structure and technical capacity to deliver, implement and monitor this project in Central Darfur and South Darfur State. International Medical Corps will also coordinates with communities, agencies and government authorities to enhance the performance of this project. This project expects to reach a total of 450,000 direct beneficiaries. These beneficiaries include women, men, girls and boys who are eligible for medical and nutrition services.

Needs

IMC used information collected from the state and federal Ministries of Health, implementing partners, community partners and relevant primary and secondary health data and information. IMC prioritized localities as per the national priorities described in the SHF allocation document. The health systems in Darfur remains weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic life saving health services and respond to outbreaks. In Central Darfur, it is reported that 26% of the health facilities are not functional. Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) coverage in health facilities is only 30% in Central Darfur. Only 40% of clinics in Central Darfur States provide Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI) services. The coverage of measles vaccination is only 55.9% while only 38.3% of children are fully vaccinated. Only 54% of the clinical OPD cases are managed according to the protocol despite training and supportive supervisions, which signals the need for strengthening support and follow up to ensure adherence to protocols. Detection and referral of pregnant mothers is weak because of low coverage of community outreach activities. The clinics are not yet fully staffed and still lack the necessary equipment and physical structure to provide comprehensive health services. Water supply, medical waste management and infection control practices are identified as a gap in the clinics. The community outreach activities need expansion to more villages. Staff need to be trained as they are relatively new to the areas for intervention. . Dispensaries do not have enough shelves and storage capacity. Health workers in targeted clinics need training on IMCI which is an important skill for better treatment of childhood diseases. According to the Ministry of Health, in Sudan, some 2.2 million children suffer from malnutrition annually (Global Acute Malnutrition) out of which over 573,000 suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition (Nutrition Sector, Bulletin, 2017). The GAM and SAM rates for Central Darfur are 17.8% and 4.3% (MICS 2014), above the critical thresholds of more than 15% GAM. In February, 2017, A SMART survey in Guildo found crude mortality and under five mortality rates (per 10000/day) 2.43 and 4.32 respectively, while the GAM and SAM rates were 15.8% and 5.4%. Exclusive breastfeeding rates remain low at 44.5% in Central Darfur. Complementary foods were introduced in timely fashion for 54.1% of children 6-8 months of age. In a survey conducted in El Serif, exclusive feeding was reported in 42.7%. In Guildo, as per 2017 SMART survey, the exclusive feeding rate was only 35.7% . In South and Central Darfur, it is reported that 28% and 26% of the health facilities are not functional. Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) coverage in health facilities is only provided in 25% in South Darfur. Only 30% of the clinics in South State are providing Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI) services.

Activities

Deliver minimum basic package of primary health care services (including maternal and child health) and support referral to secondary health care: Support and conduct routine or acceleration interventions for immunization. Expand and maintain disease surveillance system with early warning component.Procurement, storage and distribution of drugs and medical supplies. Conduct health education training for health staff. Conduct awarereness / orientation sessions at the health facility for the community. Provision of CMAM services for SAM and MAM cases of U5 children and PLW (incl. refurbishment of OTP/TSFP, provision of RUTF/SUTF, MUAC screening, referral service for SAM with complication case etc.). Conduct training for nutrition workers, community volunteer on CMAM, IYCF etc.Conduct community awareness campaign on CMAM, IYCF etc. Establish mother support group for promotion of IYCF
