Project Summary

Past successive drought seasons and consecutive seasons of below average (Deyr 2020 and Gu 2021) rains and flooding coupled by serious conflicts and insecurity and effects desert locust invasion that negatively impacted to their crop production and impacted to sharp increase in food gaps and weakened the already precarious food security situation and aggravated malnutrition rates among boys and girls aged under 5 years and PLWs in Hobyo and South Galkayo IDP camps. The situation is worsened by current serious drought conditions and projected likelihood of below-average rainfall during the 2021 Deyr season which will adversely affect food security and nutrition outcomes and high risk drought conditions by early to mid- 2022. (FSNAU-FEWS NET 2021 Post Gu Technical Release of September 9, 2021). Nutrition situation in South Galkayo IDP camps remains high with GAM 14.6% and SAM 2.3% (Post Gu).
The situation is further compounded by limited and poorly distributed nutrition and health centers owing to long distance covered to access the services coupled by poor breast feeding patterns among pregnant lactating women (PLWs),poor diet diversity and complimentary feeding among boys and girls aged 6-59 months as well as limited access to safe water and poor hygiene conditions and practices attributed to frequent cases of water borne diseases that worsen already precarious malnutrition status of Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs) and boys and girls aged 6-59 months. The worst affected are IDPs and very vulnerable host communities living under poor conditions and rely on marginal and diminishing livelihood strategies having lost all their livelihood assets to the current climatic and social economic shocks. Nutrition analysis in the project targeted locations showed an identical malnutrition rates of boys and girls at the rate of 50-50% with no clear cut difference in terms of their age as well as Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs) hence need to prevent further escalation.
The project targets Severely acute and Moderate malnourished 6382 children (3130 boys and 3130 girls) aged 6-59 months and 3010 Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs). Of particular concern are women, girls and people with disabilities that are further affected common cases of violations and discrimination when accessing humanitarian assistance hence need for their increased protection and improved consultation and inclusion of people with disabilities in the project planning phase as well as other subsequent implementation stages including monitoring to ensure that their needs are covered and that they equally benefit from the project. The project is aimed at providing equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (6-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases as well as Strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups (marginalised women, girls and children as well as people with disabilities) focusing on improving nutritional status through provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency and micronutrient interventions through the most the most appropriate feeding and nutrition treatment and ensure evidence based Basic Nutrition Services Package (BNSP) linked to Nutrition, Health, WASH, Food security and protection in the targeted locations in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts, Mudud Region Somalia

1. - Projects activities

1. Screening of 6382 children (3190 boys and 3192 girls) aged 6-59 months and 3010 PLWs through MUAC and Weight to height measures and admission and treatment through OTP/SFP programme at the 8 nutrition sites both fixed and mobile in South Galkayo and Hobyo District.2. Promoting IYCF activities at the 8 nutrition centers both fixed and mobile with particular emphasis to exclusive breastfeeding and complementary feeding targeting 3010PLWs in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts, Mudug Region, Somalia 3. Prevention and management of common diseases (anemia, diarrhea, malaria etc) by provision of multiple micro nutrients, supplements, iron/folate supplements to the targeted 3010 PLWs and Vitamin A supplementation, deworming,multiple micro nutrients supplements to the targeted 6382 children (3190 boys and 3190 girls) at the 8 nutrition sites in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts, Mudug Region, Somalia5. Dissemination of key hygiene promotion messages and practices to the targeted pregnant Lactating Women in the 8 project locations in South Galkayo and Hobyo District, Mudug Region, Somalia 6. Training of 40 OTP/SFP staff (20 men and 20 women) on case detection through MUAC measurements and weight to measures in the project locations in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts, Mudug Region, Somalia 7. Training of 60 Community health workers (30 female and 30 male) and volunteers on nutrition education and to enhance their knowledge in malnutrition to mitigate increasing malnutrition cases among children aged 8-59 months and and PLWs at the project locations in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts, Mudug Region, Somalia9. Conducting 12 Community education sessions for 120 community nutrition volunteers (60 female and 60 male) to increase awareness on malnutrition at the project locations in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts, Mudug Region, Somalia
