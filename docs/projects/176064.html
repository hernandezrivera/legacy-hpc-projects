Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face crises, including violent conflict, insecurity, flooding, and now Coronavirus (COVID-19), all of which have contributed to a protracted humanitarian crisis.
The government closed all learning institutions in March 21, 2020 as a COVID-19 mitigation and containment measure. In late 2020, schools were only opened for Grade 8 and Secondary 4 classes, while all other classes are scheduled to resume in April 5, 2021. By that time, students will have been at home for a full year, isolated from academic, social and emotional growth opportunities. For many, distance learning is severely hampered by limited access to alternative learning platforms, as well as the lack of books, guidance and materials for home-based learning. Some 2.2 million children were out of school before COVID-19 for a range of reasons, including damaged and closed schools, conflict and underlying protection issues such as child marriage, early and unwanted pregnancies, child labor and other grave violations of their rights. During the pandemic it has increased to 2.4m (HNO SSD 2021). An estimated 3.4 million (51 per cent girls) children are further in need of education services due to the pandemic-related school closures.
Education in Emergencies in South Sudan is now exacerbated due to COVID-19 and increased vulnerabilities to school children has been documented. Cases of child labour, early pregnancy, unwanted pregnancy, forced marriage and poor access to learning materials has been highlighted. Accessibility to teaching support during the COVID-19 pandemic has been limited to urban and peri-urban areas only with poor coverage to children in hard to reach context thus, widening the already existing gap in access to quality education. More girls than boys have been greatly affected and bear the full brunt of the pandemic.
The interruption of education activities will have both short and long term consequences for the stability and development of South Sudan as children who are not in school will lack the structure and stimulation for healthy cognitive, social, and emotional development. The longer children stay out of school, the more likely it is that they are recruited into armed groups.
Despite a 10% increase in national enrolment rates compared to 2017, children missing out on education, due to supply and demand barriers. SCI proposed to reach 60,260 children (3-17 years; 45 % girls) through pre-primary, primary, ALP and youth programming (soft skills), Psycho social First Aid (PFA) to address trauma, and distress; train 1205 teachers (trainings on pedagogy, learner centered, Covid awareness, PSS) and 1326 SMCs/PTAs and local education authorities (training on school management, monitoring, school based protection, referrals pathways, Covid prevention, Back to School campaign). SCI’s interventions aim to provide access to quality and inclusive education to the conflict/crisis affected communities and where possible defining linkages with other actors supporting long-term education interventions

1 - Needs

The education system and infrastructure in South Sudan are fragile. Years of conflict, displacement, insecurity and economic challenges have exacerbated the education needs in the country. Some 60 per cent of primary and secondary schools and classrooms are either partially or completely damaged. Insecurity has been the major cause behind school closure in recent years, with 20 per cent of schools non-functional. Many schools have inadequate or no basic teaching, learning and recreational supplies. Delayed or lack of payments to teachers has also affected services by causing high employee turnover. Furthermore, from February to December 2020, some 430 schools have been affected and damaged by floods and by violence and 185 of these schools have been and are still occupied by IDPs (HNO SSD 2021) in Jonglei. Due to the COVID-19, the government has closed down all learning institutions in the country as a mitigation/containment measure, and has only opened only for Grade 8 and Secondary 4 classes finalists to resume while the other classes are set to resume back in April 2021 at which point learners would have been at home for a full year with distance/remote learning severely hindered by limited mobile and radio coverage for alternative learning platforms, as well as lack of access to text books and other learning materials in home-based learning. SCI SS field report from Central Equatoria, Upper Nile and NBeG conducted in 2018 and 2019, which were based on FGD with adolescent girls in primary schools, has shown that most of the abuses and early pregnancies in schools are attributable to teachers, as adolescent girls have reported. Teachers training on PSEA, school based GBV supports the prevention and management of GBV and to streamline the referral pathways in child rights abuse and violation cases. In a country where children (age: 3-17) represent 44% of the population, they receive the greatest and immediate impact and long-term effects from crisis, violence and displacement. The interruption of education activities will have both short and long term consequences for the stability and development of South Sudan as children who are not in school will lack the structure and stimulation for healthy cognitive, social, and emotional development. The longer children stay out of school, the more likely it is that they are recruited into armed groups.There are significant issues related to the quality of education. Schools in rural and remote areas do not have enough teachers to meet the needs of students. Teacher turnover is high due to salary issues, and a limited monitoring and evaluation system in place to assess the quality of teachers. In South Sudan, only 41% of teachers are qualified and majority of the teachers are untrained-volunteer teachers that requires a lot of capacity enhancement in the areas of curriculum delivery, basic pedagogy, life skills, classroom management, child protection to facilitate effective learning. Most teachers lack or inadequate knowledge of basic skills in addressing school based GBV. As well as interrupting educational progress, the school closures also limit access to essential services such as school feeding and health and nutrition programmes; information on disease prevention; and access to clean water and sanitation services. Girls are among the most vulnerable group. The combination of being out of school and the loss of family livelihoods caused by COVID-19 can leave girls especially vulnerable to different forms of GBV including sexual violence, unwanted pregnancy and early marriages.COVID 19 presents an unprecedented challenge to the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan. The country is already facing range of humanitarian emergencies from protracted conflict to economic instability and severe food insecurity situation. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, nearly 8.3 million people (54% children, age <17, and girls 26%) are in need of humanitarian assistance

2 - Project activities

SCI will enhance children literacy and numeracy abilities; provide capacity support to teachers and facilitators on basic pedagogy (teaching methodology), strengthen the capacity of the existing school management committees (SMC) and parents’ teachers association (PTAs) to support effective and efficient school management; enhance capacity for the government education officials, support safe back to schools (B2S) campaign, introduce and install COVID-19 preventive mechanism, life skills and conflict sensitive education and peace education to learners and teachers. Through this plan, SCI will be making contributions to the Ministry of Education’s 2022 four priority targets of: i. Teacher education and professional development, ii. Support to schools and girls’ education, iii. Development of education system with a focus on literacy, numeracy and education for hard to reach children, and iv. Capacity enhancement of local education officials. The project will also contribute to the Education Cluster’s HRP 2021 and Sustainable Development Goal 4. In addition to these standard interventions and renewed mandate, SCI will ensure that schools serve as a centre of excellence to contribute to the response to protection, WASH, PSS and streamline the referral pathways in child rights abuse and violation cases. through an integrated in “one-in-a-school complete package”, which established schools as a centre of excellence where multiple services can be provided, such as education, WASH and peace education.During the school closures, SCI will re-prioritized interventions to facilitate distance learning through radio teaching and other alternative programmes including HBL if schools reopen delay. At the same time, SCI also prioritized activities include repair/renovation of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS), provision of school supplies, school WASH services, and the short orientation/training of teachers and community members on Covid-19 prevention awareness and B2S campaign. This response plan will also take adequate measures while government reopen education institutions. The measures include but not limit to the SCI’s SB2S plan, educators’ professional development, replenishment of teaching-learning (T-L) materials, remedial support, stakeholders’ engagement, strengthening system and so on. SCI will also focus on risk mitigation and protection of school children from the impact of COVID-19 through developing messaging and awareness-raising campaigns.However, the response plan will ensure a safe return to quality learning for learners, teachers and the community through preparedness activities such as a back to school campaign, school kit distributions and hygiene services in school. Some safety, protection and psychosocial support activities will also be organized to ensure the safety and protection of children focusing girls in and way-around to schools. Support school’s authorities to repair-renovation support of existing infrastructure including WASH facilities. This is to be mentioned that In South Sudan, 17 per cent of primary schools do not have access to drinking water while 31 per cent do not have functional latrines . Support local education officials on improved monitoring and evaluation system in place to assess the quality of teachers/facilitators. Project will liaison with the education cluster to get messages and information on sexual and reproductive health, menstrual hygiene management (MHM), gender and GBV risks when new materials are being developed in as part of response to COVID19—for both boys and girls, as appropriate.SCI is already implementing activities aimed at preventing the spread and transmission of COVID-19 in communities through teachers and school committees. After having additional funding, project will be able to rapidly establish or scale up COVID-19 related education activities as a result of having a long-established presence and programming in schools and communities throughout the impact area.

3 - Project indicators

SCI will develop an Indicator Performance Tracking Tool (IPTT), capturing all the indicators defined in the project design. These indicators will be tracked against the agreed targets and reported on a monthly basis.Routine field monitoring shall be conducted, and monitoring findings shall be acted upon by the project team, however, on these findings and actions taken will be consolidated and learnings ploughed back to inform programming. Similarly, findings from the complaints and feedback mechanisms shall be categorized, and managed in line with the SCI standard protocol. In addition, the MEAL team will conduct post distribution monitoring surveys, in which a representative number of the beneficiaries will be assessed at most four weeks after completion of the response. Further to this, the PDM will act as a triangulation for the distribution to ensure the right beneficiaries received the right items, the distributed items were utilized appropriately and also to inform future distributions.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

In the community engagement process, SCI will organize and conduct different sorts of awareness raising activities with the communities. Beneficiaries in the community will receive brief training on the PSEA in a systematic manner. Designing Safe Programmes: The SCI with the beneficiaries systematically identifies and addresses SEA related risks. Describe the main PSEA and safeguarding risks present in the context, taking into account gender, persons with disabilities, and mental health and psychosocial supportAwareness Raising of Beneficiaries: SCI ensures that beneficiaries are aware of the organization’s zero-tolerance policy on SEA and how to report allegations. Describe the PSEA and how safeguarding mechanisms will ensure sensitivity to age, gender and diversity and accessibility to women and girls, children, persons with disabilities and others at high risk (e.g. ethnic or religious minorities, LGBTIQ+, elderly)Reporting: SCI has set up clear procedures for personnel and beneficiaries to report SEA allegations that comply with core standards for reporting (i.e. safety, confidentiality, transparency, accessibility). Describe how security concerns will be identified, addressed and monitored for particular vulnerable groups.Assistance and Referrals: SCI has a functioning system to ensure survivors of SEA allegedly perpetrated by their personnel receive immediate professional assistance, by providing them with direct services or referring them to relevant service providersInvestigations: SCI has an established process and plan for ensuring credible, external investigations into SEA allegations involving its personnel.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SCI strongly believes that accountability to affected population creates an opportunity for community members to influence relevant decisions affecting them, and how SCI consults and work with them. To put it in practice, SCI will ensure a regular, timely and accessible information sharing, discussing together, deciding together and working together, and proactively seeking feedback from children, youth, women and communities and handling their complaints, and building staff competencies for accountable programming. SCI has developed a communication with communities procedures, identified ways to communicate with various target groups, and will apply this during this implementation. The communication procedures will be adapted and modified based on the changing context and feedback from communities. Through Focus Group discussions (FGD) with beneficiaries and community peoples during the implementation phase, beneficiary feedback, regarding the response will be sought throughout the project, and will make necessary changes and adaptations that will then be communicated back to the communities. Groups such as children, youth, women, adolescent girls and boys will be engaged in mixed, and age and sex-disaggregated manner and interviews will be conducted to ensure that all voices are represented, and people can raise their priorities in a safe way. Community members will be encouraged to raise their issues through engagement in project activities, active participation in FGD meetings, and cell phone call to a hotline and feedback and information centres. SCI recognizes that Child-Friendly feedback mechanisms play a key role in strengthening reporting mechanisms for the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and in safeguarding children and young people. SCI will use its feedback mechanism guidelines and ensure safe and accessible system are provided to make complaints, provide feedback and report cases of mismanagement, misconduct and/or sexual exploitation or abuse. Throughout the implementation, SCI will consult with communities, beneficiaries and local authorities to ensure community voices are heard and tensions mitigated amongst different groups to ensure conflict sensitive programming. SCI will strictly follow its safeguarding policy with regard to internal report/complaint handling including investigation procedures for sensitive reports/complaints which are survivor centred.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Save the Children recognizes the right of beneficiaries and community members to give feedback and seek response from projects affecting them. In this response, the Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will deliver a mechanism that enables the beneficiaries and community members to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of this response and other development actors in their communities, in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible. The initiative will promote beneficiary accountability and feedback and response mechanism to other development actors. Consultative meetings will held with the learners, communities and local leaders. The beneficiaries and community members will be encouraged to give feedback and seek responses from the projects affecting them. They will be sensitized right from the start of the project through kick off meetings and community consultation workshops on key deliverable’s including community roles and responsibilities

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will use the feedback from the beneficiaries to improve on service delivery, address the the concerns and ensure that rights of beneficiaries is protected. SCI will work with beneficiaries in coming up with appropriate and local solutions to address the concerns raised. SCI will use the feedback from the beneficiaries in re-designing program delivery approach and strategy.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

SCI is a neutral organization in humanitarian responses. SCI will ensure that all groups regardless of age, sex, ethnicity, disability are consulted by ensuring groups are representative of men, and women, boys and girls, community leaders. - Exclusion of women and girls from consultations could impact education indicators i.e. construction of learning spaces, latrines, teacher recruitment etc. SCI will ensure that PTA/SMC bodies have adequate representation of women, and they are trained on girl’s education rights, and understand importance of female participation for community development. - Youth make up half of the country’s population, SCI will ensure that in all of its education interventions, youth groups/their representatives are consulted, and their opinion factored in the decision making through, establishing youth focal points in consultations with local community leaders. SCI will also sensitize the local communities towards importance of meaningful youth engagement to prevent any conflict/violence in the community. - SCI will adopt two pronged approach towards child protection and safe guarding i.e. school based child protection helpdesks, and community led and driven protection mechanisms. SCI will train identified members to manage and respond to child safe-guarding and protection concerns i.e. referrals pathways and access to dignified and safe services. - SCI understands data protection concerns and challenges, especially in a complex context of South Sudan. SCI programme data is protected and follows organization’s strict data protection protocols. - Conflict sensitivity is a critical element of programming for SCI, it will ensure that its approaches towards implementation do not put any group or party at risk - Sensitization of the beneficiaries on the community feedback mechanism - SCI will ensure where possible to establish the schools/centres within the communities, in case of distance mobilize communities and agree on the safe and protected .
