Project Summary

IDPs and conflict-affected populations, especially women and children, remain particularly vulnerable to the risks of human trafficking. However, the problem remains largely invisible and under-addressed due to the limited resources to assess the situation, identify, refer and assist victims. With migration traditionally being a coping mechanism for Ukrainians, conflict and economic crisis prompted additional risks and rendered the population more susceptible to unsafe (irregular) migration and trafficking.
IOM Ukraine caseload also shows that out of all trafficking survivors assisted in 2017, 90% were trafficked and exploited in the period of the conflict and respective crisis. IOM’s National Monitoring System survey published on August 2018 (Round 10) reports that eight per cent of IDPs household reported encountering at least one situation involving deceit by the employer or forced labour since the beginning of the conflict. Throughout the period from 2017 until July 2019 IOM identified and assisted more than 100 IDPs and other conflict-affected people who fell victims to human trafficking.
In order to mitigate growing risks of human trafficking, IOM aims to enhance identification and referral of victims of trafficking among the conflict-affected populations, with a particular attention to those living in proximity to the contact line, and to ensure victims´ access to comprehensive rehabilitation and reintegration assistance tailored to their individual needs. IOM will leverage protection partner networks and capacities of the state-led National Referral Mechanism in the conflict area to offer assistance in safe and confidential manner. Concurrently, IOM will carry out preventive measures raising awareness about risks of trafficking and exploitation and providing contacts of the currently operating helplines available to the vulnerable population. IOM’s activities will also include legal aid, medical care, mental health and psychosocial support, financial support, vocational training, and other types of assistance to victims of trafficking.

1 - Needs

IDPs are at high risk of human trafficking both abroad and in the country for forced labour and sexual exploitation due to their vulnerable situation. Situation monitoring and counterparts working in eastern Ukraine indicate increased prevalence of labour exploitation, sexual abuse, survival sex and sexual violence, including among the children, in the regions close to the contact line. However, these cases are under-identified and not always referred for assistance, thus widening protection gaps in the overall humanitarian response to the crisis. The aggravated situation calls for increased and proactive prevention measures to inform IDPs and conflict-affected population about human trafficking risks, as well as to strengthen identification efforts, streamlining countertrafficking actions across the humanitarian assistance and thus ensuring protection and assistance for trafficking survivors.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome: Conflict affected community members, IDPs and victims of trafficking are better informed on and protected against human trafficking Output 1: Male and female victims of trafficking and labour abuse and exploitation identified among IDPs and conflict-affected population have access to comprehensive reintegration assistance. Activities for output 1: Identify VoTs directly (proactively) and via external referrals (including through referral mechanisms established with the human rights organizations); Organize trainings for border guards and relevant state officials at the border crossing points along the contact line as part of the victim identification effort; Screen VoTs and victims of labour abuse displaced from the conflict zone and develop comprehensive individual reintegration plans; Provide comprehensive medical and psychological treatment to victims of trafficking, as needed, in a safe and confidential manner. Output 2: IDPs and other vulnerable persons are better informed on the risks of human trafficking and other forms of abuse through targeted preventive efforts and general awareness raising campaigns. Activities for output 2: Outreach to IDPs, including from Crimea, conflict affected population and other vulnerable persons through information sessions and other targeted prevention efforts; Advertise the Counter trafficking hotline to IDPs and make it accessible to vulnerable population; Run the Counter Trafficking Hotline to provide consultations to IDPs and refer them to assistance if needed. The project aims to identify and provide needs-based reintegration assistance to male and female victims of trafficking, particularly sensitive to the needs of children. Information dissemination will also be targeted to vulnerable children and youth, both male and female, to improve access to trafficking prevention information and services. The project’s approach to gender is underpinned by the recognition that the phenomenon of human trafficking is a violation of human rights, rather than a criminal activity suffered by women or men, girls or boys. Equal access to all project activities is provided to all beneficiaries independently of their sex. At the same time, a rights based and gender sensitive approach is applied in assessing the needs of beneficiaries and the provision of rehabilitation and reintegration services to them. Sex diseggregated data is collected for all activities. IOM will engage in face to face interviews with beneficiaries (VOTs among IDPs and conflict-affected population) during the screening to collect information that will be used to design and develop needs-based reintegration assistance. After providing reintegration assistance, IOM continues monitoring of beneficiaries to take into account their concerns, opinions and advice and where possible structure the response according to their needs in particular resulting 1) enhanced accountability through better understanding of the purpose of the reintegration support, 2) guidance of reintegration supports on inclusivity, and 3) smooth reintegration process in collaboration with the local partners and NGOs. Thus, IOM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries from the project design and planning phase, project implementation phase and monitoring evaluation phase. Project monitoring and evaluation tools will be designed and focus on the relevance, efficiency, effectiveness, impact and sustainability of project activities and results.
