Project Summary

The project aims to mitigate the exacerbating impact of COVID-19 on human trafficking exploitation and abuse among vulnerable population in Eastern Conflict Area of Ukraine (ECA) by providing holistic and tailored social protection assistance to community members (including men, women and children) and though carrying out prevention activities.
The severe decrease of economic opportunities in Ukraine and ECA specifically due to COVID-19 pandemic in addition to the protracted conflict, compounded by ramping unemployment rates, scarcely efficient social services and precarious social safety-nets, had already increased the strain on the most vulnerable groups within displaced and host communities, who were often left with no choice but resort to negative coping mechanisms. As a result, the most fragile social segments are left vulnerable to drivers of instability and to protection risks including vulnerability to trafficking in persons, exploitation and abuse – as well as more prone to resort to undesired migration and/or other negative coping strategies. Furthermore, not all types of protection and assistance needed by VoTs returning to Ukraine or (potentially) exploited in the country are available from the State.
In order to mitigate growing risks of human trafficking, exploitation and abuse in ECA, IOM aims to ensure access of vulnerable population to comprehensive rehabilitation and reintegration assistance tailored to individual needs of beneficiaries. IOM will leverage protection partner networks and capacities of the state-led National Referral Mechanism in the conflict area to offer assistance in safe and confidential manner. IOM’s activities include legal aid, medical care, mental health and psychosocial support, financial support, vocational training, and other types of assistance to (potential) victims of trafficking, exploitation and abuse. Concurrently, IOM will carry out preventive measures raising awareness about risks of trafficking and exploitation and providing contacts of the currently operating hotlines , which provide information on safe migration and employment as well as providing further referrals to protection assistance for vulnerable population. Furthermore IOM share information on regular/safe migration and employment, prevention of trafficking and exploitation within youth, unemployed, newly graduates from vocational and technical schools etc.

1 - Needs

Over the six years of prolonged conflict in eastern Ukraine, widespread fraudulent recruitment schemes, financial desperation and other risk factors including the recent COVID-19 pandemic make certain adults and minors more susceptible to unsafe and irregular migration and falling prey to traffickers. Before the COVID-19 pandemic, most VoTs and returned migrants assisted by IOM Ukraine were successfully reintegrated into society and employed in different spheres. However, with the current COVID-19 outbreak, most of them are now without any income or any source of livelihood, given that they are mostly self-employed or just opening their own businesses. Nearly 300,000 migrant workers have returned to Ukraine due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Absence of work opportunities puts former migrant workers at risk of destitution and inability to pay for basic needs. With the loss of income as a result of the COVID-19 outbreak, VoTs and their families are already pushed into extremely vulnerable or exploitative situations. The IOM rapid assessment conducted in June 2020 shows that 50.3 per cent of surveyed VoTs (66.7% men, 33.3 % women) lost their income, and 84.2 percent of respondents reported their financial situation has worsened due to the quarantine restrictions. The protection of VoTs is also impacted by the pandemic, especially when they experienced pre-existing socioeconomic difficulties. They have higher risks of re-exploitation when they cannot benefit from assistance and care because of the suspension of services. Referral pathways – such as those for victims of domestic violence, victims of trafficking, and vulnerable children – are likely to face disruptions, leading to interruptions in case identification, referral, and protection processes resulting in immediate protection gaps for vulnerable migrants who remain unidentified and/or unassisted, and longer-term weakening of referral pathways and protection systems. The results of IOM Study on Human Trafficking in the Context of Armed Conflict in Ukraine (2019) identified the most vulnerable population to trafficking situations; those are internally displaced (IDPs) and persons living within 20 km on either side of the contact line, especially women with children, persons with disabilities or physical injuries, chronic conditions and serious health issues (including mental health issues), elderly people, persons facing socioeconomic difficulties, children and youth living near the contact line, as well as national minorities. According to the recent National Monitoring System survey conducted by IOM, four per cent of surveyed IDPs reported that they encountered situations involving deceit by employer or forced labour since the beginning of the conflict. Three percent of respondents worked without getting expected payment and another three percent worked in conditions that were significantly worse than promised. From January to October 2020, IOM Ukraine assisted over 1,350 VOTs, which is equivalent to the number of VoTs identified in in the entire 2019, highlighting a worryingly increasing trend. IOM increasingly assisted VoTs among conflict-affected population, including 79 VoTs identified in ECA, 44 VoTs were identified among IDPs (20 in 2018), and 65 IDPs were identified and assisted as persons in high risk of trafficking (22 in 2018). In total, IOM Ukraine identified 124 individuals at high risk of human trafficking, among them 37 minors (3–18 years old). At the same time, larger categories of beneficiaries in ECA being in high risk of being exposed to trafficking, violence and abuse, are extremely vulnerable and hence need same scope of individual reintegration services as provided to VoTs. The above-mentioned aggravated situation calls for increased protection assistance for (potential) victims of trafficking, exploitation and abuse, as well as proactive prevention measures to inform vulnerable population about human trafficking risks and regular/safe migration.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1: Affected community members and population at-risk are better protected against and informed on human trafficking, exploitation and abuseOutput 1: Holistic and tailored social protection assistance is available to the most vulnerable individuals in targeted communities Activities for Output 1:• Identify VoTs and most vulnerable population at-risk directly (proactively) and via external referrals (including through referral mechanisms established with the human rights organizations);• Screen VoTs and most vulnerable population at-risk and develop comprehensive individual reintegration plans;• Provide comprehensive reintegration assistance, incl. medical and psychological treatment to victims of trafficking, as needed, in a safe and confidential manner *This activity will provide holistic and tailored protection assistance to 400 identified community members in ECA (including men, women and children) who are most at risk, or survivors of trafficking in persons, exploitation and abuse. The assistance will be provided at the individual and/or household levels, in line with IOM’s beneficiary-centered approach based on the principles of confidentiality, safety, and informed consent. Each individual will receive support based on their specific needs and circumstances, which will be assessed by case managers (from IOM or long-time partner NGOs). Tailored assistance may include, among others, household support to address food insecurity, an installation grant to cover rent of living premises for families at threat of eviction, additional protection services for victims of trafficking, exploitation and abuse, including referral to IOM’s Medical Rehabilitation Centre and possibly relevant Government-run facilities (shelters, care centers for women and children). Mental health and psychosocial support will be extended to all beneficiaries, as needed, to help them overcome traumas and additional stress induced by the COVID-19 pandemic. Output 2: Information on risks of human trafficking and other forms of exploitation and abuse, and on safe migration pathways are available at the local level for all population Activities for Output 2:• Conduct trafficking prevention information sessions online targeting 5,000 vulnerable persons in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (particularly youth, IDPs and conflict-affected population); • Promote counter-trafficking online quest to raise awareness of vulnerable youth of Donetsk and Lugansk regions on safe employment and increase identification of possible VoTs and population at-risk.• Conduct general trafficking awareness-raising campaign in Donetsk and Luhansk regions to inform vulnerable persons about safe migration and employment;• Advertise the National Counter-Trafficking and Migrant Advice Hotline to IDPs and conflict-affected population and make it accessible to people at-risk; * Equal access to all project activities is provided to all beneficiaries independently of their sex, a rights-based and gender sensitive approach is applied in assessing the needs of beneficiaries and the provision of reintegration services. Sex disaggregated data is collected for all activities. Project monitoring and evaluation tools will be designed and focus on the relevance, efficiency, effectiveness, impact and sustainability of project activities and results.**In accordance with the IOM’s Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) Framework, all planned assistance will be based on the beneficiary’s full and informed consent. Safety and security risks to VoTs, IOM staff members and partners will be mitigated by adhering to “do no harm” principles and IOM guidelines on risk assessment and data protection. IOM actively seeks the feedback of affected population to improve the project implementation and to identify the existing gaps to be immediately addressed. For the urgent response to the complains and suggestion on how to improve project implementation, feedback mechanism will be established.
