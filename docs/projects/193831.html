Project Summary

UNICEF, WFP, and Save the Children (SCI) are implemnting this project in the aim of increasing access to life saving health and nutrition services for the most vulnerable pregnant and lactating women, women of reproductive age (including adolescent females and women with disabilities) and children under 5 years, both boys and girls.
The project responds to one of the Health Cluster strategic objectives:
- Strategic Objective 2: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt.
This joint project is designed to address a critical gap in health and nutrition services in the West Bank (Hebron H2, Area C and East Jerusalem) and the Gaza Strip. It will address life-threatening micronutrient malnutrition among the most vulnerable women of reproductive age (including adolescent females and women with disabilities) and under five children including children with moderate and severe wasting through provision of appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counselling, food and nonfood items, essential micronutrients, and treatment services.
The project will be implemented jointly by UNICEF, WFP, and SCI and in close coordination with Health Cluster partners including WHO, UNFPA, international and local NGOs to ensure complementarity of the interventions.
UNICEF, WFP and Save the Children aims to reach the following target groups through the different targeted interventions in West Bank and Gaza:
- 36,000 pregnant and lactating women with micronutrient supplements, of which 1,000 will benefit from social behaviour change communication interventions and restricted cash-based transfer (e-vouchers)
- 40,000 children under 5 including 1,000 children with acute malnutrition (severe) (50% female) and 35 NBF infants under 6 months
- 70 health, community, and protection workers
- 200 men to benefit from awareness sessions on SRMCHN

1 - Needs

The state of Palestine is affected by a protracted humanitarian crisis. As a result, Palestinian face economic, political, market, social, or cultural barriers to providing nutritious, safe, affordable, and sustainable diets to young children, where access to nutritious food, clean drinking water, and good quality health services are limited, and the resources and capacities of caregivers are already stretched. The State of Palestine and in particular the Gaza Strip is affected by significant rate of poverty. The national poverty rate was at 29.24 percent in 2017 and rose to 31.08 percent in 2020. Covid -19 and the subsequent two escalations in 2021 and 2022 in the Gaza Strip resulted in worsening social conditions, increasing household vulnerability. According to the 2020 Socio-Economic & Food Security Survey, 31.2 percent of households in Palestine are severely or moderately food insecure with an increase of 4.2 percentage points between 2018 and 2020. The Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) published by OCHA in July 2022 showed that 70% of households in Gaza and 20.7% households in West Bank reported difficulties meeting essential food needs because they could not afford them. The 2019 assessment conducted by UNICEF, World Food Programme (WFP) and Save the Children (SC) outlined a deterioration in the nutritional status of the most vulnerable children under 5, pregnant and lactating women in Gaza. 43% of infants are exclusively breastfed, and just 45.7% of infants are still breastfed at 1 year of age. 18.5% of pregnant women and 14% of lactating mothers in the most deprived communities in Gaza are undernourished, with around 36% of PLW anemic; more than 30% of children 6-59 months are anemic, and around 22% of adolescent girls (10-19 years) are also classified as anemic. In addition, High levels of micronutrient deficiencies alongside increasing obesity rates and undernourishment co-exist. Children under the age of five are particularly vulnerable in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. According to MICS 2019-2020, 8.7% of children are moderately or severe stunted, with an increase compared to the results in 2014 (7.4%). For children aged 12–17 months the rate is 10.5%, for children aged 18–23 months the rate is the highest reaching 12.4%. Moderately and severe wasting affects 1.3% of children U5. When children have access to an adequate food supply, safe drinking water, access to health and nutrition services, are not exposed to repeated illness, and are well cared for, they reach their growth potential and are considered well-nourished. In the State of Palestine, there is a pocket of 36,400 children under 5 living in Access Restricted Areas (ARA) who are particularly vulnerable and prone to incidences of watery or bloody diarrheal disease and respiratory infections, exacerbated by the deteriorating access to safe drinking water and health services. The combined effect of poor maternal nutrition status, sub-optimal infant and young child feeding practices, child morbidity, and inadequate water and sanitation may lead to an increased risk of acute malnutrition, impaired physical growth, and cognitive development. In this joint project, UNICEF, WFP, and SCI will continue to work in close coordination with the Ministry of Health, and other health and nutrition partners to address the critical gap in health and nutrition services in the West Bank (Hebron H2, Area C and East Jerusalem) and the Gaza Strip. The project will contribute to decreasing the life-threatening micronutrient malnutrition among the most vulnerable women of reproductive age and U5 children through the provision and implementation of structured interventions including provision of infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counselling, provision of food and non-food items, provision of essential micronutrients, and support the treatment of malnutrition.

2 - Activities

The project aims to provide urgently needed essential health and nutrition services to the most vulnerable lactating and pregnant women, women of reproductive age (including adolescent females and women with disabilities) and children under 5 years in oPt, both boys and girls. The project will ensure complementarity with the WHO’s, UNFPA’s and an international NGO partner’s HRP projects. The project will achieve its desired impact through the implementation of different activities which are implemented by three agencies (UNICEF, WFP, and SCI) in full coordination and cooperation and following a complementary approach. UNICEF Activities: - Enhancing health and nutrition status and wellbeing of vulnerable women, and children from 0-6 Years through: Procurement of essential micronutrients for at least 36,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) (Gaza: 24,000; WB:12,000) and for 40,000 children aged 6 to 59 months (girls: 19,600; boys: 20,400) in Gaza and the West Bank (Gaza: 30,000; WB: 10,000) who are attending the Ministry of Health (MoH) and NGO facilities. All micronutrients will be predefined jointly with relevant authorities to ensure specifications meet the updated national nutrition maternal child and adolescent protocol. Treatment of children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) through the provision of ready-to-use therapeutic food to 1,000 children under five (490 girls, 510 boys). This will be implemented in cooperation with UNICEF NGO partners - UNICEF will continue supporting the MoH in coordination of the Nutrition technical working group in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. Jointly with partners, UNICEF will ensure implementation of the updated National MNCH Nutrition Protocol. SCI Activities - Training of 70 health workers (50 females and 20 males) from INGOs and NGOs in the 5 Gaza governorate and West Bank on:  Detection, management and treatment of acute malnutrition, micronutrient deficiencies, anemia and common childhood illnesses Essential MHPSS integrated in IYCF-E. Provision of quality IYCF counselling  Management of NBF infants under 6 months through provision of quality counseling of IYCF practices and practical re-lactation Maternal reproductive and sexual health, including antenatal, and postnatal care. - Provision of supplies in partnership with local NGOs (safer BMS kit and generically labeled infant formula) for the safe and appropriate management of 35 non-breastfed infants <6 months living in the most vulnerable families (boys: 17, girls: 18). This will be done through strict criteria of selection of NBF according to the WHO protocol and Palestinian MOH protocol. - Screening for detection of malnutrition, anemia and common childhood illnesses for 3,000 children under 5 years and admitting of 1,000 in partnership with Local NGO- Support the provision of individual counselling on maternal nutrition and IYCF to 36,000 PLW of children under 2 years of age who are receiving micronutrient supplements by UNICEF - Awareness raising on maternal reproductive and sexual health for 400 women of reproductive age, including 100 adolescent females. Adolescent females will have hygiene and dignity kits distributed to them as part of self-empowerment and improving their hygiene status. This will be completed in partnership with local NGO- Awareness raising on maternal reproductive and sexual health for 200 men- Support the creation of 40 community-based support groups with lead mothers to PLW of children under the age of 5 years of age that are receiving the WFP food voucher/cash support in partnership with local NGO- Procurement of anthropometric equipment for the clinics of SCI local partner in order to guarantee the best quality for the provision of services including screening, management, treatment and follow up for the malnourished and anemic children. In addition, some minor WASH rehabilitation for the clinics will be completed in order to sustain better hygiene. WFP Activities- Provision of U$ 20 restricted cash transfers (e-voucher) on a monthly basis for most vulnerable pregnant, lactating women and mothers of children under five years old) with high anaemia and micronutrient deficiencies. WFP will target 1,000 (600 PL women and mothers in Gaza and 400 PL women and mothers in the West Bank) out of the 36,000 PLW who are receiving UNICEF and SCI services. The voucher value will be restricted to iron -rich foods. WFP will coordinate with relevant authorities on selected hardship cases for the e-voucher and in identifying the targeted groups for IYCF counselling. - The provision of vouchers will be complemented with Behavior Change activities to increase the dietary impact of the (e-voucher). In terms of monitoring the implementation of the project, UNICEF, WFP, and SCI will oversee project monitoring and will ensure timely reporting taking the following actions:- UNICEF, WFP, SCI will work on developing different pre and post-surveys with targeted beneficiaries mainly the health and community workers. In addition, a KAP survey will be conducted by end of the project, to measure actual changes on caregivers’ knowledge and practices will be conducted to identify percentage of targeted pregnant women, mothers and caregivers (men, women) who demonstrate improved knowledge and adoption of recommended maternal and IYCF practices. - For the e-voucher intervention WFP has a process monitoring that is conducted at all contracted retail shops on a regular basis for a representative sample. WFP conducts post-distribution household monitoring (PDM) visits with a view to monitor the improvement or deterioration in the food security status of assisted people and assess the effectiveness of its interventions.- Programmatic visits and regular monitoring field visits to project sites and implementing partners will be conducted by UNICEF responsible Programme oﬃcers

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

All 3 agencies protect and uphold the rights of their beneficiaries, to receive safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries and their guardians in case of children is considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies, and community interventions. Information gathered from beneficiaries will be accessible with the beneficiary’s permission. When there is a need to share an information, it should be shared on a need-to-know basis and limited only to the information necessary to enable the beneficiary to get services. All 3 agencies will ensure applying international standards, and WHO standards for the procurement of micronutrient supplements and other supplies. Implemnting agencies have developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and to mitigate any risks identified by end users. All three agencies implement health and nutrition interventions ensuring there is a predictable, accountable, harmonized approach and understanding to health and nutrition needs, as well as the integration of common standards in line with the principles of Do No Harm. Additionally, the Do No Harm approach will ensure assistance remained principled, needs-based, and humanitarian in character. All three agencies will work with implemnting partners to ensure the principles of inclusion of children with disabilities, gender and PSEA whenever partners implement the project. The three agencies will continue through their feedback mechanisms and through implementing partners to receive, process and act upon help requests of feedbacks, complaints and will ensure to address. Monitoring visits for safety and quality of services rendered to beneficiaries WILL BE CONDUCTED.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

All 3 agencies ensure the verification of needs working with WHO and health cluster members to ensure reaching the most vulnerable communities. The three agencies will ensure that independent needs verification is completed prior to distribution of supplies, in addition verification of the completion of the delivery of supplies to their intended locations/beneficiaries will be ensured. The three agencies ensure that a multi-vulnerability targeting is considered to guarantee equity and impartial interventions are designed.Throughout the implementation of this project, the following considerations are to be ensured:• Early engagement of selected communities will be enhanced by involving communities and their representatives at all stages of project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. • Channels of communication for feedback, complaints and information sharing will be established through satisfaction survey and recording goods of services delivered to the end user, so that assistance is delivered in accordance with the needs and in adherence to the principles of “do no harm”.• PSEA awareness raising material including information on rights and safe complaint channels will be available to beneficiaries at all project sites.
