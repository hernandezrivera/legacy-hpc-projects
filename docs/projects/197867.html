Project Summary

This project aims to address the dire humanitarian needs caused by the abuses perpetrated by the terrorist sect Boko Haram (BH) in Nigeria and the Far North of Cameroon. The conflict is primarily concentrated in Nigeria with incursions into the Far North of Cameroon where, in recent years, they have also established operational bases. With tens of thousands of displaced households, and asylum seekers entering the Far North region of Cameroon, LWF began a response in 2016 to alleviate the suffering of the most vulnerable households, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees from IDP sites, refugees, and local host communities, by addressing their most urgent needs.
In this phase of the project, the response will be through an integrated approach to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) promotion, and peace and social cohesion. LWF will implement activities through a comprehensive and participatory methodology for water source rehabilitation that includes rights holders at every level of the process. LWF will also support peace building activities by establishing a complaints management committee, setting up and training community leaders on peace and social cohesion, and organizing community awareness sessions.
The LWF will work closely with the targeted communities to ensure they improve a safe access to safe drinking water, improved sanitation and hygiene practices amongst IDPs and refugees in the localities of Mokolo and Koza. To do this, the LWF will construct latrines, and will rehabilitate non-functioning water sources and equip them with hand pumps.
Recognizing the heavy toll that the crisis has had on the displaced population and the additional pressure on resources that constitute the presence of refugees and IDPs in the region, LWF will work to strengthen social cohesion and peace among those communities. Social cohesion will take place between IDPs themselves from different communities, on the one hand, and between IDPs, refugees and host communities in the other hand.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Boko Haram attacks in the Far North region of Cameroon have become more frequent since 2013, and continued into 2022, resulting in the displacement of hundreds of people, the burning of a number of villages and farms, the abduction of girls, boys, and women, the looting of homes, and the killing of adult men.The security crisis linked to the armed conflict and the forced displacement of populations, the health crisis (COVID-19, measles, yellow fever, malaria, and cholera), and the recurrent floods exacerbate the vulnerability of communities already weakened by extreme poverty and the effects of climatic variations. Indeed, the intensification of the rains in August 2022 caused new flooding in the region. As of September 30, more than 15,330 households (88,537 people) have been affected by this phenomenon in the region (Logone et Chari, Mayo Danay, and Mayo-Tsanaga) . The impact of the flooding increased, with more than 9,410 shelters damaged or destroyed, 190 water points and 535 latrines, and 126 school structures flooded. In addition, the region is going through a cholera epidemic with 359 cases and 16 deaths as of November 12, 2022 . It should also be noted that water, which is abundant during the rainy season, seems to be a scarce commodity during the dry season in Mayo-Tsanaga . Based on these difficult situations in the region, the UNHCR report (October 2022) indicates that in the Far North region of Cameroon there are 133,749 Nigerian refugees, 377,877 internally displaced persons, and 130,690 returnees, for a total of 642,316 vulnerable persons, not including host populations living in extreme poverty and affected by climatic hazards. The humanitarian response plan estimates that $376 million is needed to reach 1.2 million people in need in the Far North . The response to the crisis is underfunded, with only 40 percent of funds acquired, leaving more than 0.72 thousand of vulnerable people in precarious conditions. If no action is taken, the humanitarian situation is likely to worsen.
