Project Summary

Despite the recent positive developments in the establishment of a transitional gov., it is expected that Sudan will continue to face severe macro-economic and political challenges. Overall, the situation remains fluid and humanitarian needs will further increase as underlying challenges are not expected to change in the immediate future. The humanitarian situation in Sudan will remain a complex mix of asylum-seekers and refugees. Sudan is also a source, transit and destination country for mixed migratory population movements. Continued small to medium scale refugee influxes from CAR, SS, Eritrea are anticipated in 2020. Sudan hosts over 900,000 ref. from over 7 countries within the region, in addition to hosting refugees asylum seekers on the move and irregular migrants as Sudan is a transit country using the East African North-bound migratory route. The eastern part of the country is the main entry point for Eritrean asylum-seekers although about 63% move on before finalization of their asylum applications. The voluntary return sustainable solutions for the issues of displaced persons and refugees” are prioritized among the “essential issues for peace negotiations” agreed upon the Constitutional Charter for the 2019 Transitional Period, signed in 17 Aug.19 agreeing on transitional arrangements for the forthcoming 39 months. The aim of the Constitutional declaration is to “end the war by addressing the roots of the Sudanese problem and handling its effects”. It envisages the completion of a fair and comprehensive peace in the Sudan no later than 6months from its signing. As of Nov.19, peace negotiations between the Gov. of Sudan armed groups are ongoing with yet to be determined outcomes. Areas of origin in Sudan have been affected by years of conflict, marginalization and neglect. They often lack basic community infrastructure essential social services, while rural livelihoods were seriously disrupted. Competition for, and pressures on, natural resources within Darfur, partly due to a history of drought and famine, compound the legacy of the conflict. Communities usually lack resources, structures governance capabilities allowing for different groups, youth, to engage collectively in peacebuilding, dialogue, public service, economic activities.
BNS. Although Sudan has not formally committed to the CRRF yet, the country has de-factor applied several components of the CRRF.
Operational priorities in 2020 are:
1. Refugee protection, with a focus on ref. registration, documentation, protection monitoring and interventions, CP
SGBV, CBP, scaling up CBIs for ref. (MP sectoral).
2. Voluntary return; durable solutions for refugees hosted by Sudan.
3. Stabilization of existing ref. populations through transition from emergency response to longer term interventions, including integration with national service systems in the spirit of the CRRF, and interim solutions for populations in Khartoum Open Areas.
4. Emergency preparedness for refugee influxes, and sudden onset emergencies (Cholera) through enhanced protection capacity, pre-positioning, the management of the Common NFI Pipeline of the Humanitarian Country Team.

1 - Needs

For SSRs in Sudan and Urban refugees, the focus areas will remain to be the provision of basic needs and essential services; mainly services related to education, water, health, ES/NFI, livelihoods, and nutrition. UNHCR will work with its partners to implement livelihoods activities that have the least financial cost but greatest social benefits benefiting both refugee and host communities. Additionally,UNHCR will enhance mainstreaming of services into national systems and inclusion of refugees into national plans; mainly education, health and WASH. UNHCR will continue provision of essential services for new arrivals including means, NFIs and transportation and ensure capacity building for NGO government partners in order to better serve POCs. Registration activities mainly completion BIMS enrolment for the remaining SSR and rollout of V4 will be supported. Further, in 2020, the operation will endeavour to expand the CBI programme in the sectors of shelter, NFIs, livelihoods and education. Protection of women and children will continue be a priority area for SSR and UNHCR will strengthen CBP services for UASC other vulnerable groups. UNHCR will support the residual caseload of Chadian refugees, after the repatriation is completed in 2019. In Darfur the VOLREP of Chadian refugees will be continued in 2020 leaving a residual caseload if some of the returnees would not opt for the VOLREP. The suspension of the VOLREP exercise due to the political unrest in Sudan was the main reason why the repatriation of the Chadian refugees was not completed in 2019. UNHCR will continue to support the provision of basic services and protection assistance for the Chadian refugees as well as CAR refugees in Um Shalaya camp while the office continue to advocate with gov. other stakeholders for the local integration of the remaining Chadian refugees as well as integration of services to the national system. Planned assistance including health, education, water, livelihood, protection will continue the smaller scale given the meagre resources allocated for this population. In addition, the ongoing biometric registration of CAR refugees will continue in 2020. NAs will receive protection assistance and NFIs while CAR refugees residing in urban and other out of camp settlements will continue to benefit from self-reliance and livelihood assistance. CBI, especially targeting vulnerable HHs PWSNs will be provided. UNHCR will continue working with partners to respond to the basic needs of refugees alongside host communities in Um Dukhan, Um Dafoug, Dimsu (Maramousa and Karakada), and El Radom. Area of priority will include WASH, Education, Health/Nutrition ES/NFI. In 2020, the East operation will prioritized key activities including but not limited to anti-trafficking protection assistance services. Safe houses will be sustained to ensure individual case-managements. Advocacy will continue with State level authorities to mainstream basic social services, but at the same time pursue camp closure and consolidation. The operation will also address needs of new arrivals by providing transportation, wet feeding, and appropriate protection (RSD) and security. Reception facilities will be adequately maintained to accommodate new arrivals. Capacity building of local authorities and line ministries to integrate basic services in addition to partners’ staff capacity development will continue. Interventions are expected to lead to reduced protracted refugees in the East by end of 2020. The office will endeavour to mainstream the services provided in camps into national systems. This will strengthen peaceful coexistence between refugees and host communities. Repatriation of Eritrean refugees is not perceived to be possible,yet, the refugees will continue to be assisted through UNHCR interventions. With the dwindling resources, interventions in 2020 would focus more on reducing aid dependency promote self-reliance of refugees over the coming years.

2 - Activities / Outputs

UNHCR will seek to ensure that: - - New arrivals and protracted refugees receive life-saving NFIs and emergency shelter in a timely fashion; - Reception border points are supported and maintained and can provide adequate assistance to newly arrived refugees (including protection support, health screenings, provision of wet feeding, support to UASC and most vulnerable individuals, and transport to hosting refugee sites); - Quality of health care services is improved and accessible through rehabilitation/construction and equipment of health facilities; - All refugees benefit from an improved water provision coverage to meet SPHERE Standards; quality of the water provided is improved; - Enhance the access quality of sanitation facilities in sites and in urban areas are improved and accessible to refugees through construction / rehabilitation of latrines - Hygiene promoters are trained; - All refugees benefit from biometric registration on an individual basis and have better access to documentation through enhanced registration campaigns coverage especially in out of camps situation; - Survivors/victims of SGBV, trafficking and kidnapping are identified and supported with better access to health, psychosocial support and mental health and legal services; - Protection of Unaccompanied Minors (UAMs) through registration, best interest determination and identification of appropriate solutions is strengthened; - Education for refugees is supported with rehabilitation / construction of schools and classrooms, and provision of school supplies;

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

During the Participatory Assessment undertaken in Kordofan states (South and West Kordofan) in November 2018, South Sudan refugees (SSR) women, indicated sexual harassment and assault during firewood collection far from the settlement. Findings from the Energy and Environment baseline assessment undertaken in May,May 2018 in the same states also revealed that refugees and the hosting community depend entirely on firewood for cooking, they travel an average of 3-5 Km to collect firewood. .The heavy reliance on firewood has negative impact on the forests on the refugees, safety and dignity and create conflict between the refugees and the hosting communities, the refugees use the inefficient 3 stones stove. The same findings were reported during the Energy baseline assessment conducted in East Darfur, April 2017 and the joint baseline assessment conducted in October 2017 for the South Sudanese refugees in Khartoum Open Areas. UNHCR in 2019 continue the distribution of Fuel Efficient stoves (FES) to the SS refugees and 20-25% of the (FES) to the hosting communities, will distribute charcoal briquettes, pilot Ethanol in as clean cooking fuel WN State, rehabilitate the degraded forest with gum Arabic species, as an income generation for the host community and planting acacia Millfera for wood fuel. The planned interventions in 2019 covering only 40% of the SSR households. There is no many actors with their own fund to implement activities in energy and environment in most of the south Sudan refugee’s locations, except WFP in White Nile and East Darfur, through trees plantation and distribution of (FES) The Energy environment comprehensive response plan for 2020 targeting 82,502 families of the SSR representing 76% have access to sustainable energy, including the SSR in South west Kordofan and Khartoum open areas, Through provision of (FES), and mix energy sources; gas stoves, charcoal briquettes and ethanol for White Nile. Provision of solar energy to ensure Sustainable light in the street and the refugee’s houses, for the protection of women and children’s, and will focus to replace diesel fuel systems by solar powered systems for the clinics, drinking water pumps and schools. Reforestation of degrade forest surrounding the SSR settlements and camps. Cocoon plantation technology will be piloted as new reforestation methodology for semi-desert area in WNS, Aljabalien and Alsalam localities. The priority response for energy and environment in 2020, is to ensure minimum 34,317 SSR families, equivalent to 55 % of SSR households have access for sustainable energy ,by providing 6000 SSR HHs with alternative cooking “1000 HHs gas stoves, 4000 HHS charcoal briquettes, and 1000 HHs with Ethanol in White Nile state”, construction and distribution of (FES) to 18,067 HHs , provision of chargeable solar lamps to 11,250 HHs and installation of solar energy centres in the refugee’s camps, to charge home lamps, street lamps and mobiles.

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The Sudan operation is a mixture of quasi emergency, with the South Sudanese refugees, as well as a protracted situation with the caseload in the East, comprising of largely Eritreans, who are relatively well integrated and with 55% of them born in Sudan. New arrival flows from Eritrea continue to be small but steady, with approximately 63 % moving onwards once brought to the camp. The risk of trafficking is a real danger for the group and other nationalities entering through Sudan’s eastern borders. The operation also has a significant number of urban refugees in Khartoum the majority of whom are South Sudanese with Eritrean, Ethiopian, Syrian and Yemeni refugees making up the other large groups.The Refugee Coordination Model is in place in Sudan, with UNHCR coordinating more than 45 operational partners through the Refugee Consultation Forum (RCF) and Technical Advisory Groups at federal level, as well as Refugee Working Groups at field state level. The RCF is represented within the Inter Agency Standing Committee (IASC) mechanisms to ensure coordination and information-sharing. UNHCR is also part of the IASC response to internal displacement in Sudan, and is the sector lead for Protection and Emergency Shelter and Non-Food items (ES/NFI ). Tripartite agreements have also been signed between the Sudanese and, Chadian government, and UNHCR for returns of refugees from both Sudan and Chad. Up until end of December 2018, close to 5,800 Chadian refugees have returned to Chad and close to 1800 Sudanese refugees have returned to Sudan (mainly to Darfur). Sudan has been experiencing an acute economic crisis since early 2018, characterized by extreme inflation of 73% and shortages of basic commodities due to the removal of subsidies for the imports of fuel, food and pharmaceuticals, caused by the drastic depletion of the country’s exchange reserves. This has also led to demonstrations taken place in Sudan since 19 December 2018.
