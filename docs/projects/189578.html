Project Summary

The objective of the intended project is to contribute and improve the living conditions in the targeted IDP camps through strengthening the predictability and effectiveness of service provision and protection for displaced communities with the aim to reach durable solutions through strengthening community-self management and in addition to that the project will strengthen coordination and information sharing among all stakeholders. Through this proposed project activities, DRC will ensure that all the displaced communities are enabled to partake meaningfully in the governance structures and management of the sites. This project will also encourage camp residents to participate community led care and maintenance activities aimed at improving safety and hygiene in sites, reducing protection and health risks. DRC will work with the camp management committees to facilitate site maintenance through cash-for-work while utilising members of the IDP community by enabling them to earn temporary income. Through this project, DRC will establish or strengthen an inclusive community governance structures and thematic committees at each site level, the engagement of these structures in CCCM coordination mechanisms alongside active humanitarian partners in the respective sites and representatives of relevant local authorities, as well as through continued support for capacity building, mainly through the delivery of trainings adapted to each type of participation (thematic IDP committee members, site governance members, local authority representatives). The proposed project will enhance Complain, Response and feedback mechanisms.

1 - Needs

The majority of South Sudanese are made vulnerable due to different shocks that led to substantial increases in vulnerabilities such as localized conflicts, devastating flooding, and food insecurity. According Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022, more than two-thirds of South Sudan’s population, 8.9 million people, are currently in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 people have been observed since 2021. Hence continued conflict and instability in the country coupled with massive flooding have resulted in a large scale internal and cross-border displacement. 1.64 million people have been identified as in need of CCCM critical interventions out of the total 8.9M people in need. An estimated 1.4 M people will be targeted through CCCM interventions in 2022. The majority of the sites are informal settlements, and living conditions of the displaced communities living in the camps or camp-like settlings continue to decline, and return to areas of origin is extremely limited as many displaced communities have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian services, which are concentrated in urban areas. According to DRC population headcounts, Malakal-POC alone hosts over 34,056 individuals in which new arrivals are recorded daily. In 2021, there was an outbreak of sub-national violence in Tambura County, Western Equatoria State, which saw the displacement of an estimated 80,000 people to informal sites both within Tambura County and neighbouring counties, including Ezo, Nzara, Nagero, and Yambio. DRC has completed several protection and CCCM assessments which have identified significant protection violations, occurring both during incidents of sub-national violence in Tambura, during the displacement journey and in the area of displacement. IDPs mainly live informal sites or settlements with limited basic needs either due to inconsistent service provision or exclusion from accessing humanitarian support due to poor targeting of sites. Coordination of services in all the settlements or sites continues to be problematic, with yet insufficient information sharing among service providers, and weak feedback mechanism between the local authorities, humanitarian organizations and the IDPs. The displacement crisis impacted in all the states in South Sudan including, Unity, Upper-Nile, Western-Equatoria and Jonglei states, but displaced communities bear the most substantial burden. Critical needs in the IDP sites are lack of information on service provision at the site level, HLP issues, and low level of community participation, and most of the IDP sites do not have fencing or proper lighting at night and often overcrowded, that can further elevating safety concerns, to be exact women, children and persons with special needs and there has been cases of human rights violations reported as sites are overcrowded and unplanned informal sites. In the light of barriers mentioned above, CCCM services are needed to reduce suffering and barriers to assistance for displaced people to ensure their access to basic services, enhance accountability of humanitarian actors to affected populations, and strengthen community participation and empowerment through consistent engagement.

2 - Project activities

The following activities make up the proposed project, which will be undertaken in line with the objectives of the South Sudan CCCM Cluster Strategy: DRC will continue and strengthen coordination role at the state level as Co-lead with strong quality and timely reporting, utilization of coordination tools, consistent meeting facilitation with consistent documentation and actioning of issues and follow-up, and a strong commitment to advocacy. DRC will also conduct safety audits in the selected sites as well as intention surveys, and conducting engagement with community governance structures, and contribute to improved monitoring, coordination and support for site management. DRC will facilitate and conduct community led site maintenance activities through Cash for Work modality. Activities will also include the establishment of Complain Response Feedback Mechanism and coordination of service providers on key service gaps like shelters that needs improvements to ensure that the minimum living standards and protection are ensured. This will include the installation of solar lights in the camps especially in the areas with high protection risks. DRC will also focus on improved information sharing and sensitization of beneficiaries living in sites on services and activities available at the site level. DRC will support the establishment of inclusive governance structures and provide capacity building through CCCM trainings to the governance structures on CCCM principles and protection mainstreaming in CCCM. Interventions will include activities that enhances protection standards with a specific focus on people with special needs, such as the construction of footbridges, regularly conducting site monitoring and community engagement. DRC will establish site maintenance committees to self-manage and peaceful co-existence will be promoted while community engagement and consultation will be conducted to identify community led initiatives that can be supported. in regards to phase-out Prior the phase-out the following critical activities will be undertaken, information dissemination to the respective stakeholders about the phase-out and subsequently providing trainings to governance structures, as well as identification and profiling of vulnerable groups through conduction of assessments as well as ensuring that the existing structures are accounted for to prepare for handover to governance structures. Continuous coordination and consultation will be done with the governance structures . The DRC CCCM team will only deploy in locations where there are no other CCCM partners responding.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

DRC will conduct emergency community-based maintenance activities through a cash for work modality to ensure safety and proper hygiene in the sites of operations. DRC will closely coordinate with site governance structures/committees to facilitate community maintenance work as per the SOP of DRC CCCM CFW. DRC will factor Cash-for-work component in its activities related to the IDP sites care and maintenance and to support in the maintenance of the dykes around the IDP sites in Bentiu. In close collaboration with the dykes committees, representation committees in the sites and protection actors, DRC CCCM will ensure the participation of the most vulnerable persons in the areas of operations to benefir from the CfW component to ensure the contribution towards their resilience and decrease their vulnerability. The project will have 10% cash for work through site maintenance work. Selected individuals will engage site maintenance activities through cash for work. DRC will follow CCCM Cluster guidelines when implementing the cash for work activities in the targeted sites.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Cash for work provides economic opportunities to beneficiaries while contributing to improving their living conditions and reduce protection risks. DRC usually operates in areas where cash is being used and markets are accessible. In case markets are not accessible or functioning markets, in-kind interventions could be considered in consultation with the communities and authorities. The markets in the areas of operations are functioning well, with enough supplies flow with a noticeable increase in the prices caused by the flooding. DRC chose the CfW approach instead of the in-kind, to factor the increase of resilience of the affected populations, and to decrease the chances of inflation of prices caused by any massive purchases of good for in-kind support. DRC will ensure that the cash provision as a result of CFW activity, does not negatively impact market forces such as inflations and adhere to the CCCM Cluster guidelines using minimum wage rate for all the segments including skilled/unskilled/semi-skilled.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedbacks are collected regularly through the different international activities on the ground; daily operational information desks, daily visits, committees weekly meetings, trainings, workshops, and other means. The information collected from the beneficiaries serve to ensure the full inclusion of the affected persons in the humanitarian programming, and to ensure their influence to DRC programming, approaches and activities, mainstreaming AGD, DRC will regularly seek the feedback from the community it serves, to ensure providing the relevant assistance that will help in building their resilience and to achieve the ultimate participation level of full ownership. Feedback will be shared with the relevant project managers and be used to adapt the project implementation whenever required and inform future project design.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC has a functional FCRM mechanism that allows beneficiaries to give feedback and complaints regarding the activities being implemented in their respective locations. The DRC FCRM desks are set up to receive complaints and questions from beneficiaries and follow-up with feedback, including referrals to external service-providers or to the DRC protection desks when appropriate, either immediately or within a reasonable timeframe, depending on the nature of the complaint. Feedback and complaints can be provided through the FCRM desk but also through community engagement activities and visits and through intermediaries working with DRC e.g. implementing partners and community governance structures/representatives. Under this project, DRC will seek feedback from various groups, including the most vulnerable and adjust the feedback mechanisms as appropriate. Whenever possible DRC will establish a joint protection and FCRM desk to ensure protection concerns are recorded immediately. DRC has a team composed of both male and female staff and conducts age and gender-segregated focus group discussions whenever required to gather inputs regarding issues affecting them as well as feedback and recommendations. Continuous training on participation and its importance, as well as close collaboration with the protection and SGBV departments will be ensured. DRC CCCM considers the AGDM approach in its activities, and sensitizes its staff and community leaders equally to its importance.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DRC staff were trained and informed on the DRC Code of Conduct and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DRC has a zero-tolerance for cases of sexual exploitation and abuse. This has promoted staff accountability and transparency to persons of concerns in service delivery. DRC provide regular refresher PSEA training to the staff and to the community it serves, to ensure their knowledge of the appropriate channel of communications and reporting if encountered with a potential case. Under this project, DRC will have IEC materials stationed at strategic locations such as the information desk, community centers which include contact information on PSEA reporting mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

As a certified member of the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), DRC fulfils the CHS benchmarks on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. Fundamental to all DRC activities is adherence to Age and Gender Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM), achieved through engagement with the various community members and existing institutions. Through formal and informal interactions with representative groups and other community members, beneficiaries are empowered to inform the type of activities and services provided by DRC and ensure their implementation is relevant, effective, and responsive to the needs of displaced populations DRC engages different community representatives across all the project locations, including women, men, youth, elderly PWD, community leaders, and community-based committees. DRC ensures information reached and is received by all affected population through different mediums such as community leaders, community mobilizers, women and youth groups. DRC information desks are accessible and inclusive of everyone, either in-person or one-on-one meeting with DRC field staff on monitoring visits. To strengthen its accountability to the affected population, DRC establishes complaint and feedback mechanisms to provide community members and other stakeholders a platform to provide feedback on the activities. The exact complaint and feedback mechanisms will depend on the target location and activities being implemented but examples of complaint and feedback mechanisms DRC has established includes complaint and feedback desks at key project sites, hosting of focus group discussions to receive feedback from beneficiaries and installation of complaints and feedback boxes at static protection desks. Beneficiaries will be informed on the feedback mechanisms during community engagement activities, at the beginning of the intervention and throughout the project. Having several mechanisms allows for beneficiaries to choose a method that is most comfortable and practical for them and ensures there is at least one mechanism in place in all locations. DRC Staff have been trained to handle personal complaints and inquiries in a confidential manner, and protection issues are referred to DRC protection team or to protection partners to ensure sensitive issues are handled by qualified staff only.
