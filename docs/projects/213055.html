Project Summary

In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of the 16.7 million people in need, 5.5 million are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites. (HNO 2024).
It is estimated that 15.1 million people will need protection assistance in 2023. Violations of international humanitarian law (IHL) and international human rights law (IHRL) in Syria continue to drive humanitarian needs. Civilians remain exposed to hostilities, especially along conflict lines, resulting in casualties64 and forced displacement.65 Widespread torture, arbitrary arrest and detention, and forced recruitment continue to be used systematically by all parties to the conflict within a context rife with restrictions on freedom of movement and expression. Grave violations against children,66 endemic gender based violence (GBV) and explosive ordnance contamination are key features of the relentless violence against civilians. The deteriorating economy and widespread poverty, lack and loss of livelihoods, drought and disease outbreaks further exacerbate protection needs and increase reliance on harmful coping mechanisms.(MSNA Syria 2023)
The proposed intervention by B&Z is in line with the HRP 2nd objective by responding to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria as well as the WoS protection sector objective.
Protection in the camps: The Protection outcome will support individuals affected by the earthquake, with a focus on children and youth, to have safe and dignified access to holistic protection services. Protection activities aimed at children and youth will be integrated as part of the education process, targeting groups aged 6-26 years old. For protection activities, support will be provided according to needs identified by the B&Z team and will take into account the needs of different age groups. Youth and children will be identified through education services. Additionally, as well as referrals received in coordination with other agencies in target locations, B&Z will provide both group support including life-skills and semi-structured PSS sessions as well as individual case management and support. Referrals will be conducted depending on the need.
2 mobile units will enable protection support which will include semi-structured PSS sessions and awareness raising activities in camp settings, specifically in Aleppo northern rural (Jenderis and Afrin) and Idlib western rural (Attareb, Harim, Salkein, Kfr Takhareim, Azmarrin) reaching out 8,840 individuals.
Life skills, Psychosocial support, case management and referrals will be conducted through established education centers in Maret Masrien, Alatarib and the newly established center in Jenderis targeting 1,200 children and youth.
Outcomes :
Inclusive and safe access to holistic protection services has been facilitated to individuals affected by the earthquake in Idleb and Aleppo governorates
Affected individuals in registered and unregistered camps access protection services
Risk: Unclear criteria for beneficiary selection amongst humanitarian partners (and possible duplication).
Mitigation Work with local GBV, Child Protection and/or general Protection teams to help ensure equitable access to services (incl. documentation). Ensure coordination with the relevant sectors to avoid duplication and address gaps
Risk Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), including harassment against affected people.
Mitigation Train staff on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respect and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA . Set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked. for ex. feedback desks, face-to-face interviews with beneficiaries.
