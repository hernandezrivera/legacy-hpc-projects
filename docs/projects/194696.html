Project Summary

The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites. The WASH severity classification conducted in May 2021 showed that all three targeted counties, Koch, Fangak and Canal/Pigi are under WASH severity Phase 4. This situation worsened with the 2022 flooding which reached unprecedented levels. The main drivers of WASH severity included; flooding of WASH infrastructure, destruction of roads and population displacement. As of October 2022, One million South Sudanese had been affected by the flooding (Situation Report 1, October 2022). With most of the WASH facilities destroyed including latrines, boreholes and handwashing facilities which is further compromised by limited access to health services, outbreak of water borne disease is imminent. The World Relief South Sudan (WRSS) WASH interventions and provisions will be integrated alongside other services like health, nutrition, protection, FSL, etc. The main purpose of this WASH intervention is to improve the wellbeing of people affected by disaster or who have been chronically exposed to lack of clean water and basic sanitation services in Koch, Fangak and Canal/Pigi counties. This will be done through reducing water and sanitation related morbidity and mortality; provision of safe, equitable access to clean water and basic sanitation services; mitigating Gender-based violence, supporting recovery and resilience building to achieve durable solutions. This intervention will be done both at community and facility levels (i.e. health & nutrition). Namely, this will be achieved through the rehabilitation of existing water points and drilling of new ones; conducting hygiene promotion campaigns; water quality monitoring; and distribution of WASH NFIs to targeted or blanket populations depending on the context. This WASH assistance will reduce the prevalence of WASH-related diseases and risk of outbreak by transmission contexts upon epidemiological investigations. Secondly, this project will contribute to reducing the high prevalence of malnutrition by creating access to safe water, and promoting hygiene in locations with high malnutrition prevalence and high WASH vulnerability (UNICEF’s normative framework of nutrition 1990). Through this objective, new water points will be drilled, water points will be rehabilitated, water/hygiene committees will be established and trained, hygiene campaigns will be promoted and WASH NFIs will be distributed to target beneficiaries. Thirdly, this project proposes to increase access to basic sanitation services, through the provision of WASH assistance to communities affected by sudden-onset disaster and/or chronically affected by a lack of access to basic WASH services. Fourthly, this project will mitigate gender-based violence (GBV) by carrying out specific activities to mitigate GBV risk for users of WASH facilities and services, especially for women and children. Safety audits will be conducted to identify GBV concerns and implement mitigation measures. The project will ensure construction of water points, latrines and other infrastructure considering the needs of the most vulnerable in society including women, girls, elderly, disabled and children under five years. This will be done with utilization of the WASH Cluster’s Protection Mainstreaming tip-sheet. Community structures including water user management committees (WUC) will be established and trained. They will manage the resources and ensure that no harm is caused by these interventions. Water points will be managed ensuring access to all without causing any conflicts among the intended beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2022 estimated that 5.3 million people would be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water. In 2022, a trend analysis shows a sizeable increase to 6 million persons probably negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water. The main drivers of WASH need in Koch, Fangak and Canal/Pigi counties remains limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of these counties. Increased displacement has placed an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and created barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities. The 2021 HNO estimated that only 36% of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64% of people across the country either rely on unimproved or surface water sources; (19%) can reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4%). According to a KAP survey conducted in June 2021 in Fangak, 94.1% of respondents indicated a use of surface water, e.g. from rivers, streams, ponds, puddles, or unprotected springs. In Koch, the Inter-Agency Flood assessment conducted in December 2021 showed that in the places visited there was no functional borehole and communities drank unsafe water collected from swamps. Access to clean and safe drinking water therefore remains a challenge that needs to be addressed, through the repair of water points, establishment of new ones and provision of aqua tabs for water treatment. Access to sanitation remains low, with only 17%of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound, 4% have access to a shared latrine, and 2% have access to a communal/institutional latrine. This matches with the findings of the Inter-Agency Flood assessment conducted in in Koch in December 2021 which showed that in the places visited, solid waste management was non-existent, open defecation was practiced, as there were no latrines. Open defecation that is practiced by most of the communities poses a great risk of communicable disease spread including diarrhea and other vector borne diseases. The assessment teams observed a generalized poor food hygiene among the population, and poor menstrual hygiene management in women of child bearing age. In addition, an Inter-Agency assessment done in Fangak, in August 2021, showed that the flooding had a strong impact on WASH infrastructure as there had been direct damage in the form of collapsed latrines and submerged water points both in institutions/facilities and communities. Koch shares the same needs as Fangak and Canal/Pigi in terms of sanitation, with open defecation widely practiced by most residents. Toward hygiene, the previous Baseline KAP Survey conducted by WRSS for WASH found that in Koch, only 53% of households were observed to have soap for handwashing. With displacement, returnees, and the status of the local markets, basic WASH items, such as jerrycans, handwashing buckets, and soap are not available for many people, which inhibits their practice of good hygiene behaviors and maintaining the safe water chain. Great efforts have been put into sensitizing households and communities on improved hygiene and sanitation practices and these have been received positively. However, continued sensitization is needed to fully realize the behavior changes needed. These findings match with the WASH needs reported by community members and local leaders in all the three counties. For COVID-19 needs highlighted, vaccination campaigns will not cover all the entire target populations and therefore hygiene promotion remains critical in managing the spread of the pandemic in the country and in the targeted counties. Handwashing, body hygiene and Infection Prevention and Control messaging will be a focus of this intervention to manage the spread of COVID-19.

2 - Project activities

This project proposes WASH activities to be organized around three key outputs: Output 1 - Increase availability of safe water and promote hygiene in locations facing high malnutrition prevalence and high WASH vulnerability both in communities and nutrition facilities. i) WRSS will construct water points located at the static nutrition sites in Koch, Canal/Pigi and Fangak counties. Unused/damaged water points will be rehabilitated and WUCs will be formed of 4 women and 3 men. They will be trained on their roles. WRSS will train 2 pump mechanics per borehole to avail repair services. ii) CHPs and volunteers will receive refresher trainings on handwashing, safe water chain management, safe excreta disposal, food hygiene and household waste management, hygiene promotion skills and messaging. WRSS has materials developed on hygiene promotion and will print more for this year. School Hygiene Clubs (SHCs) will be established in schools in Koch, Canal/Pigi and New Fangak, and will be trained using elements of Child Hygiene and Sanitation Training (CHAST) with a focus on what students can do within their schools to improve hygiene, and to educate their peers. iii) WRSS will construct 30 sex-segregated latrines, one in each nutrition/health facility. All latrines will be lockable to mitigate protection risks to children and caregivers visiting nutrition sites. Selected community members will be supported to construct latrines at their homes to provide to motivate the community to establish their own latrines. iv) The nutrition facilities will receive handwashing facilities, constructed at appropriate heights for children and persons with disabilities, located at the entrance of each facility, and one at the latrine. Nutrition site staff will ensure the constant availability of soap. Output 2 – Secondly, to ensure improved hygiene and sanitation practices among malnutrition-affected households (households with Children Under 5 with SAM/MAM), activities under this will include: i) Nutrition workers, CHWs, and CNVs will be trained on hygiene and nutrition messages within the 22 supported static Nutrition sites. ii) Caregivers/PLWs will attend hygiene education sessions alongside regular sessions on MICYN for their children U5 or for themselves to receive treatment. WRSS will conduct hygiene promotion at the community level through care group promoters and volunteers in villages identified with high SAM and MAM caseloads through nutrition site registries. Volunteers will receive refresher trainings on handwashing, safe water chain management, safe excreta disposal, food hygiene, household waste management, hygiene promotion skills and messaging. WRSS has already developed materials on hygiene promotion and will print more for this year. iii) As part of ensuring a famine prevention minimum package for children U5 admitted to CMAM programs, during the peak of rainy season, WRSS will distribute water treatment materials (e.g. PuR, Aquatabs, filter cloths and clean water storage containers) to households with children admitted to OTP who lack access to safe sources of water in the community. iv) WRSS will conduct a PDM survey among households who received a hygiene kit to assess the satisfaction with and ultimate use of kit contents. WRSS will conduct a KAP survey to assess the impact of WASH interventions on the target populations. Output 3 - Thirdly, WRSS will Mitigate WASH related gender-based violence at WASH facilities and services, especially for children and women caregivers. i) Safety audits will be carried out at site level to identify any GBV concerns and mitigation measures will be put into place in a timely manner. The WASH Cluster Protection mainstreaming tip-sheet will be utilized. ii) Caregivers and PLWs admitted to TSFP will receive MHM kits upon admission of themselves and/or their child to CMAM treatment programs. Kit distribution will be accompanied with instructions by nutrition and WASH staff on proper use of kit contents.

3 - Project indicators

The project will submit monthly progress reports through the WASH cluster and other funding agencies. The project will also submit quarterly, semi-annual and annual reports. There will also be other specific reports that will be developed and shared depending on the specific activity. After every distribution, the project will conduct a post-distribution monitoring (PDM) with 10% of the beneficiaries as respondents. A PDM report will then be developed and shared. The project will also conduct a Knowledge-Attitude and Practice (KAP) survey at the end of the project. Reports from the KAP survey will be developed and shared. The project will measure data on the indicators below: i) Number of water points established or rehabilitated-The project will establish/rehabilitate 30 water points. ii) Number of People who have access to permanent and safe water-Each water point will be able to serve on average 500 persons with minimum 20 litres of water per day. iii) Number of water user committees (WUC) formed, trained and functioning -The project will form and train at least one WUC per water point established/rehabilitated. iv) Number of people reached through water treatment. v) Number of SAM cases with access to safe drinking water-The project will serve 2,032 SAM, MAM U5 and MAM PLWs with clean water within the nutrition centres. vi) Number of Sanitation Facilities established-The project will establish 30 institutional latrines with two stands each, within the nutrition centers in strict adherence to SPHERE standards. vii) Number of handwashing facilities established-The project will establish/rehabilitate two hand washing facilities in every nutrition facility for 22 facilities. viii) Number of people reached with WASH messaging through door-to-door campaign-The project will work through the community structures and/or Care groups to reach out to households with children U5 and PLWs. ix) Number of public events conducted for WASH campaigns-The project will conduct at least one public campaign every quarter on hygiene and sanitation. These will include clean up campaigns in urban areas, world toilet day and other such events organized in strict adherence to COVID-19 IPC measures. x) Number of women and girls provided with MHM kits. xi) Number of school hygiene clubs formed and functioning xii) Number of Households supported with Water treatment items. xii) Number of GBV safety audits conducted.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community engagement will be ensured at two levels. Right from inception, beneficiaries will be engaged at various levels. Project objectives will be discussed and agreed upon by community leaders at County, Payam and Boma levels. Number of beneficiaries targeted, and geographical location will be discussed at project inception. Feedback provided from those meetings will be used to adjust subsequent plans to ensure project is aligned to the context and needs of the beneficiaries. Secondly, there will be a Project Implementation Committee (PIC) at every payam. This PIC will be involved in beneficiary identification, selection and registration. The PIC will include representatives from the vulnerable groups including women, disabled, youth and elderly. The PIC will also be involved in project monitoring and evaluation. Feedback provided by PIC at every stage of project’s implementation will be used to improve on project execution and achievements. At project level, the project directly engages Hygiene promoters on a voluntary basis. Hygiene Promoters will be selected from the respective communities. They will be the contact between the project and beneficiaries. Communication on project objectives and expectations will be done primarily through the hygiene promoters and meetings. These Hygiene Promoters will also provide feedback on project implementation. The Monitoring and Evaluation team working with the protection team will institute a complaint registration and resolution mechanism. Complaints will be registered at every stage of project implementation and solutions found as quickly as possible, where possible, or referred to other levels for resolution. During beneficiary registration and input distribution, beneficiaries who feel unfairly left out will be free to register complaints and have their grievances addressed either by the project team or referred to higher management for resolution. The same mechanism will be instituted during training and during all other project implementation stages. Water Use Committees will also provide feedback on project progress and how the intended targets will be met. Water User Committees will meet regularly to deliberate on water accessibility including quality and correct any deviations that may arise. Complaints on amount of water, conflicts on human and livestock use will be registered and addressed in the best way possible to avoid any escalation of conflicts

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The most vulnerable members of community will be included in every aspect of project implementation. The project will target to include 60% of beneficiaries as women and girls. This is so because women bear the burden of ensuring that the water is available for home use including cleaning and cooking. Providing women and girls with an opportunity to have better access to water will ensure that hygiene and sanitation at home is improved. Similarly, women and girls will be consulted to ensure that activities planned are in line with the socio-cultural needs of women and avoid over-burdening them through this intervention. For instance, distribution of inputs as well as training will take into consideration feedback from women to be in line with other roles assigned to women like fetching water, firewood and cooking at home. Persons living with disabilities will be given special consideration. For instance, during distributions, persons with disabilities, breastfeeding women, elderly will be accorded an opportunity to be served first. Trainings will take into consideration the needs of vulnerable people. Locations for NFI distributions and trainings will be agreed upon with the beneficiaries, especially women and girls. Such places will be centrally located to ensure safety of participants. Training schedule and timing will consider the needs of such groups. Similarly, training will also take into consideration needs of the illiterate population. Most of the project beneficiaries are illiterate. The project will employ most of its staff from the local community who can be able to communicate and conduct training in the local language. Training will also involve mostly demonstrations and practical sessions which emphasize more on learning by doing than theory. Information Education and Communication (IEC) Materials will include more pictorial information for ease of understanding.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All WRSS staff are trained on PSEA and must sign on to the organization’s established policies and code of conduct. It’s a WR requirement for all new employees to sign PSEA policy document. In addition, WR conducts refresher trainings on PSEA and all staff re-sign the SEA Policy in agreement with the terms of PSEA training conducted. WR will build the capacity of the nutrition sites to treat women and girls who are survivors of rape and other incidents of sexual or gender-based violence. For the community to report any incident of PSEA, WR will establish community-based beneficiary feedback mechanisms and will do consultation with community members and community leaders on how reporting will be done, to whom it should be reported and how the complaint is handled. The community will be clearly informed that any reported case will be kept confidential and will be dealt by responsible individuals at WR. During project implementation, all staffs and are expected to adhere to PSEA policy but also to sensitize beneficiaries on this policy. WRSS has one dedicated female staff charged with responsibility to ensure continuous sensitization about PSEA, she will be supported by members of senior leadership team at WR country office and field locations. Sensitization will be provided at all stages of project implementation and any public gathering. Such public events will include registration of beneficiaries, during distribution of inputs and trainings. The Project Implementation Committee (PIC) will be involved in identification and selection of distribution and training sites. Distribution and training venues will be in central places for ease of accessibility to all persons, but also in safe locations. This is to ensure that women and girls are not exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse because of their involvement in any project activity, including training or inputs distribution. Distributions will also have to be conducted and completed on time to allow women and girls time to reach home before dark. All beneficiaries will be sensitized on the availability of this feedback mechanism for their use. Confidentiality on any information provided through the feedback mechanism will be ensured to build the confidence of the community to use this feedback mechanism. Complains registered will include sexual exploitation, abuse or any other form of protection concern/violence. Inputs will be provided in small quantities. This is to ensure that beneficiaries will not have another financial burden to transport the inputs. Such financial burdens can expose those women and girls to sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

World Relief South Sudan has been operational in the three selected project locations for some time now, WRSS has had a chance to engage the beneficiaries at various stages and times. Currently WRSS has running projects in Koch, Fangak and Canal/Pigi. In the past and currently, WRSS conducts post distribution monitoring surveys in addition to Knowledge, Attitude and Practice Surveys. The baselines being used to develop this project are from those past surveys. This will continue even during this project. Beneficiaries will be engaged during mobilization and sensitization. Project objectives and key activities will be discussed. Feedback provided from those sensitization meetings will be used to tailor project interventions to meet the needs of the beneficiaries. The Project Implementation Committee (PIC) will also be very instrumental in providing feedback on the project. Selection criteria and targeting of beneficiaries will be discussed with the PIC. Feedback from PIC will be used to adjust the selection criteria and targeting. PIC will also be consulted to ensure training and distribution locations are easily accessible and are safe for all beneficiaries. The PIC will also be involved in monitoring project progress. During those monitoring sessions, views from the PIC as well as views of the direct beneficiaries will be used to adjust subsequent schedule and type of activities to address the needs expressed by the beneficiaries. For instance, if certain trainings were not well understood, they could be offered again. Feedback on timing, quantity and type of inputs will be received through post distribution monitoring (PDM) reports. This feedback will be used to adjust subsequent distributions. Based on the feedback, the project will adjust on duration of time spent in trainings or distribution centers. Frequency of training will also be adjusted based on feedback received from beneficiaries. Water user committees (WUC) will also provide feedback on water use. Timing of water use and any conflicts that may arise from water use will be documented and addressed to ensure that everyone has a safe and unhindered access to water. Information on GBV safety audits will be utilized to come up with mitigation measures.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Women and young children particularly are at risk because of implementation of project activities. WRSS will therefore undertake several measures to ensure the protection of the intended beneficiaries prioritizing safety and dignity of the beneficiaries to avoid causing harm. WASH facilities and water sources will only be established after consultation with the intended beneficiaries to ensure they are sited in safe locations and easily accessible to women and girls since they are typically tasked with daily water collection for home use. Water points will be located not more than 500 meters away from population settlements. Distribution centers for WASH NFIs will be in consultation with community members. Distributions will be conducted in daylight hours to allow beneficiaries enough time to travel home before nightfall. Other WASH NFIs including menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits and water purification tabs will be distributed on quarterly basis. By spreading out distribution timeline, this avoids providing too many kits at the same time which may require individuals to need additional transportation to return home. This would put beneficiaries at risk of attacks. The size of kits distributed will be appropriate for the beneficiaries to carry with no additional physical or financial burden. The design and construction of WASH facilities will take into consideration the safety and dignity of its users. The project will construct separate latrines for males and females, clearly marked in pictorial form for illiterate users and will work with communities to ensure that they are being properly used so to reduce protection concerns. For privacy, locks will be provided on the doors to latrines on the inside. The safety risk to children will be ensured by making the size of drop hole small enough to avoid any accidents or deaths by children falling into the holes. WUCs will be trained to manage water points. Where overcrowding at water points will be experienced, WUCs will be encouraged to consider scheduling time shifts for water collection in consultation with the beneficiaries. This will be done in a respectful manner and be inclusive of the cultural and religious practices of the intended beneficiaries. All WASH facilities, including boreholes will be placed in central locations and managed by the WUCs in such a way that there will be no community tensions. Any disputes arising from use of WASH facilities will be resolved in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of WASH facilities. The WASH intervention will also be designed to promote equitable and impartial access for all. This will include collection and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. The project will also identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. The project will as well endeavor to make infrastructure adaptions to WASH structures to make them accessible to persons with reduced mobility (e.g. persons with physical disabilities, older persons). During the WASH NFI distribution, priority will be accorded to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disabilities, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women, so that their wait time is reduced and made manageable for them to attend. Distribution teams and WUCs will also consider the gender balance to ensure that there are enough women participants who will act as points of contact for women in the community to ask questions, seek guidance, etc. Before distribution days, adequate mobilization and information dissemination will be carried out to ensure that beneficiaries know about WASH services, how to obtain hygiene kits, where and when the distributions will take place and what they will get in the kits.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The three target locations have been ravaged by floods for three consecutive years. With most of the locations in those three counties still flooded in the first quarter of 2022, a likelihood of continual flooding into a subsequent year is still very high. WRSS will work with communities to institute disaster risk mitigation measures across the target locations. The project will build the capacity of the community to institute early warning signs that will trigger a response before the flooding occurs. Through these early warning signs, the community can respond with appropriate measures. This project will work together with the community through flood control measures, mainly by constructing berms and dykes. This activity will be conducted through the Cash for Assets modality where possible and will liaise with another active WRSS project in the same location involved in Cash for Assets, to have this work done. The community capacity will be built to identify and prioritize areas prone to flooding. The community will then be facilitated to establish dykes or berms that will be used to control the flood waters. This will help to reduce flood related displacement in the target locations.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

WRSS has an integrated approach in service delivery. This includes provision of protection and health services. WRSS provides primary health care services in both Koch County in Unity State and Fangak County in Jonglei state. In these two counties, WRSS runs both PHCU and PHCC services. WRSS also ensures protection to children, women at risk and GBV survivors. WRSS will also able to work with protection partners to establish referral pathways. Through these referral pathways, children, women at risk and GBV survivors will be able to access information on prevention and management of GBV. In Canal/Pigi, WRSS implements protection programming through a national non-government organization partner. WRSS is also able to handle clinical management of rape within the PHCC, and where such cases would be beyond WRSS’ capacity, they will be referred to other facilities with appropriate capacity.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

There is a direct relationship between poor WASH conditions and high rates of malnutrition. By improving on the WASH conditions, the project aims to reduce the proportion of the population facing IPC 4 and 5 levels of food insecurity. Working alongside WRSS’ nutrition program, WASH interventions will provide handwashing and sanitation facilities to children and care givers admitted within the nutrition program. This will improve the WASH conditions within these health and nutrition centers. At community level, this project will work with care groups and/or lead mothers who are caregivers of children admitted into the nutrition program. The WASH team will sensitize care groups to establish hand washing facilities at home using locally available materials. Care groups and/or lead mothers will also be encouraged to set up sanitation facilities to improve on human waste disposal. The WASH team will also work with farmers and farmer groups to sensitize them on proper hygiene during food preparation as well as proper water treatment and storage. This will reduce incidences of diarrhea and water borne diseases, which impacts positively on health and nutrition status of the targeted beneficiaries and therefore reducing the proportion of the population in IPC 4 and 5 levels of food insecurity.
