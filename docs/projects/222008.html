Project Summary

ADA -HNRP 2025 Project aims at addressing emergency shelter and non-food items needs of newly affected IDPs, returnees and residents through in-kind and CVA in line with HNRP strategies. SO1 Vulnerable crises affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe and dignified access to life saving assistance to meet their needs. SO2 Vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action informed by communities’ priorities. ADA will conduct the project Activities in line with NFI Cluster objectives SO1. Ensure provision of timely, adequate access to safe and appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced people or populations with new vulnerabilities. SO2. Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities who are unable to meet their SNFI needs. The project will target 17,000 individuals with CVA, 70,000 individuals with in-kind. The project will target 55% of newly IDPs, 15% of returnees and 25% of vulnerable residents, this targeting aims at promoting strong community cohesion for peaceful coexistence. Of these, 60% will be female and 40% male. In the multi-sector approach, ADA plans to collaborate with WASH, Protection, FSL and Peace Building departments then integrated ES/NFI to the above mentioned departments. The overall project goal is to ensure that the 87,000 individuals target IDPs, returnees Hosts and returnees have safe and dignified access to shelter & NFIs services. Out of the total case load, 17,000 individuals will be serve with Cash base assistance in the areas where market is functional. The project will directly target conflict and disaster affected persons, returnees and refugees in Ayod, Fangak, Pigi Canal and, Mayendit, Nasir, Renks and Malakal counties through S/NFI response. During the project implementation, ADA will deliver assistance through a static response in Ayod, Fangak, Mayendit, Renk, Pigi Canal & Nasir and mobile response in Uror county. ADA will include protection, that is to say protection mainstreaming, including community infrastructures like housing, land and property issues, to all S/NFI responses; establish community social networks and safety nets and conduct protection and PSEA, GBV trainings to committees to implement, and conduct mine risk awareness sessions through the committees to ensure safety to the community. In the areas where markets are functional, ADA will implement sectoral cash approach, however will ensure integrating it with protection mainstreaming, and land, housing and property, besides engaging all key stakeholders to achieve holistic service delivery to the affected communities in all target areas. ADA will continue to strengthen the cluster state level coordination through the newly elected cluster Roving Coordinator at the National level, and SNFI CO-State Focal Points in Upper Nile and Jonglei States, which is under the technical guidance of ADA. ADA will as well implement the HLP and protection mainstreaming activities, including identification of PSNs, referrals to service providers, information dissemination on key protection needs, advocacy for gaps and life skills training, hence these will complement other ongoing protection efforts done in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity states by partners. ADA, through a mobile approach will coordinate responses with partners and SFPs and monitor both the single and multi-sector services through its static and roving teams to deep field locations in all its project areas.
The project will be implemented over a period of 12 months in all the mentioned counties through single and multi-sector interventions, which will be boosted by Agencies' existing funding streams.

1. Needs

South Sudan situation remained unchanged for the last 4 years, peace implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people including refugees and the returnees. In 2025, Ayod, Fangak, Pigi Canal, Mayendit, Nasir and Malakal and Renk, were hit hard by the triple shock of a third consecutive year of major flooding, rapid influx of the returnees due to Sudan crises, cattle raiding and sub-national inter-communal violence. All are compounding factors, hence worsening the needs of vulnerable persons. According to Inter Sectoral Needs Assessment report 2025, 9.3 million people including refugees in South Sudan are in needs, 5 million children, 2.2 million women and 1.3 million people with disabilities will require humanitarian assistance. Out of 9.3 million, around 4.2 million will have acute shelter needs in South Sudan. This increase in the need is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict in Sudan, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services greatly contributed to the increase of needs. Those in need of help are 24% women, 53% children and fifteen percent persons with disability. According to the snapshot of November 2024, it mentioned that over 32,000 flood-affected people in Renk were in needs of humanitarian assistant, the snapshot also mentioned that Renk and Malakal counties were reported by the partners that both counties were badly hit by the rise in cholera cases due to inflows from Sudan, limited access to clean water and sanitation and ongoing flooding. According to the inter-sectoral severity of needs analysis conducted in September 2024, humanitarian needs are most concerning in Fangak county of Jonglei State. ADA conducted Need analysis in November 2024 and the needs are still higher, displacement is still ongoing to date. According to the IRNA assessment conducted in Nasir county in February 2024 Continuous fighting between the white army and the SSPDF is on and off and has displaces hundred thousands of people. The snapshot by OCHA mentioned about continuation forms of crises in Sudan including floods reported in the HNO 2024 will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2025. Unprecedented continuous flooding impacted children and their caregivers` physical well-being, living standard, and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2025, as a result households’ populations have increased to more than 6 people, creating congestion and overcrowding, exacerbating the affected population. Most affected Households lack basic household kits. over 2 million remained in the neighboring countries as refugees, countries like Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia, Egypt, Central Africa Republic, DR Congo among others. Insecurity, lack of basic services such as shelters, non-food items -plastic sheeting, mosquito’s nets, cooking utensils, blankets, community dignity, and unresolved housing, land and property issues prevented people from returning home in large numbers (HNO Jan 2024). Overall food insecurity worsened, communities including the following counties Ayod, Pigi Canal, Fangak, Mayendit, Nasir Malakal and Renk are facing emergency IPC (phase 4) needs. Affected people’s coping mechanisms are weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks and lack of access to resources, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labour, sexual exploitation and child marriage. The economy continued to spiral downwards due to the dollar rate and lack of harvest. (Access to essential services, like ES/NFI health care, education, water and sanitation, as well as protection and legal services, are already limited and much of the service infrastructure like shelters were destroyed either by inter-communal violence or floods.

2. Project activities

Community mobilization, market assessment, need analysis, registration, distribution of cash & SNFI items and PDM will be conducted in Ayod, Fangak, Pigi, Nasir, Malakal, Renk, Mayendit Counties: 1) ADA will mobilize and sensitize the affected community. Local authorities including women, church leaders, chiefs and persons with disability will be engaged & consulted on the needs survey, and be used as focal and entry points for the project activities. 2) Market & Needs Assessment of affected beneficiaries: ADA with support of partners & authorities will conduct a Market assessment & rapid needs analysis to determine the vulnerability of the target populations and understand the market functionality. Data will be collected by trained enumerators, analyzed and recommendations presented for intervention, to ensure ADA clearly understands the community needs. The project will employ various data collection techniques like household questionnaire, FGD, KII and observation. ADA will use the cluster standard tools to collect, analyze and present the data. 3) Registration of most vulnerable HHs will be done, 17,000 will be targeted with CVA, 70,000 will be in-kind. The project will target 55% of newly IDPs, 15% of returnees and 25% of vulnerable residents, this targeting aims at promoting strong community cohesion for peaceful coexistence. Of these, 60% will be female and 40% male. The project will target 5% with awareness sessions on disaster/flood risk reduction. Also, 5% disaster affected elderly will receive SNFI replacement to de-congest from communal shelters to create privacy. ADA will use the S/NFI Cluster’s selection criteria of household to household verification of needs and registration of the most vulnerable affected beneficiaries to receive the assistance. Priority will be given to the most vulnerable communities especially the elderly, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), female and child-headed households, and persons with disability. Tokens will be issued to the registered beneficiaries to facilitate quick and easy identification in the distribution sites. 4) Distribution of SNFI supplies and CVA to the beneficiaries. ADA will identify distribution points at all project areas and consult its protection team and partners to conduct safety audit, to ensure affected persons’ dignity and rights are promoted and protected. Trained community mobilizers will be tasked to move from community to community to sensitize the beneficiaries about the distribution dates, venue and time. Prior to the distribution, subsequent consultation will be conducted with local community to ensure participation; distribution will take place at an appropriate agreed points where the entire beneficiary will come to receive the supplies. The distribution points will be selected not more than 30 minutes’ walk to mitigate protection incidences during return home. 5) ADA will administer a PDM tool after two weeks of distribution to evaluate the project impact and collect feedback from the beneficiaries on their level of satisfaction with the project received. Local enumerators, will be identified with support of authorities, trained and deployed to the field for data collection. Different data collection techniques will be employed including FGD, KII, household questionnaire and observation to ensure rich information is gathered for analysis. A random sampling method will be used to pick respondents for the interview to ensure each beneficiary has an equal chance of being selected to provide their opinions. Cash distribution will be conducted. ADA will conduct all the activities in line with NFI Cluster strategy and HNRP 2025: Vulnerable crises affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe and dignified access to life saving assistance to meet their needs.
