Project Summary

SAMS Health program for the year 2020 comes in line with the Health Sector objectives 1 & 3 targeting both increasing the access to life-saving and life-sustaining health services to the most vulnerable and in need while also strengthening the current health system capacity so it can continue supporting community resilience to respond to the ever changes in the in context. In this program, SAMS will prioritize the reduction of morbidity and mortality through improving the access to quality health care (primary and secondary health facilities, specialized hospitals, mobile clinics, and an ambulatory system) in 36 health facilities within both Aleppo, Idleb, Ar Raqqa governorates.
To achieve these objectives, SAMS will support facilities on different levels including coverage of staff incentives, running costs, provision pharmaceuticals and other medical and non-medical commodities, and assisting with repairs and rehabilitation to maintain the health network. SAMS will provide recruitment support, capacity building, and ongoing medical supervision to ensure the HFs are staffed with qualified medical personnel and to enhance compliance with standard treatment protocols.
SAMS will increase the capacity of the HFs so that minimal inputs will be required to sustain the services. As per the Essential Primary Health Services Package adopted by the Health Cluster, SAMS will provide the following services: reproductive health services (ante-natal care, post-natal care, family planning, normal deliveries, gynaecological consultations, and clinical management of rape), integrated management for child illness, vaccines, prevention and control for communicable and non-communicable diseases, nutrition, MHGAP, health awareness, and basic laboratory and radiology services. Services at the secondary health care level will include gynecologic surgeries and cesarean sections, trauma management, major surgeries, hospitalization services, blood transfusion, hemodialysis and intensive care units.
Moreover, to reduce the prevalence of mental health, psychological disorders and functional limitations of people affected by the conflict in Northwest Syria. This objective will target 24 health facilities with Psychosocial Support Workers (PSSWs) in each facility to provide PS services within the facilities. This program will also provide cancer treatment to Breast, colon, Hodgkin, and non-Hodgkin Lymphoma patients in NWS. Additionally, SAMS will support the ambulatory system consists of 10 ambulances working in two shifts to ensure 24/7 covering Ehsem, Ariha, Dana subdistricts in Idleb governorate. The program will also Increase access to prosthesis services and rehabilitation care in NWS through maintaining activities launched at Azaz Prosthesis centre including.
In addition, as part of SAMS focus on contributing to its goal of increasing the capacity of the health care professional in NWS through advancing SAMS’ investment in its education program, SAMS will contribute toward achieving this objective through the continuation of supporting three major medical education components:
Supporting Residents Doctors to start or resume their specialities studies in a variety of specialities.
Supporting Midwifery Education in NWS, by implementing two modules of the Midwifery Training Program due to the strategy which was developed by the SRH-TWG;
Supporting Nursing Education in Syria.
Similar to all SAMS projects, SAMS will seek all efforts possible to coordinate with all health actors through the Health Cluster whether through the routinely gathered data in 4Ws and HeRAMS. or through its constant participation and active membership within the following TWG: SRH, MHPSS, Non-Communicable Diseases, Syrian Immunization Group, Midwifery Capacity Building Strategy for NWS, Secondary Health Care, and Drug Supply Line notwithstanding its membership in the NGO Forum, SNA and other bodies. SAMS always coordinate and consult its operations with the HDs.

4 - Needs

The increase continues in both the scale and complexity of the humanitarian needs in Syria, both of its magnitude and severity, Based on NWS Needs Assessment, humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining services remains the most essential health services at primary level including first-line support for coordinated referrals to secondary care, trauma services and specialized care while MHPSS needs are critical since prolonged exposure to conflict is believed to increase the prevalence of mental health conditions above the WHO global prevalence of 15%. Recent assessments indicate the need for improved supply of medicines and services within PHCs, particularly for non-communicable diseases (NCDs), which are estimated to account for 45 per cent of all deaths. Referral capacity, including ambulances, is needed to overcome gaps in secondary care and connect patients to humanitarian life-saving care (IBID). Essentially, an urgent need for lifesaving medical support such as emergency obstetric, newborn care (EmONC), and specialized services such as dialysis, psychological first aid (PFA) and psychosocial (incl. trauma) support in destination areas. In its tenth year, the crises now witness 12M PiN for health services including 1.43M children under age 5, 3.22M women of reproductive age including 483k women who are expected to become pregnant in 2020, and approx. 350k above the age of 65 and those with early-onset suffering from chronic diseases wit displaced populations, including 5.54M IDPs and returnees. Under the project, targeted facilities are Batbue PHC, Alatareb H, Hama MC, Idleb National H, Idleb Central H, Jabal-Alzawia MC, Alikhlas H, Afrin H, Feririeh PHC, Shamarin prosthesis centre, midwifery institute Termanin, midwifery institute Idlib, Al Amal H, Hama Ambulance System, Kurin PHC-ObGyn, Qah PHC, Selwa PHC, kafer losien PHC, Ma'arret Mesrin H, Qah maternity H, Kafr Diryan PHC, Specialized Maternity H, Termanin rehabilitation centre, Dana MHPSS center.

5 - Implementation of activities

SAMS will work on the following activities: Rehab works for health facilities, Recruitment, Capacity building for medical and non-medical staff in addition to the administrative staff, Provide PHC services according to EHSP including CD, NCD, child health, RH and MH-Gap, Provide trauma, SHC, and in-patients services in SAMS specialized and general hospitals including ICU and Renal dialysis, provide SRH services including ANC, PNC, FP, BEmONC, CEmONC, in addition to CMR and NICU, Procure medical/non-medical supplies to the HFs, Raising awareness sessions, health-promoting, and medical messages to the community through CHWs, self-care and staff care activities, Provision of ambulatory and referral services consisting of 10 ambulances working in 2 shifts to ensure 24/7 coverage, Implementation of safe and effective medical waste management protocols, Sustain services of EPI to reduce morbidity and mortality in 4 centres, Provide PSS activities in 23 HFs and PSS out-reach activities, Provide medical education support through two midwiferies and nursing schools in Idleb city and Termanin in addition to the support of the resident doctors’ project, and Provision of PSEA And Child safeguarding training.Monitoring: Field monitors from MEAL team conduct quarterly observational visits using checklists/spot checks, including verifying attendance sheets, payment receipts, accuracy and patient data entry, in order to monitor activities and assess impact. Visits provide opportunities for staff and beneficiaries to provide feedback. Other random spot checks - i.e inventories - are also conducted to ensure the minimum standards are in place.Feedback and Complaint Response Mechanisms: SAMS makes available multiple channels for beneficiaries to provide feedback in a secure manner, including WhatsApp, email, interviews, and complaints boxes. CRM (Complaints and Response Mechanism) System enable patients or stakeholders at supported facilities to provide their feedback/complaints.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Attack by GoS and its allies/others. Mitigation measures: Selecting fortified locations; Security assessment (site & building); Activation of contingency plans for each center in cases of near bombing or close clashes or intrusion or siege or fire; Evacuation SOP; Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation. Risk: Hostage-taking/Kidnap for ransom. Mitigation measure: Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan. Risk: Control by unaccepted groups/entities or GoS. Mitigation measures: Anti-Terrorism Check (ATC), Pause / Cessation; Information security SOP; Strengthening the relations with the local communities; Relocating the activities locations; Inform the partner civil authorities (Health Directorates and Civil Local Authorities), of the suspension and the reasons, in order to play their role in negotiation and advocacy, and apply the agreements of neutralizing humanitarian facilities. Risk: Theft Mitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for SAMS facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records during monitoring visits; Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Quarterly physical inventory of warehouses; review of monthly consumption reports.Risk: Aid Diversion risks (Cash transfer). Mitigation Measure: SAMS utilizes official channels, authorized by the Government of Turkey, to transfer money from Turkey to Syria. The process is SAMS request all facilities managers to open a bank account in the PTT. Upon receiving confirmation of the opening the bank accounts SAMS transfer the amounts due to the designated PTT bank account
