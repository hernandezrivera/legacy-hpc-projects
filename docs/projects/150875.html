Project Summary

This project will strive to preserve the gains made in 2018 and will deliver lifesaving emergency health services including maternal, neonatal and child health and contribute to disease prevention and strengthening national and regional emergency preparedness and response capacities in the three zones of Somalia for an estimated 974,000 out of 3 million crisis affected population in need of healthcare. Through deployment of trained health workers including midwives and community health workers in facilities and mobile teams, primary health care services including treatment of common childhood illnesses, as well as emergency obstetric and neonatal care for pregnant and lactating women will be provided.
The proposed action complements UNICEF's core interventions in Somalia and the intervention focuses on an integrated, multi-sectoral response to drought, displacement and conflict, providing life-saving services. To maximize impact, this project will provide evidence-based curative, promotional and preventive services and will enable complementarity with nutrition including priorities such as deworming, breastfeeding, complementary feeding, vitamin deficiencies, & micronutrient supplementation. In collaboration with Health Cluster, UNICEF will support the appropriate management of children with SAM and cholera co-morbidity admitted in AWD/Cholera treatment centres. Scaling up the WASH component as part of an integrated and coordinated response, will focus on disease outbreaks, especially cholera. Thus, the integrated action focuses on increasing response in areas of highest need and lowest levels of predictable access in Somalia.
UNICEF supported health facilities will be equipped with essential medicines and equipment and trained staff will provide quality services. Emphasis will be laid on Maternal and Child Health, including Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care and Expanded Programme on Immunization to IDPs and most vulnerable communities and attention will be paid on application of treatment guidelines as per the Essential Package of Health Services and Primary Health Care. Support to outreach immunization for hard-to-reach areas will be promoted to ensure equity and reaching a wider catchment. UNICEF will deliver comprehensive reproductive health care to women of reproductive age, men and adolescent boys among the target beneficiaries and promote health seeking behaviour including adoption of preventive practices through campaigns, health and hygiene education sessions for men and ado. UNICEF will also integrate surveillance and emergency preparedness ensuring adequate stocks for epidemic outbreaks of AWD/Cholera and measles are prepositioned. The project will also contribute to strengthening early warning disease surveillance through weekly submission of Communicable and Disease Surveillance reports from all the functional supported health facilities. Referral system for patients seeking secondary healthcare services will be strengthened. The project will also refer GBV victims to health facilities for comprehensive care and psychosocial support.

Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Somalia, exacerbated by frequent outbreaks, displacement, fragile health system, drought, inaccessibility and security threats in some areas remains challenging. Despite the large-scale humanitarian assistance delivered in 2017-8, there are pressing humanitarian needs according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted across Somalia in June and July 2018. Acute food insecurity is prevalent among populations who lost most of their animals and who became destitute during the 2016-2017 severe drought, those affected by flooding in April and May 2018 and others who were affected by large-scale and protracted displacement due to a combination of conflict and natural hazards. These population groups are expected to face large food consumption gaps and need humanitarian assistance to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation. An estimated three million people need lifesaving emergency healthcare (female 930,000.00, male 750,000 and 600,000 under-fives respectively). From January to September 2018, more than 6,466 cases of AWD/cholera have been reported in 22 districts. The figures may increase with the expected rains. Equally challenging is the large nationwide outbreak of measles with more than 7,765 suspected cases reported in the same period. Despite improvement, routine immunization for measles is at 67.6% nationally against the recommended 95 per cent. Data from OCHA indicates that the current total population of IDPs across Somalia is 2.6 million, most of them in areas classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3). More than 294,000 acutely malnourished children, including 55,200 who are severely malnourished require urgent treatment and nutrition support in Q4 of 2018. Due to multiple risk factors and highly compromised nutritional status of children under 5, the clinical complications and measles case fatality rate is expected to increase, as will their vulnerability to other infectious diseases.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project is linked to building the resilience of communities as the response strategy builds on lessons learned from the 2017 pre-famine response, as well the 2018 post-drought response, focusing on timely action, maintaining momentum of the response, expanding reach in difficult to access areas and sustained partner presence. To strengthen community resilience, the response strategy includes the need to deliver a core package of integrated services. Priority will be given to the delivery of life-saving services concurrently addressing underlying causes of exposure to the risk of malnutrition, disease outbreaks and poor sanitation and hygiene. Critical linkages to child protection for the most vulnerable children and women in IDP camps will be prioritized as well. Community level demand creation, through empowering women in targeted communities (IDP and hard-to- reach areas) to participate in the identification and referral of malnourished children will be promoted. Facility level quality service delivery will be strengthened by capacitating service providers to deliver quality services. Community structures such as District and Facility Health Committees will be established. This action will therefore enable UNICEF to contribute to the broader cluster objective of transforming health facilities from serving as mere fire-fighting treatment centres of humanitarian aid to quality service delivery centres that enhance community resilience. This action will strengthen multi-agency and multi-sectoral linkages. In line with the priorities of the AWD Working Group, comprising the Health Cluster, UNICEF, WHO, MoH and the NGO Consortium, UNICEF will sustain and expand response to seasonal AWD/Cholera outbreaks by supporting a set of CTCs and pre-positioning supplies for treatment. Social mobilization activities will also focus on cholera hotspot areas and expose communities to health education on the risk of communicable diseases.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations. Lack of static facilities that can provide comprehensive package of health services in IDP settings due to poor shelter is a risk factor denying them timely response and needed referral services. Poor sanitation and hygiene within the IDP camps causes the easy spread of communicable diseases. To address these risks, UNICEF will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive package of health services within the IDP camps by providing health services within the IDP camps. Mobile teams will be supported to provide services to IDPs and referral mechanism to secondary healthcare outside IDP camps. In order to avoid community tensions, host communities will be provided with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. Both displaced and host communities will be consulted about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. In cooperation with protection actors, reporting mechanisms will be established to report security incidents concerning GBV cases and preventing of displaced populations from accessing services. IDP communities will be involved in identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

To overcome these risks of exclusion based on gender and age, the implementation will be based on a conducted needs assessment that has desegregated data by sex and age. For example, 302,064 females, 243,600 males, and 194,880 children under-fives (107,184 female and 87,696 male) will be supported. Location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services will be accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. Aged and disabled groups will be supported by ensuring facilities they access have railings and handlebars. Where possible, transportation and enhanced referral will be provided so that they can access services. Health facilities will have lights outside and on the access route to the facility and health services will take into consideration cultural and religious practices and will consider separate waiting areas for males and females. Facilities will be encouraged to have proportionate number of female health staff to address cultural preferences and sensitivities. To ensure equity, mobile teams will be used to reach rural communities avoiding their exclusion. Health staff and health committees will be gender balanced and will have representation from all groups within the community.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The protracted conflict in Somalia is characterized by its longevity, intractability and mutability. Lack of respect for IHL means lack of respect for human rights and causes immense human suffering. The conflict in Somalia causes widespread displacement, multiplying the cumulative impact on basic services and livelihoods over time, and the sharp increase in “war poor” populations. Destruction of facilities in conflict is real and this deprives communities’ critical services. UNICEF will mitigate the impact and the consequences of armed conflict through the promotion of rights based approach to service provision, providing access and services to all affected people without discrimination. Prepositioning essential supplies in strategic hubs for immediate response, providing outreach and mobile health services to displaced communities, providing referral services for critical cases. Rehabilitation of facilities will be given consideration so that damaged facilities can be operationalized to provide services. Strengthened monitoring and risk assessment will ensure data informed intervention and promotion of the rights of the most vulnerable groups.
