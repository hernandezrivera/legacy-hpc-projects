Project Summary

The ongoing Israeli blockade has had devastating consequences on all aspects of life. It is estimated that 1.3M people need multisectoral humanitarian assistance in the Gaza Strip (MSNA, 2021), which has one of the highest unemployment rates in the world. World Bank projections (2020) indicate that 64% of Gaza's population will live in poverty in 2021. In a context characterized by prolonged strain, economic isolation and developmental decline, vulnerable families, including those with many children, female-headed households, and families with people with disabilities, have adopted different coping mechanisms to deal with the high level of poverty and vulnerabilities. The deteriorating socio-economic condition in Gaza, exacerbated by the covid-19 pandemic and the May escalation, has had negative consequences for children’s rights and their ability to access education and protection services, manifested in an increase in school drop-out and child labor (Tdh, 2020; Tdh, 2021).
This project involves multisectoral interventions that are consistent with the HRP Strategic Objectives and with the objectives of the Protection and Education Clusters. The project aims to decrease the prevalence of child labor in the Gaza Strip and increase children's access to the formal education system and other vocational training opportunities through provision of child protection services, psychosocial services, educational reintegration, family support, and increased community mobilization on issues of Child Labour and school drop-out.
In addition to protecting children's rights to access education, this project focuses on the right of the child to be protected from economic exploitation and from performing any work that is likely to be hazardous and could negatively affect their overall wellbeing and development. The project will target 11,750 beneficiaries, including 1,000 working children, children who have dropped out of school, or who are at risk of dropping out (300 working children, 250 out of schools and 450 at risk of drop out).
The proposed activities include (1) provision of individual case management and MHPSS services to child laborers, out-of-school children, and children at high risk of dropout, (2) referral to specialized services as needed, (3) re-integration of out-of-school children (100 boys, 50 girls) in official education and vocational education, (4) provision of school stationery, uniforms, assistive devices, and the printed self-learning material, (5) provision of catch-up and remedial classes to out-of-school children, (6) conducting training sessions for school staff, focusing on issues such as dealing with children with low academic achievement and disciplining a child for misbehaving at school, (7) provision of 3 months of cash assistance to vulnerable families with children at risk of drop-out or child labor, to cover their basic food and non-food needs and restore their coping capacities. In total, 560 persons will benefit from the food security component of this project. 120 school staff will benefit from training sessions on PSEA, child safeguarding policy, and protection mainstreaming. 6,000 community members, mainly caregivers will benefit from training on the importance of eliminating child labor. The project team will provide services to the identified cases through the child protection centers, distributed across the Gaza Strip, one in each governorate. A high level of coordination with relevant ministries and relevant clusters, including Education and Protection will be utilized in this project. Finally, Tdh will report its intervention to the Education and Protection Clusters, UNRWA, CP AoR Working Group. Tdh will be actively involved in the cash-programming working group, relevant ministries, and all other relevant actors. The total project budget is USD 480,000. The primary cluster for this Project is the Education Cluster.

1 - Needs

The ongoing Israeli blockade has devastating consequences on all aspects of life, OCHA estimates at 1.3 M people in need in the Gaza Strip of multisectoral services, including almost 0,5 M people in need of humanitarian assistance to access education in 2022. The Gaza Strip has one of the highest unemployment rates in the world, and is expected that 64% of Gaza's population live in poverty in 2021. On top of the chronic crisis, the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic and the recent May hostilities have further compounded the already fragile situation leaving families and caregivers unable to provide for their children’s basic needs. The most commonly used coping mechanisms are reducing the quality and quantity of consumed food, sending children to work, and withdrawing children from schools (Tdh 2021; Tdh 2020). The number of identified cases of child laborers has significantly increased after the emerge of the COVID-19 pandemic compared to previous years. In the Tdh’s survey (Impact of COVID-19 on vulnerable children and their families in the Gaza Strip, 2020), 12.5% of the participants reported that child labor continues to be a negative coping mechanism that vulnerable families use to secure their daily income. In addition, the May escalation has also disrupted the livelihood of numerous families, pushing more children into child labor, with 5% of the households (HHs) in Gaza with school-aged children reporting having at least one child dropped out of school (MSNA, 2021). Across the oPt, it is estimated that 10.3 % of children (age 5-17) are engaged in child labor including hazardous labor, with boys twice as likely as girls to be engaged in child labor and six times as likely to be working under hazardous conditions (MICS, 2021). About 25% of children aged 16-17 throughout the oPt, and 5% of children between 10 and 15, are out of school (either dropped out or never enrolled). Dropout rates are higher for boys, who are expected to contribute to household income in poor families. Early marriage, commonly used by families as a coping mechanism to reduce expenses, is the most significant cause of early dropout among girls (HNO, 2022). Primary and secondary data findings of the Tdh RNA in June 2021 following the May hostilities reveal the urgent need to repair damaged schools and return children to a state of normalcy as soon as possible. Indeed, this school disruption due to the May escalation impacts furthermore the COVID-19 school disruption, and results in 88% of HHs in Gaza Strip with school aged children reported that their children were in need of catch-up learning programs because of the loss of learning due to COVID-19 related schools closure (MSNA, 2021). Based on Tdh’s RNA 2021, children from poor families and children with learning difficulties are perceived to be at a high risk of school dropout. Children at risk of drop-out need focused education services. The enrollment issue is higher for the children with disabilities (CWDs), with 46% of CWDs aged between 6-17 years old, who are not enrolled in school in the oPt (MSNA, 2021). For the rest of the CWDs enrolled in education, the risk of dropout is higher due to the lack of accessible educational facilities, gaps in assistive devices, transportation barriers, lack of trained school staff for these children. The result is that 71% of the HHs in the MSNA reported that their CWDs are facing challenges in accessing education services. This deteriorated situation has also exacerbated the need for protection and especially for children regarding psychosocial distress, mental disorders, and a higher risk of GBV. Given the scale of the violence visited upon the Gaza Strip, it is clear that the mental health needs of children must be addressed, both in schools and via community-based psychosocial health programs, since war leaves enduring episodes in children and adolescents. Finally, integrated child protection services, including case management and MHPSS services should be provided to children who display symptoms of distress. The most affected groups at children aged between 10 to 16 years old. It is estimated that some 678,000 children in the oPt are in need of protection and MHPSS services (MSNA, 2021) and that some 198,000 children in the Gaza Strip alone are in need of structured protection and psychosocial support, while nearly 36,000 children require individual case management (HNO 2021). In general, CWDs and girls are more subjected to GBV. Access of vulnerable children to comprehensive child protection services that involve the provision of individual case management needs to be enhanced. Additionally, activities that could empower their families like cash assistance. Under this Project, Tdh and partners will continue to provide protection services to vulnerable children mainly out-of-school and working children. Additionally, under the Action, the needs of children living with different types of disabilities will be met through the provision of protection and educational services provided by the Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children.

2 - Activities

Identification of vulnerable working and out-of-school children is a priority for the project team, the aim is to identify about new 1,000 children working, out-of-school or at risk of drop-out, distributed as 500 boys and 500 girls, distributed as 300 working children, 250 out of schools and 450 at risk of drop out. Boys are more at risk of child labour but girls are more at risk of early marriage resulting also in a drop-out of school. The process of identifying children will involve conducting field visits to working areas and the most deprived areas in Gaza. It will also involve visiting government schools and obtaining a list of children who have dropped out. Data obtained from the government schools will be validated by home visits, case by case, as the reliability and validity of MoEHE data on out-of-school children is not high. Concerning working girls, special attention will be dedicated to identifying them through home visits. Activities under the Protection Cluster will include (1) provision of individual case management and MHPSS services to child laborers, out-of-school children, and children at high risk of drop out, (2) referral to specialized services, as needed. (3) provision of cash assistance to vulnerable families whose children are at risk of drop-out or into child labor to cover their basic food and non- food needs and restore their coping capacities. In total, 100 vulnerable households (560 individuals, calculated based on an average of 5.6 members per household, per the PCBS publications) will be supported with conditional multipurpose cash. The customized Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool (UVAT) will be used to identify vulnerable families. The cash support will be conditional to reintegrating out of school children into schools or vocational training centers and not engaging children in any form of child labor. The cash assistance is calculated based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) to support families to cover their needs. The cash assistance will be 1,046 ILS per month for a 3-month duration, per the recommendations of the CWG. The cash will be disbursed through cheques from, Bank of Palestine. At least 10 female-headed households will be targeted and priority will be given to families who have children with disabilities and chronic diseases. The main activities under the Education Cluster will include (1) reintegration of school children in official education and vocational education, including for children with disabilities who will benefit from the adapted education services and assistive devices, including hearing aid and eye glasses; (2) provision of school stationery, uniforms, the printed self-learning educational material developed by the MoEHE; (3) provision of catch up and remedial classes for 150 of out-of-school children and 350 of children risk of drop out, and (4) conducting training sessions for school staff, focusing on different issues such as dealing with children with low academic achievement and how to discipline a child for misbehaving at school. The project interventions will be carried out by Tdh and partners. Tdh will liaise with MoSD, Protection and Education Clusters and CWG to provide inclusive quality protection services. The targeted beneficiaries, including children and adults from different age groups and genders, including seniors were involved in the design of the project, including the proposed activities. Additionally, both men and women from different targeted localities were involved in the project by identifying the needs and activities. During the implementation, beneficiaries will be involved in monitoring the project activities. The activities of the project are tailored according to the specific needs of children, taking into account their gender and their age groups. Over the past years, Tdh and the partners' team have identified a good number of child laborers and out-of-school children aged 7 years as such children were not enrolled at school at age of 6. Tdh has also identified cases that will not be enrolled/have not yet enrolled in schools due to the absence of a birth certificate. Tdh works closely with UNRWA to make sure to issue a birth certificate for such children. It is unfortunate that the main cause that prevents families from issuing the birth certificate is the limited financial ability to pay the registration fee and the accumulated penalty fee of not registration children earlier.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Tdh and partners will fully respect, protect beneficiaries’ safety and fully respect their dignity. Tdh and partners will achieve this by protecting beneficiaries’ personal and sensitive data such as names and Id numbers. Tdh and partners will provide services that are trailed according to identified needs of beneficiaries, taking into account the context, and will have in place all the mitigation measures to ensure beneficiaries' safety. As a good practice for Tdh, no action would be planned and/or implemented without the child’s agreement, under the supervision of her/his legal gradient. Additionally, Tdh will not publish or report any data that could reveal any identification data, all publications/reports will include only aggregated data. Tdh and partner organizations will also involve beneficiaries in as many decisions as possible, including monitoring the project implementation. All interventions will seek to avoid exacerbating the situation for children by ensuring properly planned and implemented interventions that take into account children’s and their family's needs to access humanitarian services. All services will be provided in a safe protection environment. To ensure CWDs are safe while accessing Tdh's services, partners will be committed to ensure accessibility of services to CWDs such as having ramps, adapted signage and other disability adapted measures. The Action will also cover transportation costs allowing access to the services to CWDs and children from remote areas. To ensure the beneficiaries' protection related to the cash assistance, tailored household financial management visits, and financial management coaching for the family will be conducted ensuring that the cash intervention does not cause harm. In addition, a communication channel in case harm resulted from the cash intervention will be available. Tdh and partners have zero tolerance for exposing beneficiaries to violence and or harmful practices, all measures will be taken into account to grate this, including full compliance to Tdh Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP), PSEA Policy, Code of Conduct, and confidential mechanisms. All staff members are trained on the CSP, PSEA, and the Code of Conduct and have signed the policies. Project staff will receive training on disability inclusion approaches and practices.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In respect of equality and impartiality, all identified cases, regardless of their gender, age groups, and disability status will receive the protection services according to their assessed and identified needs. All Project beneficiaries will have equal access to services, including beneficiaries with disabilities and beneficiaries living in the Access Restricted Areas and the most vulnerable areas like camps. Tdh will also target equally men and women without any regard to any discriminatory factors such as age. The proposed activities are based on the different needs that were identified through different methods, mainly focus group discussions, monitoring visits, and interviews with key informants. During the design phase, project beneficiaries were engaged heavily and all their needs were included in the project activities. During the project design, beneficiaries were also consulted through conducting a series of FGDs and interviews and in-depth interviews. Beneficiaries will also be part of the project monitoring. Children were also involved in the assessment and design of the project and will be actively engaged in monitoring the project activities. Finally, as with any advocacy product of this project, children will propose, design, and implement such products. To overcome access problems related to COVID-19 mitigation measures, Tdh and partners will ensure access to children living with disabilities through obtaining lists of CWDs from organizations that provide services to CWDs. Also, Tdh and partners have already a list of CWDs in need or previously benefited from MHPSS services. Those children will be contacted using different blended methodologies that involve in-person modalities and remote modalities. Partners will make sure to have disability adapted environment and transportation fees will be covered by the Action. If needed, Tdh and Partners might conduct MHPSS services in places that are accessible to CWDs that could involve home visits and provision of services in grassroots organizations. Also, Tdh and partners will develop the MHPSS manual that could be used by CWDs themselves. Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children has already developed such tools for children with hearing disabilities.
