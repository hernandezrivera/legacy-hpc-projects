Project Summary

CAFAD FSL HRP 2023 plans to reach 540,933 beneficiaries segregated by age& gender based on FAO standard criteria . The appeal aims to reduce the number of people in IPC 3, 4 and 5 in the selected counties (Uror, Nyirol, Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Longochuk and Kapoeta North) through emergency food assistance, complimentary livelihood and livestock support. These together with efforts to increase resilience and recovery of the disaster-affected population are to create strong communities that can withstand shocks and reduce over-reliance on relief. The proposed activities are linked to 2022 FSL HRP Strategic objectives 1, 2 and 3.

In alignment with FSL CO1 that “to provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations”, CAFAD plans to support 5,000 men, women and youths with Food For Asset (FFA) in the selected counties targeting 50% women of the total beneficiaries and roles mirroring those on men in committee membership. In addition, 2000 persons will be supported conditional cash transfer for assets building, training, communal savings support, CAFAD plans to support vulnerable and food insecure 3,000 HH with fishing gear and nutritious fast growing vegetable seeds.
CAFAD plans to support 90,156 HH in the target counties with main season crop, vegetable and fishing equipment aligned to FSL CO2 that to “Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support”. Also, 716,410 cattle, shoats, dogs, chicken and donkeys will be vaccinated against CPPP, CCPP, rabies, RVF and other endemic conditions in the selected counties and 15% of the vaccination target will be targeted with treatment against prevalent disease conditions in the counties including worm infestation, diarrhea, pneumonia, trypanosomiasis and ectoparasites. In addition, 150 CAHWs (25 in each county) will undergo refresher training on vaccination, treatment and disease surveillance in livestock.
In order to reduce the over-reliance on food aid and the dwindling donor resources, CAFAD also proposes to support 5000 persons including women, men and youth with training/ capacity building activities marrying with FSL HRP CO3 that to “Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks” establishing a nexus between humanitarian assistance and peace-building. The selected beneficiaries in each county will undergo training on farm production, income generation, nutrition sensitive food security, climate smart agriculture and reduction of post-harvest losses.
Women needs, rights, prevention of GBV and their voices will be taken into consideration in all the interventions and ensure integration, inclusion of youths in peace building. Accountability to the Affected Persons (AAP) will be taken into account by formation/training of gender-inclusive committees with complaint and feedback mechanisms including regular monitoring/review meetings in all interventions. All the activities proposed here-in will be conducted in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 through maintenance of social distance, use of masks, frequent sanitization of hands and compulsory use of other MoH SOPs and guidelines.

1 - Needs

The most recent IPC data indicates dire food insecurity (Emergency phase 4 and IPC5) concerns in the proposed counties for intervention above in October- November2022 while the projection period from April-July 2023 might see a larger population slip into destitute as the lean period progresses. The current IPC for Acute Malnutrition (January- March 2023) in children under 5 years; SAM and GAM rates remains at phase 4 (Critical) with a high likelihood to deteriorate in the coming months without intervention. The major factors contributing to high acute malnutrition in the Counties include high prevalence of diseases (up to 36%), poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum dietary diversity: 15%) and elevated level of food insecurity.Reports from the local authorities in late 2022 indicated dire needs of 27,800 IDPs and returnees in Wunayen and Koliet payams in Canal/Pigi County. IPC projects 5, 000 IPC5 in Oct-Nov2022 and April-July 2023! In Canal Pigi, the community depend on seasonal non-sustainable livelihoods and with the four years consecutive floods, crops destroyed before maturity exacerbating food insecurity. There is severe food consumption gap; HHs report consuming cereals every second day and 53% consuming 1 meal a day. The HHs hunger scale stands at 76%, 16% and 6% for moderate, severe and catastrophe hunger scale respectively. The households sell dry fish for sustenance though they lacked fishing equipment. There is over reliance on gathering wild fruits/leaves and barter trade. A flood impact assessment by UNFAO 2022 reported that the widespread waterlogging in Canal/Pigi had affected 74% of total cattle population, crops and displaced households creating conducive environment for outbreaks of animal and human diseases including malaria, diarrhea, rabies, rift valley fever and worm burden.3,500 people displaced to Canal/Pigi County due to fighting in Atar and Diel in Fangak County (Sept2022 OCHA SS Humanitarian snapshot report).A rapid Protection assessment in Pamai (Uror County) by ADA identified serious protection concerns of girls and women as they went about daily farming activities ranging from fear of abduction, rape/GBV. Child and forced marriages are a common place in the area mostly due to lack of resources by affected families who give out their daughters to older men who have cattle. Prolonged closure of the main road networks from Bor due to current floods will severely affect markets in the county that relies on supplies from Juba.The OCHA July snapshot reports Longochuk County as one of the five counties with highest access constraints due to active hostilities, physical environment and constant violence against humanitarian personnel and assets. 15.3% and 23.8% rated extreme & catastrophic needs phases and this is most likely to worsen into 2023 due to multiple effects of macroeconomic crisis, flooding, localized drought , low preparedness by state authorities, the Ukraine Crisis, conflict and lingering effects of COVID 19 pandemic.An IRNA assessment in Waat Nyirol County (Aug 2022) reported dire humanitarian needs after attacks by Murle herdsmen who left trails of destruction of farmlands, homesteads and livestock. The families resorted to eating wild fruits and leaves to sustain themselves. Many households were displaced into safer payams of Pading and Lankien where they are residing in schools and poor makeshift houses that have no basic sanitation facilities putting the women and children on the risk of contracting malaria or waterborne diseases in cases of outbreak. Women & girls in SS continue to be denied ownership rights to land, property and housing (SSLS Housing Land and Properties disputes 2019) due to customs. 22% of male respondents thought women should be prohibited from owning land hence the need for effective restitution and women empowerment through provision of resources they have control over and capacity building activities.

2 - Project activities

All targeting and transfers will be area specific in consultation with all stakeholders.1: Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits1.1: Main Season agriculture inputs are provided to 90,156 vulnerable women, men, girls and boys headed households in Uror, Nyirol, Canal/Pigi, Longochuk and Kapoeta North including Crop seeds (3 kg sorghum, 2kg maize, 2kg cowpeas), fast maturing vegetable seeds assortment(50gm Okra, 30gm green pepper, 20gm onion, 30gm Tomato, 20gm Collard, 30gm amaranthus and 30gm pumpkin seeds), fishing gear (1 monofilament, 1 box of 100 hooks and 2 twines) and a “no harm” hand farming tool (maloda or hoe according to preference) for each beneficiary distributed to increase food production. Time lines: Purchase or Preposition and distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits will be done in conjunction with the pipeline manager for FSL UNFAO. Rationale: Distribution will be done in early in the day (08:00 am 17:00 pm) and in an open area to ensure safety and protection for women and girls against sexual abuse and exploitation (PSEA). The process will be done in ways to prevent the spread and transmission of COVID 19 including strict follow of MoH guidelines and SoPs. 2: Livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment of common livestock disease conditionsMain activity: Livestock treatment and vaccination campaign; 716,410 livestock heads targeted for vaccination including cattle, shoats, donkeys, dogs and chicken, 15% of the animals treated plus 150 CAHWs (re)trained for 7 or 14 days in Uror, Nyirol, Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Longochuk and Kapoeta North. Time lines: Targeting for mass treatment, vaccination campaigns will be maximized during the dry season when the cattle are easily accessible near the homesteads. Rationale: Refresher training of CAHW teams will be planned in the dry season in order not to disrupt their agricultural activities at the onset of rains in May. The “one health” approach will be used in the treatment and awareness campaigns especially when dealing with zoonotic diseases in the target locations 3: Conditional food assistance through food for assets (FFA) 3.1: Community participatory identification of productive assets for creation/rehabilitation targeting 2, 000 IPC 3,4 and potentially 5 population in the target locations of Uror, Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Longochuk, Nyirol and Kapoeta North with conditional food. This is to build resilience and assets benefiting at least 50% women and girls.3.2: Conditional cash transfers to 5, 000 vulnerable households Rationale: Cash for social skills & safety nets distributed Conditionality on investments in assets, training, or communal work groups and planning committees in Uror, Canal/Pigi, Longochuk and Nyirol 4: Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality assist traders to re-supply markets/ local procurement for FAO ITFs & aggregation of produce for WFP assistanceRationale: This will target 5000 vegetable and crop kits locally sourced from farmers and traders in Uror, Nyirol and Longochuk Counties. A voucher system will be employed for seeds procurement. Accountability with complaint and feedback mechanisms will ensure inclusion of vulnerable target groups. 5: Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production of 2000 farmers, community nutrition volunteers (50% being women) and establishment of vegetable and crops farmer schools mentor programs for the farmers in Uror, Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Longochuk and Kapoeta North Main activities 5.1: Support value chain addition through agriculture, fishery and livestock products/ by-products preservation & marketing through trainings for 89,551 HHs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community engagement will involve consultative meetings with representatives of women, youth and PLWD to deliberate on their rights, voices, special needs and on the modalities of intervention including but not limited to beneficiaries selection, identification, distribution and the most important/urgent asset preferred for rehabilitation by majority of the community members. Our interventions will also target to reach not less than 10% PLWD and 50% women and girls.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CAFAD will hold consultative meetings with local community leaders including Boma chiefs, youth and women representatives, community elders and religious leaders to sensitize and inform them on intended activities, expectations and roles for each in monitoring progress and evaluation Community mobilisers and volunteers will be employed to ensure that information reaches the furthest of the villages. Information is also provided in printed banners, leaflets that will be in the local dialect of the target location for wider dissemination and understanding.AAP committees formation and training will be conducted by CAFAD to assist in beneficiaries identification, selection of sites for distribution, distribution process review and lodging of complaints through out the project activities.All these activities will be accompanied with COVID 19 messaging including mitigation measures like minimal contact with beneficiaries, use of face masks, regular hand washing and strict adherence of other MoH guidelines and SoPs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CAFAD will prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by CAFAD personnel and Service Providers; CAFAD adheres to its work plan on the prevention of SEA; and preventing and responding to SEA by CAFAD personnel. CAFAD ensures that the affected population knows and upholds their rights and dignity throughout our interventions and our staff are prohibited to offer No favors – whatsoever-are to be exchanged for the services offered. Our staff are prohibited to have sexual relationship with our beneficiaries and if found guilty, the management have the right to suspend the contract with culpable individual and follow due laws of South Sudan in procecution.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CAFAD will offer means for communities to provide feedback on programmes and to submit complaints, and to ensure that they receive a timely response; During program implementation CAFAD will review how complaints are received and handled, consult with communities on how they could best submit complaints, and develop a plan for establishing mechanism. CAFAD will have community communication agents at all the project Counties whose main function is to collect all feedback from the community and channel it to the concerned personnel for further communication and action as well as receiving feedback on the community concerns and closing the loop by reporting back to the community on the way forward/ action taken.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The patterns of relations between men and women are influenced by the marked inequality within the community. CAFAD’s Gender approach (2021-2025) aims at ways of increasing the impact of program and is committed to gender-sensitive indicators to assess the impact of actions on the situation of men and women and extend of power relations between them. CAFAD acknowledges that gender inequalities impact negatively on project outcomes and hence the need for strategies that enhance gender equality. So, the organization endeavors to take account of differences, experiences, concerns and needs specific to both sexes so that the projects it implements benefit both men and women to the same extent. This has increased the effectiveness and improvement of project quality. Gender equality empowerment will be integrated into the entire awareness programme. This will be targeted to women and girls to ensure that they understand their rights particularly on matters related to food security and how access those rights. Men will be targeted with the same in order to re-socialise young boys on government’s commitments to gender equality as a constitutional right and the reduction of gender-based violence. CAFAD has continuously encouraged women leadership in the entire project sites through involvement in the Boma Project Management Committee . CAFAD Policy seeks to mainstream gender throughout all programming, ensuring that indicators reect the importance of different roles that men and women play in sustaining their household’s livelihoods. CAFAD will seek to increase women’s involvement in decision-making and promote equal power relations in all processes. Tackling the issue of gender-based violence (GBV) in communities is a key component of CAFAD’s approach to gender equality. CAFAD Country Office and the Field Offices will adopt a “do no harm” policy to ensure that Project approaches do not give rise to gender-based violence or other forms of intimidation on women.Where land is required for purposes such as group farms and demonstration plots, written consent from the owners if private or local authorities if communal will be sought before use of such lands. Beneficiaries risk to violence during registration and distribution will be minimized by having teams assisting women and men on separate desks. The distribution will also be restricted to between 8am and 5pm to allow the beneficiaries to reach home in time- for the women to perform their family roles and avoid GBV caused by delays at the distribution sites; the timing also helps in safeguarding against abduction ( of boys and girls)as these perpetrators fear daylight. The number of Distribution points will also be increased to make sure beneficiaries do not travel over long distances to access our services. This will also be helpful in this era of COVID 19 as it goes a long way into making work flow faster.
