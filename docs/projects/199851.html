Project Summary

To mitigate the threat of explosive ordnance in Borno state Nigeria, DRC will continue to disseminate Explosive Ordnance Risk Education and conduct Non-Technical Survey of communities in local government areas of MMC and Jere. This intervention will allow people to live and work as safely as possible in the face of explosive weapons until contaminated lands are cleared. DRC targets 70,000 beneficiaries reached with EORE and 20 communities to survey. EORE and NTS activities will contribute to the freedom of movement, safe access to agricultural land and access to humanitarian assistance in the affected communities.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Northeast Nigeria remains critical, with the 11-year-old conflict stillaffecting the lives and livelihoods of millions of people in the region. According to the 2021Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 8.7 million people currently require humanitarianassistance across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States (BAY States) - a 10% increase from last year,and the highest figure ever recorded since current humanitarian operations began (excludingCOVID-19).1 Women and children account for 81% of those affected by the crisis, and 87% of allnew displacements; 58% of children are in need of protection services.2 Additionally, over 1.9million people have been internally displaced in the Northeast since the start of the conflict.3 Bornohas been particularly affected: indeed, the State hosts 81% of the total number of internallydisplaced persons (IDPs) in Northeast Nigeria, with Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) andJere Local Government Areas (LGAs) hosting an estimated 19% and 18% of the IDPs in Borno,respectively. Frequent attacks from Armed Opposition Groups (AOGs) and governmentcounterinsurgency initiatives continue to generate violence and insecurity, resulting in direconsequences for civilians, including poor living conditions, widespread famine, and human rightsabuses, which have in turn all contributed to significant displacement since 2014.

2 - Activities

DRC will focus on Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE), in order to reduce the riskposed by explosive devices to a level where people can live safely. This includes the deliveryof EORE sessions to at-risk communities. DRC will also train and equip community focalpersons, community leaders (Bulamas), youth leaders, teachers, women associationmembers, and scrap metal collectors on how to conduct EORE sessions to ensuresustainability. To complement the EORE efforts, DRC will carry out Non-Technical Surveys(NTSs) to collect and analyze data on the presence, type, and level of contamination, tosupport land release, in coordination with the Nigerian Police Force Explosive OrdnanceDisposal team.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The Risk and Compliance (RC) department, an independent unit at DRC manages the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) with an interface with protection department for complaints related to DRC programs. During field activities, the RC team creates awareness and sensitization about CRM, beneficiaries are informed how to access, what type of complaints to be raised and the various platforms to use which include the following; - Complaint's desk set up at distribution points. - Toll free lines are available in three different networks including Airtel, MTN and Glo. - Feedback days/ community meetings. - Feedback forms filled by Protection staff who encounter communities. - Complaints boxes are set up in strategic locations at DRC Offices Examples of complaints to be reported using the above CRM entry points include but are not limited to: 1. Sexual Exploitation, Harassment and Abuse 2. Take money or favours for assistance 3. Favouritism of family and friends 4. Exertion of pressure on beneficiaries or staff 5. Corruption 6. Theft 7. Falsification of records 8. Disregard of health and safety standards 9. Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information, including about an investigation or its parties 10. Retaliation against people for reporting suspected misconduct or witnessing in investigations thereof The RC department maintains a database where all records of all complaints are registered, categorized, and escalated to the various stakeholders for quick resolution.
