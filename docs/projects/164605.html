Project Summary

The project aims to:
 Increasing access to PHC through the provision of essential primary health services to cover the health care needs of vulnerable populations, including Reproductive, Maternal, New-born, and Child Health (RMNCH), as well as continuity of treatment for people who live with NCDs.
 Expanding access to specialized services for conflict-affected populations, including MHPSS and GBV services.
 Supporting public health awareness, as well as training of staff on key essential services, with task shifting of basic services to staff to provide care in their communities when mobility restrictions or health facility destruction occurs.
 Continuing support to mainstreaming of cross-cutting issues across all levels of healthcare response, such as age, gender, and disability, through incorporating Gender Equality Measures (gems) based on the Gender and Age Marker’s (GAM), integrating of MHPSS and physical disability care at facility and community levels, and support to GBV survivors.
These objectives will be achieved by supporting 6 PHC centers in Idleb and Rural of Aleppo (G0705018!Ariha CPHC ,G0703032!Qourqeena PHC ,B0200114!Qabtan Eljabal PHC,B0200188!Kafr Naha PHC,B0203008!Afrin PHC,and G0704020!Jisr-Ash- Shugur PHC).Each PHC center consists of an internal medicine clinic,GP clinic,pediatric clinic, gynecology clinic,dental clinic, pharmacy, and laboratory.in addition to the maternity center in Ariha (Ariha CPHC).
The services which will be provided are according to Essential Health Services Package (EHSP) which was developed by WHO and and are the following:
1.Integrated management of childhood illness (IMCI)
2.Reproductive health (RH) through the gynecology clinics and maternity centers that can receive the referred cases by protection service providers especially the SGBV survivors where all RH staff were trained on clinical management of rape (CMR) and (S)GBV.
3.Management of non-communicable diseases (NCD) through the internal medicine clinics where all referred chronic cases (elderly, women, and children) can be treated.
4.Management of communicable diseases (CD) through all of the clinics where the preventable and curative treatment is provided as well as the vaccine service that has a great role in the lifesaving of children. WHO announced that Syria is free of polio, where UOSSM was one of the health partners to terminate the polio-virus through its PHC centers.
5.Dental services.
6.PSS services and MH-gap consultations provided by trained GP on the MH-gap package.
7.Community health services.
8.Nutrition services.
9.Referring the cases with complications by creating a referral pathway from PHC to a high level of health facilities and to specialized centers like Woman and Girl safe space (WGSS) that is operated by UOSSM or operated by other health partners in the same area.
•Finally,the presence of primary health care centers will help to identify suspected cases of GBV and SGBV problems that will be referred to service providers and specialists when necessary without the beneficiary feels of the stigma that still exists in the Syrian context and culture.
Before starting the project implementation, MEAL team will make a regular collecting the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. Monitoring and evaluation of the quality of the service provision through analyzing the collected data based on the Log-Frame, the project will be monitored and evaluated through three different dimensions:
1) The operational dimension, for which the project officer is responsible,
2) The technical dimension, for which the technical team are responsible.
3) The objectives dimension, for which the MEAL team is responsible.
Monitoring tools:
•Performance Indicator Tracking Tables (PITTs), issued on a monthly basis.
•satisfaction survey among beneficiaries and stakeholders will be analyzed and reported to stakeholders.
•Project Evaluation Report, by the end of the project-term.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO 2019, provision of health services continues to be disrupted by the ongoing conflict. Attacks on health have rendered 46 percent of hospitals and PHCs in Syria as either partially functional or not functional, while in some areas humanitarian partners have been unable to secure sustained access to populations in need.Displaced populations and returnees, particularly, are vulnerable to outbreaks of infectious diseases due to unsanitary living conditions, overstretched health services, and low coverage of routine immunization.37% of the affected population requires (RMNCH) services; and 41 % requires treatment for (NCDs), and at least 15 % requires (MHPSS) services. nearly 2/3 of women reported delivering at home with a skilled attendant. Pregnant adolescent girls and their babies are at high risk of medical complications and death. Family planning is widely available, with free contraceptives were reportedly available in 192 out of 271 sub-districts surveyed in 2018, consistent with the fact that 28% of surveyed health workers reported the non-availability of organized FP services in the community.WHO estimates that approximately 12% of the population have diabetes and 20% hypertension.UOSSM manages 6 PHCs within which the medical and non-medical staff have provided different types of services.The cessation of funding for this program will create a large gap in the delivery of health services in northern Syria and deprive a large number of people affected by the conflict, who are mostly below the poverty line, where the most vulnerable groups (elderly, children, women, and persons with disabilities) are considered the largest percentage of beneficiaries(1,171,442) consultations were provided to (736,381) beneficiaries during last two years# of female beneficiaries was 62.5% and the percentage of children > 5 was 46%151,000 vaccinations doses were provided during 2019.There were 1.600 deliveries in the maternity center during the last two years.

5 - Implementation of activities

Provide essential PHC services, including, BEmONC and referral, by selecting the safe and accessible locations, recruiting medical staff, training staff on the EHSP, equipping the PHCs with the necessary equipment, purchasing medical supplies, providing the following services by the qualified staff: BEmONC, IMCI, NCD, RH, CD, IYCF, MH, -creating service mapping of the different humanitarian actors, and coordinating with health partners in the area to create referral pathways linked with the PHC clinics and to refer patients who need high level of health care.Provide HFs with essential medicines and medical supplies, by preparing the list of essential medicines, supplies according to the EHSP that meet the needs of the targeted beneficiaries, purchasing the medical supplies, delivering the supplies to facilities. (medicines will be provided in various types to suit all gender and age groups.Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and CHWs, by conducting training need assessment according to EHSP, providing training packages in the first half of the project based on the need assessment results, providing refresh training in the second half of the project training to ensure the quality of the provided services.Increase access to health services by establishing functional health facilities and mobile medical units and supporting referral by supporting six PHCs-rehabilitating the selected HFs to be more suitable for beneficiaries and creating areas that secure the privacy of patients and prevent the sexual exploitation ( RH clinic, isolated WC), equipping the health facilities according to EHSP, coordinating with Health Partners in the area to establish effective referral pathways.Applying IPC Procedures: UOSSM developed IPC SOPs where all staff were trained on theses SOPs, all PHCs provided with IPC Kits , and Supervisors used a check lists for monitoring and evaluation. Ariha CPHC was part of IPC project supported by WHO.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding and shared toilets as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery: possibility: high, effect: highmitigating measures: Establishing separated toilets for men and women-Ensuring that the waiting areas are sufficient for patients –Allocating the guard to regulate the flow of patients –Establishing an easy-to-reach complaints mechanism (complaints box, WhatsApp number, exit interviews) and activating the role of the M&E team to evaluate the satisfaction of patients and to ensure that the project management team is dealing with the project problems. Where UOSSM adopted this mechanism in the ongoing PHCs and benefited from the feedback of beneficiaries in redesigning the project to meet the beneficiaries' needs and that shows the engaging of beneficiaries in the in-project designs-Training all employees on the policy of preventing harassment in the workplace and including this policy in their contracts 2- Neglecting the most vulnerable (children, women, the elderly and the disabled): possibility: moderate, effect: highmitigating measures: Employing female staff to ensure that services are being provided women–Ensuring that the logistics are suitable for elderly and disabled people (e.g wheelchairs)- Activating the role of the monitoring and evaluation team to evaluate the satisfaction of patients and to and to ensure that the project management team is dealing with the project problems.3- Discrimination in service delivery: possibility: moderate, effect:high mitigating measures: A comprehensive services mapping of the area - Activating the role of the CHWs to communicate with the population in the targeted communities and to identify the main needs - Selecting accessible and safe locations of HFs. Monitoring the risks: Post-monitoring/client feedback forms are used. Resources Required: Monitoring staff.Acceptable Level of Risks: Yes.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• UOSSM will coordinate closely with the health clusters, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustments are needed to the intervention.• Location remains accessible for the team and for the beneficiaries.• Inflation and currency exchange rates remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned deliverable quality and quantity.• Local qualified staff required for project activities are available and adequate.Risk:• The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities.• The close of borders which prevent the shipments of supplies and medications to access to facilities.mitigation:• Develop a strong risk management strategy, (preparing an emergency plan /plan B/ according to the expected scenarios in conflicted areas.• Capacity building plan for the staff.
