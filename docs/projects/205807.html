Project Summary

Between July 2022 and June 2023, an estimated 1.4 million children under five are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition based on analysis and estimations from the results of the SMART nutrition surveys, Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) and program admission trends. As for the severity of the situation, during July and October, 30 counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical), 15 counties in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious), 19 counties in IPC AMN Phase 2 (Alert) and 13 in IPC AMN Phase 1 (Acceptable). Approximately 345,893 will need SAM treatment, while another 1,057,703 will need MAM treatment. Acute malnutrition burden will be highly concentrated in the four states, Unity being one of them.
In the case of Eastern Equatoria State as projected from the IPC Acute Food Insecurity And Malnutrition Analysis Report of November 23, 2022, from April to July 2023, the food security situation was projected to remain dire with an estimated 578,000 people (51.3% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 213,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 365,000 people are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity.
As presented by the FSNMS, the proposed project seeks to address the prevalence of malnutrition in Unity and Eastern Equatoria States, covering Rubkona and Torit Counties respectively by promoting increased access to prevention and treatment services for children under 5 suffering from severe and moderate acute malnutrition and pregnant and lactating women in targeted vulnerable communities through referrals, increased percentage of households with improved food security and nutritional status by 20% in the target community, and enhanced access to functional nutrition services through the renovation of nutrition treatment centres, delivery of equitable WASH services within target locations. Targeting 30,000 children under the age of five, and 20,000 PLWs.

2. Project activities

Overall Objective: To significantly reduce malnutrition rates among severely malnourished girls and boys under five years and pregnant and lactating women affected by humanitarian crises in Rubkona and Torit Counties via emphasizing the nutritionally critical first 1000 days. Outcome 1: Increased access to prevention and treatment services for children under 5 suffering from severe and moderate acute malnutrition and pregnant and lactating women in vulnerable communities of Rubkona and Torit Counties. Output 1.1: All children under 5 years old and PLWs are actively screened to detect malnutrition and are timely referred for treatment in the Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) / Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP). Activities:1.1.1. Organize regular community based MUAC screening, case identification, and referrals of children under 5 years and PLWs.1.1.2. Train health, nutrition workers and community volunteers on CMAM and MIYCN. Output 1.2: Women and men caregivers and other community members are equipped with knowledge to prevent acute malnutrition among children under 5 and PLWs. Activities1.2.1. Provide regular sensitization sessions (on MIYCN, Nutrition, Health, WASH, and childcare practices) for mothers and caregivers of children U5.1.2.2. Organize Mother-to-Mother Support Groups (MtMSGs) within catchment areas to facilitate open discussions and demonstrations and utilize these peer groups as a channel to further promote and protect adequate MIYCN practices.1.2.3. Strengthen the capacity of CNV to provide key messages on health/nutrition/WASH and childcare practices, as well as conduct regular nutrition screening and referral of children U5 and PLW and train Mothers/caregivers on the Mother MUAC approach. Outcome 2: Enhanced response to nutritional interventions due to a supportive and child-centric environment. Activities: 2.1. Construction and maintenance of Child Friendly Spaces, and water points.2.2. Build the capacity and equip the CNVs, and WHH staff to supervise and implement activities at the CFS.2.3. Conduct Sensitization and counselling sessions for caregivers/guardians on different topics related to MIYCN, health, WASH (hygiene promotion) childcare practices and participatory cooking sessions. Outcome 3: Increase the percentage of households with improved food security and nutritional status by 20% in the target community. Output 3.1: Target participants are trained in sustainable vegetable gardens and small animal-keeping husbandry.Output 3.2: Vegetable garden seedlings and livestock breeds that are more resilient to climate change are distributed. Activities3.1.1. Training identified target participants on sustainable vegetable gardens integrated with biofortified crop cultivation techniques and small animal husbandry. 3.1.2. Conducting awareness-raising sessions on the nutritional benefits. 3.2.1. Distribution of vegetable garden seedlings and livestock breeds. Outcome 4: To enhance the nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2024.Output 4.1: Availability of a comprehensive and actionable dataset that includes specific indicators related to nutritional status within the surveyed population.Output 4.2: Series of well-organized and successful exchange visit programs conducted. Activities 4.1.1. Nutrition SMART Survey 4.2.1. Identify key project focus areas for exchange visits. Key indicators1.1.1. Number of children (6-59 months) and PLWs screened and referred for treatment of either SAM or MAM1.1.2. Number of health and nutrition workers and community volunteers trained on CMAM and MIYCN2.1.1. Number of well-established and/or renovated CFS, and water points. 2.1.2. Number of children (0-5) using CFS/ and Number of parents of children (0-5 years) participating in CFS sessions and participatory activities.4.1.1. Number of SMART surveys conducted in targeted areas.
