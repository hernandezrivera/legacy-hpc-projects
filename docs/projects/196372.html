Project Summary

This project aims to support the most seriously affected and vulnerable categories in Rohingya community. To operate the project, at first we consult with SNFI Sector and Shelter Focal points of the targeted camp and collect the data or needs before engagiging in an activity, then we ensure basic requirements including relevant approvals by the Government and follow the technical standards and policicies by the Sector. We adopt an integreted approach in implenting the project. The project complies with shelter criteria and non-food items (NFI) for the Rohingya Refugees and Host community. The project covers 500 new Mid Term Shelters in camp 8W (block F) and repairs of MTS (490) in camp-5 (block-D), in camp-10 (block-C) and in camp-17 (block-C). We are also planning to distribute NFI support in Block-F in camp 8W through In-kind modality. In the construction of New MTS, we ensure the design and quality provided by the government (RRRC) & ISCG and we will supply bamboo after getting treated from the suggested place. We also ensure the HH level site improvements (HH level drainage, slope stabilization for individual shelters), accessibility improvements ( i.e. passage over the drainages, ramps, handrails based on the people with disabilities needs). For the repairs of MTS, we collect the data from HH to HH and find the needs where repair is needed. This is how our participatory activity also be met and a relationship with the community will be developed. In the provision of shelter activities, we also involve women, older people, people with disabilities and other forms of gender diverse population. Women will be invoved in basket production, older people will be involved in bamboo mat production and people with disabilities will be involved in distribution point. MSI will make their Project staffs and volunteers get trained in The Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) with the help of ISCG's PSEA team.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

It is reported in J-MSNA that, 81% and 69% of households, respectively, reported issues with their shelter. 32% and 28% of households, respectively, reported not having made improvements despite have reported issues with their shelter. This is why Multi Serve International (MSI) wants to take this project with importance. We have found that there are needs of New MTS construction in Camp 8W. This is why MSI has taken initiative to build 500 New MTS in those camps in 2023. And also we distribute NFI through In-kind modality in camp 8W in block-F. On the other hand, we have constructed 490 shelters in camp-5 (block-D), in camp-10 (block-C) and in camp-17 (block-C) in 2022 where we will provide repair support when it is needed. We distribute NFI to the households in block-F in camp 8W and hope a community relationship will be developed with them in participatory activity for HH level site improvement. We also involve women, older people, people with disabilities and other forms of gender diverse population in our projects. MSI hopes, this is how at least we can make a contribution to fulfill the need assessment and objectives of JRP will be met.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Before engaging in an SNFI activity, MSI will consult with the SNFI Sector first, then we will go for funding arrangement. After getting a positive suggestion from the Sector we will go for government approval according to the policy. We also adopt the integrated approach and inter-sectoral camp-based coordination with the sector and government (in particular, with CiCs). To improve relations with Rohingya refugees and government, MSI also ensure the predictability and continuity & transparency of information. We think, this is how our project aligned to the Rationalization Principles.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

To mainstream protection, MSI marks the people at risk and prioritize their safety, dignity and avoid causing harm. To prevent or recover from violence and exploitation we refer them to the ISCG or Protection Sector. In the phase of our project design, we emphasize on the internal partitions having minimum 6 feet height and a door with latch, woven mats for partitions between neighboring shelters to ensure their privacy. We maintain a minimum distance of two feet between shelter rows and consult with individual households when constructing new MTS for their privacy and dignity. We include HH-level site improvement in our shelter programs to improve the access and mobility and reduce disaster risk. For the assistance to EVHHS (Single female-headed household, person with disability, person with a chronic illness, older persons, child-headed household) with no able-bodied male member, our Rohingya volunteers (18 to 45 years old) provide porter and labor support.

3.2 - Gender

To mainstream Gender in our project, we reflect gender analysis in the planning documents and situation reports using SADDD. We collect and analyze SADDD and coduct a participatory gender analysis to understand different shelter and identify the gaps, needs, capacities, barriers and aspirations with special shelter support needs. We try to meet gender-specific needs. We involve women, girls, men and boys equally and meaningfully in decision-making, implementation and monitoring and training of the shelter programs. We ensure the training of all our staffs and beneficiaries including men and women on PSEA.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Throughout the project, MSI provide special support to child headed households. We ensure gender specific needs such as existence of child- and women- friendly spaces. We pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing services, e.g. persons with disabilities, elderly and children. According to our child-safeguarding policy, we don’t engage any child age below 18 in our projects or cfw-programs. Besides, EVHHs (Single female-headed household, person with disability, person with a chronic illness, older persons, child-headed household) with no able-bodied male member, identified based on the SNFI Sector criteria are assisted with porter and labour (18 to 60 age) support when MTS construction, repair assistance and NFI are provided. Particular protection cases are going to be considered as vulnerable HHs (based on protection referral on case-by-case basis).

3.4 - Diversity

When designing and constructing our shelters, we pay special attention to the diverse group. We ensure equal and inclusive access to shelter and NFI that addresses the specific needs of women, girls, men, boys and other at-risk populations (LGBTIQ, persons with disabilities) etc. Especially our assistance to people with disabiliteis is designed as we provide them porter support when shelter materials are distributed. We also provide them labor support in repairing the shelters. Even the design of MTS also will be according to consultation with them.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure AAP, MSI sets-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can be involved in all participatory aspects of project cycle from design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation and establish measures for affected populations to measure the appropriateness of interventions where we can address concerns and complaints. We support the development of communities' and individual capacities and assist people to claim their rights. We provide the code of conducts, PSEA policy and complaints procedure of our organization routinely to the community. MSI has a PSEA focal point assigned to each of its project implementation areas and maintains its own code of conducts and PSEA policies. We prioritize direct/face-to-face sessions/meetings. We ensure community participation where all the beneficiaries can contact with our PSEA focal point through mobile or at our office, we listen to them and provide them the need based appropriate assistance.
