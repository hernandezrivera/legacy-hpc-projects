Project Summary

This project is planned to target conflict affected girls and boys and their families in Abu Kershola locality in South Kordufan; who have been exposed to the risks of family separation, Sexual Gender-based Violence (SGBV) , mine risk, psychological distress, and child labor were reported in all the assessed locations. During May and June 2019, twelve cases of SGBV were reported in Umbrembeita, Khor Aldaleeb and Alfait Um Abdalla villages. Women were attacked as they fetched water, collected firewood or worked the fields. Some 13 cases of separated children were reported in Alfait Um Abdalla village. Some of those children had been placed in alternative families where immediate family members could not be traced. Presence of UXO and ERWs was reported in Umbrembeita, Alfarsha and Kaleng. This limits access to land for farming.
This will be done through the delivery of quality preventative and remedial interventions to improve response to emergency humanitarian child protection needs in communities affected by conflict. Save the Children (SC) will ensure the provision of timely and rights based child protection services to the most deprived and marginalized girls and boys. These services will include Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) for the Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), prevention and response to the needs of child victims of Sexual and Gender Based violence (SGBV), vocational training, support services, and life skills training for extremely vulnerable children and adolescents affected by conflict, as well as assistance to child victims of landmines. Although the number of identified of UASC is relatively low, 13, we expect to identify more children. To prevent and mitigate child protection risks in communities affected by conflict, SC will strengthen capacity of social workers, teachers, law-enforcement staff, and to prevent and raise awareness around key child protection risks, such as family separation, sexual gender based violence, and landmines. SC will invest in capacity building of CP Sub-sector members including the National Council on for Child Welfare and other SC humanitarian partners to ensure the quality of child protection interventions. In order to reinforce the resilience of communities and strengthen capacity of child protection institutions, SC will support community based child protection mechanisms and civil society organisations to disseminate information on relevant services and contribute to advocacy and the referral of child protection cases. Accordingly, gender sensitive measures will be adopted to mitigate these programmatic risks including adapting schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, promoting a girl-friendly space once a week to ensure that adolescent girls are able to participate, adapting case management to the different types of violence faced by boys and girls. To mainstream gender in this project, the IASC Guidelines for integrating Gender-based Violence interventions in Humanitarian Settings were adopted as follows: there will be a reference document for ensuring quality in planning and coordination and staff will be trained on the guidelines. A SGBV screening of any alternative care facilities to be used for UASC will also be included as a first measure.

1 - Needs

In Sudan, number of people who need humanitarian assistance is 5.7 million; of these, 2.7m children (55% girls, 45% boys) need protection. With the decline in armed conflict and large scale intercommunal clashes, the security situation in the country has improved and IDPs have experienced more freedom of movement. This improved security situation has been bolstered with the implementation of the Government’s weapons collection campaign that started in 2017 in some states. Despite this however, IDPs—particularly women and girls—still face high protection risks and continue to be harassed, and occasionally killed or raped. During 2018, missions by the Special Representative of the Secretary-General (SRSG) on Sexual Violence in conflict, the SRSG for Children in Armed Conflict, and the UN Independent Expert on the situation of Human Rights in Sudan all noted some improvements, although violations continue to be reported. Survivors of sexual violence may be fearful of reporting or seeking assistance, which may lead to underreporting, according to the Sudan HNO 2019. Children affected by the fighting in the Jebel Mara, particularly those displaced, remain exposed to various protection risks such as exposure to violence, family separation, deteriorating living conditions and recruitment by armed actors. These risks have a devastating impact on their well-being, physical security and future prospects with girls bearing the heaviest brunt of the conflict. There is an urgent need to support children and families in Central Darfur (Zalingie, Golo and Nertiti) as well as North Darfur (Kutum, Elfasher, Tawilla, and Kabkabiya). Although the overall security situation in Sudan slightly improved in 2017 with an estimated 386,000 IDPs voluntarily returning to their places of origin across the country, an estimated 1.6 million IDPs are still living in camps. In Darfur region, despite this improvement, inter-communal conflicts between sedentary farming communities and nomadic-pastoral communities over access to, use of, and management of resources, especially land continue, to cause insecurity resulting in new displacements. This coupled with limited economic opportunities has increased the strain on the available resources to cater for the host, IDPs and the returnees. In addition to the protracted conflict, Sudan has been experiencing an influx of refugees from neighboring countries, poverty and deteriorating economic situation. With an estimated 8,500 refugees from Chad living in two sites in Central Darfur, it is resultantly decreasing the capacity of the host communities to support the population needs including the much-needed protection services.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

SC will work on neutral impact on environment with mitigation or enhancement to ensure that the proposed activities will have no negative impact on the environment. Materials will be procured locally at state level to avoid extra charges of transport such as doing bulk procurement at country office and transport to project location. If there is an existing structure to accommodate the CFS, the structure should be inspected for its appropriateness before usage. Moreover, all prevention activities including Family Tracing and reunification (FTR), training, awareness and strengthening of community mechanisms are expected to positively affect the ongoing and any future conflicts, through restoring family links and reduce further separation. The project will support the effort of minimizing energy use and instead use renewable sources of energy through orientation sessions at community level to influence the mind-set of the local people on how to positively deal with their local environment and avoid negative actions contributing to damaging/destroying their environment. The project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and local communities to be part of planning mitigation measures and better environmental preservation. The environmental/climate change impact will be monitored during the implementation of the project through updating the section of environment on CRSA and including it in the yearly reporting of project implementation. Most of the project activities will be focused on knowledge dissemination and capacity building of different categories of community groups, the construction is limited to child friendly spaces where environmentally friendly local materials will be used such as mud and solid blocks as well as tents made from cloth for shading. The CBCPNs will attempt to intervene in other community issues including response to potential land conflict, plans for education, training and common activities in local environmental issues as part of the training plans.

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Accountability:To ensure accountability to the affected community, SCS has consulted communities, government authorities and other relevant partners in the design of this project. The consultation with communities has involved both women and men as well as children. The accountability mechanism explained in the accountability section will provide opportunity to the affected population to raise their concerns and claim their rights. The project considers conflict sensitivity by understanding the situation, examining how each activity may influence the conflict and work to maximize the positive and minimize the negative impacts of the proposed interventions. Both the newly IDPs and host communities will benefit from the project interventions. The integrated protection and education interventions will enhance the capacity of communities through formation and training of community-based structures; PTAs and CBCPNs to sensitize them, raise their awareness and capacity towards protection of children and communities. Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm:Save the Children will strive to ensure that the proposed interventions will minimize unforeseen or unintended negative effects on the targeted population which could potentially expose them to further physical and psychosocial risks. Save the Children will work closely with its various stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to analyze the security situation and to avoid tensions between host communities and IDPs through its holistic approach to projects whereby both old and new IDPs as well as host communities are targeted based on the prioritization of needs.
