Project Summary

Since March 2020, the already challenged healthcare system in the oPt is experiencing added strain from the COVID-19 outbreak. The reallocation of already scarce resources to respond to the pandemic, along with the contagion of healthcare workers, has undermined people’s access to health. Direct mortality from the virus and indirect mortality from preventable diseases is of major concern. Overall, the pandemic has rendered an estimated 115,000 people in need of health humanitarian assistance. In Gaza, the rapid spread of the virus within the community since late August has posed a heavy burden on a healthcare system, already over-stretched due to 13 years of the Israeli blockade, internal Palestinian divide, recurrent hostilities, and violence. The care of non-communicable diseases has been postponed, affecting more than 64,000 people based on HNO 2021. Moreover, Diabetes Mellitus cases prevalence are 15.3 of Gaza populations, 98% are type II. Thus, they are one of the most vulnerable cases regarding Coves 19 based on CDC 2020. Based on the Diabetic Foot Clinic report at Shifa hospital 2020, there are about 3000-3500 new cases each of Diabetic Foot Ulcers. Above 50 years who represents 52% of the total number of diabetic foot patients. From 55-56% which is 1600-1900 cases of 3000-3500 needs admissions. 55% of the 3500 cases have moderate to complicated ulcers and need special foot care. Those cases suffer from complications like low and high amputations as for example 8% of the complicated ulcer cases (25% of the 3500) have high amputations and low amputations are 30% of the 25% complicated cases. 65% of the complicated cases have DMT (deep vein thrombosis), cardiac diseases, and hypertension. AVH and AAH will have a multi- dispensary team (MDT) to manage the cases. The MDT will include Endocrinologist, Cardiovascular, general surgery, PSS people, PT, nutritionist, and nursing. AVH and AAH will Target group are 500 patients with diabetic foot systems, 150 who have complications, 50 in need of admission and intensive medical care

Ahli Hospital (AAH) in Gaza an implementing partner for three previous Country Based Pooled Fund (CBPF) projects either in elective surgeries, trauma management, and limb reconstruction with the support of Augusta Victoria Hospital (AVH) operated by the Lutheran World Federation (LWF), found it crucial to support vulnerable women and men who are suffering from diabetes. AVH will further develop AAH’s capacity in all matters related to diabetes. AVH will train 4 staff from AAH either through coaching, mentoring, and onsite demonstrations. AVH is aiming to visit AAH 2 times, the 1st for follow-up visit/training and another one visit for follow-up. LWF has strong financial, monitoring, and accountability systems in place and adheres to policies ensuring sustainable quality programming, such as the Code of Conduct, Child Protection, Core Humanitarian Standards, Gender Justice and Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) all to be shared with AAH. Furthermore, AAH will work with Handicap International to develop a checklist to determine the People with Disabilities (PWD), to track and report on them. GBV cases will be referred to specialized centers to address this issue. Scenario risk analysis was done based on previous experiences and similar projects and based on the new proposed COVID 19 possible scenarios as mitigations were planned such as remote management with AVH. Coordination with MoH, the Health Cluster (HC), Women’s Centers, the Union of Health Work Committees (UHWC) for complementarity and referrals are well defined in the project. Regarding finance and procurement, AAH has implemented successfully USAID projects, CBPF projects and followed either USAID or OCHA guidelines, and has a strong PMER (planning, monitoring, evaluating, and reporting) system in place. This project links to Health cluster Obj. 1 and to strategic obj.2

1 - Needs

In Gaza and related to the general situation, the care of non-communicable diseases and palliative care has been postponed, affecting more than 64,000 people. The July MSNA found that on average 47% of surveyed households in Gaza have at least one member with chronic disease. Diabetes mellitus (DM) is one of the most common non communicable disease (NCDs), a leading cause of sickness and death for women and men. Globally, the NCD rates are expected to increase by 17% and locally it is 15.3 % based on the diabetic clinic report of Shifa 2020. Thus In the next ten years and the biggest rate of NCD increase will be in women. Left untreated, diabetes can cause major health complications, including higher risk for bone and joint disorders, retinopathy, cardiovascular diseases, and peripheral artery disease. This is the case especially in the absence of a screening program and a protocol for screening diabetes. For instance, in the absence of a screening program eye complications will only be discovered at a very late stage. Diabetic foot complications may necessitate amputations. Also based on the Diabetic Foot Clinic report at Shifa hospital 2020, there are about 3000-3500 new cases each of Diabetic Foot Ulcers. Above 50 years who represents 52% of the total number of diabetic foot patients. From 55-56% which is 1600-1900 cases of 3000-3500 needs admissions. 55% of the 3500 cases have moderate to complicated ulcers and need special foot care. Those cases suffer from complications like low and high amputations as for example 8% of the complicated ulcer cases (25% of the 3500) have high amputations and low amputations are 30% of the 25% complicated cases. 65% of the complicated cases have DMT (deep vein thrombosis), cardiac diseases, and hypertension. As well as those patients suffer of many diseases as DM complications such as cardiovascular disease, retina problems, renal problems and HTN. Therefore, AVH and AAH will have a multi- displenary team (MDT) to manage the cases where retina and sever renal problems cases will be referred to other specialized hospitals.To respond to this need, AVH and AAH will Target group are 500 patients with diabetic foot symptoms, 150 of them who have complications, 50 are in need of admission and intensive medical care. AAH is committed to treat and follow up 500 DM patients with foot symptoms, by providing the below services. 1. Screening and management of patients for foot problems 2. Eye screening 3. Diabetes management 4. Lab testing (HbA1c, MAU) “blood and Urine collection) 5. Nutrition counselling 6. Referring patients with complications for advanced treatment (Retina problems) It is estimated that 150 patients suffer from diabetic feet complications and will multiple visits to the diabetes clinic for follow up and wound dressings and other minor surgeries whereas 50 patients will need hospital admissions for an average of 50 days due to complications associated with diabetes where they will need to have surgeries in the operating theater and treatment at Ward level. The AVH/AAH partnership adds value in developing AAH’s capacities in all matters related to DM guidelines and protocols and will help AHH in strengthening the capacity of the diabetes team that consists of a doctor, nurse, and nutritionist. AVH is a leader in the treatment of diabetes. Its Diabetes Care Center was established in 2003 to face the accelerating prevalence of diabetes and its complications and to create a model that can be replicated by the national healthcare system. The Diabetes Comprehensive Care Model (DCCM) aims to introduce a comprehensive diabetes care model in the Palestinian health sector, and to establish a national referral and resource center for diabetes care, prevention, and research. DCCM replaces the prevailing physician-centered approach in the Palestinian healthcare system, where patients only see their physicians to renew their medication prescriptions. The AVH diabetes team will transfer the knowledge and the experience they gained to AAH and will educate them and build their capacities to help patients with the adoption of a healthier lifestyle, including physical activity and exercise, nutrition, weight control and smoking cessation. After training AAH will be able to provide foot care, eye care, individual and group counselling, standardized diabetes laboratory testing (HbA1c and Micro albuminuria (MAU)), patient, family and community education regarding diabetes care and prevention of complications.
