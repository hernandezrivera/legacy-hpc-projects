Project Summary

DRC will continue to provide general services for the prevention and response of a range of protection risks observed in the camps. Direct case management services will be provided by strengthening synergies and integration with other protection areas (child protection and GBV) and other non-protection sectors (wherever possible and appropriate). These include among other individual protection assistance (IPA), referrals to specialised services, PFA. Capitalizing on past accomplishments, DRC will continue to invest in strengthening engagement with community-based protection mechanisms. This will include further capacitating existing community groups to identify, prevent and respond to protection risks, including through community-led awareness and sensitization, case management follow-up and referrals, protection monitoring and community-led peacebuilding and conflict resolution initiatives.
DRC will ensure its programmatic response is based on evidence that is context-specific and on a disaggregated analysis of age, gender and diversity (AGD). Accordingly, DRC will strengthen its protection monitoring activities to inform protection monitoring and advocacy work. This will include further capacitating staff and volunteers to collect data on a range of risks, including CP risks currently not monitored, and improve the sampling methodology to ensure data is systematically collected on specific vulnerabilities such as PwDs, single-headed households, sexual minorities. To strengthen its commitment to AAP and ensure a right based and AGD approach is implemented across its program cycle DRC will also seek to increase representation of diverse groups in its community structures to increase effective participation of more marginalised groups such as PwDs, women and adolescent girls in decision-making processes around issues that concern them. In addition, the pool of community volunteers will be diversified to expand participation of women volunteers. DRC caseworkers will continue to coordinate interventions with existing complaints and feedback mechanisms to avoid overlaps and duplication of assistance. This includes communicating and coordinating with the CFRM and CFP for every case management client to ensure he/she has not already been supported with assistance as well as referring all relevant non-protection cases to the CFRM/CFP.
DRC has a zero-tolerance policy to sexual abuse and exploitation. DRC is an active member of the PSEA Network and has a PSEA policy and an internal mechanism to report SEA concerns. DRC is arranging PSEA trainings to all of its staff and volunteers in the next weeks and regularly shares data on SEA with the Network. DRC is also an active member of the PWG and associated sub-sectors. Going forward, DRC intends to ensure more regular sharing of protection monitoring data with the PWG and sub-sectors in the form of summarised overviews of risks observed in the camps, continuing to comply to routine reporting requirements by the sector and ad hoc requests on information that may arise. DRC will also continue delivering protection mainstreaming training to non-protection actors according to the resources and guidance provided by the sector with the goal of facilitating meaningful access to assistance provided by the DRC and other actors, ensuring that humanitarian assistance and protection activities do not harm, that actors respond appropriately to instances of violence and coercion, and that refugees are assisted.
Finally, DRC will continue to coordinate with RRRC and CiCs. Coordination aims to ensure timely access, to avoid resource gaps and duplications. Advocacy briefings are conducted regularly with the CiCs to raise awareness on the importance of protection interventions and report on DRC’s work in the camps. DRC will continue to support in developing government officials' capacities in the area of protection and its importance during an emergency.

1 - Identified Needs

Protection needs are identified by DRC predominantly through its protection monitoring that ensures systematic data collection on a range of risks including SGBV, armed group activities, access to humanitarian/protection services. Needs are also assessed through a review of reports shared by the sectors including the PWG and GBV and CP Sub-Sectors. Over the past months, DRC protection monitoring reports have observed a deterioration of the protection environment in the camps. Increases in instances of forced recruitment by armed groups, threats/intimidation, indiscriminate acts of violence perpetrated by criminal gangs against the camp population such as physical abuse, torture, enforced disappearances. According to monitoring results, feelings of fear, mistrust and uncertainty are widespread among camp residents. Armed actors are not only consolidating their power and influence in the camps through violent and coercive means but are also increasingly stepping in to cover those roles traditionally occupied by humanitarian actors. Armed groups are increasingly involved in arbitration of GBV cases or community disputes, in the provision of safety, in providing informal jobs and distributing food and other items. All of this is further contributing to eroding community trust towards protection actors as well as further entrenching conservative socio-cultural norms/practices among the resident population, accounting for a significant reversal in gains on gender equality achieved in the pre-pandemic period. DRC protection monitors and DRC case workers have also documented alarmingly high levels of SGBV in the camps. This is corroborated by GBVIMS reports shared by the Sub-Sector. The latest 2021 Q1 GBVIMS report (April-June) reported that intimate partner violence constituted 84% of all reported GBV incidents and it was higher than the previous quarter. However, the report emphasized that the scale and prevalence of SGBV continues to be underreported, particularly for Child sexual abuse (currently accounting for 1.1% of incidents). Lockdown restrictions reduced access of GBV actors and services that severely limited avenues for women and girls to report abuses or access critical supports. Worsening security in the camps, due to the presence of criminal and armed groups, is also increasing risks for women and girls outside of the home. Women and girls often report being physically or sexually assaulted by male members of the community especially while visiting the latrines at night or during night patrolling, volunteers and armed group members often try to break into the shelters of widows and women-headed households where male family members are absent (Source: DRC protection monitoring). Moreover, reports from the Child Protection Sub-Sector in Cox’s Bazar highlight the increased risk of trafficking and smuggling of children and adolescents. There are numerous anecdotal reports of young boys being recruited for domestic labour in Cox’s Bazar and other districts. DRC case management has observed an increase in violence against children in the camps with children witnessing or experiencing violence in the home due to heightened stress, reduced resources, and lack of support services (DRC Case management, GBVIMs reports). DRC protection monitoring reports, while not specifically collecting data on children, are reporting increased instances of young adolescents being forcefully recruited by armed groups for military activities or engagement in illicit/criminal trades in the camps. Anonymized DRC case management data on child protection concerns, while not statistically representative, suggests a rise in the number of cases of child physical abuse, child marriage, child labour and neglect, compared to the pre-lockdown period (from January to April 2021). This is corroborated by results from the Joint MSNA (May 2021).

2 - Activities or outputs

Protection monitoring reports (12) are generated through the collection, storage, and analysis of systemic data to identify protection risk, threats, vulnerability that enables the evidence-based protection programming. DRC PM is targeting the entire population of 55031 from 4 camps and 2 host communities. However, DRC protection monitors will interview a sample of 4586 people per month from the locations that will rotate throughout the year. Increasing the capacity of 50 Rohingya and host community volunteers to collect data, referral pathways while adhering to the protection principles who can also be the fast responders during emergency Produce 4 rapid and ad hoc assessment reports to find relevant data and information during any emergency (flood, fire, landslide) and to have a relevant coordinate response by addressing the gaps, challenges, and presence of the service providers. Provision of 285 individual/household protection assistance (IPA), either one-time cash or in-kind to address specific protection needs Conduct 480 prevention message awareness sessions with Community Protection Groups (CPGs), as well as ad hoc sessions based on the findings of community protection meetings. These will include, but are not limited to, identifying protection risks and threats such as CP and GBV issues, rumour control, conflict resolution, positive parenting, COVID-19, menstrual hygiene, life-skills and many others. Implementing more than 8 community-led initiatives in four different locations; addressing one specific protection issue from each location identified through community discussions. Community-led initiatives will strengthen the community's capacity by building on the existing community-based protection mechanism. A series of capacity building sessions (48) on DRR, community in negotiation, non-violent conflict resolution, problem solving, critical thinking, and communication will include participants from both communities, with the goal of bringing different communities together to increase social cohesion within their communities. 1200 individuals from both host and camps will be receiving the trainings. Enhancing the capacity of relevant government authorities, service providers, and security forces to provide protection through training and advocacy. The staff's capacity will be built in order to facilitate trainings (16 workshops/meeting) and to develop resources to ensure the seamless delivery of messages about the importance of protection. Protection mainstreaming in all other sectors by increasing the capacity of humanitarian staff in protection mainstreaming and protection principles that will improve humanitarian programming in an integrated way through 6 Trainings
