Project Summary

The planned inclusive & gender –sensitive community based WASH interventions will help to create sustainable access to sufficient quantities of safe drinking water at institutions and community levels (rehabilitate, upgrade and establish water infrastructure), and assist to promote best sanitation practices and hygiene behaviour. The project interventions will be well-coordinated with technical government departments at state and federal levels, together other actors in the targeted areas to ensure synergies/complementarities and avoid any overlapping.
Participatory approach with full engagement of targeted communities (women & men) will be applied to ensure equal access to sufficient quantities of water. Locations of water points will – importantly - consider women accessibility (through women consultation), as availability and accessibility of safe drinking water will reduce work load and risks of violence on women and girls who are traditionally responsible of water fetching in many parts of the country.
Associated women and men WASH committees will be established & trained to manage and maintain rehabilitated/established water sources (resourced from user fees) in order to enhance the sustainability. Women consultation in site selection and participation in water management committees will be a condition to ensure women interests are reflected.
Water monitoring systems will be established for the different water points under the WASH committees’ management to ensure water safety, and enhanced through support to water quality testing capacities, evaluating and promoting indigenous water treatment methods.
The project approaches will also include implementation of CLTS and building the capacity of targeted communities in sanitation management to ensure sustainability and continuity of services. The project will support the construction/rehabilitation of latrines in schools, health facilities and markets following the international standards considering gender and disabled needs. Public latrines will be equipped with solar lights to enhance protection especially for women and girls considering GBV risks associated with open defecation.
Hygiene campaigns will be organized to raise awareness about sanitation and hygiene at community and institution levels (e.g. school hygiene clubs). Larger public campaign will be organized to raise awareness around COVID 19 and other hygiene related diseases. The campaign will use different tools (e.g. radio, TV, mobile microphones, drama & puppets shows, IEC materials ...etc).
The project will also support the targeted communities with hygiene supplies for promotion of handwashing and personal hygiene (mainly soap at both HH and institutional level). The project will also focus on establishing a considerable number of handwashing facilities in public places including schools, health facilities, & markets. Handwashing points will be supplied with soap, while including attractive messages & pictures for handwashing promotion. For gender sensitive sanitation and personal hygiene, women will be supplied with essential sanitary kits, and communities will be encouraged to initiate related small business for local production of essential sanitary supplies.
Nutrition sensitivity will be ensured through prioritizing areas with high malnutrition rates and providing sufficient quantities of clean water for households’ consumption as well as improving hygiene at community level in order to reduce the associated health hazards and avoid further deterioration in nutritional status especially among U5 Children &PLW. Families with malnourished children will also prioritized for access to different WASH related services and supplies.
To improve the efficiency and reduce the negative environmental impact; the project will establish/ replace/upgrade diesel operated water yards to solar pumps. Water harvesting infrastructure will also be introduced to improve water supply systems (e.g. Simple Check dams).

1 - Needs

In 2020, the WASH vulnerability remained at high level with about 8 Million people in need for WASH services in Sudan, with the rural areas showing a lower level of access to basic services (in which 263K in Blue Nile, 441K in Kassala, and 1.2M in North Darfur states). The number of people at risk of contracting any water-related diseased increase from 5.6 million in April to more than 10 million in October 2020, following the recent rains and floods in Sudan, and the massive destruction of key water and sanitation infrastructure (OCHA, Oct 20); heavy rainfall has damaged/contaminated hundreds of water sources, destroyed/inundated several thousands of latrines, forcing people to rely on unsafe water sources or practice open defecation. Importantly, Fuel shortage and high local prices continue to impact access to services and increase communities’ vulnerability. WHO rated Sudan as at high risk and categorized in the priority one group for countries in need of support for CoVID‐19 spread based on risk profile and capacity of the country to respond to a potential outbreakAccording to OCHA (2020), more than 63 % of the population in Sudan have no access to basic sanitation; in particular, 12 million people do not have access to toilets and as a consequence have to practice open defecation – often after dark – which is especially dangerous for girls and women as it increases their risks of becoming the victim of harassment, sexual violence and abuse violence (UNICEF 2019). Lack and dysfunctionality of sanitation services and infrastructure is also severe in schools and health facilities. OCHA reports has also revealed that 40% of the population do not have access to basic drinking water services (detailed in the recent MICS 2014 as; 28.7 % in Blue Nile, 42.8% in Kassala & 49.4% in North Darfur). More than 96% of Kassala population do not treat water for human consumption compared to 85% in Blue Nile & North Darfur state. Percentage of households those use soap or other cleansing agent is about 55.4 % (4.6 % in Kassala, 26.6% in Blue Nile and 8.8 in North Darfur) MICS 2014. Joint assessment conducted by Practical Action and its local partners in Telkok and Hamashkoreib localities in Kassala state (2018) reflected community urgent needs for safe drinking water sources as well as sanitation and hygiene promotion services. Practical Action led Assessment in Damazin, Geissan & Wedelmahi in Blue Nile (Dec 2018) showed that hand pumps represent the main source of water supply where women and children are the more responsible for water collection for the household. In addition, due to the lack of spare parts and maintenance expertise locally, most become non-functional during dry season leaving population dependant on contaminated river streams and traditional unprotected shallow wells. A survey conducted covering Damazin and Kurmuk localities revealed that women are responsible for 75% of water collection business from an average distance of double the minimum standard indicated by SPHERE. Available data in North Darfur show that per capita water consumption is only 57% (25% during summer) of the minimum level according to WHO; depending on Shallow unprotected hand dug wells and few hand pumps. Women & girls are traditionally responsible for water fetching added to the burden of domestic and cultivation work. Young boys & girls are responsible of carrying water using traditional carts for transporting heavy volumes of water what negatively affects their health & growth. A serious mix between drinking water and drainage water commonly appears too. Area is known for high Typhoid & Diarrhoea infections especially among children. Moreover, access to sanitary facilities is very low & insignificant.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

• Practical Action has a long experience in adopting Integrated Water Resource management approach that ensures equal access to water for different users while maintaining ecosystem balances and enhance environmental restoration, where possible, PA will apply IWRM in its interventions (especially for multi-sectoral actions). That will be ensuring access to drinking water as well as water for sanitation, farming and livestock in the catchment area. • The project will also emphasis in upgrading number of diesel operated water yards into solar-operated systems to reduce hazardous gases emission from burning fuel for water pumping. Solar lanterns will also introduced to light public latrines for eliminating the use of Kerosene and other unclear energy sources.• Communities' women and men committees will be trained and sensitized to establish forest nurseries utilizing waste water to start community forests around water supply points (That also apply to women group farms pilots). • Conditioning user fees pay (Tariff) will -in addition- to providing for maintenance and sustainability, encourage water use conservation. • Increase in water supply amounts and points will reduce concentration of livestock at few points and cause environmental degradation by overgrazing.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Practical Action ensures understanding and commitment to the safeguarding policies package by all employees, partners and contractors, which states Zero tolerance to all types of sexual exploitation and abuse especially against children, women and vulnerable adults. Complaints system will be put in place to ensure safe and effective communication channel is active for all beneficiaries to raise their concerns. The system include different tools including hotlines, emails, complaints boxes and others. As communities will be well-oriented and engaged in initial consultations and need assessments, Practical Action will ensure inclusion of the needs of different community groups including women and disabled people to assure that no one is left behind!Doing No harm principle will also be maintained to ensure no negative implications will arise from the project interventions that violate people safety, security, dignity or create damage to the resources. The main group of the project beneficiaries is the crisis-affected people those are in urgent need for WASH services for protection from the implications of lack of clean water and limited access to sanitation services. The project will enhance communities’ protection through provision of these essential services and further focus on COVID 19 protection with organizing awareness raising and provision of hygiene kits and protection supplies. Establishment and rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure in health facilities will enhance the capacity of local health system and thus better access to lifesaving health services. Also, gender segregated schools latrines will enable girls and boys safe access to sanitation facilities and protect them from the different types of physical and psychological hazards. Moreover, well-designed public latrines that are gender and disabled sensitive will ensure protection, safety and dignity of women and people with disabilities. Solar lights used to equip the public latrines will further enhance women safety (against GBV) as it protects women from the hazards associated with open defection especially in the evening hours.
