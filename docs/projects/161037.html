Project Summary

This project intends to provide integrated and equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services to IDPs, host communities and returnees in the affected areas. The project will build on existing WASH services and emergency response mechanisms that will be coupled with GBV risk mitigation and accessibility inclusion components. IOM will ensure WASH services benefit conflict-affected populations in hard-to-reach areas in Protection of Civilians (PoCs) sites located in Bentiu, Malakal and Wau, including the collective centres in Wau. IOM WASH services will target 149,608 beneficiaries (40,394 women, 34,410 men, 39,347 girls and 35,457 boys). IOM will continue providing and maintaining WASH services through operation and maintenance of safe and accessible water supply systems; care and maintenance of accessible and secure sanitation and handwashing facilities; and solid and liquid waste management. IOM will sustain and improve the promotion of hygiene and public health, focusing on community ownership methods. Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPnR) teams will expand access to WASH services to underserved and vulnerable populations in remote and hard-to-reach locations lacking access to basic services, as identified by the Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG), Inter-Cluster Working Group (ICWG) and in coordination with WASH Cluster coordination mechanisms. The intervention aims to target 214,628 beneficiaries (57,950 women, 49,364 men, 56,447 girls and 50,867 boys). IOM will ensure provision of safe water through borehole rehabilitation, manual drilling of boreholes, rehabilitation of surface water treatment systems, distribution of water treatment supplies and water trucking. IOM will ensure the sustainability of water supply through the formation and training of water management committees, pump mechanics and the provision of spare parts for ongoing care and maintenance of boreholes. For manual drilling, IOM will ensure the community members are engaged throughout the process, through site selection, accessibility and safety, selection and training of drilling crew members from the community, and through contributions such as food for drilling team. IOM will additionally promote good hygiene and sanitation practices through hygiene promotion activities and distribution of basic household hygiene items, including training and distribution of menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits to women and girls of menstrual age. IOM will ensure that prevention and mitigation of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and AAP activities are mainstreamed throughout activities. As mitigation plan, IOM will continue with the EVD surveillance and preparedness activities along South Sudan, Democratic Republic of Congo and Uganda through community hygiene and health promotion, and installation of handwashing and sanitation facilities in the point of entries (PoE) and health facilities. This intervention will target 57,470 individuals (15,517 women, 13,218 men, 15,115 girls and 13,620 men). IOM also plans to expand WASH and mitigation and prevention GBV services to assist IDPs outside PoCs and collective centers, and returnees and host communities through community participation and engagement. IOM will facilitate the reintegration at community level by improving access to WASH services as most infrastructure was destroyed during the conflict, including GBV risk mitigation activities. Through capacity building, IOM will ensure that communities are trained and able to respond during diseases outbreaks or conflict-affected displacement before humanitarian organizations are able to access and provide additional support.

1 - Needs

Despite renewed efforts to create a countrywide enduring and sustainable peace, the protracted conflict in South Sudan has continued throughout 2019, compounding WASH and wider humanitarian needs of an already vulnerable population. Although sustained efforts have been made by humanitarian partners, WASH needs continue to be widespread in South Sudan. The lack of safe drinking water, adequate sanitation facilities and the prevalence of poor hygiene practices have left a large portion of the population at risk of preventable diseases. Lack of access to WASH services further increases protection risks such as GBV and intensifies inter-communal conflict. As the conflict spread to new areas, boreholes were damaged or made dysfunctional due to lack of repairs. Without access to basic sanitation facilities, the majority of the population, especially in rural areas, practices open defecation. In locations where hand pumps are not functioning, people collect water from ponds, open wells or rivers. Thus, the lack of access to safe and potable drinking water, adequate sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices have left a significant number of people exposed to waterborne diseases. Lack of water also compounds risks for women and girls, who are often exposed to violence when reaching remote water sources. Furthermore, mobility tracking round 6 released in October 2019 by IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) has mapped 1,271,487 returnees since the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS). The increased number of returnees registered and expected will intensify the pressure on the existing functioning infrastructure and could become a source of inter-communal conflict. In the second half of 2019, several inter-communal conflicts were reported in Greater Equatoria, Greater Bahr el Ghazal and Warrap, leading to further displacement, and adding to displacement caused by heavy flooding affecting several communities in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Warrap (OCHA, 2019). Increased flooding across the country has exposed communities to waterborne diseases, such as acute watery diarrhea (AWD) and cholera (MSF, 2019). Due to a lack of access to sanitation facilities, open defecation is a common practice which increases the exposure of women and girls to sexual assault. In some contexts, women may wait until evening hours to seek out a field or private place, in order to defecate under cover of darkness, making them more vulnerable to assault, particularly at night and in areas with little or no public lighting. This situation is further aggravated during menstruation, which adds additional need for privacy (Sommer et al, 2014). GBV in these situations can ultimately lead to serious implications: unwanted pregnancy, sexually transmitted infections or rejection by the family or community (House, Cavill, 2015). Poor access to hygiene items, such as soap, increases the risk of diarrhea diseases due to poor hand washing practices. Additionally, the displaced women and girls in menstrual age are not able to properly manage their menstrual periods which affects their dignity and protection. On 1 August 2018, the Ministry of Health of the Democratic Republic of the Congo notified WHO of an outbreak of Ebola virus disease (EVD) in North Kivu Province, in the eastern part of the country. Neighboring countries, such as Uganda, Rwanda and South Sudan have raised the alert and established monitoring points along the borders to mitigate risk of spreading, and other preparedness activities, such as risk communication and WASH services. Considering that these areas are also affected by ongoing clashes, between the government and opposition forces; interventions are thus reliant on good communication and coordination with both parties to ensure services are reaching the affected population as soon as access is granted (OCHA, 2019).

2 - Activities

• Contribute to the WASH Cluster’s overall coordination of WASH activities through the sustained support of a dedicated WASH Cluster Sub-National Coordinator for Upper Nile. They will work with all WASH partners and other sectors to ensure WASH related needs are identified and addressed, mitigate any risk of overlapping and/or duplication; and promote proper exit strategy to prevent gaps;• Provide WASH services in Bentiu, Malakal, and Wau PoCs in line with Humanitarian Sphere standards (protection principles and WASH standards). If displacement increases in PoCs, IOM will increase/adapt services to ensure safe access to WASH services. This includes collaboration with the GBV sub-cluster to conduct regular safety audits to identify GBV risks to access WASH facilities and completing timely mitigation actions;• Facilitate the construction/rehabilitation and maintenance of sanitation facilities in adherence to the GBV and WASH facilities check list;• Sustain the operation and maintenance of water treatment and supply systems, including monitoring access to facilities in Bentiu, Malakal and Wau PoCs;• Sustain water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion services in Nazareth Collective Centre in Wau;• Implement water quality monitoring to ensure vulnerable populations are supplied with safe water to avoid transmission of water borne diseases;• Conduct hygiene and public health promotion activities, conduct refresher trainings for established networks of hygiene/health promoters;• Conduct hygiene promotion sessions focusing on menstrual hygiene management, targeting displaced women and girls of menstrual age. • Ensure prevention of GBV and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) activities are mainstreamed, this includes orienting WASH staff and volunteers on SEA and GBV risk mitigation, conducting GBV safety audits for access to WASH facilities and NFI distributions, and promoting women’s participation and leadership through skills building opportunities in WASH;• Ensure that wider AAP activities are implemented in all WASH operations, including community engagement so that beneficiaries are aware of their rights, complaints and feedback mechanisms are adapted to be accessible for people of different sex, age and abilities;• Provide adequate WASH services for returnee communities in Bentiu, Malakal and Wau. Locations and interventions will be determined based on multi sectoral needs assessment;• Provide integrated WASH Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPnR) to populations affected by natural and manmade disasters or diseases outbreak. This includes provision of safe drinking water, access to safe and accessible sanitation and handwashing facilities, provision of WASH NFIs, and hygiene promotion. GBV prevention and mitigation will be mainstreamed based on community consultations and safety audits;• Integrate WASH, GBV, transition and recovery activities in stable communities through community engagement; and improving access to water, promote latrine construction at household level (CLTS), construct institutional latrines adapted to PSN/PWN, conduct hygiene promotion activities and promote women’s empowerment through WASH market-based programming;• Expand access to safe and secure waterpoints to vulnerable population in remote and hard to reach locations through manual and mechanical drillings;• Conduct EVD preparedness activities along Uganda, DRC and South Sudan border through training of community health promoters, community leaders and key stakeholders; conduct hygiene promotion throughout targeted locations; construct handwashing facilities in points of entry, health facilities; improve sanitation and access to water in health facilities, schools and any selected public facilities;• Capacity building of NNGOs and Local organizations (implementing partners) through trainings, coaching and funds allocation to implement integrated WASH and GBV projects.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, IOM ensures beneficiaries’ rights are recognized and they are protected against exploitation. The Project implementation is guided by IOM’s protection mainstreaming guidelines and the IASC centrality of protection (2013), IOM’s Institutional Framework Addressing GBV in Crisis, and the IASC CBV guidelines. IOM has also an operational PSEA system enforced through the PSEA Policy (IN/024) and is an active member of the South Sudan PSEA taskforce at national level, and co-leads State level groups in Wau, Bentiu and Abeyi. IOM ensures staffs and volunteers are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse in line with the IOM Code of Conduct and are held accountable. The activities are designed based on the Humanitarian Sphere standards to ensure beneficiaries have access to safe, secure, dignified and sufficient potable water and sanitation facilities. IOM ensure the number of local staffs and volunteers recruited are able to disseminate the messages within their communities. Finally, the community feedback mechanism is established to give the opportunity to the beneficiaries to voice their opinions on IOM interventions.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, community participation and engagement is used to ensure that community members identify their needs and WASH and GBV programmes are addressing them accordingly. Approaches such as cash-based interventions (CBI) and skills trainings in lieu of in-kind distributions capitalize on existing coping mechanisms and community participations in order to promote ownership by transferring decision-making power away from humanitarian organizations and into the hands of the communities.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, IOM adheres to the national strategy and ensures its interventions are aligned with existing policies. IOM ensures good coordination is maintained with the government or community representatives in charge of humanitarian interventions (RRC, ROSS), and provides relevant information on the objectives and the activities that will be conducted. Through the National WASH Cluster, IOM has responded to several cholera disease outbreaks, and supported with the National Cholera Strategy. IOM’s implementation strategy is based on the direct and active participation of the population; this includes the participation of local authorities (community leaders, social structures). IOM’s approach includes a consultation committee integrated by local authorities that will be part of the monitoring and decision making mechanism.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, IOM WASH proposal has been designed based on the needs of the population (IDPs and Host people) and the activities defined to respond to those needs are directly linked to national objectives. The project activities are framed into three main components: • Improvement of the water services for the population (IDPs and host communities) through the rehabilitation, operation and maintenance of water source infrastructure. This component will have a positive impact in terms of quality and quantity of water available for the population and consequently there will be a reduction in the risk of outbreaks. This component is directly related to SO 1.3 (reduce excess morbidity and mortality rates from epidemic prone diseases (Malaria, diarrhea, ARI, measles) in priority areas• Empowering families to improve the service management capacity through the promotion of the active participation of families in each phase of the project (assessment, design, implementation and monitoring). Under this line, the promotion of woman participation will be a priority and include systematically activities related to protection and gender (Mitigate WASH-related gender based violence). This component is directly linked to SO 2.1 (Equitable access to quality and inclusive cross-sectoral basic services, including safe water, sanitation and hygiene promotion, education and health is extended to women, children, older persons and persons with disability in priority areas)• A third component focuses on Hygiene Promotion, Community engagement and monitoring. The activities related to this component are aimed to create technical capacity of the population and promote the autonomy of the families, which will have an impact on the sustainability of the results and the capacity of people to respond to crisis. The results of this component are related to SO 3 (Restore / Recovery activities / Coordination / Resilience / Market-Based Approach).

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Although the recently revitalized peace process promised to offer new opportunities; clashes across some areas (including those where IOM is planning to deliver activities), have contributed to perpetuating the current poor protection environment. Furthermore, as the country is about to enter a critical period of political transition for the implementation of the R-ARCS, uncertainties remain as to whether this will improve. Despite that such levels of instability and precariousness, IOM projects are designed and implemented in such a way to ensure do-no harm, and to guarantee protection issues are embedded in project activities. IOM WASH team is well trained in GBV prevention and mitigation, for example. Furthermore, this is also done by conducting Safety Audits for WASH NFIs and WATSAN infrastructure.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, IOM WASH will maintain community participation, promoted and sustained by Hygiene Committees, as well as hand over of management of activities such as water points, latrine management constructed in schools and health facilities, to the communities. Whenever possible, IOM will hand over the activities to the Community Based Organizations. As example, for water points, the trained Water Management Committees together with the local leadership and other stakeholders will take the operational and maintenance responsibilities. IOM WASH programme has a strong partnership and capacity building component, with an aim to engage and capacitate national and local CSOs/NGOs on WASH and GBV prevention and response. IOM engages in capacity assessment of local partners, defines a technical and organizational capacity building plan and engages in an intensive mentoring approach with partners.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse risk identification and risk mitigation for WASH program is defined with the Protection Officer and monitored on a regular basis. Additionally, IOM will ensure the community is fully engaged and informed on IOM code of conduct and the reporting mechanisms on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. IOM will ensure the complainants are feeling safe and secure. IOM will ensure that information is clearly shared, and beneficiaries understand their rights to access humanitarian services. In addition, IOM will emphasize on the necessity to report any case, either from humanitarian staff, local authorities or community workers, and appropriate measures are taken, while guaranteeing that the complainants feel safe and secure. WASH NFI kit post-distribution monitoring as well as satisfaction surveys are conducted. Distance to the service tends to be far from villages, as most of the areas and services (boreholes) are inaccessible, thus women and girls are more exposed to violence. IOM WASH aims to mitigate GBV risks by ensuring that consultation and safety audits are conducted prior to any intervention; ensure women and girls adhere to the proposed locations; ensure local authorities are trained on GBV risks mitigation and action plans are developed and implemented; and that case workers go to the communities with other outreach staff, meet clients in a neutral space and community leaders are engaged to support with protection.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM WASH promotes community participation and engagement throughout the project cycle. IOM ensures that communities are fully engaged throughout the assessment and implementation process to ensure the needs are clearly identified, activities are adapted, and beneficiaries feel safe to access services. The community consultations process is conducted with the representatives of sub-groups, such as women, girls, men, youth, people with disabilities (PwD), people with special deeds (PSN), elderly and minority groups. Of all consultations conducted, approximately 52% of respondents were female and 48% male, of which 0.14% represented PSNs. Thus, IOM will be in position to provide evidence-based interventions. All groups in the targeted community can contribute to the main objectives of the project based on the approaches used, such as Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTS), 24 hours clock (GBV), Safety audits, and community based feedback mechanisms (CBFM). IOM conducts monitoring of interventions and collects community feedback through surveys, semi-structured Interviews, focus group discussions, community meetings and verbal reporting, and ensures that all the groups in the targeted community have opportunities to voice their opinions on assessing the results of interventions. In addition, IOM has established a complaint and feedback mechanism where individuals can reach out to the organization to provide feedback on the intervention, complain on the quality of the work, and report any potential SEA that occurred. The project implementation of IOM is based on the participatory approach of the beneficiaries in each stage of the project, from the identification of general needs in terms of WASH services and the identification of strategic needs of vulnerable populations. The strategy includes the creation of technical capacities at local level, the involvement of all sectors of population and the promotion of women participation.
