Project Summary

This project aims at improving the food security and nutrition situation of vulnerable households targeted by the intervention while protecting and gradually restoring their agriculture-based livelihoods. The project will strengthen the food production capacities of a total of vulnerable households in the Governorates of Aleppo and Idleb. Priority will be given to women-headed households.
the proposed interventions will also encourage the targeted households to plant legumes with their cereals to improve soil health and fertility, besides diversifying their income sources. This practice will minimize the erosion of soil and compensate for the reduced use of fertilizers due to their high prices and/or unavailability.
This intervention will strengthen the production of vegetable crops widely used in the diet of Syrian people to address food insecurity while protecting and gradually restoring agricultural-based livelihoods and strengthening the capacity of people to manage and recover from shocks. By integrating immediate relief with livelihood-recovery activities, the proposed action will contribute to the strengthening of the resilience of the targeted crisis affected people and their host communities.
SECD Organization will target Atareb, Idleb, Harim, Salqin, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, and Mhambal sub districts in Aleppo and Idlep governate through the distribution of 4000 agriculture input kits, 10 of Solar powered irrigation system.
The project will provide some workshops for training those who are interested in receiving these kits and Solar powered irrigation systems. so that they will be able to use them correctly and efficiently inside their Farms. 4000 households will be targeted by this project; some of them are already agricultural workers, while about 200 person will be trained to start this kind of job for the first time, the training will be about:
1-Winter cropping techniques.
2-Modern irrigation systems.
3-Solar energy system needed for irrigation.
In the absence of robust support for livelihoods and agriculture, Syria’s dependence on humanitarian food assistance will continue to worsen in 2021 and beyond. Access to adequate dietary diversity continues to be a key need, especially for persons with specialized needs including children, pregnant/lactating women, and the elderly.
Self-reliance and resilience inside Syria can only be improved through better supporting.
The core components of food value chains alongside continued humanitarian assistance.
Key areas of value-chain support include the provision of productive inputs, the creation of income-generating opportunities, and the strengthening of community services and markets.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented on many steps as mentioned in the time frame below.Firstly, we need to conduct a need assessment in target areas for three weeks. Second step is hiring trainers who will give some training courses for the beneficiaries in a suitable place that we will rent for this purpose. At the same time, we will start to procurement the winter vegetable homestead kits which require some trucks and workers to achieve the mission. After distributing the items to the beneficiaries, a specialized team will visit the beneficiaries at homes regularly to provide any assistance and respond to any questions raised by beneficiaries. Along with the visits, an evaluation team will be conducting a post distribution assessment to fill any gap and fix any problem. Finally, a report will be written and prepared for feedback to the donor.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies are in place, practiced and monitored. Policies will cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Policies will address risks at the organizational level and the program level. Trainings and refreshers on PSEA will be done systematically. The targeted communities and learning facilities should be aware of SECD policies and procedures. SECD staff and targeted communities will have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously. SECD will ensure a survivor centered approach that ensures the safety and confidentiality of the affected person. The survivor will be an active participant in the processes. A poor handling of allegations of sexual explanation and abuse can put the accuser/survivor, alleged perpetrator and others at risk.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SECD conducts a security assessment by receiving information about security situations from its network of local councils and civil society activists, as well as by remaining continuously apprised of security situations through bulletins released by the international community. For emergency relief projects, when a security situation arises that threatens the safety of staff, the staff is redirected to a nearby, safer location until the area is safe enough.For non-relief projects, an assessment of the security situation is conducted to determine the location of the project. Risk (1) of overcrowding living conditions and the absence of adequate services might expose to risk vulnerable beneficiaries. Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium. Mitigating Measure: Ensure that adequate space is provided by family, after the completion of the rehabilitation, Raise awareness among the beneficiaries of the dangers of overcrowding, Ensure that a management system is in place for big collective shelters rehabilitation, Consider sphere standards when planning the response, and ensure that management is coordinating services with other actors to cover needs and gaps. Risk (2):The outbreak of conflict prevents access to project locations. Likelihood: low, Impact: high.Mitigating Measure: Lesson learned from 2013 operations found that while conflict occurred, access could be restored within a few days. A cautious approach that plans for these types of delays is expressed in the project work plan.Risk (3): Airplane shelling.Likelihood: medium. Impact: Very High Mitigating Measure: The risk of the Security situation, the risk caused by airstrikes is a very low probability according to the information for 10 years ago.
