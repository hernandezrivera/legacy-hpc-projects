Project Summary

The overall estimated Sector PIN for 2023 – of food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity – is estimated at 13.9 million, with 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity (HNO 2023). The acute food insecure figure includes 1.9 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 percent food insecure. The water crisis in Syria has resulted in a deterioration of the food security situation for thousands of people, most notably farmers – further reducing primary food production. The continuity of socioeconomic difficulties has driven more people into food insecurity. The 2021/2022 rainfall season was significantly poor and erratic, in terms of temporal and spatial distribution, combined with a significant delay in the onset of rainfall. This was further worsened by several successive seasonal dry spells. This significantly affected critical crop growth and development, especially grain filling. The seasonal constraints were also coupled with significant shortages and high cost of agriculture inputs, including diesel fuel, which resulted in limited access to water and ability to provide supplementary irrigation.
In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 92,754 vulnerable crisis-affected food insecure farming households (equivalent to 556,522 people) in Syria. The objective of the proposed action is to improve access to staple foods for vulnerable farmers through increased cereal and pulse production.
The project outputs include:
Output 1: Access to agricultural inputs to facilitate production of cereals, pulses and vegetables for 92,754 households increased.
The response package under this output will be in line with sector recommendations for provision of 200 kg of wheat/barley seeds (adequate to plant 1 ha), Foliar fertilizer supplement or cash support for land preparation (adequate for 1 ha). Or 25 to 30 kg of various legume seeds (adequate to plant approximately 0.4 0.5 ha) or 15-20 kg per dunum for vegetable will be provided per household.
Output 2: Skills for cereal and pulses production of 92,754 targeted households enhanced.
This output aims at building the capacity of the 92,754 households that will benefit from cereal and pulses production inputs. Beneficiaries will trains to support use of the inputs and the training programme will be built on the following FAO concepts: “Save & Grow”,

3 - Needs

HNO 2023 estimated the overall FAS Sector PIN at 15 million, resulting of the prolonged food security crisis in Syria. It is important to note that the sharp decline in crop production, and the entrenchment of food insecurity during the 2021-2022 cropping season was not only driven by weather anomalies, but also other drivers referenced in chapter 1, including water scarcity and availability, disrupted services, limited availability of production inputs, and their high prices, especially seeds, fertilizers as well as pesticides and herbicides. The scarcity of energy supplies, especially electricity and diesel, and their very high costs, which hindered the implementation of agricultural operations as required. The decline in agricultural production output was not only limited to rainfed food production systems but also to irrigated. Based on the FAO assessment, and driven factors and conditions, as well as periodic updates for the 2021-2022 cropping season, there is an anticipated shortage of wheat seed for the upcoming season. Farmers continue to experience challenges in accessing high quality and locally adapted seeds, with a knock-on impact on production and productivity. FAO proposed improving access to high quality and locally adapted seeds will deliver immediate productivity benefits and will help restore the quality of seeds that farmers use in subsequent seasons.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1).Beneficiaries being harassed because of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries’ selection. Mitigation: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (IDPs, HC; returnees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based on clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees. 2) Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation: local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; and clear, precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process;.3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. Mitigation measures: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring. The project taken into account protection elements, in line with the do-no-harm principles. Measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted because they are selected by project. Women, youth will be prioritized, inputs and trainings will take account of the different requirements of them, boys and girls. Returnees will also be targeted. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the HC will be included as well, to limit possible friction. Implementation of field activities will be in line with the Protection Cluster principles and guidelines on protection. An accountability to beneficiaries’ participation, information and feedback/complaint mechanism will also be put in place to provide communities with pathways to express their concerns and grievances.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation. Probability: Likely. Impact: Severe. Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; advocate with RC/HC and other stakeholders to regain access; develop contingency plans in coordination with service providers and, if necessary, move activities to alternative locations.2. Risks: Major displacements that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities. Probability: Possible. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Continue advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover the additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities and conduct advocacy with other organizations to include new arrivals in assistance programmes.3. Risks: Changes in the conditions related to cross-border movements and operations. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Moderate. Mitigation measures: Ensure that service providers have capacity to deliver the programme within the changed legal framework. Diversify activities complementing international procurement with local procurement and ensure different delivery modalities (scaling up cross lines to balance reduced cross borders).4. Risk: Limitations on partnership agreements. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Major. Mitigation measures: Continuous advocacy and negotiations with Government and counterparts for partners to have access to targeted areas and establish agreement
