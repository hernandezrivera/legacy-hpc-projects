Project Summary

CARE/partners (Palestine Medical Relief Society “PMRS” and Health Work Committees “HWC”) will respond to the health needs with a comprehensive intervention that combines elements of direct service delivery to the identified vulnerable communities, with special focus on women and children, elderly, and people with disabilities. The proposed intervention will be targeting 37 vulnerable communities in the WB that were identified by CARE/partners and the HNC (including Ministry of Health-MoH) during the recent HRP 2020. CARE/partners, with close coordination with MOH, will provide life-saving/basic health services in targeted communities in the WB through the use of mobile clinics/teams that will deliver basic curative and preventive level II PHC services, including women’s health, reproductive health (ante and post-natal care, obstetric care, family planning, etc.), general practice care (GP), health awareness with focus on communities’ urgent needs and priorities, basic lab tests, home visits, children health, in addition to the Psychosocial/mental health services that will be provided as part of the mobile services package. The team will be trained on basic life support and first aid and will be provided with emergency kits that enable them to respond to emergency incidents in these communities while working in the clinics to ensure lifesaving before transferring to the hospitals. The project will build the capacity/preparedness of the targeted communities to better respond to emergencies through conducting first aid training for selected community members (Focal Point) and equip them with necessary first aid kits.
The protection specific activity aims to report all incidents of violations of IHL and IHRL that are related to access to health such as access of staff to the communities, access of patients to health services, injuries due to settlers and IF violence, etc. This will help working toward coordinated advocacy, mainly through AIDA, for accountability for those violations with HNC and relevant stakeholders/actors. Moreover, CARE/partners will identify, report and refer identified GBV cases in line with the national referral mechanism. CARE will continue working with trained community representatives (focal points) and project staff to report/refer these violations using agreed upon mechanism.
The project is designed in full coordination and cooperation with other actors mainly MOH, HNC, Medico, and UNRWA. CARE had MoU with UNRWA to provide the health services to refugees in Barta’a enclave and in Khallet an Nu’man in Bethlehem, and other locations since many of the targeted communities have big number of refugees and UNRWA can’t reach these communities. A new agreement will be signed once the project approved, to l ensure better use of the resources, covering some of the gaps resulted in seizing UNRWA mobile clinics, avoid duplication, ensure synergy and complementarity with other actors, and maximize the number of beneficiaries/communities in need in the most effective and equitable manner.

1 - Needs

The design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of target communities, based on a number of key sources, including the data gathered from an on-going project funded OCHA/ CERF that has been implemented by CARE/HWC in partnership with WHO, data provided by Health and Nutrition cluster and Protection Cluster ’s needs analysis, and CARE’s/partners internal needs analysis. According to all assessments, access to primary health care for many communities in the WB is problematic largely due to various restrictions on movement and access imposed by the Israel authorities, including the separation barrier, settlers violence, checkpoints, permits regime and the isolation of East Jerusalem. At particular risk are communities living in Area C, H2 areas, Seam Zone and Wall affected communities in East Jerusalem. About 18% of the WB has been declared as a closed military zone for training or a “firing zone”. In many of these areas, the problems are exacerbated further by settler violence. All aspects of healthcare provision are affected by these challenges, including access to healthcare, and movement of patients, medical staff and supplies within and across the WB Movement restrictions have significant impact on vulnerable groups, particularly women and children, elderly and persons with disabilities. According to the HNO 2020, In the West Bank, 377,803 people are in need of humanitarian health interventions. In Area C, H2 and the ’Seam Zone’, 165 out of a total of 176 communities now need humanitarian assistance, a total of 162,663 people and have limited access to basic primary healthcare services; in particular, pregnant and lactating women, children, patients with NCDs, the elderly and people with disabilities who are totally neglected. Across the oPt, unmet health needs derive from insufficient access to health care due to a combination of chronically depleting medical supplies, insufficient human resources, and imposed access restrictions mainly in the project’s targeted communities in Area C and H2. . In March,2019, CARE has updated its mapping of health services through Focus Group Discussions and meeting with stakeholders and MOH, health care providers, local community representatives and clinics beneficiaries taking into consideration the gender needs and age groups in the identified communities in the targeted districts in the WB. The mapping revealed that approximately 37 communities are lacking basic health services ; 30 out of 37 are totally without any type of services, while the remaining 7 have only level 1 services with ad hoc coverage of 2 hours every 2 weeks. This was confirmed in the HRP 2020 process. OCHA/CERF funded two projects through UNFPA and WHO/CARE to cover the gap till end of December 2019. These communities will be left with no services after that date!. Data collected from these communities through the current project reveals that approximately 50% of the women health were related to ante and post-natal care, while the remaining 50% were distributed as (25% acute illnesses, 15% gynecological diseases, 10% puberty and post-menopausal related problems). Difficult access to PHC combined with traditional and conservative culture in many of the communities affects women. Inadequate treatment of reproductive health issues is common, often leading to complications. The incidents of GBV, under reported even under the best of circumstances especially in the vulnerable communities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PWD considered as one of the main targeted vulnerable groups in the proposed project as they were identified by HNC as one of the vulnerable groups whom needs special consideration, for that the project team will map out, with the communities’ representatives and actors all cases of PWD in the targeted communities and will provide the necessary medical services to them. Referral will be also an option for those who need to be referred to advances or specialized services, this will be coordinated with other health providers as needed. Additionally, the social worker, who will be part of the medical team, will work with them on the psychological aspect. PWD will have special attention when they come to the clinics and it is planned to conduct home visits for those who can’t access the clinic. Elderly and bed ridden patients will have special needs as target groups. For the elderly the project will provide the medical treatment and the social worker will work with them on special needs, follow up to the elderly patient could prevent serious medical problems. Home visits will also be planned for those who can’t access the clinic.
