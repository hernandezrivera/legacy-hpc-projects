Project Summary

Caritas Bangladesh aims to ensure the protections services to Rohingya communities and adjacent host community people living in ukhiya sub-district. The project activities will be tailored focusing on the community-based protection approach in the targeted areas with the protection, gender integration and mainstreaming as well as meaningful inclusion of the different age, gender, disability and diverse community people living in camp 4 ext., 19 and additional two new areas- camp 20ext and Jaliapalong union . To avoid duplication of service, consulattion meeting and service mapping will be followed to target the most vulnerable. Accessible and inclusive community centers will be established to ensure the safety, dignity of the community people throughout the facilitation of awareness raising session on negative coping mechanisms, changing harmful cultural and social norms. Individuals from both communities segregated by all age, gender and disability will form community-based protection committee to identify the community protection risks and mitigation measures. For the greater benefit of gender diverse group- women forum, male forum and for the inclusion of the person with disabilities a self help group will be formed and trained to reduce the risks, violence, exploitation and ensure support. In this project Barefoot counselor (BFC) approach is one of the core and successful community volunteer mobilization model for CB, which will be adapted to conduct the community outreach activities. Also, they will identify the potential psychological needs and referred to the psycho-social support services expert to arrange individual and group session without duplication. Self-help group and community volunteers will play the role to build the awareness on anti-trafficking, gender equality, peace and security, inclusion of gender diverse population and person with disability. Comprehensive case management support will be delivered for protection issues. CB has already established and functioning CFRM mechanism. Affected population will take part from the designing to the evaluation phase of the project. Speceific monitoring tools will developed and timley monitoring will be ensured for addressing the person in needs. Community consultation meeting will be arranged with the religious actors, community leaders and community service providers with the person specific needs. Appropriate communication channels and method will be followed to ensure the strengthening of the referral mechanism in this mainstreamed intersectoral project. This projects also aims to self-reliant and resilient approach by building the capacities of the affected population on trafficking, gender diversity and equality, protection mainstreaming and inclusion. To promote the peace and security and rebuild the social cohesion caritas will be part of peace and security campaign and will also arrange community led recreational and peace initiatives. Also dialogue session will be arranged to support community people specifically women and youth lead initiatives on peace and security. Engagement of the Government officials including Camp in charge, APBNs will be ensured in capacity building on reduction of disputes, violence, extortion, exploitation, promotion of human rights, community protection risks and concern, psycho-social support and gender based violence. These efforts align with the project's broader goal of enhancing community ownership, community led capacity building and facilitating the potential solution of forcibly displaced Myanmar nationals (FDMNs) to their country of origin.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

In the Rohingya camps, security conditions have deteriorated significantly, exacerbated by several critical factors. The reduced presence of the Armed Police Battalion (APBn), coupled with limited patrolling efforts and a weakened law enforcement response, especially during the national protests in July and August, has emboldened organized groups. This environment has allowed these groups to operate with near impunity, resulting in a notable surge in violent incidents, including gunfire, abductions, murders, and severe physical assaults. Although APBn resumed its functions, the camps remain volatile and dangerous. The influx of new arrivals from northern Rakhine State, fleeing intensified armed conflict, adds further strain to the camps. These individuals remain unregistered, with critical needs for shelter and humanitarian aid going unmet, thus heightening protection challenges. Tensions have also escalated due to ongoing confrontations between armed groups, including ARSA and RSO, with 62% of reported killings in attributed to these conflicts. RSO has reportedly established an active presence in several camps, where they engage in forced recruitment efforts to bolster their ranks in the conflict against Arakan Army. Economic exploitation has increased, with incidents of extortion up by 32% (J-PMR, 2024, Jan-Sep). Disputes over marriages, enforced night patrols, and community confrontations fuel these incidents. Additionally, reports indicate that APBn officers have detained refugees at checkpoints, often accusing them of spying for ARSA. These has led 17% household raises in security issuesconcerning theft (50%) and 11% neighbor conflict arises into the Rohingya community (ISNA, 2024). Furthermore, there have been allegations of local law enforcement agents (LEAs) and camp leaders, extorting bribes from residents under threats of false accusations. This has placed young men and adult males at the highest risk of recruitment, abduction, and violent retaliation from organized groups, while women and girls are also significantly affected; 10% of total reported incidents involve serious physical assault against females. The security landscape has worsened. Protests against APBNs ARSA, and other groups have heightened fear, anxiety, and uncertainty within both host and refugee communities. Criminal activities, primarily occurring at night, have intensified, with the theft of solar streetlights further eroding the fragile security and increasing fear among residents. Recorded security incidents reflect this dire situation, with over 2,232 occurrences reported, including 492 abductions, 282 physical assaults, 199 cases of extortion, 92 gunfire incidents, 137 recruitment cases, and 61 murders. Access to essential services has also been severely compromised, with movement restrictions, internet blackouts, curfews, and the absence of Camp-in-Charge (CIC) officials, which delay registration, family assistance certificates (FACs), and vital SMSD and WASH services. Additionally, 220 cases of trafficking have been reported, comprising forced labor, exploitation, forced marriage, and even cases of organ trafficking, disproportionately affecting young males (72%) and females (28%) (J-PMR, 2024) . This volatile environment underscores the urgent need for strengthened security measures, reliable law enforcement presence, and comprehensive humanitarian assistance to address both the physical and psychological (33%) toll on the refugee population (ISNA-2024).

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Caritas is deeply committed to addressing the unique and substantial safety and dignity risks faced by Rohingya individuals, particularly those with heightened vulnerabilities such as children, adolescent girls, boys, and pregnant women, Female headed household, persons with disabilities will be engaged by non-discriminated way. The location selection for the facility-based services focused on the accessibility of the all gender, age and diversity group of adolescent and children. The establishment of community Centers prioritizes safety, strategically located away from dark footpaths, risky slopes, and isolated areas. Moreover, these centers feature separate, clearly labeled latrines for male and female and person with disabilities, ensuring a secure and dignified environment. During the project activity implementation CB consider participatory approach consisting of decision making, risk mitigation , involve in project designing and monitoring of the project activity. A comprehensive approach such as mainstream with Wash, shelter and education sector that guarantee equitable access to services. Caritas strives to ensure active participation, empowerment, and the strengthening of local communities. The community-based planning approach actively diminishes risks and hazards while fostering resilience. This proactive approach acknowledges the dynamic nature of the situation, allowing for agile adjustments that enhance the effectiveness and relevance of the project over time. Caritas remains dedicated to mitigating risks, upholding dignity, and ensuring the well-being of Rohingya individuals through a comprehensive and adaptive humanitarian response.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project systematically prioritizes the inclusion of persons with disabilities, taking into account age and gender diversity, and integrates their participation throughout the program. To further this inclusive approach, a community-based protection committee will be formed, focusing on the best interests of diverse groups, particularly different age categories of persons with disabilities.Appropriate communication with gender diverse organozation will be made frequenlty to address the needs and gaps of the PoC. Also, Gender segregated groups will be formed. Specific assessment will be done to address the identified needs of women, men an other gender diverse group. Capacity-building training will be provided to both staff and community members to ensure effective needs assessments. Age and disability considerations will be incorporated into all project activities throughout the project cycle. In the selection process for vocational trainees, the project will emphasize a minimum of 3% inclusion of individuals with disabilities. This will be considered during assessment and selection, ensuring that locations and facilities are accessible for people with disabilities to participate in awareness-raising sessions. Women-led support and interventions will be adapted to include disability-sensitive assistance, such as providing porter support for households headed by persons with disabilities. Focus group discussions and community consultations will incorporate disability and inclusion sessions, ensuring that capacity-building activities are accessible to all. Equal opportunities for participation in capacity-building will be provided to persons with disabilities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project incorporates a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) mechanism, integrating Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, exisiting Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), and regular stakeholder consultations without duplication. Stakeholder engagement is prioritized to enhance the overall quality of protection initiatives. CB will participate in the peace and security campaign for building the awareness on protection risk reduction perpetrated by armed groups. Communication materials will be developed and circulated with the consultation of Protection sectors. AAP is a core responsibility and central to Caritas Bangladesh’s (CB) mandate. Refugees participate in deciding the priorities for activities implemented by CB and lead their own initiatives through community-based mechanisms throughout the operational management cycle. CB conducts regular community consultations in all camps to identify priority issues and obtain feedback in order to adapt its protection programming as needed. CB also operates community feedback and complaints boxes, email service, and an existing helpline in order to provide information to and elicit feedback and complaints from refugees. CB ensuring that individual complaints are responded to and that communities are provided with collective feedback. A fundamental part of CB’s work on AAP includes its commitment to the Code of Conduct (CoC) and a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and harassment for its staff.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment (PSEA) is a top priority for Caritas Bangladesh. As an active member of the PSEA Network—a coordinated body focused on preventing, mitigating, and harmonizing responses to SEA/SH incidents across Bangladesh—Caritas Bangladesh is committed to upholding the highest standards of safety and respect. The organization maintains a dedicated team for the Rohingya response and an additional team based in Dhaka. Caritas Bangladesh conducts regular PSEA training for staff, volunteers, vendors, and third parties and organizes awareness sessions for beneficiaries. These sessions are complemented by the development and distribution of PSEA materials and community consultations on Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanisms (CFRM), SEA risks, and communication channels. Staff, volunteers, women forum, male forum and Community-Based Protection Committee (CBPC) members receive annual and as-needed training on the Code of Conduct and PSEA/SH standards. Caritas Bangladesh emphasizes accessible, non-verbal communication in complaint and reporting systems, ensuring that the importance of reporting is understood along with the protection and reputational risks associated with SEA incidents. The PSEA policy of Caritas Bangladesh aims to continue using these resources to empower the community and foster a safer environment within the camps.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

To identify specific protection risks, the hazard and vulnerability mapping will be done at the beginning of the project. As part of early warning communication adoption, the climate resilient community center will be established and used as disaster related message sharing and safe space during any emergency. Also Person with disabilities group, women forum, male forum and community based protection committee will be trained to disseminate the early warning into their communities. Considering the environmental aspects, plastic free item will be used. For instances, no IEC and visibility materials will be used made with plastic. Community people will be encouraged to plant trees for greenery aspects and during any consultation meeting, recreational activities and other capacity building training, the tree sapling will be distributed to the participants as reinforcement. For rapid response emergency NFI stock will be arrange. Along with the coordination of CB’s Shelter sector and WaSH sectors camp cleaning campaign. Psychological First aid (PFA) will be delivered for right after any crisis. Community volunteer, self-help group will be trained on trauma focused psychological support. Also to understand the impact of climate change in protection risks, an indicator will be developed to monitor based on local and national adaptation.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

CB will conduct a vulnerability assessment through regular monitoring and identify the potential hazards. With the collaboration of local communities, an emergency plan will be outline with clear role and responsibilities and actions to protection in emergency. CB is part of the PERU team, so collaboration with sectors and other multiple partner will be done in the meantime. CB will arrange training on protection service in emergencies for the staff, volunteer will so ensure the smooth PFA, Case management and referral pathways. With the help of DRR team of Caritas Bangladesh a simulation and drills will be arrange at both communities and engage the community people to practice. As per the contingency plan the in kind support such as dry food items, hygiene kits, NFI (Blankets, cloths etc.) will be rapidly deployed. To reduce trafficking, exploitation in the emergency situation CB will arrange awareness session for community people, leaders including person with disabilities. Complain & Feedback mechanism will be function and monitored timely and appropriate manner.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

For youth aged 15-24 years in the Rohingya context, CB will focus on fostering safety, resilience and personal development while addressing other specific vulnerabilities they face. Essential life skills including conflict resolution, communication, decision making, empowering youth to navigating challenge will be arranged. For the ever-increasing psychological distress, sessions on stress management, trauma awareness and emotional resilience will arrange to help to build the inner strength and cope with psychological impact of crisis situations. Interested youth will be selected at the female forum and male forum to discuss about SRH information and be the part of soft life skill training and the Community center to access the information, support and engage in recreational activities. Activities will be arranged to equip them with the skills of raising voice for their needs and participate in community decision making.
