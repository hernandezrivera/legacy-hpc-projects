Project Summary

UNFPA commits to continue providing leadership to emergency GBV responses, keeping with its mandate under the IASC. UNFPA, therefore, working together with its implementing partners, and in coordination with the GBV AoR, will continue to provide and further expand quality life-saving GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response services in the natural disaster-affected areas in coordination with the respective provincial governments and government institutions in three provinces.
The proposed interventions aim to increase the availability and accessibility of quality life-saving GBV prevention/response in conflict-affected and IDP-hosting locations where women and girls have limited access to survivor-centered GBV multisectoral services.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Mozambique has been facing a humanitarian crisis caused by natural disasters in the past few years. Due to its geography, a high share of the country’s districts are at risk of recurring climate-related disasters, including tropical cyclones, droughts, and floods, especially those in the coastal and riverside areas. From 2013, more than 25 weather hazards have been reported in Mozambique, among them the devastating cyclones Idai and Kenneth that hit the country in 2019, creating severe disruption to critical services and posing serious threats to health outcomes. Thousands have been displaced due to natural disasters, the most recent being Tropical Cyclone Freddy March 2023, which hit the country twice, bringing heavy rains and floods. The disruptions caused by Freddy, intensified by a cholera outbreak, led to around 1.3 million people needing humanitarian assistance in 2023. Considering that natural disasters have a disproportionate impact on the most vulnerable populations, specially on women, girls and Youth which are more at risk of experiencing all forms of gender-based violence (including sexual exploitation and abuse, sexual violence, harassment, etc.) at the onset and after a disaster. As the lead agency for GBV AoR, UNFPA is mandated to coordinate efforts for the prevention, mitigation of and response to GBV in emergency contexts under the Protection Cluster. Therefore, within the forecasts of the upcoming rainy season, UNFPA is committed to working together with its implementing partners, and in coordination with the GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR), to continue to provide and further expand quality lifesaving GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response services in the areas of Mozambique most at risk of natural disasters in coordination with the respective provincial governments and government institutions in three provinces. The focus will be on improving the availability and reach of critical GBV prevention and response services, especially for women and girls. Thus, UNFPA together with the cluster, will prioritize emergency services, including critical GBV response services, enhanced referral pathway, GBV case management and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) initiatives that address survivors' immediate needs and psychological well-being. The strategy underscores community engagement through capacity building, structured awareness initiatives, and information dissemination in safe spaces for women and girls. Community engagement leads to improved outcomes and by preventing a dependency relationship on outsiders from forming, capacity building encourages local people to take action on local issues themselves. Mobile brigades offer integrated GBV-related services, focusing on clinical management of rape and post-rape kits. Safety audits identify and mitigate risks across humanitarian sectors. Capacity building extends to various stakeholders, including GBV service providers, local authorities, Linha Verde staff, activists, and humanitarian actors. Leveraging partnerships with local authorities, the strategy strengthens the national multisectoral mechanism (Centro de Atendimento Integrado - CAI) for integrated assistance to women survivors of violence. UNFPA, as a lead of the GBV AoR, will continue providing crucial guidance for GBV prevention, mitigation, and response in the disaster-prone areas of Mozambique in general. Additionally, it will spearhead GBV data collection and information management through GBVIMS. This will involve reinforcing leadership by enhancing human resources and the GBV capacity of AoR partners.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The priority activities of the HRP 2024 will be to; While targeting the provinces of Gaza, Inhambane, Sofala, Manica, Tete, Nampula, and Zambezia, the intervention will mainly focus on: 1. Procurement and distribution of dignity kits with GBV and PSEA awareness-raising initiatives2. Safety and risk mitigation activities through GBV Safety Audits, Referral pathway mapping and strengthening activities, GBV risk mitigation training and assessments. 3. Set up women and girls’ safe spaces/community centers and psychosocial support services.4. GBV awareness-raising including public communication campaign sessions organized during mass awareness-raising events such as International Women's Day (IWD), 16 days of activism against GBV, and others.5. Provide orientation sessions and awareness to humanitarian actors on PSEA and Code of Conduct, as well as on referral mechanisms for SEA survivors6. Provision of survivors-centered and specialized care for disasters-related gender-based violence, including sexual violence (CRSV) and MHPSS. 7. Individual GBV Case Management including support to existing protection mechanisms, including the Centros de Atendimentos Integrados (CAIs) or One-Stop Centers. Furthermore, UNFPA will continue leading the GBV AoR - GBV coordination mechanisms and improving the quality of GBV response by enhancing the capacity of member organizations and ensuring coordination among partners. This will involve updating referral pathways, training packages, advocacy messages, and SOPs.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project promotes meaningful access to assistance by employing inclusive approaches that consider the diverse needs of different groups. Providing GBV services is critical to ensuring survivors are able to recover and heal, if these are not confidential, they may put survivors at further risk of stigmatization or even death. As a result, UNFPA carries out a thorough risk analysis of its interventions and works with communities to reduce the risks of GBV. UNFPA prioritizes community engagement and facilitates decision-making processes that are inclusive and transparent. By actively involving community members in project planning, implementation, and monitoring, we aim to foster a sense of ownership and avoid any potential harm. UNFPA, as GBV AoR lead, will organize and facilitate training for a variety of humanitarian actors to ensure that actions to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV are included in their responses. Besides, UNFPA strives to ensure that all services are confidential by building the capacity of its partners on the guiding principles of working on GBV so that survivors of GBV will feel comfortable accessing them but also to make sure that there are no negative consequences in accessing them.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

To remove barriers to access to assistance, UNFPA implements different targeted measures. This includes making facilities (WGSS) and services (for example, Mobile Brigades) physically accessible and ensuring staff training on disability awareness and inclusive practices. Additionally, UNFPA tailors assistance to address specific needs arising from different disabilities, ensuring that support services are accommodating and responsive. Continuous feedback mechanisms and consultations with individuals with disabilities are integrated to adapt and improve the intervention, ensuring meaningful access and support. In addition to involving people with disabilities and disability advocacy groups in various consultation processes, UNFPA broadens its collaboration with national non-governmental organizations specializing in disability support. This expansion of partnerships aims to strengthen our efforts in addressing the needs of individuals with disabilities more comprehensively.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project maintains a commitment to minimizing its environmental footprint and implementing measures to mitigate any potential impact on the environment. UNFPA Mozambique and its implementing partners will be guided by the UNFPA Social and Environmental Standards (SES) as outlined in its Environmental Efficiency Strategy (EES) 2021-2025. The thematic areas that most apply to the Mozambique Context are Biodiversity, Ecosystems and Sustainable Natural Resource Management, where UNFPA and its partners utilize natural resources responsibly to construct Women and Girl Friendly Spaces (WGFS), and recycle Capulanas, old school uniforms for the socioeconomic activities within the WGFSs.To mitigate environmental impact, UNFPA will further implement various measures such as waste management protocols, utilizing eco-friendly materials where feasible, and promoting energy efficiency in operations.
