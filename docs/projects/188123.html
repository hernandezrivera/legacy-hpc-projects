Project Summary

‎30% of this project fund has been secured-The project will secure the food need of part of newly ‎displaced HHs and Host communities in north west of Syria who could flee from security ‎escalations through providing RTE rations for 2 rounds as a lifesaving rapid response, in addition ‎to provide food baskets for newly displaced HHs and most vulnerable HHs of the host ‎communities for 8 months throughout two modalities in-Kind and vouchers, aids modality will be ‎determined after a communities market assessment to check market access and availability of ‎essential food items, QRCS also will support bakeries managed by local councils with 1920 MT of ‎flour to produce bread to around 11,000 HHs with subsidized price for 8 months in daily basis, ‎targeted beneficiaries by bread will not be the same targeted beneficiaries of GBF. 91,000 of the ‎most vulnerable crises affected people will benefit from the project including women, men, girls and ‎boys in all age groups and disabled people in the targeted communities. Beneficiaries will be ‎involved in project designing and implementation through a close coordination with local council ‎and community representatives, also beneficiaries will be engaged in evaluation through ‎satisfaction survey and complaint mechanism.‎

4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies characterized by ongoing ‎hostilities which have killed hundreds of thousands of people, triggered one of the worst ‎displacement crises of our time, and led to the widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural ‎infrastructure, including homes, schools, health facilities, water supply and irrigation systems. Today, ‎‎13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance - a 21 per cent increase compared to ‎‎2020 - with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline.‎ with income insufficiency reported in all governorates The situation in Syria has drastically ‎deteriorated, with an estimated 14.2 million people in need of some form of food and agriculture ‎assistance, representing 68.6 per cent of the total population. Based on a countrywide assessment of ‎‎36,484 households and technical reviews and consultations, at least 12.4 million people are estimated ‎to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure. HNO, 2021 ‎advanced copyQRCS implemented a need assessment in 2021, The findings showed that the food consumption costs ‎more than 80% of the families living expenses 54.5% of the communities, which confirm the low ‎income of the populations in the targeted communities. In addition, the households spend between ‎‎61% and 80% of the total household’s expenses on the food in 32.1% of the targeted communities,

5 - Implementation of activities

The project intervention will be throughout: - Providing in-kind RTE rations for 8,000 newly displaced ‎HHs for two rounds, - Providing in-kind food baskets for 4,000 internal displaced HHs and host ‎communities for 8 months. in kind RTE rations and food baskets will be prepared as Food Assistance ‎recommendation in Humanitarian Response Plan 2021- Providing food assistance via voucher modality ‎to 4,000 internal displaced HHs and host communities who lives in communities with full access to ‎active markets, each HHs will receive food baskets with 60 Kg/month for 8 months, transportation ‎reimbursement is included. - Providing 1920 MT of flour (about 10 MT per day for 6 months) to ‎bakeries managed by local councils to produce 11000 bread bundles with subsidized price in daily basis, ‎the produced bread will be distributed to ID and host communities HHs in communities with high ‎severity.Project implementation cycle will start from coordination with the communities ‎representatives and local councils, performing market assessments to evaluate market access, food ‎availability, diversity, and price. Beneficiaries selection will be built according to their vulnerability and ‎need prioritizing displaced families affected by last escalation with the focus on female/children ‎headed HHs and disabled persons, the project has been designed to mitigate the conflict ‎consequences on adolescents as well by included them in the targeted beneficiaries.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The program has considered the Do-No-Harm principle in that it will use local field staff that will evaluate and assess the security situation, so that mitigation and response measures can be taken where and when needed. Risk1: Risk of violence from beneficiaries among themselves/with humanitarian workers when huge onset occurring (crowd control) Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: 1. Establish a feedback mechanism 2. Recruit female staff and train them. 3. Train staff on code of conduct 4. All staff contracts to include PSEA 5. Community committee with equitable representation of women 6. Train staff on the Inter-Agency Coordination in Community- 7-Based Complaint Mechanisms 7. Ensure distribution is well-organized (e.g. identification of sites/environment first; set up several distribution spots on sites; do joint distribution with /5 another partner) Risk2: Covid-19 related delays in project implementation linked with number of infected persons and their work load, increasing the health care commitment. Likeli.: Medium Mitig.: attending all the precaution measures during all project stages which recommended by ST -FSL.
