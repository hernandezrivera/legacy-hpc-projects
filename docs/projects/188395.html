Project Summary

The project targets two locations in Dana /Idleb governorate and Jindires /Aleppo governorate which are classified as underserved areas.
To plan this project, POINT started consultation with the local councils in the targeted locations and held an FGD with each of them (report attached)
The project has 3 components and all of them are solely ERL and aim to enhance access to livelihood opportunities and increase the resilience of conflict-affected and vulnerable households and communities.
The first component is market rehabilitation in Dana district in Idleb governorate which was prioritized during the FGD held with the LC and local community representatives, the market is public property and belongs to the LC. The rehabilitation will cover market shops (175 shops) and all supporting facilities, infrastructures, and access roads. There is a potential for rehabilitation of industrial Zone in Dana city, It consists of organized shops and open streets, the infrastructure needs to be maintained mainly as the streets are not graveled and not easy to access. It’s worth mentioning that POINT has good experience in rehabilitation and worked in several rehabilitation works like market and road rehabilitation with UNDP, light and heavy rehabilitation activities in 40 camps with IOM and SCHF - OCHA in NWS.
The mentioned market will be used during the project period to serve the project beneficiaries and after that, it will be handed over to the LC according to a handover plan.
The second component is vocational training in Jindires in Aleppo governorate and Dana in Idleb governorate targeting 160 participants with a specific focus on the most vulnerable persons according to eligibility and vulnerability criteria with giving priorities to higher vulnerability scores and women-headed HHs and PWDs
POINT will contract with 15 workers for CFW activity for market rehabilitation.
2nd Market mapping phase: to identify market gaps and needs according to which the VT training topics and domains will be identified, then POINT will announce for the VT and will conduct advocacy and community sensitization activities that aim to engage the communities, explaining the merits of the intervention and the idea of SMEs, raising awareness of the importance of sustaining small businesses, and communicating the selection criteria to reduce the risk of tensions among communities
After that, the selected beneficiaries will be verified and then will be invited to join the VT which will target 160 persons (80 in Jindires and 80 in Dana) and last for two and a half months on topics that will be identified according to the market mapping with giving priorities to unique and least available vocations in the market
The third component in the SME program and grants: POINT will work according to its developed sub-grants SOP and mechanism which was approved by ERL cluster and SCHF in previous projects
After finishing the VT, Point will announce for SME program and will select participants according to a set of eligibility and vulnerability criteria and then will deliver 15 SME training of five days each for 300 selected applicants. This announcement and selection will be open to all people who already have business ideas, knowledge, and experience to perform this idea with giving priority to join for the VT participants who successfully finished their training. During this SMEs training, the participants will learn about the basis of business establishment, marketing financial study, feasibility, competition evaluation
After that, each participant will submit a business plan which will be evaluated by a formed committee for this purpose.
Best 150 business plan with the highest scores will be supported with cash grants of 1200$ to be paid on three tranches with mentoring and coaching till the end of the project
In the end, end-line evaluation will take place to evaluate the improvement in beneficiaries' lives after the grant and establish their businesses

4 - Needs

In NWS, 3.4 million persons are in need of humanitarian aid out of the total 4.4 million persons residing there. Long-term damage to civilian infrastructure has led to a direct negative impact on communities’ access to services. With the economic crisis and devaluation of currencies, over 90% of the Syrian population is living under the poverty line. Access to livelihood opportunities and income-generating activities are limited. According to the Early Recovery and Livelihood (ERL) Cluster, the gap between livelihood opportunities and access to services provided and people in need of ERL services are in the ninetieth percentile.The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated this economic downturn by further reducing already sparse income-generating opportunities in a context where 50% of the work-age population is now estimated unemployed, and most enterprises lost significant revenue ranging from 40 to 100 %, and about 13 % had to close their operations. Specific need assessment for this project has been conducted by POINT team which aimed to assess the local markets and livelihood conditions, civilian infrastructure and productive and income-generating facilities that were damaged during the war and need to be rehabilitated to get back to the production phase and secure more livelihoods opportunities, and the needs for vocational training and financial support in the market to secure more livelihood opportunities for small scale businesses.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to participate in improving access to livelihood opportunities and increasing the resilience of conflict-affected and vulnerable households and communities in order to reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping strategies in the context of the Covid-19 emergency.Moreover, this project aims to help the targeted communities to overcome the severe economic deterioration situation and enable them to get back control of their lives and reduce their reliance on humanitarian assistance.Besides that, the project aims to increase social cohesion through working for and with communities by including them in the need assessment and the project design phase, and later, by targeting both host communities and IDPs in the project activities. On the other hand, the project aims to mobilize the local resources by rehabilitating one of the public markets and linking it to the other activities in this project.-Evaluating market system’s functionality/proximity/capacity and assess potential impact on markets and non-cash recipients.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Collaboration from local councils in Dana for approval from local community and availability of rehabilitation needed resources.Risk1: intervening from local authorities and local council:Mitigation: coordination with the local council and local authorities before starting the rehabilitation and get required approvals and sign an agreement identifying each party's role and responsibility, additionally prepare the handover plan of the shops after project end.Risk 1: COVID-19 spread in target communities: Mitigation: POINT will take all necessary measures to avoid the transmission and spread of the disease infection, such as wearing a face mask, gloves and securing them for each participant, providing hand sanitizers, and measuring the temperature of the beneficiaries before entering the training halls.Risk 2: Due to social norms, girls’ parents may not allow them to attend as planned; the training activities and the mini-grants may lead to increased risk.Mitigation: announcing and community sensitization for the project in the name of Point in collaboration with local councils will increase the level of trust on the project and will allow women to participate safely. Risk1: grantee receive first payment and doesn't continue the project establishment. Mitigation: the second payment will not be received before confirming that the first payment was consumed for project activities, coordination with the local council legally to restore the first payment.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

POINT is strongly committed to protecting children and women from abuse, a commitment that is reflected in their Child Protection and PSEA Policies and is a member of PSEA network and follows the guidelines. Gender is mainstreamed in the design of proposed project, ensuring that the needs of women are considered and properly addressed. During the beneficiaries’ selection phase, specific emphasis will be put on women-headed households and their specific vulnerability to protection concerns and reduced access to employment opportunities due to their gender, ensuring women have equal access to the provided services.POINT will closely monitor protection risks throughout the implementation period and will ensure coordination with Protection Cluster partners. POINT has developed mitigation measures set in place to ensure that protection is mainstreamed in all project activities and GBV risk is minimized to the minimum level: Ensuring that protection principles are incorporated throughout all project activities, including the promotion of meaningful access, and ensuring the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. POINT trains its field staff to safely identify and refer protection concerns to the correct unit(s) and external actors to reduce risks and support safe and dignified access for all affected populations. Ensuring that staff members are trained on following do not harm principles in interventions and signed a code of conduct, receive PSEA training and have a reporting system

Mitigation measures: 1. Suspend activities till peace is recovered. 2. coordinate with POINT programs to resume providing services in nearby safe areas once safe is taking long time to be restored. risk that economic circumstances could change, which could potentially cause supported businesses to fail Mitigation observing markets and coordinating with the ERL cluster and the CBR-TWG Assumption: The project will be able to maintain operations despite the fluid security context Acceptance by private sector representatives Availability of resources to implement community based initiatives Local authorities continue to cooperate with partners mitigation measures : From inception of operational activities the project will be implemented in consultation with local stakeholders, including authorities, Conflict sensitivity/social cohesion: PO will consult inclusive community committees to identify the selection criteria and prevent any conflict or negative impact caused by the intervention i.e market distortion / risk of markets volatility.
