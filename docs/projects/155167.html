Needs

Humanitarian crises affect each person differently depending on their gender, age, disability and other personal characteristics. Older people and people with disabilities are often overlooked in humanitarian relief and response and they may find it harder than others to access the assistance and protection they need. The humanitarian principle of impartiality – providing assistance on the basis of need and without discrimination – requires agencies working in emergencies to reduce barriers so that people with disabilities and older people are not purposefully or inadvertently excluded from the humanitarian response. As per survey of HelpAge, around 10% of the camp population are older people (categorized as 50+ old). Total population of Older People (OP) is around 90,000. HelpAge has been serving 20,000 older people in 6 camps that only cover 22% of the population. 78% of Older Population (OP) remains unserved or underserved. Older women and men, including those with disabilities, often experience serious challenges in accessing humanitarian services and information about the services. Findings of the Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) conducted by HelpAge International indicated a very concerning situation for older people (OP) in relation to meaningful access to humanitarian services as they face significant difficulties to access basic services due to absent of OP inclusive Humanitarian response, low coverage of OP focused program, mobility issues and monsoon threat. Although during the year some interventions have mitigated the impact for Older People and PwD, there is still lack of access to Older People friendly protection services. Due to their low self-esteem, physical challenges they are the regular victim of multiple exploitation in having humanitarian services. They are at highest risk of psychological harm. Older People and their inter-section of PwD are excluded in prevention of violence, exploitation and abuse. Finally, a protection mainstreaming approach must be effectively implemented throughout the response for the older people. Need to initiate specialized services for survivors of gender-based violence (GBV). Older people must be recognized a group with Specific Needs and prioritized accordingly, possibly via the establishment of a Task Force on Age and Disability. HelpAge is the global lead on the Age and Disability Capacity Programme (ADCAP) which has resulted in the Minimum Standards for Age and Disability Inclusion in Humanitarian Action. HelpAge would use these Inclusion Standards as a basis for its sensitization and capacity building activities both in terms of providing initial training and developing Joint Action Plans to support the target actors to achieve compliance with the Standard relevant to their activities. An Inclusion Adviser and Officer will be brought on board to ensure the necessary human and time resources are available to oversee this process in a supportive and flexible manner. Targeted community highly vulnerable for landslide, waterlogging, monsoon challenges. To cope with the situation, community need to build their community-based resilient through DRR initiative.

Activities or outputs

10 Age Friendly Spaces (AFS) comprising both static and mobile services will be developed. The AFS will be in closed proximity to health clinics, women’s centers, mosques and other community facilities. The AFS serve as a central point for information provision and referral mechanisms to services including registration, health, food, WASH, Shelter NFI and other essential humanitarian services. Regarding PSEA, Helpage will facilitate community protection groups, institutional arrangement and will closely work with other actors in camp and Cox’s Bazar.In addition, there will be direct provision Protection Case Management and recreational and social activities. Each Age Friendly Spaces will have adequate staff along with community volunteers roaming between all cluster (block/sub-block). Half of this staff works at the static site while the other half work on outreach service delivery. There is a team of 8 Community Protection Volunteers per AFS and 20 Older Person Representatives who serve as community protection volunteers. An AFS Management Committee will be formed for each AFS from older people in the catchment area and will seat fortnightly to review ongoing protection activities, share information on the situation of older people in the camp and inform planning for ongoing and future activities. Field Supervisors and Facilitators along with Community Protection Volunteers/OP representatives will ensure a participatory, community led approach which is adaptable to emerging and evolving needs. Referrals to basic services will be both ensured and supported as necessary via financial or accompaniment support. Follow up on all referrals will be assured as part of the case management strategy. In the case of older person specific Protection concerns, case management processes will be in place, particularly around issues related to PSEA, Violence, Abuse and Neglect (VAN) of older people, an issue which HelpAge has global expertise on and will deploy a GBV/VAN Technical Adviser. Older people will be provided with age specific and appropriate NFIs, using an Individual Protection Assistance approach. Survivors will be provided cash and other support as well. This project would place a strong emphasis on the sensitization and capacity building of duty bearers on the inclusion of older people and people with disabilities in humanitarian response activities. HelpAge is the global lead on the Age and Disability Capacity Programme (ADCAP) which has resulted in the Minimum Standards for Age and Disability Inclusion in Humanitarian Action. HelpAge would use these Inclusion Standards as a basis for its sensitization and capacity building activities both in terms of providing initial training and developing Joint Action Plans to support the target actors to achieve compliance with the Standard relevant to their activities. An Inclusion Adviser and Officer will be brought on board to ensure the necessary human and time resources are available to oversee this process in a supportive and flexible manner. HelpAge has global expertise on VAN who is directly involved with the protection process. HelpAge has a localized safety and safeguarding policy and a global code of conduct, which were shared with both partners.
