Project Summary

The goal of the project is to contribute to the reduction in health-related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high quality lifesaving emergency health interventions for the most vulnerable populations in the densely populated but so far completely underserved area of Jur River County about 10km North of Wau Town.
The overall objective is to maintain and sustain the existing basic essential emergency health services with improved emergency referral services for communities in Jur River County with a focus on women and children including persons with disabilities. This project will provide support to host communities, Internally Displaced persons and returnee communities in under served areas in Jur river county.
There is still low coverage of antenatal care (ANC) first and fourth visits as well as deliveries by skilled birth attendants. Maternal mortality in the country is still one of the highest in the world. Mary help hospital will prioritize essential life saving health care services including maternal, child and adolscent health provision through MHH and its mobile units. The Child Health indicators associated with low immunization coverage which is extremely very poor with Penta3 coverage of only 35% which is attributed to barriers due to insecurity limiting mothers and children to access and utilize health care services at Mary Help Hospital. There is high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (55%)diarrhea (15%) and pneumonia (20%) in U 5 years and other diseases (10%) which have a direct negative impact on women and young children (Mary Help Hospital register 2022)
Jur River County has 40,523 (M=19,224, F=21,299) vulnerable population in need (PIN 2022) in which this project will target 30,000 (M=14,000, F= 16,000) vulnerable population (PIN 2022) of which 1200 (M= 500, F=700) under 1 will receive PENTA3 and 1400 deliveries will be assisted by a skilled birth attendant. MHA will also reach 600(M=400, F=200 are persons who will receive MHPSS and CMR services respectively) with 700 (M= 300, F=400) disability will receive rehabilitation services.
The project will support 2 activities that include Hospital (Mary Help Hospital) and mobile units supported with all the clinical packages.
The package of services to be supported includes Child health services, Maternal and neonatal health services, Basic emergency obstetric and newborn care, Comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care, Mental health services, gender-based violence services, referral Distribution of essential medicines and supplies.
The project is in line with 2022 cluster objectives and is link to Protection, Education, FSL, Nutrition and WASH program; long-standing partnership with WFP, CHD, SMOH, UNICEF, WHO and other partners in Jur River County.

1 - Needs

Jur River County is among the counties in South Sudan that is severely affected by intercommunal conflict, the economic crisis and COVID-19 that interrupted provision of basic life-saving primary health care services due to destruction of basic social services. The county is inhabited by an extremely vulnerable and traumatized community majority being women, girls, boys, and elderly people and persons with disabilities who are physically and psychologically exposed to a multitude of stressors. These unmet needs will increase vulnerability for populations in 2022 as more people are likely to access health care services. The key drivers are multifaceted, including severe acute food insecurity, the impact of COVID-19, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease, inadequate sanitation conditions and hygiene practices, and limited access to health and nutrition services driven by the elements noted above. Those who are malnourished are more vulnerable to common and infectious diseases such as cholera, malaria, diarrhea diseases, acute respiratory infection and measles. In South Sudan, 1 in 10 children die before their 5th birthday. Major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include the high prevalence of diseases which affects up to 36 per cent of children under five, and poor quality of food and limited food diversity. Barriers to accessing nutrition services include insecurity, flooding, distance and poor road conditions. Another barrier to access reported is the lack of supplies at health centers. Coping mechanisms reported for those unable to access health facilities to address their needs In spites of Mary Help Association intervention the County health situation is still fragile with extremely low health care coverage, high infant mortality and under five mortality and morbidity rates. The high poverty and long history of conflict has left an already very basic health care system extremely weakened, with severe shortages of health workers and vandalized health facilities leaving vulnerable communities with limited access to health services in addition to the huge influx of internally displace persons and returnees. The county primary health care system has many challenges, such as insufficient qualified staff, poor infrastructure, inadequate health facilities, lack of drugs and medical supplies despite of significant efforts made by the NGOs. There is low coverage of antenatal care (ANC) first and fourth visits as well as deliveries by skilled birth attendants (SBA) are extremely low with ANC 1 at 40% and ANC 4 at 30% while skilled delivery is less than 40%. The Child Health indicators associated with low immunization coverage which is extremely very poor with Penta3 coverage of only 30% (Facility register 2021) which is attributed to barriers due to insecurity limiting mothers and children to access and utilize health care services at Mary Help Hospital. There is high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (60%), diarrhea (20%) and pneumonia (10%) in U 5 years and other diseases (10%) which have a direct negative impact on women and young children.Jur River County has 40,523 (M=19,224, F=21,299) vulnerable population in need (PIN 2022) in which this project will target 30,000 (M=14,000, F= 16,000) vulnerable population (PIN 2022) of which 1200 (M= 500, F=700) under 1 will receive PENTA3 and 1200 deliveries will be assisted by a skilled birth attendant. MHA will also reach 600(M=400, F=200 are persons who will receive MHPSS and CMR services respectively) with 700 (M= 300, F=400) disability will receive rehabilitation services. The project is in line with 2022 cluster objectives and is link to Protection, Education, FSL, Nutrition and WASH program; long-standing partnership with WFP, CHD, SMOH, UNICEF, WHO and other partners in Jur River County.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Using translated messages, the project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls are put into consideration to make sure that the project is meeting its intended goals, MHA will work closely with these groups during participatory monitoring and evaluation approach to foster a greater sense of ownership. The joint participation approaches will enable the beneficiaries to make informed decision on the progress of the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will prioritized vulnerable or marginalized groups , child headed households, elderly and people with disability, through support from the community leaders and other protection actors operating in the selected locations. Furthermore, meeting locations shall be adapted to reduce thedistance and to ensure that these most vulnerable/marginalized have access to services with simplified translated feed back mechanism.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Complaints regarding any protection-related issue are handled by a Protection Officer to ensure confidentiality and to be able to determine an appropriate response to the feedback received, particularly in the event that such responses require CP or GBV intervention from the case management team. Any complaint involving sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be immediately escalated to the human resources team in WAU. All MHA staff are trained on SEA and all have signed MHA ’s code of conduct. Cases of SEA will be investigated by a team led by MHA South Sudan’s human resources staff in collaboration with the Safeguarding Manager in a safe and confidential manner. Strengthening the capacity of the stakeholders and staff.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In order to keep track of the project outputs and make sure that the project is meeting its intended goals, MHA has in place monitoring systems using a participatory monitoring and evaluation approach between MHA and the beneficiary communities to foster a greater sense of ownership. The joint participation approaches will enable the beneficiaries to make informed decision on the progress of the project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

MHA team will ensure safety of beneficiaries during implementation of project activities in communities. Interventions will take into consideration the approach of do-no-harm, where activities are done with adherence to zero effects on community lives for instance, distribution venues must be in safe location where beneficiaries have access to a proper shade or protection from sun heat, and drinking water is accessible. Need assessment meetings with community members and the time of beneficiary registration will involve awareness sessions on various issues like child protection, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), and Domestic violence. As communities may be exposed to the risks of explosive hazards, they will also be sensitized on how to protect themselves and how to pass information of suspected hazardous objects to agencies to support. This will be done by ensuring that the local project committees that are formed in each area to champion project activities are empowered with knowledge on protection of men, women, boys, girls and disabled beneficiaries. They will be assigned to continuously disseminate key messages on protection to keep their community members aware. Additionally, MHA will work closely with UNMISS and UNDSS to respond to emergencies or cases that are related to explosive hazards. MHA will implement this project in close collaboration with SNFI cluster at national and state levels to minimize overlaps of activities, and most importantly, to reach the most in need people. MHA will achieve this by participating in cluster meetings like the OWG (operations working groups) where cluster interventions are discussed. Rigorous needs assessments will be done prior to distribution missions. Post Distribution monitoring will help to gather feedback from beneficiaries to inform MHA on the effectiveness of the project and where there will be need by changes in the design of future project.
