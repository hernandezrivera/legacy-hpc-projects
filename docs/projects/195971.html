Project Summary

The proposed project will target camps 1 E, 4, 14, 21, 24,25, 26 and Palangkhali, Haldiapalang, Nhila, Teknaf Pouroshava, Baharchara and Whykong under Ukhiya and Teknaf region respectively for planned interventions. FIVDB is successfully implementing livelihood projects in camp 21, 24, 14, Teknaf Pourashava and Baharchara and learnings from the projects will be incorporated in the proposed project. The project participants will be identified from most vulnerable HHs i.e. female headed HHs, HHs with person with disabilities, HHs with large number, orphan hosted HHs, HHs with elderly person, LGBTI etc. maintaining SAAD criteria from age 18 to 40 to provide them with livelihood capacity building training and enhance self-resilience among the vulnerable targeted community as per needs. Protection mainstreaming, gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion through WGQ questionnaire, meaningful participation, community engagement from design to implementation, AAP are always prioritized by FIVDB. 4 % persons with disabilities will be targeted enabling a person with disability friendly project design. The project will try to identify and include vulnerable LGBTI person initiating enabling separate modality for the targeted if they face any challenges in the activities due to social stigma, taboo and prejudice. 210 FDMN (60% women) will be capacitated through livelihood and skill development training i.e. manufacturing, electrical, general mechanics, handicraft etc. followed by a non cash input support. Women will be encouraged to receive training on electrical and general mechanics and men will be encouraged to receive training on handicraft as a role transformation approach. A training need assessment will be conducted with 70 project participants (30 female) to find out in which skills the community prefers to be trained on and standardize the curriculum to standardize the curriculum. To enable income earning opportunities for respective participants a training cum production center will be established wherever possible so that at least 105 participants may sell their products targeting at least 3 camps. For rest of the 105 participants, the project will target to link them with existing camp markets.240 adolescent and youth (75% female) from Host community who studied at least up to class 4- 8 and dropped out of school due to economic vulnerability from age 18 to 24 will receive 24-day long training on financial literacy, life skill, communication skill and DRR based on developed module along with training on digital literacy and TVET based on standard curriculum engaging private sector expert and participants’ choice and availability of trades. A boot camp will be organized engaging employers and TVET participants establishing a linkage between the participants, financial service providers and employers. Also the project will link all the participants with Department of social service providers so that they can receive further capacity building training and other support when required. FIVDB has always maintained good coordination with RRRC, CIC and other local government and has been an active member of FSL sector and will continue to do so under the proposed project. 60 participants from HC will receive training on vermin compost and receive a start-up capital to capacitate them in the skill and enable their opportunities to sell their product in local market as a part of green skill development and business. The project will follow result based project management and monitoring approach including role of community with establishing complaint and feedback response mechanism to recognize, respond and incorporate community feedback in the project. The impact of the project will be evaluated following OECD- DAC criteria. All the staffs will be provided protection gears and FIVDB’s own security protocol and PSEA policy will be active throughout the project to address and deal with any context specific risk and PSEA concern.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

At present more than 0.9 million FDMNs are living in Bangladesh in 33 camps in Teknaf and Ukhiya region of Cox’s Bazar. Food, access to livelihood, access to income generating activities, access to NFI, Wash facilities, Education etc. have always being the top priority needs in FDMN camps. Followed by the influx, adjacent host communities have been affected as well and prior to COVID 19 the situation has worsened. The instability in economy has also played a major role in boosting the vulnerabilities in the host community. The identified priority needs among host community are access to food, access to livelihood, access to IGA etc. (MSNA, REVA). Most vulnerable HHs are identified as Female headed HHs, HHs with person with disabilities, HHs with elderly persons, orphan hosted HHs, HHs with large numbers etc. in both FDMN and Host communities. Since 2017 several organizations have extended their support to affected population in terms of food security, enabled livelihood scopes, shelter, NFI support, education. WASH etc. But for such as large number of population all the assistances combined are never adequate and vulnerabilities continue among the population. 80% HHs are found to be moderately and 95% HHs are found to highly vulnerable in FDMN community. In HC, 52% HHs have found to be highly vulnerable due to economic downturns, lacking adequate and expanded assistance, adequate livelihoods recovery initiatives, and skyrocketing price spikes of essential commodities, especially food. (REVA, 2022). Due to limited access to access to regular income & livelihood opportunities, the FDMN population are highly reliant on humanitarian assistance. According to JMSNA, 72% HHs in refugee camps is living with food security and livelihood gaps. 59% HHs reported food and 30% reported access to self-reliance activities as priority needs. 70% of HHs have adopted livelihood based coping strategies in FDMN community. About 33 percent of Rohingya households and 4 percent of host community households had no work-based income sources other than assistance. No significant change was observed in the different types of economic activity in which men and women engaged among Rohingya households. Men continued participating in daily wage labour or running small businesses and shops, while women continued engaging in home-based activities or relatively more secure salaried jobs. In FDMN community 30% income comes from work based income sources whereas in HC 95% income comes from work based income sources. Gap between men and women in labor force participation is very high as 65% men is participating in labor work whereas % of women is only 12. In HC 71% men is engaged in labor force participation whereas the % of women is 17. In case of Education number of drop outs are increasing more and more due to economic down turns prior to COVID 19 and number of drop out girls are more compared to boys. According to Labor Market and skill assessment report DCA 2020 major constraints in FDMN and Host communities in Teknaf are limited job/employment opportunities, limited access to product or service market, low business skill among HC, weak linkage between vocational education with labor market in HC, gender discrimination etc. The situations have reported to have worsened prior to COVID 19. According to JMSNA, 2021, In host community 9% HHs is living below SMEB, 20 % between SMEB and MEB, 71% above SMEB. In FDMN camps, 4 % is living below SMEB, 22 % between SMEB and MEB and 74% is living above SMEB. To address the need more and more affected population should be enabled with sustainable livelihood options, skill development, self-reliance activities and access to income generating activities.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

All the refugee population living in Cox’s Bazar depend on humanitarian assistance and services to meet their basic needs and since 2017, already vulnerable host population in this area are also in stress due to the huge influx and current global economic fallout. The proposed project will ensure access to diversified livelihood options for the targeted households from both communities. An integrated and coordinated approach – capacity building, input support, risk mitigation, linkage with government and non-government services, linkage with financial service providers and private sectors, encountering early marriage by engaging adolescent and youth girls, and protection mainstreaming, has been proposed under this project based on the previous learning of the organization since 2017 from its own assessment and from the sectors, camp administration, local government and community consultation. For every project, FIVDB always prioritizes coordination with and reporting to the sector, camp level coordination, coordination with the CiC, local government and administrations. The interventions proposed under this project are fully aligned with the sector priorities and Bangladesh Government priorities and strategies. FIVDB believes that the impact and sustainability will be remarkable as the approach and modalities that FIVDB is proposed are already tested in development projects and in humanitarian settings with changes in Cox’s Bazar. Over the last five years, FIVDB implemented blended approaches based on need and that was useful to bring expected results. Lastly, from the beginning of FIVDB’s Humanitarian Response program, multi-dimensional community engagement approaches - community consultation, door-to-door visit, help desk, and interactive popular theater were undertaken for trust and relationship building with the communities which played the most vital role in successful implementation of every project and it will also be followed for this proposed project.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

During beneficiary selection priority will be given to most vulnerable HHs such as persons with disability, women headed HHs, HHs with elderly person, HHs with large house, LGBTI group maintaining SAAD criteria. Proper consent will be taken before data collection and confidentiality will be maintained as per implementing partner’s policies. Protection mainstreaming, gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion, meaningful participation, community engagement, accountability to most affected population are always prioritized by FIVDB. Community engagement in every phase of implementation and monitoring of the project and it will carry out by arranging formal and informal community consultation session, door-to-door visit, introducing feedback mechanism and regular observation and satisfaction survey etc. All the staffs will be provided protection gears and FIVDB’s own security protocol will be active throughout the project to deal any context specific risk and natural hazards.

3.2 - Gender

The activities will target atleast 60% women or more in every activity. As a part of transformative change electrical and general mechanics training will be enabled for both male and female participants. Male participants will be encouraged to take part in handicraft training and All the self-reliance activities will be enabled for the participants based on needs and vulnerabilities considering coordination, participation, transformative change. Most vulnerable HHs will identified and empowered under the program irrespective of sex, gender, ethnicity, religion etc. ensuring disability inclusion. The program will try to identify person of concern from LGBTI community and ensure inclusion of person of concern from the community and will initiate to respond to their needs separately if they face challenges to participate in the programs due to social barriers and taboo. The program will initiate to aware community people on GBV, violence against women and child and related PSEA issues.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The activities will be enabled for age group 18- 35 under the project. FIVDB discourages child labor and any violence against children. Inclusion of child in any cash for work activities, distribution program is strictly prohibited. Additionally, FIVDB will introduce nursing and breast feeding corners for lactating mothers in every distribution point and a nursing corner will be placed in project office for female participants with children. FIVDB’s child safeguarding policies will be active throughout the project to deal with any safeguarding concerns. HHs with pregnant and lactating women will be prioritized during selection of persons of concern. The project will essentially include awareness and sensitization session on child labor, child trafficking, violence against child, chill marriage. FIVDB will additionally include drop out adolescents and youth in the self-reliance activities.

3.4 - Diversity

The program will be designed to address and respond to the needs of more vulnerable communities irrespective of gender, sex, religion, ethnicity, culture etc. All the programs will be designed in person with disability friendly way and target to ensure disability inclusion in the program The program will target to include 4% HHs person with disabilities in overall programs. The person will also initiate to identify vulnerable LGBTI persons of concern and will initiate to respond separately to the needs of LGBTI person if they face any barrier in the program due to social stigmatization and barriers. The program will respective toward local culture and practice. All the modules and materials will be designed considering local and cultural context and practiced.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Community engagement in every phase of implementation and monitoring of the project. Information sharing, regular communication with donors and other stakeholders, maintaining transparency will be regarded as top most liability by the organization. FIVDB’s own safeguarding policy & PSEA policy (assessed by UNICEF) will be translated into very local language and sign language and disseminated among the project participants and every staff will be oriented as per the policy. Any PSEA incident reported will be dealt with by authorized investigation committees with trained members as per policy. FIVDB will prepare a risk assessment and mitigation plan at the beginning and will address and take mitigation measures for any risk that arise during project implementation. Hotline number & complaint box will be disseminated and established respectively in project areas to address and deal the complaints and feedback by the community people.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. All the participants under vocational training activities will receive disaster risk reduction and disaster preparedness training. Additionally, a number of participants from host community will receive training on vermin compost as a part of green skill and green business. Also, for ensuring environment friendly approaches, FIVDB encourage and monitor beneficiaries to not to use any materials for any purposes. Over the last five years, FIVDB was able to reduce significantly the use of such materials which are not environment friendly.
