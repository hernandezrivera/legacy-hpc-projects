Project Summary

The living conditions of Palestinians in the West Bank especially in Area C and H2 area of
Hebron city are still deteriorating because of the Israeli occupation and the prolonged
political crisis resulting in various negative impacts to the whole population, particularly
for vulnerable communities and families. Recently, the war in Ukraine has added extra
burdens to the vulnerable families in the West Bank manifested in the inflation rates and
rising prices for basic living commodities and production inputs mainly for agriculture
purposes. In this context and in line with the Food Security (FS) Cluster 2023 HRP
Framework, the proposed intervention led by Action Against Hunger, Palestinian
Agricultural Relief Committees (PARC), and Union of Agricultural Workers (UAWC) will
target the food security and livelihoods needs of the most vulnerable communities
affected by protection threats (including of the most recent targeted areas by Israelis
including of settler violence) in Area C and H2 area of Hebron city in the West Bank. In
addition, the proposed project will provide tailored agricultural assistance to targeted
communities to cope with the negative consequences of the different crises. The
proposed intervention will have two major implementing dimensions and activities: non-
agricultural and agricultural activities. Specifically, the activities will contribute to
protecting vulnerable communities in Area C and H2 from forcible transfer by improving
the main factors involved in food production (roads, water, livestock shelter), income
generation, and agricultural inputs. By doing so, the proposed intervention will contribute
to improving the food security of marginalized families living under the poverty line. The
project will also participate to the increase of income for vulnerable women and men in
non-agriculture sectors. This will include cash for work schemes as well as support to
existing small businesses and different Income Generation Activities (IGAs) aiming to
assist targeted communities to face deteriorated situation. The project activities will
beneft all members of the household and will be carried out on two levels: 1. At
household and individual level through the rehabilitation of animal shelters, provision of
cash support (cash for work), and providing support to existing IGAs as well as through
the provision of agricultural inputs. 2. At community level by improving the access to

water and the communities’ accessibility (road rehabilitation). The non-agriculture-based
activities will be designed to serve the targeted communities including individuals, with
focus on women affected by the crisis and other factors related to the war negative
consequences. The proposed intervention will deal with the protection threats to the
livelihoods of the targeted families. Fomenting a community participation approach in the
assessment, design and implementation of the activities, all the community’s sub-groups
will participate in different focus group discussions, technical assessments and in the
implementation process. The proposed project is in line with the FSS Strategic Objectives
3. The communication with communities (CWCs) will
mitigate potential security and protection risks and, ultimately, empower the beneficiaries
at community, family and individual level. As outlined, the proposed intervention ensures
protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and
dignity; meaningful access; accountability to affected population (AAP) as well as
participation and empowerment.

1 - Needs

The West Bank faces a protracted crisis, with repeated shocks and restrictions on freedom of movement, constrained productive capacities and lack of economic opportunities resulting in high unemployment and low household incomes especially in Area C. This, together with the high cost of living resulted from the war in Ukraine leave 1.8 million people (33.6% of the population) moderately or severely food insecure, making almost one out of three Palestinians suffering from food insecurity (FSS HNO 2023). More than 1.5 million Palestinians are in need of assistance (23% in the West Bank). Despite the slight improvement in the labour market in the West Bank in the first two quarters of 2022 (13.8% compared to almost 16% in 2021 based on the PCBS), the situation remains fragile. Based on the MSNA in 2022, more than 20% of the surveyed households reported facing difficulties to secure their essential and basic food needs, more than 37% reported using debts recently to cover their needs, more than 35% reported an increase in their income compared to previous years, and 21% reported that at least one family member face difficulties to find a job. In a latest survey conducted by AAH in 2022 in cooperation with the MoSD to assess the socio-economic conditions of the families registered under the MoSD’s system to receive social assistance, it was noticed that the conditions in the H2 area is deteriorating further; high % of unemployment rates (67%), particularly among females with no reliable sources of income. Although the employment rate is 33%, most of the job types are not sustainable. The people in the area suffer from low monthly income and high poverty rates with high dependence on cash assistance (22% with zero monthly income and 75% with less than ILS 1000). In addition, 100% live below the poverty line and 98% below the extreme poverty line which has intensified the reliance on negative coping mechanisms (selling productive assets and children dropping out of schools, 29% reported drop out of school). Additionally, communities in West Bank are under threat of forcible transfer (OCHA). So far, 2022 has recorded a significant increase in the number of demolitions, settler violence, and confiscations compared with the equivalent period in 2021 with a severe effect on the agricultural sector in Area C of the West Bank. Up to October 2022, more than 220 agricultural structures were demolished out of 694 demolished structures, representing 32% of the total demolished mostly located in Area C (557 structures). Out of the total demolished structures in Area C, 34% are agricultural related including livelihood infrastructures (OCHA monthly report, September 2022), which affects the vulnerability of families. Winter could bring high winds, storms, flash flooding and frosts, exacerbating the already poor humanitarian status for women, girls and boys. The ongoing assessments of AAH’s interventions in 2021 and 2022 as well the partners’ assessments in the targeted areas enabled AAH to identify communities requiring priority emergency support due to their vulnerability (protection needs, sectoral needs, preferences for interventions, unemployment rate, income, demolition/eviction orders, and sensitivity of their location). This suggested project relies on several assessments and updates where several data sets from multi assessments were double crossed to ensure accuracy and up to date needs. Based on the CPA’s annual assessment carried out by AAH and finalized in May 2022 within the West Bank Protection Consortium (WBPC), 89 communities were surveyed in all West Bank governorates including of having individual interviews and focus groups for men and women (at least 2 separated focus groups for men and women per community that includes different aspects of gender, AGD, PWDs). The final results were double crossed in line with different preferences and needs in the targeted areas within a clear mechanism that is carried out every year by the WBPC with clear ranking/prioritization mechanism for communities, sectors, and interventions. Accordingly, more than 680 interventions in the FSL sector were ranked to target the most vulnerable families in Area C most of them (more than 80%) are identified in the 6 governorates and targeted communities/areas as the most venerable areas. In addition, PARC has carried out a survey (June-July 2022) in coordination with AAH in 73 communities in the 6 targeted governorates followed by 6 extended focus groups (95 participants: 55 male and 50 females) to validate the survey’s findings and prioritize the interventions and locations. In addition to the above 6 validation focus group (one in each governorate), a series of coordination meetings conducted with working agency and related ministries to validate the logic of the intended interventions and required updates. The current political and economic negative consequences disproportionally affect the different gender and age groups. Female headed households were identified as particularly vulnerable due to their limited access to livelihood opportunities. Women and children are the most disadvantaged regarding food security status, women are responsible for cooking, while children are affected most deeply by malnutrition. AAH mainstreams PSEA actions to mitigate any risks to these vulnerable groups, especially in light of the disparate needs. AAH’s experience along with the previous and continuous assessments to animal shelters, particularly for 6 specific governorates, confirm that herding is the main source of income for most herding families (90% of the targeted families) - especially in Area C. Most of the animal shelters in the targeted areas are not adequate for the flock size resulting in overcrowded shelters. In addition, a proportion of the flock spent extensive periods of time in the open thus resulting in high abortion and mortality rates (25% in most of the targeted communities).

2 - Activities

The activities including the specific locations and governorates were shared and some adjustments (removing and replacing specific communities) were rejected based on the coordination with other applicants/partners to ensure no duplication. The project will contribute to enhancing the protection of the targeted beneficiaries from forcible transfer and violence against communities, belongings including of agricultural income sources, and individuals and any related negative consequences by improving the main factors involved in food production (roads, water, livestock shelter), income generation, and agricultural inputs. AAH, PARC and UAWC will coordinate the final selection of localities and beneficiaries with MoA, WASH Cluster, the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), MoSD and MoL directorates, the FS Cluster and with organizations working in the same area. To ensure diversity and a balanced gender and age perspectives, women children and elderly family members will participate in all stages of the project. Each partner will cover certain areas and AAH will lead the entire intervention. Partners will adopt specific risk mitigation plans to ensure smooth implementation of the set activities. Agricultural-based interventions: A.1 Support water infrastructure for agricultural purposes and provide water infrastructure rehabilitation to serve irrigation as well as ensure provision of water storages systems (Hebron, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Jericho, and Tubas), which will noticeably reduce the farming input costs. It will cover construction and rehabilitation of water pipes (20 Km), 80 agricultural cisterns and bonds, and rehabilitate 3 springs (2 springs in Batteer and Artas and 1 spring in Halhoul). A.2 Construction/Rehabilitation of livestock shelter for 200 vulnerable herders (Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem and Tubas). It will help ensure hygienic conditions and maintain quality production levels in light of the increased prices for inputs. A.3 Supply of agricultural inputs for the most targeted areas: tools and material for rehabilitating 260 greenhouses and 50 irrigation systems to improve productivity of farming of the most vulnerable areas and as identified in the FSS response matrix (Hebron, Jericho and Tubas). A.4 Rehabilitation of road to improve safe access to livelihood assets and services, agriculture land and grazing areas (20 km roads in Hebron, Bethlehem and Ramallah) A.5 Rehabilitation of lands located in areas under threat of confiscation in Hebron and Bethlehem (rehabilitation of 100 Dunums). This activity will support vulnerable farmers to protect and improve lands productivity. A.6 Provision of support to encourage alternative agricultural solutions and to provide drought tolerant seeds to improve access to locally produced pastures (20 dunums per herder, for 500 herders in Hebron, Bethlehem, and Tubas). Female herders and farmers who own livestock, land, greenhouses will be supported to improve the productivity of their land and to increase household income. The non-agriculture-based interventions: A.1 Cash based support (cash for work) and employability for families with limited access to income opportunities in Hebron including H2 area, Jerusalem, and Tubas. The project will mainly target workers in most affected areas who lost their jobs due to the current crisis and the effect of increased prices mainly in the agricultural sector. This component will assist them in securing basic cash assistance to support the household income and, therefore, contributing positively to the entire household’s food security. The planned work activities will include participating in constructing and rehabilitating public buildings (school rooms and facilities) within the community, roads, agricultural roads, land rehabilitation etc. This activity will be coordinated with the local councils and other relevant bodies involved in the coordination for this project to ensure smooth implementation of the activity and in line with the other projects as well being carried out by AAH and partners in the targeted areas including having a clear announcement and with specific criteria to be selected as outlined above. 50 individuals will be supported for a 75 day period based on a daily salary rate of NIS 80 for unskilled workers and NIS 120 for skilled workers. A.2 Support deteriorated (mainly agricultural) IGAs and small-scale businesses affected by the current crisis and the price fluctuation (Hebron, Bethlehem, Tubas, and Jerusalem) by assisting 40 small-scale businesses headed mainly by women. AAH will apply its customized international tool in IGAs (VIVEs Approach) which is being implemented in other similar projects in the West Bank since 2014 and involves training on business development, technical guidance and field visits as well as detailed follow up support to equip the targeted group with the required skills to maintain their income generation activities and thrive, During implementation, partners will ensure active participation by the targeted beneficiaries through a clear participatory monitoring and evaluation approach that feeds the process of activities adjustment/improvement. In general, all different approaches/plans/mechanisms are developed in full participation by the targeted groups to ensure their preferences and clear understanding of the different approaches/mechanisms in place. Additionally, PSEA is always considered during AAH projects, there is a well-established feedback and complaints mechanism for processing any concerns that participants or community members might have about the direct implementation or staff related to the project. These are processed sensitively, particularly for vulnerable groups and we make allowances to gender, age and disability, accommodating to those groups with home visits or private spaces to discuss any concerns.

3 - Indicators

AAH has a well-established MEAL unit where all projects are monitored during the entire project life cycle. The MEAL unit will follow up on all activities and work progress to ensure adherent to the main set goals and indicators. The process involves periodic reporting based on several means of verification including field visits, focus group sessions, questionnaires which follows a clear participatory approach by the targeted beneficiaries. A baseline survey will be conducted at the start of the project by the three partners which will be updated and compared to an end line survey after the activities’ completion. Information will be collected through semi structured interviews, household visits and field observations. Focus group discussions with female family members and female head of HHs will take place separately to eliminate any cultural and social barriers preventing them to actively participate in the project activities. Selection criteria will be identified by the partners in coordination with MoA, MoSD, MoL and the related local councils and the partners will coordinate with the related directorates in each governorate to benefit from their assessments and data. Particular attention will be paid to beneficiary selection based on diversity including their economical and displacement vulnerability, methods of working with village councils, mechanism of asset distribution and gender sensitive approach. Ongoing monitoring will allow AAH, UAWC and PARC to evaluate methodologies and the impact of the activities, to identify challenges and best practices. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through of AAH’s standard beneficiary feedback mechanism. In general, the project will benefit from a diversified population in terms of age and gender. The percentage of beneficiaries age will be: males age between 0-18 years 24%, females age 0-18 years 23%, males age between 18-65 years 24%, females age between 18-65 23%, males above 65 years old 1.5%, and females above 65 years old 1.8%, and PWDs 1.6%. The expected objective verifiable indicators at outcome level will include: The livestock mortality and abortion rates for the households targeted by livestock accommodation decreased by 30%. The agricultural water cost for the communities targeted by water interventions will decreased by 35%. The targeted farmers targeted by agricultural inputs will improve their productivity by more than 25%. To further ensure protection is mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, the indicator “% of communities/project beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” will be measured through an end line survey of the beneficiaries. total of at least 12,069 beneficiaries will benefit from the project’s non-agricultural and agricultural-based activities (F 18-59 yrs old: 2,803 M 18-59 yrs old: 2,901 B: 2,960 G: 2,810 Elderly Female above 59: 215 Elderly Male above 59: 188 PWDs: 192). For agricultural-based activities, the following are the main output indicators will be used: number of farmers, herders, breeders, fishers receiving inputs and services (11,834 individuals: 6027 females and 5,807 males). 820 female and male headed households (3,854 individuals: 1,889 female and 1,965 males) receive technical assistance to rehabilitate the water resources (pipes, springs and cisterns). 4 communities with improved physical access through the rehabilitation of 20 km of agricultural roads (2,880 individuals: 1,406 females and 1,474 males). 200 herders (940 individuals: 461 females and 479 males) receive technical and material assistance to rehabilitate their animal shelters. 500 herding HH (2,350 individuals: 1,152 females and 1,198 males) received climate adapted (drought tolerant) seeds for locally produced pastures in addition to support for alternative solutions to grow fodder (20 dunums per herder). 35 farmers (165 individuals: 81 females and 84 males) benefited from the rehabilitation of 100 dunums. For the non-agriculture-based activities, output indicators will include: 235 individuals (: 115 females and 120 males) mainly women receive support through the cash for work component. 40 (23 female headed and 17 male-headed) mainly small businesses in agriculture and IGAs (188 individuals: 92 females and 96 males) affected by the negative impacts of the crisis and the Ukrainian war mainly women will be assisted.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH, PARC and UAWC will ensure protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles of Do No Harm. The partners consider safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. Overall AAH seeks to complement its own actions with for instance legal support to inform risk mitigation measures especially in Area C and consequently to increase complementary humanitarian action. For activities that include physical interventions (including constructions of animal shelter and roads, water pipes rehabilitation), AAH will coordinate with the relevant legal entities to evaluate how this intervention will affect the legal status of the targeted communities. AAH, PARC and UAWC will do the hard construction works during Fridays and the holidays of the ICA to attract minimal attention from them, using low visibility implementation and coordinating with beneficiaries (especially animal shelter rehabilitation) to cover the new structures while ensuring that the new works will not change the overall appearance of the communities. In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH, PARC and UAWC will continuously work on understanding gender roles. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff has the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels. as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, all staff received specific in person training on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH has conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. Also, AAH started engaging with Humanity & Inclusion as inclusion experts to sensitise and inform AAH’s programming. For instance, instead of asking directly if people have a disability, AAH is increasingly using ‘The Washington Group Short Set of Questions’ to detect people with disabilities. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, AAH started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs. AAH has safe and accessible beneficiary feedback mechanisms in place which allow beneficiaries to provide their inputs, complaints, and satisfaction regarding the activities implemented. Beneficiary knowledge of this system are promoted during field assessments and monitoring visits during which AAH and partners staff explain how the mechanisms work. With regards to access to assistance and in this specific project the partners will 1) Use their respective data and knowledge of the areas to prioritize in a transparent and relevant manner more at need communities 2) Consult widely with community members, community leaders and MoSD, MoL and MoA directorates 3) Call for applications with a fair competition mainly for the income generation activities to select most relevant and promising business to be established and access business ideas based on criteria made public.
