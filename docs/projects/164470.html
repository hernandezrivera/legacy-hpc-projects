Project Summary

The project aims to increase safe and equitable access to formal and non formal education for crisis affected children and youth (aged 5 17 years) and improve the quality of formal and non formal education for children and youth (aged 5 17 years) within a protective environment. The project will be implemented in close coordination with MoE and related DoEs ensuring the protection of all beneficiaries through a ‘do not harm’ approach. ADRA Syria through this project will improve access to formal and non-formal education and learning opportunities for girls and boys by creating safe and protective environments for 14,005 children and teachers who have been affected by the crisis. This will be done through education interventions within the governorates of Rural Damascus (Kafa Batna), Quneitra (Quneitra city), Homs (Quseir, Al Rastan, Telbisa and Tadmor), Aleppo (Deir Hafer, As Safira, Jebal Saman), Dara’a (Deraa City, Bushra Al Sham) and Deir Alzour (Mayadin and Deir Al Zour city). ADRA Syria through the project will enhance the formal and non – formal education level for the children, both boys and girls. This will include a holistic program with psycho-social, familial and educational support, encouraging the engagement of mothers and fathers in educational activities. ADRA will target 2,100 students for informal education, 374 classrooms in 16 schools for rehabilitation, provision of furniture and WASH facilities, provide 4,95 students will school supplies, train 325 teachers and provide 1,600 students with PSS.
ADRA will coordinate with key stakeholders to ensure the most vulnerable boys and girls are identified in the target locations, and ADRA will conduct comprehensive needs assessments in the areas identified to locate OOSC and students with priority educational needs.

4 - Needs

The protracted nature of the Syrian crisis has had a devastating effect upon the educational psychosocial wellbeing of boys and girls across Syria. Estimates state that over 2.1 million boys, girls and adults remain out of school in Syria for reasons which include lack of access to educational facilities, child labour, and early marriage, especially for young and adolescent girls. In addition, approximately 1.3 million children (with approximately equal numbers of boys and girls) are at risk of dropping out, and 5.85 million school-aged children and about 245,000 education personnel female and male (mostly adult and older adult women) are in need of education assistance inside Syria. (HNO 2019). OOSC, ranged in age from 6 to17 years old, including married adolescents, have been out of school for between 2 to 9 years due to family responsibilities, the lack of support for female education, a lack of documentation and severe poverty. In addition, there is high infrastructural damage, widespread psychological trauma and malnutrition amongst school-aged children, both boys and girls. The infrastructural damage in conflict-affected areas also results in inadequate and overcrowded education facilities with the average number of students per classroom reaching up to 80, and children sitting on the floor in classrooms without doors and windows. In other areas where there are no functional schools, or where there is a shortage of schools, students walk 2-4 kms per day along dangerous roads to attend alternative facilities if they attend at all. Schools are often ill-equipped to accommodate students with disabilities as well as teachers with mobility challenges. Further to the conflict-related physical impairments, ADRA field staff estimate that 90% of children attending school require additional PSS and educational support.

5 - Implementation of activities

Project activities have been designed based on the identified needs of boys and girls, as well as male and female teachers. Rehabilitation of damaged schools will ensure equal access for boys and girls including children with disabilities, through gender segregated and disability friendly WASH facilities, and including accessibility to classrooms. The intervention will include repair of doors, windows, open spaces, painting, electrical systems, access ramps, handrails and the installation of western style toilets to facilitate access to WASH facilities for those with mobility issues. Provision of non-formal education programs, including the self-learning program and remedial classes (with incorporated PSS) will be provided to at risk boys and girls including out of school young and adolescent boys and girls, including children with disabilities. Beneficiaries will also receive school kits. Capacity building for male and female teachers and education personnel will be provided on topics including active learning, classroom management and psychological support. Feedback from the training showed it had a good effect on renewing the passion and energy among the teachers in schools. Throughout all stages of the project, ADRA will receive feedback from potential and actual project beneficiaries including boys, girls, adolescent boys and girls as well as men and women and people with disabilities. ADRA will elicit feedback through multiple means, including direct communication with project beneficiaries, constant follow-up with key stakeholders and anonymously via the established ADRA Syria complaints mechanism. This feedback is used to plan project activities, undertake adjustments to ongoing activities, as well as help measure the impact of the activities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. ADRA will train teachers classroom management including non-violence, as well as monitor non-formal education activities. Gender segregated WASH facilities will be established. All staff will sign a code of conduct and a zero tolerance policy will be applied. (Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Moderate; Acceptable level: Yes) Distribution of learning material and school kits might create conflict or tension between host community members and IDPs, especially in that most of the targeted communities include a large number of IDPs which could place beneficiaries or staff at risk. Thus, ADRA Syria will create certain criteria for distribution, depending on the vulnerability level and need of the beneficiaries which will include both IDPs and host community beneficiaries. (Likelihood: High; Impact: High; Acceptable level: Yes). Beneficiaries placed at risk due to project interventions, including SEA. To mitigate these potential interventions will be discussed with key stakeholders and beneficiaries and follow education sector guidelines to ensure a standardized response across all humanitarian responders and that no harm will inadvertently come to project beneficiaries (Likelihood: Low; Impact: High; Acceptable level: Yes). Insufficient support from, or undue influence by, members of the community or local authorities. To mitigate against this, ADRA will develop good relationships with all levels of stakeholders and address any concerns with different levels (Likelihood: Low; Impact: High; Acceptable level: Yes). ADRA has an established complaints mechanism and monitoring and evaluation mechanism that incorporates the feedback of children and teachers, including males and females, at all stages of the project through interviews, informal focus group discussions and complaints mechanisms.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Several potential risks have been identified that may impact the success of the project. One is the changing security situation. To mitigate this risk, ADRA Syria will monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct security assessments. ADRA will also choose locations which have been stable for some time and unlikely be directly impacted by renewed conflict. If needed, ADRA will negotiate with the donor to confirm the flexibility to change the project locations as required. (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes). On the other hand, as the proposed project is mainly community-based level, and targets mostly children, it is unlikely to create protection risks of project beneficiaries. However, these is a slight risk that by reopening schools, children will be exposed to protection concerns either on the way to and from schools. ADRA will select schools in safe locations that will not result in increased exposure to danger for the children. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes). Delays in obtaining the necessary approvals. To mitigate this ADRA will maintain strong connections with all levels of stakeholders to follow-up on necessary permissions and approvals and discuss any delays with the Ministry of Education and the Education sector in Damascus (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes). Also, another risk is that the procurement of supplies and materials could be subject to corruption or conflict of interest, to mitigate this ADRA Syria has developed and follows a clear procurement policy under which all purchases and contracting takes place. The policy includes a clear segregation of duties, levels of authorization, and conflict of interest declaration to help prevent fraud, corruption and conflict of interest. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes).
