Project Summary

The proposed project is the new version of the mobile clinics project, where these mobile clinics will be converted into health mobile units , due to the following reasons:
- Sustainability of services
- Assuring privacy of beneficiaries
- Assuring medicine supplies management-storing
- More effective follow-up mechanisms for patients
- Better equipped facility to provide telemedicine
- Provide quality services
The proposed project will ensure outreach and inclusion of 20,040 vulnerable individuals (women, men, and children) from IDP camps and remote locations in Idleb ( Harem, ) and Aleppo (Afrin) through the provision of 2 Health Units.
General clinic: one doctor and one nurse, they will provide medical check and follow ups for patients from all ages and genders, medicine description as needed and available and provide first aid services. Referral to other clinics (Nutrition – RH) or to another service providers.
Gynaecology clinic: one midwife Provide Reproductive health and family planning consultations. ANC-PNC follow up visits. In addition to referrals to other clinics (GP-Nutrition counselor) or another service provider.
Pharmacy: One pharmacist. Dispensing described and available medicine, educate beneficiaries about medicine use and regular inventory check
Community health team/ outreach: 4 CHWs. Awareness raising activities about related Health and Nutrition topics and community screening and referring cases to relevant clinics
During CHWs visits to target locations or beneficiaries' visits to the center, severe cases will be referred to Der Hassan and Sarmada maternity hospital, Bab Al Hawa hospital, and Azaz hospital.

4 - Needs

As the Syrian crisis is hitting its 10th year, the health system has been heavily disrupted impacting access to services and leaving millions of people living below emergency standards for health care. While health actors have made progress in addressing communicable disease, disrupted water networks and waste management, combined with food insecurity, and continued high levels of displacement, leave millions vulnerable to epidemic-prone diseases and place heavy demands on surveillance networks and immunization activities. Chronic shortages of health care workers, particularly in north Syria, prevent full functionalization of health facilities and essential services with reproductive health and maternal health services heavily impacted. Shortfalls of essential medicines are key barriers to health access, particularly for non-communicable diseases which are estimated to account for 45% of all deaths. Ongoing hostilities in north-west Syria continue to trigger large-scale. Today, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance - a 21% increase compared to 2020 - with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline.The crisis continues to have a gendered impact, with women and adolescent girls paying a high price for harmful and discriminatory gender norms, including gender-based violence, while men and boys face elevated risks linked to arbitrary detention, forced conscription and explosive ordnance, among others .

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

In line with the health cluster, SR will ensure " Don no harm "principles are applied to identify and mitigate against potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the health sector strategy and related activities. We will ensure that SPHEREstandards are strictly followed and adapted to the context during each part of project implementation, especially during the emergency health services when the risk of GBV is higher. Measures will also be taken to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female‑headed households) to greater protection risks, and particularly to GBV. health staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls, and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation, and monitoring phase of each project, with a need to put the necessary mechanisms in place to facilitate feedback from users. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity, and non‑discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SR will create a risk register, in which it will identify relevant risks, conduct their analysis and their evaluation by identifying mitigation measures. This will allow to have understanding of the risks and agree on the internal controls to be applied and monitored. Overview of the risks and internal controls: The risk of fraud and misappropriation of assets and supplies due to inadequate financial controls, the lack of segregation of duties and personal integrity. This will be mitigated by having robust internal procedures with the adoption of checks and balances allowing the prevention and detection of fraud. A thorough stock management process is established with a field capacity to spot-check warehouses and conduct stock counts. A whistleblower hotline is available to all project stakeholders and they are aware of it early in the project to report any suspected act of fraud or corruption. An emphasis will be put on the risk of mishandling Personally Identifiable Information (PII) related to project beneficiaries, staff, contractors, vendors, etc. SR will ensure to adopt data protection and privacy measures and ensure compliance throughout the implementation. Particular attention will be given to data protection in case management due to the sensitive nature of its related activities. COVID-19 may have a negative impact and affects Programme implementation. To prevent the spread of COVID-19, staff are trained on COVID-19 protocols issued by Health cluster
