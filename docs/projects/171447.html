Project Summary

This project aims to address the urgent needs of Limb Reconstruction patients in Gaza, especially those who suffered from complex LR injuries as a result of the Great Marches of Return (GRM) events.
This project is in rhythm with Strategic Objective #2 “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.” and Health Cluster Objective #1 “Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt”. Almost 2.7 years after the GMR, many of those injured are still struggling with the devastating consequences of their wounds. They are stuck in painful waiting while the effects of their injuries take an ever greater toll on them and their families. To add, the situation with COVID-19 and the closure of elective surgeries as part of the emergency response made things even worse for many of the LR patients who have been pleading for people to help them amid this most recent crisis.
The category of complex limb injuries has placed impossibly high demands of the Ministry of Health to deal with as these cases require years of treatment and are financially overwhelming. Plus, the skills' set available in Gaza are only able to meet a tiny proportion of these numbers and complexity of injuries.
MAP started the LR work in 2014 establishing the first dedicated LR unit in Palestine and have had huge successes in terms of LR interventions, this project is designed to meet the continuous needs of the very recent and late-complex limb injuries of the GMR but also non-GMR patients as these far exceed any local capacity that exists in Gaza. As per the MAP database, there are still 369 GMR LR patients under treatment and also, weekly, MAP adds 5 new patients to the database on average (Oct, 2020).
As stated previously, this project will aim to reduce the suffering of the LR patients via bringing medical missions from the UK (if situation allows) and also via the supporting the local teams in Naser with the needed supplies to perform the LR interventions locally.

1 - Needs

The ‘Great Marches of Return’ (GMR) demonstrations at the Gaza fence ended but resulted in more than 6,300 live ammunition gunshot wounds to the limbs which are 88% of the total of the live-ammunition injuries (HC report January 2020). Out of these, and as per MAP’s assessment, there are 388 complex LR injuries that MAP has been helping the MoH to address since March 2018. Only 19 of these were discharged leaving 369 patients that still require urgent and complex care. This number of casualties reflects the surge in demand on the already overburdened health sector in Gaza either in terms of drugs, supplies, human resources, equipment, or training. This all has to be added up to the drastic financial and timely needs of LR patients. All the above was faced by a new reality that is the COVID-19 pandemic. The MoH started implementing emergency status level D in which a full closure was imposed on Gaza preventing medical missions from entering, cancellation of all surgeries that were deemed elective (such as LR despite the fact that these are urgent in nature of care), the stoppage of shipment from entering Gaza due to border temporary closures but also the halting of coordination services at both Palestinian and Israeli sides.Having said that, please note that the MoH has resumed LR surgeries at Naser unit but on a very limited scale. However, during the suspension of services, there were at least 42 surgeries that have been added on the waiting lists of LR. As stated previously, there are still 369 cases to be treated in under the LR service at Naser hospital. MAP coordinates its efforts with all relevant stakeholders and it should be mentioned that currently, amid the suspension of missions, MAP coordinates for a biweekly online meeting between MoH, IDEALS (our partner in UK), MSF-Belgium and MSF-France to discuss clinical cases and provide tele-medicine support to local teams and organizations dealing with LR injuries in Gaza. Our experts indicated at these tele medicine meetings that there is a great need to sustain clinical and surgical services for patients with LR to avoid imminent complications as amputations, osteomyelitis and eventually disability. This comes in line with the new pandemic which already delays service provision for patients with LR since the suffering of LR patients is regarded now as a compounded humanitarian suffering. Due to COVID-19, MAP is planning to change its approach in 2021 to accommodate the new reality. More details will follow under the activities section, but new activities will include providing local training courses, conducting master classes and provide online learning methods for ortho residents in Gaza related to LR in specific. For missions, if these are not allowed to come, the surgeries that would have been conducted during missions will be conducted by local team and MAP will increase its support more towards procuring more items to enable the local team to conduct this work. In addition, the aforementioned biweekly online meetings will help setup treatment plans for the local team to enable them better.GAM:MAP provides the LR treatment to both male and female patients and all ages according to their specific needs with no discrimination. Also, the in-patient physio services (supported by WHO at the Naser unit) are provided to both genders considering the cultural settings via a male and a female physio. The project is addressing the needs of all patients regardless of their ages or gender. Data protection:Additionally, MAP is committed to GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation), this means that all patients’ data saved by MAP are encrypted and restricted. Not all MAP staff have access to the data. Also, MAP is utilizing a specific consent form for every patient and avoid any photos showing the patient face at all to provide them with some protection if these materials were to be used online or publicly. MAP also is very active on CHS and is part of the alliance globally.Having said all of the above, it must be said that the needs are still greater than all the stakeholders’ capacities combined, and that donors support is key to addressing the needs of LR patients in Gaza.

2 - Activities

Overall, the activities of this project will target 450 unique patients. Medical expats in addition to the local team will deliver 1840 consultations through the outpatient clinics and will conduct 408 complex limb reconstructive surgeries. Furthermore, the educational component of the project will help build the capacity of 75 healthcare workers from across the LR system of surgeons, nurses and physios. The activities are summarized as below: - Conduct 6-8 medical missions (this depends on the quarantine situation in Gaza and worldwide but we hope to be able to bring the missions as usual, if not then we will increase the local team’s capacity through procuring more items to enable them to do more surgeries and via conducting more tele-medicine conferences to decide proper treatment plans)- Provide 400 consultations through clinics conducted with physical or remote support from medical expats- Perform 120 surgeries on the patients who are in most need for complex interventions by medical expats- Conduct medical MDT 6-8 symposiums for 30 attendees of ortho/plastics related health care providers- Provide local trainings for 14 ward and theatre nurses and 6 physios staff at Naser unit (after coordination with WHO and MoH and the relevant staff at the LR unit, these could be easily facilitated remotely too)- Conduct two Master classes with the local teams for 25 orthopaedic residents (Master classes are tele medicine platforms with an interactive learning approach, UK missions can easily join remotely too)- Provide online learning platforms for the orthopaedic residents in Gaza (25 residents)- Procure the needed LR items and donate these to LR units at Naser- Local team to conduct the weekly clinics in Naser delivering 30 consultations and receiving 3 new patients in average every clinic (we support the data collection and patient coordination at these clinics) 30*4*12 = 1440 consultations- Local team to operate on 6 patients weekly without assistance of expats 6*4*12 = 288 surgeries- MAP to follow up with the cases using EQ-5D-5L assessment form- Maintain the project’s database- Conduct bi-weekly online clinical discussions with MoH and other relevant partners to address 2 cases every meeting. 2*2*12 = 48 cases Please note that this project will serve all people regardless of their gender. As per gender mainstreaming in this project and since the majority of injured patients enrolled in the database of the project are males since they are GMR patients, male to female ratio was 1:0.3. Number of injured children who were below 18 years old was 173 with a ratio of 1:0.016. This has to be taken into consideration while planning the project activities. For example, MAP core MDT team that has been trained by our partner expats includes male and female physios that help in the examination of female patients and ensure their dignity. In addition, the visiting teams have a reasonable balance of male and female practitioners which takes into consideration cultural differences and social variances. At least every quarter, a pediatrician LR specialist examines the children cases.As for protection, please note that protection of data and beneficiaries has been one of the founding pillars to the LR services in Gaza. Extra care is taken into consideration treating patients’ data, soft copies are stored in an online safe place controlled by our London IT manager with access limited to few MAP staff only, hard copies are secured in metal cabinets with keys made to only two staff members, data is shared online without any unique identifiers such as ID or names but via unique codes (we are currently drafting an agreement with other stakeholders such as MSF-F to unify these codes), no data is taken from patients unless they provide full written consent and patients can even ask MAP to alter their names and not to publish photos.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Patients’ engagement in the LR project is done during the implementation phase with continuous feedback that we receive from them using either verbal feedback through the two project assistants or using EQ-5D-5L that reflects their satisfaction about their health status. All the feedback is sent back to the officer and senior programme manager and if any problems are found, then we try to identify solutions on the spot. MAP is currently in process of implementing a case series study focusing on the patients on waiting lists affected by COVID-19. The study will help us determine the needs of the patients and also their treatment outcomes and will inform MAP’s decision making in 2021. This is considered as feedback that will be used to influence the designing phase of the project. On the other hand, MAP involved the local surgeons and MDTs in the design of this project. For example, MAP consulted with the local team to agree on number and dates of medical missions, approach of intervention, focus on residents, to send local staff for fellowships and list of equipment, etc. Two FGDs will be conducted mid-life of the project: one with beneficiaries to examine how their treatment is ongoing and if something could be enhanced and another with the local MDT members to seek their feedback and make it enrich the project. A full M&E plan will be created with the custom and cluster specific indicators which will enable MAP to keep a close eye on the project. Management decision will be adapted based on the feedback received from the beneficiaries in an evidence-based manner to implement any necessary changes as deemed necessary.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

MAP has developed an organisational algorithm for responding to Safeguarding concerns and region-specific algorithms for the initial investigation of such concerns. These documents were developed in conjunction with safeguarding experts and are considered fit for purpose and consistent with current sector guidance and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS).They contain clear lines of responsibility and reporting and, where appropriate, confirm the timelines to be followed when responding to safeguarding concerns. All these documents are reviewed at least every two years. An incident report template is used to report any suspected breaches in MAP’s Code of Conduct or Safeguarding policy to MAP’s Safeguarding focal point.In addition, MAP applies very strict measures ensuring the privacy of the data of patients by not using names or any identifiers, rather unique codes that are known only to MAP and the relevant stakeholders to protect the privacy of data.For this project specifically, while MAP does not directly operate on patients or run the OPCs at MoH, we help organize all these activities and will make sure, with the MoH and other stakeholders, that COVID-19 protection and prevention measures are taking place during all project activities.For PSEA, please note that MAP has developed an organisational algorithm for responding to Safeguarding concerns and region-specific algorithms for the initial investigation of such concerns. 9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MAP is neutral and does not lean toward any group over the other in the context we live in.MAP is impartial, and we provide aid to those who need it regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc.MAP is an independent organization and is not affected by commercial powers.MAP is humanitarian and provides aid to those vulnerable and suffering all the timeWe support the MoH to deliver the LR services during its one and only LR unit in Gaza. The MoH and MAP do not have exclusion criteria based on severity of the injury (unlike other stakeholders).
