Project Summary

The primary purpose of this project is to assist vulnerable female and male farmers through the provision of one or more of the following services:
• Rehabilitation of open lands: 230 dunums for 115 farmers (both female and male) whose age is between 18-70 years. The cost of rehabilitating one dunum is expected to be around $430 (NIS 1,500).
• Rehabilitation of greenhouses: 120 greenhouses for 120 farmers (both female and male) whose age is between 18-70 years. The cost of rehabilitating one greenhouse is expected to be around $860 (NIS3,000).
• Installation of solar systems for agricultural 10 water wells. The cost of installing the solar system for one water well is expected to be around $30,000 (NIS 105,000).
The project's objectives match the HRP 2023 oPt, Strategic Objective country #(3): The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. The project will improve the resilience of households and communities to cope with and mitigate shocks and environmental hazards. The three project’s activities are expected to increase support to communities’ livelihoods which in turns will improve the general food security situation and will have a positive effect on their overall nutrition and health status.
Furthermore, the project is specifically in line with the Food Security Sector (FSS) objective#(1) The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced. The project will be based on the BBB approach to increase resilience and reduce vulnerability.
The project activities will be implemented throughout the Gaza strip (the five governates). NDC will partner with a locally registered cluster NGO, Palestinian Al Nakheel Association for Progress and Development, who has extensive agricultural experience which makes it to be the most capable one to assist NDC in implementing the project.
NDC will be responsible for the overall management of the project activities, procurement, financial disbursement, monitoring & evaluation, and reporting. In addition, NDC will be responsible for implementing the project activities on the ground in the Gaza and the northern governates. NDC will be also responsible for the formation of the fie local committees, finalizing the beneficiary list in coordination with MoA and the rehabilitation activities. In implementing the project activities, NDC will coordinate with the MoA, FAO, FSS, & INGOs.
Our partner NGO will be responsible for implementing the project activities on the ground in the middle and southern governates, including the formation of the fie local committees,

The estimated number of direct beneficiaries is 235 farmers, while the total number of the farmers and their families as indirect beneficiaries is 1316 (667 males, 649 females,). The 1316 also composed of 596 adults, 683 children, 37 elderly). The project will also benefit 29 PwDs. The estimated total budget for the project is $ 691,336.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip faces a chronic humanitarian crisis make that made most of the persons in need for some form of urgent humanitarian assistance. The last large-scale escalation of hostilities in Gaza and the blockade imposed on Gaza for 15 years ago, the internal & Palestinian conflict extremely increased poverty & unemployment rates. According to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the overall unemployment rate in Gaza reached 44 per cent during the second quarter of 2022. According to (WFP Palestine Country Brief September 2022) “one third of the Palestinian population, 1.78 million people, suffer from food insecurity. Of those 1.1 million Palestinian are severely food insecure. Most of them (90 percent) live in the Gaza Strip”. Furthermore, according to OCHA June 2022 Fact sheet 1.3 million out of 2.1 million Palestinians in Gaza (62%) require food assistance. Furthermore, the Ukraine conflict worsening the food crisis and decimating families' purchasing power in Gaza. According to the World Food Program, the Ukraine crisis has increased food prices in the Occupied Palestinian Territory such as wheat four (up by 23.6%), corn oil (26.3%) lentils (17.6%) and table salt (30%), decimating Palestinians’ purchasing power. According to OCHA Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) published on July 2022, 60.3 % of household that reported a member of their household being unemployed and looking for work at the time of data collection. Furthermore, 70% of household the reported difficulties in meeting food needs in the 30 days prior to data collection. According to (WFP Palestine Country Brief September 2022) “ In Gaza, the recent August 2022 escalation of violence left many additional families in need of assistance. Moreover, according to the MoA August 2022 Conflict Damage Report, the total agricultural losses reached one million USD. This has exacerbated the dire humanitarian situation there after 15 years of the Israeli blockade, aggravated by having one of the world’s highest unemployment rates and deep poverty”. In October 2022, NDC and its partner NGO started a rapid needs assessment process. During the assessment, 7 focus groups were conducted with farmers in the Gaza Strip. The objective of these focus groups was to discuss the main agricultural damage & losses as well as to determine the most urgent needs that address the sustainability of their livelihood. Two focus group were conducted in Gaza and in the north of Gaza by Beit Lahia Ass. and attended by 21 famers (17 males and 4 females). Five focus groups were conducted in Khan Yunis, the Middle area & Rafah by Al Nakheel Ass. and attended by 75 farmers (61 males and 14 females). In total, the 7 focus groups were attended by 96 persons (78 males and 18 females). The focus group participants’ ages were from 22 to over 65 with different marital statuses. During the needs assessment, both male & female farmers indicated that thousands of dunums of open land, greenhouses, water wells, solar systems, and irrigation networks were either partially or completely damaged by the recent Israeli aggression. They indicated that the rehabilitation of; open land, greenhouse, water well, and existing water ponds are the top priorities for them. All of the farmers that were consulted (100%), both male and female, & from different age group including PWD, indicated that utilizing solar systems to irrigate their lands is the highest priority due to the severe lack of electricity and the high costs of fuel. Solar systems will provide them with continuous access to water at very low cost. In particular, 83% of the female farmers indicated that the second priority for them is open land rehabilitation since many women own open fields and they usually work in their fields. In addition, the farmers, both male and female confirmed that they suffered severe losses not only due to the recent Israeli aggression, but also because of the spread of Covid-19, low purchase power, difficulties in marketing their products, etc. This led them into significant debt. In fact, due to the nature of these activities, there was no significant difference in the needs of male and female farmers as well as between age groups and ability. All the farmers that were consulted (male & female & from different age groups including PWD) indicated that they prefer to receive cash assistance for rehabilitating their land, greenhouses, and water ponds. For the rehabilitation of the water wells & installing solar systems, all of the women preferred that NDC do the rehabilitation/installation for them while around 40% of the male farmers preferred to do the rehabilitation/installation themselves (cash assistance and not service delivery). Both male & female farmers also indicated that they prefer to receive the cash from the banks or at the CBOs premises. The farmers, both male & female, indicated that they are willing to attend a PSEA workshop. In fact, 100% of the female farmers were enthusiastic about attending the workshop. They indicated that they would like to increase their knowledge about these concepts & they would like to know more about their rights.

2 - Activities

This project is designed in a participatory manner taking into consideration the interests of the project's stakeholders. Beneficiaries' dignity and independence are of utmost importance while preserving gender equity and sensitivity without any bias. Furthermore, these activities will be implemented based on the needs identified ensuring active participation of men, women, boys, girls, and PwDs as analyzed through the AGD tailored interviews and focus groups. In addition, the project will be based on the Do No Harm framework with a gender transformative approach to empower female farmers in the Gaza Strip. All targeted farmers will be selected according to protection-related, and socio-economic vulnerability criteria such as:• Size of the family (the larger the family the higher the chance)• Land ownership (not more than 3 donums for OL and 2 donums for GH)• Number of beneficiaries and number of dunums irrigated for Water Wells• Preference will be given to:o female farmers/land owners o farmers benefiting from the CTPo families having PwDs o families having students at local universitieso farmers affected by the last August conflicto farmers who are not employed by any institutions (public, private, or NGOs)The beneficiaries’ selection criteria are based on the identified needs, gender equity, sensitivity without any bias, and Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Attention will be paid to target female farmers who practice agriculture farming by themselves. NDC and its partner will encourage women and PWDs to apply to benefit from the project activities. NDC will announce that preference will be given to female farmers & PWDs (will get additional marks). In addition, the local communities that will be formed by NDC will have 50% women representation. Furthermore, Gender & Protection against PSEA were embedded in the needs assessment and design of the project, and will continue to be emphasized during implementation. All project beneficiaries will be required to attend one of the 15 PSEA & GBV awareness workshops. This is a condition for any beneficiary to receive assistance from NDC. The protection section includes additional details about these workshops. NDC will make sure that 100% of the beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age, and disability) reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable & participatory manner. The project has three activities: 1. Rehabilitation of open lands: 230 dunums: 30 in North, 30 in Gaza, 50 in Middle, 60 in Khan Younis, & 60 in Rafah for 115 farmers (female & male) whose age is between 18-70 years. The cost of rehabilitating one dunum is around $430 (1,500NIS) 2. Rehabilitation of greenhouses: 10 in North, 15 in Gaza, 25 in Middle, 40 in Khan Younis, & 30 in Rafah for 120 farmers (female & male) whose age is between 18-70 years. The cost of rehabilitating one greenhouse is around $860 (NIS 3,000). The rehabilitation work of the open land and the greenhouses will be done through the BBB approach for increasing resilience and reduce vulnerability. NDC will implement the rehabilitation by hiring a qualified agronomist. 3. Installation of solar systems for agricultural water wells: (10 water wells with water pumps capacities that range from 25 to 30 HP) distributed equally all over Gaza Strip (The cost of installing solar system for one water well is around $30,000 – NIS105,000). To implement these activities, NDC will use conditional restricted cash assistance for open lands, &d greenhouse rehabilitation. NDC will provide each farmer with 3 installments: 30%, 40% and 30%. The installment modality is used to ensure farmers use the cash assistance for its intended purpose. The farmers are required to provide NDC with copies of all invoices of the agriculture inputs that they bought. For solar systems installation, NDC will use a direct service modality (section 5.2 has more details). To benefit from the project activities, NDC will ask beneficiaries to attend PSEA/GBV workshops. Furthermore, NDC will conduct 2 days training for the staff working on the project (NDC staff as well as the partner NGO staff). To ensure the dignity and security of the beneficiaries, especially female farmers, NDC will use local banks to provide their cash assistance payments. The project targets three main categories. The first is the affected farmers from the May 2021 aggression on Gaza, affected farmers in border areas (ARA), & farmers who are suffering from severe electricity cuts and who are facing difficulties in running their water wells. 235 farmers will benefit directly from the project's activities. Their immediate family members (1316 persons - 667 males & 649 females) will also benefit indirectly. The 1316 also composed of 596 adults, 683 children, 37 elderly), also 29 PwDs will benefit. At the start of the project, a local advertisement will be published in each of governorate to ask farmers who meet the criteria to submit applications. The advertisement will be published in mosques, local NGOs, municipalities, etc. Five selection committees from NDC, its partner, local committee members, and local figures will be formed to select the best applications. Finalized lists of applicants will be passed to MoA & FSS for feedback on the selected beneficiaries. Based on the feedback, a final list of beneficiaries will be developed. NDC will be responsible for overall management of the project activities, procurement, financial disbursement, M&E, and reporting. Our partner will be responsible for implementing project activities on the ground, including the formation of the five local committees, finalizing the beneficiary list in coordination with MoA & the rehabilitation activities. In implementing the project activities, NDC will coordinate with MoA, FAO, FSS, & INGOs. Coordination with NGOs, local committees, municipalities, and other governmental bodies will be the role of the partner.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

NDC and its partners will use a combined modality for implementing the project. NDC will use direct assistance (conditional and restricted assistance) to the selected beneficiaries for the following activities: open land and greenhouse rehabilitation. The main reasons for selecting this modality are: 1. Fast and efficient. 2. Flexibility 3. Give power of choice to the beneficiary 4. Dignity and limit sexual exploitation. For the installation of solar systems to power agriculture wells, NDC and its partners will use a direct service delivery modality. The main reason for this is that this activity requires technical specifications and design requirements to ensure efficiency and effectiveness. It would be extremely difficult for small-scale farmers to come up with high quality designs and technical specifications. NDC will procure the service of specialized consulting companies or qualified individual consultants/engineers to develop the technical specifications and the design requirements. Moreover, NDC will advertise for those activities in the newspapers and the relevant websites in order to benefit from the economy of scale.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

NDC will train local partner staff on the kinds of feedback mechanisms available at NDC, how to collect and forward feedback and concerns received, and how to follow up on the feedback. Any recommendations, suggestions, and concerns of the beneficiaries, along with the mitigation measures will be discussed during the bi-weekly senior management meetings. The team will make sure that all the issues and concerns are followed up with and appropriately resolved. If NDC or its partner receive any negative feedback or complaints related to the quality of the services, how the project staff deal with the beneficiaries, or any PSEA matters, a task team that consists of NDC Gaza Program Manager, the partner NGO Manager, and one member of the local committee will be immediately formed. The team will study the complaint, suggest solution, discuss the suggested solution with all concerned parties, implement the suggestion, monitor and evaluate the implementation of the suggested solution, and finally close the case upon satisfactory results. The whole process will be fully documented.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

NDC will ensure the dignity and safety of the targeted farmers by conducting the following four steps: 1. Before implementing the project activities: NDC and its partner NGO will conduct 20 awareness raising workshops (four in each governate) about the project objectives, outputs, and activities. This to ensure that all the farmers are fully aware of the project and if any of one of them thinks or believes that they will be negatively affected, they will be given the opportunity to inform NDC and partner NGOs of their concerns. Consequently, NDC and partner NGO will immediately take into account the farmer’s concerns and will solve all issues that might negatively affect them. 2. Beneficiary selection: Beneficiaries will be selected through the use of standardized social and technical vulnerability selection criteria consistent with humanitarian principles and practices and through gender-appropriate activities 3. Throughout the implementation of the project: NDC will conduct 3 community scorecard sessions in each governorate at the beginning, middle and end of the project. In total 15 scorecard sessions will be implemented. These community scorecard sessions will ensure that no harm is done to any member of the targeted community and will also work on improving the quality of services provided by the project. 4. Complaint mechanism: NDC already has an active complaints system that will also be available to the local communities should any project-related issues arise. NDC will take immediate corrective actions. Furthermore, to avoid any sexual exploitation and/or abuse, NDC will make sure that all necessary measures are taken throughout the project design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation such as staff training, enforce PSEA through NDC's established procedures, conduct awareness workshops for beneficiaries, etc. In addition, at the start of the implementation of the activities, a one-day workshop about protection be implemented in the 5 governorates of the Gaza Strip for the selected beneficiaries. The workshop will be repeated 3 times in each governorate (in total 15 workshops will be implemented all over the Gaza Strip). The aim of the workshop is to educate beneficiaries about protection guidelines and concepts, and how to deal with any abuse or harm that they might face. Furthermore, the workshops will inform the selected beneficiaries of the grievance/complaints system that is available through NDC and how to use it if they were subject to any abuse or harm. NDC will distribute the PSEA booklet to the participants (please refer to section 91.d for more information).
