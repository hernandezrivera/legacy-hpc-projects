Project Summary

It provides a comprehensive response to survivors of gender-based violence through new seven (4) safe spaces for women,in: (Al-Hassakeh - Tartous "Banyas, Altoun Al jourd" - Aqrab - Hama" and Daraa city) including specialized services, empowering women through vocational training and income-generating activities, as well as building the capacity of GBV partners to provide prevention and response programs Quality
Expected outputs of program implementation:
1. Provide integrated awareness sessions:
1.1 on reproductive health, especially for people affected by the crisis, including host communities and displaced communities, with special emphasis on adolescent girls.
1.2 Conduct activities aimed at raising awareness of legal issues related to their daily lives
1.3 Recreational activities for women and girls in order to ease the pressure they suffer from the crisis in Syria.
1.4 Strengthen the capacity of community leaders and young people to advocate against gender-based violence, including early and forced marriage
2. Empowerment of women and girls through vocational training courses:
2.1 Reading and writing courses for computer use, providing them with job opportunities in line with the requirements of the labor market
2.2 Develop language skills for women and girls through English and French courses offered to them in safe places
2.3 Cooking, handicrafts, knitting
3. Training to enhance the implementation capacity of SSSD to prevent and respond to gender-based violence, and the actor's work on gender-based violence against gender-based violence (women / men) and humanitarian workers who have been trained to reduce Of the risk of gender-based violence (ie, IASC GBV guidelines), with a particular focus on vulnerable women in humanitarian situations.
4. Provide specialized services:
4.1 Provision of case management services to people who have survived gender-based violence.

Needs

SSSD staff and volunteers surveys,during FGDs in 8 governorates and monitoring and assessment documentations,showed the following needs:13 protection issues were surveyed (child labour preventing school attendance,child recruitment,domestic violence,early marriage,economic exploitation,explosive hazards,family separation,harassment,housing,land and property issues,kidnapping,lack/loss of civil documentation,sexual harassment and sexual violence).Further information was collected on movement restrictions (causes and population groups affected); lack/loss of civil documentation (reasons for not having documentation,type of documents that were not available,impact); housing,land and property concerns coping mechanisms,types of protection services present and needed and concerns related to delivery of humanitarian assistance. Attention was specifically devoted to sex and age disaggregation and each of the protection issue was detected for male,female,boys, girls,adolescent boys,and adolescent girls and–in most cases–persons with disabilities. The weakest and most vulnerable groups at risk suffering from marginalization and rejection within their families and in the hosting community have no independence to secure their basic needs,Including: women heads of household,who suffered physical violence,sexual violence,psychological violence,survivors of forced marriages and early marriage,women which finished the specialist servises . Both, IDPs and people from the host communities,need time to build mutual understanding on how to establish better life conditions. Crises threats may arise from non compliance with international humanitarian law or human rights law during armed conflict,or the breakdown of law and order in the aftermath of a natural disaster. That makes women,girls,children,youth,persons with disabilities, or older people more vulnerable to abuse and exploitation. The loss of many families in the city to their dependents,which in many cases contributed to the disintegration of the family and the dispersal of their children and the loss of some children to their right to education and other rights. Many families are denied access to health care,particularly vulnerable groups (elderly,children,and children) due to dispersal of families,whether due to displacement,migration or other reasons,As there are no safety spaces,Such safe spaces are an appropriate opportunity for them to receive psychological and health care and cover part of their many needs. for the elderly and the elderly to protect them from all aspects of violence. For private care (health-social ... etc). The prevalence of gender-based violence in families and society in general. We note that girls are deprived of education,for example,as a result of fear of going to schools or universities only because they are girls. The spread of the phenomenon of early marriage of minors for social or material reasons related to the inability of the family to bear the daily costs of their children,sending their male children to work in harsh conditions and marry their daughters to relieve the burden. The spread of new phenomena on the city between teenagers and young people, such as bets and random shooting at events and other extraneous phenomena caused by the crisis,which in turn strengthened the disintegration of families,either through the extortion suffered by adolescents as a result of gambling or the loss of many people as a result of exposure to random bullets and the subsequent Tension in society.These areas have not been worked on in advance with the most vulnerable women and in particular they were areas of security tension, and the presence of organizations and services is very few, for example: (Banias - Daraa - Aqrab) Especially that Daraa is a new open area where a vast majority of women and girls remained. As well as the Aqrab region, has not been interfered with by any organization or association other than SSSD.

Activities will be implemented in all locations:1. Provide integrated awareness sessions:1.1 on reproductive health, especially for people affected by the crisis, including host communities and displaced communities, with special emphasis on adolescent girls.1.2 Conduct activities aimed at raising awareness of legal issues related to their daily lives1.3 Recreational activities for women and girls in order to ease the pressure they suffer from the crisis in Syria.1.4 Strengthen the capacity of community leaders and young people to advocate against gender-based violence, including early and forced marriage2. Empowerment of women and girls through vocational training courses:2.1 Reading and writing courses for computer use, providing them with job opportunities in line with the requirements of the labor market2.2 Develop language skills for women and girls through English and French courses offered to them in safe places2.3 Cooking, handicrafts, knitting3. Training to enhance the implementation capacity of SSSD to prevent and respond to gender-based violence, and the actor's work on gender-based violence against gender-based violence (women / men) and humanitarian workers who have been trained to reduce Of the risk of gender-based violence (ie, IASC GBV guidelines), with a particular focus on vulnerable women in humanitarian situations.4. Provide specialized services:4.1 Provision of case management services to people who have survived gender-based violence. case management and psychosocial support in a safe and confidential setting, beginning with thorough intake, consent and assessment; awareness raising of available services; counseling; safety planning; client accompaniment; and regular follow up, always in the best interest of the child and GBV survivors with their consent

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Backlash from family and/or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment activities2. Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and3. Expose women and girls to further economic violence in livelihood programming or due to locations of the activities4. Risk of inter or intra community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served5. Risk of increasing violence in the home and community for girls and boys receiving specialized services

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Activity 1: Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle and adaption of GBV messaging Activity 2: a. case management with survivors to be conducted by trained personnel only b. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centered service c. Mainstream messaging on the risk of the available legal systems which is likely to cause harm to survivorsActivity 3: a. Conduct gender livelihood assessment and gender analysis of the community, and design WMBG programs b. Conduct sessions with men/families on family budget and resources management
