Needs

As per FSS HNO needs assessment of 2018-2020, the productive capacity & stability of income for about ‎‎25-30K farmers, herders, breeders in Gaza continue to be impacted by the consequences of the 2014 ‎conflict & identified as a need in Gaza Strip. The restrictions in accessing available natural resources is one ‎of the main underlying causes of vulnerability & impediments to development. We Effect conducted a ‎Rapid Need Assessment (RNA) in Oct 2017 to the targeted areas & verified the needs again Sept 2018. 5 ‎field visits & 4 direct interviews were conducted. Coordination w the FSS & MoA was completed to endorse ‎the project target area; the N area, the methodology for the RNA was collecting info from vulnerable M & ‎F small-scale farmers in the access restricted areas, both in direct interviews & focus group. The visits were ‎conducted for local farmers owners of local agricultural wells & head of local women capacity building ‎center. ‎The main findings of the RNA are:‎‎-‎ M&F farmers suffer from deprived livelihood due to the inability to sustain the irrigation for their ‎crops, which exposes them to damages & economic loss due to the electricity outages, ‎salinity of H20, ‎high cost of irrigation H20, poor irrigation systems & H20 carrier line, ‎unrehabilitated greenhouses & ‎open land, H20 ponds.‎‎-‎ ‎% of the women owning land in the North does not exceeding ‎‎20%, while men own 80% of the land. ‎Challenges for F farmers in North are the necessity & responsibility of spending longer hrs ‎compared to ‎men on farming which includes leaving their boys & girls ‎w/o care & exposing them to hazards.‎‎-‎ Extended power shortages are now the norm in Gaza & threaten food security. The irregular & long hr ‎power cuts16-20 hrs/ day is severely affecting the livelihoods of farmers & communities from losing ‎their crops or using high cost fuel operated generators to keep their crops from drying; causing cost of ‎producing & selling vegetables to increase. While these farmers have a source of H20 for irrigation ‎from the main reservoir that is privately owned, the H20 is pumped to farmers’ H20 pools/ponds ‎during & not always continuous w pauses in supply, nor is it at regular intervals. The gap in irrigation ‎time for farmers is especially serious in N of Gaza Strip, namely Beit Lahyia, where there are 50 ‎dunums of agricultural lands that are at risk of damages, especially given recent border events. To ‎overcome hardships, farmers H20 storage & land rehab so that they can resume their farming activities ‎& safeguard their livelihood. ‎ ‎As a member of FSS, We Effect will work w sector to ensure timely & effective response to any food crisis ‎in ‎‎Gaza Strip & now w a feedback mechanism to MoA. We Effect received & filtered MoA list to include ‎communities that ‎‎are most in needed & to ensure no duplication of interventions.‎ In general women & girls will be affected positively after improving the food security conditions on the HH ‎level. Women are vulnerable to adverse impacts: shortage &/ or lack access to food resources since they ‎are responsible for the food management & inadequate availability of food exposes women, youth ‎children, & elders to high risks of malnutrition. The BNF are vulnerable M & F farmers & their families ‎‎(including children & elders) affected by the food shortage availability & living in Gaza. Women ‎involvement in the project activities is ensured by forming committees for these activities, and agreeing ‎about works-related initiatives, from which under 18 shouldn’t be involved (child work). We consider under ‎age 18 are the result, where the output of the livelihood activities is observed in terms of the whole HH. ‎No group will be excluded, & equality will be ensured by planning & implementing the activities in a way ‎that benefits F & M equally but in a manner that addresses their needs & concerns stemming from ‎prevailing gender norms.‎

Activities

All project activities and interventions have been reviewed, evaluated & ‎coordinated with the below parties: - National and international NGOs ‎active in the geographic area - Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) - Local ‎CBOs and agricultural producers. The outcome is improved food ‎security 588 vulnerable people by supporting 105 smallholder farming ‎HHs in the Occupied Palestinian Territory. Major activities or ‎outputs of the project: ‎‎1.The selection of several target farmers will be done in close ‎coordination with a local CBOs and local steering committees & in ‎close coordination with the MoA, (44 F and 61M) and based on the ‎vulnerabilities as specified the RNA. Farmers selection criteria will be: The farmers should be existing in the same geographical area of the ‎agricultural well suffering of lack of adequate water for irrigation & able to maximize expected yield for the most amount of farmers. Priority will be ‎given to the most vulnerable groups such as FHH, single HH ‎with children, HH w elders (60+), chronically ill and disabled ppl as well as HHs w no active employment. The criteria for farmer land is btwn 1-5 Dunums. ‎‎2.We Effect is the main responsible party for the project management. The ‎project will jointly be implemented with ESDC. We Effect will provide all ‎the needed technical and financial support to ESDC to ensure best quality ‎of project implementation ‎ 3.Coordination: -Both MoA and FSS were coordinated with for endorsing ‎the project target areas and avoiding any overlapping in the plans of ‎interventions and activities. -Coordination with local farmers and local ‎committees will be conducted at the beginning of the project. ‎‎4. Project: Rationale: The project is to be for a 1 year with ‎engagement of local farmers, w a focus on women farmers in the ‎management of the project delivered services after the project ends. The ‎proximity of selected farmers to the needs and problems of surrounding ‎communities makes it the right organization to effect local development ‎roles and make them local development agents both as a channel of ‎service delivery that undertake productive activities by putting available ‎resources to their most efficient and sustainable use and offer appropriate ‎resolutions in conflicts of resource management at the community level. ‎Farmers to be responsible for the management of the project rehabilitated ‎well after the end of the project. Full responsible for the management of ‎the project well at discounted price, consequently, the project will provide ‎the target group farmers with the needed humanitarian services to improve ‎their livelihood. Project aims at including women, men, boys, girls and ‎older people throughout every phase in the project. Children under 18 will ‎be represented by their parents or guardians. All data will be collected also ‎based on FSS matrix for age and gender. Further, it is realized given the high poverty rates in the targeted communities, a CFW modality will be implemented where possible.‎‎5.Project Activities ‎‎1.Rehabilitation Activities 1.1 Rehabilitation of 1 agricultural well using ‎renewable solar energy to produce needed electricity for pumping water ‎for a main pond. (1 well (including water pump) with solar energy and one ‎main pond). The water well will be selected based on accepted salinity and ‎serves farmers in the same geographical area. (10M &10F) ‎‎1.2 Rehabilitation of 35 small ponds for farmers who will have access to ‎water pump (25M/10F)‎‎1.3 Rehabilitation of 50 dunums of open land. (35 M & 15F) ‎ ‎2.Coordination activities:‎‎ 2.1 Local farmers will be selected to manage the rehabilitated well after ‎the completion of the project‎2.2 Local steering committees will be formulated in support during the ‎selection process of male & female farmers Close Coordination with the ‎MoA will be conducted during the selection process of the well and the ‎local farmers.‎
