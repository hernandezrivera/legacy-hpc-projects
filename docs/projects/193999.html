Project Summary

The humanitarian nutritional needs in Sudan are expected to remain at high levels or worsen further in 2023, making Sudan one of the countries in the World with highest estimated acute malnutrition caseload. While the overall Global acute malnutrition (GAM) remains at 13.6%, the prevalence of GAM varies significantly across the localities with very high situation (GAM above 15% and above as per WHO classification) reported in 64 localities. The proposed nutrition project aim is to improve equitable access to quality life-saving curative and preventive services through the systematic provision of nutrition services in South Kordofan, North Kordofan, Central Darfur, North Darfur, West Darfur, Sennar, Blue Nile and Red Sea states in which 29 localities will be covered. Vulnerable children under-five and PLWs are the main target of the project. Through SC -Nutrition interventions, a total of 362,874 beneficiaries will benefit from the services provided, among them (87,131 boys, 90,680 girls, 184,383 women, 680 men). Save the children will implement lifesaving, preventative interventions, treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition among children under five years and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs), referral of SAM children with medical complications and minor rehabilitation to nutrition sites. To reach the estimated targets and planned response scale up, the nutrition sector will deploy integrated response modalities with health sector, WASH, FSL, protection, education at facility and community levels. SC will deliver nutrition services through existing health facilities, mobile and outreach teams and at community such as mother-to mother support groups activities including IDPs and hard to reach areas, use of cash-based interventions to increase access and utilization of nutrition services through FSL will be used for most cases in need. In term of emergency response, rapid response mechanisms for timely delivery of nutrition interventions in the onset emergencies will applied.

Specific nutrition response strategies will include scaling up the minimum preventive and protection lifesaving intervention package for prevention of malnutrition, treatment, and geographical coverage of severe and moderate acute malnutrition among the under-fives, strengthening referrals for children suffering from SAM with medical complication through provision of transportation costs to and from the SC, nutrition system capacity strengthening (capacity and infrastructure) will be done to facilitate provision of quality and people centered life-saving nutrition responses, timely procurement and delivery of core nutrition supplies. Additionally, infant young child feeding (IYCF) and social behavior change, and communications (SBCC) will be strengthened to promote good feeding and access to services. SC will strengthen national and sub national partnership with all stockholders to deliver nutrition services. SC will ensure monthly and quarterly monitoring of the evolving nutrition situation through an early warning and early actions and implementation of SMART surveys in prioritized localities with GAM 20% and above as well as supporting other assessment initiatives such S3M and MICS.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian nutritional needs in Sudan are depicted to remain at high levels or getting worse in 2023, making Sudan one among the top counties in the World with highest estimated acute malnutrition caseload. While the overall Global acute malnutrition (GAM) remains at 13.6%, the prevalence of GAM varies significantly across the localities with very high situation (GAM above 15% and above as per WHO classification) reported in 64 localities. These localities include four where 2022 SMART surveys were conducted. Five of the 64 localities have GAM prevalence above 30%, classifying the situation as catastrophic. The projected worsening of the specific drivers of malnutrition including food insecurity, deterioration in provision of Health and WASH services, economic decline and suboptimal caring and poor feeding practices for under-five children are likely to contribute to deterioration of nutrition situation, based to IPC for acute food insecurity findings in July 2021, only 20% children aged 6-23 months were provided the minimum acceptable diet (MAD).The new admissions trends at Save the children (SC) have significantly increased in 2022 compared to 2021 (database report-Jan- August), in which SAM and MAM cases are higher by 26% and 27% in 2022 respectively. The project will be addressing humanitarian nutrition needs of the most vulnerable population, including IDPs and refugees, through integrated delivery of lifesaving-preventative-protection minimum nutrition backage, SAM& MAM treatment and increase resilience among girls, boys and PLW in prioritized localities through preventive multi-sectoral responses in SK, ND, CD, WD, BN, NK, RS and Sennar states.Save the children has adequately trained technical staff and service providers on CMAM and IYCF, SC will continue support basic and on the-job training to increase the capacity of health care providers to provide standard case management of malnutrition and IYCF-E, including CP mainstreaming. Mother support groups will receive training on MUAC screening, GBV and CP and referral mechanism to improve access to treatment.Save the Children has an environmental sustainability policy and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of it is programs and operations, Save the Children will include environmental considerations into all interventions. In particular, the sourcing (local, national, international) of all supplies and materials, especially those used for construction and rehabilitation of infrastructure including fencing of nutrition facilities will be carefully considered to select the option with the least negative environmental impact, and where appropriate from locally available materials. Nutrition/IYCF messaging will encourage the use of locally made improved stoves to reduce charcoal consumption and encourage growing of vegetables and trees which can contribute towards dietary diversity as well as an improved local environment. The empty sachets of the ready to use food (RUTF/RUSF) is regularly returned by caretakers to the facilities to be collected and burned through the incinerators to avoid littering. The project will support safe referral systems for SAM with medical complications to nearest Stabilization Centers (SC) and continuously monitor the evolving nutrition situation for early warning and early actions.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Save the children will implement lifesaving preventive and protection minimum nutrition package which includes counselling session for Pregnant women and caregivers of children 0-23 months on MYCN, distribution of iron and folic acid during pregnancy, early detection of malnutrition among under 5 through MUAC screenings/ referral and regular at household level, early detection of malnutrition among under five children by mothers MUAC (household) and provision of deworming of under-five children. The project will improve access to quality lifesaving nutrition services to treatment severe and moderate acute malnutrition among under-fives, pregnant and lactating women, this in addition to Stabilization Centre (SC) services for SAM cases with complication. preventive services for IYCF including corners to promote, protect and support breast feeding will also be scaled up. The project will improve the quality of nutrition services through capacity building, monitoring, and reporting, strengthening referrals. SC will include eight (8) activities that contributing to the resilience while 65% out of the total activities will be implemented by SC, a 42% ($5,791,469) out of the total budget is needed to implement above mentioned 9 resilience activities. The intervention will also contribute to the reduction of malnutrition and increase resilience among girls, boys and PLW in prioritized localities through preventive multi-sectoral responses to address causes of malnutrition in integrated manner with health, WASH, education, and food security as well. Furthermore, will Improve understanding of the nutrition situation in prioritized localities with critical levels of acute malnutrition through evidence generating assessments and analysis to support in planning. The project targeting IDPs, returnees and host communities in the targeted localities with focus on severity ranking and highly vulnerable groups. The planned coverage will contribute to achieve a minimum coverage of 50 per cent in rural and 70 per cent in urban and camp settings as per the sphere standards. SC is currently supporting 133 OTP/TSFP facilities in 8 states (SK, NK, CD, ND, WD, Sennar, RS and BN) within 29 localities. 90 facilities provided integrated TSFP/OTP services within the same facilities. SC supporting MOH facilities by providing comprehensive health, nutrition, and wash services, providing the PHC services, ante/postnatal care, water purification at the facilities, soap, water for drinking and handwashing, SC also provide running cost for the facilities and incentive for MOH staff and CNV supporting screening and referral of malnourished cases. SCI covers the transportation cost for referral and treatment of SAM cases with complication.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Save the Children will ensure that girls and boys, women, and men (of all ages, abilities and backgrounds) are consulted in a meaningful way in programming at all stages of the project lifecycle and have regular opportunities to provide feedback and concerns. Gender, age, and disability sensitive measures will be adopted, including through adapting meeting/service schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, considering ways to support the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries, and ensuring facilities are physically accessible to all, and adopting child-friendly methodologies. Save the Children will provide timely and accessible information (in local languages and culturally appropriate mediums) to affected communities on proposed interventions and deliverables, expectations of staff behavior especially with respect to our Child Safeguarding policy and Code ofConduct, and how to raise concerns or provide feedback. SC Sudan has a well-established Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanism, including a telephone hotline, and multiple further modalities which provide affected populations opportunities to give feedback anonymously and/or privately, and options for those literate and illiterate. Field level MEAL staff are responsible for receiving complaints, managing responses, and reporting them to Country Office MEAL staff who report to Senior Management. Data is anonymized, analyzed, and utilized for project management and planning. There are clear processes for responding to PSEA and other safeguarding concerns raised through any feedback channel. Save the Children has safeguarding specialist staff at Country Office level, who will provide additional support and conduct monitoring visits.
