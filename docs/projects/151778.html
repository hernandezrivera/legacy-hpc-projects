Project Summary

In line with the objectives of the “Somalia Recovery and Resilience Framework”, the project aims to respond to immediate, lifesaving needs of vulnerable communities in Somalia, and to strengthen resilience in health in the country.
The protracted conflict in Somalia, coupled with recurrent natural disasters, has forced people to flee their homes time and again since the civil war broke out in 1991. With an already weak health system, with little presence in some areas of the country, this has resulted in vulnerable communities such as women and children having little or no access to health.
To address this scenario, WHO proposes a pilot project in 15 camps for the internally displaced in Somalia, to be conducted in phases. Phase One of the project targets internally displaced communities. Phases that follow will cover other populations in need.
The country’s immunization coverage falls well below international benchmarks. Through an integration programme, the current polio workforce – which has presence in every district of Somalia – would take on the role of EPI, delivering vaccination services against Polio, measles, and other antigens to Somali children, who have largely been underserved for decades in the absence of a functioning public health system.
The project will deliver vaccinations using static and outreach mobile services, and will use detailed, comprehensive microplans developed by the polio programme to reach out to the target audiences.
The ultimate objectives of the project are to prevent and respond to Vaccine preventable disease outbreaks to Somali communities, and develop the capacity of Somali polio staff and health professionals to equip them with the skills, knowledge and ability to provide immunization services to Somali children.

Needs

Only 51 percent of children under the age of one have received diphtheria, pertussis, and tetanus (DPT) 3 vaccine/pentavalent (2016); merely 53 percent of children have been vaccinated against measles (2016)*. By rolling out this project, WHO will train health workers and polio staff on delivering vaccinations to children, particularly in underserved areas, while increasing overall immunity within Somali communities. *Source: Eastern Mediterranean Region (2017): “Framework for Health Information Systems and Core Indicators for Monitoring Health Situation and Health System Performance”.

Activities

1. Rapid assessment of immunization status in the selected IDPs camps: WHO will conduct a rapid assessment to determine details on IDPs in Somalia - where camps for IDPs are, how best to reach out to them and the most urgent immunization needs that will assist already vulnerable children. Assessment will factor in details of immediate host communities and will entail security details. During this stage, WHO will determine educational levels of community members for use in creation of communications materials. 2. Communications activities (communication for development, development of IEC materials, external communication): Based on the needs of the community, WHO will develop communications materials that will inform IDPs and their host communities of the benefits of vaccinations, and training materials for health workers and onboarding community vaccinators. Any rumors or misconceptions around vaccines will be dispelled, and all materials will be shared with focus groups during design and ahead of production. 3. Trainings for polio staff and IDPs community vaccinators: WHO will train polio staff and community vaccinators from within IDP camps to deliver vaccines to target populations. 4. Transport and logistics of vaccines: WHO and partners will ensure vaccines are transported in line with international standards, for safety and storage at optimum temperatures. 5. Supervision of staff during activities: A key component of this project will be to ensure all trained staff are meeting required standards in terms of vaccine safety, vaccine delivery, hygiene, and interpersonal communication on the job. 6. Deploy 3 to 5 team of community vaccinators per IDP camps (3 persons per team): A key component of this project will be to ensure all trained staff are meeting required standards in terms of vaccine safety, vaccine delivery, hygiene, and interpersonal communication on the job. 7. Provision of vaccination in the selected 15 IDP camps

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

When humanitarian emergencies – such as drought and floods – strike in Somalia, this results in large population movement and the rapid spread of diseases such as measles and other vaccine preventable diseases. Children often bear the brunt of all these challenges - according to UNICEF, one in every eight Somali children dies before the age of five. The project will have a two-pronged approach to providing durable solutions to Somalis and building resilience: (a) to improve children’s immunity, reducing the overall impact of disease outbreaks, and ensure children lead full and productive lives; and (b) to build capacity of Somali nationals, to empower them to strengthen their own health service delivery systems. The project is in line with the Health Sector Strategic Plan (2017-2021) and aims to support Somalis attain Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Although access to IDP camps and informal settlements in high insecurity areas of the country can be unpredictable at times, WHO Somalia has a long track record of cultivating local partnerships and relationships that facilitate relatively continuous service delivery. WHO will consult the members of the Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Cluster as well as focal points from security agencies, active local NGOs, and community leadership structures. WHO Somalia will mitigate the risk of creating resentment toward IDPs among host communities by ensuring that host community members are included in communication activities and are able to access services provided by WHO on a nondiscriminatory basis.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

WHO will work to include female health workers, polio vaccinators, and community leaders in the design, implementation, and any modifications to this project.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

WHO Somalia will mitigate the impact of armed conflict and violence throughout the implementation period for the project by conducting frequent risk assessments and by working through well-established and trusted partners on the ground, especially in areas of the country experiencing ongoing and escalating violence. In areas where humanitarian organizations have been targeted or threats have been made against humanitarian actors, WHO will implement activities through the local polio teams, community leaders from IDP camps and host communities, and by partnering with local non-governmental organizations that have broad and longstanding access to beneficiaries.
