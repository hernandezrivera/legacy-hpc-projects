Project Summary

The idea of the project is based on providing protection & PSS services for children aged 6-17 years old affected by conflict related violence & violence in households (from both sexes including PWDs), the activities will be provided through AWDA’s Community Centers in Gaza & Rafah, and for the successful implementation of the project, the local community will be communicated with information about the project’s activities & locations through: (i) Radio spot (ii) Facebook ads (iii) Meetings with the local committees affiliated to AWDA.
In order to ensure the maximum protection for the children who will benefit from the project services, the capacity of the project staff (M&F) will be built through conducting 3 training courses as follows:
• 1st training course in disability inclusion aiming at strengthening protection mainstreaming throughout the humanitarian response.
• 2nd training course in the guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support and child protection standards/tools.
• 3rd training course in AWDA’s safeguarding policy, PSEA policy and Code of Conduct to guarantee the highest protection of the beneficiaries during receiving the services
To relieve the psychological pressure on the project staff, 3 days of self-care will be implemented with them. From the other side, capacity building will be implemented for non-child protection professionals (whether from AWDA’s workers or CBOs) in child protection detection & referral (children who are affected by conflict related violence and household violence and who subjected to protection threats) aiming to mainstream CP in other humanitarian action.
The project staff will provide the following services for the targeted children & their caregivers:
(i) Structured PSS sessions (ii) Collective & individual PSS services (iii) Case management & referral services, while special needs will be provided to some children, such as medicines, clothes & shoes. (iv) PSS caregivers’ sessions in positive parenting, stress management & cyber violence protection. It is worth to mention that these services will be provided by using IDEAL, WE DEAL and PARENTS DEAL manuals
The project will include community-based activities as follows:
(i) Publishing radio spot & Facebook ads with the aim of expanding awareness of the community in the field of child protection (ii) Publishing motion graphic videos (interpreted to sign language to reach PWDs) with awareness messages related to PSEA (iii) Conducting awareness sessions with different groups of the community members, including women, men, boys & girls (PWDs), with the aim of raising their awareness about child protection PSEA in order to encourage them to detect cases & refer them to specialized centers.
In order to achieve the principle of AAP, the beneficiaries will participate in all the project cycle phases & will be informed about AWDA’s Feedback, Complain and Response Mechanism (FCRM). Noting that simulation on submitting feedback & complaints will be carried out with the children in order to encourage them to submit feedback & complaints that will help us to improve the services.
Within the framework of coordination & complementary work & to avoid duplication in the provision of services, coordination was made with the Protection Cluster Coordinator, the Child Protection Coordinator and the Ministry of Social Development who in turns supported the idea and confirmed its importance.
For complementarity in the implementation of services and to avoid duplication in service provision, coordination has been made with GCMHP and WEFAQ association.

1 - Needs

The protracted protection crisis in the oPt means that around 1.2 million Palestinians across the Gaza Strip, experience or are at risk of conflict, violence and displacement, including forcible transfer. 513,758 out of them in Rafah and Gaza. (HNO 2022)Children in the Gaza Strip are exposed to conflict related violence, which is clearly evident during the Israeli attacks on the Gaza Strip, the most recent of which was on August 2022, that resulted in an accumulation of the humanitarian crises and had devastating effects on the population of the Gaza Strip in general and on the children in particular. The 2022 report of the Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights confirmed the killed of 46 Palestinians, out of them 15 children, while the MoH reported that 360 Palestinians were injured out of them 151 children. Moreover, children in the Gaza Strip are exposed to high levels of domestic violence, since 90% of children face violent discipline at home (97.9% of girls and 92.3% of boys), while 20.1% are subject to severe physical violence from their caregivers. (Secondary Data Review - SDR Report Sept. 2022). According to 2022 study issued by Save the Children (SC) children are feeling less safe when away from their parents (90% in 2022 compared with 60% in 2018), and are experiencing higher levels of emotional distress (on average an increase from 55% to 80%). Moreover, Children reported feeling fearful (84%), nervous (80%), sad (77%) and grief (78%) in 2022 compared with fearful (50%), nervous (55%), sad (62%) and grief (55%) in 2018. While Children’s distress is exhibited by more worrying behaviors, such as bedwetting (79% in 2022, 53% in 2018) and reactive mutism4 (59% in 2022, 42% in 2018), and they are less likely to be able to find positive ways to cope with their situations. The results of the 2021 study conducted by Save the Children & War Child found that approx. 198,000 children in the Gaza Strip need psychosocial support, 36,000 children need case management services & 79% of the researched cases showed distress signs & behavior disturbances. The study also pointed out that 38% of the researched cases do not feel safe even after the end of Israeli attacks on the Gaza Strip, while 65% confirmed that there is a scarcity of safe play places available for children & 98.7% emphasized the lack of safe play places for children. According to the HNO2022, more than half (53 per cent) of all children in Gaza, and 137,000 caregivers, are in need of various MHPSS services.From the other side, too many children still do not enjoy their full rights and don’t get a fair chance to realize their potential due to being born with disability, and they are the most affected by conflict-related violations, and are exposed to violence at home, at school, or in the streets. This means that children with disability must be targeted according to the inclusion approach in the services provided. The HNO2022 indicated that 63% of PWDs in the Gaza Strip lack access to MHPSS services. According to MSNA2022, the percentage of households estimated to include at least one family member with a physical or mental disability: • 14% Occupied Palestinian Territory • 21% Gaza • 10% West Bank.Moreover, the participants of the FGs implemented at AWDA’s center emphasize the importance of the sustainability of providing the PSS and protection services, that response to their needs and effectively contribute to solving many of the issues they face. They also stressed the long-term positive impact on their humanitarian situation.

2 - Activities

The project activities are designed to response to the protection needs of children aged 6-17 years old (from both sexes including PWDs) affected by conflict related violence and violence in households in Rafah and Gaza through AWDA’s permanent community centers located in those areas adding to renting a place in CBOs located in Al Shatea Camp to implement the project activities, as follows: 1. Publishing Radio Spot & Facebook Advs about the project's activities & locations adding to AWDA complaint system including the complaint channels for SEA where it is expected that 120,000 persons (10000 boys, 10000 girls, 50000 men, 50000 women) will benefit from these advs. 2. Building the capacity of 14 persons of AWDA’s CP & PSS service providers (4 M, 10 F) including (10 project team + 4 workers from AWDA’s protection program) through the implementation of 3 training courses as follows: (i) 24 hour training course in disability inclusion aiming at strengthening protection mainstreaming throughout the humanitarian response. (ii) 24 hour training course in the guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support and child protection standards/tools. (iii) 12 hour training course in AWDA’s safeguarding policy, PSEA policy & Code of Conduct to guarantee the highest protection of the beneficiaries during receiving the services. 3. Building the capacity of 12 persons (4 men, 8 women) of non-child protection professionals (whether from AWDA’s workers or CBOs) in child protection detection & referral (children who are affected by conflict related violence and household violence and who subjected to protection threats) aiming to mainstream CP in other humanitarian action, for 18 hours (3 days, 6hours/day). 4. Providing structured PSS sessions to 540 children (270 boys, 270 girls including 27 PWDs) (270 children/center in Rafah & Gaza) aged 6-17 years of both sexes (including PWD) (12 children/ group). The 1st age group (6-9 years old), the 2nd group (10-13), & the 3rd group (14-17 years old). It should be noted here that (I DEAL & WE DEAL) manuals will be followed during the implementation of this activity and each group will receive 20 sessions. 5. Conducting 108 collective PSS sessions for 216 children (108 boys, 108 girls including 11 PWDs) who have been detected from the above-mentioned sessions,(18 groups - 12 children/group – 6 sessions/group) with the aim of dealing with some problems such as lack of motivation, social isolation & poor social skills. 6. Providing individual psychosocial sessions for 140 children (70 boys, 70 girls including 7 PWDs) (who detected from the collective sessions or who come directly to receive this service) who have psychological, behavioral & emotional problems (with average 8 sessions/case). 7. Providing case management services for 120 children of both sexes (60 boys, 60 girls including 6 PWDs) who are exposed to protection threats, including referral to receive MH, legal services and speech therapy services, in addition to responding to the basic needs of some children such as clothes, shoes and medicines .. etc. Cases are referred by the case manager internally to the specialists working in the project through the internal referral form. While external referral to more specialized services is made by the case manager to the institutions working in the child protection field by using the national information system or through referral forms. As for the referral to other clusters, they will be contacted by the case management supervisor at AWDA Association.8. Conducting PSS caregivers sessions on positive parenting, stress management & cyber-violence for 120 persons (60 men, 60 women, including 2 PWDs) who will be divided into 10 groups (12 participants/group) (7 sessions/group) as follows (4 stress management, 2 positive parenting & 1 cyber-violence protection). 9. Awareness raising for vulnerable communities' members on CP issues through implementing the following activities: (i) Conducting 100 community awareness sessions targeting 1200 people (300 men, 300 women, 300 boys, 300 girls including 24 PWD), (12 persons/ session), with the aim of raising community awareness about child protection issues and spreading the concept of child protection. (ii) Publishing 1 motion graphic videos about PSEA interpreted to sign language to reach (50000 women, 50000 men, 50000 boys, 50000 girls). (iii) Publishing 1 Radio Spot & 2 sponsored Facebook Ads with the aim of educating the community (10000 boys, 10000 girls, 50000 women, 50000 men) about child protection issues. 10. Organizing 3 self-care days to the project staff (2M - 8F).

The success of the project will be measured by achieving the following indicators:# of people who have information about the project activities and locations adding to AWDA complain system. # of AWDA’s staff (women and men) who received specialized training in disability inclusion. # of AWDA’s staff (women and men) who received training on guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support. # of AWDA’s staff (women and men) who received specialized training AWDA’s safeguarding policy, PSEA policy and Code of Conduct. # of non-child protection professionals who have received training in child protection detection and referral. # of children affected by conflict-related violence and violence in households benefiting from structured psychosocial services. # of children affected by conflict-related violence and violence in households benefiting from specialized child protection interventions. # of children at risk and affected by conflict-related violence benefiting from specialized individual case management. # of persons (women, girls, men and boys) participating in child protection awareness raising sessions. #of children's caregivers (men and women) affected by conflict-related violence in households benefiting from structured psychosocial services. knowing that a MEAL plan will be prepared by AWDA MEAL officer including methods for data collection, frequency, responsibilities and reporting Indicators as follows:MEAL Plan: will be divided into the levels of the log frame so that the project staff ensures each level's achievement by describing the levels, indicators, and baselines. It also will identify the process of collecting data, including the tools, responsibility, data sources, frequency of data collection, types of analysis, and finally, presenting and submitting the information obtained by making comparisons between groups and variables to the intended users. Indicator Performance Tracking Table (IPTT): The MEAL Plan will contain IPTT, which clarifies the time frame of tracking the performance of the indicators of each level of the log frame. Regarding the project, a time frame of 12 months will be adopted, as it will contain the expected performance ratio and the realized ones during the implementation of the project.It is worth to mention that, once the project started, the project coordinator will conduct a meeting with the project staff, where he/she will present the project activities, goals, indicators and results to the project staff to prepare action plan and to distribute responsibilities and duties. The project coordinator and the MEAL Officer will follow up and monitor the project activities on the ground on a daily basis through direct contact with the project team. AWDA project coordinator will conduct regular meetings and field visits to the project location to ensure continuous flow of information on the progress of activities, and to supervise work on the ground. Weekly reports and statistics will be sent from the field to the project coordinator where all statistics will be desegregated according to age, gender and PWDs. AWDA project coordinator will coordinate with the donor for issues related to the implementation of the project. Moreover, he/she will compare the financial plan with the real expenses as well as action plan with the achieved activities in order to find out if there is any deviation in the implementation of activities according to the time plan or the financial cost. In the context of involving the local community in the project monitoring, 4 focus groups meetings will be held with the project beneficiaries from both sexes and from different age groups including PWDs in the middle of the project duration with the aim of discovering any deviations in the implementation of activities and to work on modifying them. At the end of the project duration, satisfaction measurement questionnaires will be filled by beneficiaries to determine the percentage of their satisfaction with the project's services and to benefit from their suggestions for future work strategies.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Al Awda Association has great experience in the field of providing protection and PSS services for children that spanned for tens of years through 6 child protection centers spread across all governorates of the Gaza Strip. Hence the selection of service delivery modality for the importance of the provision of these services and their positive effects on the lives of affected children on the one hand, and on increasing the awareness of local community members on issues related to child protection on the other hand, while previous studies such as the War Child and Save the Child studies indicated the urgent need for the provision of protection, PSS support and case management services for children who are affected by conflict related violence and domestic violence. Hence, the services provided through this project will contribute to protect the targeted children and provide a safe community incubator that provides protection for children in its various forms, that will contribute to a positive deviation in the proper development of the children.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback and complaints will help AWDA decision makers to: • Develop future intervention strategies that include the provision of new services or the development of existing services based on the needs and suggestions of beneficiaries. • Hold accountable employees involved in violations of the protection policy and code of conduct. • Helps to respond quickly to the needs of beneficiaries.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

WhatsApp, Face to face complaint, call center.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

AWDA set several mechanisms to receive feedback & complaints while ensuring that they can be accessible to all population groups, including PWD (whether hearing, visual or physical disabilities) as the means of submitting the complaint varied in terms of written complaints directly to the complaint box or voice complaints through the use of the Toll-free No. (which is a free No. specified for complaints), this in addition to electronic complaints via a generic e-mail and WhatsApp, besides receiving complaints face to face or by filling out questionnaires. AWDA developed a Feedback, Complain and Response Mechanism (FCRM) which is active and includes the following classifications: • Sensitive complaint. • Complaint & dissatisfaction. • Inquire about existing services. • Request for individual support & assistance. • Thanks & appreciation. Where the call center employee, through the electronic form, fills in all the details of the complaint and forwarded it to the MEAL manager, except for sensitive complaints that are forwarded to the General Manager directly. As for the complaints on the General Manager himself, they are forwarded to the Chairman. In order to response to gender needs, there are call center workers of both sexes. All the above-mentioned complaints mechanisms are announced to our beneficiaries through social media and radio advertisements. It is important to mention that simulation on submitting feedback & complaints will be carried out with the children in order to encourage them to submit feedback & complaints. Where all types of complaints are received, including those related to SEA using AWDA complaint channels. While the target group will be informed about the mechanisms for submitting SEA complaints, including calling the number 121, which is managed by the SAWA organization.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project idea, services and implementation mechanisms built on respecting the principles of protection stipulated in the humanitarian charter including the right to live with dignity, the right to receive assistance and the right to protection. Through this project various procedures will be taken to ensure the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries, and the principles of Do No Harm will be considered as follows:• In order to prevent SEA during the provision of the project services; 14 of AWDA staff (4 M, 10 F) will attend PSEA training. As well as the contracts of the project staff will include standard clause on PSEA in accordance with IASC principles and guidance. • In order not to expose the beneficiaries to more harm, they will be informed about PSEA and AWDA complaints system, and children will be introduced to this system through simulation sessions.• The project services will be provided without any discrimination.• The project services will be provided through 2 community centers in Rafah and Gaza, where the geographical locations of these facilities are distinguished by their presence in populated places, and the windows of these centers are protected that ensuring safety for the children.• Cases consultations and documentation are private and confidential. • Children’s caregivers will be provided with all the necessary information about the intervention plan. • Gender needs will be considered by having female staff among the work teams to meet the gender needs of girls. • Through the project, IASC guidance on mental health and psychosocial support and minimum child protection standards will be integrated through the implementation of AWDA safeguarding and PSEA policies (which the project staff will be trained on).

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AWDA adopts the principle of equality and impartiality in providing its services to beneficiaries, especially marginalized groups who need humanitarian interventions to improve their protection and humanitarian situation. AWDA provides its services without any discrimination based on age, sex, religion, race, or political affiliation. Knowing that through this project and to achieve the principle of impartiality in providing services, AWDA will provide services to the targeted children on the basis that each case has his/her own individual characteristics and special needs, especially PWDs as the most vulnerable in society and who need special care and protection while maintaining dignity, confidentiality and nondiscrimination will be guaranteed. The project services will be easily accessible to the target groups, as they will be provided through 2 community centers affiliated to AWDA in Rafah and Gaza governorates that are well known to the beneficiaries. It is worth noting that the project staff and other CP specialized staff from AWDA will participate in training about protection disability inclusion to ensure that the services are provided without compromising the beneficiaries’ dignity.
