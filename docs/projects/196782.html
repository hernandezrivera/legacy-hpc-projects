Project Summary

The proposed project aims at improving food security of conflict-affected communities in Northeast and Northwest Syria. This will be achieved through a combination of different complementary interventions at the individual household and community-level. At the household level, emergency responses assistance will be delivered through distribution of Ready-to-Eat Rations, cash and voucher assistance. In addition, ACTED will enhance food production at HH level, through kitchen and home garden kit, enhancing individuals' food security. ACTED will also target farmers and livestock holders with longer-term support, delivering agricultural inputs to redeem seeds, fertilizers, polytunnels and tools, and training on climate-smart agriculture and best practices. In addition, ACTED will rehabilitate communal productive assets such as mills, bakeries, etc and provide flour assistance to one public bakery in Al-Hasakeh governorate, serving the overall targeted community.
All project activities will be designed in a way that enables resilience to be built at a local level and that builds the capacity of men, women, boys, and girls to access basic items and meet basic needs themselves. Specifically, at the household and community levels, ACTED will support men and women in building their own capacities to meet their basic needs so that they are more resilient and better able to access commodities and services in the future. This project is estimated to benefit 127,788 individuals overall.

3 - Needs

Across Syria, 15 million persons are expected to be in need of FSA assistance in 2023, with over 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 2.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity as per the 2023 HNO. The acute food insecure figure includes 2 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure. FSA sector foresees an increase in the number of food-insecure households in 2023. In Northeast Syria, the traditional breadbasket of the country is experiencing a severe water crisis affecting the ability of the population residing in the area to meet their basic needs. This is compounded by limited energy supply and population movements. This crisis is also impacting the food value chains of the country. The most affected crops as highlighted by the farmers are wheat and barley. Households across the country face a gap between incomes and expenditures on essential needs, and this has worsened in 2022: at the end of the third quarter of 2022, the national average price of a WFP standard reference food basket reached a new record at SYP 329,526, up 40 percent since the onset of the crisis in Ukraine and fourfold since two years. Affected populations cope with the purchasing of lower quality food, increasingly borrowing money to afford food, cutting down on non-food expenses, and skipping meals. Lack of access to agricultural tools and to quality seeds further compounded the situation, resulting in reduced agricultural production.

4 - Implementation of activities

At the household level, emergency responses assistance will be delivered through distribution of Ready-to-Eat Rations, cash and voucher assistance. Additionally, ACTED will roll out monthly food assistance over eight months or more, mainly through in-kind food baskets or voucher modalities, depending on market accessibility in each targeted area. ACTED will also enhance long-term food security and resilience to future shocks at the household level through the distribution of home garden and kitchen garden kits, allowing targeted households to produce food at a small scale for their own consumption, thereby reducing their dependency on humanitarian assistance and unstable market accessibility and affordability. ACTED will also support farmers and livestock holders with vocational training, as well as grants for seeds, fodder, and tools. At the community level, ACTED will rehabilitate communal productive assets such as mills, bakeries, etc and provide flour assistance to one public bakery in Al-Hasakeh governorate, serving the overall targeted community.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementation Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitization, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: all ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to PSEA and GBV. As part of ongoing efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as that of the contractors, and to relay any issue, including SEA or GBV.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Information on the CRM will be communicated to community leaders and as much as possible directly to beneficiaries. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. The presence of women staff, in the programme and monitoring teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff, programme and monitors, are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Conflict escalation: movements are suspended in specific areas; relocation of staff to other offices to continue activities • Community acceptance: monitored daily by liaison assistants, regular meetings with local community leaders and Key Informant network in order to prevent any tensions; regular dialogue ensured with all relevant authorities, with support from various coordination mechanisms at area level. • Road closures: ACTED diversified its network of suppliers through tender processes and reinforced local suppliers’ capacities to ensure that items are procured regularly and prepositioned • Procurement related risks: 2-week local advertisement in multiple locations and ACTED’s transparency@acted.org email, analysis of all offers led by regional office. Evaluation of technical and financial offers conducted separately between field team and Regional Capital. Preparation of bid analysis, and full documentation is then done by Capital. • Recruitment related risks: process entails 2- week advertisement. Longlist prepared by HR office in the area. Selection process is completed after interview records are received and reference check completed by HR office in the area and cohesion dynamics: • CRM inside Syria: stakeholders can safely provide feedback and report any project issues or concerns.
