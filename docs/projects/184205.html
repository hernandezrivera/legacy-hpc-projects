Project Summary

In general, farmers have benefitted from improved access to water for irrigation. Worryingly however, the damage to road infrastructure and market access, have adversely affected terms of trade with higher prices of maize, and significantly lowering the prices for goats and daily labour wage rates by up to 30 and 102 per cent below the five-year average.
The current high levels of food insecurity in the country and more within Hirshabelle region, has been driven by drought conditions from late 2018 through mid-May 2019, exacerbated by below-average and erratically distributed rainfall during the 2019 Gu’ season (April-June), which began late and finished early. (FSNAU sept 2019) estimated 4.8 million people across Somalia are expected to face a humanitarian crisis, of these 2.6 are internally displaced people in various part of the country.
Previously, RRP carried out interventions in Hiraan region in partnership with FAO that offered rounds of cash plus agricultural inputs to 2120 farmers both agro-pastoral and the riverine settlements. This was accompanied with trainings done to champion the nutrition sector, and lead farmers to ensure sustainability of the same. This was similar to another project that was almost getting to an end in Beletweyne targeting 1700 beneficiaries with the same kind of intervention. Cash plus agricultural input before the start of the rains in every season has so far yielded fruits.
Therefore, in this proposed plan, beneficiaries will be benefiting from such interventions that are a continuity of what we previously implemented. Enhancing the capacity of the rain-fed and riverine farmers to rebuild their productive assets and reconstruct their capacity to absorb the recurrent climatic shocks. The project provides farm inputs, animal-drawn tools, water pumps, and extension services to the affected farmers. It also provides training on soil fertility amendments and good farming practices for ensuring sustainable food production in agro-pastoral and riverine livelihood zones. Through biometric registration, the identified HH headed with 30% prioritization of women will be done. The registered will within 3 cycles be provision of unconditional cash transfer to 1500 HHs with malnourished children as well as people living with disability. Provision of farming inputs to vulnerable riverine and agro-pastoralist farmers to 1280 HHs considering women headed HHs. RRP will also establish nutrition champions in the program who will be trained as well as lead farmers who will assist even after the stop of the response in guiding the community.
A call center will be in place to ensure that there is a good flow of feedback from the beneficiaries as well as the community.
RRP has on regular basis worked with the CWG to ensure that regular assessments are done to know how viable is the cash based intervention can be, with the intervention from FAO the livelihood situation is currently still necessary to have cash distribution to ensure that there’s no further deterioration in the previous state.
Coordination with other actors will be done to ensure that no duplication of interventions beneficiaries. Monthly, mid-year and end year reports will be done to the respective donors as well as the cluster.

1. - Projects activities

Through provision of both conditional and unconditional cash transfers, selected through inclusive community-based targeting RRP will target riverine and Agro Pastoral settlements of Hirshabelle region. RRP will give seeds and farm inputs like sorghum, maize, cowpeas and vegetable and farm implements like hoe and forks to both Agro Pastoral and riverine communities in target of the rainy seasons. CFW activities will be identified in consultation with participants to ensure nature of work is selected in consideration to specific gender needs. Local authorities will be consulted as well as the relevant partners and clusters. targeting 2500 (1450 men and 1050 women) RRP intends to target populations in IPC3 and 4 especially the newly displaced and worst affected Agro Pastoralist and riverine communities. Payments will be done through mobile cash transfers, equivalent to 70 USD for 3 months of the food component of the Minimum Expenditure Basket. 1200 (650 men and 550 women) Provision of livelihood specific and seasonally appropriate inputs for vulnerable households: Emergency response will be supported through the provision of livelihood specific inputs to both rural and urban populations. For rural beneficiaries, activities will include formation of Agro Pastoral Field Schools, fodder provision for Agro Pastoral communities, livestock restocking and support to community animal health workers this will target people living with the disability, women, and elderly within the society . 1200 (650 men and 550 women) For sustainability of the interventions, RRP will train nutrition champions as well as lead farmers who will be able to offer continuous support to the community all year round. targeting 100 (45 women and 55 men) Restoration of community productive assets and disaster preparedness: This will be done through rehabilitation of priority community assets which will be identified by the community through an inclusive process of consultations with Village Committees, the local community and authorities. - targeting 2500 (1450 men and 1050 women) Rehabilitation of assets will be done via the CFW modality to ensure that beneficiaries earn an income from rehabilitating these assets.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash transfers are inherently unrestricted, meaning each transfer can be spent according to recipient’s priorities; and potentially address multiple needs. Cash assistance will offer people in need choice and support individual empowerment, while increasing efficiencies, supporting local actors, and stimulating local economies. CVA is often the most effective way to help people get back on their feet and on their way to recovery after devastation occasioned by drought such as the current one. Using cash based programming will provide RRP with the the ability to spend less per person in need on overhead costs, thus increasing the amount of people that can be helped by removing a number of supply chain costs and inefficiencies, and reducing staff head counts given the ease to which cash can be disbursed.The cash distributions will be aimed towards funding immediate food and non-food items such as rent, fuel, household goods, WASH costs, and transportation.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Given the lack of fully functional financial services cash is required to ensure convenience for the individuals. At the start of the project, we will undertake a market analysis to determine the availability and price of food and NFIs before any intervention. This market analysis may help in deciding if in CBI or in-kind will be sought. RRP also recognizes that, market functionality may also be affected by extreme disasters events like drought or floods but also by insecurity. All these factors will be considered during intervention.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

RRP through proper community engagement, developed agreed-selection criteria for selecting beneficiaries in our previous FAO projects in Hirshabelle, which was endorsed by the community elders due to its inclusive. We will ensure women and girls and at larger scale the entire community are made aware that humanitarian assistance is free and no conditions are attached to it. Beneficiaries have a one channel to report concerns through Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM). RRP shall establish accessible, confidential and well-understood beneficiary feedback mechanism in consultation with different groups. RRP will ensure appropriate equity and quality measures are put in place by ensuring services provided benefit to all community members. During consultation, women, boys and girls will collect accurate information about their specific food and nutritional requirements and preferences for food distribution points and livelihood services. RRP will ensure good working relationship with all stakeholders through genuine participation in the project areas.1. The organisation shall establish confidential complaints mechanisms to receive/investigate allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) experienced by women, girls, boys or men in seeking/receiving assistance through food security programmes. 2. The organisation shall ensure that identified locations for the sites are not too far from the communities we shall be serving and also services shall be offered at a convenient time and close early enough to give caregivers and beneficiaries sufficient time to reach their various homes without exposing them to any danger along the way. 3.Establish confidential complaints mechanisms to receive/investigate allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) experienced by women, girls, boys or men in seeking/receiving assistance through nutrition programs. 4.Consult women, girls, boys and men at all steps in assessment, design, implementation and monitoring of nutrition projects, programs, policies and strategies.
