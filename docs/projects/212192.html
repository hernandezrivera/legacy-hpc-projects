Project Summary

Needs:
The Syrian crisis, now in its twelfth year, remains highly complex and significant, with ongoing hostilities and community erosion worsening conditions. Millions have been displaced, infrastructure damaged, and basic services like electricity and healthcare disrupted. Particularly affected regions include the communities of Koniko and Muezelah in Deir-ez-Zor governorate that are facing severe hardships such as lack of teachers and infrastructure damage. According to the 2024 HNO, there are more than 2.45 million out of school children, and protection risks and worsening economic situations are putting even more children at risk of dropping out of school, especially in areas such as Deir-ez-Zor where education is not a priority. Humanitarian organizations like Action for Humanity offer vital support, focusing on education and psychological services.
Urgent needs persist, especially in education and child protection, with demand increasing annually. Efforts to improve education support and rebuild infrastructure are crucial to alleviate suffering and empower communities for a better future. According to the December 2023 HNO report, there has been an increase in the number of people in need in the education and protection sectors in Syria, especially in the Deir-ez-Zor governorate.
Intervention and Implementation of activities:
I. Education
AFH will facilitate the provision of non-formal education to vulnerable children through three schools in Deir-ez-Zor. A target of 1200 vulnerable children will be provided with non-formal education, 800 with UNICEF’s Accelerated Learning program (ALP) and 400 with UNICEF’s Self Learning program (SLP). To increase sustainability of the intervention, AFH will build the capacity of 20 project staff as well as 45 teachers and education staff on education strategies and INEE. AFH will also conduct 3 campaigns to mobilize children to access learning opportunities. Additionally, AFH will establish a new school made up of 10 tents and 3 caravans to increase the capacity of education facilities in the area in addition to conducting light maintenance work for the other 2 schools.
II. Youth capacity building and Community initiative
Action for Humanity supports the youth community and implements activities to build their capacity. 50 youth will be provided with afternoon IT literacy courses in the schools of Ezba and Moezleh while another 50 will be provided with a training course on sewing. These courses will support 100 youth in mastering a skill and therein increasing their livelihood opportunities. 50 additional youth will be targeted through the Win-Win initiative and provided with structured sessions.
III. Child Protection
AFH will provide 91 local staff with trainings on PSEA - CSG to build their capacity on child protection and increase the awareness of the local community on such matters. Action for Humanity’s protection team will develop and conduct awareness raising activities for 8000 children and caregivers. In support of the protection needs of the children, protection staff will identify and provide 300 children with case management services, referring the cases in need of further support to other actors, and providing 150 of the cases with in-kind support for protection.
IV. PSS Component
Action for Humanity will support the provision of PSS activities to the school students. 12 PSS facilitators will be provided with capacity building training on the PSS activities. The project staff will establish 3 child-safe spaces, one in each of the target schools. All 1200 vulnerable children targeted in the education activities will also be targeted within the protection activities and will be provided with structured and semi-structured protection sessions. AFH ensures that all family members are targeted for a holistic approach by providing 100 caregivers and parents with Be-There sessions and 800 caregivers and parents with ECP activities.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Monitoring:AFH MEAL team will conduct a baseline, end line, and quality of participation evaluation with the targeted students in the selected locations/schools. The baseline will be followed-up with light monitoring surveys based on the project needs. AFH staff will conduct regular field visits and exit interviews. AFH MEAL team will track all project documents in the data base platform. Regular meetings will be conducted with partners and other stakeholders on a quarterly basis. AFH MEAL team will also conduct a follow up evaluation with the students after the end of each cycle. Risks:Learning facilities face potential threats from conflict parties or communities. To address this risk, local committees comprising of community members are formed to protect school property. AFH also ensures to appoint guards from the same area. Sexual harassment incidents involving boys and young girls in latrines or unsafe areas necessitate the rehabilitation of sanitation facilities by ensuring privacy and gender segregation. To address the mismatch between services and the needs of people with disabilities, AFH commits to making all facilities, including classrooms and sanitation facilities, accessible for persons with disabilities and our services will be disability inclusive. Learning facilities, or their surroundings, may be structurally unsafe or lack adequate protection from the elements, impacting attendance. AFH proposes measures such as providing cold water and ventilation in the summer, ensuring safe heating in winter, and securing potable water while maintaining the cleanliness of the sanitary facilities. Action for Humanity (AFH) is taking proactive measures to address and prevent sexual exploitation and abuse within its humanitarian operations. This includes comprehensive training for all staff on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and adherence to a strict Code of Conduct. AFH has established clear policies on this issue, and employees are required to report any violations, which may result in termination. AFH aims to reach out to targeted children and those out of school, involving meetings with parents and community leaders to introduce the project and encourage participation. Security concerns are addressed with an updated Matrix of Threats, Risks, and Mitigation Measures, including a contingency plan for minor threats such as temporary program suspension. An emergency plan for potential security issues involves delivering distance education through WhatsApp groups. Ongoing monitoring and analysis of security risks, along with consideration of environmental and political developments, inform the development of security and contingency plans in Syria. In the event of a significant security threat, such as the potential entry of the Government of Syria (GoS) into the area, measures include returning schools to local authorities, closing AFH offices, and facilitating staff evacuation if necessary.
