Project Summary

Darna Al-Aman project aims to support safe return of Raqqa’s civilian population through protection and health integrated services and the set-up GBV and CP response.
The protection component includes the set-up and launch of a Women and Girls Safe Space and a child-friendly space in the same location adjacent to the Raqqah National Hospital, acting also as entry point for women, girls and children allowing better access to services including integrated case management.
Linking the reproductive health to the space will indeed ease the access to the services and the spaces themselves. Services targeting women and girls will include informative sessions, psychosocial support, and will benefit from the multi-sector services addressing GBV or from the referral to other existing services. Women and girls’ circles between mothers and young girls will be organized to tackle topics like mental health, RH, GBV and Women Empowerment. Moreover, parenting skills sessions and sessions for young mothers during breastfeeding will be provided, also through the support of the medical staff. Adolescent boys’ activities will be conducted in the nearby implementing partner’s Community Center.
A key element of the action will be the integrated training plan for protection, medical and nonmedical staff. To ensure high standard services, all the staff will be trained on PSEA, Safeguarding, CoC, CP, WPE, GBV basic principles & Core Concepts, safe identification and referral of cases, etc. The health staff will also be trained on CMR to improve the management of these cases within 72 hours.
The project tries to address as much as possible gender and age markers. As a matter of fact, the intervention is based on a thorough needs analysis. The widespread GBV, SEA, child labor, trauma and the stigma resulting from all this kind of issues is taken into account into the project design. Given the absence of services and the limited participation of the community, the project intends to tackle some barriers hindering GBV prevention and response.
Moreover, the Action seeks to address the specific needs of women and men, boys and girls, including PWDs and FHHs and older persons in a view of ensuring inclusion and tailoring the activities according to the specific target. Data collected is of course disaggregated by age and gender (SAAD).
Case Management will also be provided, and the satisfaction of the beneficiaries will be measured thanks to anonymous surveys, internal and external referrals database, feedback and complaint boxes.
The expected result is to reach up to 6,000 beneficiaries – 4,000 women and girls and 2,000 children and parents - with integrated protection services, including awareness sessions, informative sessions, PSS and referrals. At least 100 case management services are expected. As per the capacity building, the target is 60 staff members including both medical and non-medical staff.
The intervention will foster synergies among medical staff and protection staff, and a closed cooperation between Health, CP and GBV Working Groups and CM Task Force.

4 - Needs

According to a report from REACH, issued in May 2020, child labour is one of the major Protection concerns in Raqqah. The same report states that GBV and especially early forced marriage, intimate partner violence-IPV, rape, honor crime, and other kinds of violence are a widespread risk (39% for host communities and 49% for IDPs). This situation occurs both in the private life and in the public one, hence women and girls lack a space where to feel free and safe. As a consequence, a safe space and PSS services and GBV life-saving services are one of the main gaps in Raqqah. Moreover, a gap was identified in terms of access to medical treatment for GBV survivors as well as the need for female medical staff, trained on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR). In addition, CMR services are not always available within 72-120 hours after surviving an act of violence. Syria HNO 2020 also shed light on the high number of children seeking assistance. Indeed, 236.402 face harsh conditions such as violence, alcohol and drugs abuse, school dropout. Their mental health is therefore undermined. Concerning the children situation, the lack of a child-friendly spaces and scarcity of case management services for children who have survived exploitation and abuse were identified as urgent needs. Special attention is given to unaccompanied minors and children subject to child labor. Finally, the lack of services provided to children also shows the urgent need for awareness raising on children rights and child Protection principles.

5 - Implementation of activities

For the WGSS, the implementation will be constantly monitored and evaluated through satisfaction surveys, FGDs with WGBM, KAP, safety audit, KII with key community stakeholders, safety & community mapping, interviews and feedback boxes that will be tailored to gender and age specific needs. The Prevention officers will be in charge of the outreach of the most vulnerable beneficiaries. The Protection Mainstreaming action plan will prioritize individuals who are the most at risk (women who are householders, pregnant or breastfeeding, GBV survivors, etc.). PSS activities, recreational sessions and informative sessions will be organized for women and girls. Through the staff working in the hospital, they will cooperate to identify and refer the most vulnerable cases to internal or external specialized services. The intervention intends filling the gaps within the lack of services provided and the activities tailored to children. Thus, activities tailored for age groups between 4-6 and 7-12, will include: FGDs with children, parents, Community based and child friendly assessment; Public information events; PSS activities for children; Child-friendly activities, with awareness raising campaigns on health, protection, and rights; Activities and focus groups with parents; Service mapping; Referrals for medical cases, mental health, and protection cases to medical staff, case workers/PSS counsellors/psychologists and other actors; Survey and child-friendly complaints and feedback system. UPP will guarantee regular field visits and online mentoring and monitoring to guarantee quality CM services and coordination with CP, GBV, health and other relevant WGs. PSEA Network tools will also be used to ensure integrity and accountability

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

It is very likely that the population is not aware of the PSEA policy, and of the mechanisms in place to report an allegation. As a mitigation measure, the PSEA policy is shared with the local partners and all the staff members have to abide by it. Moreover, regular trainings on PSEA are foreseen as part of the capacity building plan for health and non health staff both in the WGSS and the CFS and the Health Facility as well. Awareness sessions on PSEA are planned thoughout the year to inform the community about the policy and how to report cases of SEA. Finally, a complaint box for FCRM including PSEA is available for the population to be able to report if needed.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security issues in Raqqah are still a main concern. Indeed, though the situation appears stable, security alarms continue in Raqqah city. For instance, tension might be heightened by terrorist groups or Daesh sleeper cells.For operations in both Syria and Iraq, UPP refers to the internally adapted the manual "Security Risk Management NGO Approach". Moreover, UPP staff regularly benefit from training courses on Security in crisis contexts. Risk assessments take place on a regular basis and a thorough monitoring phase of the activities and the sites occurs constantly to prevent or deal with security issues. Concerning the pandemic, a high risk exists that the number of people infected with COVID-19 will continue to increase. This could slightly slow down the implementation of the activities and undermine the full functioning of the WGSS and the CFS. However, preventive measures as social distancing, limited number of participants and PPEs will always be in place to prevent the spread of the virus.As per the implementation of the activities, socio-cultural barriers exist in Raqqah area concerning the Protection sector and especially the GBV component. As a mitigation measure, activities will be designed and implemented as much as possible with the involvement and the participation of women and girls, who will themselves express their needs, the gaps in the society. KII, FGDs, surveys and assessments will take place in Raqqah area to identify the main gaps and provide an adequate response as much as possible tailored to the community. A full capacity building plan is in place for all the staff to tackle humanitarian principles and facilitators and prevention officers will be trained on effective outreach strategies.
