Project Summary

According to the Somalia health sector Strategy (2018-2021), the health and wellbeing of the Somali people remains in a critical situation with some of the worst health indicators in the world and the country is ‘off-track’ to reaching health related SDGs. Under-5 mortality is very high (137 per 1,000 live births), Coverage of routine immunization is low in all the districts of Lower Shabelle. In six targeted districts of the project coverage of Penta-III is 54% and measles vaccination coverage is 69%. Due to the ongoing conflicts and Najural disasters in the 4 districts in Lower Shabelle region AYUUB is seeking and proposing with the interventions to Provide and Strength equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (06-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases by targeting 24800 (9,500 Boys, 9,700 Girls and 5,600 PLW) drought affected direct beneficiaries in Merka, Kurtunwarey , wenlaweyn and Qoryoley districts.
Therefore The project is responding through the provision of Regular identification of acutely malnourished children U5 and PLWs including through Mothers MUAC approach in 4 Mobile and 11 OTP's in Merka, Kurtunwarey, Wenlaweyn and Qoryoley districts, Micronutrient support for vulnerable groups (PLW and U5 children) e.g Vitamin A & MMN in 4 Mobile, Therapeutic feeding support for treatment of acute malnutrition cases for PLW and U5 children ,hence the complicated cases of severely malnourished under five children will be transfered to Merka and Qoryoley Hospital, Infant and young child feeding counselling and IYCF promotion will be the targeted activities also in target project sites to 5600 PLW’s as well as conduct caregivers infant and young child feeding practices and capacity building of 24 men and 25 female nutrition staff on IMAM/IYCF services, infant’s young child feeding best practices (IYCF), Nutrition, health and hygiene promotion (NHHP)
Referral of malnourished children with complicated cases will be done to the Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) centers integrated with primary health care services managed by AYUUB and other actors in the L/Shabelle region.
AYUUB intends to provide Mothers MUAC led sessions tat the static outpatient therapeutic centers to ensures mothers gain knowledge and skills in undertaking MUAC measurement at their own, this will ensure better communty caregivers knowledge as well as provide localized way of doing things differently. However, AYUUB will coordinate with MOH (Minstery of Health) -SWS (South West State) as well as Nutrition clusters to coordinate response activities for maximum impact to beneficiaries. AYUUB shall also coordinates with other local actors in the target district to enable avoid any duplication of services as well work in a coordinated manner to enable increase access to life-saving emergency nutrition response to the drought affected population in the areas.

Mitigation Measures

1b. Protection of civilians: The area where the project implementation will be either forced into temporary displacement because of Clan conflict, Since all the Health interventions will be channeled through cluster approach and involvement of relevant local authorities, thus the impact of armed conflict and violence will be mitigated through proper coordination, advocacy at national and international level, media and referral mechanisms. 2a. Exclusion and marginalization: The Federal Government of Somalia has committed to women’s empowerment and eradicating violence against women, recognizing that without institutionalizing the equal rights of women, the country will never be able to stabilize and develop in a sustainable way. Somali women and girls continue to bear an unequal burden of the hardships occasioned by poverty, conflict and clan-based culture, which promotes strict male hierarchy and authority. This is further exacerbated by cultural limitations on the role and status of women in Somali society. Women with disabilities are often marginalized on account of their disability and their gender. They may be more vulnerable to poverty and social exclusion, and often have limited social, political and economic opportunities and lack of access to basic services. Women with disabilities may also be at greater risk of sexual and physical violence and abuse. Girls with disabilities often experience discrimination, for example in education and family life. This project will ensure engagement of people, especially women with disabilities, and the elderly at the co-management level and will ensure that programs and interventions do not generate negative side-effects. The beneficiary selection will be conducted in a participatory approach with the community leaders and the local authorities that will identify the most vulnerable people. Women and Child headed households will be given special priority. 2b. Exclusion and marginalization: Targeted assistance will be provided to the most vulnerable groups including women, girls and boy. Beneficiaries will be selected through a community-based approach, which will identify the most vulnerable based on certain criteria as female or child-headed households, elderly, disabled, large families and the chronically ill. The beneficiary selection will be conducted in a participatory approach with the community leaders and the local authorities that will identify the most vulnerable people. Women, girls, boys and Child headed households will be given special priority. 4b Protection mainstreaming: Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during implementation, The project will include Women, girls and boys in consultations and decision-making processes for the project and during beneficiary identification and registration will be selected beneficiaries with Specific needs, Marginalized communities, Female headed house hold and families with orphans, and the people in need (can’t afford) but do not fall in the health categories will be prioritized by the setting up an other criteria and will be discussed with local Authority and community representatives. also during our health Messages/Sessions and community training we also ensure representation from all the above-mentioned groups

Please provide further explanation

The areas being targeted in Merka, Kurtunwarey, Wenlaweyn and Qoryooley districts, Lower Shabelle have been secured which gives an assurance that the activities will be implemented without any interruption. Insecurity in general is inherent in Lower Shabelle Region, in overcoming the chances which might lead to insecurity we have security access information from the local administrations and own security assessment out of outstanding presence in Lower Shabelle for many years. We have unrestricted access to any of the locations including where our projects are being targeted. AYUUB has it’s own policy of risk Management and will apply it for the risk Management, a risk mitigation plan is designed by AYUUB to eliminate or minimize the impact of the risk events. AYUUB is also developed risk checklists based on experience from past projects. These checklists can be helpful to the project manager and project team in identifying both specific risks on the checklist and expanding the thinking of the team. The past experience of the project team, project experience within the Organization, and experts in the Organization can be valuable resources for identifying potential risk on a project. After the potential risks have been identified, the project team then evaluates the risk based on the probability that the risk event will occur and the potential loss associated with the event. After the risk has been identified and evaluated, the project team develops a risk mitigation plan, which is a plan to reduce the impact of an unexpected event. The project team mitigates risks in the ways of Risk avoidance, risk sharing, risk reduction, risk transfer and each of these mitigation techniques can be an effective tool in reducing individual risks and the risk profile of the project Regarding risks related to movement to these IDP Camps in the two districts , AYUUB staff has so far mitigated the risk by discussing AYUUB staff purpose in advance with community leaders and putting into place a security protocol consisting in monitoring and evaluating the security situation before movement and check in calls to the Field Coordinator who is the person in charge of the security of the staff. Risk of fraud and corruption will be minimizing by the delegation of duties and sharing of responsibilities, as well as monitoring visits and quality control of activities implemented by the Head of program. AYUUB HR Manager will conduct a training on AYUUB Policy on Code of Conduct. Beneficiaries will be provided with AYUUB hotline number to refer complaints. A strong feedback mechanism will enhance the capacity of AYUUB Staff to prevent challenges human errors thus improving risk management in relation of the delivery of services. Review of the project achievements (including lessons learned and good practices) will be promoted periodically in order to identify risks and avoid and/or mitigate them in future actions
