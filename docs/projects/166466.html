Project Summary

In adapting to suspension of education activities in the camps, Save the Children plans to roll out caregiver-led education modalities in which students are given some tasks to continue within their homes and facilitators or teachers follow up with the parents/guardians about children’s education. In addition, awareness sessions for parents and caregivers will be conducted maintain social distances to sensitize on the importance of children’s education. SC also plans to develop e-learning courses for teachers and collaborate with local radio agencies for disseminating instructions to the learners. SC addresses that the current suspension of education activities may limit the risk of losing education gains, and as such, plans to advocate for inclusion of education as an essential service and the permission of data in the camps so that the ICT/remote learning activities can be introduced. SC ensures that the principles of protection, gender equality and inclusion and accountability to affected populations are central and cross-cutting in the proposed actions and implementation.
SC will reach more than 34,000 refugee children and adolescents, 42,200 parents and caregivers and 160 master trainers and 1,000 teachers with alternative modalities of teaching-learning actions – such as - e-contents, e-learning courses, psychosocial-support and Interactive Radio Instructions.

Needs

On March 24, 2020, the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) released a statement defining the essential and critical activities in all 24 camps in Cox’s Bazar. This severely restricted access for UN and NGO staff and resulted in the closure of learning facilities, as the statement defined education as a non-essential activity. The Education Sector closed nearly 6,000 education facilities across the camps for the foreseeable future, denying over 315,00 children (of which, 49% are girls) and adolescents aged 13 to 18 years, access to education. These students are missing vital learning opportunities and have had their social support systems further disrupted. Moreover, without access to learning facilities – and in isolation – there is little scope to provide clear communication messaging, psychosocial support to both students and educators, as well as meet their social-emotional learning or provide life skills education. Teachers, parents and caregivers lack the tools and strategies to home-school their children without structured guidance, while also ensuring children’s safety, security, and physical-mental wellbeing. Rohingya caregivers or parents, mostly with low educational attainment, do not have experience in distance schooling, and will struggle to support their children’s learning. Additionally, the mental and psychosocial wellbeing of children during this stressful time will be compromised. Girls will become more vulnerable to exclusion, more isolated without access to their usual peer networks or community-based learning opportunities, and more susceptible to gender-based violence. Children who experience learning and/or physical challenges will face additional difficulties through further lack of support, frustration at home, and the discontinuation of positive outlets. Furthermore, without access to internet in the camps, many technology-based solutions are not viable. Over, 8,500 teachers and education facilitators, both from the host and refugee communities, will be disconnected from their students and from regular continuous professional development activities, as Education Partners have suspended all training and coaching programmes. There are over 5,100 (42 percent female) refugee teachers/facilitators impacted by the crisis in the same way as other community members. There is a need to assess and address their challenges and needs holistically to ensure that they can care for themselves, their families and their students – with attention to the most vulnerable teachers, especially women. With learning facility closures, the over 3,300 (81 percent female) host-community teachers do not have access to the camps, and like their refugee teacher colleagues, they may be required to gain additional knowledge and skills to support distance learning. Yet, the crisis also disproportionately affects all opportunities for professional development and learning. Online platforms for customized professional development opportunities are nearly absent and the current teaching force lacks the necessary IT literacy, skills and attitudes (confidence, competence, agility) to continue self-learning to equip them with the new skills for remote teaching. Teachers may also require support to access technology (hardware), the internet and digital learning portals to support online learning. The Bangladeshi teachers are better placed than their refugee counterparts to access e-learning, but these online professional development opportunities are yet to be developed and tailored to their needs. At this stage, Education partners will continue to pay all teacher salaries but the on-going, disruption to non-formal learning may result in reduced earnings for teachers/facilitators in the future.

Activities or outputs

The following activities are planned to implement to adapt to the COVID-19 crisis:• Roll out the guide for home-led learning activities during the closure of temporary learning centers• Scale up of distributing learning materials• Organize small awareness sessions with caregivers/facilitators to promote home-led learning activities and hygiene messaging (maintaining social distancing)• Install handwashing facilities complemented with soap, hygiene promotion materials and training both now and for future when learning facilities may re-open• Continue advocacy for the need for internet access during this crisis (this is not certain and currently accessibility to digital resources are almost non-existent)• Develop e-learning courses for master trainers and teachers from host communities that may include – content gathering, set-up of an e-learning package and schedule, monitoring and tracking, etc. • Develop self-learning module for partner staff • Introduce interactive Radio instructions for the learners in the camps in collaboration with local radio agencies• Conduct a baseline survey/ situation analysis and end-line evaluation to determine project achievements • Roll out Accountability Feedback Response and Reporting Mechanisms Principles of protection, gender equality and inclusion and accountability to affected populations are central and cross-cutting in all SC’s thematic interventions. Moreover, SC has a strong monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning (MEAL) system in place. The MEAL approach ensures systematic improvement of the project through sharing, reflection and learning what works and what did not throughout the project cycle. The MEAL approach will cover five components - i) Monitoring Indicator Performance Tracking Table (IPTT), ii) quality benchmarks, iii) evaluations iv) accountability, and iv) learning.
