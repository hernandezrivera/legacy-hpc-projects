Project Summary

HI will continue its 12+ years of broad support for refugee and host communities by addressing the immediate acute and chronic health needs of refugees and host communities through multi-sectoral mobile units, center-based services and a strong referral pathways.
The proposed intervention will be delivered through a twin-track approach:
- Multi-sectorial mobile units composed of qualified Nurses and Midwives, Physiotherapists, PSS/ Protection Officers, and social workers will be deployed to identify and provide tailored and individual assistance to the most vulnerable, especially the persons with disabilities and their caregivers who might be excluded from services due to mobility or social barriers. Integrated outreach teams (PSS + Physiotherapist) deliver home-based services focused on education for beneficiaries and their caregivers on safe, effective exercises and interventions.
- At HI service centers, teams support beneficiaries with enough mobility to access the facility with rehabilitation, PSS, basic healthcare, through nurses and protection. The Rehabilitation team provides assistive devices and other health items when necessary to people (adults/children) with physical and functional limitations in order to improve their mobility, enhance recovery, and prevent complications. Training is provided to persons and caregivers on basic home-based rehabilitation and proper use, maintenance and repair of mobility devices.
- In home-based and center-based services, Nurses will support beneficiaries with: wound care, bedsores care, ante and post-natal care. A key focus will be to provide maternal care for women who cannot access health facilities (i.e. women and girls with disabilities), including the prevention and detection of pregnancy-related risks and complications. Nurses refer to the nearest Health Facility and follow up to ensure they received needed specialized services.
- Support to government hospitals and health facilities both in the host and refugee communities. Physiotherapists will provide direct comprehensive Rehabilitation services within the Health Facilities and will train and mentor health workers.
- PSEA principles are mainstreamed through all activities through extensive training of all staff and volunteers using PSEA Network materials and recommendations. Feedback is regularly elicited through comment boxes, a dedicated hotline for host and refugee communities, community discussions and 1:1 interviews. A dedicated Focal Point to receive complaints is installed in the Cox base to coordinate with camp level team leaders to ensure referral for needed services and ensure survivor advocacy as needed.
HI is currently implementing four projects that addresses above mentioned health services funding under different donors. To provide the service for the host community people, HI support 5 hospitals It includes Cox's Bazar Sadar hospital, Ukhiya Upazila health complex, Teknaf Upazila health complex, MSF hospital and IOM hospital with rehab staff (physiotherapist) provide services in those facilities. Both host community people and beneficiaries from the camps can receive services from these facilities.
-HI welcomes complaints and feedback from the beneficiaries to integrate beneficiaries views in the project activities. HI MEAL team leads the complaints, feedback and response mechanism. Currently different complaints and feedback collection modalities exists in both host communities and camps. Modalities are face to face interviews, group discussions, hotline and feedback box. Based on the sensitivities of the complaints HI provide solution as soon as possible.

1 - Identified Needs

Three years later after the largest influx of Rohingya community from Myanmar in Bangladesh, the Rohingya refugee community continued to live in extremely precarious conditions in 34 extension and makeshift camps spread between Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas in Cox’s Bazar district. As of UNHCR Factsheet October 2020, 862,277 individuals were recorded, among which 52% are children, 45% are adults and 3% elderly. Women and girls represent 52% of the Refugee population. The lack of recognized legal status of Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh placed them on a precarious legal footing, denying rights attached to the status of refugee and fulfillment of their basic needs through income generating activities. As a result, Rohingya refugees are considered the most vulnerable group in Bangladesh remaining highly dependent on humanitarian assistance. Particularly exposed to risks of human rights violations and recourse to negative coping mechanisms such as early marriage, survival sex, and child labor, it is well documented that Rohingya women face particular protection concerns and barriers accessing services that can partly be attributed to social cultural norms that prevent women from engaging more fully in the public sphere (J-MSNA, July-August 2020). Limited or no access to quality sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services during emergency and crisis conditions puts women and adolescent girls at higher risk of morbidity and mortality that requires utmost importance in terms of service design, delivery and implementation. Moreover, it is recognized that the refugee population is presenting symptoms of mental and psychosocial distress and functional impairment resulting from the persecutions, violence and forced displacements that the Rohingya population faced in Myanmar but also exacerbated by their dire living conditions in the camps triggering secondary stressors stemming from their inability to meet their own basic needs including existing burden of unemployment. In addition, older persons and persons with disabilities face increased risk to violence, abuse and neglect, which are often exacerbated due to attitudinal (stigma and discrimination), communication and environmental barriers. In this regard, negative perceptions about persons with disabilities’ and their perceived reduced capacities to report violence and defend themselves expose them to further protection risks and violence. Additionally, persons with disabilities and older persons face significant physical barriers (e.g. lack of accessibility) in accessing mainstream services, including specialized services. In turn, a lack of available specialized services (i.e. rehabilitation services of Physical and Functional Therapy to prevent and reduce the functional limitations and/or the development of secondary complications, access to assistive devices, access to medication, treatment of chronic diseases, etc) puts them at greater risk of exclusion. In addition, the influx of humanitarian support has concentrated essential services like health and protection services in camp areas and the ongoing fencing of the camp creates additional barriers for services to reach the host community with 15% of host community respondents having to walk an hour or more to receive health services. In an assessment 56% of host community member surveyed said that assistance they received was not frequent enough, only 8% were visited by a community health worker in the last 14 days and 43% said not enough relief was provided. Resentment and frustration at the lack of support for the extremely poor area coupled with property disputes and fears of COVID 19 spread have created a conflicted environment. In regards to the aforementioned needs, HI has begun to expand services into host communities in the camp 24 and 26 areas to provide dynamic and multi-sector services at a household and center basis to the most vulnerable households there.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: (Objective 1) Provision of physical and functional rehabilitation sessions including provision of assistive devices and specific items at health facilities/hospital to persons (including women, men boys and girls) with injuries and/or with disabilities. Activity 2: (Objective 1) Provision of technical support to the relevant health facilities (3 government hospitals and 2 partners' clinics) through training for health workers on basic early physical rehabilitation care to improve referrals, understanding of rehabilitation care within the health facilities and ensure comprehensive and optimum care for the patients/beneficiaries. Activity 3: (Objective 1 and 3) Provision of physical and functional rehabilitation sessions including assistive devices through centre and home-based care to persons (including women, men, boys and girls) with injuries and/or with disabilities. Activity 4: (Objective 1) Capacity building of the caregivers of persons with disabilities and/or injuries on basic home based-care to ensure the understanding of the importance of rehabilitation and optimum care. Activity 5: (Objective 2) Provision of individual level psychosocial support to overcome stress related symptoms that effect individuals with long-term chronic disabling conditions. Activity 6: (Objective 1) Provision of community-based PSS group sessions with focus on to self identification of psychological distress and symptoms and to support the healing process of individuals obtaining specialized PSS services (psychological/psychiatric support). Activity 7: (Objective 1) Internal and external referrals to basic and specific specialized services. Activity 8: (Objective 1) Provision of SRHR (ANC, PNC, awareness) through individual, family and group sessions to persons (including women, men, boys and girls) facing mobility issues in the camps.
