Project Summary

The project objective is to provide early recovery and flood risk mitigation support to flood-affected local communities in Atma camps for ~15,000 IDPs (9000 inside camps, 6000 outside camps). The rehabilitation will target damaged infrastructures and therefore also improve the population's access to basic services.
as well as, it proposes activities for improving the exit roads and improving the drainage to minimize the mud and sewage, and facilitate the population movement under flood and rainstorms.
This project aims to address the Early Recovery sector objectives, Imaar will provide critically needed upgrades/rehabilitation/construction for "access way" and flood-affected infrastructure as follows:
- Rehabilitation of 10 km of the main roads outside the camp (new asphalt layer) with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure
- Rehabilitation of 6 km of the sub-roads in the camp (graveling) with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure
- Capacity and equipment support to local public service providers,for instance, local organizations, local administrative service providers; extension services supported with rehabilitation, equipment and operational support
- Implementing sidewalks through cash for work approach next to the new rehabilitated roads
- Establishing and training six rescue teams during the rainy season to assist in responding to the floods risks
- Conducting regular awareness sessions for the camp population.
- Coordinating with the Turkish authorities for conducting regular cleaning for the culverts under the wall border and/or expanding the current culverts
The proposed locations of the infrastructure rehabilitation/improvement are the most affected locations from the previous two floods, where the proposed intervention can assist the camp residents to have the option to move when similar disasters happen.

4 - Needs

Two significant floods occurred during the past year, affecting thousands of households in Northwest Syria, with the most detrimentally affected being IDPs. According to CCCM cluster reports, a combined total of 17,683 households were affected by both floods. During the discussion in the CCCM cluster reported 6,079 households were negatively impacted by torrential rainfall that caused widespread flooding across Northwest Syria, in total, the CCCM cluster verified 4,983 tents were affected. Imaar organized a rapid assessment in Atma Camp and confirmed that 1,200 households were affected by the floods in Atma alone, with 250 shelters completely collapsed, 565 shelters were highly affected, and 265 shelters sustained partial damage as a direct result of flooding; in addition to, the main access ways has been stoped.The assessment showed a clear need for rapid response interventions such as tent and NFI distributions, and with the floods partially or severely damaging roads and culverts, some lifesaving essential WASH services were disrupted, thereby exasperating the situation. Outside of Atma Camp, access roads also sustained damage during both floods, particularly near culverts, where sections of the roads eroded due to culverts being clogged with debris and water-shedding over the top of the road.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Security situation allows access to the project location- Project scope and design is accepted by the community and key stakeholders, including landowners- Needed equipment, goods, supplies, and contracting services are available in the local market Risks:- Roads are not constructed to specifications. We will overcome this risk by performing daily monitoring with a mobile-based remote monitoring system that updates Imaar teams on a real-time basis, thereby allowing us to make quick and informed decisions. - Housing land property (HLP) issues between landowners and camp population, We will prepare HLP including checklists with taking into consideration the Guidance of Due Diligence of Shelter; in addition to, the recommendation of HLP TWG and protection guidance. We will take int consideration the contractual status between stakeholders and beneficiaries, and the legal procedures after the end of the period project. - Design changes are required due to acceptance issues with the local community landowners. - The border closure has a negative impact on the availability of needed equipment, goods and supplies in the local market. - Local authorities, community, landowners and other key stakeholders do not agree with public ownership plan of the roads.
