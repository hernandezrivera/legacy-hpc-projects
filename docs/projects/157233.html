Project Summary

In 2019, IOM Sudan is planning to continue providing assistance to Internal Displaced Persons (IDPs) in North, West, South and Central Darfur to end displacement by addressing core drivers of migration and displacement. IOM will address these drivers by providing IDPs with sustainable long-term support that addresses protection, stability, social, livelihood and economic concerns and supports communities to better prevent, absorb and recover positively and effectively to future risks and shocks.
More specifically, the proposed project will first seek to conduct a pre-action verification and assessment to better under the intentions and needs of IDPs in the target area in coordination with the government. Using this information IOM will design and engage with IDPs and local communities to hold community-led workshops to promote social cohesion and integration of IDPs within the newly established urban areas. To ensure a successful (re)integration, the project will also provide, and rehabilitate, critical basic service infrastructure and address constraints to accessing sustainable income generating activities as well as essential livelihood training (based on community priorities and market assessments) to enhance resilience to cyclical and humanitarian challenges. Additional assistance will focus on the most vulnerable households providing them livelihood assistance to foster self-reliance.
As part of IOM’s global mandate on mobility and migration, IOM has developed institutional capacity in transition and recovery programming that uses development-principled approaches to comprehensively address root causes driving forced and irregular. This project will reflect IOM’s development-principled programming in line with IOM’s Progressive Resolution of Displacement Situations Framework and the Humanitarian-Development Nexus to maximize opportunities that employ mobility strategies to foster the resilience of displaced persons, migrants and affected communities to support transition towards durable solutions and development. IOM Sudan’s Transition and Recovery Unit will deliver the project drawing on its extensive experience implementing locally-driven and contextualized longer-term development-principled activities that empower affected populations ownership over addressing the conditions of their displacement and restore their ability to make informed mobility decisions and reduce their vulnerability.

Needs

The protracted crisis in Sudan has contributed to the internal displacement of nearly 2.4 million persons. At present, there are a large number of IDP camps and as the security situation improves many IDPs are now considering returning to places of origin. Whilst it is a Government strategy to promote return, equally there is a push to turn larger IDP camps into more permanent urban areas. Such a move will require support and durable solutions to ensure long-term sustainability, social cohesion and productive (re)integration into host communities. The length of the crisis has reached a stage where IDP camps, such as Abu Shouk and Al Salam in the El Fasher locality in North Darfur, are now becoming urbanized as second and third generation IDPs, with limited options to return home, begin to permanently settle. The situation is consistent across North, West, Central and South Darfur however, further assessments are required to profile the camps and locations to determine the level of ongoing urbanization where the project's activities can provide the most benefit. The project will aim to facilitate the smooth transition of suitable sites from temporary camps to more permanent urban neighborhoods by facilitating a coordinated implementation of camp closure guidelines (CCCM) following a collaborative and people centered approach that is informed by the initial assessments and profiling activities. Additionally, to foster social cohesion, this project will facilitate community engagement campaigns with the aim of sensitizing and preparing host communities for dialogue with government and IDPs. Local (re)integration and returns will be further supported by rehabilitating basic services and community infrastructure. The most vulnerable IDPs and members of the host community will also receive vocational livelihood training. The project will follow the Early Recovery Approach focusing on reducing community dependency through increased community ownership and involvement. Such activities will include the establishment of community committees, community based livelihood interventions and the implementation of a WASH-tariff system via community-led water management committees from the start. This project will also benefit from the results of pilot Durable Solutions profiling project. The pilot project was recently completed, within the context of Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus, in close collaboration with Resident Coordinator’s Office, UNHCR, UNDP, UN HABITAT, and other international and national partners including the Government of Sudan.

Activities

1: Pre-action verification and assessment support better understanding on the IDPs’ intentions and specific needs. 2: Conduct a mapping exercise to identify a list of interventions needed to ensure the smooth transition of an IDP camp into an urban area.3. Establish community committees who will be responsible for specific areas (i.e. WASH).4. IDPs choosing local integration are offered the opportunity to participate in community-led interventions and capacity-building workshops. These workshops will also include elements aimed at building institutional capacity and improving community awareness of both human rights, and their individual rights.5: Crucial basic service infrastructure is rehabilitated and expanded at the sites of return and / or at local integration sites (camp sites) in line with the initial assessment and a view for long-term sustainability. 6: Improve access to quality and quantity of agricultural inputs and income generating activities through targeted agricultural training and through other income generating opportunities. The training sessions will be paired with nutritional trainings to increase women's knowledge of feeding practices based on the nutritional value of food available within their locality. As a result, targeted communities will have improved agricultural inputs, improved economic opportunities and better knowledge of healthy nutritional practices.7: Work with the community and private sector to identify and set up income-generating and value-added activities to catalyze and drive economic and community development.8. Provide additional assistance to most vulnerable households to foster self reliance and promote inclusiveness and basic equality. 9. Monitor activities to assess the progress of the project and make adjustments as needed. A final evaluation will be conducted to assess the implementation and impact of the project and make recommendations on the suitability of replicating and/or scaling up the project.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Disasters and environmental degradation threaten to hamper progress towards the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and durable solutions, especially for those countries left furthest behind. Climate change is expected to trigger growing population movements within and across borders. IOM aims to build the resilience of communities to risk factors and expected changes in their natural environment, while contributing to sustainable development of livelihood opportunities; supporting efforts to strengthen the interaction with responsibility holders (national and local authorities) and develop their capacities. IOM is working closely with the Higher Council for Environment and Natural Resources (HCENR), in line the National Adaptation Plan (NAP) to climate change, on establishing integrated water management systems and will also continue working on establishing sources of renewable energy (solar energy) and improving local practices to reduce the environmental footprint and waste of natural resources. Livelihood and agricultural training will continue to focus on improving the targeted beneficiaries' knowledge of agricultural practices so that they are able to better respond to climate shocks. The training will also introduce communities to better coping strategies in order to replace the negative coping strategies currently used and that contribute to ongoing land degradation and desertification (e.g. firewood collection). IOM will consult with UNEP/ISCG on the community environmental action plan tools and conduct area impact assessments before implementation of any supports on rehabilitation of basic service facilities such as water and sanitation facilities.
