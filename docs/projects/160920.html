Project Summary

Given the ongoing protection crisis in South Sudan, DRC will continue to work with individuals affected by conflict and displacement, and other relevant stakeholders to enhance the protective environment of individuals and communities, and identify, prevent and address protection risks. Overall, the project will target a total of 120,000 IDPs, returnees, and host communities. The main objectives of the project, aligned with the Protection Cluster objectives, are:
- Enhance protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response.
- Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience.
- Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other affected populations.
- Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialised services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, boys, and girls in hard to reach and priority geographical areas.
The project is designed to alleviate suffering of the most vulnerable individuals, and ensure safety and well-being of conflict-affected communities are prioritised and achieved. The project will focus on three main components:
- Protection analysis and advocacy: DRC will focus on protection data collection and analysis to ensure an in-depth understanding of the protection environment, allowing for appropriate prevention and response activities. In parallel, DRC will ensure protection concerns are captured in protection information products, such as assessment reports, monitoring reports, and protection snapshots, allowing for appropriate and relevant advocacy with the clusters, humanitarian partners, and other main stakeholders. DRC protection programming will ensure evidence-driven interventions, at the individual and community levels, ensuring appropriate and principled action is taken to prevent and respond to protection risks.
- Protection risk prevention: DRC will continue to work closely with communities, particularly existing community structures, and vulnerable groups and individuals, to identify protection risks, coping strategies, community and individual intentions, and resilience among the affected population. DRC will work with community-based protection networks, facilitate focus group discussions, awareness raising, trainings on human rights and protection mainstreaming, and establish community-driven action plans, aiming to prevent and mitigate identified protection risks.
- Protection response: DRC provides tailored protection responses to individuals at risk and communities affected by violence and displacement. The responses include community-based protection monitoring and referrals, individual protection assistance (IPA), case management, and mobile responses, identification and support to vulnerable individuals at risk, psychosocial support, service mapping and referrals, community based protection networks, and other necessary interventions based on needs in various locations, such as housing land and property (HLP) and legal aid.
DRC’s approach in South Sudan is both through static presence (mainly in Upper Nile and Unity States) and mobile teams, who rove throughout the country based on new shocks and movements of populations. In Unity State, DRC forms part of a group of agencies implementing the “Beyond Bentiu Response” strategy, which aims at assessing locations beyond the Bentiu POC, and providing services to hard-to-reach communities in different counties. DRC adopts an integrated approach, linking general protection and HLP with sexual and gender based violence (SGBV) prevention and response, food security and livelihood (FSL), shelter and non-food items (SNFIs) and camp coordination and camp management (CCCM). DRC will continue to engage with other humanitarian partners and the relevant clusters to coordination and ensure aid effectiveness, as well as focus on mainstreaming protection.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has experienced a long history of civil war, and since its secession from Sudan in 2011, two violent conflicts have erupted in 2013 and 2016. In spite of the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) on 12 September 2018, fighting and displacement have continued to affect the civilian population in various locations in South Sudan. The implementation of the peace agreement by the warring parties has been slow, particularly around the defining of boundaries and cantonment. Armed conflict in certain areas, and increased inter-communal violence and criminality have demonstrated that the prospect of political peace may not necessarily lead to an end of the ethnic conflict, and that protracted protection crisis affecting the civilians could persist for multiple years in spite of a potential improvement in the political environment. According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), approximately 5,725,000 million South Sudanese women, men, and children would face protection risks and violations in 2019. These risks are a direct result of the continued violence and conflict affecting civilians since 2013. Displacement, caused by conflict and rights violations, continues to be one of the main protection concerns, with 2,183,028 South Sudanese refugees in neighbouring countries, 299,123 refugees in South Sudan, and 1,465,542 internally displaced persons (IDPs) , of whom 182,112 are living in protection of civilian (PoC) sites. Lack of access to food and basic services are some of the challenges IDPs face, while South Sudanese refugees in neighbouring countries face harassment, discrimination, and attacks, sometimes leading them to return to South Sudan, further exposing them to different types of protection risks. The South Sudanese population continues to be subject to human rights violations, including deliberate attacks, deliberate destruction of property and assets, forced displacement, conflict-related sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), abductions, forced recruitment, restrictions on freedom of movement of civilians, occupation of schools, and family separation, among others. People living in Upper Nile State and Unity State, where DRC has a well-established presence, are highly affected by these violations and the widespread impunity. However, communities in other states, such as Jonglei, Western Bar-el-Ghazal, and Central Equatoria, where DRC’s mobile protection teams have conducted various protection assessments, equally face displacement caused by violence and deliberate deprivation. Oftentimes, men and boys face risks of killing and forced recruitment, women and girls face risks of rape and sexual abuse, among others. DRC, through its static operational presence, and its mobile protection teams, has developed a strong understanding of the general and complex protection environment. It has become apparent that protection needs remain high across the country, and will continue to be high throughout 2020 based on the current analysis. A recent (August 2019) HLP assessment in Bentiu identified many issues for displaced populations to access housing, land, and property, including barriers for women to access land, secondary occupation, destroyed property, and evictions. Identifying solutions for displaced people has been an urgent and essential discussion over the past months (and years) in South Sudan, where topics of returns, relocations, and local integration are heatedly debated. Various stakeholders have indicated that large numbers of people are returning to their areas of habitual residence since the signing of the peace agreement; these would include returns of displaced people from Uganda, Kenya, Sudan, and internal displacement sites within South Sudan. However, the lack of evidence concerning these figures and the multitude of actors reporting different figures makes responding to the situation difficult.

2 - Activities

DRC’s protection intervention in 2020 will focus on addressing the main protection needs as identified by DRC’s needs analysis. Protection activities will focus on protection analysis (evidence-based programming), protection risk prevention and protection response. Based on these needs and in line with the protection cluster’s strategic objectives for HRP, DRC’s proposed activities are as follows. Under strategic objective 4, DRC plans to implement mobile and static protection assessments, and in static locations, including in POCs, DRC will implement protection monitoring. This protection data collection will be processed, analysed, stored, and shared with relevant actors, following the protection information management principles (people-centred and inclusive, do no harm, defined purpose, informed consent and confidentiality, etc). Through its data collection and monitoring activities, which include monitoring visits, focus group discussions (FGDs), key informant interviews (KIIs), observation, population data gathering, protection desk management, and community based protection networks (CBPN), DRC will identify vulnerable people at risk and will provide appropriate responses, include case management, individual protection assistance (IPA), referrals, and legal awareness and counselling. Under this objective, DRC aims to reach approximately 11,500 persons at risk, and persons with specific needs. Under strategic objective 2, DRC will implement community based protection risk mitigation and support activities to reduce vulnerabilities and enhance the resilience and coping mechanisms of communities facing protection risks. These activities include: community based protection networks and structures who are trained to identify protection risks, and supported to mitigate these risks. DRC will also provide material support to vulnerable persons at risk, and who have specific needs, based on vulnerability criteria. For prevention, DRC will implement protection awareness raising sessions and risk mitigation discussions, to empower the communities and strengthen their resilience to threats. DRC will also target specific beneficiaries who require income generating or livelihood activities in order to mitigate protection risks. Under this objective, DRC aims to reach approximately 185,500 persons affected by conflict and displacement. Under strategic objective 3, DRC will engage with communities to understand their intentions and provide support for individuals seeking solutions. DRC will support through identifying housing land and property needs in specific locations that are deemed stable, and provide appropriate awareness and counselling on housing land and property concerns. In addition, DRC will work with communities consisting of different ethnic groups, displacement backgrounds, and social situations, to ensure transitional solutions are achieved, including peaceful coexistence and inclusivity. The activities under this objective will only be implemented in static responses, in Upper Nile and Unity states, and will target approximately 20,500 people. In addition, DRC will provide training on protection mainstreaming to humanitarian partners, trainings for community based structures on human rights and protection risks, and advocate with main stakeholders on protection concerns, with the aim to improve the overall protective environment of the affected populations. DRC will continuously engage with the community, and seek input from the affected persons on the needs and relevance of the response, according to protection principles and Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS).

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.1. Members of marginalised community’s/minority groups are not included in consultation and/or excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. DRC will carry out sensitisation among its own team members to raise their awareness about the need to engage members from all segments in the consultations/project implementation. DRC will also carry out home to home visits to ensure the inclusion of community members who may be unable to leave their household due to physical or cultural/social restrictions. 8.10. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. As was observed in Bentiu, Unity State, late last year, women and girls face an increased risk of sexual violence when accessing humanitarian assistance. Considering this, DRC will ensure protection mainstreaming across its programming and seek to reduce/mitigate protection risks that may be incurred during the course of the project. Examples, of steps that will be taken by DRC include engagement of beneficiary members around selection of the locations for latrine construction and screening of locations with a GBV risk assessment. 8.6 Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas. The conflict has seen a rise in the incidents of housing, land, and property (HLP) disputes with the land of displaced households often resettled in their absence. The situation is particularly challenging for female headed households, who have land rights under the constitution and national framework, but often experience discrimination in fulfilling these rights at the local level due restrictive social and cultural norms at the household level. Considering these challenges, DRC will carry out community sensitisation and awareness raising on HLP issues. DRC will also provide legal assistance to those who seeking to secure land tenure.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC adopts a community-based approach, where beneficiaries and local authorities are included in the prioritising, planning and implementation of activities as an important measure of rehabilitation and capacity building. DRC is a certified member of the Core humanitarian Standards (CHS) and as such fulfils the CHS benchmarks on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. DRC has deployed beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism and operates complaints desks to enable community members to flag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. DRC also has a feedback hotline Number (9686) that beneficiaries can call to register complaints, feedback or requests. Through its activities, DRC will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols. Complaints and feedback submitted to the F-CRM will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the project
