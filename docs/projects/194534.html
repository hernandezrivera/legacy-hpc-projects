Project Summary

In line with our organizational Core Commitment to Children in Emergency, UNICEF will continue to provide the population of South Sudan especially children, pregnant and lactating women, with quality preventive, and curative services as well as outbreak response activities. The IRRM enables UNICEF, through direct implementation as well as in partnership with IPs, to provide high impact lifesaving emergency response in inaccessible and conflict affected areas. UNICEF will ensure availability of core pipe line vaccines and cold chain supplies to respond to vaccine preventable disease outbreaks.
This project aims to increase access to quality equitable essential primary health care interventions through IRRM and provide a core pipeline to increase access to quality equitable immunization services by vulnerable populations living in host communities and IDPs living in Protection of Civilian Sites (POCs), formal IDP camps or host communities in conflict affected areas as well as refugee returnees.
The activities to be implemented are:
• Procurement and distribution of vaccines for emergency response .
• Procurement, distribution and maintenance of cold chain
• Technical support for improved vaccine and cold chain management
• Conduct 10 IRRM missions
• Procurement and distribution of anti- snake venom
• Procurement and distribution of LLINs
• Procurement and distribution of Health Emergency Kits, Clean Delivery Kits and items for diarrhea (IEHK2017 basic unit kit and Supl.1-medicines kits, AWD Periphery Kit drugs), malaria and Pneumonia prevention and treatment
The expected beneficiaries of core pipeline supplies are 650,000 vulnerable children aged 6 months-15years (323,988 Female; 326,590 Male) living in camps, PoCs, formal IDP camps or host communities in conflict affected areas and refugee returnees. The children will benefit from emergency vaccination to sure up their immunity to vaccine preventable diseases. Vaccines against measles, and traditional childhood vaccine. UNICEF will also support repair and maintenance of the damaged cold chain equipment and devices as well as replacement of the non-repairable equipment to ensure the continuity of the vaccination services.
The curative consultations will be provided for an estimated 28,000 children (13,972 female; 14,028 male) during the Rapid Response Missions. These will include children aged 6 months-15 years who will be reached with immunization services and curative consultations and pregnant women who are expected to benefit from ANC services, tetanus toxoid vaccination, curative consultations and the provision of long-lasting insecticide treated nets (LLITN).
Under the IDP response through IRRM, provision of Emergency Maternal and Newborn care will prioritize provision emergency health kits for obstetric emergencies during curative consultations. Also mainstreaming GBV referrals and strengthening the referral pathway for Psychosocial first aid.

1 - Needs

About 5.4 million people in South Sudan are in need of humanitarian health care services in 2022 with an increase from the 5.2 million people in need in 2021. Out of this, 3.6 million people are from host communities and 328,610 people are refugee and asylum seekers. A total of 53 per cent of the people in need are children, 24 per cent are women, and 23 per cent are men. The capacity to provide general health services (in terms of having basic amenities, equipment, IPC standard precautions, diagnostic capacity, essential medicines) continue to be limited in 2022. According to Ministry of Health and WHO health facility functionality system 2020, 7.5 per cent of the 1,763 reporting health facilities provide basic health services. Damage of cold chain equipment continue to be reported from the States, in 2020 and 2021 the State cold chain store was damaged, the equipment was spoilt because of the floods and cold chain equipment were also vandalized in the health facilities. This makes access to health services difficult for the large proportion of the vulnerable internally displayed person and host community members. Overall, health service operation in South Sudan is very expensive due to poor road networks, necessitating the use of costly charter flights to transport supplies to some locations. During the dry season, whilst more road transport routes are open, the likelihood of ambushes and looting is very high which makes transportation of supplies by road very difficult. Meanwhile, rains during the wet season render approximately 60 per cent of roads in the country unusable. Routine immunization coverage remains very low (57 per cent) and much lower in former conflict affected states is about 34 per cent. Even though 71 per cent of the facilities provide immunization services, only 40 per cent offer the services daily and 28 per cent offer the services weekly (SARA 2018). The consistently low immunization coverage predisposes children, living in PoCs, IDPs and refugee returnees, to vaccine preventable diseases such as measles, pertussis, and diphtheria disease. Modeling shows that a large number of measles-susceptible children, exceeding the size of the estimated birth cohort will occur in 2022 and 2023, due to the cumulative effect of the low MCV1 coverage, the weak delivery of routine immunization services in some areas, and admin results seen in the 2020 SIAs coverage put South Sudan at risk of large measles outbreaks. South Sudan lies in yellow fever belt and Yellow fever outbreak was declared in Kajokeji county in 2021, after confirmation of 2 cases; as a result reactive vaccination campaign has been conducted. South Sudan is in the African Meningitis belt, hence is prone to seasonal outbreaks, in 2018 about 107 suspected meningitis cases and 28 deaths have been reported. Cholera outbreak ended after one-year long transmission in 2017, four counties and three POCs are identified as hotspots for cholera transmission hence vaccination needs to be given to the population to prevent cholera outbreak. Against this backdrop, in hard to reach, conflict-affected areas with intermittent periods of stability and access, a key strategy for delivery of lifesaving health services is the use of IRRM. This is because the IRRM platform enables the delivery of essential healthcare package that can be accessed by IDPs in inaccessible areas, hard to reach areas in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity states. Meanwhile, improving the immunization core pipeline through procurement of vaccines, especially measles and meningitis vaccine, and distribution of oral cholera vaccine, installation of cold chain equipment and use of portable cold chain equipment will assist in increasing access to quality equitable immunization services among the IDPs. Combined, these efforts will contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality among affected vulnerable populations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project activities will target children, women both IDPS and vulnerable in the host communities in the hard to reach areas and other areas with limited access to services. In order to create community ownership. RRM teams engage with the communities and local authorities in each mission and upon arrival. Once deployed, UNICEF/WFP/FAO teams’ leaders organize a preliminary meeting with the local authorities of the location to discuss population movement and estimated figures of the displaced population to be covered by the rapid response services. They also explain the content of the mission and the services that will be delivered. The teams then recruit community mobilisers to inform and mobilize the communities to benefit from the services made available during the duration of the RRM mission. At the end of each mission, the RRM teams organize a meeting with the leaders of the community and encourage them to give their feedback on the services provided, to raise any concerns or discontent they might have and to flag any protection or other issue related to the mission. In locations where there is a presence of implementing partners and where there is a health centre, community feedback can also be channeled after the departure of the RRM teams through the implementing partners and health facility staff who remain in the location. This feedback then forms part of the project performance reporting to the Health Cluster and will help guide the fine tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experiences

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community feedback is incorporated in the design and delivery of the RRM missions, the community leaders are engaged in organizing and delivery of the services during RRM missions continuously

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All partners working with UNICEF have been assessed for PSEA and mitigation measure is put in place to ensure zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse. UNICEF staff members have been trained and live up to the UNICEF policy on PSEA. The missions will use the already developed PSEA communication plans and material to the context and target audience, and involve as many stakeholders and beneficiaries as possible.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In every IRRM mission, the community leaders and influential figures in the community will be consulted and encouraged to provide feedback on how to improve the services. After every mission review meetings are held and the community feedback is addressed to improve the services.
