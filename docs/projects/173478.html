Project Summary

"HELVETAS will continue it’s Rohingya response programme in 3rd year 2021. The overall objective of the proposed Rohingya response programme is to enhance the food security and self-reliance of the Rohingya refugees, while strengthening, at the same time, the livelihood bases and resilience of impoverished host families that bear an excessive burden from the massive refugee influx. HELVETAS plans to achieve this through following interlinked projects interventions-
• Continue to support 9,000 refugee HHs (corresponding to 41,400 individuals based on an average ratio of 4.6 individuals/HH) for improving their capacity to produce a certain amount of own vegetables inside the camps in Ukhia sub-district.
• Continue to create lasting benefits and sustainably improve the income of host community 12,000 selected host community HHs (corresponding to 60,000 individuals based on a ratio of 5 individuals/HH) farming households through productivity gains, production increases, asset improvements and market system development. This include to continue supporting 7,000 HH in host community in Ukhia and expansion of interventions for 5,000 HH in Tekhnaf.
• Continue to private Local Service Providers (LSP) and their association to support local farmers in agricultural value chains in Ukhia and Tekhnaf sub-districts. They will bridge host community and refugee community through market linkage by establishing collection centers.
• Support 3,000 participating HHs (Corresponding to 15,000 individuals based on a ratio of 5 individuals/HH) in Ramu sub-districts to improve and/or recover their actual livelihoods situation and to boost the local economy in target areas, by offering them apprenticeships, small and medium sized business start-up support, entrepreneurship development for micro business and Cash for Work (CfW) activities.
Based on above calculation, cumulative total for the Rohingya response programme comes to approximately 24,000 beneficiary HHs (corresponding to 116,400 individuals)."

1 - Identified Needs

"The needs analysis for the rohingya response programme that started in 2019 through a livelihood’s assessment study. This has been further updated based mainly on REVA 3 and J-MSNA 2020.A continuing very high degree of overall economic vulnerability within the Rohingya refugee population resulting in equally high levels of dependence on humanitarian aid to cover even the most basic needs; (according to J-MSNA 2020 FCS has notable drop in FCS (poor and borderline) from 46% to 65%. J-MSNA 2020 data shows that 66% Rohingya reported having reduced food expenditures since the COVID-19 outbreaks, while 23% reported that they have limited access to food due to impact of COVID-19 outbreaks. The economic vulnerability, although with a different magnitude compared to the situation of the Rohingya refugees, has also been affecting the host communities. Higher financial resources of Bangladeshi living in host communities ensure relatively higher access to food and mitigates food insecurity in the absence of direct food assistance. According to J-MSNA 2020, notably drop in FCS (poor and boarder line) from 28% (2019) to 57% (2020). 78% HHs in host community reported that they have reduced food expenditures due to COVID-19 outbreaks. However, financial resources are not sufficient to meet all food and non-food related needs for the vast majority of Bangladeshi living in areas with a high presence of refugees. A total of 93% HH in host community reported that have diminished income as impact of COVID-19 outbreaks. "
