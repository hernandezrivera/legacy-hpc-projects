Project Summary

TThe proposed project seeks to improve water supply, sanitation and hygiene services to the vulnerable crises affected populations (affected by severe drought, floods, disease, conflict related displacement and chronic food insecurity) considering the severity of WASH needs by location where an estimated 428,468 people (336,466 local and 92,002 IDP) in Lower Juba, Gedo, Hiran, Galmudug, Bari, Owdweyne and Awdal regions of Somalia. Project locations can, however, be targeted discretely through a scalable approach.
The project will deploy a community-led approach to deliver on its objectives and will purpose to deliver on: (1) Life-saving emergency WASH assistance through emergency Water services, Sanitation services and Hygiene Promotion; (2) Providing sustained access to quality WASH services for the target population following durable solutions approach, and (3) Upholding commitments to the centrality of protection across the interventions. Besides being responsive to the emerging emergency situations, the action will endeavour to fix, rehabilitate non-functional while improving on strategic water sources especially in rural areas. Through an inclusive process, the weak water supply management models will also be strengthened while providing alternatives and solutions to the high operational and maintenance costs, and support with spare parts where lacking, and build or improve the technical limitation of service providers. Innovative durable solutions will be promoted to provide solutions to quality problems through treatment while striving to address high groundwater water salinity affecting vast project areas, in especially Somaliland and Puntland.
The capacities and the technical knowhow of water operators, management committees and local authorities will be developed to effectively operate, manage, oversee service delivery through promotion and application of public-private partnership frameworks to effectually sustain the operations of existing functional water supply and sanitation systems. Additionally, the youth will be involved in WASH service delivery as income generating opportunities to support livelihoods through; provision of basic O&M, construction of WASH facilities, installation of solar/pumps, garbage collection, waste recycling and management and any other appropriate technologies that would bring value addition at the different levels of WASH service provision.
Access to improved sanitation facilities will be enhanced. In the rural settings, community-led total sanitation (CLTS) will be used to promote latrine coverage and use in areas where open defecation is practiced. For public places, the project will rehabilitate and/or construct public toilets and handwashing facilities in schools and health care facilities. Public latrines will be constructed for IDP settlements where public health challenges are dire. Hygiene promotion will be a core component of this action to curb the spread of communicable diseases especially diarrhoea, Covid-19, etc., and handwashing with soap and water will be key. Access to Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) material will be promoted for vulnerable communities (in IDP settlements, poor households and in schools where girls will also be supported. A functional market system, which offers MHM materials at the local level will be encouraged to enhance supply and quantity of WASH commodities sold in markets. Other critical WASH goods at risk of stock depletion in the open markets are chlorine tablets, soap bars, and drinking water, etc.
The action will also uphold commitment to the centrality of protection across its interventions. Protection risks that are WASH related, including gender-based violence (GBV), will mapped out and addressed to ensure safety and protection of users. Gender equity and inclusion will also be considered in all aspects of implementation during this action.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In some areas, the project will need to issue the vulnerable households with money to be able to buy the basic household items, which may not be easy to put together and manage by the organization in some contexts. The items may include basic WASH NFIs (e.g. sanitary pads, soap) for displaced and very poor households and emergency water supply (water trucking) in some contexts, especially in very far-flung areas where frequent monitoring is extremely difficult. Upon proper contextualization and evaluation of the functionality of the local market, it could be prudent to allow the households to exercise their freedom by enabling them to procure the most pressing WASH items for their households on their own. Provision of drought-affected households with money (through a cash based transfer process) to buy water for their households themselves sometimes becomes imperative where vendors that supply water are quite erratic in their delivery timings. Even though they respond to the local demand-supply forces, their timings are difficult and thus monitor using routine voucher systems becomes extremely difficult. The vendors, however, still adequately serve and meet the local demand. In order not to disrupt a working system by introducing new operators, a cash based programme solves the problem. While allowing the beneficiary households to operate in a more dignified manner, provision of cash and letting the local market function normally is usually the best option. Also, the process of procuring and transporting items in some places is dangerous. there are dangers of the materials being looted during transportation or being stolen while in the stores or during distribution. Ordinarily, there is no much respect for goods that belong to organizations and blackmail and pillage are risks that usually hover around the agencies' operations. Cash based approach (CTP) is also usually the best when operating in insecure and hard-to-access environments where vulnerable populations require humanitarian support. This prevent both the beneficiary and the organization the risk of exposure thereby turning out to be a safer option and the surest way to ensure the beneficiaries access what is due to them. also, this modality offers one of the best accountable process thus enhancing accountability. The CBT will be undertaken by mobile money transfer service provider. The service provider will be contracted either by ADRA/partner to disburse cash to the selected beneficiaries. All beneficiaries will be required to have active and registered SIM cards. The cash will be disbursed monthly for a specified period (at least three months). The mobile money transfer service provider will provide a disbursement report after every disbursement cycle. The report will indicate the name of the recipient, amount, date and time of disbursement and status of the disbursement

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

In every target locality, there is an operational local market but the success of their functionality varies from place to place. ADRA, with almost a nationwide coverage, has offices in most of the regions targeted by this action. ADRA conducts own markets surveys within the main towns and market centers and in the rural areas to assess prices and price variations within the market. In the context of this project, the main purpose of the market functionality assessment is to determine the functionality of market systems where the following nine areas are evaluated: 1) Assortment of essential goods, 2) Availability, 3) Price, 4) Resilience of supply chains, 5) Competition, 6) Infrastructure, 7) Services, 8) Quality, and 9) Access and protection. It is all these factors that are put into context to determine whether the CTP is suitable for a location or not. Relating WASH, the items like sanitary pads, soap, cleaning materials, water prices, etc. are keenly observed. The assessments carried out by ADRA in mid-2021 helped to inform the design and implementation of assistance programmes in 2021 and 2022. Specific objectives of the assessment include: (a) Inform the appropriateness and feasibility of cash-based transfer (CBT) programming, considering existing macro-economic challenges, natural disasters and currently the impact of the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic; (2) Update the database of traders phone numbers who can be contacted on a regular basis through remote mobile monitoring systems for the purpose of market monitoring and propose a monitoring methodology that can allow for easy and agile tracking of key market indicators (i.e. Assortment, Availability, Price and Resilience) in near real-time, which will help to inform programme modalities and adjustments of transfer values to beneficiaries; and (c) Provide recommendations on the most appropriate assistance modality (cash vs. in-kind) for selected areas (i.e. identify markets with higher functionality that are generally better prepared for cash-based interventions than less functional ones).
