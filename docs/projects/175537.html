Project Summary

The HNO for South Sudan estimates that there will be 8.3 million people who will be in need of humanitarian assistance. The need for support on food security and livelihoods is ranked as a top most need by a majority of the people across the counties. Most of the counties including Koch county in Unity state and Fangak county in Jonglei state are ranked as IPC 4. Without humanitarian intervention, this situation could worsen and plunge populations into famine situation.
The main objective of this intervention is to support the vulnerable populations in these two counties with livelihood kits and training so as to help these communities produce their own food to sustain their livelihoods. It is expected that through this intervention, target populations will be able to supplement food provided through general food distribution which had proved to be irregular and therefore unreliable but also increases dependency to external support. The intervention will support the target population with crop, vegetable and fishing kits as well as provide them with training in use of inputs provided to avoid causing any harm but also avoid diverting these resources towards unintended use.
A total of 12,500 beneficiaries comprising on 60% women, elderly, youth, disabled and other vulnerable groups will be targeted for crop and vegetable kits. Out of this number, a total of 2,500HHs will be targeted for training on improved agronomic methods, post-harvest handling and utilisation. Similarly, 6,500HHs will be targeted with fishing kits, out of which 1,300HHs will be included in training on appropriate harvesting methods and fish preservation. This intervention will also target 1,000HHs with Food for Asset intervention to help them build community structures that will help them to mitigate effects of flooding. Another 500HHs, mainly caregivers of children with SAM cases will be targeted with small ruminants to help them have sustainable access to milk and income. To help improve sustainable access to quality seeds, the project will pilot working with 100HHs to produce high quality seeds and avail them to other farmers for subsequent use.
The intervention will run from January to December of 2021

1 - Needs

The HNO for 2021 estimates that 8.3 Million people will need humanitarian assistance. Food, WASH, health, Nutrition and protection are ranked top among the humanitarian needs expected in 2021. This situation is as a result of multiplicity of factors all exerting pressure on the humanitarian needs in the country. The fighting that started in 2013 has become precarious taking on various forms and manifested as sub-national violence exacerbated by cattle raiding and revenge killings. In both Koch County Unity state and Fangak County, Jonglei state, violence has persisted through cattle raiding and revenge killings resulting in population displacement and a feeling of insecurity which prevents populations from accessing their farms to cultivate. The IPC report of December 2020 indicates that 78.3% of the population in Jonglei will be in IPC 3,4&5. This proportion is expected to rise to 84.7% by the pick of the lean season. In Fangak alone, 153,425 people will be in need of humanitarian assistance while in Koch, 70% of the population, translated to 67,380 persons will require humanitarian assistance(IPC, Dec 2020). Environmental factors have also contributed greatly to poor livelihood outcomes being experienced in the country. South Sudan has continued to experience conditions of prolonged draught, erratic rainfall and flooding which in 2020 reached levels that have never been experienced in the country for a long time. This affected the physical infrastructure and worsened the already poor state of these physical infrastructure. A recent multi-agency assessment reports shows that flooding affected crop in farms before maturity and has greatly compromised the food security situation for 2021(IRNA KOCH, August 2020). Due to the flooding, Malaria and other water borne diseases have been on the rise and are expected to persist in 2021(IRNA Fangak, October 2020). It is reported that over one million people in 43 counties along the Nile River have been affected and this situation is expected to persist in the first quarter of 2021 (HNO, 2021). The COVID-19 Pandemic first case was reported in South Sudan in April 2020 and has continued to threaten the stability of the economy. COVID-19 has had a negative impact on the economy. South Sudan is a net importer of food and other commodities. COVID-19 led to closure of trade routes with neighboring countries which resulted in scarcity of these commodities, increase in food prices and increase in inflation. The country has also continued to witness decrease in oil revenues as a result of decrease in international oil prices and persistent insecurity in the country. In a country where oil contributes 90% of the countries revenues, such a decrease has a had a net negative impact on the economy. Basic commodities are scarce to find in the market and when available prices are high, including price for food which is imported from neighboring countries (WFP, March 2020). The joint market monitoring initiative report of December 2020 not only reports increase in cost of food in Fangak Market but also lack of some of the basic food supplies in Koch Market (JMMI Dec 2020). Owing to the COVID-19, it was difficult to conduct any surveys in the country. However, previous SMART survey conducted in Fangak County revealed a GAM rate of 16.2% and SAM prevalence of 3.3% among children under five (ACF, Sept 2019) and in Koch a Mass MUAC Screening showed a proxy GAM of 11.8% and SAM prevalence of1.9% this are considered significant particularly because of the season in which the data was collected, but the findings also identified several pockets of malnutrition among the population in the expansive county (WRSS Mass MUAC Screening ,Koch Nov 2019).

2 - Project activities

The project proposes to provide crop, vegetable and fisheries kits to beneficiaries to help them produce their own food to ensure self-reliance. The project will also train beneficiaries and impart them with skills and knowledge to enhance productivity. Activities will be categorized into main season and dry season with the aim of ensuring a year round production cycle. This is to ensure increased availability of food even during the dry season where availability of vegetables becomes a challenge and therefore complicates the food insecurity and malnutrition situation. Dry season training will focus on water efficient farming methods to produce sufficient and nutritious vegetables for consumption and possibly sell for some cash. Main season activities will concentrate on crop production specifically maize, sorghum and legumes. Fisheries activities will cut across the main season and dry season as there are sufficient fishing grounds for both seasons. There will be a strong coordination at county level with other partners to reduce duplication and ensure efficiency and wider coverage. The project will emphasis integration with other sectors to achieve greater impact in meeting household needs. For instance, the project will support establishment of kitchen garden within Nutrition centres and target mothers and caregivers of children enrolled in nutrition program with training and input support. This is to ensure that those children discharged from nutrition program do not relapse but have sufficient and nutritious food at home. The project will also incorporate WASH messaging at home, especially with reference to preparation and utilization of food, human waste disposal and provision of WASH items including water containers and purification tablets, cooking utensils and food preservation containers from other partners. The project will also incorporate Food for Asset (FFA) activities. Intended activities will include construction of dykes around farms to prevent perennial flooding, excavation of drainage channels, construction/rehabilitation of access roads, de-silting water pans to collect water during the rainy season, excavation of shallow wells. FFA will help communities establish communal assets, mitigate effects of flooding on food production and enhance preparedness and response to flooding which has become a yearly occurrence in Koch and Fangak.To improve access to milk and household income, the project will incorporate small ruminants targeting caregivers of SAM children within the Nutrition centres. This will be implemented though the Care groups for both distributions and training. The project will also work with a few selected farmers for seed multiplication. This intervention is meant to ensure continued access to quality seeds at community level without relying on external seeds supply.The project will carry out regular monitoring with participation of Boma management committees, develop and share project reports on monthly, quarterly and annual basis. Specifically, the project will:• Support 12,500 vulnerable food-insecure households with crop and vegetable kits • Support 6,500 vulnerable food-insecure households with fishing kits• Support 12,500 vulnerable food-insecure households with agricultural hand tools• Train 2,500 community leaders, lead farmers and extension staffs on improved agricultural practices including SMART Agriculture • Establish and maintain one demo farm per Payam for training farmers and support establishment of kitchen gardens in all nutrition sites to complement training and messaging to mothers and care givers in Nutrition program.• Train 1,300 fisher folk on improved fishing and fish preservation methods• Provide small ruminants to 500HHs• Provide 100HHs with Basic seed for seed multiplication• Conduct post-distribution, post-planting and post-harvest surveys.• Work with 1000HHs on FFA activities during the lean season

3 - Project indicators

The project will submit monthly progress report through the cluster and other funding agencies. The project will also submit quarterly, semi-annual and Annual reports. There will also be other specific reports that will be developed and shared depending on the specific activity. After every distribution, the project will conduct a post-distribution monitoring with 10% of the beneficiaries as respondents. A PDM report will be developed and shared. The project will also conduct a post-planting and post-harvest survey. Reports from these survey will also be developed and shared. This food security and livelihoods project will focus on the second and third cluster objectives. The first project objective will be to Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

World Relief South Sudan (WRSS) has in place a PSEA policy. All staffs are sensitized on the policy and are required to sign on it as a prove of having gone through and understood the policy. During project implementation, all staffs are not only expected to adhere to PSEA policy but also to sensitize beneficiaries on this policy. The protection team within WRSS is charged with responsibility to ensure continuous sensitization about PSEA. Sensitization will be provided at all stages of project implementation and any public gathering. Such public events will include registration of beneficiaries, during distribution of inputs and during training. The protection officer will be provided time to explain to beneficiaries concerning PSEA as well as the role of community in ensuring PSEA. The Project Implementation Committee (PIC) will involved in identification and selection of distribution and training sites. Distribution and training venues will be located in central places for ease of accessibility to all persons, but also in safe locations. This is to ensure that women and girls are not exposed to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse as a result of their involvement in any project activity, including training or inputs distribution. Distributions will also have to be conducted and completed on time to allow women and girls time to reach home before dark. WRSS will also provide a feedback mechanism. All beneficiaries will be sensitized on the availability of this feedback mechanism for their use. Confidentiality on any information provided through the feedback mechanism will be ensured to build the confidence of the community to use this feedback mechanism. complains registered will include Sexual Exploitation and Abuse or any attempt of such. These complains will be handled with uttermost respect and confidentiality and will be handled firmly.Inputs will be provided in small quantities. This is to ensure that beneficiaries will not have another financial burden to transport the inputs. Such financial burdens can expose these women and girls to sexual Exploitation and abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The most vulnerable members of community will be included in every aspect of project implementation. The project will target to include 60% of beneficiaries as women and girls. This is so because women bear the burden of ensuring that the Household is fed. Providing women with an opportunity to produce food will ensure that Households food security situation is improved. Similarly, women and girls will be consulted to ensure that activities planned are in line with the socio-cultural needs of women and avoid over-burdening them through this intervention. For instance, distribution of inputs as well as training will take into consideration feedback from women to be in line with other roles assigned to women like fetching water, firewood and cooking at home. Persons living with disabilities will also be given special consideration. For instance, during distributions, persons with disabilities, breastfeeding women, elderly will be accorded an opportunity to be served first. Training will also take into consideration the needs of vulnerable people. Training schedule and timing will consider the needs of such groups.Similarly, training will also take into consideration needs of the illiterate population. Most of the project beneficiaries are actually illiterate. The project will employ most of its staff from the local community who can be able to communicate and conduct training in the local language. Training will also involve mostly demonstrations and practical sessions which emphasize more on learning by doing than theory.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community engagement will be ensured at two levels. Right from inception, beneficiaries will be engaged at various levels. Project objectives will be discussed and agreed upon by community leaders at County, Payam and Boma levels. Number of beneficiaries, targeting of beneficiaries and geographical location will be discussed at project inception. Feedback provided from these meetings will be used to adjust subsequent plans to ensure project is aligned to the context and needs of the beneficiaries. Secondly, there will be a project Implementation committee (PIC) at every Payam. This PIC will be involved in beneficiary identification, selection and registration. The PIC will include representatives from the vulnerable groups including women, disabled, youth and elderly. The PIC will also be involved in project monitoring and evaluation. Feedback provided by PIC at every stage of project implementation will be used to improve on project delivery.At project level, the project directly engages Lead Farmers on voluntary basis. Lead Farmers are representatives of the community. They will be the contact between the project and beneficiaries. Communication on project objectives and expectations will be done primarily through the lead farmers. Lead farmers will also provide feedback on project implementation.The Monitoring and Evaluation team working with the protection team will institute a complaint registration and resolution mechanism. Complaints will be registered at every stage of project implementation and solutions sought for immediately where possible or referred to other levels for resolution. During beneficiary registration and inputs distribution, beneficiaries who feel unfairly left out will be free to register their complain and have their grievance addressed either by the project team or referred to higher management for resolution. Same mechanism will be instituted during training and any other project implementation stage. There will also be structured means to collect data from beneficiaries. There will be a post-distribution monitoring (PDM) conducted after every distribution. The feedback provided from these PDM will be adopted to improve on service delivery. The project will also conduct post-planting and post-harvest surveys. Similarly, feedback provided from these surveys will be used to improve programming including future projects.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback will be the gist of project development. Given that WRSS has been operational in these project locations for a long time, WRSS has had a chance to engage the beneficiaries at various stages and times. In the past, WRSS has been able to conduct post distribution monitoring, Post-planting and post-planting surveys. The project has also been able to conduct Knowledge, Attitude and Practice Surveys. The baselines being used to develop this project are from these past surveys. This will continue even during this project. Beneficiaries will be engaged during mobilization and sensitization. Project objectives and key activities will be discussed. Feedback provided from these sensitization meetings will be used to tailor project interventions to meet the needs of the beneficiaries. The Project Implementation Committee (PIC) will also be very instrumental in providing feedback on the project. Selection criteria and targeting of beneficiaries will be discussed with the PIC. Feedback from PIC will be used to adjust the selection criteria and targeting. PIC will also be consulted to ensure training and distribution locations are easily accessible and are safe for all beneficiaries. The PIC will also be involved in monitoring project progress. During these monitoring sessions, views from the PIC as well as views of the direct beneficiaries will be used to adjust subsequent schedule and type of activities so as to address the needs expressed by the beneficiaries. For instance, if certain trainings were not well understood, they could be offered again. Feedback on timing, quantity and type of inputs will be received through PDM. This feedback will be used to adjust subsequent distributions. Based on the feedback, the project will adjust on duration of time spend in training or distribution centers. Frequency of training will also be adjusted based on feedback received from beneficiaries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Beneficiaries could be exposed to sexual abuse and exploitation during registration and distribution of inputs. To mitigate this risk, beneficiaries will be consulted on location and timing of registration and distribution of inputs. The training and distribution locations will be located in central areas that can be easily accessible. Distributions and training will also be conducted timely so as to allow beneficiaries time to walk back home before dark. The inputs will also be provided in quantities that beneficiaries can easily transport with having to require money or other man-power to transport which can easily risk women and girls to sexual abuse and exploitation. Owing to the low literacy levels and cultural taboos that emphasize male dominance, women and girls tend to take a back sit even during community meetings. Boys are accorded more chances of accessing education which makes literacy level within women and girls even higher. This exposes women and girls to more exploitations as they may not be aware of their rights and entitlements from project interventions. Women and girls and elderly people may not even be aware of the available complaint mechanisms especially where such mechanisms involve reading materials that may be written in English or other unfamiliar languages. This project will therefore ensure that people in vulnerable groups are specifically targeted and accorded an opportunity to have their views heard and included in the project's decision making process. Available complaints registration and resolution mechanisms will be communicated verbally and explained in the local language. The project will also ensure that common resources like demonstration sites are equitably accessible. Before Demo farms are established, there will be consensus on location and management so that all beneficiaries have unhindered access when they want to access for training and other uses. Demo farms will also be sited in locations that have no dispute on land. The tenure of the Demo farm has to be ensured for the whole length of the project and even after the project life to ensure that beneficiaries can still continue to learn from it.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

WRSS has an integrated approach in service delivery. This includes provision of protection and Health services. WRSS provides primary health care services in both Koch County in Unity State and Fangak County in Jonglei state. In these two counties, WRSS runs both PHCU and PHCC services.WRSS also ensures protection to children, women at risk and GBV survivors. WRSS will also able to work with protection partners to establish referral pathways. Through these referral pathways, children, women at risk and GBV survivors will be able to access information on prevention and management of GBV. WRSS is also able to handle clinical management of rape within the PHCC and where such cases would be beyond WRSS capacity, WRSS is able to refer such cases to other facilities with better capacity.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

This intervention will aim at increasing food self-sufficiency by three months. This will be achieved through provision of inputs and training on improved agronomic practices. Inputs provided will include crop, vegetable and fishing kits. Crop kits will include at least one cereal crop and one legume. Beneficiaries will be trained to cultivate at least one fedan (acre) of crops which can be able to feed one Household for an extra three months. Access to fishing kits will ensure quick turn around in accessing fish which is a major source of proteins especially during the lean season from April to July when food resources are seriously depleted.This intervention will also emphasize on year round farming. Crop kits will be emphasized during the main season while during the dry season, the focus will shift to vegetables and fishing. Vegetables become scarce during the dry season which exposes children to malnutrition. To ensure continuous access to safe and nutritious food, dry season farming will be emphasized with focus on vegetable production through water efficient and climate smart techniques as well as organic pest control measures. Through Education intervention, children will be provided with fortified meals during lunch hour to ensure retention in school but more importantly access to safe and nutritious food. The school feeding will target early childhood development centers and primary schools.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

WRSS integrates intervention to ensure equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to the vulnerable populations. FSL interventions will be conducted alongside WASH interventions. During distributions, the project will continue to sensitize the community on use of appropriate water treatment techniques, the importance of proper hygiene and sanitation. Messaging on COVID-19 Infection Prevention and Control will also continue to be disseminated during these events. Same messaging will also be disseminated during trainings. High standard of hygiene will be emphasized especially during training on food utilization to ensure that food is prepared in a safe and hygienic environment. Beneficiaries will also be encouraged to ensure they have adequate sanitation and hygiene facilities at home. Working with lead farmers, the project will promote construction of gender sensitive latrines with designated use for both males and females cleared labeled. To ensure safety of use, the latrines will have a lock that can be controlled from inside. Similarly, every Lead farmer will be encouraged to have a handwashing facility, made from basic and locally available materials but sensitive to the needs of the vulnerable populations. The handwashing facility will have to be constructed so as to be at a height that can be used for children and any disabled members of the household.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

This intervention will aim at reducing prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children, Pregnant and lactating women. WRSS has organized intervention activities through integration centered around nutrition services. WRSS will organize care givers of malnourished children as well as PLWs through care groups which are groups of 12-15 families with malnourished children as well as PLWs. These Care groups will meet regularly to share experiences on managing malnutrition at community level facilitated by care group supervisors, promoters and volunteers. The Care group supervisors, promoters and volunteers will continue to impart knowledge to these beneficiaries through trainings and mentorship. The same Care groups will be targeted for Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Interventions. They will be provided with inputs and training so as to be able to produce their own food and have sufficient supply at home to prevent relapse of malnourished children as well as PLWs after discharge from the Nutrition program. Similarly, WRSS Nutrition programme working with the FSL program will incorporate kitchen gardens at every Nutrition center to be used for training care givers of malnourished children. Part of the training will involve use of improved agronomic practices as well as cooking demonstrations to impart beneficiaries with knowledge on preparation and utilization of different food staffs including vegetables. These beneficiaries will therefore be able to produce and utilize high nutrient vegetables at home from the inputs and training offered and thus be able to reduce prevalence of global acute malnutrition
