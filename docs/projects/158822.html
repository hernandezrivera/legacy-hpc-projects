Project Summary

The main objective of the shelter coordination is that vulnerable Palestinians who are displaced or at risk of displacement, exposure or protection concerns obtain secure and adequate shelter solutions, through coordination and capacity building of actors working in the shelter sector. 33 and 15 various shelter actors registered at shelter cluster in Gaza and West Bank respectively, including INGOs, NNGOs, UN agencies, and Government counterparts are benefiting from the shelter cluster services including coordination, information management, strategy and standards, monitoring and evaluation, contingency planning, advocacy, communications, and cross-cutting issues. The Project is in line with the HRP 2018-2020 strategic objectives and allows Shelter Cluster to continue its role by ensuring that the IASC cluster core functions are fulfilled and well designed to suit the context in Palestine.

1 - Needs

The Shelter Sector faces different challenges in Gaza, the West Bank, and East Jerusalemthat requires continued collaboration between actors as well as representation to the HCTand donor/diplomatic community to remove barriers and ensure assistance to the mostvulnerable Palestinians. The Cluster collects regular updates from partners to identifychallenges and outstanding needs, as well as using data from cluster and partnerevaluations/assessments, and OCHA data such as IDPs and demolitiondatabases as well as PCBS information. In Gaza, the high unemployment rate and poverty hinder the people to meet their basic needs including adequate housing . the vulnerable people (9,356 families) suffer from multiple displacements due to inability to pay the rental costs, the IDPs (8,550) from the frequent conflicts adopting different negative coping mechanisms , part of them living in store or one room shelter with unhygienic overcrowded conditions. While the pace of reconstruction has made progress, and the Gaza Reconstruction Mechanism has facilitated controlled entry of vital construction materials, continued information tracking, and advocacy is required to promote the completion of the reconstruction caseload. The Shelter Cluster has played a vital role in gathering and triangulating data and sharing this to internal and external stakeholders. Chronic housing shortages and the dire economic situation as a result of the blockade, frequent conflicts, salary cuts, and poverty have compounded concerns for adequate shelter protection for acutely-vulnerable families, as identified in the partners’ assessments (NRC and SIF), in addition to the MPWH and MoSD updated database. Continued efforts are needed to consolidate and improve databases and information sharing to ensure prioritization and efficient use of resources.In the West Bank, the unprecedented rate of housing demolitions during 2019 continues to put Palestinians at risk of displacement, while restrictions on construction due to a range of administrative barriers imposed under the occupation also challenge the living conditions of Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, mainly the Bedouin and herding communities. As a result of the restrictive planning environment and threat of demolition and displacement, over 3,200 households need upgrading or improvements to achieve the minimum protection and acceptable living conditions. Careful coordination is required to address this need. Several agencies are currently working to respond to housing demolitions in the West Bank through emergency shelter provision as well as advocacy and legal advice. Inter and intra cluster work is ongoing to increase predictable and coordinated response, improve technical standards and engage with donors and authorities on finding ways to avoid re-demolition. Like Gaza, the West Bank also faceswinter storms and possible forcible transfer requiring preparedness and continued engagement to implement and improve contingency plans. In both contexts, children are particularly vulnerable, as are the elderly and those who are disabled. Women face a particular burden as primary carers and face specific vulnerabilities accessing their housing land and property rights. A cluster gender and protection focal points were appointed to address different concerns and to provide support and guidance to partners on gender-responsive interventions and protection mainstreaming. Shelter sector coordination across the oPt has contributed to improved collaboration, common standards, and clearer guidance, and will continue to work on greater preparedness and coordinated response. There has been good engagement and coordination with local government which should continue to increase understanding of humanitarian principles and overcome barriers to shelter provision to the most vulnerable.

2 - Activities

NRC has led the Shelter Cluster for several years, bringing together the work across Gaza and the West Bank and supporting the ‘one Palestine’ approach. Throughout the current and previous years, the Cluster has produced regular information updates that have been highly valued and relied upon to track the status and progress of the shelter response and support advocacy and resource mobilisation. The cluster hosts the Demolition Working Group in the West Bank and has coordinated the emergency response to the crisis inGaza, as well as providing a bridge to longer term reconstruction. The Cluster is a respected and relevant forum through which relationships between key stakeholders are strengthened, reliable information is collected and disseminated and common approaches and standards are developed that reduce inequity and provide safe, adequate and dignified shelter to affected populations in Palestine. The overall objective of the program is to ensure that displaced Palestinians and Palestinians at risk of displacement in oPt obtain secure and adequate shelter/NFIs solutions, by continuing to support service delivery through monthly Shelter Cluster meetings, clear and regular information sharing and informing strategic decision making through assessments, strategy development, conducting assessments, developing different standards, activating different working groups to complete specific tasks and ensure stakeholders engagement, The Cluster will continue to provide a forum for data collection, analysis and dissemination; contingency planning and preparedness; and information management support. The Cluster will promote the mainstreaming of protection and gender in cluster activities through dedicated workshops and tailored support from the gender and protection focal points. It will also continue to play a role in M&E of HPF projects, while highlighting gaps and advocating for appropriate solutions to meet the needs of affected populations. The Cluster will focus on technical support to partner organizations in relation to contingency planning, shelter impact, and gender needs. The cluster also aims to support the partners to target andprioritize the poor families and keep monitoring and updating the gap and caseloads for different needs including IDPs. Through the project Shelter Cluster will ensure: • Gender vulnerabilities and protection mainstreaming requirements are identified and addressed through regular support from gender and protection focal points, dissemination and training on the findings of the Gender Analysis, and greater involvement of women’s organizations. • Effective Shelter Cluster coordination mechanisms are maintained through regular meetings and contact with partners • Shelter Cluster information management mechanisms are maintained and improved through regular requests for information and mapping and training/capacity building to organisations • Shelter Cluster advocacy activities and initiatives are continued and strengthened through contributions to the AWG, participation in joint briefings, updates tothe HCT/UNSCO and NRC lobbying • Shelter Cluster fact sheets and publications are produced • Trainings on shelter response are facilitated (as requested by partners, e.g. on HLP, implementing the minimum standards, agency preparedness plans) •Shelter Cluster contingency plans are revised in coordination with main stakeholders and work of the CP&NFI technical working group in Gaza. • Monitoring of the overall shelter response through monthly (Gaza)/ quarterly (WB) updating of the online4Ws and joint field visits to encourage lesson sharing and peer review •Accountability to affected populations is promoted through familiarity and monitoring of partner projects through bilateral field visits, sharing best practice on community participation/beneficiary feedback mechanisms and increased outcome level/impact monitoring in all HRP projects •ensuring the necessary updated assessments are conducted to guide

3 - Indicators

The Cluster in 2020 will coordinate activities related to Strategic Objectives 1,2 and 3 to mitigate the risks and impact of displacement, protect against the effects of severe weather, and build preparedness and response capacity. In addition, activities that focus on ensuring access to a basic level of adequate shelter will contribute to Strategic Objectives 1 and 2. The Cluster will also work to promote the inclusion of cross-cutting issues in the work of its members and continue to pursue transition options through buildingpartnerships and linking with long term actors and government. Accountability to members will be fostered through regular feedback and internal performance mid-year review, while overall cluster and cluster members' performance will be monitored by OCHA through the Periodic Monitoring Review (PMR). The Cluster through the SAG will develop a strategic plan at the beginning of the year (Jan-Feb) to identify partner priorities and layout concrete and time-bound activities in an annual workplan. The cluster will continue to increase knowledge and capacity in national authorities, local NGOs and build relationships with NRC Global and the Global Shelter Cluster in order to access surge support where required and ensure cluster performance oversight. Selected indicators: • # of Shelter Cluster meetings facilitated (6 WB; 6-12 Gaza) • # of 4Ws updates on online system (12 Gaza; 4 WB) • # of information management products shared (12 national) • # of joint advocacy initiatives undertaken (1) • # projects showing increased recognition of gender needs and response in Shelter & NFI sector • Contingency plans updated (1 WB; 1Gaza) The budget includes a team consisting of 2 Coordinators and 1 Information Manager (all fully cluster dedicated). The coordinators to oversee coordination of the current level of shelter activities and fulfill core cluster coordination functions, with some support from the Head of Lead Agency in representation to external audiences and championing the needs of the shelter sector. In addition, in order to achieve the above objectives and provide better analysis, technical assistance to partners, or additionalsupport to the cluster team, the cluster in collaboration with the partners will provide the required technical support and capacity building for the existing and newly joined members.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Shelter and NFIs Cluster activities adopt an integrated protection approach to ensure adequate climate protection, safety and security, privacy and dignity and improved wellbeing for occupants. Protection is measured at the outcome level by ensuring accordance with minimum standards for which it’sreported through improvements in living conditions. In addition, the cluster has developed protection mainstreaming guidance to ensure a “do no harm” approach, non-discrimination and the inclusion of vulnerable populations.Gender equality and gender-responsive programming will be advanced through gender analysis; partners will address the gender needs based on the new GAM system for of men, women, boys and girls through active beneﬁciary engagement with all aﬀected groups (not only community leaders) and disaggregated beneﬁciary data and monitoring indicators and analysis. In 2019, the Cluster team and focal point participated in a training about the Gender in Humanitarian Action (GiHA) training, the tip sheet provides guidance on how to approach and target different groups to strengthen gender integration in shelter projects.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

All shelter projects require community engagement at all stages of the project cycle, starting from needs identiﬁcation, beneﬁciary selection, project design and implementation, and evaluation. In the West Bank in particular, where vulnerable groups are found in high risk areas, community engagement is vital in identifying risks and ensuring collaborative decision-making and informed consent as to the appropriate actions. Communities are increasingly involved in the planning and implementation of projects, enabling selection of the right materials and timing to prevent and/ or minimize exposure of beneficiaries to protection risks. The Cluster will ensure the inclusion of a sound M&E component in all the submitted projects as vetting criteria, to reflect clearly the complaint mechanism and how it will be accessible by beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, and communication channels identified for this purpose. The cluster will ensure the partners comply with main factors to ensure equal and meaningful access to aid through: Treat displaced persons equitably, whether they are living in host-family arrangements, collective centers, are self-settled in urban or rural locations, are self-settled in camps, or are living in planned camps;  Ensure that agencies consider the needs of different ethnic, racial, national or social groups in shelter allocation, ensuring that the quality of shelter is equitable across all groups; Prioritize people and groups on the basis of need – do not prioritize certain groups because their solutions are easier to achieve; Ensure that shelters are accessible and appropriate to all groups and individuals, note in particular concerns of persons with physical or mental disabilities and older persons– where necessary, make individual changes to household shelters, or build all shelters to be universally accessible;  People with disabilities and older persons may not be able to come to distribution/facility sites. Plan additional measures to reach persons with disabilities and older persons; Ensure that bathrooms are accessible to persons with disabilities and older persons, and safe for small children; Ensure particularly vulnerable groups such as female headed households, older persons and persons with disability have equal access to Core Relief Items and ability to transport them; Recognise the joint ownership rights of both male and female heads of household and prevent discrimination; Provide both women and men with the same benefits for their input and their work in construction: e.g. if work is paid, ensure that both women’s and men’s work is paid and is equal; Ensure that unaccompanied older persons are assisted in constructing their own shelters; Develop appropriate response to help vulnerable groups to access aid, transport shelter material and construct shelters. Pay particular attention to assistance needed by persons with disabilities and older persons to ensure construction of accessible shelters.
