Project Summary

According to the ZimVacc 2019 Rural Livelihoods Assessment document, severe acute malnutrition stands at 1.4% and this is of public health significance where zero tolerance to severe acute malnutrition is advocated for. School based nutrition project in the form nutrient dense corn soya blend (CSB) porridge significantly prevents severe acute malnutrition among the infant learners. The clear implication is that if children receive a meal, they will be sent to school, especially when food is scarce at home
Sexual and reproductive health interventions are significant in all communities facing emergencies. Inadequate provision of the above disproportionately affects the poorest and most vulnerable girls and women. According to Policy document number 4 of October 2017 on Menstrual Hygiene Management the subject of menstrual hygiene is a taboo to the majority of families in Zimbabwe. Many poor girls, especially those in their adolescent age find their monthly cycle a nightmare as they have to endure the discomforts of using unorthodox means such as rags, old pants, tissues, grass, leaves and many others as sanitary wear. Most families do not afford requirements of sanitary wear. The United Nations has noted that the cost of sanitary wear is a problem in Africa and estimates that one in ten girls in Africa miss school during their menstrual period and eventually drop out of school. In Zimbabwe, this reproductive health challenge is exacerbated by the economic hardships during emergencies. It is in light of these challenges that the project seeks to address the reproductive health gap of young girls without sanitary wear to reduce school drop-out and for them to get the right to life with dignity.
According to Child Fund Alliance child protection in emergencies is preventing and responding to abuse, neglect exploitation and violence against children caused by natural and man-made disasters Ending children violence has been cited as the 2030 Agenda for children at the End Violence Solutions Summit in Stockholm Sweden. According to the September 2006 Parliament of Zimbabwe Research Department Fact Sheet cases of child abuse are on the increase in Zimbabwe especially in the education sector where more than 700 rape cases of girls aged 5 to 16 were reported in 2005 to the Girl Child Network Zimbabwe. The formation and strengthening of Child protection systems at school level is the most cost effective way to address child abuse cases at local level.
Psychosocial support is a continuum of care and support which influences both the individual and social environment in which people live ( ARC Resource pack 2009). Emergencies create a wide range of problems experienced at individual, family, community and societal levels. At every level emergencies erode normally protective supports, increase the risk of diverse problems and tend to amplify pre-existing problems of social injustice and inequality. According to UNICEF the development, provision and strengthening of psychosocial support services for children in emergencies such as drought is key for the well-being of children. It is important for organizations responding to any crisis to include psychosocial support for boys and girls so as to prevent further harm. It is in light of the above that The J.F Kapnek Trust proposes to carry out psychosocial support activities in the two districts Zvimba and Buhera.
The JF kapnek Trust intends to implement a school feeding program, provision of dignity kits, formation of school child protection committees and Psychosocial support services. School feeding will reach 32 000 boys and girls in infant schools in Buhera and Zvimba districts. There is also provision of 5 000 dignity kits (reusable sanitary pads, soap & towel) to 5 000 vulnerable girls in primary schools. A total of 170 Child Protection Committees will be formed in schools and psychosocial trainings for orphans and vulnerable children.
