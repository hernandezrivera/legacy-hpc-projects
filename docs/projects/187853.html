Project Summary

The proposed project aims to enhance agricultural production, food, and cash for work of affected households and communities in NWSyria especially among the most vulnerable households and communities. The proposed interventions are three interventions:
1-To improve the marketing of wheat products of farmers and support wheat/bread availability in conflict-affected regions;
2. Distribution of free bread in the camps
3. Providing cash-for-work opportunities within the agricultural services provided to wheat farmers.
2500 farmers will benefit from the distribution of wheat inputs, including seeds, fertilizers, pests control materials, and harvesting costs, among which 500 farmers, will receive TOT and capacity building on wheat crop integrated management through the establishment of 20 farmer field workshops in the targeted areas.
320 cash-for-work workers will be selected to implement activities related to firefighting and integrated pest management
SADAD intervention will be aligned with the wheat crop calendar and improve the purchasing power of 1000 households. 2000 tons of wheat seeds will be purchased; 800 tons of wheat will be used as seeds for the next season’s planting, after sifting and sterilizing. The other 1200 tons of purchased wheat will be supplied to the contracted mill to be ground, and, as a result, 960 tons of flour will be obtained and supplied to the bakery contracted by SADAD. Consequently, bread for 2500 households can be produced and distributed free of charge for 12 months. The remaining amount (240 tons) is used as It will be used to pay for wheat milling.
The project falls under planning, as no funding has been secured for it so far

4 - Needs

SADAD conducted Wheat value chain assessments in proposed locations. The main purpose was to get a better understanding of the respective domestic agricultural value and supply chains and the challenges which in particular smallholders are facing along the chain the HNO 2021, the situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated with an estimated 14.2 million people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68.6% of the total population. Furthermore, all 1.7 million people living in camps are deemed to be 100% food insecure.The estimated sector PiN for 2021 - of food insecurity plus those at risk of food insecurity - has increased from 9.8 million to 14.2million. The evidence highlights an alarming deterioration, caused by a combination of factors including currency depreciation and associated loss of purchasing power, considerable hikes in food prices, and the Covid-19 pandemic.The results of the national assessment showed that 50% of Syrian households reported spending at least 75% of their income on food, while 28% are adopting crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies.Subsidized bread prices have continued to increase throughout 2020. The increase in the price of bread has been caused by the Syrian currency depreciation as well as the wheat shortage in the country. In NWS, prices of bread have also tripled for a bundle of bread and only 58% of the population's minimum bread needs were met in 2020.
