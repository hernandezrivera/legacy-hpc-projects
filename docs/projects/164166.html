4 - Needs

Up to one million IDPs are residing in last-resort sites in the north-west Syria communities are over burdened as they host more IDPs than resident populations. There is a dire need for multisectoral response specifically NFIs to ensure access and availability to basic services. Within these affected populations. The MSNA data also shows that female-headed households, youth, elderly and people with functional difficulties have reduced access to basic services including shelter as well as reduced livelihood opportunities and income that supports access to NFIs. Muzun runs the IDP Site Monitoring Team SMT which is focusing on collecting timely, holistic and precise information on all IDP movements in Aleppo and Idleb governorates. This project will support and enhance the proposed NFI distribution by providing regular new IDP arrival data on timely basis.- The total number of IDP families in need reached 1000 families, it is 6,000 individuals.The percentage of IDP families in need in Jarablus 73%, while in Marma Elhajar was 11%, in Jamel was 8%, and in Yusef Elbeik was 7.5%. As for age divisions, the percentage of children under 18 reached 42%, while the percentage of women reached 28%, the percentage of males was 30%, while the percentage of people with special needs reached 0.1%. 50% of the challenges that hindered IDP families market access are financial obstacles.As for the displaced, these six regions (Salqin - Harim - Kafr Deryan - Eskat - Qourqeena - Kafr Hum) have received a huge number of displaced people which theyare seeking safety away from their homes and their families.The total number of families was 14,818 IDP families / 86000 individuals.As for age divisions, the percentage of children under the age of 18 reached 48%, while the percentage of women reached 27%, the percentage of males was 25%, while the percentage of people with special needs reached 1%. Most of the needs according to age groups were NFIs.

5 - Implementation of activities

1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs (e.g. mattress/mat, plastic sheet, blankets, jerry can, kitchen set, solar lamps).This activity will contribute in life-saving and life-sustaining NFI support as rapid response in Northern Syria to the newly arrivals, as following: - Supplying the items will be done by Contracting with suppliers. Shipping the items to Syria as a preposition. - Monitor the context . - In case any emergency a RNA will be done within few days. - Coordination with the other humanitarian actors.The local communities also play a good role in the safety and security of MHD team, we coordinate with them during the movement of the team and the items. 4. Distribution Process: distribution was done in coordination with local councils and local communities in the targeted locations. The process including the final list of beneficiaries and their location, informing the beneficiaries about the date of distribution and the items that will be distributed , beneficiaries arrangement during distribution. MHD distribute the items according to the list that they have collected prior distribution, they call beneficiaries by names, check their IDs and let them sign the beneficiary list. All collected beneficiaries will be shared later with Gaziantep office to be checked and archived. 5. Monitoring Process: monitoring is taking place during all project phases through a direct follow up of all project activities by the program department based in Gaziantep and by the related project team. Monitoring is done by follow up meeting with team based in Syria and field visits of senior staff to the distribution sites.Muzun will have a team of trained enumerators (both male and female) conducting house-to-house assessments and distributions with oversight from the Programme Manager. A PDM will be conduct within a month after each distribution to monitor acceptability of the items provided and beneficiary satisfaction with the distribution process.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Societal tensions / inclusion and / or exclusion of a specific community group; This will be mitigated by engaging all community ethics groups during project coordination and activities. In addition to forming a field community committee consisting of three MHD field staff from various departments, to promote and support the principle of neutrality for the selected beneficiaries. This measure has been implemented in previous MHD projects and has proven successful and valuable. • Gender-related risks: Women encounter difficulties receiving group; this will be mitigated through;1. Muzun to choose a nearby, safe and easy to access location for kit distribution.2. All NFI distributions have female staff present at the distribution site• Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety) this will be mitigated through;1. Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding.3. Pre-consultations with communities. 4. Ensure access to distribution points by all groups taking into consideration people with specific needs. 5. Application of checklist at distribution sites.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

We will agree to minimum assistance package across sector ,assess and validate any changes before they occur and identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. In other hand, we will ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status and selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed.We planned to standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners and ensure beneﬁciary selection criteria is uniform/agreed among sector members and with the government and is clearly communicated widely within a community and among different groups , whereas this engage with community leaders is pivotal to raise awareness in the community. Finally, we will establish.complaint mechanism at community level, it is one of important point in the project. MHD adheres to all other international minimum standards, including the SPHERE, Inter-Agency Standing Committee, Gender Based Violence, Child Protection minimum standards.This commitment to gender equality is especially critical in complex emergencies, such as Syria, which can reinforce existing gender discriminatory practices and deepen the vulnerabilities of women and girls in society, while also presenting an opportunity to redefine gender roles. Where women and girls experience higher levels of exclusion than their male counterparts, within both their families and their communities.Also for PSEA and Child Safeguarding: MHD has included this policy in all contracts with suppliers, employee contracts and code of conduct and insists on continuous training of teams in Turkey and inside Syria on the policy of exploitation and reporting. MHD has a clear complaint mechanism and a committee to investigate and follow up the complaint in addition to the focal point in Turkey and Syria that will be male and female due to the sensitivity of the subject and hotline to complain directly.
