Project Summary

Under this project CARE is planning to run full package of CMAM & IYCF services in 24 CMAM sites the activities include the following: Provision of CMAM SAM services to treat severe acute malnutrition to 4770 in OTPs, and 715 in SCs, Provision of CMAM MAM services to treat Moderate acute malnutrition to (11989 children 6 - 59 Months, and 6263 Pregnant, Lactating women). 7571 reached with Micronutrient supplementation, and 7,571 beneficiaries reached with FBPMAM,; established 20 MSGs to provision of IYCF services to 11,012 People, men and women. provide basic construction/ rehabilitation to 20 CMAM centers. conduct quarterly nutrition assessment/ MUAC screening in 20 location. training of community volunteers and establish 20 community health and nutrition committees (CHNC).

1 - Needs

Acute and chronic malnutrition have been problems of public health significance in Sudan for the past several decades. The drivers of malnutrition are multi-sectoral that include high income poverty, poor WASH conditions leading to epidemics, limited access to health services, illiteracy and high food prices contributing to inadequate food intake and dietary diversity at household level. Inappropriate feeding practices and cultural norms affect children’s growth and development from an early age. A reported 29% increase in displacement due to floods or inter-communal clashes in 2021 compared to 2020 can exacerbate the main drivers of malnutrition as it disrupts affected communities’, access to health and wash services and therefore increasing risks to disease outbreaks and food insecurity due to limited access to farming land, availability, access to and utilization of food. COVID-19 continues to contribute to loss of livelihoods, disrupting access to food and the health care system. The overall nutrition sector people in need (PIN) has slightly increased by 8.8% from 3.6 million in 2021 to 3.9 million in 2022 with under-five children accounting for 77% and 23% pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in need of life-saving treatment and preventive nutrition responses. To mitigate and prevent morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition, the sector will focus on the immediate life-saving nutrition needs that include: Out-patient treatment and in-patient treatment of severe acute malnutrition, treatment of moderate acute malnourished under-fives and global acute malnutrition among PLWs) in prioritized localities. Preventive nutrition needs are Infant and Young Child Feeding practices, emergency supplementary feeding programme (e-BSFP) for under-fives and PLWs, within the crisis hit populations, Food based prevention of malnutrition (FBPM) for under 2 children and PLWs at the risk of developing acute malnutrition, provision of micronutrient powders to children under 5 years (Home Fortification) and salt iodization (USI; treatment and prevention of anaemia, Vitamin A supplementation, capacity building of partners and system strengthening. Implementing multi-sectoral nutrition sensitive interventions (WASH, Health, Food Security, social protection education) ,. Strengthening evidence generation and analysis through implementing SMART surveys in prioritized localities, improved analysis of nutrition information and operational studies to guide informed decision making and planning are critical. These needs have significant impact on physical, mental and wellbeing of children, pregnant and lactating women, and the community in general.

2 - Activities/Outputs

CARE will support the management of severe acute malnutrition through establishing Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTPs) static facilities (and the integration of OTP into primary health care system. The support will include provision of essential medicines, consumables, equipment, cleaning materials, stationeries as well as provision of training to health and nutrition workers and to CHWs. Training for health and nutrition workers will be provided on outpatient care for malnourished children aged 6-59 months, including admission and discharge criteria, medical treatment, treatment of malnutrition, follow-up and referral processes, surveillance and reporting. CHWs will be trained on outreach, including MUAC screening, referral process, defaulter tracing process, home visits process, health and nutrition messages. Children aged 6-59 months with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and without medical complications will be admitted into the OTP, with the admission and discharge criteria based on the national Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) which is in line with international CMAM guidelines. Medical procedures including physical examination and routine medications will be followed up biweekly until discharge. All malnourished children aged 6-59 months will be de-wormed at the second week after admission, and they will receive vitamin A on the fourth week of treatment per latest international best practices. The nutrition staff will be trained on rapid assessment for mental health of caregivers and will make referrals for appropriate mental support. The nutrition team will also follow up on mothers discharged from hospital after delivery, to help support breastfeeding. A breastfeeding corner will be set up at the CMAM sites, that will provide breastfeeding promotion and support for mothers. Sensory stimulation toys and ways for responsive feeding and caregiving will be incorporated in breastfeeding promotion and support.Nutrition education and IYCF messages will be provided to the mothers of the children receiving treatment. Additionally, education on the use of the ready-to-use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) will be provided to Caregivers once the child is enrolled in the OTP. Once children are cured, they will be referred to supplementary feeding programs. Additionally, Care will strive to provide safe water availability at supported OTPs. To this end, Care will integrate its WASH and health/nutrition services and will ensure that their locations achieve a geographic spread such that OTPs have access to clean water either at the facility level or mobile team, where possible. Care works in collaboration with other health actors and local health authorities, and in close coordination with UNICEF, nutrition cluster, to maintain steady supply chain of nutrition commodities for treatment of SAM. Care will also support nutrition surveillance and reporting through regular collection and analyzing of nutrition data and share the reports and updates with the Ministry of Health, and nutrition cluster. Care will support prevention of chronic malnutrition through support to infant and young child feeding (IYCF). Each health facility will have a dedicated IYCF corner to provide IYCF assessment and support. The midwives or female nurses in supported facilities will provide counselling and information to pregnant women and caregivers of children under two on the importance of exclusive breastfeeding, infant complementary feeding, and balance diet. In addition to that, CHWs and midwives will provide the mothers of children under two with information about the causes of malnutrition. Based on WHO and UNICEF IYCF feeding practices guidelines, the IYCF training will be integrated into the CMAM/nutrition training and a full day will be dedicated to IYCF. Furthermore, the training guideline for IYCF emphasize the importance of referring cases of acute malnutrition to the CMAM/nutrition program.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will be will contribute in environmental and natural resource protection; CARE ensures that 100% of supported Nutrition and Health facilities, are implementing environmental protection measures; reliable sanitation services, safe waste disposal Standard Operating Procedures, and maintaining functioning incinerators. CARE will ensure all the relevant staff (especially nurses and cleaners) are trained in infection control and waste management, using the safety boxes to transport waste to the incinerators. in facilities where there is no incinerators, CIS will construct quality incinerator to ensure proper medical waste management. also CIS is promoting planting of trees at health facilities, and operation areas.Care accompanies its distributions with written materials that provide information and instruction on the use of and consumption of supplementary foods, and if the distribution location/setting/time permits, also be accompanied by IEC materials and/or counselling on a broader set of nutrition messages (e.g. IYCF messages, breastfeeding, hygiene messages, among others), this also ensuring safely disposal of empty sachets.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE will take extra caution in analyzing the protection of at risk groups, including children and girls. Efforts will include: no creation of new risks, address discrimination, analysis of the most vulnerable, safety and dignity in service delivery, orientation and familiarization on humanitarian principles and rights, and promoting empowerment. Furthermore, CARE and partners will exercise principles of protection mainstreaming: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of the communities about the best way to deliver the services and considerations for vulnerable groups, accountability mechanisms and Field visits. CARE and partners will empower communities by training & capacity building & awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consulting them in decision making process, identifying volunteers and establishing and training community committees. Strengthening community protection mechanisms and improving access to services through information dissemination on available services, good selection criteria and identification of the most vulnerable and referrals will be prioritized. Do no harm analysis will be carried for each sector and the activities will be screened not to enhance risks for all especially women, girls and children. staff and volunteers will ensure screening of all children boys and girls during the HH visit and nutrition and health facilities; also Facilities such as latrines and water points, income generating activities and trainings will be analyzed and discussed with at risk groups to ensure that the beneficiaries are protected and that the services are easily accessible and safely usable by all. Protection risks faced by women and girls, and in particular S/GBV risks, will be the specific focus of capacity building for staff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. Staff will also be oriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm and that they are able to respond safely and appropriately to protection cases when they are reported, using a confidential and survivor centered approach to disclosures of violence. An internal mechanism for reporting complaints and ensuring that required measure are in place will be promoted throughout the implementation period
