Project Summary

The purpose of the WASH intervention is to deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services to most vulnerable people and support to water, sanitation and sewage systems to ensure continuous services for affected people in Syria. NRC will respond to the diverse problems and needs faced by vulnerable populations throughout Syria through the delivery of a multi-faceted WASH response. The different components of NRC’s WASH project are intended to contribute to HRP WASH Sector Objectives 1 and 2.
NRC will deliver this project through a direct implementation modality
Geographic areas will be targeted based on the severity and scale of needs, inter-agency coordination at hub-level, and access. NRC will provide assistance to vulnerable conflict and displacement-affected populations, including IDPs, refugees, members of host communities and refugee returnees, and non-displaced households suffering the effects of the conflict. All interventions will seek to prioritize the most vulnerable households. Where appropriate, multi-sectoral vulnerability scoring techniques will be used that integrate vulnerability indicators
This project includes:
- Emergency response through the provision of one-off support as part of an integrated, multi-sector response in accordance with the Emergency Preparedness and Response Plans (EPRPs). Comprises of three Result Areas: Temporary WASH services, household assets (Hygiene NFIs) and support to Education in Emergencies (WASH Services for Temporary Learning Spaces).
- The provision of medium-term shelter support at household and settlement-level to populations living in and out-of-camps. Comprises of two result areas: community infrastructure and services in camps, and household assets (hygiene vouchers)
- The provision of longer-term assistance at household and settlement-level in urban and peri-urban out-of-camp areas. Comprises of three result areas: community infrastructure and services, housing rehabilitation for WASH facilities, and school rehabilitation (WASH Facilities) (see NRC’s Education project submission).

4 - Needs

The 2020 HNO estimates that a total of 10.7 million (53% of the overall Syria population) people who require WASH assistance (people who are at elevated risk of consumption of unsafe water, including people vulnerable due to displacement), in which 28% considered are in acute need. Despite the slight improvements from the past year, the WASH needs continue to exist in many parts of Syria, where access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for IDPs living in IDP sites. An inter-agency assessments indicate high scale and severity of needs in Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor, Al-Hasakeh, Idleb and Aleppo governorates. UN OCHA estimates that up to 42% of surveyed HH are relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet/ complement their water needs and the main need remains access to sufficient quantities of safe water. IDP’s have more difficulties to afford the majority of WASH items, especially female head of household, although they are available in the local market. PwD and Elderlies (17%) lack access to adequate sanitation facilities and together with female-headed households (19%) face more constrains in accessing other WASH services in IDP sites. Sanitation, lack of investment in water safety, distribution of raw untreated water and lack of sewage management have contributed to increased public health risks and which proportion of water borne diseases; at least 85% of sewage is untreated due to dysfunctionality, half of the sewage systems are either not functional or partially functional. And at least 10% of garbage is left in public areas. WASH infrastructure is in critical need of attention to facilitate socioeconomic recovery and addressing the needs of the IDPs and the spontaneous returnees. Schools and their related WASH infrastructure continue to be damaged with an estimated 24% non-functional.

5 - Implementation of activities

NRC will contribute to the inter-agency objective 1 of “Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria”, through the following HRP activities:1.Repair and rehabilitation of water systems; through installation of and/or upgrade to essential water and sanitation services2.Support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems3.Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems; NRC will contribute to addressing settlement-level needs by supporting the rehabilitation/upgrade of community infrastructure and services. NRC WASH programme will support the Education programme with support to water and sanitation services. NRC will contribute to the inter-agency objective 2 of “Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behaviour and practices of most vulnerable people” through the following HRP activities: 1.Distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion; Household Assets (Hygiene Kits with supporting Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials) will be provided through the appropriate modality depending on the particular context; Multi-Purpose Cash, In-Kind or Vouchers/Restricted Cash and/or Hygiene campaigns.2.Improved access to humanitarian lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services; through provision of water-trucking, temporary water storage facilities and temporary latrines with hand-washing-points (with de-sludging where required) delivered in camps/informal settlements where NRC already has a presence and when new displacement over-burdens the capacity of existing infrastructure and services.NRC will continue its housing rehabilitation programme and this will include the upgrade or installation of household-level WASH facilities. NRC’s WASH programme will support the Creating Welcoming Schools initiative with support to WASH facilities and services

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk (1): Lack of sufficient gender modified and disaggregated sanitation facilities within formal/informal camps, as well as lack of sufficient lighting might result in safety risks, particularly for women and girls. Likelihood: High Impact: HighMitigation Measures: 1) Consultation throughout project cycle with diverse representatives of affected population. 2) Increased provision/budget for gender modified and separated facilities. 3) Increased provision/budget for communal lighting gender separated facilities, access routes and other areas. 4) Complaints/Feedback mechanisms in place.Protection Risk (2): WASH activities might cause unintended impact on local markets (e.g. cost inflation) or contribute to 'war economies' and supplier monopoly. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigation Measures: 1) Systematic market monitoring is conducted to understand market dynamics and the impact of programming. 2) Suppliers are chosen based on an extensive procurement process, overseen by Compliance Units.Protection Risk (3): Distributions exclude vulnerable populations in need of assistance due to inability to reach them for registration or missing days of distributionLikelihood: Medium Impact: MediumMitigation Measures: 1) Independent registration and outreach, reducing reliance on gate-keepers, 2) complaints/feedback mechanisms in place, 3) hold multiple days for distributions, 4) include questions related to exclusions in post-delivery monitoring, 5) conduct messaging to those eligible for distributions ahead of the distribution dates

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

-Threats to health, safety and security of staff, partners and beneficiaries linked to conflict or criminal activities: NRC has dedicated Health, Safety and Security staff, both at coordination and filed level, to ensure a close context follow-up and the correct implementation of NRC Security policy and standards. NRC produce regular risk assessments and updated security and contingency plans for all operational areas. All NRC staff receive proper security and safety training.-Limitations from local authorities (Government or oppositions) preventing NRC to access or work in specific areas and to select independently beneficiary or staff: networking with peer organisations through different fora to advocate for unified policy positions, regular meeting with local key stakeholders to reinforce humanitarian principles, transparent recruitment policy and criteria for selection of beneficiaries, clear red lines identified while negotiating with local authorities, careful liaison work.-Non-renewal of UNSC resolution 2165 after 10 July 2020: Continue negotiations with GoS to expand access from Damascus. Keep strengthening LHPs’ capacity to ensure continued operational quality. Scale up internal procurement to guarantee access to supplies. -Neighbouring states push for large-scale return of refugees to Syria (including coerced return): Collaboration and coordination with other NRC country programmes in the region. Development of specific contingency plans. Adaptation of programmes. Advocacy efforts.-Fraud/Corruption/Bribery: Enforcing NRC’s policy of zero tolerance of corruption through the continuous strengthening of procurement and operating procedures, promote the “whistle-blowing” policy, corruption risk mapping, ensure partners implement anti-corruption measures.-Changes in territorial control: good understanding of context, contextual analysis, Security Plans and procedures, contingency planning, community acceptance and good relationship with local authorities
