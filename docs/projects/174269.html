Project Summary

The planned interventions of Plan International's entire Lake Chad Programme are set out in the Lake Chad Programme Strategy 2018-2023. The overall objective of the Strategy is that "Girls and boys in the Lake Chad region are resilient and realise their rights in safety and with dignity". The present program-based project funding focuses on Specific Objective 2 on protection (SPO2): "Improving the protection of girls and boys from violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation in the Lake Chad region". Within SPO2 we have 3 outcomes. Outcome 1: Girls and boys are actively involved in their own protection situation and receive qualitative family care. Outcome 2: Community-based and government-led child protection systems prevent and respond appropriately to child protection and GBV needs. Outcome 3: Girls, boys, women and men have access to adequate services to avoid GBV and child protection incidents and to respond appropriately to incidents. Specifically for the humanitarian intervention to which this programme is limited, 3 humanitarian objectives have been set. 1. Girls, boys and their parents in the Lake Chad region can prevent and/or respond to risks related to child protection and GBV. 2. Community-based child protection mechanisms are able to prevent and/or respond to child protection and GBV risks in the Lake Chad region. 3. Children and adolescents in the Lake Chad region have access to quality services to prevent and respond to child protection and GBV incidents. In addition, this programme will carry out accompanying measures in order to strengthen the collective GBV and CPiE response in the Lake Chad basin region through improved knowledge, capacity and coordination.

1 - Needs

Basic services and infrastructure have been destroyed in northeast Nigeria. 2.5 million children (61% female, 39% male) and 700,000 legal guardians need CPIE services. An estimated 34,000 children need case management services. These include approximately 6,000 unaccompanied children, 5,500 separated children and 15,000 orphans. At least 1.75 million children and 490,000 legal guardians need psychosocial support. Accordingly, a protective environment must be created for children. In addition, the establishment of community-based structures to protect children and those affected by gender-based violence (GBV) must be promoted and referral mechanisms to suitable service centres established and strengthened. In addition, about 1/3 of the children in northeast Nigeria do not attend school. NA, implemented by Plan, recommends the creation of a safe and non-violent learning environment and increasing support for teachers. Another recommendation is the provision of alternative, non-formal educational opportunities for children and young people who do not attend school. A basic recommendation is to improve access to (child) protection and education through an integrated mobile programme approach.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1: Awareness sessions for children and young people on child protection risks, where and how to report child protection and GBV concerns, how to protect themselves from harm, what services are available and how to access them. Activity 1.2 Organisation of age- and gender-sensitive sessions on life skills for adolescents & youth that promote positive coping mechanisms, security and protection, sexual and reproductive health and rights, and gender equality. Activity 1.3: Parenting sessions to strengthen positive educational methods and skillsActivity 1.4: Establishing and supporting networks to support families in order to reduce the social isolation of families and increase social support.Activity 1.5: Strengthen the focused and specialised mental health and psychosocial support for parents and carers Activity 2.1: Mapping the main CP/GBV risks (including the different types of GBV, the circumstances in which they occur and the characteristics of the perpetrator) and existing child protection groups/structures.Activity 2.2: Establish and/or strengthen community-based child protection groups/structures to prevent and reduce CP and GBV incidentsActivity 2.3: Provision of community-based psychosocial support for children and adolescentsActivity 2.4: Establishing and/or strengthening child-friendly spaces (CFS), including safe spaces for young girls.Activity 2.5: Establishment of feedback and complaint mechanismsActivity 3.1: Carrying out rapid needs assessments as requiredActivity 3.2: Development of CP/GBV payment methods and standard operating procedures (SOPs) Activity 3.3: Provision of CP and GBV case management services adapted to gender and age specific needs and capacities.Activity 3.4: Provision of dignity kits for women, adolescent girls and male affected personsActivity 3.5: Capacity building of service providers, including legal and health service providers Activity 3.6: Family livelihood support, including money transfer measures for parents and carers to promote their children's access to protection, education and other services. Activity 3.7: Promoting access to cash for work measures for young girls and boys, income-generating activities/training and other livelihood opportunities for older working-age youthActivity 3.8: Increase the capacity of child protection and GBV staff through training, mentoring and coaching to provide a high quality, gender and age appropriate response to the different protection needs identified.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Plan International shall consider differentiated needs women, men, girls and boys may have and give attention to persons with special needs, such as elderly, disabled or chronically ill people. Disaggregated data is key. Plan International shall work with GBV cluster to put in place a standardized dignity kit in close collaboration with the Protection Cluster during pre/post distribution exercise. Protection considerations, notably the principles of do no harm and non-discrimination will be adhered to during assessments, programme design, while implementing as well as when monitoring and evaluating the project. Thus Plan International will prioritize (1) Safety & security, including land tenure security, and dignity; (2) Meaningful access, including for women, children, persons with disabilities or other persons with special needs; (3) Accountability, for example through community-based feedback mechanism; and (4) Information, consultation, participation and community empowerment.

Plan will liaise with the GBV sub-sector coordination to establish a joint monitoring and performance review mechanism to provide a platform for feedback and collective action in response to emerging issues and for the beneficiaries to give feedback on project implementation.
