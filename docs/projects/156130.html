Project Summary

The project was structured According to the following:
- The First Phase Presentation: Establishment of an agricultural Union
During the first phase, WATAN supported olive farmers in establishing an Agricultural Union providing basic agricultural services for olive trees (250 Farmers)
- The Second Phase Presentation is Production of olive oil and manufacture of table olives (250 Farmers)
- The Third Phase Presentation:
The third phase of the project will continue to support the farmers’ union with the running costs and build their own capacities to produce Extra Virgin Olive Oil, as well as helping to market their products.
Therefore, the following actions will be performed:
- Integrated Pests Management of Olive (IPM):
• RAHMA Union is planning to control the olive pests (Dacus oleae) spreading which play a critical role in olive products quality, by using the IPM approach (Climate prediction stations - Nutrients and Pheromonic Traps - Examination of olive samples - Determine the economic threshold - Decision-making regarding partial or full therapeutic intervention).
• WATAN through technical staff will provide professional training on the related topics mentioned above to ensure delivery of both practical and theoretical information to the Union members.
Nearly 750 farmers benefited in the same mentioned support mechanism from the technical support and services provided to improve the productivity and profitability of their olive farms. At the same time 25 home-based production women benefited from table olives manufacturing services.
Service offered included:
1- Support in harvesting the olives for farmers.
2- Transfer harvested olives crop to the olive mills.
3- Support in olive oil extraction for 500 beneficiaries (through the RAHMA Union).
In addition, 25 women, benefited from assistance to manufacture table olives as an economic empowerment goal to strengthens the most vulnerable women (Households Headed Women).
4- Metallic fertilizers distribution for the new beneficiaries.
5- Provision of pruning service for beneficiaries’ farmers olive trees.
6- Support plowing services for beneficiaries’ fields.

Needs

According to HNO 2019, an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies. Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations. Almost 40 percent of the surveyed households reported to be an IDP or returnee of whom 74.5 percent reported to be displaced for more than 12 months. Among them 478,283 displaced people living in last resort IDP sites/camps are of particular concern as they have limited access to a diversified diet or income opportunities. With an 83 - 89 percent people living below poverty lines , and with cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 – 2016 out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector alone, the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary thus demonstrating the existence of “working poor” in Syria. The worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production in 2017/2018 winter season in Syria. This has come as an additional burden on the prolonged crisis with a shortfall of 1.2 million metric tonnes of wheat to meet domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tonnes. Unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation are affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies. The humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2019 and multiple actions are needed to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, utilization and stability of food are adequately addressed through lifesaving, life sustaining, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions. Emergency response at the early onset of any sudden population movement as well as targeted food assistance and lifesaving emergency agricultural assistance to meet the nutritional and kilocalorie deficits of the most vulnerable population as per food security indicators is crucial across Syria. Agriculture sector is a major contributor to GDP with more than 25/30 percent of Syria’s GDP reliant on this sector, and it is also producing food for more than half of the population. Therefore, saving, restoring and protecting agricultural and related livelihoods and value chains will be critical to increase the self-reliance and strengthen resilience of Syrians.

Protection Risk Analysis

Although all Syrian population has been heavily affected by the current crisis, due to traditional local norms, women are exposed to additional protection related risks. All types of violence are cited as occurring in opposition areas of Syria, with domestic violence, sexual exploitation and child marriage as top three. While sexual exploitation is somehow new, child marriage and domestic violence are often normalized and ingrained in the culture. Women and girls are exposed to structural and societal risks as the lack of safe spaces and public transportation, limited freedom of movement, poverty, unemployment, displacement, stress associated to war, gender role change, lack of documents. All these poses enormous pressure, men feeling they failed in protecting and securing a good life for their families. With men perceiving women as a threat to their control due to gender roles change, domestic violence has increased in Syria. Whereas men actions are explained as a result of stress, women are often blamed for provoking the violence or allowing it to happen. Parental violence against children has also been reported as increasing. Displacement and living in camps are noted as being particularly unsafe environments for women/girls due to the lack of privacy (in tents or in latrines), overcrowding and mixing of people, poverty, financial desperation, and lawlessness.The WoS Protection analysis reveals that 17 sub-districts in Idleb and 23 In Aleppo are facing severe, critical and catastrophic situation regarding protection. While some services are available, GBV cases are less addressed and the assistance is focusing mainly on survival pathway support and less on community-based interventions, mandatory for mind-set change. Women and girls occupy a central role in WATAN’s strategy for 2018-2023. Part of the development approach, WATAN seeks to empower women to become more resilient in the conflict setting while securing their safety at all levels. When designing the project, WATAN has applied all its security protocols to ensure that interviewed GBV survivors and leaders of communities are not exposed to any risk for their involvement. For this purpose, WATAN and its partners have a set of policies and procedures to be respected when working with GBV cases.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Security risks related to the war in Syria:Permanently being informed on the ground situation and avoid the hot zones.2- Not finding the necessary inputs/equipment on the local market:The team has already implemented projects in Idleb governorate and has a database with vendors that can be approached to purchase the needed goods. Priority, however, will be given to local vendors from the communities for stimulating the market and secure any harmful effect of bringing goods/services from other places. If the vendors don’t have in stock the needed quantity, an agreement shall be negotiated for the vendors to supply to a certain deadline all the needed inputs, or inputs/ equipment buying from Turkey.3- Market failure to absorb the production (Olive Oil):Considering exporting alternatives, or Postpone sales process.
