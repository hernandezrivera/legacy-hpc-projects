Project Summary

The overall goal of the War Child programme is to increase the resilience and wellbeing among vulnerable children and youth in North East and North West Syria. Under this action, War Child would respond to urgent Child Protection needs in Idleb and Aleppo, and expand the service provision for highly vulnerable children in Raqqa and Deir Ez Zor through CFS, structured PSS, awareness raising, and developing specialized service capacity within local partner organisations, so that they can deliver a quality case management service.
This prevention and response approach will address immediate and urgent identiﬁed child protection risks while contributing to long-term change. The project aims to reach parents and caregivers as well as local leaders to build a more protective environment for vulnerable children affected by conﬂict.

4 - Needs

Children are among the worst affected by the on-going conflict in Syria. Violence, protracted displacement, and extensive destruction of infrastructure and services has steadily eroded coping mechanisms of individuals and communities and disrupted community protection mechanisms (HNO 2020). To ensure life-saving protection response, sustained support for existing programs and services addressing the immediate as well as medium to long-term needs of IDPs is critical (NWS Readiness and Response Plan, Jan 2019). After the Turkish offensive in October 2019, populations in Raqqa and Deir Ez Zor governorates that were already vulnerable received a new movement of displaced persons, with associated Protection risks. Humanitarian services were temporarily suspended and although response is now back up to pre-crisis levels, it has not been able to expand sufficiently to respond to the increased level of need. 56% of communities in Raqqa city highlighted child labour as a concern, reaching as high as 72% in Deir Ez Zor communities (Reach Assessment, Dec 2019). The OCHA Situation Report 7, 29th January highlights that children who are displaced are particularly vulnerable, due to reduced family and community protection and lack of positive coping mechanisms. Since December 2019, delivery of child protection services have been affected in seven sub-districts in the Northwest, and activities that have been suspended include psychosocial support, life skills, parenting programmes, and case management and referrals. Needs assessments have indicated that 90% of the newly-arrived children are out-of-school, and are in need of psychological first aid (PFA). Over half a million school-aged children (aged 6 to 18 years) in NWS are out of school, 34% of the total 1,712,468 children in the same age group.

5 - Implementation of activities

War Child will support the most vulnerable groups by engaging in identified priority activities. For the first engagement with the affected populations, War Child will focus on life-saving messages and PFA support for children, caregivers and community members, as well as particular support for persons with disabilities. Recreational activities will be established through mobile teams as soon as possible, before transitioning to more structured support where possible in accessible locations (DEALS methodology, combined with SRA and distribution of small PSS kits for children, a CFS where feasible). Awareness raising messages on service mapping, safe identification and referral, and risk education messages will also be integrated into these activities for both children and adults, taking into account gendered concerns. For all field staff (including facilitators and teachers) and before activities begin, War Child will provide training on Safe Identification and Referral, Psychological First Aid, and PSEA. Training will be given on War Child Child Safeguarding policy, to ensure awareness, referrals and compliance across all partners. Child-focused feedback mechanisms, as well as community-based resources, will be put in place, and systematically addressed. Children with specific child protection concerns will be referred to dedicated case workers in each location. Recognising the severe lack of specialised case management services in the targeted governorates, War Child will work with its implementing partners to assess current organisational capacity in the first month of the project, as well as to identify case workers, and provide intensive case management training based on accepted inter-agency standards, SOPs and case management forms. War Child will also build a team of case supervisors, identify any field-level support from other actors, and offer coaching and supervision sessions to the supervisors.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Sexual exploitation or abuse - Staff safety incident - Burnout or poor health of staff due to activities - Data breach Mitigating measures: - Partner development and use of organisational Duty of Care policy - Access and effective use of Staff care personnel on War Child staff, including capacity building for partners - IT security measures and training on procedures to prevent data misuse - Contingency planning for loss of access through change of control or non-renewal of cross border resolution. As mentioned in the Protection sector PRA, each of the project locations will be identiﬁed and set up using War Child CFS guidelines, which will also be used to train partners on minimum standards for assessing the risk and establishing safe spaces. The spaces will be monitored with contextualised tools, including assessment using the War Child CFS checklist. To ensure they are ﬁt-for-purpose, the spaces will be lightly rehabilitated where required and then equipped with minimum standard safety equipment, winterisation materials, stationery and recreational kits. In all cases War Child will ensure the sites and their surrounding entry and egress points for families and children alike are cleared of ERW. To increase equitable access to the facilities, an inclusion checklist will be applied, so that the needs of disadvantaged groups are considered into programme design (speciﬁcally IDPs, CwDs, adolescents, and girls). The checklist will assess accessibility and meaningful participation in the needs assessment; outreach activities and inclusive development of activities; engagement of marginalised groups; and representation in monitoring and evaluation activities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

As the operating environment continues to be volatile in both the Northwest and Northeast, strong contingency planning is a necessity. This is reﬂected in the budget, with a shared contingency across both hubs in case of changes in control and access. As part of the contracting process, emergency response plans will be reviewed with partners to cover staff and information security, Duty of Care policies and ﬂexible programming in case of changes to the context. If relevant, a shared information platform on risks and security reports will be established, at the request of the local partners. The remote management modality requires a strong commitment to partner operations, and support to them in access negotiations, humanitarian principles, and advocacy. As laid out in partner SOPs, senior partner staff in Syria have authority to make any emergency decision about suspending activities, and/or temporarily closing centres or any other protective action on a short term basis. If attacks occur in the local area, partners will directly suspend activities, ensure safe evacuation of any centres, and assess the situation on a daily basis. Relevant safety measures will be put in place at the establishment of the project (safety equipment, evacuation planning, communications protocols, etc). Travel to and from project sites has been identiﬁed as a key risk for both affected populations and project staff. From the project design phase, partners have reduced the amount of transportation needed during the implementation of the project to the minimum, locating services close to target communities as far as possible. Largescale public gatherings are to be avoided, and invitations to programmatic events are low proﬁle to maintain focus on humanitarian objectives
