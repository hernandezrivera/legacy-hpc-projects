Project Summary

The integrated protection project aims to meet life-saving acute, urgent, immediate Child protection (CP) , Gender Based Violence (GBV) and protection needs of individual among newly internal displaced persons( IDPs) and their hosting communities in North Darfur,(ND) Central Darfur,(CD) White Nile(WN) , Northern ( NS),River Nile (RN) and Khartoum (KRT) states after the clashes between Sudan Army force (SAF) and Rapid Support force (RSF) on April 2023 The project will strengthen community-based prevention mechanisms and improving protection response systems through : provision of specialized services and enhancing the capacity of the local communities on protections risks, preventions, concerns and needs . The project will provide of mental health and Psychosocial support services (MHPSS)including provision of Psychological first aid, clinical counselling and recreational, social and life skills interventions for vulnerable persons with special needs (PSNs) with focus on unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) ,GBV survivors , elderly , and persons with disabilities (PwDs). This will be provided at individual level such as through provision comprehensive case management, FTR or referral services at group level through provision of structured group sessions, peer to peer or /and recreation and life skills sessions .To enhance the local technical capacity, the project will rain service providers on GBV, in particular, health care providers and social workers and establishment and training of 12 community based protection networks and 12 CB feedback and complaints committees , and support the running and operationalization of 6 women centers
To ensure dignified response the project will , procure and distribute of dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age as well as provision of mobility aids, crutches clothes etc to PSNs , The project will conduct protection monitoring Improving community awareness on protection violations, risks and prevention through conducting public awareness campaigns protection‐related issues, during emergencies and availability of services.

1 - Needs

This project aims to provide protection support and create protective environment for extremely vulnerable newly IDPs and host communities in 6 states KRT,ND and CR which witness the clashes and RN,WN and NS are among the biggest states hosting IDPs from the current crisis. With the conflict in the country entering its second month, the displacement and death are increasing on daily basis. Bilateral discussions during rapid assessments conducted by NADA teams in ND,CD,WN,KRT and RN after April 15th highlighted that existing community support networks and coping mechanism needs additional capacity to respond to the community protection . NADA assessments have revealed that children especially UASC, single female household , elderly and people with disability (PWDs) are considered among the most vulnerable groups and face major difficulties in accessing humanitarian services. There are no any specialized support mechanisms or social networks for supporting children and GBV survivors and disability in project areas. Thus, they are left with very limited support. Family separation and recruitment among children and strangely in ND among young girls is widely reported. In some cases, vulnerable families consent or even encourage child soldier recruitment as it offers them some degree of financial stability. CP needs are critical and the area is underserved. Children are exposed to different forms of abuse, negligence and violations as the militia forces are actively campaigning for child recruitment among the rebel forces in the project areas. 73 % of women and girls respondents mentioned that they have been exposed to some forms of GBV. More than 86 % of GBV survivors are children. However, only few incidents have been reported due to the stigma ,social norms and fear from the protesters . Problems faced by victims of GBV are exacerbated for women living in IDPs gatherings, remote areas in Khartoum as they do not have access to various forms of assistance. Services for GBV survivors are extremely limited in these settings. In addition, a significant number of survivors do not seek GBV support where services exist due to cultural and social stigma, and fear of retaliation.. In addition, lack of sustainable income and jobs pushes many children to work in markets, sell various commodities in the local markets or work in farms. Child labor, sex slavery is reported in White Nile and River Nile .This present significant protection risks for children, especially girls. NADA assessment reports in RN and NS estimate that 83 % of children of UASC are considered child labors . This becomes even more significant in the context of broken traditional community-based value and protection systems, which leaves minors unprotected and extremely vulnerable when they are exposed to abuse. Displacement has also left considerable number of UASC unverified in particularly in KRT ,CD and ND Darfur. There are more hundreds of traumatized / stressed women and children left without PSS or recreation spaces

NADA will implement the project activities in close consultation with SCCW, MoSHA, CBPNs as well as FCPU Output 4: Improve the measures in support of protection from and response to PSEA.Planned Activities: [Child Protection]: Provision of mental health and Psychosocial support services (MHPSS)including provision of Psychological first aid, clinical counselling and recreational, social and life skills interventions for 500 children and adolescents, Provision of case management services and FTR services to 120 UASC, Provision of structured group sessions, reached 300 families with risks factors through home visits, parent/ caregiver support on child protection and conduct 24 community based awareness campaigns on child protection‐related issues, MRE, prevention from separation during emergencies and availability of services.[GBV]: Train 100 service providers on GBV, in particular, health care providers and social workers , provision of comprehensive case management services to 300 GBV survivors, provision of PSS support through recreation sessions and support the referral of 500 GBV Survivors, procurement and distribution of dignity kits to 3,000 vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age, running and operationalize i of 6 women centers and conducting 24 community based awareness‐raising campaigns against GBV and SEA.(General Protection]: Provision of MHPSS support through group sessions peer to peer, mutual PSS support, recreation, music and art therapy targeting 1500 individuals, provision of referral services to 250 identified most vulnerable families/individuals to specialized services, provision of mobility aids, crutches clothes etc to 300 PSNs , conduct protection monitoring through establishment of 12 community based protection networks and 12 CB feedback and complaints committees , Improving community awareness on protection violations, risks and prevention through conducting 24 campaigns

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

NADA recruited and will continue to recruit suitable project staff from targeted communities, staff will be involved and fully dedicated to implement and follow up project activities. Project staff will be provided by two days training on PSEA, GBV, protection monitoring and reporting ,code of conduct to avoid potential risks of violence, exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries. The integrated community based approach which will be adopted during the implementation , promotes active engagement and participation of all community segments at all phases to ensure community by-in and sense of ownership. NADA team confirmed that composition of CBPNs considers balanced representation of gender, age, social status and minority to improve t decision making and allow the voices for these groups. CBPNs already received training on different, GBV and protection risks, needs and concerns to be able them to contribute to sustainability of protection education and capacity among local community. This will further support self-protection capacity to be embedded deeply in the community coping mechanisms. Following participatory and community based approaches, targeted communities will be involved in discussion, consultations and decision regarding planned activities. Through this, project intends to ensure full participation and strengthen the ownership of communities. This will contribute to provide the targeted communities with concrete plate forum to identify their concerns, needs, challenges and needed actions to improve the protective environment. APP is core activity, additionally applying Do No Harm principle to clearly done in this project through involvement and inclusion of the communities and hosting communities with equal access to direct assistance based on vulnerability assessment .As part of the Do No Harm approach, NADA will provide training to all project team members and CBPNs on PSEA and code of conduct. This will help to mitigate risk of harassment, abuse and other violation to be committed by staff members against the beneficiaries. NADA will consider the dignity and confidentiality of beneficiaries, particularly when delivering CP and GBV services in particularly provision of case management. NADA will strongly prioritize the best interest of children/ GBV survivors/beneficiary by following protocols and standards of service delivery for the identified GBV cases especially child survivors. The information about these cases will be handled and managed anonymously and confidentially. Counseling for GBV survivors will be done in a designated private space with maximum confidentiality. In addition, the project team will conduct regular consultation with CBPNs, community leaders/ members and local authorities and receive their feedback about project delivered services through complaint and feedback mechanism to identify any negative consequences that might be cause as result of the project implementation.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Project will be implemented on participatory and community based approaches . Regular meetings and information sharing activities will be delivered to community to inform about project services. Targeted communities will continue to have equal access, participate and benefit from project services. Project will highly consider best interest of women and children when handling, sharing data and information about identified cases. Consultations with local community and line ministries will be integral part of this intervention. Project will hold consultations for full engagement of partners as an entry point for smooth implementation with clear exit strategy. Protection interventions have always been sensitive issue for authorities and communities. NADA has incorporated lessons learn from previous intervention in relation to AAP. it has been identified that extensive consultation with local authority and community as is instrumental for smooth and effective project implementation. This is considered as an area of strength for NADA, as all designed intervention have focused on this element as success factor for achieving intended project objectives. Project will also ensure constant feedback and open channels for receiving complaints. NADA will train 30 CRCs whom will receive and respond to complaints and feedback including SEA and abuse complaints .A simple written procedure will be developed, this will be coupled with a complaint box to enable submission of private, confidential or sensitive complaints. A specific tool (Beneficiary Satisfaction Form) has been designed for this purpose. Process, time for response etc should be clearly communicated to beneficiaries. Multi purpose community / Women center and NADA offices are equipped with complain box. Training of CBPNs on protection and GBV , PSEA and how to report its cases will highly contributed to APP. Community based PSS sessions will be conducted by committees and community members. This will ensure that beneficiaries’ opinion are incorporated in future intervention. Information sharing will be articulated in different ways such as readable, watchable IEC material .Awareness raising messages will be disseminated in accessible ways for PWDs. Message will be field tested among targeted groups prior to printing Project will apply Do No Harm approach through considering these sensitivities for successful project implementation without making any harm on communities. To achieve that, NADA has conducted analysis to identify any possible harm that to be caused by the intervention. The project team understands that many communities in the project areas are diversified and explored ways to unite the community; one of the ways is to promote participation of people from various ethnic backgrounds together as members of community networks, with aim to increase coherence and connectedness.
