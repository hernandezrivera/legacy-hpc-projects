Project Summary

The project will seek to mitigate the major causes of morbidity and mortality in Mayendit County. It will improve availability of healthcare services to 14,883 women, children and men. The project targets the most in need persons whose access to access health care services has been comprised as a result of multiple crisis. 14,883 persons will be targeted there will be 5,719 host community, 2,631,IDPs, and 6,533 returnees respectively.
The breakdown according to gender , age and disability will be as follows. 12% of the targeted will be people with disabilities translating to 1,786 people. Children under five years 1,827 ( Males - 877; Females - 950 ) Children Five to 17 years 4,890 ( Males - 2,347; Females - 2,543 ) Adults 18 to 60 Years 5,410 ( Males - 2,597; Females - 2,813 ) Adults over 60 years 2,295 ( Males - 1102; Females - 1,193 )
The project will support provision of critical lifesaving interventions aligned to the Humanitarian Response Plan criteria and the South Sudan Basic Package of Health and Nutrition through a network of 3 health facilities (2 PHCUs and 1 PHCCs) The project activities will focus on prevention and case management of epidemic-prone diseases such as malaria, pneumonia, acute watery diarrhoea,
cholera, and measles through both static and outreach services; provision of essential medicines and medical supplies accessed
through the core pipeline; capacity building of staffs through onjob mentorship, disease surveillance and routine monitoring of health information through strengthening the EWARS/DHIS2 system.
The clinical package will include; Health education and promotion, curative outpatient and inpatient services, routine immunization including supplementary immunization activities focusing on measles and polio, Maternal New-born and Child Health Services (MNCH), and HIV/TB services including e-MTCT and referral for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS).

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face severe health crisis due to multiple factors. About 8.9 million people in the country need one or more forms of humanitarian assistance, especially in the counties most affected by IDPs (Internally Displaced Persons), returnees and disease outbreaks(Measles). The health system in South Sudan is unable to cope with the emergencies and providelife-saving services. It relies heavily on international aid and faces many challenges, such as lack of funding, staff, medicines, and equipment. The people suffer from hunger, violence, and poor health. Women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities are the most vulnerable groups. They have limited access to health care and face high risks of death and illness In 2024, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistanceIn 2024, Health Cluster partners are responding to multiple and concurrent disease outbreaks, including measles, yellow fever, poliovirus, meningitis, hepatitis E and cholera. The ongoing conflict in Sudan and reported disease outbreaks such as cholera in Upper Nile State and Sudan puts the county at risk of imported cases. The County has also experienced outbreaks of measles in 2024.The coverage for immunizable diseases in the county is low with HMIS data estimating Measles and Pentavalent vaccination coverage at less than 50% in the county. The Health Sector Transformation Programme (HSTP) is currently supporting 70 per cent of health facilities leaving out 30% of health facilities , some of which are in hard to reach and in inaccessible places.In 2023, a total of 59,356 individuals (8,840 households) were displaced by floods, while in 2024 a total of 17,000 individuals (2,833 households) were newly displaced, bringing the number of internally displaced people in the county as a result of floods to 39% of the population.Most health and water sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities, crops, and animals are submerged, heightening households exposure to food insecurity and health hazards. In Mayendit South, movement and delivery of services or supplies is nearly impossible, with small canoes made from the trunks of palm trees being the available means of transport. Leaders in the county listed maternity services and medical outreach as major needs.A recent health assessment in the County found that only 5% of households lived within a 5-kilometer radius of a health facility. The remaining households had to travel further to reach a health facility, with the most common distances being 6-10 kilometres (45%), 11-20 kilometres (35%), and 21-40 kilometres (15%).

2. Project activities

The Project will focus on providing emergency health services in line with the South Sudan Health Humanitarian Response Plan. The focus will be on providing primary health care that is aligned to the South Sudan Basic Package of Health and Nutrition through 3 health facilities located in hard to reach areas and not supported by the current HSTP project. (2 PHCUs and 1 PHCC). The package of services to be provided will include 1. Conduct Emergency preparedness and response and ensure all data will be submitted in the EWARS/DHIS platforms. 2 Provide case management for common causes of morbidity. Support will include the provision of incentives, medicines, medical equipment, HMIS tools, and job aids. 3 Conduct routine and supplementary immunization activities. RHR will support the health workers to provide both routine and supplementary immunization activities targeting vaccine-preventable diseases. 4 provision of hand washing facilities, training of health workers in Infection Prevention and Control, and provision of waste segregation buckets. In addition, hand washing will be promoted at all health education sessions. 5 Strengthen sexual and gender-based violence services through training in CMR: The project will work closely with the nutrition departments and ensure the establishment of a robust referral and follow-up mechanism to Strengthen WASH in health facilities. The project will support the establishment of gender-disaggregated pit latrines, construction of incinerators and placenta pits, provision of handwashing facilities, training of health workers in Infection Prevention and Control, and provision of waste segregation buckets. In addition, handwashing will be promoted at all health education sessions. The health workers will be provided with CMR kits received from UNFPA, and GBV registers for easy documentation. (g) Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS). The project will ensure that special groups such as old people, women, and people living with disabilities are reached and are using services. Tools such as the disability tracking tools will be used to monitor service uptake and the project will also Conduct health awareness and promotional activities.
