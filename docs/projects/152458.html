Project Summary

Socioeconomic situations in both Ulang and Nasir have remained dire since 2013, with various escalations of security situations last in March 2018 which resulted in population displacements. With return of calm and continued clashes in other counties, Ulang and Nasir have hosted over 40,000 IDPs. This situation is worsened by natural disasters such as flooding, drought and diseases, leading to increase in suffering and displacements of the already vulnerable populations. This is demonstrated in the high GAM rates for both counties i.e. 21.8% and 20.1% for Ulang and Nasir respectively.
UNKEA operates only 28 OTP sites (14 per county) and 2 SC in Nasir to serve a population of 288,704 and 95,880 in Nasir and Ulang respectively. This is inadequate and UNKEA intends to increase to 34 OTP sites. OTP sites do not provide much needed WASH and FSL services that would improve the well-being of children and women.
UNKEA will scale up service provision by providing the most critical interventions such as Treatment of SAM among children 6 – 59 months (2,540 targeted); Treatment of MAM among children 6 – 59 months (6,994 targeted); Treatment of 2,546 PLWs with MAM; Training 34 health and nutrition workers on CMAM; Training 34 Nutrition and Health workers on IYCF/MIYCN counselling; Conduct individual IYCF/MIYCN counselling for PLWs and caregivers of children 0 - <23 months; Provide Nutrition education session to IYCF/MIYCN support groups in the community; Monitor availability of nutrition supplies at nutrition sites; Establish demonstration gardens at all nutrition sites for on-site dietary diversity demonstration; Screening/testing and treatment of all SAM children in the OTP sites for Malaria; Conduct safety assessment at nutrition sites to identify and address women and children’s protection and safety needs including GBV referral path ways; Provide WASH kits to children with SAM in all OTP sites; Establish child/mother friendly spaces in all (34) OTP/TSFP sites; and Provide ECD kits for all OTP sites to enhance mental and physical development of children in the programme.
The project will be implemented with consideration of best practices and key interventions for gender/protection mainstreaming, and in total consideration of community participation.
All activities will be reported as per the timelines of the Nutrition cluster using existing NIS system i.e. bi-weekly, monthly and quarterly as needed. Data collection tools, monitoring tools, and reporting tools of the cluster shall be used.
Total estimated budget to cover the 2 counties is $800,000 (Eight Hundred Thousand US dollars).

Needs

In early March 2018, fighting between armed actors in Ulang and Nasir resulted in population displacement (>20,000 IDPs). Since then, the Counties have remained relatively stable and continue to host over 40,000 IDPs from the neighboring counties. Natural resource access also drives fighting between communities in the lean season as others come to graze and raid cattle. Nasir and Ulang Counties are affected by major hazards that include; drought, seasonal flooding, animal and human diseases, and armed conflict. These areas remain under-served and largely inaccessible. The already vulnerable host communities are overstretched and unable to host the IDPs who are in dire need of food assistance and other basic services. It is anticipated that with the signing of the peace agreement between government and opposition, calm shall return which would cascade to the return of refugees from neighbouring Ethiopia. This therefore shall overstretch the ability of the host community to cope up with various shocks that face them. Nasir and Ulang County are IPC phase 4 (Emergency level). Further, food security outcomes projected by FEWS NET for the period between June and September 2018 indicated that Nasir and Ulang would still remain in emergency phase.Nasir has an estimated population of 288,704 in 2018, with 54,854 children under 5 years, and 23,096 Pregnant and Lactating Women while Ulang has an estimated population of 95,880 in 2018, with 18,217 children under 5 years and 7,670 Pregnant and Lactating Women. The FSNMS round 20 surveys conducted in July 2018 revealed GAM rate 21.8%, SAM 5.8% and MAM 16.0% for Nasir county meanwhile in Ulang, the March 2018 SMART survey revealed GAM rate 20.1%, SAM 2.7%, MAM 17.4% and CMR 1.32% (emergency) among children under 5 years and 7.8% for PLWs. In both counties, morbidity among children under 5 years of age is high with diarrhoea accounting for 24.5% of total morbidity due to common ailments. From the existing health programme data from January to September, diarrhoea accounts for 19% of all common childhood ailments.UNKEA provides nutrition services in 14 OTP sites in each county to be increased to 18 in Ulang (Ulang, Ying, Doma, Yomding, Kuich, Ruplet, Rupbuot, Bimbim, Makak, Riang, Barmach, Nyangore, Rirnyang, Waga, Duk, Toaloare, Nyarkueth, and Torkiel) and 16 in Nasir (Jikmir, Kuetrengke, Kiechkuon, Mading, Roam, Kierwan, Maker, Dinkar, Mandeng, Torpuot, Lualeyak, Gurnyang, Malek, Gaireng, Dhekdhek, and Homkor). Due to low funding, the sites are understaffed hence provision of a holistic nutrition services is a great challenge therefore gaps exists in counselors for IYCF/MIYCN and GBV/protection per OTP site. During displacements (IDP, returnees), provision of IYCF-E services is a challenge due to staffing gaps and additional resources required.WASH activities are not being provided at the OTP sites, this increases the likelihood of relapses due to WASH related problems among SAM children. This explains high diarrhoea rates and other related problems due to the complete lack of the much needed WASH services such as hand washing facilities like soap & functional latrines at the OTP sites.The OTP sites do not provide screening/testing of children with SAM for malaria using the RDTs, as such opportunities for detecting missed infection of malaria are missed and this hence deteriorates the conditions of the children and hence prolonging their stay in the programme.Due to the complete lack of demonstration sites where mothers of children with SAM could learn how to better feed their children on nutrient reach food stuff especially vegetables and other nutrient reach foods in both Ulang and Nasir, compliance to provision of alternative foods during lean session has been difficult as such dietary diversity for children during their first 1000 days is difficult.Child & mother friendly spaces and ECD kits are not available in all OTP sites for mental and physical stimulation of children.

Activities

UNKEA will increase the number of nutrition sites from the current 28 sites to 34 sites. Ulang will have 16 sites and Nasir 16 sites. All the sites are located within existing health facilities. Other programmes such as FSL, WASH, Education and health under UNKEA shall be integrated and ensure they target nutrition beneficiaries as well. The FSL staff shall support the establishment of kitchen gardens in all nutrition sites to act as a demonstration site for mothers of children in the nutrition programme. A separate space shall be provided within the premises of the sites. Another space shall be given within the premises for erecting a shade for children and their mothers to feed and play. This child/mother spaces shall be equipped with ECD kits that shall be used by children in the programme in order to support stimulation of the malnourished child. All sites will have a counselor for GBV/Protection and IYCF. During screening, children will be tested and treated for malaria. The list of activities which UNKEA shall implement is as below:• Treatment of SAM among children 6 – 59 months• Treatment of MAM among children 6 – 59 months• Conduct micro-nutrient supplementation i.e. Vitamin A and de-worming every after 6 months to children 6 – 59 months.• Conduct quarterly mass MUAC screening (active case finding) in the community to identify cases, and refer for admission into programme. • Training health and nutrition workers on CMAM• Training Nutrition and Health workers on IYCF/MIYCN counselling• Conduct individual IYCF/MIYCN counselling for PLWs and caregivers of children 0 - <23 months, • Provide Nutrition education session to IYCF/MIYCN support groups in the community,• Monitor availability of nutrition supplies at nutrition sites,• Establish demonstration gardens at all nutrition sites for on-site dietary diversity demonstration, • Screening/testing and treatment of all SAM children in the OTP sites for Malaria,• Conduct safety assessment at nutrition sites to identify and address women and children’s protection and safety needs including GBV referral path ways, • Provide WASH kits to children with SAM in all OTP sites,• Establishment of child/mother friendly spaces in all nutrition sites• Equipping nutrition sites with ECD kits for child stimulation.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Beneficiaries will be informed by the use of awareness creation activities using portable public address systems, community nutrition volunteers and MSGs during home visits, defaulter tracing, and at the OTP sites. Community local authorities will be used to pass information about available services and exact locations for the services including days as deemed necessary. Protection and GBV sensitive issues and services shall be communicated through the same channel and especially during service provision beneficiaries identified to be needing counseling services are directed.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All nutrition messages, shall as per the available guidelines such as counseling charts/cards for IYCF, WASH key messages, which are context specific and tailored to suite the target group. All key messages shall be translated to the local language which can easily be understood by the general community.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

UNKEA has zero tolerance to any form of sexual exploitation and abuse by anyone with affliation to UNKEA. UNKEA has rolled out its policy on sexual exploitation and abuse, and staff have signed to adhere and follow both UNKEA's policy and humanitarian code of conduct which are guiding principles and documents to that effect. UNKEA understands that any breach of these standards have negative impacts on the operations of the organization. All programmes of UNKEA are designed to ensure there is distinction between staff providing services and beneficiary reducing the chances of exploitation of beneficiaries as services are provided to the general community during agreed upon times with the community.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

With the existing 14 OTP sites in each county which all have CNVs and MSGs who are used for community level activities, feedback from the community about what they would like UNKEA to consider for service provision was used to access information from the community. Community members ideas are also shared during individual counseling sessions and group sessions where ideas are generated and documented based on the existing difficulties facing the community.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

UNKEA has an established feedback mechanism from the community to the organization. The community people act as monitors to the activities of the organization and staff executing the services delivery to the community are monitored by the community members. In an event there is any form of concern, community members can directly raise their concerns through the field coordinator, local authorities and suggest immediate attendance to the issues raised. UNKEA upon receipt of such complaints from community about their concerns, acts quickly to alleviate the problem and appreciate the whistle blower, informant, etc. This has helped to curb the rampant theft of supplies from the stores to zero since staff or community members who have been involved in such acts have been sorted equally.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will use the existing feedback and complaint mechanism established to support in monitoring and evaluation of the service delivery of the projects deliverable to the community. At mid year community consultations/assessments shall be conducted to ascertain safety/protection needs and any other project effects both positive and negative to the community. The information shall help guide the next project design as they will be lessons learnt.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Women carry the burden of care for all family members including breastfeeding and feeding children regardless of their physiological and physical status and yet are vulnerable shocks and difficult situations that they face and hence need protection. Children, due to their dependence on other adults for care and support are weak and therefore need support. Other people with special needs such as those with disabilities need special added support whether they are women, children or caretakers. These have all been factored in the project design, therefore the project focuses to support most vulnerable population groups aggregated in gender and age group i.e. IDPs, returnees, and host community due to their different short comings. The project will hence support PLWs (100% females), children 6 - 59 months (boys and girls) and support other support groups through the care support groups such as moter to mother support groups, individual counseling, group counseling and educations sessions. At the nutrition sites, the project shall ensure that IYCF/MIYCN/GBV counselors are female staff who are of age to be able to support PLWs and caretakers of children.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

UNKEA understand that within the community there could be possible barriers to access services by those targeted due to either community perception of either gender or age group segregation based on cultural or individual norms. For example men are the sole decision makers in households and at times without seeking opinions of women and children for issues that affects them. The project will put deliberate efforts in engaging the women directly in issues that affect them such as feeding practices, counseling and decisions within the project that would affect their well-being. Men shall also be engage at various levels in order to encourage their support to the women and children in ensuring services benefit the economy of the household since a health mother and child tantamount to improvement of nutrition status and economic status of the household. Men's involvement in supporting women to care for the family will be upheld throughout the lifespan of the project. Physical barriers to access shall be reduced by ensuring OTP sites are located within the reach of the surrounding community/IDP, and necessary supplies availed by having a good supply chain mechanism such as availability of quad bike to transport supplies to locations far from the river, repositioning of supplies in difficult locations, and other avenues shall be sort upon presentation of the situation. Security related barriers shall be mitigated using existing channels of communication between the security groups, local authorities and the general community.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

UNKEA as a non-partisan, non-governmental organization who works with impartiality in order to deliver services to affected population with total disregard of religion, creed, tribe, or origin equally. UNKEA understands the existence of inequalities related to GBV, neglect, child hunger, etc from various sources and causes. The project shall have a keen focus in identifying and mitigating potential inequality or protection threats that may impose difficulties in accessing services by project beneficiaries. Project shall be implemented in a friendly environment for both beneficiaries, and service providers, people with special needs who fit the target group will be support without any form of segregation. In conduct of activities at community level, the project engagement shall be based on the agreed strategies with the community e.g. areas identified to be used for service delivery shall be adhered to by the project service providers in order to avoid any threats.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities shall be geared towards a specific target group of interest due to their vulnerabilities when exposed to shocks, these includes pregnant and lactating women, children under the age of 5 years who will be screened for signs of acute malnutrition and enrolled for management. The project will have recommended tailored key messages in regard to inequalities or protection, Knowledge and information sharing with community, Feeding support groups shall be established address any form of threats, referral of cases identified which the project cannot manage shall be done e.g. victims of GBV to the relevant care level.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project shall use the MOH and nutrition cluster recommended tools for data collection and reporting including systems which are based on sphere standards. The project monitoring tools which are aggregated by gender and age group shall be used for the monitoring and evaluation of the project. The data collection and reporting tools are disaggregated by sex and age group in order to capture the necessary information regarding access to services by the specific groups. The reports from the nutrition sites shall be bi-weekly and monthly, which will be submitted to the Nutrition cluster and MOH as appropriate. Community level feedback mechanism shall act as a community level project monitoring measure of access to beneficiaries. The project shall use the CNVs and MSGs who will be involved in daily activities in the community and shall be tasked with responsibility of giving feedbacks between the community and the nutrition sites, which will help in measuring access.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The community level feedback mechanism shall give detailed feedback information about the safety of beneficiaries in the course of the implementation of the project. The project's service points shall be within safe areas for the target group and the community being served. The nutrition sites are situated within the existing health facilities which are located within the Bomas in which the villages are. The facilities are within a day’s walking distance to and from their homes. In places/villages that are beyond 1 day walking distance or has a physical barrier, such places shall be served through outreach activities from the nearest nutrition site. The nutrition sites shall be operational 5 days per week with 40 working hours, community will be informed of the opening and closing hours.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project shall respect the dignity of the beneficiaries as services that shall be provided will be in respect of existing culture, community norms and national law. Existing places of convenience such as latrines will be labeled male and female in order to ensure individual dignity of all beneficiaries. The project shall not infringe the rights of individuals or groups in the process of service delivery.
