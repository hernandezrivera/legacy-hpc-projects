Project Summary

The project will contribute to the HRP strategic objectives 3, and the Food
Security cluster objective in the WB. It will do so by providing rehabilitation, productive
assets and inputs to farmers with lands in Area C, specifically in 6 villages in the Qalqilya
and Tulkarem governorates. These farmers face specific challenges to cultivate their
lands due to the different measures imposed by the occupation regime, including settler
violence, demolition, confiscation, the wall, etc.
The project is based on a needs assessment conducted by PUI and UAWC in September
2020 that identified the most pressing needs of vulnerable farmers working in area C
close to the wall or settlements. In addition, PUI coordinated with FSS, MoA Central and
MoA directorates of Qalqilya and Tulkarem, in order to identify and prioritize FSS needs
and localities in these 2 governorates. In order to respond to the identified needs and to
contribute to the HRP, HF and cluster objectives of this second allocation, PUI and UAWC
will provide 3 types of assistance:
- Provide 5.5 kilometres of water carrier lines to ensure the delivery of water from the
resources to the agricultural lands. The project will also provide water storage, when
needed (A 300m3 reservoir is planned to be constructed in one of the 2 communities). The Village Councils will contribute meaningfully in providing feedback regarding the impact of this activity. This activity will benefit 250 farmers in total.
- Support 240 vulnerable farmers working in lands adjacent to the settlements or the wall
and whose livelihoods are threatened by occupation measures. Agricultural roads will be
rehabilitated (8 km). This will increase safe access to lands and contribute to the
prevention of land confiscations. An additional 25 farmers will receive land rehabilitation
support to increase their production capacity. The project will also provide cisterns to

another 6 farmers to enhance the supplementary irrigation to trees and crops in their
lands;
In total, 521 farmers households (2505 individuals) will benefit directly from the Action.
Selection criteria for the farmers will depend mainly on socio-economic vulnerability
criteria, as PUI understands that the needs of people intersect with their different
characteristics. Groups of people identified as being in greater need (FHHs, PwD, older
persons, etc.), people facing increased IHL violation, or located closer to settlements, the
wall or prior coordination areas, will be prioritized. Technical criteria including topography of the land, slope
percentage, soil depth, land ownership, and planted trees will also be taken into
consideration.
The project will be implemented with UAWC and each organization will bring their
expertise: UAWC supports agriculture from a development perspective, focusing on water
activities, while PUI rehabilitates lands and assets, easing access and providing support to
farmers through a protection lens through emergency and protective responses to settler-
related incidents. Furthermore, the project will complement the Action of the West Bank
Protection Consortium by covering a gap in the provision of assistance and livelihood
support to vulnerable farmers in Area C. Moreover, PUI will mobilize its Consortium
experience (tools, methodology, network) at all different stages of the project.
PUI and partners recognize that different age, gender and diversity groups are faced with increased risks and usually enjoy less opportunities to develop positive copping mechanisms to deal with them. Thus, service provision itself, as well as the way it is delivered, will be tailored to the unique needs and capacities of different AGD groups in the target communities. The tailoring aimed to address and mitigate different needs and risks of diverse groups will derive from community consultations with these groups, including women and girls, people with disabilities, older persons, etc.

1 - Needs

The current C19 pandemic added an additional layer of suffering to the Palestinian population. One of the most affected sectors was the economic one. The FSS C19 Crisis Sitrep-09, published in May 2020, highlighted the difficulties farmers are facing due to the ongoing pandemic. Those difficulties include lack of human resources (due to movement restrictions), and reduced production for low-scale farmers due to limited cash flow and the inability to provide cash advances. According to the UNDP: "The Palestinian economy is expected to incur losses of about US$2.5 billion 3 months into the pandemic". One of major occupation regime measures affecting Palestinian livelihoods is settlements expansion and subsequent SV. Settlements have been established and expanded across the WB and East Jerusalem for the past 53 years. This expansion has a continuous negative impact on Palestinian daily life (attacks on Palestinian livelihoods, residential movement restrictions and other types of harassment). Since 2017, more than 2,580 SV incidents have been reported, including 27,656 vandalized trees. PUI/UAWC conducted a rapid needs assessment in September 2020 with the communities of Qalqiliya (Jit, Sanniriya, and Beit Amin), and Tulkarem (Qaffin, Zeita, and Kafr Jammal, in addition to Kafr Sour). Outcomes indicated that 46.1% of the families in these locations depend on agricultural practices as their main source of income, while 10% depend on agriculture as a secondary source of income. The average monthly income for 35.5% of the surveyed HHs ranges between 1450 NIS- 2999 NIS. 54.6% of the families confirm that in the last six months, they witnessed income decline, with 29.1% of the participants unable to cover their basic needs. 52.5% of the participants reported being indebt, (20% of them for the purchase of agricultural inputs. Based on FGDs conducted with community representatives, the protracted humanitarian crisis associated with the Israeli occupation and restrictions is affecting around 51,750 dunums of land, of which 6,200 dunums are located near to the coordination area, and can be easily access by the Israeli settlers. In area C, around 25,500 dunums of agricultural land can be accessed. 20,000 of those are currently cultivated, while the remaining lands needs rehabilitation or reclamation. 51 Km of roads are also in need for rehabilitation. These roads are vital for two reasons. Firstly, to facilitate reaching the land for all family members and take the required tools and plants to the land using vehicles. Secondly, it is considered in most cases as a safe exit for the farmers. New roads opening to care to 14,359 dunums of land would benefit 5,150 farmers. Part of the targeted communities (Kufur Sour and Qaffin) stated that the lack of water access can be observed up to 90% of time, while it is estimated at 50% of the time in Kufur Jamal and 32% in the community of Sinira. Following the farmer’s HH assessment as well as the FGDs with experienced farmers and community representatives the following needs were highlighted: 1. Road and Land Rehabilitation 2.Rehabilitation / provision of water carrier lines 3/ distribution of agricultural tools. The priorities for interventions have been discussed with MoA and village counsels. Kafr Sur village has been excluded from the project as the needs assessment indicated that most farmers' lands are located in Area B or in the seam zone. It is worth noting that HHs belonging to different age, gender and diversity (AGD) groups (FHHs, PwD, older persons, etc.) were already facing increased risks in terms of livelihoods, which are now further exacerbated by the current pandemic. PUI takes into consideration the disproportionate burden falling into different AGD groups and aims to address it by ensuring people with different needs and capacities can safely access all provided services and that activities are tailored to fit their characteristics and needs. To support this process, PUI is currently developing a gender analysis to act as a roadmap reflecting needs, risks and opportunities related to different gender and age groups. The specific project-related nuances associated with gender and age will be identified through the planned community consultations and compliment the general gender analysis in presenting specific needs more comprehensively.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Land rehabilitation will be carried out for 100 dunums (50 in Tulkarem and 50 in Qalqilya), including building of retaining walls, ploughing, fencing, cleaning and planting seedlings and seeds for intercropping agriculture. 25 vulnerable farmers will benefit from rehabilitation of their lands. Rehabilitation works will be implemented directly by the farmers.. Mixed approach of cash and in-kind will be used to implement this activity. Agreements with farmers will be signed to govern the payment methods, conditionality of assistance, standards and qualifications of the expected outputs. The Action will encourage the participation of people from different AGD groups and promote equal access to productive resources and joint decision-making by engaging a diverse representation. The methodology of working with entire HH instead of only with HH representatives will be adopted. Additionally women, PwDs and people belonging to different AGD groups will actively be called upon to apply for land rehabilitation activities and state their specific needs. They will be prioritized through the scoring system used for beneficiary selection. Activity 2: The construction of 6 cisterns to benefit additional 6 farmers. The objective of this activity is to enhance the supplementary irrigation to trees and crops. That would increase the production by 20-30% per dunum according to MoA. Support will be given to farmers whose lands are adjacent to the Prior Coordination areas or the wall part of which was confiscated. Cash approach will be used to implement this activity. Agreements with farmers will be signed to govern the payment methods, condionality of assistance, standards and qualifications of the expected outputs.by the IF earlier, and in areas exposed to SV/vandalism The cisterns will an average capacity of 70 cubic meter each. PUI/UAWC staff will follow up the construction and support the farmers throughout. The support will include technical support and legal advises. Activity 3: Rehabilitation of agricultural roads. This activity includes the rehabilitation of 8 km of agricultural roads (4 km in Tulkarem governorate and 4 km in Qalqilya governorate). 240 HHs will benefit from increased safe access to their agricultural lands, which will accelerate pruning and/or support land use - which in turn will contribute to preventing land confiscations. The rehabilitation of agricultural roads will increase the number of persons accessing the land, resulting in increased protection of farmers; furthermore, it will secure the access of ambulances/fire fighters in case of protection incidents related to SV. Materials will be provided by PUI/UAWC and the local councils/municipalities will carry out the work. Agricultural roads will be selected through a consultative process involving local councils/municipalities, MoA, and UAWC committees, giving priority to areas exposed to threats of SV, settlement expansion, demolition, etc. The selection will be based on a scoring system that will take into account protection concerns and the number of farmers who can benefit from the rehabilitated road. Activity 4: Rehabilitation/ Provision of water carriers lines / water storage This activity includes the provision of 5.5 km of water carriers’ lines (1.5 km in Tulkarem governorate and 4km in Qalqilya governorate). 250 HHs will benefit from the sustainable source of water to use for irrigation of their trees and crops. Consequently, this will increase the production of their lands. This activity will encourage farmers to invest in their lands and diversify their crops. In parallel, a water storage (reservoir) of a capacity of 300m3 will be constructed in one village based on needs assessment to complement the activity of water carrier lines provision. All activities will take into consideration specific age, gender and diversity (AGD) needs and opportunities by consulting in an accessible and appropriate manner diverse community members. Specific modifications will be made to ensure different AGD groups enjoy equal access to service provision, as well as to mitigate any possible risks associated with the assistance itself. Including the voices of groups of people traditionally excluded from decision making and the public sphere, like women, children. PwDs. provides a unique opportunity to enrich humanitarian aid, and promote equitable social norms and pluralism within the target communities.

3 - Indicators

PUI and UAWC M&E teams will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan at the beginning of the project to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets, insuring in each step disaggregated representation. Disaggregation will reflect different age, gender and diversity groups of people within the target communities. This plan will describe the whole M&E system, including indicators, tools, breakdown of responsibilities and communication channels within the organizations and between them. • Monthly monitoring visits will be conducted to follow up the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are being implemented in accordance with the work plan developed at the beginning of the activity. Written report summarizing the main findings will be issued after each monitoring visit. • Introductory and information meetings with targeted population and community representatives will be arranged to provide accessible and timely information on organizational procedures, types of project activities, beneficiary selection process, criteria of selection, etc. • A complaints and feedback mechanism will be set up to make sure that the opinions of target population are heard and taken into consideration. PUI will ensure that all beneficiaries are informed of the process. Complaints regarding the beneficiary selection process or suggestions related to the design or the implementation of project activities will be timely responded to. Awareness regarding PSEA will be raised at all stages of the project implementation both with staff and beneficiaries. SEA related complaints will be received and handled with utmost respect to confidentiality and with a survivor-centred approach. • Focus group discussions and/or semi structured surveys will be carried out to assess the impact of project activities against baseline survey results Indicators By the end of the project, 80% of farmers (disaggregated by AGD) benefiting from land rehabilitation activity are satisfied regarding the quality of the in kind assistance provided. -By the end of the project, 80% of farmers (disaggregated by AGD) benefiting from cistern construction activity are satisfied regarding the quality of the assistance provided. -By the end of the project, 80% of farmers (disaggregated by AGD) benefiting from Rehabilitation/ Provision of water carriers lines / water storage activities are satisfied regarding the quality of the assistance provided. -85% of beneficiaries (disaggregated by AGD) reporting that assistance is delivered in a safe, accountable ,accessible and participatory manner. -All local committees founded for the purpose of the project include a diverse representation of all different AGD in the communities.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Beneficiaries are involved in all aspects of the Action through a shared decision-making process. The community-based protection approach involves a continuous process of self-assessment and analysis of their vulnerabilities and needs, used as an entry point for the elaboration of local protection strategies. As soon as the action starts, PUI/UAWC will meet with community representatives and all selected beneficiaries to inform them about the accepted project and explain how beneficiaries are expected to participate and contribute for the success of the action. This meeting will allow participants to inform PUI/UAWC about their needs and involve them in a participatory approach. All participants will be informed about PUI Feedback & Response Mechanism (FRM) as well as PUI’s commitment to ensuring PSEA. During those meetings, PUI / UAWC staff members will ensure to hand out information cards on the mechanism and explain the FRM. They will pay attention to highlight that feedback, whether positive or negative, will all be taken into consideration and addressed by PUI representatives independent from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. PUI will work with the established committees to ensure full transparency and make sure to hand out cards and posters to partners in sufficient quantity. Meanwhile, feedbacks may be provided verbally in the field, through text message, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards). Feedbacks may originate from beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries impacted by PUI / partners interventions as well. PUI FRM document and SoP might be provided upon request. All activities, assessments, community consultations and the FRM will be modified in terms of modes and modalities to ensure different AGD groups can access them equitably.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

How have you prioritised the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and considered the principles of Do No Harm?Protection mainstreaming is crucial in humanitarian aid delivery. PUI is dedicated in ensuring do no harm and dignity to all people accessing services regardless of their personal characteristics. PUI is currently developing a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on the Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy and on the planned community consultations, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed Action onset. Some of the key intervention areas will be:• A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis will be drafted Action onset. The analysis will be further refined and triangulated with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEA is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects for protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEA, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEA reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEA training will be provided to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEA will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries. All partners are required to sign and adhere by the PUI PSEA policy. • Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed or drafted. At the same time, staff members will be trained on “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “ Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures” . • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some on the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEA and child safeguarding, and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected field staff members will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As described above, PUI is very vigilant in acknowledging and addressing the intersectionality of different characteristics with different needs and capacities. To ensure people with different characteristics and coping mechanisms are able to access assistance equally, specific mitigation measures will be adopted. These mitigation measures will derive from community consultations, with people from diverse groups and different vulnerabilities. The protection and gender risk and mitigation analysis that will be further enriched and complimented throughout the project duration, identifies the potential risks and systematizes the response. The community consultations, assessments and information dissemination methods and modes will be tailored to ensure people with different characteristics are included equally. The accessibility of the project consultations, assessment, FRM and SEA reporting mechanism and of the awareness raising to people belonging in different age and gender groups, as well as to people with different diversities including people with disability, will be ensured and monitored throughout project duration. COVID-19 specific issues in terms of access will also be taken into consideration. Specifically, hygiene, prevention, and control measures and protocols will be in place in all planned project activities. The protection and gender analysis will be updated regularly to reflect new emerging needs associated with any enforced changes in the project delivery modalities. These enforced changes in modality with also be part of regular consultations with the communities to identify and assess how they might contribute to the access in humanitarian service provision of different groups of people. Accessible information in different modalities for different groups of people regarding COVID-19 will be provided. Lastly, the continuation of the FRM, regardless of COVID-19 related changes in programming will be ensured
