Project Summary

ACF will continue its efforts to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with acute malnutrition in children under 5, primarily by operating Stabilization Centers (SC) in Kutupalong and Balukhali Makeshift Camps. These SCs will provide critical care for severely malnourished children, infants with low birth weight, and those referred from other nutrition facilities. ACF will also work with Integrated Nutrition Facilities (INF) teams to improve timely referrals and follow-up care.
In addition to treating complicated SAM cases following the 10 essential steps, the Stabilization Centers also offer Supplementary Sucking Technique (SST), wet nursing, and relactation for infants under six months of age. This further enhances the centers' capability to address a wide range of nutritional challenges faced by this vulnerable population.
ACF will continue the support CMAM program implemented by the Ministry of Health (MoH) in Upazila health complex following inpatient management of SAM and in Community clinic level IYCF including MNP distribution 6-23 months children as per national guideline, hygiene promotion, and Sexual Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) services including IFA distribution to Adolescents girl and Pregnant Women focusing women and adolescent girl including gender transformative approach to access quality health and nutrition services. Further more the project will continue to targeting GVB , PSEA sensitization, male engagement including active case identification and referral
Furthermore ACF will implement CMAM research through implementing partner icddr,b in collaboration with IPHN and WFP. In addition, ACF has plan for contributing Coverage survey at Rhogingya Refugee /FDMN and Host community. The surveys will be discussed and agreed at the Nutrition sector level and with approval from the IPHN/NSS.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As of March 2023, significant reductions in General Food Assistance (GFA) rations have been introduced for Rohingya refugees. Initially set at $12 per person, equivalent to 2100 Kcal, the food value vouchers were lowered to $10 in March and further reduced to $8 in June. These cuts are expected to have adverse effects on the nutritional well-being of vulnerable Rohingya refugee populations, particularly children under five and pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW).These ongoing ration cuts, along with programmatic findings in the nutrition sector, indicate a rising trend in malnourished child admissions, potentially leading to increased acute malnutrition prevalence. Among children aged 6-59 months in refugee camps, the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM), based on weight for height (WHZ), has reached 12.3% (95% Confidence Intervals 9.5 % - 16.1 %). , categorizing it as a high emergency threshold. Stunting prevalence among these children is 41.0%, classified as very high by WHO/UNICEF. Only 22.7% of Rohingya children aged 6-23 months met the WHO criteria for a Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD), which includes adequate meal frequency, dietary diversity, and milk intake. Low adherence is influenced by socio-cultural beliefs, leading to limited meal variety and frequency, thus hindering proper nutrition in the refugee camps. In host communities, GAM stands at 9.3% (95% Confidence Intervals 7.5 % – 11.5 %), with 23.5% prevalence of underweight children and 23.7% prevalence of stunted children, both categorized as high. Only 26% meet the Minimum Acceptable Diet criteria, and just 31.3% achieve Minimum Dietary Diversity, indicating a very low dietary quality. However in both situations, there is a high potential for increasing the number of severe acute malnutrition cases with complications, given the significant challenges and vulnerabilities in the refugee camps and host communities.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project will be implemented in both the refugee and host communities under the direct guidance of the nutrition sectors, in partnership with NNS/IPHN. We anticipate collaboration with UNICEF, WFP, and other implementing partners for Stabilization center activities, and we will adhere to the nutrition sectors' instructions for coverage and assessments. The Stabilization Centers will act as referral points for integrated facilities, ensuring there is no duplication of activities and maintaining full collaboration with the INF teams.In the host communities, ACF will provide support to the MoHWF in running the Stabilization Centers/SAM Corner, and we will work closely with WFP, SHED for CMAM research activities. Inpatient management of SAM will involve close collaboration with all Nutrition Sectors partners, following national guidelines on CMAM. The preventive activity at host community will be implemented as per the approval from NNS/IPHN in collaboration with upazila health complex.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

ACF will continue its active engagement with the affected people throughout the project cycle, aiming to better comprehend the various risks and threats they confront and assist them in devising protective measures. With over a decade of involvement in this humanitarian crisis, ACF has integrated a protection-focused approach into its operations. Staff members have received training to identify and mitigate potential and actual risks, while also remaining sensitive to factors such as age, gender, and diversity within the affected population. ACF's core principle centers on upholding the dignity and rights of individuals, aligning with the "Do No Harm Policy." The organization will persist in supporting individuals to assert their rights and access appropriate remedies through Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and the protection referral system, which includes addressing safeguarding issues.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

ACF places a strong emphasis on community involvement in its humanitarian interventions, ensuring that programs are based on surveys and assessments in which affected communities actively participate. Their country strategy and program design prioritize the participation of the affected populations, engaging various groups such as male forums, mother support groups, community leaders, and adolescent groups for input on program development. A Feedback and Complaints mechanism has been established, which has proven effective in capturing and addressing the concerns of program beneficiaries.Furthermore, ACF acknowledges the importance of adapting to the language and cultural context of the target population. The organization ensures that all interactions, education, information, and awareness sessions are conducted in the spoken language of the community. In refugee camps for SC, Rohingya volunteers assist in translation, while ACF staff with experience in the camps have developed basic language skills to effectively communicate with the Rohingya population.The project's goals include reinforcing the quality of services and aligning with recognized standards such as the SPHERE Standards, with a particular focus on nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive interventions. ACF is committed to maintaining its high-performance standards and will continue to collaborate closely with implementing partners, including in the areas of and other key sectors.Through participatory monitoring, the affected population is actively engaged in all project stages, ensuring that the project meets their specific needs and language preferences. This comprehensive approach aims to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with acute malnutrition in children under five, adolescent girl and pregnant and lactating women. ACF's commitment to community engagement and adaptation will be integral to the success of the project.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

ACF has a strong policy and mechanism in place to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), aligning closely with the standards recommended by nutrition sectors and PSA networks. To ensure effective implementation, ACF will undertake several key technical measures and operational procedure.Staff will receive comprehensive training to emphasize the significance of PSEA principles and actions to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse. The organization will establish clear and confidential reporting mechanisms to encourage individuals to report any concerns related to PSEA. Any reported violations will undergo thorough investigations, and appropriate actions will be taken against offenders, adhering to a zero-tolerance stance. ACF will also focus on raising awareness among beneficiaries, staff, and partners regarding PSEA policies and mechanisms. Ongoing monitoring and evaluation will be carried out to assess the effectiveness of these measures and identify areas for improvement.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

ACF leverages its extensive experience in Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) for the project, fostering active collaboration with ACF's DRR team to establish a functional contingency plan that prioritizes emergency preparedness, response, and early recovery, aligned with the nutrition sector's emergency response plan. The focus is on ensuring that Stabilization Centers are fully functional and equipped to sustain continuous service delivery, bolster rapid response capabilities, and anticipate changes in case loads, particularly in the face of recurrent disasters and potential crises.ACF's goal is to integrate Disaster Risk Management (DRM) into its undernutrition and emergency operations. This approach is rooted in several principles: it's unethical to wait for the onset of suffering from a disaster to trigger a reactive response; understanding the dynamics of undernutrition and emergencies allows for proactive measures before hazards strike; it's operationally wise to safeguard gains made by humanitarian and development initiatives in communities and households; and it's economically sensible to spend proactively before an emergency occurs, protecting investments made by communities, governments, and their aid partners.In both the host community and the refugee camps, ACF's team has received training in Nutrition in Emergency, with concrete actions integrated into ACF-supported facilities, including key messages shared during regular health and nutrition community forums and awareness sessions.
