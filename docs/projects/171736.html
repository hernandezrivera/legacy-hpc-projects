Project Summary

An estimated 2.6 Million internally displaced persons (IDPs) are recorded to be in need of humanitarian assistance. The protracted nature of the conflict, floods and violence has impacted the living conditions of IDPs and returnees. Many IDPs face significant difficulties in securing affordable and adequate housing with some being at risk of eviction. Poor living conditions, negative coping mechanisms, limited access to services, sexual violence are among the protection risks reported to face IDPs. Security, lack of livelihoods opportunities, limited public services and lack of social cohesion due to weakened governance structures are some of the challenges facing IDPs, returnees and Host communities.
Actions targeting GBV risk reduction, response and mitigation is in line with Sustainable development goals (SDG) number five (5) which calls for the elimination of all forms of violence and discriminatory acts against women and girls. Somalia has not signed or adopted any of the major international instruments for women’s rights and gender equality and therefore SGBV is not seen as a pressing issue.
The main GBV risks are related to safety and security, exacerbated by poor quality shelters and poor lighting at night; a lack of male/female segregated and lockable toilets, and the distances women and girls have to walk to water and fuel wood collection points, as well as to health facilities and markets. Girls in particular are persistently harassed along dark paths in IDP camps and host communities as they go to fetch water.
Intimate partner violence, Rape, sexual assault, physical assault, forced marriage, denial of resources, opportunities or services; and psychological/emotional abuse are the most frequently reported type of GBV in Somalia.
Further, there are a range of rigid discriminatory gender, cultural and socio-economic factors that underpin and perpetuate the occurrence of GBV in Somalia such as social norms and stereotypes, illiteracy, poverty, family breakdown and unemployment, among other things. However, due to the unequal and discriminatory nature of the clan system, minority women face double discrimination on account of both their gender and minority status and therefore women and girls from minority groups and clans are the most affected by incidents of SGBV.
On the basis of assessed needs and working in close cooperation with local authorities and close coordination with other field partners, SFH proposed to support the returnees and IDPs through the provision of individual case management, specialized assistance and facilitation of access to services.
SFH is will provide counselling and safe house for prevention and response to SGBV and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Vulnerable individuals, as women-at-risk, children-at-risk, unaccompanied and separated children, elderly persons, persons with disabilities will be provided with case management support that would inter-alia cover PSS and MHPSS services.

Project Activities

The proposed GBV risk reduction, mitigation and response action will focus in Lower juba and will address the GBV needs of women and girls. 1) The intervention will target key duty bearers and service providers to improve GBV service delivery, strengthen referral pathways and coordinated Multi-sectoral response to GBV (CMR, medical, PSS and Legal aid where applicable). This will be provided through information kiosks/one stop centers targeting 900 GBV survivors. SFH will procure dignity kits and made it available at the centers/Kiosk. SFH will request post rape kits from UNICEF/UNFPA.2) In addition to addressing the psycho-social needs, the project will also provide GBV case management to ensure that women and girls safely access quality, survivor centered, psycho-social support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery. Female Case workers will be recruited and trained to provide survivor centered, timely, culturally appropriate and confidential treatment of rape and psycho-social counseling. 3) Strengthening GBV referral systems and strengthening the relationships between the various GBV actors in lower juba.4) 900 women and girl’s survivors of GBV including those with disabilities will directly benefit from access to gender friendly safe spaces 5) The intervention also intends to address the protection needs of women and girls including those living with disabilities in emergencies by transforming negative social norms which perpetuate GBV and which often worsen in times of emergencies. The intervention will support men and boys and the wider community to challenge and transform systems as well change norms using well known strategies such as Engage men.6) Provide skills training, emotional learning, financial skills, numeracy and literacy that will be delivered in the safe spaces and start-up kits to 150 GBV survivors as empowerment and mitigation for GBV.All actions will be implemented in strict compliance with Ministry of Health's recommendation to prevent spread of COVID-19 pandemic. BCCE activities will be mainstreamed accordingly.SFH will use age, gender and diversity approach to address the needs of the affected population. Different population groups including men, women, children, older people and disabled will be consulted and their specific needs will be considered in planning and implementation of the planned interventions.Any form of exclusion based on disability, gender or social status is considered as strong protection concern and will be mitigated through the following approach:A) Through the community based case workers SFH will ensure person with mobility impairment are reached and referred appropriately.B) To accommodate persons with impaired hearing, vision or mental health, SFH will disseminate information about the availability of services through both verbal or written forms.C) SFH will ensure the location, distance and access routes to the safe houses, health facilities are accessible to all members of the community, including persons with disability. D) Where applicable SFH will coordinate with other actors in Lower Juba such as Humanity & Inclusion (Handicap International) who are specialized in working with persons with impaired mobility and disability to provide data of such individual to improve service delivery.

Mitigation Measures

1) Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. Mitigation Measures: a) SFH will identify and work with community patrol groups, where possible, to support prevention of forced recruitment, looting, GBV and other threats. b) SFH will liaise and coordinate with mine action actors to map routes with safety risks and associated with landmines, explosive remnants of war (ERW)and busy roads where applicable. As a member of Jubaland mine action network consortium, SFH will pay special focus on this subject and ensure camps and camp-like settings as well as safe houses are not placed in areas associated with mine and UXOs. c) SFH will conduct safety audits in 30 sites and will also coordinate with other protection actors to identify paths and routes that are highly associated with protection risks and particularly GBV and will subsequently share this information with camp leaders, community leaders, men, women and girls for prevention untill durable solutions to reverse the situation is achieved. 2). Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.). Mitigation measures: a) In cooperation with other protection actors, enhance the capacity of camp staff and managers to monitor, report and refer protection concerns, CMR and PSS services will be provided as wellb) Establish safe house/ social hall where community members will meet share ideas and receive appropriate information on available protection assistance as well as referral services. c) All SFH staff, Volunteers and vendors will sign and get oriented/trained on PSEA policy, violation of the policy will lead to disciplinary action including termination of contract. 3) Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms Mitigation measures: a) SFH will establish community response and feedback mechanism that is responsive and accessible to all beneficiaries including real-time free lines, complaint desks during registrations and mount the numbers in various strategic locations within the camp settings. b) Create awareness and sensitization sessions on how the mechanism works without exposing the complainant c) SFH will establish inclusive AAP committees who will be part of the selections, verification and disseminating relevant information while coordinating with other existing governance structures within the camps
