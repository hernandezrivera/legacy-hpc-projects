Project Summary

Support for the improved food security status of IDPs and vulnerable host communities in Borno State. The improved household food security of vulnerable households, especially of women and children will be achieved through integrated cash transfer scheme, support for income generating activities as well as livelihoods support, especially for pregnant and lactating women and vulnerable households

Needs

Approximately 1,926,784 individuals (364,635 households) remain displaced in North-East Nigeria and the ongoing conflict is the primary reason (94%) for displacement. Borno state, is the most affected and hosts the highest number of IDPs (1,441,635) and in Yobe State there are 137,588 IDPs (IOM DTM Round 24, 2018). As per the IOM DTM Round 24, August 2018, 54% percent of IDPs needing assistance are female, 27% are children under five, 81% are children plus women. The host communities support 91% of the IDPs, placing burdens on households and existing infrastructure that are already stressed. According to the December 2017 DTM Round XX, the IDP population in selected LGAs is as follows: Jere (44,736HHs/250,819 IDP); Magumeri (3,472HHS/18,858 IDP); MMC (51,961HHs/ 265,782 IDP); Nganzai (4,317HHs/ 22,144 IDP); and Monguno (20,679HHs/ 122,997 IDP). The IDPs have very limited access to livelihood opportunities and are vulnerable. Insecurity and sporadic attacks still occur in various areas. This insecurity disrupts livelihoods, restricts access to farming land, discourages production and puts the vulnerable populations at continuous risk of starvation. As a result, food availability and access is still significantly constrained. Action Against Hunger will focus support to address the Immediate food needs of targeted vulnerable households (Output 1). In response to the high malnutrition rates cited above (that are expected to keep increasing as the lean period intensifies) and as a means of protecting households not to further engage in destructive coping mechanisms, and minimize the possibility of increase in mortality, Action Against Hunger, in coordination with other humanitarian agencies, is proposing food assistance project in the most affected LGAs with the aim of reaching the most food-insecure people and provide them with timely lifesaving assistance. In line with the harmonization guide from the food security sector working group, Action Against Hunger proposes to have in-kind food distributions. In addition, ACF will improve the nutrition and care practices among pregnant and lactating women and children under five.

Activities

Undertake Household vulnerability assessments to identify nutritious vulnerability and the following activities will be implemented to cater to the community needs through provision of cash transfers and general food distribution, Register/select beneficiaries through the RedRose Platform• Transfer cash 18,000 Naira to 6,000 households for three months (50% cash/50% food in vouchers) in MMC and Jere • Transfer cash 25,000 Naira to 11,890 households for three months in Monguno • General Food Distribution to 5,000 households in Nganzai. • General Food Distribution to 5,000 households in Magumeri. • Distribution of kitchen kits to 600HHs• Perform monthly PDM surveys• Perform baseline & end line surveys• Training• Human resources• Vehicles Action Against Hunger actively engage and coordinate with other partners through Food security working group. At the LGA level we already have coordinate with other partners to avoid duplication of efforts and support. In MMC/Jere we have worked with other partners upto house hold level to avoid any duplication and keep GIS (coordinates) to ensure that it is maintained that way. In regard to other areas where mobile network is not effective we share the number of house hold to be supported with clear demarcation of the formal, non-formal camps and host communities. The details of our activities upto ward level are shared with FSS on monthly basis. In some of the areas we have humanitarian and early recovery projects in the same LGA, for that we generate database of house hold which can be supported via humanitarian approach and while others get the early recovery initiatives. Action Against Hunger participate in various coordination groups for humanitarian action and early recovery one to streamline the efforts and inform other partners of our work. the Early recovery is entirely led by RRR Commissioner while the rest of the activities has SEMA/NEMA and other partners. Complaints response mechanism is established with a view to improve the programming, assisting in understanding the beneficiary and community perceptions, promoting beneficiaries empowerment and assisting in the early detection of problems such a targeting criteria, misconduct including sexual exploitation and abuse, food diversion and fraud. AAH tries to provide meaningful access to vulnerable household, and we do not discriminate against any groups and it is performed in such a way that they are not considered to be perceived as doing so. We also take in to consideration whether women and men may have different capacities to access cash compared to in kind resources, this is ensured by providing separate sitting areas access points along with discussion and agreement with community leaders which also has women participation. As mentioned earlier AAH has established the system of CRM (Complaints Response Mechanism) which identifies the key areas which challenges or issues or even positive feedback. It also generate a sense of security as the complaints are not only followed up internally with AAH but we engage the community members in case the matter needs resolution from their side. Our targeting criteria entry point is via community leaders committee which identify one area while we conduct registration exercise to collect information on all the house hold including the less vulnerable people to avoid any exclusion error. Once that is completed than we run our criteria checks and identify the most vulnerable who are later verified as well. As part of Accountability to Affected Populations, Action Against Hunger has already put in place steps to ensure that their participation is considered in actions e.g. Community leaders forms the committee to identify the area most affected, as a second step for verification process they are included. In following the same process AAH also has tollfree phone number which could access to register complaints if one doesn't agree with committee even if a person is not from area
