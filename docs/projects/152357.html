Project Summary

Mercy Corps is proposing a 12-month project aimed at strengthening the protective environment in Libya by identifying and responding in a dignified manner to the protection needs of the most vulnerable individuals. The proposed project will take a multi-sectoral approach to address these individuals' needs, including providing information, counselling and legal assistance (ICLA) to refugees through community day centers; psychosocial support for conflict-affected youth; and quick impact projects to enhance social cohesion. Through this comprehensive approach, vulnerable individuals will have increased safety, dignity, inclusion, improved psychosocial well-being and will be better able to meet their basic needs.

Needs

Libya currently has 348,372 IDPs, 310,265 returnees and 276,958 migrants (IOM DTM August 2018) and 55,877 refugees and asylum-seekers (UNHCR September 2018) all with needs related to the increasing prices of accommodation, food and basic supplies. Key commodities are no longer easily accessible or affordable for a large percentage of the Libyan population, impeding their access to basic needs. Concurrently, the economy is undergoing a process of `black-marketization', itself a negative coping strategy as it increases the capacity of armed actors to enact violence, a main push factor for displacement. Although the majority of employed Libyans work in the public sector, the liquidity crisis continues to prevent them from accessing or using their income to make cash purchases. As the economy is largely cash based, this increases vulnerability and limits households' ability to meet their basic needs. Mercy Corps assessed the protection environment of displaced and returnee populations across the coastal areas of Libya, and provided detailed, comprehensive and up-to-date information to UNHCR and other international and national protection actors. Through this assessment, Mercy Corps was able to identify trends suggesting the likelihood of conflict around access to basic services and the functionality of those services. Mercy Corps also identified IDP populations that were not receiving support or able to effectively access services, particularly in mixed (urban) areas. Mercy Corps also found that the level of psychosocial support is dramatically low in Libya. Access to those services remains low, while clear needs are reported by vulnerable groups of populations. Behavioral changes in children have been observed and reported by parents, especially in specific communities.

Activities

----Implementation of community-driven quick impact projects that enhance social cohesion by benefitting host community, IDPs/returnees, and therefore promote inclusion and stability. Mercy Corps Libya’s approach is unique in that it employs a dedicated localized contextual analysis to support the team’s presence on the ground, helping to facilitate access and relationships with local communities, leaders and government actors, resulting in conflict sensitive and increasingly impactful implementation of activities. With an emphasis on community engagement and analysis, Mercy Corps’ Humanitarian Access Team will carry out detailed contextual assessments in specific communities to further understand dynamics and provide location appropriate recommendations to inform new programming. The QIPs selection will be based on recommendations from the assessments which will then vetted and adapted based on feedback from local authorities, communities, and other stabilizing actors. Mercy Corps will continue to build upon established relationships with communities and work closely with leaders, associations, and organizations connected to communities at risk. Identification of IDP, returnee and host community leaders or groups will be priorities as the team works with them to develop initiatives to improve their own protection situation and access to basic services within the communities they are a part of. ----Provide information, counselling and legal assistance to refugees and asylum seekers through community day centers. Mercy Corps’ ICLA programmes will enable people affected by displacement to claim and exercise their rights and to find immediate solutions around access to basic human rights. Through working in Community Day Centers in and around Tripoli and Mistrata, MC’s ICLA activities will align with the IASC Protection Policy, promoting understanding and respect for the rights of affected people in Libya and the obligations of duty bearers under international law. Mercy Corps will also ensure that women and children’ needs in particular are taken in to consideration and addressed. Mercy Corps has experience with implementing ICLA activities in similar contexts, including Jordan, where legal services were provided to refugee women and youth to address the prioritized needs and concerns, including legal counselling and mediation for husbands, brothers or sons. Under this program, Mercy Corps also conducted awareness campaigns to help inform refugees of their rights and access to legal support. Mercy Corps' proposed ICLA activities in Libya will build off the lessons learned in Jordan and adjust based on the assessed needs and context. --Increase women and youth capacity to create a safe and protective enabling environment within their communities: Mercy Corps will address the protection-based factors that prohibit women and youth from successfully engaging and succeeding in livelihoods activities – while building their capacity to create a safe and enabling environment within their households and communities. Mercy Corps will recruit coaches and youth leaders and train them on child protection and profound stress attunement which will lay the foundation for psychosocial work in these safe spaces. Through these activities, youth and women will have improved access to safe, secure and stable environments for youth to contribute positively in the rebuilding of communities, thus reducing the risk of negative or harmful behavior; youth and women gain improved sense of well-being and self-esteem through improved engagement within their communities. At the community level, Mercy Corps will conduct community sensitization/awareness raising campaigns to increase the overall awareness of protection risks and measures/community protection strategies, which can have positive impacts on community cohesiveness and overall levels of violence/abuse/ exploitation.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

The proposed project has been informed by participatory assessments conducted by Mercy Corps through household surveys and key informant interviews. Efforts are made to ensure community involvement at every possible stage of project planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. During the proposal development stages, interviews with key stakeholders will be conducted in the target areas to better understand protection needs and prioritization, and both positive and negative coping strategies that support households experiencing the shock of a new displacement or stresses of prolonged crisis. Beneficiaries and local communities will also be involved during the implementation stages through access to a feedback mechanism and programme monitoring activities such as monitoring and activity closure documentation and FGDs. Appropriate community accountability and reporting mechanisms will be tailored so that populations could measure the adequacy of the intervention, or address concerns and complaints. During the program's start up, Mercy Corps will consult with men, women, adolescent boys and girls, with recognition of the diverse dynamics present within varied communities in conflict-affected areas. This consultation will lay the foundation for meaningful engagement with each group. Mercy Corps will maintain a gender lens throughout each stage of program implementation to ensure equitable participation of each key group. Program models will be adapted where Mercy Corpsidentifies challenges. Mercy Corps upholds the highest personal and professional conduct at all times, enforcing a zero tolerance approach to any violation of SEA policies and procedures aimed at preventing sexual exploitation and abuse, including those on whistleblowing, anti-sexual harassment, anti-trafficking and child safeguarding. Additionally, Mercy Corps ensures that the protective impact of aid programming is maximized through protection mainstreaming by incorporating key protection principles into our programming. This approach ensures that Mercy Corps activities are able to target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote human rights. Mercy Corps will make sure its team and partner are equipped and trained to ensure protection sensitive programming. Mercy Corps will be particularly proactive in taking measures to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Mercy Corps will make sure all staff are aware and trained on the agency’s Prevention Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse policy. Beneficiary accountability (CARM) and whistleblowing mechanisms will be put in place and communicated widely to allow beneficiaries or any stakeholders to report any misconduct. Mercy Corps will adopt a zero tolerance policy and make sure to investigate and support survivors in case of incidents.
