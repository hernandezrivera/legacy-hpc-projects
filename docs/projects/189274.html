Project Summary

This project is aimed to increase access to clean and safe water, improve sanitation and hygiene with focus on protection and dignity of women and girls to vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities across the counties with the objective of decreasing mortality and morbidity rates caused by poor WASH services which have been amplified by the pandemic. Total target population: 29,536 (Men 4,430, Women 7,384, Boys 7,975 and Girls 9,747) The beneficiaries will mostly constitute IDPs, returnees and the host communities while considerate of other vulnerable groups such as people living with disabilities by15% and the elderly. HFO will encourage and support infection prevention and control (IPC) The beneficiaries will mostly constitute IDPs, returnees and the host communities while considerate of other vulnerable groups such as people living with disabilities and the elderly. HFO will encourage and support infection prevention and control (IPC) and WASH in health facilities including COVID-19 appropriate prevention and management activities. HFO will use integrated approach with health, nutrition and protection 4 payams of2 priority counties (Fangak) as per the targeting guidance Guidance 2022. SAM children and their care takers and preganant and lactating women (PLWs) are targeted at nutrition facilities with WASH services to reduce disease transmission at household levels. HFO will target nutrition facilities in providing a basic water and sanitation package to reduce risk of patients and health workers cross infectious disease transmission due to poor WASH conditions. In addition, communities showing high malnutrition prevalence combined with limited access to safe water. HFO will rehabilitate boreholes at health, nutrition sites and catchment areas. Selected water points will be monitored for quality before and after rehabilitation. Distribution of menstrual hygiene kits, general hygiene kit items, water purification tablets to caregiver families with consideration of both gender and age-specific hygiene. Water User Committees will influence hand pump selection for rehabilitation and consist of women and man members to enhance inclusiveness, as women are considered as responsible for collection of water for household needs. Sanitation committees will be identified and trained to oversee the latrine construction. In order to ensure women have a voice all committees will be female to male 50:50 and women will be in leadership roles.
HFO invests in environmental protection activities and in the project locations, Manajang, Mareang and Paguir in Fangak County, Diel/Atar 3 Kaldak and Atar 1 in Canal/Pigi County of Jonglei,

1 - Needs

The highest proportion of households relying on surface water are in Upper Nile and Jonglei states. Every fifth person is unable to collect enough water for drinking, and every third reports insufficient access to water for handwashing. Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a negative impact on the health of children, women and elderly in Fangak and Canal/Pigi as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases like acute water diarrhea cholera and water wash disease eyes and skin infections. Fangak and Canal/Pigi were cpmpletely flooded in 2020-2021 and more flooding is threatening. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs and are prioritized by the WASH Cluster for multi-sectoral response for famine prevention. Displacement, insecurity, flood and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure in these counties as it is in other parts of the country. The economic crisis has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes. In areas hosting displaced people, above-capacity demand results in water points being unable to supply enough drinking water to the people, with the situation worsening when additional people are displaced with no water point at all. Insecurity is also another factor that has affected water access. Protection concerns are also present when people access water points, with >18% of households, both rurall and in urban settings, reporting they felt unsafe while collecting water in the two weeks prior to data collection (FSNMS, 2021). Of those, 12% reported of women and girls reportedly avoiding water points due to GBV concerns (FSNMS, 2021). The issue was particularly acute in certain counties, such as Fangak and Canal/Pigi, where more than 40% of households reported feeling unsafe while collecting water (FSNMS 2021). Sanitation facilities across Fangak and Canal/Pigi are completely destroyed by flood. Overall, >80% of households did not access a latrine in 2020-2021 due to flooding (FSNMS, 2021). This aggregated figure however hides some extreme geographical variations. In certain states, such as Jonglei and Upper Nile, the percentage of households accessing a latrine is consistently <10%, with open defecation the norm (FSNMS, 2021). On the opposite side of the spectrum, 60% of Central Equotoria (FSNMS, 2021). Several factors contribute to this bleak situation. Long-lasting development challenges largely explain the fact that South Sudan has displayed one of the lowest sanitation coverage globally (WSC, 2021). Secondly, using a latrine is considered a taboo in many parts of the country, especially in states with a larger rural and/or pastoral population like Jonglei and Upper Nile (WSC, 2021).

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

HFO multi-sectoral approach helps in ensuring that we meet most of the needs of the targeted beneficiaries including protection, inclusion and equality. Healthcare Foundation Organization during the initial stages of the projects conducts a general risk assessment and once the project is assessed to cause risks to both the beneficiaries and the organization, mitigation measures are always employed. In previous projects, mitigation measures such as clear and open communication with the beneficiaries will help in protecting the beneficiaries from being exploited, choosing locations for registration and distribution which are close and safe for the beneficiaries and an inclusive decision making process with the beneficiaries. HFO also works with the government and the community leaders to ensure that adequate information reaches the beneficiaries and the community leaders usually take responsibility of managing the crowds as the community members listen to them more. During distributions and registrations, vulnerable groups such as sick, disabled, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, children and the elderly are prioritized so that they spend little time in waiting. Confidentiality is also key to protecting the beneficiaries and HFO normally conducts thorough training for the staff before any activities in the field which includes data protection policies etc. 1. During consultation of OPD outreach, ANC, Immunization services, protection activities have to be considered and ensrureed by the team to keep an eye on the vulnerable groups and they are given high priority to get services under registration and treatment. The project will work collaboratively with all protection and GBV partners to mark referral pathways and ensure every survivor has access to the services.
