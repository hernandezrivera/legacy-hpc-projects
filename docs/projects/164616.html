Project Summary

This project aims to respond to the needs of conflict-affected newly IDPs to Harem, Salqin, Kafar-Tkharim, Maret Masrin, Daret Azzeh, Atareb, Akhtarin, Darkoush, Azaz, Afrin sub districts by providing life-saving assistance through Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), and also providing people in need with wider and more dignified assistance, giving them the flexibility to choose what to purchase based on their preference and needs. It also results in more aid directly reaching beneficiaries, which ultimately ensures the maximum impact for those in need and better value-for-money. The MPG contributes to meeting a Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) for 2020 as recommended by CWG to be 120$ per household (as one-off distribution) in order to meet their minimum monthly needs. The distribution of the MPGs will be through Hawala Transfer Agent system, where all beneficiaries will receive cash vouchers which they can redeem at selected money transfer vendors and receive cash aiming to reach 10,000 HHs in the above-mentioned locations.
Shafak will ensure feedback mechanism and channels are communicated to MPC recipients through brochures distribution and posters as well as awareness sessions for the beneficiaries and local authorities before and during the distribution to ensure all beneficiaries have information on how to complain and communicate with accountability department.
In addition, Shafak will coordinate with the relevant clusters and other actors on the ground to ensure complementarity and to avoid overlap.

4 - Needs

Since 1 December 2019, nearly 689 K people have fled from their homes, mainly from southern Idleb and western Aleppo governorates, mostly escaping hostilities by moving to urban centers and IDP camps in northwest Idleb. Tens of thousands of others have reportedly moved to areas such as Dana, Harim, Salqin, Afrin, A’zaz and Al-Bab in idleb and northern Aleppo. Most of the people displaced in northwest Syria since 15 January have been displaced multiple times before, with each new displacement introducing new risks and intensifying existing vulnerabilities. Daily bombardments perpetuate the unstable security situation in northwest Syria, affecting civilian infrastructure including IDP camps, schools, health centers and hospitals. Winter conditions are compounding the vulnerability of displaced people. Many who fled are in urgent need of humanitarian support, particularly shelter, food, health, non-foodö winterization assistance. Devaluation of the SYP is driving up prices, unaffordability of essential goods and services in northwestern Syria, exacerbating the dire situation and reliance on humanitarian assistance, according to CCCM Cluster. The mentioned needs differ for the different groups; the need to provide multi-purpose cash assistance will offer the flexibility for the various gender groups and will allow the vulnerable households to secure their specific needs for their families members (including women, girls, boys, elderly ...etc.) According to CCCM data (as per OCHA’s Flash Update - As of 6 Feb 20), all new displaced people are in need of non-food items while 79% needs shelter assistance and 57 percent needs food and cash assistance. REACH report (Feb 25), highlighted three major factors that are currently severely affecting displaced households and represent the main causes of their suffering and ongoing vulnerability, among which: lack of food and the subsequent adoption of negative coping mechanisms with an estimated 12,000 HHs eating 1 meal or less.

5 - Implementation of activities

-Community mobilization and outreach with the local representatives, conducting orientation sessions with community representatives to explain the project criteria.-Conducting rapid needs assessment and market assessment, current availability and quantity of shelter-related items/services along with the accessibility of markets to local population.-Beneficiaries selection, door-to-door verification (100%)-Coordinating with Hawala office.-Arranging and conducting distribution: selection of distribution sites in coordination with safety and security teams, communicating project locations and dates to the selected beneficiaries, organizing beneficiaries for cash distribution, on-site distribution of multi-purpose grant involving the finance, MEAL, Projects and Hawala agent.Voucher distribution steps will be as the following: a) identification of beneficiaries through their IDs, or other personal documents (a community member will be present with the program teams).b) cross-checking the pre-printed list with all beneficiary ID information.c) distribution the vouchers/coupons to beneficiaries (one-off distribution) in USD currencyVouchers to be paper-based to facilitate the reconciliation process knowing that the voucher paper will be with the beneficiary during the distribution process. Vouchers will be printed in Turkey and moved to Syria.d) redemption of vouchers through money transfer agent (in the same site) after second cross check by finance team along with Hawala agent. - On-site distribution monitoring by MEAL team- Reconciliation process held by finance team ensuring compatibility with redeemed vouchers to release the payment for Hawala office.- Conducting PDM with within 1-2 weeks with a representative sample of the beneficiaries (95% confidence, 7% margin of error) to measure the satisfaction of beneficiaries regarding quality and quantity of support as well as the satisfaction regarding the selection criteria and accountability mechanism.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of special needs during project design/implementation Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with Shafak's policies, which prioritizes specific groups and follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action. Shafak incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitization, Shafak encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: All Shafak staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, Shafak is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and givefeedback on Shafak work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk #1: Losing access to the targeted communities. Likelihoods: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures:A) Mainly good relationship with local communities, B) Targeted communities are consulted during the project. C) Relocation of the project after getting approval from the donors. Risk #2: Interference from the local authorities. Likelihoods: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: A) Carry out community mobilization to ensure accountability and transparency with the targeted communities. B) Suspension of project and reporting to the donor and consider relocation or changing the modality of implementation.C) Continuous negotiation and discussions among the relevant stakeholders. Risk #3: Price inflation. Likelihood. Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures: A) Proper planning and budgeting including contingency planning. B) Competitive procurement process to ensure getting the most acceptable quality and price.C) Budget revisions following approval from donor. D) Changing the project scope/modality after approval from the donor. Risk#4: Turkey-Syria border closure and inability to transfer vouchers: mitigation: a)Shafak will include in the procurement announcement that the vouchers delivery to be in Syria. b) voucher could be printed inside Syria as plan B. Risk#5: tension between IDPs and HC. Shafak will only target IDP sites and will coordinate with the local authorities and explain the project scope/criteria to mitigate any conflict that may arise and ensure appropriate interference from local representatives in such cases.
