Project Summary

Mercy Corps’ Recovery and Assistance Initiative for Borno 3 (RAI Borno 3) will continue to build on and expand the emergency humanitarian response currently taking place under RAI Borno 2. RAI Borno 3 will primarily focus on immediate lifesaving humanitarian protectionneeds – especially those needs faced by new arrivals and people living in overcrowded and underserved camps and host communities. Mercy Corps will target substantial, unmet needs with specific focus on current LGAs (Ngala, Dikwa, Gwoza, and Damboa). Given the fluidity of the situation, Mercy Corps will remain flexible to adjust the geographic areas based on priority needs, access, and ongoing consultations with OFDA. Building on lessons learned, Mercy Corps will introduce a rapid response mechanism allowing us to react quickly and provide life-saving assistance to beneficiaries beyond the initially targeted areas.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Northeast Nigeria is a protection crisis. An estimated 5.2 million people are projected to be in need of protection assistance in 2019. The conflict continues to be characterized by deliberate attacks against civilians, including: killings; sexual violence, in particular against girls and women; abductions; burning of homes and destruction of schools; arbitrary detentions and restrictions on freedom of movement; and targeted use of IEDs. Adolescent girls are particularly at risk. In Ngala, Mercy Corps’ assessment found that many parents have been forcing young daughters into early-arranged marriages as a means of earning a livelihood. Adolescent boys are also a target for non-state armed opposition groups (NSAGs) and security forces, both for recruitment and as suspected enemy combatants. In locations such as Dikwa, Ngala and Gwoza, women and girls are tasked with the responsibility of sourcing firewood. FGDs conducted by Mercy Corps established that fuelwood collection is a core challenge for communities at large and involves a number of protection challenges

2 - Activities

1. Continued engagement with traditional and religious leaders for protection information sharing: Mercy Corps has continually found that the only recourse for survivors of GBV is to go to the traditional and religious leaders to ask for help. These leaders are often ill equipped to handle such cases, and do not understand what means of assistance can and should be provided. Much too frequently, the survivors end up being blamed for the GBV. 2 Recruitment and deployment of community master trainers: Mercy Corps has a pool of master trainers we have worked with in previous interventions. Mercy Corps will seek to engage some of these existing master trainers and recruit others in target locations, which were not covered in previous projects3 Enhance protection coordination, and protection actors’ capacity through training in particular referral pathways: Mercy Corps will continue to strengthen protection coordination in the targeted locations. Mercy Corps will train the relevant actors to ensure they are equipped to provide the minimum agreed standards of service to survivors4 Coordination and management of referrals: In all proposed locations, trained community caseworkers from various agencies are present. Additionally, Mercy Corps has a pool of trained community master trainers in all proposed locations. Cases identified or reported to community leaders, religious and camp leaders will be linked to Mercy Corps’ master trainers.5. Training on PSEA and Do No Harm: Mercy Corps will lead activities on PSEA awareness creation in the communities in the intervention locations.7. Protection actosr coordination: In Dikwa and Ngala, FHI360 is currently coordinating and hosting the GBV sub-sector working groups
