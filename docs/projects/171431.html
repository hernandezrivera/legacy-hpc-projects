Project Summary

The need to strengthen, improve, and increase awareness among exposed communities on reporting and documenting settler violence incidents has been widely identified. Moreover, the need to increase community participation and feedback on the type of responses necessary has been highlighted. Lastly, the essentiality of holding the international community and duty bearers accountable towards the affected communities in terms of IHL and IHRL violations resulting from settler-related incidents has been highly documented as well. Based on the above-identified needs, the purpose of this project is to actively facilitate the participation of communities and ensure their contribution to the methods of mitigating and/or preventing the risk of settler-related incidents. Thus, the resilience and self-protection capacity of the communities most exposed to violence will be supported.
To achieve the above, the project will target 354 direct beneficiaries in identified vulnerable communities, while the entire population of these 53 communities is estimated as indirect beneficiaries. The action, implemented by PUI and Al Haq will contribute to the protection cluster strategic objective SO1: “Increased respect for International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL) and accountability for violations” and will be both remedial and environment building in nature. The project contains four layers of action designed to support the communities and the inter-cluster mechanism led by OCHA. This mechanism was established with the support of PUI and OHCHR, to trigger and coordinate protection as well as material responses to SV incidents. In this framework and beyond the response side, OHCHR and PUI agreed that there is still a need for a deeper analysis of the settler related threats.
The first part of the action will focus on strengthening policy and advocacy efforts. The second part will supplement the first part by strengthening alerting skills at the community level to support the OCHA inter-cluster SV tracking mechanism. The targeted communities are considered to be heavily impacted by SV, but without having incidents comprehensively recorded and triggered in the existing tracking tool. The communities targeted are either located close to new outposts that are thus expected to face severe threats of settler violence and military related operations, or are identified by key stakeholders as highly affected by settler violence, but with a very limited documentation incidents in the inter-cluster tracking tool.
The third part of the action will target clusters of communities identified as highly affected by SV “hot-spots” and exposed to the same sources of threats arising from specific settlement and outposts. The project will facilitate joint discussions on the threats of SV (similar in types and origin) - to foster collective reflection on possible methods of mitigating threats and holding duty bearers accountable.
Lastly, the fourth part of the action will aim at addressing internal community protection risks identified, through the provision of targeted awareness raising and capacity building activities. This layer of intervention addresses the intersectionality of needs of different groups of people within the community face (e.g. PwD, FHHs, etc.). To further reinforce local resilience and self-protection, it is of utmost importance to also address internal protection related risks and threats.
Identification of needs for capacity building & activity implementation will be planned and coordinated together with community members, putting great attention to the community representation reflecting the wide range of characteristics in terms of age, gender and diversity (AGD). The action will specifically emphasize women participation in identifying protection threats, & decision making processes on designing collective strategies, including ways to tackle specific risks faced by diffrent groups of people.

1 - Needs

The project is designed based on various needs assessments conducted by PUI during 2018, 2019 & 2020 with 24 communities across the WB. PUI updated its SV survey in 2018 and reviewed the rankings of 281 communities to list the most at-risk communities as identified by key stakeholders. In June 2019, PUI conducted three regional meetings with main stakeholders monitoring and responding to settler violence. The meetings covered the vulnerabilities of communities across the WB. The list of most exposed communities, when triangulated with incidents documentation from sources such as PUI Early Warning System (implemented under the WB Protection Consortium) and the Protection of Civilians, indicated gaps in incidents reporting from communities highly affected by settler violence. The consultations also pinpointed the need to include communities in proximity to new outposts. In 2020, PUI conducted another series of community and stakeholder consultations to inform a SV gap analysis. The main objectives of the analysis were to systematically identify and document the current key gaps in the interagency, cross-sectorial coordination, prevention, and response to SV incidents. Some of the key gaps and needs identified in the analysis were: 1) Lack of comprehensive documentation of settler harassment despite it having a significant impact on communities’ safe and dignified access to their land (both natural and cultivated areas; 2) Under-reporting of settler related incidents (of property damage and casualties) against particularly area C Communities mainly the Jordan Valley, the South Hebron Hills and in East Jerusalem; 3) Lack of awareness of village councils both in procedures to legal challenges such as the access to land when incidents occur in prior-coordination areas as well as how to register agricultural losses; lack of knowledge about legal possibilities or distrust in the Israeli legal system and frustration over the well-documented impunity. The recommendations in terms of addressing the identified gaps and needs in the most vulnerable communities include adopting the clustering methodology, which supports the cooperation between communities exposed to the same sources of protection threats. This way, communities have the necessary space to share experiences, lessons learned, and develop effective strategies to strengthen their resilience and collectively engage in ways of holding stakeholders accountable for IHL/IHRL violations. It is also a great tool to promote community self-advocacy strategies. Focus is also given to community-based protection interventions, which can be instrumental, like the provision of community capacity building initiatives and the strengthening of the involvement of the communities in designing protective and remedial measures. The gap analysis also identified and documented the different needs, risks, and capacities diverse community members face both as a result of external pressure (SV, environments of impunity, etc.) as well as internal (prevailing societal norms, patriarchy, etc.). While the community-based capacity building and interventions planned will focus mainly on addressing settler-related incidents, attention will also be given to protection issues that may arise from the prevailing inter-community social norms, as well as from the provision of project activities. Based on the outcomes of the gap analysis as well as on the gender analysis PUI is currently developing, specific activities will be designed and/ or tailored to address internal risks and support local capacities to respond to them. In terms of possible negative effects associated with service delivery, a protection and gender risks, prevention and mitigation analysis is available for this project. It will be further enriched and refined through continuous community consultations and the proposed activities will be systematized in a protection and gender mainstreaming action plan specific to the project. It is important to note that the current COVID-19 pandemic has added an extra layer of risk and needs to communities already under immense internal and external stressors. COVID-19 prevention and control measures, including home quarantine, reduced contact with social support networks, loss of livelihoods, the additional economic burdens associated with hygiene measures, halting or changing modality of formal education, all play a significant factor in exacerbating the already compromised psychosocial wellbeing of the community, disproportionately affecting already vulnerable groups of people (like FHHs, PwDs, etc.).Domestic violence is reportedly on the rise, access to life-saving services is compromised for a lot of people and MHPSS needs are also increasing. The new and emerging risk of COVID-19 and how it affects people with diverse needs and capacities differently should be taken to consideration throughout humanitarian service provision.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Community outreach in the targeted communities with the purpose of increasing community participation in identifying needs and improving accountability towards highly affected communities with little or no reporting of settler-related incidents in the OCHA tracking tool. Meetings will be held with the communities to introduce community members to protection principles, to present settler violence trends as well as the objectives of the project. Community representatives will be nominated ensuring a wide representation in terms of age and gender and diversity including persons with and without disabilities. Those representatives will also discuss protection threats both internal and external to their community and needs of support in terms of awareness-raising for community members or types of assistance coordinated through the OCHA tracking tool. To ensure safety in terms of COVID-19 prevention, all the necessary hygiene measures and guidelines will be adhered to, and in case of widespread contamination, remote modes of service delivery (like online or over-the-phone outreach) will be considered, in consultation with the target community. Specific consideration will be taken to ensure all community members enjoy equal access to the modified service delivery mode. Activity 2: Awareness raising and training sessions will be conducted for the selected community members and a number of teachers from each community (to ensure that the knowledge will be passed on through the school system as well), covering the subjects of IHL and IHRL, or other protection related issues, reporting and documentation of settler violence incidents, settler violence trends & settlement expansion trends, analysis and observations, tailored to accommodate the needs highlighted in the initial meetings. Sessions will be facilitated by PUI and Al Haq teams. Coordination and consultations with the education cluster will be ensured before, during, and after the implementation of this activity with teachers. Training will involve 5 members per community and 2 teachers (total target: 210 community members/teachers). As mentioned above, depending on the infection prevalence at the time of implementation, training sessions might be conducted face-to-face, adhering to all the necessary infection prevention and control measures, or remotely taking into consideration the capacities and access for all community members. Activity 3: Joint resilience-building activities for clusters of communities (11 clusters pre-identified including 36 communities). Workshops will tackle the need for joint and collective efforts from communities facing similar threats (in their types and origin) to share strategies to prevent and mitigate the risks and consequences of settler violence and related military operations and hold duty bearers accountable. Initial discussions with community representatives will focus on identifying key members across communities to participate in workshops. Participants (4 members per each community, divided into 11 clusters total target 44) will jointly identify collective needs, preferred responses, and ways to strengthen resilience across the cluster of communities. As above, all necessary prevention measures in regards to C-19 will be respected, while different modalities of service delivery (online training, etc.) will be considered. All changes in service delivery will be adopted only after the appropriate consultations with the target community, to ensure appropriateness. Diverse groups of people (like people with disability, older person) will be included in the consultations to ensure they are able to access the modified (in terms of delivery) activities in an equal manner and to also reflect on possible mitigation measures in case they cannot. Activity 4: The project team will support the clusters in designing strategies, plan implementation, and support with resources by establishing connections with relevant stakeholders or train community members in tools to build self-advocacy campaigns and communication strategies. Additionally, the Action will have an increased focus on intimidation and harassment and how they affect the communities. Besides having communities carefully reporting on all such incidents, Al Haq will complement by monitoring emblematic cases to highlight and further explore this aspect of settler-related incidents.

3 - Indicators

A monitoring and evaluation plan will be developed at the beginning of the project to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets. This plan will describe the whole M&E system, including indicators, tools, breakdown of responsibilities, and communication channels within the organizations and between them. Monitoring visits will be conducted by PUI M&E team to follow up on the outcome and impact of project activities. A complaint and feedback mechanism will be set up to make sure that the opinions of the target population are heard and taken into consideration. Complaints and suggestions related to the design or the implementation of project activities will be timely responded to. Focus group discussions and/or semi-structured surveys will be used to assess the impact of project activities by the end of project implementation. Specific attention will be given to SEA related complaints, by following specific SOPs regarding appropriate reception and investigation, centering around the best interests of the survivor/s. Indicator 1: (outcome indicator) 80% of supported communities increase their rates of SV incidents reporting Indicator 2: (impact indicator) 75% of the community cluster participants report improved coordination between communities exposed to the same sources of threats (disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity). Indicator 3: (outcome indicator) Each cluster of communities targeted by the project implements at least one key action of their designed collective strategy to address the threats of SV Indicator 4: (outcome indicator) All meetings and capacity building sessions include a diverse representation of the communities Indicator 5: 80% of project beneficiaries reporting safety, dignity, meaningful access, accountability and participation were respected during intervention delivery(disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity) Indicator 6: 80% of project beneficiaries reporting that they feel involved in the way the humanitarian assistance is conducted (disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity) Indicator 7: 70% of project beneficiaries report an increased sense of resilience and well-being

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The primary concern of the Action is to respond to the needs of the population affected by protection risks, expressed and identified during consultations, needs assessments, as well as through focus group discussions (FGDs) and surveys. The Action is designed to increase the participation of highly affected communities by settler violence to improve the accountability towards their needs and priorities. During the entire action PUI and Al Haq will ensure the continuous collection of feedback from the targeted communities during the implementation of the project through PUI Feedback & Response Mechanism (FRM). Information cards on the mechanism will be handed out during the initial community outreach and at the beginning of each training and awareness session. Feedback will be collected on a regular basis throughout the project, through verbal communication in the field, or text messages, email, or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards), and responses will be provided for each in a timely manner. Where necessary, adjustments to the implementation of activities may be made based on the received feedback. Feedbacks may originate from beneficiaries, nonbeneficiaries impacted by PUI / Al Haq interventions as well. PUI/ Al Haq will ensure that all communications with the communities (assessments, the FRM, awareness-raising) are accessible to community members with different needs and capacities including people with disabilities, children, and older persons. To do so different diverse means of communication will be utilized to reach those that might traditionally be excluded from the social sphere.It is also important to note that PUI and partners adhere to PUI’s PSEA policy, and its built-in SEA complaint and response mechanisms, ensuring that all SEA related complaints are received and handled with the utmost respect to IASC standards and guidelines.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is crucial in humanitarian aid delivery. PUI is dedicated to ensuring do no harm and dignity to all people accessing services regardless of their personal characteristics. PUI is currently developing a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy to reflect on the type of actions required at each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on the Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy and on the planned community consultations, a project-specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed Action onset. Some of the key intervention areas will be: • A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis is currently available for the project. Nonetheless, Action onset the analysis will be further refined and triangulated with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs, and capacity. The protection and gender analysis will have a special focus on the risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disabilities. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project-specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEA is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects of protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEA, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEA reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism, guided by PUI PSEA policy, is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff, and other stakeholders. SEA investigative procedures are also set and guided by PUI policies, always putting the survivor in the center of any activity. PSEA training will be provided to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness-raising and key messaging on PSEA will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries. • Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed or drafted. At the same time, staff members will be trained on “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “ Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures” . • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some of the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEA and child safeguarding, and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected field staff members will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As described above, PUI is very vigilant in acknowledging and addressing the intersectionality of different characteristics with different needs and capacities. To ensure people with different characteristics and characteristics and coping mechanisms, including people with disabilities, are able to access assistance equally, specific mitigation measures will be adopted. These mitigation measures will derive from community consultations, with people from diverse groups and different vulnerabilities. Action onset a specific vulnerability mapping will take place, to complement the protection and gender analysis. The vulnerability mapping exercise will be participatory and inclusive to all diverse community groups. The outcome of the exercise will be a clearer picture of the kinds of groups within the community that might be experiencing added vulnerability, including the prevalence of disability and types, the prevalence of other protection concerns (e.g. unaccompanied children), age and gender groups with specific needs, etc. The protection and gender risk and mitigation analysis that will be further enriched and complimented throughout the project duration identifies the potential risks and systematizes the response. The community consultations, assessments, and information dissemination methods and modes will be tailored to ensure people with different characteristics are included equally. All data collected during needs assessments or monitoring exercises will be disaggregated according to age gender and diversity, to reflect the unique needs and capacities of different groups of people. The feedback collected from different age, gender, and diversity groups will subsequently inform the way services will be delivered, e.g. Disaggregating awareness-raising groups according to gender to ensure women and girls are comfortable participating meaningfully. Disability mainstreaming is also quite important in ensuring safe access to service provision for all members of the target community. To ensure disability mainstreaming, consultations with people with disabilities and disability organizations active within the community will be pursued to inform the initial vulnerability mapping and protection and gender risks and mitigation analysis. The consultations aim at providing a greater understanding of the prevalence and type of disabilities present in the target community as well as the specific needs and capacities of people living with different disabilities. Those needs and capacities will then be taken into consideration and used to inform the methods and types of activities to be delivered, as well as to mitigate possible risks associated with service delivery for people with disabilities. The inclusion of people with disabilities in community consultations throughout the project will be insured, and all collected data will be disaggregated to reflect age, gender, and diversity including disability. COVID-19 specific issues in terms of access will also be taken into consideration. Specifically, hygiene, prevention, and control measures and protocols will be in place in all planned project activities. The protection and gender analysis will be updated regularly to reflect new emerging needs associated with any enforced changes in the project delivery modalities. These enforced changes in modality will also be part of regular consultations with the communities to identify and assess how they might contribute to the access in humanitarian service provision of different groups of people. Accessible information in different modalities for different groups of people regarding COVID-19 will be provided. Lastly, the continuation of the FRM, regardless of COVID-19 related changes in programming will be ensured.
