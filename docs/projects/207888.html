1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

More than 1.5 million people in Cabo Delgado, Niassa, and Nampula provinces are estimated to be suffering “crisis” or “emergency” levels of hunger, of which over 860,000 are in Cabo Delgado alone, as per the latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis. WFP’s assistance to conflict- and displacement-affected communities in northern Mozambique in the context of a deteriorating humanitarian crisis has a three-pronged approach: 1) Provision of life-saving food assistance through General Food Distributions (GFD) and Food Assistance for Assets (FFA) or Cash for Work (CFW) using different transfer modalities that include in-kind and cash-based modalities, 2) Provision of life-sustaining livelihood support for mid- and long-term food security and 3) Facilitating the provision of complementary assistance for other agencies and organizations. WFP targets 837,294 people in the provinces of Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa, including IDPs, refugees and host communities. WFP is aiming to reach 644,646 people with humanitarian food assistance under Objective 1 of the FSC and 192,648 people under Objective 2. Where security and market conditions allow, assistance will be provided in the form of food vouchers. In-kind assistance will take place in locations where conditions are not conducive for market-based modalities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Objective 1: WFP will continue to prioritize regular lifesaving programme interventions. Considering the context of Cabo Delgado with concurrent returns and protracted displacement, WFP differentiates between resettlement districts and return districts in its targeting approach to reflect these two different contexts. In addition, WFP will apply the following approach to geographic and household targeting, prioritization and rations in its response: Prioritize districts of return: prioritize the allocation of available resources to the districts of return. Mitigate push and pull factors: As much as possible, ensure a balanced distribution of caseloads of return and non-return districts to mitigate the creation of push and pull factors. Seasonality: Apply varying vulnerability thresholds, geographic targeting and rations to calibrate levels of assistance between the lean season and post-harvest. Rations will be reduced by half out of the lean season, maintaining regular rations during the lean season (October to April). Responses to sudden displacements or to people in hard-to-reach areas, including through the Joint Response Programme (JRP), will be considered on an ad-hoc basis, and based on availability of resources. Objective 2: Host communities and resettled displaced populations will be receiving support to emergency livelihoods which aims to restore and strengthen productive assets and create livelihood opportunities. To support social cohesion and peacebuilding, FFA will be implemented in a manner that brings together IDP and host communities for shared objectives and to enable fair sharing of increased resources and assets.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

WFP is committed to ensuring the centrality of protection throughout the humanitarian assistance. WFP takes calculated measures to ensure a conflict sensitive approach so that actions do not negatively affect humanitarian access in the immediate nor long term, considering the rapidly changing dynamics in the evolving conflict. WFP works with humanitarian actors through local and national level coordination groups such as the protection cluster and community engagement/ AAP working group to share and apply feedback from the affected population. Findings from surveys and other community-based assessments are analyzed and reflected in operational planning, in parallel with reports from the CFM as relevant and possible in the dynamic context. WFP is committed to ensuring zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, ensured through codes of conduct, online and face-to-face training, and several reporting mechanisms (see above). As the lead agency for Linha Verde 1458, WFP works with the PSEA network to ensure awareness of the service and to determine constructive approaches to handle SEA cases in a timely manner internally and with the Government as relevant. WFP is committed to action on SEA reports within 24 hours, prioritizing the safety and well-being of the survivor, working in collaboration with WFP’s ethics office and OIGI.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

WFP involves beneficiaries in all stages of the project lifecycle. In the design phase of this project, beneficiaries have been consulted on the choice of modality, considering security, distance from markets and food preferences. WFP has established community committees via a community election process. The beneficiaries engage in consultations via the community committee platform and via focus group discussions ensuring continuous feedback on the assistance provided. Minority and vulnerable groups are targeted for focus group discussions to ensure that they have a voice. Well-established community feedback mechanisms exist, consisting of help desks staffed by CP staff and complaints and feedback boxes at distribution sites. Linha Verde 1458, the tollfree inter-agency hotline, managed by WFP is accessible to the affected population, providing clarifications on beneficiary rights, capturing needs and associated gaps in the assistance as well as linking the affected population to vital services in the cases of SEA, SGBV and child protection. The field office benefits from a designated protection and AAP team that ensures that communications are up to date and that issues raised by the affected population cases followed up on accordingly in order to close the feedback loop in a timely manner. Finally, WFP will continue operating an inter-agency hotline, 'Linha Verde 1458', on the behalf of the HCT.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Understanding the significant access barriers for disabled people to access timely information and attend distributions, WFP works with implementation partners and local authorities to ensure clarity on vulnerability criteria, seeking targeted assistance to directly identify and support people with disabilities to access their entitlements in a safe and fair manner. In addition, WFP works through the protection cluster and in collaboration with the disability working group to identify access barriers and identify measures to address these effectively.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

All program activities will be screened for environmental and social risks during design, at a stage when the implementation details (i.e. location, targeted beneficiaries, inputs, transfer modalities, outputs, and timeline) are being defined, but before the design is finalized. From 2020, The WFP Environmental and Social Safeguards are the set of principles, standards, and tools to avoid or manage the risk of causing environmental or social harm to people, communities, or the natural environment. The WFP Environmental and Social Standards will be used serve as a benchmark for the risk screening and risk management. All the asset creation activities planned as part of the FFA will be analyzed using WFP’s Environmental and Social Risks assessments tools. Risk screening not only helps identify environmental and social risks: each activity will be classified by the level of the risk: low, medium, or high. Any program activity that entails some risks (medium or high) will come with risk management measures that aim to avoid or reduce the risks or mitigate or offset the expected adverse impacts.
