Project Summary

The project adopts a comprehensive household-based food security strategy, strategically enhancing technical skills for climate-smart food production and boosting income to meet fundamental food requirements. This initiative spans both Rohingya camps and nearby host communities.
The household-centered methodology involves a meticulous analysis of income and agricultural production trends within each household. Subsequently, a Family Development Plan and Business Development Plan will be crafted, empowering households to fortify their food security. This includes initiating alternative farm or non-farm-based income-generating activities, with a deliberate focus on the active participation of 80% female members or young women. The project aims to introduce additional income sources by engaging female household members, enabling them to purchase nutritious foods and sell surplus products after family consumption.
To address vulnerability and high-protection risk families, such as those affected by child marriage, gender-based violence, and human trafficking, the project will provide minimum complementary food support. This will be done through consultation and coordination with relevant sectors, camp management, and site authorities.
The project employs a two-step approach, combining immediate income generation and food production through activities like year-round kitchen gardening, poultry and small livestock rearing, with a long-term business plan development for sustained income generation. A strong contextual analysis underpins the project's commitment to sustainable food security at the household level.
Target individuals will undergo training in climate-sensitive food production and business development, encompassing skills in chicken, duck, and goat rearing, as well as homestead gardening. Business capital will be transferred to each household to acquire productive assets for climate-sensitive food production. A community-based purchase committee, with a special emphasis on involving 80% female members, will be formed to enhance negotiation capabilities and market access.
Market linkages will be strengthened for coordinated input and output purchasing. Regular meetings between market actors and target individuals will ensure access to the agro-based value chain. A risk mitigation plan will be developed for the security of food production.
In addition, the project will integrate various issues and approaches, such as Natural Resource Management, Unconditional Cash with E-Voucher Assistance, Disaster Preparedness, Environment Conservation, Food Preservation, Food Safety, Food Utilization, Social Cohesion, Disaster Risk Reduction, Cash For Food, Cash for Livestock, and many more. These interventions will be strategically implemented to create a comprehensive and sustainable impact on the targeted communities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Both the Rohingya and the host community need support for food security because they are under double pressure with the present political unrest situation and price hikes due to Russia-Ukraine War in the country. This is linked to a reduction in labor opportunities, shrinking availability of agricultural land, environmental destruction, restricted access to Naf River (for fishing), and increasing labor and market competition from the displaced community. The ‘Self Reliance Assessment Report’ of BRAC showed that 35% of the host community people had decreased income. The main reasons for the decrease of income were price hike, less work opportunity-ty, reduced wages, agricultural farm and some small businesses got impaired due to Rohingyas being set-tiled (or informally taking the land to settle by themselves) on the land.In the 2023 J-MSNA, food is ientified as a much higher priority compared to the 2021 MSNA. The 2023 J-MSNA explored that households' rated priority needs for access to food in 33 % at host community and 75% at camp level. It is also found that Refugees’ Preferred Modalities of Assistance are interested in 52% from In-kind Assistance, 24% from cash assistance and 19% from combination and mixed modalities. Reduced entitlement of food for Rohingya camp people and decreasing fund continuously will hamper the food security for people at both camp level and host community level. From the past, the people in the Rohingya camps were consuming inadequate food. The people in the host community had a similar situation with inadequate food consumption.

To avoid overlapping with other organizations, Plan International Bangladesh will implement the project in the selected camps. Before the start of any project, Plan International arranges meetings with the NGOs and relevant Government departments in the locality or camps to share the objectives and avoid overlapping. As Teknaf is a disaster prone area and people have skill gap for adaptation to climate and DRR vulnerability, It is essential to support food security resilience of both Rohingya and host communities through climate sensitive food production as well as enhancing the capacity to manage natural resources and disaster risk sustainably. PIB has taken initiatives to adjust its activities in line with the priorities of JRP 2024. Plan is continually working to ensure multi-year funding which has already started in Teknaf. Therefore, the proposed project is fully aligned with the rationalization plan.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the DRM and climate change issues are embedded in the project. The target people are oriented on the preparation and recovery mechanism from the courtyard sessions. As a risk mitigation plan, the target people are linked with the Micro Finance Institutes (MFIs) for savings and loans. so that they may use it as further capital after any disaster. The project encourages environment-friendly technologies for agricultural production, specifically for compost production, vegetable production & fruits production at the household level. The target people will be trained in the preparation of compost also. As a global humanitarian organization, Plan International has a well-equipped DRR response team who are ready to respond immediately after any disaster. There are 2 warehouses of Plan equipped with Non-food items. Plan is responding to the affected people of Sitrang in Teknaf in 2022 and MOCHA at Teknaf in 2023. The project included plantation activities along with the kitchen garden as a long-term impact on climate change. Plan International Bangladesh will assess the Climate Resilience Assessment for both host communities and refugee camp before taking the interventions on climate smart agriculture practices in consultation the people from Department of Agriculture Extension.
