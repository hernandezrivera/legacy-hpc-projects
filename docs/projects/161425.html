Project Summary

OCHA in South Sudan coordinates and leads humanitarian response efforts through effective, principled and gender-appropriate humanitarian action and solutions. Through dedicated support to the HC and HCT, OCHA in South Sudan will: 1) Strengthen strategic coordination at national level (in Juba) and among the main stakeholders including Government, donors UN agencies, non-governmental and international organizations in order to better respond to humanitarian crisis; 2) Strengthen operational coordination at the cluster and state levels and through humanitarian hubs and operational centres to optimize the humanitarian response to people in need; 3) Improve humanitarian access and space to support rapid provision of humanitarian assistance for people in need.

1 - Needs

Narrative to explain the need of the projectYears of conflict, violence and human rights violations have taken an enormous toll on the country’s people. Women and girls have been raped, children separated from their families, homes destroyed and property looted. The conflict is estimated to have led to nearly 400,000 excess deaths in South Sudan’s population between late 2013 and 2018, with around half of the lives lost estimated to be through violence. Up to 900,000 South Sudanese children are afflicted with psychological trauma as a result of witnessing violence or experiencing it directly during attacks on schools or similar violent incidents. Hunger reached a new high in 2019 with nearly 7 million people estimated to be severely food insecure during the lean season between May and July. This is historically the highest number ever recorded in South Sudan. Conflict remains the main driver of food insecurity. Annual crop production has reduced during the conflict, with the 2018 deficit being the lowest since the conflict broke out in 2013. This is likely attributable to lower engagement by households in agriculture and reported erratic rain patterns. Year 2019 saw an upsurge in measles, the leading killer among vaccine-preventable diseases. Every third school has been damaged, destroyed, occupied or closed since the conflict broke out in 2013. Years of eroded livelihoods and prolonged humanitarian assistance have increased aid dependency and people’s capacity to cope with new shocks. One year into the R-ARCSS, more than 2.3 million South Sudanese remain displaced as refugees in six neighbouring countries with another 1.5 million internally displaced. At the same time, many South Sudanese people are cautiously exploring options to return home from internal displacement and abroad. As of August 2019, an estimated 736,000 IDPs had returned to their places of origin since 2016.

2 - Activities

Provide all activities:OCHA will continue to support and facilitate key activities that include: • Chairing of the Humanitarian Coordination Forum, Humanitarian High-level Oversight Committee, Humanitarian Country Team, Inter-cluster Working Group, Operational Working Group, Information Management Working Group, Civil-Military Coordination Group, Access Working Group, Needs Analysis Working Group and the South Sudan Humanitarian Fund Advisory Board• Providing Information Management services to the humanitarian community to inform a rapid, effective and principled response• Facilitating the Humanitarian Programme Cycle including assessments, Humanitarian Needs Overview and the Humanitarian Response Plan• Mobilize and engage humanitarian financing instruments such as the South Sudan Humanitarian Fund (SSHF) and the CERF to meet humanitarian needs• Facilitating Humanitarian Policy work such as on the Humanitarian-Development nexus, AAP, Gender, PSEA and Protection• Facilitate the work of humanitarian hubs and operational centres to enhance coordination for humanitarian preparedness and response• Facilitate support to expand safe access and ensuring humanitarian space/enabling environment
