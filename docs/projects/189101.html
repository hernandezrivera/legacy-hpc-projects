Project Summary

CMC aims at provision of integrated prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition intervnetion in children 6 to 59 months and PLWs through customized intervention mainstreaming protection, enhancing resilience, communities coping mechanisms and sustainability, targeting population with extreme and severe needs under nutrition vulnerability for Persons In Need (PIN) 4&5 mainly disaster affected population within the IPC5 to achieve nutrition cluster objectives CLNUT/CO1, CLNUT/CO2, CLNUT/CO3 & CLNUT/CO4. Tackling root causes and associated causes of malnutrition, treatment of malnutrition related activities and availing critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of vulnerable women, men, girls, boys, and elderly, people with disability and chronic illnesses in hard-to-reach areas of Ageer, Dong Chack, Panyang and Payuel in Duk County and Boma, Kiziongira, Mewun and Marow of Pibor county inline with the humanitarian respond plan 2022 strategies. Pibor is currently classified at extreme with GAM rates of 21.6% while Duk is currently classified as severe. CMC envisages an integrated delivery of nutrition services with health, WASH, FSL and protection sectors. This strategy will enable tackle the direct, indirect and associated root cause of malnutrition such as: food insecurity, suboptimal IYCF practices, inequitable access to safe water and poor hygiene and sanitation practices. CMC through this project will improve malnutrition prevention activities and provide high quality, timely and equitable management of cute malnutrition using the CMAM strategy. The project will assist communities in the two counties including IDPs, returnees and host communities with special emphasis on inclusion of the disabled, elderly and other groups of concern like PLWAs. The project implementation strategy will prioritize emergency lifesaving treatment services targeting children between 6 months and 59 months and pregnant and lactating women in compliance to the GOSS/MOH guidelines. Under 5 children and PLWs remain to be the most at risk and vulnerable groups to malnutrition due to their increased physiological demands driven by rapid growth. CMC nutrition sites both static and outreach will act as centers for integrated programing and response, CMC nutrition team in will provide malaria testing for children admitted in the OTP program and refer them to the health partner for treatment, at the same time CMC team will work hand in hand with the WASH team to provide WASH kits to HHs with malnourished children. The project will also collaborate with the FSL partner to establish kitchen demonstration gardens within the vicinity of the nutrition sites to enhance knowledge on nutritionally recommended practices and provide nutritious locally grown veggies to children admitted in the program and their caretakers. ECDE and stimulation activities will be incorporated into the project. CMC will engage the protection partners to target caretakers of children admitted in the programs to create awareness and educate them on child protection activities and rights. CMC team will include a protection specialist and all CMC nutrition staff will be trained on mainstreaming protection and GBV prevention including GBV referral pathway into nutrition activities, the staff will also be provided with GBV pocket guides for referral where there is no GBV actor. coordination with the clusters working groups, and ensure feedbacks & complains addressed, and participatory safety audit. To enhance sustainability and avoid disruption of services CMC will coordinate with the respective CHDs to take over responsibilities of acting as overseers and coordinate the nutrition activities. Data collected will be adapted to employ gender and age sensitives approaches with disability inclusion to mitigate barriers and obstacles towards improved nutrition.

1 - Needs

South Sudan remains to be among the top ten countries with the highest under five mortality rates at 90.7 deaths per 1000 live births, and 1 in 10 children is not expected to survive to their 5th birthday. This high under 5 mortality rates is contributed to by among other factors; limited or lack of access to adequate health services and preventable illnesses such as diarrhea, malaria and upper respiratory tract infections. 8.3 million people are expected to experience severe food shortage with intensifying shock, while an estimated 2 million persons including 1.3 million children under the age of five of which 302,078 children will develop SAM, 1,039,214 children MAM and 676,000 PLWs are expected to be acutely malnourished (HNO 2022). This will further predispose these women and children to high chances of contracting preventable illnesses and ultimately dying without proper nutrition care. A child with severe acute malnutrition is 12 times more likely to die while a child with mild acute malnutrition is 3 times more likely to die. Communities in South Sudan cutting across host communities, IDPs and returnees perceive food assistance as the primary priority need. Pibor is among counties classified to be in catastrophic severity phase with 200,000 plus people in need and also as a flood risk area. It is also among the 26 counties having a GAM prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15% classified as very high acute malnutrition at 21.6% .The key drivers of acute malnutrition in Pibor food insecurity due to damage of crops and death of livestock as an aftermath of floods, poor feeding practices, high morbidity rates more so from preventable illnesses, poor sanitation and inadequate hygiene at individual and household level, recurrent violence from the intercommunal fighting between the Murle of Pibor and the neighboring Dinka Bor and Lou Nuer and the prevailing economic stress from the COVID 19 restrictions that that disrupted supply chains and caused food prices in the market to reach a level high. To illustrate this 52.9% of households, have unsafe water, 68% of the population are food insecure, 11.8 % of children suffered from diarrhea, 9% of women meet the maternal minimum acceptable diet and only 68.1% of children are exclusively breastfed (FSNMS round 27). These drivers along with long distances to the nutrition sites have proven to be barriers to access to nutrition services in Pibor. The flooding also disrupted movement of nutrition supplies causing delays in arrival of the important supplements thereby forcing caretakers of malnourished children to seek alternatives means as coping measures.

2 - Project activities

CMC will work closely with strategic partners to avail a comprehensive basic package of malnutrition prevention and treatment services at community level and build capacity of service providers to deliver quality malnutrition management services in line with MOH/GOSS guidelines. CMC nutrition team will provide a package of services whose scope of activities will include: 1. Provision of individual and group IYCF counselling to caretakers of children aged 0 to 23 months at CMC’s static and mobile nutrition sites 2. Conduct 2 campaigns in semester 1 and 2 of vitamin A and deworming to under 5 children 3. CMC nutrition team will conduct complementary cooking demonstrations with participation of caretakers both men and women 4. Establishment of kitchen demonstration gardens within the vicinity of static nutrition sites 5. Capacity building of frontline nutrition and health workers including community nutrition volunteers by conducting training on MIYCN, CMAM guidelines mainstreaming protection and integrated with ECD, GMP kitchen garden establishment and cooking demonstrations. 6. Conduct active and passive screenings case findings and referral for treatment, the active screening will employ the popular family MUAC approach where caregivers of malnourished children shall be trained on conducting easy MUAC and urgent referral for care. 7. Admission of children and PLWs with malnutrition to OTP and TSFP programs as appropriate. 8. Strengthen reporting by use of NIS, weekly, monthly and quarterly reports and adaption of this report to learn and improve service delivery including making decisions. 9. Conduct active social mobilization to encourage communities send malnourished children to the nearest nutrition site. CMC will adhere to the COVID 19 guidelines by conducting door to door mobilization campaigns by use of megaphones. 10. Screening and treatment of malaria for children with SAM. CMC shall screen using rapid diagnostic test all children 6 to 59 months admitted with SAM for malaria working hand in hand with the health partner to offer treatment for those found positive for the malaria parasites. 11. Provide WASH kits to children in OTP program. CMC shall collaborate with the WASH partner to provide WASH kits to children admitted in the OTP program. 12. Establish kitchen demonstration gardens at the nutrition sites. CMC will establish kitchen gardens at the vicinity of the nutrition sites. 13. Conduct cooking demonstration sessions to the caregivers of malnourished children 14. Promote community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery in collaboration with the community leaders, a community-based feedback and complaint mechanisms will be established to serve as a platform to collect feedback from the community for documentation, analysis, and resolution in a transparent manner.

3 - Project indicators

An M&E plan will be developed for this project to determine the type of data that need to be collected, frequency of data collection, tools used for data collections, responsible persons. The plan will also track the progress against key results and output indicators and the information captured in different databases. CMC’s M&E team will develop standard cluster approved data collection approaches. They will participate in joint needs assessments together with the cluster’s M&E working groups. Periodic data analysis and interpretation through weekly reporting to the donor, NIS and the 5Ws reporting for the cluster will be established at diverse levels of project implementation and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be engaged for collecting of data in relation to the key indicators and the findings to be incorporated into the CMC’s M&E reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. CMC will utilize sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the project progress towards the end of the project. Some of indicators that will be used to measure the success of this project include: # Number of girls and boys (6-59 months) and PLWs screened for malnutrition by family MUAC, # Of caregivers trained on family MUAC, # of girls and boys (6-59 months) and PLWs admitted for treatment and proportion who recovered and who defaulted, # of primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months who received Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) counselling, # of health workers/ nutrition Staff (M & F) trained in MIYCN,CMAM Guidelines mainstreaming protection and integrating ECD, Proportion of girls and boys admitted with acute malnutrition who are tested positive and treated for malaria, # of girls and boys admitted with severe acute malnutrition receiving WASH kits, # of kitchen gardens for demonstration established and cooking demonstrations conducted and finally # of nutrition sites with functioning community complaints / feedback mechanism. CMC will collect qualitative data about assistance, satisfaction and changes resulting from the project. CMC will conduct periodic Lessons Learnt reviews which fuels the process of constant improvement. All reports will be shared between relevant stakeholders, other partners and clusters. . In order to protect the health of the community and staff and to curtail community transmission of COVID-19, these surveys will be conducted in consideration to COVID-19 situation in South Sudan
