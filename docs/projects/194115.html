Project Summary

The project focuses on ensuring and maintaining access to essential life-saving health, Nutrition (SAM) services and vital public health interventions for refugees and their closest host communities. Direct health service delivery through operationalization of 35 health facilities in 7 states of Sudan (White Nile, East Darfur, South and West Kordofan, Khartoum, Kassala and Gedarif), to cover around 850,000 refugees (South Sudanese, Eritrean and Ethiopian). The plan will be mainly focusing to address the pressing needs for high rates of communicable diseases, lack of basic services delivery and a collapse of basic infrastructure due to impact of the long-standing camps for several years.
The functionality of the clinics through provision of free-of charge health services will be supported in areas (not covered through partners), with medical supplies mainly from country pooled fund, and partially from WHO other sources. The essential public health interventions for the protection of the life, health, and well-being of targeted humanitarian caseload will directly implemented by WHO, NGOs and states MOH through the trainings on case management and treatment protocols, Rapid Response Teams in charge of assessment and initial response to public health events such as alert investigation and initial response implemented by WHO. By using a comprehensive public health approach that allows the timely identification of imminent public health threats and vulnerabilities will guide evidence-based of effective control measures and weekly EWARS (early warning) data will be collected and analyzed.
The expansion and management of emergency health information system and early warning, alert response systems (EWARS), will be established/strengthened to facilitate the prompt identification and timely response to emergencies, that are critical for preventing the avoidable mortality and morbidity amongst the refugees and affected communities, as well as health and well-being of targeted humanitarian case-load in these areas.
Support water quality monitoring missions at refugee and other affected areas to assess free residual chlorine levels and identify source of contamination in sources of drinking waters and respond accordingly. The project support required supplies and equipment as required.
The project support integrated vector control campaigns, the activities will include vector breeding source reduction through environmental management and chemical control of aquatic stages of mosquito vector, adult control though indoor and outdoor space spraying in areas of high transmission and high adult vector density. House inspection and destruction and modification of actual and potential breeding sites for aedes mosquito will be applied in addition to the monitoring of the suppressed adults’ mosquitoes.

1 - Needs

In 2022, Sudan hosts over 1 million refugees that are living across the country with Khartoum and White Nile states hosting the largest refugee populations. While South Sudanese refugees form the largest group with over 799,500 in March 2022, Sudan hosts sizeable groups of refugees and asylum seekers from Eritrea, Ethiopia, Syria, Chad, the Central African Republic and YemenSince November 2020, the crisis in Ethiopia forced more than 59,000 Ethiopian refugees and asylum-seekers into Kassala, Gedarif and Blue Nile State. Furthermore, up to March 2022, some 75,000 South Sudanese have crossed into Sudan due to food insecurity and internal conflict while the situation in the CAR and Chad requires close monitoring with contingency plans in place to respond to any potential large refugee influxesSudan is also a source, transit and destination country for mixed movements of refugees, asylum-seekers and migrants across the sub-region towards Europe (mainly through the Central Mediterranean route) and other destinationsThe assessments showed that the gaps on the access to quality and lifesaving emergency health services are significant in 8 states, where refugees are living in several locations. The health services need to be augmented by support to existing health facilities to cover the needs of an increasing number of people coming from South Sudan and Ethiopia. The most common diseases amongst the refugees are Diarrheas, Acute Respiratory Infections, Malaria and Skin and eye infections, diseases related to poor access to water and sanitation, Healthcare needs are expected to remain high in 2023. The country continues to witness outbreaks of endemic water-borne and vector-borne diseases such as hemorrhagic fever, Hepatitis and malaria. Vaccine-preventable diseases may recur in areas hosting refugees due to weakened children's immunity and lack of immunizations. This may be exacerbated by continued shortages of essential medical supplies and medicines.As of mid-September 2022, a total of 63,280 confirmed cases of COVID-19 were reported across Sudan with 4,961 associated deaths. The case fatality ratio CFR of 7.7 is one of the highest globally and has been so throughout the pandemic. The COVID-19 vaccination coverage is standing at around 10 per cent of the population, with continued effort to bridge the gap through national vaccination campaigns.At least 2,676 suspected Hepatitis E Virus cases, including 24 associated deaths, were reported across Sudan, out of which 2,052 cases were reported from the refugee camps in Gedaref State. While North Darfur State reported 95 cases it had the highest case fatality ratio CFR of 22.3 percent. The level of reported cases of malaria in September 2022 crossed the epidemic threshold in 14 states, with a 2.2-fold increase compared to the same period of last yearSupport for diseases confirmation and public health interventions (surveillance, case management, referral of emergency cases, health education, vector control, vaccination, and inter sectorial coordination) became challenging with the steady increase of refugees caseload and decreased humanitarian funding for Sudan. Women and children are the most vulnerable due to long distances of travel exposing them to gender exploitation or affecting the health care seeking behavior. There is a need for more durable solutions that support the development of opportunities for refugees to gain greater self reliance and strengthen health system, and these solutions require multi year considerations and long term planning. As a result of poor sanitation, weak water infrastructure, and compromised access to chlorinated drinking water, over 850,000 refugees will be at risk of water-related diseases, including cholera.

2 - Activities/Outputs

All WHO field offices are structured with epidemiologists and public health officers for monitoring of the health situation and coordination of the overall health services deliver. • Coordination mechanisms in these states are already established with involvement of all partners on the ground and MOH, to provide access to the refugees and host communities to vector control, SAM management and curative consultation. • Procurement and distribution of emergency medicines (for most common diseases), medical supplies including trauma and cholera kits, and laboratory reagents (PHC level) and Rapid Diagnostic kits for malaria and trauma surgical kits enough to cover essential needs of 850,000 refugees and their closest host communities in 7 states. • Conduct alert investigation missions, including collection of samples and their transport for confirmation, identification of source, initiation of response through outreach and active case finding. • Strengthening of EWARS for weekly collection of epidemiological data and enhanced detection of communicable diseases outbreaks.• Training of medical staff categories on case management, surveillance, and infection prevention control. • Training of CHWs to deliver integrated health promotion messages at clinics including psycho-social counseling and support on mental health. • Monitoring and supervision of the health situation.• Implementation of public health screening at entry points including nutritional status, vaccination status, HIV/AIDS tests.• Training of community volunteers and supervisors on water sampling, protection of water sources, HH level water safe management, and integrated vector control.• Conduct water sampling for quality testing; only operational costs for new caseload are included. The state laboratory personnel and portable water testing kits have been provided through previous funding.• Conduct regular vector control, with a focus before and during rainy season when the campaigns will be conducted at 2 weeks interval (WHO guidelines) when health awareness and education messages.• Support the collection, centralization, and analysis of SAM inpatient care to identify the trends and challenges (admission, recovery, death, defaulter, re admission rates) dis aggregated by sex and age. Disseminate to all stakeholders.• Strengthen the linkages with communities and their awareness through active social mobilization conducted by trained community mobilizers/CHW in coordination with UNICEF to promote early treatment and referral of SAM patients

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

All planned activities in this project will use environmentally friendly procedures and equipment, support appropriate waste management of biological materials, medicines, sharp medical tools, disinfectants, lab reagents etc to prevent contamination of staff, other patients and environment outside the clinics and to prevent severe risks for population living in the areas. The training program of WHO includes modules on Infection Prevention and Universal Precautions that includes infection prevention, injection safety and solid and liquid medical waste safe disposal for all levels of care.
