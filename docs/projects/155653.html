Project Summary

Through the proposed intervention Prime Foundation (PF) focusing the provision of Primary Health Care (PHCs) services in Orakzai tribal district, PF will restore PHCs service through static health units and mobile health camps. Services include the provision of medical consultations and treatment for various infectious diseases, outpatient management of acute and chronic health problems, routine EPI, delivery of anti-natal, natal & post-natal care and health and hygiene awareness through community outreach. Beside this, PF will also do the minor repair work in the existing health facilities along with tools provision. PF is targeting approximately 51,870 individuals (girls, boys, women and men, elderly and people with disability) through the proposed interventions.

Needs

Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) of Pakistan is one of the disadvantaged regions in Pakistan and has been through a turbulent phase since 2008, due to reprobate activities by the non-state actors, resulting in displacement of millions of people from FATA to nearby districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and other provinces. The destabilization affected the delivery of social services within FATA. Over the last few years, the security situation in FATA has markedly improved due to concerted efforts of the government law enforcement agencies and civil institutes and about 98% TDPs have voluntarily returned to their homes in Khyber, Kurram, Orakzai, South Waziristan and North Waziristan, while only 2% in displacement. In Orakzai, 28,797 registered families have returned till June 2018. Access to health and other social services in the return areas are poor as a result of damaged hospitals, schools and water facilities. As previously there were no comprehensive assessments been conducted in the region to assess the availability of health services therefore WHO conducted HeRAMS in the IDPs hosting and returnee areas which was completed in April 2018.In Orakzai, tribal district, the assessment team visited 53 health facilities out of 71. The remaining health facilities could not be visited to security reasons. The needs / gaps related to health sector in Orakzai, tribal district identified through this assessment are listed below. A.There are 71 health facilities in the tribal district, out of which 14 are non- functional. Out of 57 functional health facilities, 6 are not providing any health services and the ones which are functional are providing basis health services that are also very much constrained due to structural damages in the health facilities ranging from partial to extreme damages, lack of basic furniture, medical equipment & supplies and cold chain equipment. Out of the visited health facilities, 27 were overall damaged, 21 were partially damaged and 6 were extremely damaged.B.There is dearth of all cadre human resource especially Medical Officers, Women Medical Officers and Female Paramedics, Health Technicians, Lab Technicians and health educators in different categories of health facilities. C.There is massive gap of basic lab facilities at most of the centers. There are 23 primary care facilities of various descriptions that are without the required equipment and instruments and HR.D.The minimum health care package recommends that all health facilities (CD, BHU, RHC and AHQ) should provide immunization services. The availability of immunization services is generally poor. About 52 primary care facilities lack the capacity to provide routine immunization under EPI due to lack of human resource and cold chain issues due to power outages.E.The overall situation of IMNCI protocols is poor in the tribal district. There is no application of IMNCI protocols in 52 primary care facilities of various descriptions.F.Availability of antenatal services are very low at all level of health facilities due to dearth of technical human and material resources. There are no antenatal services available in 52 primary care facilities of various descriptions.G.Availability of EmONC services are very low at all level of health facilities due to dearth of technical human and material resources. There are no EmONC services available in 30 primary care facilities of various descriptions.H.Availability of Post-Partum care services are very low at all level of health facilities due to dearth of technical human and material resources. There are no Post-Partum care services available in 30 primary care facilities of various descriptions.I.Lack of services for non-communicable diseases and psycho-social support among both the genders of population.

Activities

A.Rehabilitation of Health Facilities: Prime Foundation will rehabilitate Five Government health facilities based on a rapid need assessment. The tentative rehabilitation work will include Repairs, e.g. masonry work, electric supply, whitewash, repair of cupboards, tables & chairs. Provision of basic equipment, furniture, Cold chain equipment etc. Rehabilitate the existing water system (water tanks, pipes, sanitary work) within health facilities and latrines to ensure adequate water and waste managementB.Human Resource Support: PF will provide Human Resource Support at each health facility including medical officer, male paramedic, LHV, lab technician, dispenser, community health workers and security guardC.Capacity Building of Health Care Providers on DEWs: A one day training event on Disease Early Warning System will be arranged by PF to build the capacity of Government health care providers and PF medical team in early detection, notifying and response mechanism of an outbreak. D.Provision of Primary Health Care Services at the health facility and through Mobile Camps: Provision of free access to primary health care services (consultations, treatment, medicines and referral) will be made available at the targeted health facilities and mobile health camps. PF will ensure that adequate medical staff and basic equipment are available at the target health facilities and mobile health camps. E.Diagnostic Services: Target health facilities will be equipped with laboratory equipment for conducting basic tests. These tests will be done free of cost. Functions of the laboratories will be limited to routine investigations while more advanced investigations will be referred to the nearest Hospital laboratoryF.Referral Mechanism: Referral mechanism will be established to refer complicated cases to the nearest hospital. These patients will be transported to the hospital through an ambulance, free of cost. G.Health Committee formation: In order to enhance the ownership and sustainability of the project, health committees will be formed at village level. The committee members will include members of local communities, Directorate of health staff and local religious & political influencers. Each committee will comprise of 5 – 10 membersH.Treatment of Communicable diseases: Qualified doctors deputed at the health facilities and mobile health camps will provide treatment of communicable diseases and free of cost medicines will be provided. National guidelines and protocols like Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness (IMNCI), will be complied with for the treatment of these illnesses. I.Care Before, during and After Delivery: PF will try to make the motherhood as safe as possible during crisis situations by providing antenatal care, natal care and postnatal care. Pregnant and lactating women will be given awareness about Infant & Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices with special focus on early initiation of breast feeding, exclusive breast feeding, dietary diversity and frequency, vaccination, etc. These messages will be reinforced at the health facility and also at the community level.J.Treatment of NCDs, Injury and Mental Health: Qualified doctors appointed at the target health facilities and mobile health camps will provide treatment to patients against NCDs, Injuries and Mental Health free of cost. K.Community Outreach: Community Health Workers (CHWs) will be responsible for providing health education, behavior change activities, to the communities and referral of patients from the community to the health facility. CHWs will be trained on disseminating key health messages designed to increase the adoption of key preventative health practices and improve health seeking behaviors.
