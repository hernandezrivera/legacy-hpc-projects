Project Summary

The project aims to address access to civil, identity and other documentation, basic services, and housing, land and property issues faced by both refugees and host communities through information dissemination, counselling and legal assistance.
In order to prioritize safety and dignity of refugees over other considerations, ICLA will conduct protection risk assessments (Do no Harm), adopt conflict and context sensitive approach to dealing with beneficiaries, ensure informed beneficiary consent and acting on instructions of beneficiaries, securing confidentiality and data protection. In addition, ICLA will use additional protection technical expertise to supplement its interventions; coordinate with specialised protection actors to assist with conducting protection risk assessment of ICLA beneficiaries, carry out combined ICLA/protection campaign around protection issues; in collaboration with gender / child protection actors, conduct joint legal and protection research around a particular protection issue or risk in order to inform programming or recruit staff with expert protection skills in NRC. For example, although the Bangladesh legal framework might enshrine certain rights and entitlements that NRC would ideally like to pursue for refugees, NRC will not do so, if the process of accessing justice were assessed as likely to do harm to the claimants.
NRC’s Protection Policy, Gender Policy, Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy and guidance notes and tools including Core Competency Quality Assessment tools and the Protection Resource Pack all contribute to reinforcing and providing guidance on how to action the protection policy. NRC’s Code of Conduct holds staff, partners and associates (e.g. contractors) accountable, while projecting a common set of standards to the beneficiaries being served. PSEA prevention and response training is part of the induction for all staff.
ICLA will support multi-sectoral, sustainable, and integrated programming with the NRC Camp Management (CM) and Education programmes and with other humanitarian actors, which will allow people to preserve or increase their resources and opportunities, thus making them more resilient, and more likely to find durable solutions. ICLA will conduct needs assessments, service mapping and where relevant, refer to other NRC core competencies or other partners providing specific services.
NRC will actively and regularly participate in the PWG and its Sub-sectors and relevant Task-teams and collaborate with SMS and CwC on awareness raising, sensitisation and information dissemination activities to ensure that refugees have meaningful access to information and feedback mechanisms. In addition, ICLA will work to include training on basic CDR principles and skills in the package of training to be provided on/to Camp Community Governance structure, for NRC SMS sites at a minimum and, if possible, sector-wide. ICLA will conduct joint planning and harmonize interventions on developing CDR skills with PWG and Education through life-skills programme. Moreover, ICLA will ensure to prioritise advocacy activities aimed at removing barriers to access protection and assistance as jointly identified by all PWG.
NRC ICLA aspires to have a local partner to ensure sustainability of the programme once ICLA exit the country. Assessment is currently ongoing to identify possible partner(s). The partnership modality could be one of sub-granting or of no-cost cooperation based on skills-sharing.
NRC ICLA will support SMS, CwC and PWG in the assessment, dissemination of information and implement referral pathways in order to ensure coordinated prevention and response to seasonal emergency.

Needs

Cox’s Bazar has experienced prolonged and continuous influx of refugees from Myanmar for several decades. As of 15 October 2018, there are 897,733 Rohingya individuals / 206,845 families living in Cox’s Bazar district, of which 80% arrived between August and December 2017. Out of these, 34,172 registered Rohingya refugees/6,321 and over 213,000 of undocumented refugees have been living in Cox’s Bazar District since 1991-2. The responses by the Bangladeshi government (GoB) to Rohingya refugee crisis have focused on enabling transitory relief and repatriation. Apart from those recognised refugees who acquired prima facie legal status in the 90s based on an executive decision, Rohingya refugees are considered foreigners in Bangladesh, with limited access to civil and identity documents, accredited education or other public services, including access to the justice system and with restricted freedom of movement and choice of residence.Most Rohingya refugees are located in refugee camps (RC): 67% in Kutapalong RC and extension site, 28% in other RCs and only 5% in host communities. Severe congestion, aid dependency, insufficient water and sanitation facilities, risks of outbreaks of diseases and casualties from landslides, floods, cyclones and monsoons remain the critical challenge to effective service provision. Settling mostly in the congested Ukhia and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’ Bazar District, the presence of the refugee has put an immense strain on the half a million Bangladeshis who live there, impacting access to livelihood and employment, agriculture and environment to name a few. Competition for limited resources and communication challenges (language barriers) have resulted in increasing polarisation and disputes, including violence between the communities.The Protection Sector partners have identified a number of unmet needs that require prioritisation including, service provision for the host communities to support peaceful coexistence and conflict prevention and the need to strengthen linkages between refugees and host communities; need to further strengthen the role of the community and community-based protection initiatives and mechanisms; lack of livelihoods and self-reliance opportunities and the need for cash-based interventions to support self-reliance; youth and adolescent engagement and education, including skills training for both refugees and protection staff; impunity and lack of rule of law and access to justice and alternative dispute resolution; the need for trained law enforcement presence in the camps; the need for camp governance based on inclusion, representation, participation and accountability; and inclusion, protection and gender mainstreaming across sectors and in the overall response. Sustained advocacy efforts are needed to address other protection concerns and to recognize the legal status of refugees in Bangladesh in order to progressively achieve an enhanced protection environment enabling enjoyment of basic rights.The displacement has disproportionally affected women and children who comprise the majority of the refugees (52% are women and girls; 55% are children under 18). Sensitivities to issues of gender is critical to ensuring that even the most vulnerable and marginalized refugees benefit from relief, services and information.

Activities or outputs

NRC/ICLA activities will be complementary to other existing Protection activities in order to avoid duplication and ensure holistic protection interventions. Information dissemination and counselling: ICLA’s main priorities will be to empower refugee women, men, girls and boys to have equal access to information and services. In coordination with Communication with Communities (CwC) and Site Management Sector (SMS) and the Protection Working Group (PWG), ICLA will develop information and communication products to support refugees and host communities’ access to basic services, rights and entitlements in and outside of the camps. Accurate, timely and relevant legal information and awareness-raising messages, such as the relevance of registration for access to rights, entitlements and services, will also be disseminated through outreach activities (in cooperation with the CwC working group), including face-to-face information and counselling by ICLA field staff. ICLA will also use a community-based and participatory approach to identify and train members of the communities to disseminate information, recognise protection concerns and refer cases to specialised service providers. Legal assistance and/or collaborative dispute resolution: Subject to needs assessment, ICLA may use judicial and administrative remedies, customary or religious mechanisms and collaborative dispute resolution methodologies to address refugees and host communities’ access to justice, including housing land and property issues. Approaches and procedures will be selected on a case-by-case basis to allow for a customised response that meets the specific needs and interests of (a) client(s). In this respect, ICLA officers and/or partners may provide legal information and advice, carry out conflict coaching or facilitate negotiations, and if relevant, provide legal representation. Capacity Building: Subject to needs assessment, ICLA will also provide technical support (sensitisation, training, orientation, etc.) to local NGOs, youth and women leaders and community representatives, as well as humanitarian actors with a view to strengthening their capacity to ensure protection to Rohingya refugees and support host communities. Particular emphasis will be placed on international humanitarian, human rights and refugee law and laws, rules and regulations about the rights and issues of women and other vulnerable groups. ICLA may also provide capacity building on organisational management – client handling, service delivery and accountability. Advocacy: Research and technical support on legal status, freedom of movement, civil and identity documents, access to rights and services and adequate housing will be used toinform the major protection actors,donor community, and other relevant stakeholders, with the respective analyses of the current protection situation, strengthen the networks and seek allies in order to influence policies and decision making in relation to the targeted population. In coordination with Shelter and Protection sectors, ICLA will support the GoB and local partners in enhancing peaceful coexistence and social cohesion within and between refugees and host communities. ICLA will also continue to explore avenues for implementing HLP programming (as informed by the 2018 rent assessment, underway at the time of the JRP submission), and will also explore strengthening host community household economies.
