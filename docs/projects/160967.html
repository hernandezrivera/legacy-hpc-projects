Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to provide timely and critical lifesaving ES/NFIs to 15,000 (4,000 girls 6,000 women, and 2,000 boys and 3,000 men) conflict affected IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host communities in Unity State. The project targets Panyijiar (3000), Rubkona (3000), Mayom (3000) Leer (3000) and Mayendit (3000) that has experienced a cycle of violence and flooding that has made it difficult to access for a long time. The same conflict has displaced many of the target population to neighboring settlement areas, POCs or neighboring countries and is now returning to settle back in their original homes and is in need of immediate ES/NFI support and or resilience support. The host communities in these areas are getting it hard to cope with the number of returnees who are in need of their support and care with the little resources that they have. Several strategies will be used including joint interventions with other clusters such as FSL, WASH, CCCM, and ensuring that our activities and the whole intervention complements other sectors and partners activities and not duplication. HRSS using ES/NFI cluster strategy and methodologies will provide a static 80% in-kind and 20% cash based intervention where markets are functional that includes more HLP engagement, to enhance resilience building and copying mechanisms. HRSS through a multi-sectoral approach will mainstream and integrate protection, health, WASH, Nutrition, Education, FSL in its intervention through intentional massaging, joint missions among others and will integrate HLP, MHPSS, PSEA, and GBV issues and ensure gender balanced teams. HRSS has a wide experience in the locations currently implementing CCCM in Panyijiar supported by SSHF, monitoring refugee returnees and offering protection including NFIs distribution in Panyijiar and Mayendit supported by UNHCR and implementing protection and livelihoods in Rubkona supported by UNDP and UN Women. HRSS has 3 trained staff on ES/NFI cluster methodologies based in Juba and other 5 staff being trained by ADA and WVI in Mayendit and Rubkona and a compound in Rubkona, Mayendit South, Panyijiar and is using its compound in Leer as a humanitarian hub. HRSS proposes to intervene in three (3) cluster objectives as follows:
1. Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving NFIs to newly displaced or populations with new vulnerabilities.
2. Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees and host communities/affected but not displaced unable to meet their ESNFI needs
4. Enhance the community’s dignity and increase their ability to respond to new shocks and build on existing skills

1 - Needs

UNHCR estimates in its fact-sheet report of August 2019 that there is 1.82 million internally displaced persons in South Sudan, 10% of which are staying inside six PoCs. The UNHCR report indicates that there are 209,071 refugees who have returned to South Sudan in a self-organized manner from November 2017 until 31 August 2019. The September 2018, DTM IOM survey in Bentiu and Rubkona towns indicated that a significant majority of the residents of both towns expected more returnees (Bentiu 91%, Rubkona 96%) and IDPs (Bentiu 84%, Rubkona 94%) to settle in the towns in the future and will be friendly to them. This is the reason why there are many IDP returnees in Rubkona at the moment. 70% of households in Bentiu and 57% of households in Rubkona reported perceived additional pressure on little resources due to returning populations, households in Rubkona town reported higher levels of inadequate infrastructure compared to Bentiu town, and sharing food distributions and other items such as S/NFIs is the primary way host communities support IDPs and returnees. Nearly 16,000 South Sudanese refugees returned, mainly from Sudan, in August 2019, according to UNHCR. HRP 2019 estimates 74,804 people in need in Panyijiar, 14,049 IDPs as per DTM reports in June 2019. Many returnees from Sudan have been reported to be trickling back via port of Tayar Port and Mayom Counties back to where livelihoods and shelter were destroyed without resilience support because the community safety nets have been disintegrated during conflict and needs copying mechanisms. The Humanitarian Snapshot by UNOCHA of September 2019 intimates that during August 2019, seasonal floods have affected more than 364,600 people in different locations including about 4,000 people in Mayendit County in Unity State. The heavy rains have destroyed shelters, crops, water sources, public infrastructure like schools and health facilities, increased the risk of water-borne diseases among communities, and rendered many roads impassable, constraining access to affected areas. An inter-agency flood crises assessment report in Makuer Payam neighbouring payams, South Mayendit in August 2019 that Hope Restoration participated, the total number of flood affected people were 1,563 individuals (520HHs) with high percentage of women headed family, youth and children. The flood crises has now spread to many areas of Unity State including Mayendit, Panyijiar, Leer, Mayom and Rubkona destroying property, shelter and displacing thousands of people leaving them homeless and hopeless. Women, persons with specific needs and children are the most affected. Hope Restoration has been monitoring and registering refugee returnees in various entry points of Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar with support from UNHCR and many of these returnees come carrying nothing with them and with very high expectations making them the most vulnerable people in South Sudan. A total of 2,920 returnees have been received and registered by HRSS team in Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar counties since June 2019 to end of September 2019. Over 80% of these returnees want to permanently stay in their areas of origin if only the community facilities pull factors and services are available or improved. Some of the pull factors are related to taking care of personal or ancestral land and property, availability of basic services and shelter among others. During this time, one of the question and challenge has been; after registration, what else? In October 2019, HRSS FGDs in Leer, Rubkona, Mayom, Mayendit and Panyijiar to assess the conditions of the shared community resources and the need for building/improving their conditions and improve local resilience of returnees (IDPs and refugees). HRSS learnt from the FGDs that the massive spontaneous return of people to their original homes has led to an increase in the population that has led to depletion of community shared resources affecting persons with specific needs, women and children.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. HRSS is mainstreaming protection in its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information during assessment and also during distribution of items and that the whole process is confidential. HRSS is a lead national NGO on GBV in Unity State and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. HRSS with other partners have developed a referral pathway in Leer (that includes Panyijiar), Mayendit and Rubkona and will also ensure that the distribution sites are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the distribution locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools HRSS will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in-charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. HRSS staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs including NFIs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. HRSS is also an active member of Protection Cluster.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

HRSS will participatory implement this project in a conflict sensitive manner, ensuring that inclusive participation of all stakeholders and in particular the beneficiaries in all stages of this project cycle. Participation will be enhanced through conducting participatory needs assessment in an inclusive and participatory manner and frequent consultations of key beneficiary groups in decision making particularly women. HRSS will spend at least two weeks in the field to engage and work closely with beneficiaries to set up a project implementation committee that will last throughout the project, nominated by the target beneficiaries that are composed of beneficiaries. This committee will share with all the beneficiaries including persons with disabilities, the elderly, those who are illiterate, women and girls issues agreed upon during their meetings such as types of assistance needed, safety audits, distribution locations and sites. HRSS will also set up a distribution committee composed of protection specialists, beneficiaries and local leadership to monitor the distribution process, and to provide feedback to HRSS on issues concerning protection such as SEA, HLP issues, livelihoods, the distribution process and the whole intervention process from the beginning of the project. HRSS will also use the presence of beneficiaries to pass messages such as health, livelihoods, SGBV, HLP among others through messaging and awareness creation. The feedback team will be composed of the project implementation committee representatives, representatives of various constituencies of beneficiaries and HRSS’s protection advisor. Conducting both pre and post distribution awareness and monitoring respectively will provide feedback from the various groups of beneficiaries. Reports developed by HRSS will be available for community, partners and even government authorities to access for references. This action will address key lifesaving threats identified during needs assessment.
