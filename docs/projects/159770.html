Project Summary

Gedo, Lower Juba, Bay regions have 441,000 people in IPC 3&4 combined and are need of humanitarian assistance (FSNAU/FEWSNET Sept. 2019). In the IDPs settlements in these regions there are significant lack of access to clean water sources and poor sanitation facilities increasing the risk of AWD/Cholera outbreaks. A quick survey by SADO undertaken recently in these districts revealed that about 60-75% of respondents in the IDP camps had access to unsafe water from unprotected shallow wells, while 25% of host community respondents were unsure of the quality and safety of the water they used and have minimal knowledge of how to treat water and their capacity to store water is severely limited. Most of the latrines in the IDPs camps are either damaged or filled up, posing a major health risk. The drought conditions from late 2018 through mid-May 2019, has affected water availability in pastoral areas in rural areas regions. These regions will continue to experience water scarcity in late 2019. Water pans in these regions have either dried or the salinity level has become very high in some shallow wells. Water is not affordable for many vulnerable households. In Bardera district, 73% of the population indicated access to drinking water as their most urgent need (WASH cluster assessment sept 2019)
SADO’s proposed response is designed in alignment with the WASH priorities and objectives and will focus on improving access to WASH services targeting support to both displaced persons living in urban centres and preventing displacement in rural areas in Gedo, Lower Juba and Bay regions. The targeted populations are considered to be in extreme WASH vulnerability due to drought related displacement resulting in high gaps in terms of access to safe and affordable water, clean sanitation facilities and good hygiene practices as well as protection services. The districts of Bardera, Elwak, Garbaharey and Kismayo and Baidoa are generally considered AWD/ cholera prone areas due to the inadequate access to water and poor hygiene practices.
This action seeks to address both immediate urgent WASH needs and long – term WASH gaps of target beneficiaries by improving access to safe and sustainable water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion. The activities will seek to improve the management and sustainability of WASH services by rehabilitating strategic water sources (1 borehole, 5 shallow wells and 1 sub–urban piped water system) and sanitation infrastructures (repair / desludging of 180 latrines and installation of hand washing unit and providing 60 solar lighting to provide protection to women and girls); delivering 2000 hygiene kits and hygiene promotion in all locations targeted with water, sanitation so has to have a complete WASH action. To ensure acceptance SADO will engage with the government and other WASH, health partners to ensure no duplication and overlapping of interventions. The target number of people for the project is 22,145 people.

Project Activities

Activity 1: Conduct WASH needs assessment in targeted areas to determine the gaps and needs. FGDs will be done separately to ensure that the views of women and men are captured. Activity 2: Rehabilitation of strategic water points: 2A: Rehabilitation of 5 shallow wells - In order to cover the additional water needs and to reduce public health risks associated with obtaining water from unprotected shallow wells, SADO will rehabilitate and upgrade 5 shallow wells in Kismayo, Bardera and Baidao to serve 2400 people. This activity will involve de-silting, cleaning the intake and deepening of the wells to increase the yields. 2B: Rehabilitation of one borehole: SADO’s proposed action will rehabilitate one existing borehole in Tullo Barwaqo in Garbaharey and separate points for human and livestock watering. SADO will undertake the repair / extend water distribution systems, facilitating a separation from livestock water resources, and providing water collection points that are easily accessible to women and girls. SADO will make water line connections to kiosks, and women and girls will be consulted on their views to help decide the water point locations. This will ensure that there is equitable access to safe water, that the distance to collection points is maintained within acceptable standards, and that water kiosks are secure for women and girls at all times. This activity will benefit 5000 persons as per the sphere standards2C: Rehabilitation of sub-urban distribution water system- In Bardera urban, SADO will repair of 1 sub–urban piped water system and underground tank to improve the filtering and treatment process & chlorination. Extension of the of water distribution system will be undertaken to provide water collection points that are easily accessible to women and girls. This will serve 5000 people. Activity 3: Capacity building - SADO will revive and train 7 water management committees for each of the water assets i.e. one borehole, 5 shallow wells, I sub – urban water system) each consisting of 5 persons (35 persons in total). Thus, one committee will be established per village that benefits from water supply interventions. The capacities of the existing committees will be reviewed and areas for strengthening will be identified by SADO. Activity 4: Collection of garbage: SADO will improve on the community management of waste through mobilisation and awareness of the appropriate waste management practices. SADO will provide sanitation tool sets to groups for house hold latrine digging and waste management. A total of 25 kits will be distributed to 25 groups comprised of 15 members each. A total of 375 households will be reached (2250 people) Activity 5: Desludging of Latrines -Total of 180 latrines will desludged and 60 solar lighting provided for each 3cubile latrine with one handwashing unit. Existing private waste exhausters will be identified, trained and engaged in desludging the latrines. This will lead to effective faecal sludge management and sustained use of the latrines. Each of the latrine will be shared by 5 hhs totalling to 5400 people reached. Activity 6: Hygiene kits and hygiene promotion - 2000 hygiene kits will distributed to vulnerable IDP households with each household receiving: 2x Non-collapsible jerry cans (20L); 3 x pieces of soap; 3month supply of aqua tabs and sanitary items, including sanitary pads. The target is 2000 people. Activity 7: 60 community hygiene promoters will have recruited and trained on hygiene promotion activities and skills. Women will constitute a higher percentage of the hygiene promoters as they are the primary caregivers at domestic level and will therefore reach other women and children. Hygiene promoters will target the beneficiaries benefiting from access to water and hygiene kits. The target is 22,860

Mitigation Measures

Interventions will be implemented in a non-discriminatory and impartial manner that promotes the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance. This will be supported by the establishment of the local village relief committees. Protection measures will be implemented throughout the project ensuring the needs of men and women, the elderly, children, and the disabled are taken into account. These measures include, among others: • Sex and age disaggregated data as well as conditions of vulnerability; • Sensitizing the CoC regarding Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (SEA) to project staff and RCs; • Beneficiaries will be sensitized to the nature of the assistance and an accountability system will be established for feedback and reporting; • The timing and location of beneficiary registration will be organized to accommodate women’s roles as caregivers and the needs of vulnerable groups. As well, community based targeting will be used to minimize exclusion based on gender and age.A Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) will be set up during project initiation stage with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards and procedures. They will be provided with brochures, banners and pamphlets containing these standards and for those unable to read or in the case of children will receive visual illustrations from SADO field based staff on the accountability standards. A toll free telephone contacts will be provided through which they can call and register their complaints. A database will be developed by our IT staff in which all the complaints will be recorded and appropriate redress taken within the shortest time possible and recorded as well. All complaints will be responded to within a maximum period of 72 hours and the responses will take into consideration the specific needs of women, men, girls and boys. This field based CRM database will be analyzed on a monthly basis. During meetings with project beneficiaries, 2 desks will be set up, one manned by a male staff and another by a female staff to receive feedback from beneficiaries. Child friendly accountability standards will also be displayed in these meetings.

Please provide further explanation

SADO will only implement the project in accessible areas in rural, peri urban areas, and urban areas where IDPs have settled after being displaced. SADO is accepted in the project areas through on-going and recently completed project (SHF funded projects Gedo). SADO will conduct strong community mobilization at the outset of the project and will identify the WASH assets to be worked on through a consultative process with the target communities, in addition to the information gathered during the needs assessment. By working closely with the targeted communities, engaging local leadership and prioritizing the sensitization of communities to support activities and participate in project design and implementation, this ensure sustainability of the project. Prior to the start of any project, SADO engages with the government and other stakeholders to avoid duplication of efforts and to learn new innovative approaches and technologies in WASH. Before rehabilitation of a community asset, MOU is signed to show that the community is willing to participate in the rehabilitation of the WASH asset. This way the beneficiaries are aware of their entitlement prior to the commencement of the project.
