Project Summary

Zimbabwe is at risk of multiple hydro-meteorological hazards namely drought, flooding and diarrhea diseases epidemics (Cholera and Typhoid). The projection of the El Niño phenomenon will negatively affect the provision of safe water thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases among the vulnerable communities. Currently, the country is experiencing a cholera outbreak in Harare and 17 other districts. As of 07 December 2018, the country recorded a cumulative 10,550 (10,269 suspected and 281 confirmed) cases and 59 deaths with a case fatality rate of 0.56%. The WASH Response to the eminent El Niño phenomenon and risk of cholera will be guided by three (3) strategic objectives – (i) Restore access to sufficient water of appropriate quality and quantity to fulfil basic needs, (ii) Increase awareness of safe hygiene and sanitation practices, with a focus on participatory health and hygiene education (PHHE) and water conservation and (iii) Provide access to critical WASH related Non-food items (NFIs), with a focus on the most vulnerable families in the targeted areas. The response is specifically targeted at WASH needs arising from and aggravated by the current cholera and eminent drought

Needs

The drought will negatively impact surface and underground water sources in the country. According to the Rural WASH Information Management System (RWIMS) of October 2018, 28.8% of the water points nationally are seasonal and only 12.74% are good yielding and perennial. The same RWIMS report points out that 49.4% of water points (mainly hand pumps) are fully functional. The projection of the El Niño phenomenon will negatively affect the provision of safe water thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases among the vulnerable community members. The risk of drought will further increase the burden (especially for children and women) of fetching water. As of April 2018, the ZIMVAC Assessment report showed that 16% of households travelled more than 1km to fetch water from the nearest main water source. Matebeleland South, Matebeleland North, Midlands and Masvingo provinces showed the highest number of households travelling more than 1km to fetch water. This is expected to increase as more water sources dry up. The risk of drought situation will result in reduced water yields in the functioning boreholes exacerbating the risk to water related diseases especially diarrhea and cholera

Activities

• Provide or re-establish access to safe water through the construction of new sources and or repair / rehabilitation of existing sources. This includes identification of alternative water sources, and or expansion/ upgrading of perennial sources to cover needs of additional population. This will cover communities and institutions such as health centers and schools in affected districts• Strengthen water quality sampling, monitoring and analysis• Promotion of critical health, hygiene and sanitation practices (including household water treatment and storage) to reduce the risk of the WASH related diseases.• Provision of hygiene kits to vulnerable communities. Special emphasis and priority will be given to caregivers of severe and moderate acute malnourished children. These will provide a means to increase good hygiene practices targeting the most vulnerable households in the affected communities.• Strengthening and supporting the existing Coordination mechanisms at a national level (WASH Sector Coordination and Information Forum that meets monthly and its technical working group (The Emergency Strategic Advisory Group) and sub national (Provincial and District Water and Sanitation Sub Committees). • Continued support for the Near Real Time Monitoring System – the Rural WASH Information and Management System (RWIMS) that tracks the functionality status of water points including the sanitation coverage
