Project Summary

The project objective is “Improved protection and respect of rights of acutely vulnerable women 18-59 and >59 years and girls <18 years survivors of GBV and increased access to essential services based on IHL and IHRL.” WAC partners with RCS4GS and WEFAQ to make incremental changes in situation of women and girls, targeted community and duty bearers as reflected in project outcomes:1. Vulnerable women >18-59 and >59 years and girls <18 years manifested well-being, knowledge and confidence, good health status, economic self-reliance, legal enablement and protection; 2. Targeted communities actively engaged in GBV prevention and protection; and 3. Duty bearers are accountable to vulnerable women >18-59 and >59 years and girls <18 years and CBOs and increasingly act to protect them and reduce incidence and impact of human rights violations against them. The project targets 16814 direct beneficiaries (9389 women and 2010 men>18-59 and>59 years, 4275 girls and 1140 boys<18 years) identified based on defining vulnerability and acutely vulnerable groups according to their location, status, gender, protection risks and needs severity. Beneficiaries are selected from most affected locations in North (east/west Jabalia camp, Jabalia, Beit Lahia, Beit Hanoun, Ezbet Beit Hanoun) handled by RCS4GS; Gaza (Gaza City: Shijaia and Zaitoon, Juhor adDik) and Deir ALBalah (Bureij camp, An Nuseirat camp, Mussadar, Deir Al Balah) handled by WAC; and Khan Younis (Bani Suhaila, Fukhari, Qarara, Abasan AlKabira) and Rafah (Shuket Elsoofi, Rafah camp, Rafah, Mawasi) handled by WEFAQ. The project links to HRP (2018-2020) Strategic Objective 1” The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL, while duty bearers are increasingly held to account,” and Protection Cluster Objective 2 "Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of occupation and conflict related violence", and standard activities/ indicators. Partners introduce an effective partnership model that consolidate their human capital to implement responsive, remedial and environment-building activities to address urgent needs of women/girls reflected in HNO/ HRP 2019 and achieve project objective/outcomes. Activities include group psychological support for 960 women and 480 girls; recreational days for 960 women and 480 girls; individual psychological counseling to 120 women and 60 girls; referral of traumatized 42 women and 18 girls; psycho-therapy for 150 couples; group legal counselling to 960 women and 480 girls; individual legal counseling to 120 women and 30 girls; legal representation to 120 women and 30 girls; emergency hotline; emergency health care for 480 women and 240 girls; cancer diagnostic and treatment services for 90 women survivors of cancer; dignity kits for 225 women and 75 girls; training and grants to 21 women; awareness for 960 women, 960 men, 480 girls and 480 boys; training of 25 women and 25 men of GBV service providers; training of 25 women25 men from humanitarian actors; periodical monitoring and coordination with GBV humanitarian key actors; and 3 advocacy initiatives. This project is first phase of 2-year program in line with HRP multiyear strategy and “do no harm” principle. The second phase will be designed based on documenting/systematizing good practices/lessons learned from community engagement (CwC, AAP) like 9 community meetings, 18 FGDs, complaints mechanism, 3 radio ads, 9900 brochures, 3 fact sheets, 9 press releases and pre/post evaluation. Partners follow 4W (Who does What Where When) activity tracking matrix to coordinate with organizations executing activities in project locations in a way that ensures humanitarian needs are met, complementarity with ongoing projects, resilience of beneficiaries, participatory monitoring GENDER and AGE differences, environmentally neutral, protection mainstreaming, security and full access to project.

Needs

HNO (2019) estimated 52,098 persons including 26,049 children with MHPSS problems associated with GMR demonstrations, exacerbates already acutely vulnerable of 210,000 adults and 295,913 children at end of 2017. Women and girls from families affected by casualties and injuries due to GMR were in high risk of GBV. Women mostly affected were mothers of injured children; widowed women; women whose husbands have been killed/ injured; girls who lost a father or have a parent with disability. The demand for GBV services has doubled due to electricity crisis, salary cuts, GRM and overcrowdings in service provider facilities. Estimated 148,478 women/ girls need GBV services which place a high pressure on GBV service providers who have inadequate professional human capacity. UNFPA (2018) presented that 40% of GBV survivors were exposed to physiological abuse, 25% to physical abuse and 18% to resource denial. Around 40% of these cases identified a current/former husband as the alleged perpetrator and 31% other family members. Around 15% of ever-married women have been exposed to sexual violence. UNFPA recommended that service providers embed scattered emergency GBV systems into a unified and shared development framework. Highlights from UNWOMEN (2017) included: one-third of married women who experienced physical violence were exposed to severe acts like attempt to strangle (3.6%), attacking with a knife (2.3%), and beating resulting in a coma (2.3%); household poverty raises women’s vulnerability to repeated physical violence; loss of a close family member does correlate with women’s greater vulnerability to physical violence; and women in Gaza and Deir al Balah had a higher vulnerability to physical violence. Factors that make women less vulnerable to GBV are higher educational achievement, stopping early marriage and women’s employment. NGO GBV services were used by married women (63.3%), divorced (16.5%) and single (9.5%). (32.3%) of these GBV services were used by women aged 20- 29 years. A quarter of women had been referred to the service provider by another formal institution. UNCSW62 (2018) calls to support women>18 years and girls< 18 years survivors of GBV, to develop holistic approach that provides access to full range of quality services. WAC, RCS4GS and WEFAQ conducted 7 FGDs with beneficiaries under ongoing OCHA-funded project and concluded following needs to be addressed for adult women and men 18-59 and eldrely>59 years and girls and boys<18 years as they have differentiated needs: group and individual psychosocial support, recreational open days, mental health treatment, case management, psychosocial support for girls and boys, psychotherapy for couples, group and individual legal counselling and representation in courts, emergency hotline for legal and psychosocial support, emergency health services, diagnostic/treatment services for women survivors of cancer and home visits to support caregivers, dignity kits, business management training and grants to women entrepreneurs, awareness raising of women, girls, men and boys in risk of GBV, communication with and accountability to communities (radio ads, community meetings, FGDs, fact sheets, brochures, complaint system) and advocacy activities. Partners assessed the needs of vulnerable women in eastern Deir AlBalah, particularly ARA using mixed methods to collect/ analyze data from 131 questionnaires with women, a focus group with women and 3 interviews with GBV service providers (CBO, Public Health Clinic, municipality). Findings included women are exposed to physical, sexual and economic violence and denied inheritance; while they refuse to register their cases; women have psychological trauma; there is a referral system to other health clinics/ hospitals; some battered women ask for assistance from service providers; women don’t resort to protection mechanisms like police, courts and informal justice; women need MHPSS, legal aid, health care and economic support
