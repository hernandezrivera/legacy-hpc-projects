Project Summary

All children have a right to human dignity and to their physical and psychological integrity. Like any other right, the right to protection from all forms of violence and right to education are inherent as well as each and every child has the right to live a life free from harm. Education actors and partners at all levels therefore work towards establishing/implementing a range of measures so that children can grow and study in a safe, nurturing and enabling environments.
Undoubtedly concerning the ongoing emergency in NW/SW of Cameroon children are suffering most and longest because when it comes to bearing impact of war’s brutality children face it most. One of the worse evils of militarism in this conflict is that children’s destinies (education/welfare) have been used as battlegrounds and commodities that can be traded. And, since men are responsible for most critical decisions surrounding war and peace the children often don’t have a voice. Presently over 855,000 children have suffered multiple displacements. And while over 44,000 schools remain closed, more than 600,000 children are out of school, with over 270.000 in need of psychosocial support services.
Concerning these multiple challenges faced by children we belief that resilience (ability to resist or recover from the conflict) and social cohesion (sense of belonging and enhancing relationships and wellbeing of members) can enable affected communities’ to manage other systems not to get worse amidst persistence conflict.
In this light, this project therefore suggests that through access to educational opportunities, the children’s ability in enhancing communities’ resilience and ability to contribute to social cohesion are invaluable in order to rebuild basic social systems; so as strengthen the ability to resist in their own way the damage imposed by the crisis.
Specifically the project intends;
• To provide access to educational opportunities to conflict affected out of school children in order to improve their reading and writing abilities
• Provide the link between non formal and formal education through the use of the radio
• Provide school feeding programs for resiliance
It is however important to recognize that enhancing resilience/social cohesion is not necessarily a guarantee for peaceful/efficient society.
Green Partners Association possesses the capacity and access required to enhance effective/efficient project delivery in 34 subdivisions in 7 divisions of NWR. This is thanks to partnerships with UNICEF. We equally belong to a network of organizations whose members are available to each other technically and access support when need arises.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Due to the ongoing protracted crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon over 44,000 schools remain closed with more than 600,000 children out of school, in need of Learning opportunities accompanied by psychosocial support, WASH, health care, civil status registration documents and nutritional support. As the conflict persists, schools remain closed in most parts of the North West region. Proir to the commencement of the ongoing academic year the Government embarked on ‘’Back to school’’ campaign, which involved education key stakeholders, elites in government, administration, CSOs, Faith Based Organisations and international organisations/INGO. This gave parents/guardians hopes that schools would resume. Ironically all these campaigns were inadequate to counter persistent separatists’ school boycot/closure enforcement. That is the reason for the slow pick up of school activities in the month of September. Despite this, there was a marked improvement in enrolment and the number of schools opened this year. As of October 2022, 54% of expected primary and secondary school children are present in schools during the 2022/2023 academic year. The NW recorded more attendance of school children this year with 58% while the SW recorded 49 %. About 884 schools (322 in the NW and 562 in the SW) whose doors were formerly closed opened this academic year.Results of the survey carried out by GPA in October indicate that there is some return to schooling confirming the above situation report from OCHA. Though we have this gradual return to schooling in some areas, education is still not effective as we found out in the results of the survey. Education is not effective due to several reasons amongst which we have, lack of trained teachers in the affected communities, limited access to educational materials, lockdowns and persistent ghost towns. The returnees who find a place in the school environment after 5 years of no schooling have a very long way to go in order to bridge the gap. As we noticed in the survey, most of them even in the final year are unable to read and write. The introduction of the Radio Education Program by GPA with support from UNICEF in several hard to reach areas have served as a panacea in some communities. But much is still left to be done, because we have not been able to reach 15% of the out of school children. And, while the communities remain volatile children/adolescents continue to be the most affected and hopeless. While our original focus was to provide only alternative learning opportunities we have been challenged by new experiences/empirical evidence from the field that despite operating in non-formal setting the programme on its own cannot create reasonable impact without providing psychosocial support, WASH, learning materials, school feeding, infrastructure and birth certificates. This project therefore aims at:• To provide access to educational opportunities to conflict affected out of school children in order to improve their reading and writing abilities• Provide the link between non formal and formal education throungh the use of the radio education and other alternative learning platforms• Provide school feeding programs for resiliance• Provide psychosocial support training to teachers and parents
