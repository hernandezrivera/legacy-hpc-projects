Project Summary

Given the chronic state of conflict and disasters in Somalia, children remain vulnerable to ongoing violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect. Security challenges, conflict and widespread poverty have led to the breakdown of family protective mechanisms, hence damaging the lives of children. The lack of livelihood opportunities is fuelling the recruitment of young men and boys into armed forces, as well as the sexual exploitation of girls and women. At the household level, male members of the family have authority over the lives of girls and women are controlled in every aspect. Older children, (both boys and girls) are seen as a resource instead of being protected and nurtured for effective growth and development.
In this action, SOYDAVO intends to deliver a raft of interventions that will strengthen community-based protection mechanisms to ensure impartial and timely access to protection services against all forms of violence through a robust information gathering framework leading to an effective protection and management information system (Information management system (CPIMS+), protection monitoring, awareness-raising campaigns on child rights and violations (FGM, SGBV and Corporal punishment), key child protection risks, social norms, harmful practices, MRE and related information on CP service and mitigation measures.
Besides, SOYDAVO also intends to fight relentlessly against the early marriage of school-aged girls in collaboration with other child protection actors in Somaliland including NGOs, government authorities and community members. Towards this end, there will be the provision of legal education on Child rights through paralegals and peer facilitators and IEC materials as well as access to justice for victims of violations through the legal process. To strengthen, solidify and reinforce community-based structures (Child Protection Committees, police and Child Protection Personnel) SOYDAVO intends to provide training to these actors who will then demonstrate increased knowledge and skills in providing age- and gender-sensitive child protection services.

Besides, taking cognisance of the trauma that both boys and girls particularly children with disabilities have endured due to the security and humanitarian challenges in Somaliland, SOYDAVO intends to apply a two-thronged approach that involves reintegration of children with their families from armed forces and groups, plus the reunification of girls and boys with their families/communities or primary caregivers or are placed in appropriate alternative care. This will be supported with the provision of and access to community-based prevention and response, mental and psychosocial support, child protection services including children with disabilities.
In this action, SOYDAVO targets children (boys and girls) who have been grossly affected by the current humanitarian and security challenge in Somaliland, and it also targets child protection actors to enhance their capacity to deliver gender and child-sensitive protection services, use CPIMS+ to protect, monitor, track and follow up on child protection cases and also, understand the effective use of Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM) and fundamentally strengthen child protection in Somaliland.
The action targets a protection case-load of 195, 502 displaced children (boys and girls) in Sool and Togdheer region of Somaliland to be reached with appropriate Child Protection services. Beneficiary selection will ensure that 15% of all beneficiaries are and PWD, provide them with appropriate IEC materials and have representation in the project team. A dedicated project staff a (PWD) shall be set aside to support the PWDs. In addition, SOYDAVO will communicate from the onset that all people, including people with disabilities, have value, are respected and welcomed, acknowledge, understand, and embrace the widespread nature of the disability.

Project Activities

In this action, SOYDAVO intends to implement the following activities: 1. Train Child Protection Actors (social workers, community volunteers, government officials, field coordinators and non-child protection actors on Child Protection and Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM);2. Train members of CMCPMs to identify and refer cases of children at risk in their camps and communities;3. Provision of Mental and Psychosocial Support services to affected children (boys and girls) particularly children with disabilities; 4. Conduct robust awareness-raising campaigns with messages on key child protection risks, social norms, harmful practices, MRE and related information on CP services and mitigation measures;5. Track, identify and either reintegrate or reunite separated children with their families/communities or primary care givers or place them in appropriate alternative care;6. Provide caregivers with psychosocial support, parenting messages, positive parenting and other forms of kind support leading to improved relationships with the children under their care; 7. Provide training to child protection actors on how to effectively use the Information management system (CPIMS+) to enable them to identify, follow up and track individual cases within their jurisdiction;8. Provide CP related training to the Child Protection Working Groups in the major districts of Sool and Togdheer regions of Somaliland to equip carry out their roles per set CP standards and legal framework;9. Provide training to parents on early marriages, GBV and FGM in Sool and Togdheer regions of Somaliland to empower them to be at the forefront in fighting and advocating against these practices;10. Conduct Case Management Training for parents and community leaders Sool and Togdheer to equip them with the right skills to handle affected children (boys and girls) especially those with disabilities;11. Conduct Community outreach sessions on GBV and Early Marriages in Sool and Togdheer to create awareness on the danger of exposing children to these practices;12. Develop and provide training on Referral Mechanisms to Community leaders and Parent groups in Sool and Togdheer to empower them to appropriately refer cases to the right CP personnel and act as gatekeepers for CP in their communities.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.):- Engage the beneficiaries from the on-set on where protection assistance or referral services are found;- Link each beneficiary category with a project team member to easily identify and report incidences that need immediate action;- Create visual and audio messages with information on how to access protection assistance or referral services;- Incorporate representatives from protection assistance or referral services into the project steering committee with continuous data collection to ease referral mechanisms. The risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities:- Engage the community in the mobilization phase ensuring the marginalized groups are by default represented in consultations;- Carrying out inclusive community and authority consultations to identify vulnerable, under-represented households, with specific attention to age, gender, minorities, disability and clan representation using the WFP Community Based Targeting Guide; - Inclusive and continuous consultation with different groups of households, and beneficiaries at all stages of the project life cycle;- Establish a project steering committee with membership including persons with disabilities and representatives of the boys and girls in-cases where they may not understand their role. The risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms:- Reinforce complain and feedback mechanisms to identify any situation of exclusion, and to condition implementation on an equal and direct access to all communities;- Train beneficiaries on the import the BFMs and support them on how to use the feedback and complaint mechanisms anonymously;- Design and implement easy to use feedback and complaint mechanisms with a particularly for people with disabilities so that they don’t miss out;- Adopt and contextualize a best practise complaint and feedback mechanism for the project taking cognisance of the dynamics in Somaliland.
