1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project aims to improve learning conditions of 33,736 children in need (20,242 girls, 13,494 boys) including 2,907 children with disability (1744 girls and 1163 boys aged 5-19) in Cabo Delgado (Mueda and Nangade) Nampula (Nampula City, Meconta, Memba and Erati) and Niassa (Lichinga and Cuamba) provinces by (i) establishing 844 Transitional Learning Spaces (TLS) in targeted districts, (ii) supporting enrolment and integration of 33,736 (20,242 girls, 13,494 boys) children IDPs in existing schools, (iii) providing learning materials to 10,000 affected children (IDPs and host communities), (iv) providing teaching materials to 115 teachers (69 female and 46 male aged 20-64) affected teachers in targeted areas, (v) facilitating psychosocial support to all children in need, (vi) supporting establishment of child protection referral mechanisms in collaboration with local child protection actors in targeted districts, and (vii) promoting teachers’ training on PSS, VAC, school related GBV and PSEA, so that they are able to assist children at school, family and community levels. ActionAid will support 115 teachers (69 female and 46 male) with different capacity needs towards acquiring pedagogical skills to improve teaching methodologies in emergency.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

ActionAid will work closely with local community leaders, local Government, particularly the SDEJT and SDPI to build 846 TLSs (each for 40 children) in targeted districts. Communities will be mobilized to identify the location for the TLSs construction, and to grant their labour during the construction process. Using the ActionAid Participatory Vulnerability Analysis, the project will identify the 10,000 most vulnerable school children, inclusive of 2,907 with disability, to benefit from learning materials distribution, such as school bags, school uniform, books and Covid-19 protection materials (soup and reusable face masks). The project will also pay attention to adolescent girls (aged 14 to 19) providing them with reusable sanitary pads for their menstrual management. In coordination with child protection actors available in targeted districts, ActionAid will establish the girls clubs in each TLS to serve as referral pathway for GBV prevention and case management. Each Girl Club will be led by a matron (female teacher) who will be trained on GBV case management and other relevant child protection tools. About 115 teachers will be trained on non-specialised PSS, VAC, school related GBV and PSEA, to strengthen the existing referral mechanisms in each TLS. Teaching materials will be provided to 115 vulnerable teachers, who will have a mandate to provide PSS to children in need. In coordination with SDEJT and public teacher training institutions, the project will support training sessions on pedagogical skills for education in emergency to ensure quality teaching and learning in TLSs.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

ActionAid has a responsibility to support the protection, dignity and safety of vulnerable communities, their well-being and property during emergencies, conflicts, occupation and crises in line with the human rights-based approach, ActionAid’s humanitarian signature and ActionAid’s approach on anti SHEA (Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse) and other safeguarding concerns. As such, ActionAid will ensure that all humanitarian response interventions are built as part of this approach, before, during and beyond the disasters. ActionAid’s emergency response will be guided by a human rights-based approach, our humanitarian signature, our approach on PSEA and other safeguarding concerns, building capacity in local communities, coordination through strategic partnerships and include protection of civilians, especially women and children. ActionAid recognises the differential impact of emergencies on women and the targeted violence they often experience during emergencies. Responding to this is a core part of our strategy and we will ensure women’s leadership and promotion, protection and fulfilment of their rights in emergency preparedness, response and recovery.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

Building on Code of Conduct and feminist approach, ActionAid is committed to preventing any form of sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse responding robustly when these harms take place. ActionAid will take all concerns seriously and carry out timely and robust responses to allegations of harm No one will be victimised for making a complaint and ActionAid is committed to taking a survivor-centred approach throughout. We will work with survivors to ensure they are central to any response, are not further harmed or disempowered by any processes. We are committed to carrying out robust and fair investigations that protect the rights of all involved, with a particular focus on the survivor and the subject of the complaint, ensuring that confidentiality is maintained and that the wellbeing of all is protected. As part of our survivor-centred approach, survivors can choose if, when, and how to make a report and decide whether they want ActionAid to take formal action. However, there may be occasions where ActionAid has a duty of care to respond even if the survivor does not want to take forward action. All programmes will establish a training programme, including stand-alone and refresher training on PSEA so that all staff receive at least one training per year, with further training for specific teams as required.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Increased vulnerability of persons with disabilities is created by a range of factors, including environmental barriers, stigma and discrimination, as well as the design and delivery of the humanitarian response itself. If persons with disabilities are not adequately considered at all phases of this project, there is a risk that humanitarian action may fail to address the specific factors that place them at risk, including barriers to equitable access to protection and assistance. ActionAid will coordinate with local structures and partners, including households to gather sufficient information about the number of persons with disabil¬ities in targeted areas, considering age, sex and type of disability, to better understand their situation, needs and the barriers and risks they face, as well as their capacities, views and priorities. Prior to needs assessment, ActionAid will integrate questions related to disability to better inform planning decisions at all project implementation phases. ActionAid will conduct awareness raising sessions with communities about the need and importance of mobilizing persons with disabilities to participate and engage in decision making spaces.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

ActionAid recognises that different people are affected by environmental impacts in different ways and value different aspects of the environment. To be effective, the identification, assessment and management of environmental impacts needs to consider these differences. The potential impacts of climate change and disaster risk reduction will be considered in environmental impact assessments and associated management plans. As we also recognises that humanitarian aid projects can result in physical or economic displacement of communities, we will ensure that this project is informed and shaped based on and in collaboration with the affected communities, seeking to avoid any unintended consequences, assessing the potential for any displacement as a result of development activities is an important part of project risk management. This will enable us to consider alternatives in the design process and mitigate any negative impacts through appropriate resettlement planning and restoration of livelihoods.
