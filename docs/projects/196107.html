Project Summary

With the aim of strengthening protective factors and reducing risk factors, Save the Children (SC) will operate an Integrated and Holistic Child protection program in Cox’s Bazar at every level of the socio-ecological framework. SC will reach 197289 (10% person with disabilities) beneficiaries which include 98,656 children in 12 camps and 7 unions of Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila. SC’s Child Protection Program will be functioning both in the preventive and responsive wings for ensuring a protective environment for the children. The program will focus on critical interventions and services to address the needs of children of different ages, gender, and other diverse groups keeping special consideration for UASC, Child Headed Households, child marriage and pregnancy, children with disabilities, domestic violence, and child labor. To promote the overall well-being of children and to enable a protective environment for Children the program entails the provision of services including case management service for children at-risk, structured and unstructured PSS support, life skill-based session, adolescent-led initiatives, parenting training, community-based activities, and System Strengthening. SC has 104 Child Protection Multipurpose facilities in the appealing camps. Reinforcing community mechanisms will be one of the core project interventions for building a resilient community.
The program will initiate surveys following Washington Set questionnaires to identify people especially children with disability so that they are included as the project participants. Different ages of children will be accommodated in different time slots and shifts in Multi-Purpose Child and Adolescent Centers (MPCAC). Also, the different age ranges of the children will be considered while procuring and distributing materials (with age-sensitive specifications and size). As a part of Emergency Preparedness and Response DRR messages will be disseminated in the community, and staff will be also involved in different responses such as representing in Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU) in respective camps or playing similar roles as needed.
Capacity-enhancing activities will be scaled up for the different stakeholders such as Government authority (CIC, police, LEA agencies), other service providers, community-based groups and community volunteers, including PSWs (Para social workers), parents, and caregivers as part of System Strengthening. SC will also take initiative to make the Child-Friendly Police Desk operational through technical and logistic support. To address the Early Childhood Development need of Children a dedicated ECD corner will be established in the existing MPCAC, home-based ECD sessions will be conducted by the trained facilitators and caregivers will receive training and awareness focusing on ECD. SC maintains regular coordination with CPSS and operates the program align with the sector's strategy. will continue coordination with CPSS, PWG, and relevant working groups on a regular basis and contribute to the different initiatives such as dedicated staff for CP focal, and submit regular 5W reports.
SC will conduct periodical training, and awareness sessions for the staff, volunteers, and other related stakeholders on different policies including safeguarding and PSEA and Child Safeguarding. SC will give special attention to this policy so that protection against sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse can be ensured throughout the project cycle. By targeting equality in access to services, social cohesion will be strengthened. Multiple accountability mechanisms will be applied in line with the AAP principles to ensure that feedback is actively collected, whilst also ensuring the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries. By creating further partnership scopes through periodical training SC can bolster its strength in technical aspect and address the area of improvement head-on.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

During the Influx in 2017, the humanitarian responders witnessed the death and injuries of the surviving children, and adults, with signs of violence, abuse, and severe stress. The initial impact of violence reduced over time, however, more than five years after the influx, the Rohingya refugee crisis remains a protection emergency at its core. Children are still living in insecure, overcrowded spaces with specific risks related to family separation, sexual abuse, physical violence, child marriage, trafficking, psychosocial distress, and child labor. They continued to face risks related to SGBV and trafficking into the forced sex trade, particularly for adolescent girls. Bangladesh is currently hosting 943,529 displaced Rohingya in the camps, with a 52:48 female-male ratio, amongst which 52% are children, 4% elderly, and 1% are persons with disability identified as of August 2022. Among 3.85% of total individuals identified with specific needs, 0.43% have legal and physical needs, 1.62% are single parent/caregivers, 0.25% include children at risk, and 0.26% are unaccompanied/ separated children (UNHCR fact sheet, August 2022). In Cox’s Bazar Host communities and Refugee, populations experience several shared protection concerns and risks. Both are vulnerable to the growing impacts of climate change and the number of disasters the district experiences, including monsoons, flooding, landslides, and fires. Children in both communities are struggling to cope with the current situation, which includes decreased access to services, stressors in the home caused by income loss, and increased anxiety. Child protection concerns also keep changing based on newly emerged crises, and it is critical that their needs are understood and met. The Child Protection Trend Analysis of CPSS shows that Neglect, Child Labor, Physical abuse, and Child Marriage are the top reported cases from January to June 2022. In 2022, there is an increasing tendency for Child Marriage in the Rohingya Community. Adolescent girls are most at risk of Child marriage whereas adolescent boys are mostly at risk of Child Labor. Children age from 5-11 are mostly experiencing Physical and Emotional Abuse. According to the Child Labor Study of 2022 by IRC, approximately 22% of boys and 5% of girls were involved in paid employment in the host community, whereas 15.6% of boys and 1.5% of girls worked in the Rohingya community. In host community, the number of children engaged in paid employment as an impact of a disaster or shock they experienced among child laborers was 28.6%. On the other hand, 18.9% of Rohingya parents or caregivers stated their children were forced to work outdoors owing to the disaster they faced. In an internal trend analysis of SCI Child Protection Cases, found that (12 -14), (9-11) and (6-8) age group children are mostly experienced on Physical abuse. On the other side (0-2) and (3-5) age group children are mostly experienced on neglect. Due to the socio-cultural norms, lack of knowledge and awareness about Child Protection among the parents and caregiver are identified as a contributing factor to such incidents. According to the girls participating in the children’s consultation by SCI and Plan, eve teasing, and other security threats are common concern in both Registered and Makeshift Camps. While boys enjoy freedom of mobility irrespective of their age, girls’ movement get severely restricted as they get older. Restricted physical mobility hampers education, empowerment, and overall development of the girls. Living with such restrictions largely affect their mental health and wellbeing. At the same time, secondary data indicates a general increase in protection issues in camps, especially in crime and theft, as well as an increased presence of criminal groups with heightened levels of control. Now, more than ever, there is a critical and urgent need for life-saving child protection services in both the host community and refugee populations.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

SC fully adheres to Rationalization Principles and actively participated in the rationalization process of Child Protection CPSS in 2021 and 2022. Based on the rationalization the program is designed to implement in 12 camps. In our operational camps, we provide a holistic Child Protection package to address the need of different age groups of children, caregivers, and the wider community. SC focuses on integrated programs with other sectors such as education, health, nutrition, livelihood, and WASH. Also in our programs, we have dedicated activities targeted to maintain coordination with other sectors including the authority. These include capacity enhancement, discussion sessions, regular coordination meetings, and joint campaigns to address the protection need in the wider community. SC have a strong fund-raising strategy for the smooth continuation of the project without any interfere. Since 2015 SC is operating program in different sectors including Child Protection and have an acceptancy to the community and government. With our system strengthening activities we have established a good engagement of Child Protection activities with Government and other service providers which will be continued to enhance the outcome of our program.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection is the core of all the program of Save the Children. SC adhered to the protection principles which are Prioritize Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm, Meaningful Access, accountability, participation, and empowerment at every phase of the project. Risk mapping, mitigation plan and regular risk monitoring is a mandate of SC for every project. To ensure the safety and avoid further harm we conduct safety mapping with the participation of adolescents which is a child friendly way to identify the safe and unsafe places and reasons for them. At the same time, they also find out the way to mitigate the risks. SC also emphasize on child participation and in our program, we set some good examples of meaningful participation of the children such as child led initiative, periodic discussion session among CIC and children, regular feedback session with the children and address those feedback according to their recommendation.

3.2 - Gender

SC CP program promotes equal access to participation opportunities taking gender consideration into account through awareness and capacity strengthening as well as outreach activities. SC will ensure the active engagement and participation of women, men, youth and children (boys and girls). SC acknowledges within the targeted beneficiaries the different needs of women/girls and men/boys, analyses these needs, and integrates these needs into the planned activities and outcomes. SC recognizes that women and men respond differently, have different roles and power dynamics, and bring different priorities and issues for the strategy and programming (design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation). The project will address the unique needs of vulnerable populations including women and adolescent girls with disabilities, pregnant and lactating mothers, female headed of households, and unaccompanied or separated adolescent girls.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

SC has prioritized maintaining a robust internal mechanism for PSEA and child safeguarding consisting of codes of conduct, mandatory induction training of staff/volunteers/partners, refresher training, development and monitoring of risk registers, and capacity building of PSEA and CSG focal points. Child-friendly CSG and PSEA signs are visible in all project locations, and children/caregivers/communities can choose from a range of mechanisms to give feedback and hear a response. In light of Safeguarding Awareness, Prevention & Reporting, some mandatory safeguarding activities are, SG training for staff and volunteers (100%), visibility materials printing for staff and beneficiary awareness, monitoring visit to the program, Safeguarding Focal Point orientation for being SG resource person in the program and socializing safeguarding reporting mechanism, Socialize Community Champions as part of beneficiary representation on Safeguarding and facilitating safeguarding investigations.

3.4 - Diversity

SC CP intends to contribute to the protection of all diverse groups in terms of age, gender, and disability. All the initiatives for children and adolescents will be adapted and customized considering the different age groups of children. Also, children and adults with disability will be considered within the project activities. Diverse groups will be considered whether it is the recruitment of staff or selection of project staff and volunteers. SC will actively participate in different external platforms including Age and Disability Working Group, Gender in Human Action, GBVSS and other platforms for promoting better coordination among other actors so that a uniform approach is followed to include such diverse groups. SC acknowledges the fact that person with disability and children have special requirements when it comes to impairments and age, hence their participation will be ensured throughout the project cycle specially in implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Child participation at all levels will be strengthened to hear the voices of boys and girls spanning various ages and capacities in designing, implementing, and monitoring program interventions. This is done to minimize harm and ensure a program that is responsive to the needs of men, women, boys, and girls. Children are engaged in project design through FGDs and participatory approaches; the findings of assessments and consultations are shared. Committees in each protection space, including children, will steer community-level consultation and decision-making. SC is following the Child-friendly versions of quality benchmarks for each CP service. SC maintains a toll-free Hotline number to receive feedback, conducts regular group discussions, and visits door-to-door to collect feedback and complaints from the community. SC also focuses on aware the community about complaints and feedback mechanisms and has a well-functioning mechanism to review the feedback and act accordingly.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Save the Children will continue to promote the Do No Harm principle by prioritizing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) activities. This will be achieved through awareness raising of weather and pandemic-related events and risks of family separation, psychosocial distress, and trafficking. Child-friendly DRR messages and awareness-raising will be mainstreamed across all protection spaces; community-led responses to family separation will be strengthened, and staff/volunteers will be trained to activate a safe network to work in an emergency. Accordingly, funds are dedicated to the project for repairs and upgrades of protection spaces. SC has developed comprehensive resources on DRR to ensure children and communities are prepared. To mitigate climate change SC will ensure that the program is using an environment-friendly approach such as replacing plastics with biodegradable alternatives, gardening, and tree plantation, using local resources, and recycling.
