Project Summary

The healthcare system in northwest Syria (NWS) and north central Syria (NCS) has been profoundly impacted by 13 years of war, rendering it fragile and vulnerable. Given this context, supporting the Early Warning Alert and Response Network (EWARN) is imperative to address the escalating health needs of the population, including ongoing outbreaks of cholera, measles, and COVID-19. Timely interventions by EWARN can help mitigate the risks of disease outbreaks and promote the health and well-being of vulnerable communities in the region.
As of 2023, the total number of people in need in Syria has surged to 15.3 million. Northwest Syria (NWS), with a population of 4.4 million, primarily comprising women and children, faces severe humanitarian challenges as outlined in the Syrian Arab Republic: 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (December 2023). Additionally, special attention is warranted for the northcentral Syria (NCS) area, encompassing Tal Abyad and Ras Al Ain districts receiving humanitarian relief from Turkey. This underscores the urgency of implementing the EWARN project to ensure comprehensive disease surveillance and control measures across the entire northwest and northcentral Syria region.
The region is still grappling with the aftermath of the February 6 earthquake, severely impacting families, especially those headed by women or children. The enduring conflict in Syria has led to recurrent outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases since 2013, including two polio outbreaks in 2013 and 2017, as well as periodic measles outbreaks in 2014, 2018, 2022, and 2023. Northwest Syria has witnessed a troubling increase in water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH)-related diseases, with multiple cholera outbreaks and a recent surge in acute hepatitis cases, particularly affecting vulnerable children under 5. Disease outbreaks significantly impede access to healthcare, further burdening the fragile health system.
Investing in EWARN's preparedness and response capacity can substantially reduce the long-term costs associated with responding to outbreaks. By supporting early detection and rapid response to disease outbreaks, EWARN can prevent further disease spread, alleviate strain on the health system, and ultimately save lives. This proposal outlines a comprehensive plan to bolster EWARN's capacity to respond to health emergencies, including enhancing surveillance and early warning systems, improving laboratory services, and conducting targeted health education and awareness campaigns.
EWARN serves as a vital platform for information sharing and coordination among local health actors, facilitating timely and accurate dissemination of information to mount an effective response. It plays a pivotal role in guiding vaccination activities, particularly in areas with fragile health systems.
Furthermore, the project acknowledges the heightened risk of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) during emergencies like disease outbreaks such as COVID-19. To address these concerns, WV and ACU aim to mitigate GBV risks in healthcare settings by respecting privacy, utilizing platforms like WhatsApp and anonymized lists to promote autonomy and privacy in health-related communications, and integrating gender specialists' input into project planning.
WV maintains a zero-tolerance policy against Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA) and supports mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and caregivers. This includes technical assistance, policy review, training for frontline workers and field focal points, monitoring of child safety concerns, and an online platform, Ethics Point, for unbiased complaint management and quick reporting and response in all locations.
