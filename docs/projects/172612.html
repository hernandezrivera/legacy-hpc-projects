Project Summary

The protracted conflict in Darfur region of Sudan remains unresolved since it broke out in 2003 up to now .More than 1.97 million internally displaced persons are residing in about 55 camps and other non-camp settlements across Darfur.ARC protection department is proposing an intervention focused on strengthening the protection of 550000 IDPs, host communities and returnees affected by conflict and displacement through improved and sustainable access to basic protection services and durable solution. The proposed intervention will focus and provides community-based protection services to the IDPs and host communities in South and East Darfur states of Sudan. This proposed 12-month project will enable ARC to provide a full package of preventive and direct protection services to the target communities (the IDPs and host communities) both state (ED and SD).
The overall objective of the project will be to promote and support community self-management, durable solutions, support for persons with specific needs and highly vulnerable households and conduct protection and returnee monitoring amongst IDPs and host communities and other relevant communities to identify their basic needs.
ARC will support the strengthening of community structures (CBPNs, youth and women groups) and facilitate the development and implementation of community-based protection action plans for durable solutions and support to vulnerable community members. Community structures will be established and strengthened through capacity building and supporting to promote equal and meaningful access to these facilities, monitor protection concerns and develop a community-based response. ARC will contribute to the participation and information of displaced and returnee communities in the planning and implementation of durable solutions, through the profiling of IDP and returnees and promoting access of IDPs and returnees to information required to enable them to take informed decisions. Also ARC will support Community members need to be empowered through capacity building initiatives to work with Persons with Specific Needs (PSN), conduct protection monitoring and reporting as well as their own advocacy and assistance delivery monitoring.

1 - Needs

Darfur region remains the epicenter of large-scale protracted displacements. The conflict situation in Darfur region still remains unresolved. Several Humanitarian Situational Reports since December 2017 up to 2020 show that many factors have constrained the IDPs’ access to alternative basic needs or options. With limited or no alternative protection, livelihood options, humanitarian assistance to the IDPs in the form of basic lifesaving services and protection has remained largely critical to meeting their most basic needs and for saving lives. The Inter Agency assessments conduct in both 2019/2020 and Humanitarian needs overview(HNO) 2020 highlighted a number of critical needs and protection gaps that need to be addressed. These include;1- Long-term safety, security and freedom of movement2-Access to employment and livelihood opportunities3- Access to mechanisms to restore housing, land and property or provide compensation4- Access to and replacement of personal and other documentation.5- Voluntary reunification with family members separated during displacement;6- access to justice, reparations, and information

2 - Activities/Outputs

ARC will provide integrated project activities for protection services that promote durable solutions and self-reliance among the target populations in selected IDP camps in south and east Darfur. Community-based protection services will continue with provision of basic needs to most affected persons and PSNs in target locations. Among the services included are consultations, trainings and awareness sessions, and provision of para legal, protection, and small projects/income generating activities for the most vulnerable people. The project will place a special emphasis on supporting persons with specific needs and women and youth. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ARC places protection at the center of its programming by undertaking efforts for immediate and life-saving activities throughout the duration of a crisis and consistently working with the affected communities to strengthen community-based protection. ARC employs a strategic, comprehensive and collective approach to protection in order to enhance the overall ability of our staff to analyze, prioritize and respond effectively to protection needs of the affected populations including the risks and consequences of violence, abuse, coercion and deprivation. ARC adheres to the set of guidelines that are an intrinsic part of our program design paradigm. Based on the IASC/ global Protection Cluster Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action, per different Thematic Area Guides (TAGs), the HELPAGE Minimum Standards for Age & Disability Inclusion in Humanitarian Action , and Handicap International guidelines on design of interventions for people with disability, ARC provides special consideration to making the services accessible to older people. ARC invests extra effort in design of services to ensure their accessibility across the age categories and vulnerability scenarios, with special provisions for pregnant women/ new mothers and infants, older and severely ill and people with disability.
