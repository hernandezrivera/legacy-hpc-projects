Project Summary

Save the Children is committed to strengthening protective factors and reducing risk factors in Cox's Bazar through its Integrated and Holistic Child Protection program. This program will operate at every level of the socio-ecological framework, aiming to reach 2,38,510 people of concerns, including 161783 children in 14 camps and 06 unions and 01 Municipality in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila. It will focus on critical interventions and services tailored to the needs of children of varying ages, genders, and diverse groups.
The program will promote the overall well-being of children and establish a protective environment through an inclusive, community and resilience-based approach encompassing both preventive and responsive measures. The services will include case management for children experienced or at-risk of protection concerns, structured and semi-structured psychosocial support (PSS), life skill-based sessions, adolescent-led initiatives, parenting training, community-based activities, and System Strengthening. SC will operate 102 CP Multipurpose facilities in the camps and host to provide these services and strengthening community mechanisms to build resilient communities.Community Based Child Protection Committee will be supported to mobilize the community and play an active role in child protection.
SC utilized the Washington Set questionnaires to identify individuals, particularly children with disabilities, for inclusion in its child protection programs.MPCAC will accommodate children of different ages in various time slots and shifts, and materials will be procured and distributed with age-sensitive specifications and sizes. MPCAC also will act as child protection hub where capacity-building training, awareness-raising sessions, workshops, and meetings on child protection will be organized.
Capacity-building activities will be scaled up for various stakeholders, including government authorities (CiC, police, LEA agencies), service providers, community-based groups, and community volunteers, including Para social workers, parents, and caregivers as a part of System Strengthening. Also take the initiative to make the Child-Friendly Police Desk operational, quarterly Child Welfare Board functional by providing technical and logistic support.
SC will implement Girl Decide Curriculum in Girl Friendly Space which aims to empower and protect girls in situations of migration and/or displacement so that they have safer journeys and better outcomes.
SC will provide comprehensive support to children involved with or affected by armed groups. Through the case management system, SC will ensure assistance to Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG), addressing their specific needs and aiding in their recovery and reintegration.
SC will maintain regular coordination with Child Protection Sub-Sector and operate the program in alignment with the sector's strategy. Coordination with CPSS, PWG, and relevant working groups will continue, and SC will contribute to various initiatives, including dedicating staff for CP focal points and submitting different reports, including 5W and child protection situation monitoring reports.
Periodical training and awareness sessions on policies, including child safeguarding and the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, will be conducted for staff, volunteers, and related stakeholders. SC will prioritize these policies to ensure protection against sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse throughout the project cycle. By targeting equality in access to services, SC will strengthen social cohesion and co-existence.
Multiple accountability mechanisms in line with the Accountability to Affected Populations principles will be applied to actively collect feedback while ensuring the safety and dignity of program participants. By fostering partnerships through localization, SC will enhance this program’s technical capabilities and address areas of improvement proactively.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

More than seven years after the influx, the Rohingya refugee crisis continues to be a protection emergency at its core. Children are still living in insecure, overcrowded spaces, facing specific risks such as family separation, sexual abuse, physical violence, child marriage, trafficking, psychosocial distress, and child labor. Adolescent girls remain particularly vulnerable to risks related to sexual and gender-based violence and trafficking into the forced sex trade while boys are more likely to be involved in the worst forms of child labor and child recruitment. The actions of criminal gangs have been a concern for many years; however, the emergence of armed groups has a direct impact on children.The 2024 Quarter 2 Protection Monitoring Report indicates that child recruitment is widespread in nearly all refugee camps, primarily affecting boys aged 14 to 18, who are often sent back to Myanmar for training. As a coping mechanism, children were reported to limit their movements to avoid recruitment impacted on the overall participation of boys and adolescents in camp activities.The CPIMS+ data shows that Neglect (31%), psychological distress (12%), Physical abuse (10.48%), psychological abuse (7.84%), disability (9.50%), separation (4.92%) and Child Marriage (4.53%) are the top reported cases in Rohingya Camp from January to September 2024. In focused group discussions many children expressed that neglect was the result of large family sizes, parental separation, or substance abuse by caregivers. Children with disabilities are especially vulnerable as they are frequently left isolated without adequate care. Girls are often expected to assume childcare responsibilities, limiting their participation in social activities. Children also reported to experience violence at the hands of parents, teachers, and peers. Boys were reported to face punishment from their fathers while girls reported being punished by mothers.Mental health and psychosocial challenges are a growing concern, with 58% of key informants identifying behavioral or psychological issues in children. The children identified family conflict, separation, restricted freedom, and feeling unheard by their elders as significant sources of stress. Uncertainty about their future, reliance on aid, and social exclusion further exacerbates their anxiety.Girls face additional stress due to restrictions on their movement and fear of violence. Child labor continues to disrupt children’s education and well-being, with 61% of informants reporting that children are engaged in labor. Children revealed that boys must work due to family pressure, large household sizes, lack of aid, and limited access to education or vocational training. Child marriage remains a deeply rooted issue with 29% of key informants acknowledging its prevalence in their communities. Cultural norms, family pressure, and fears about unsafe relationships drive families to arrange child marriage. According to MSNA-2023 by SCI occurrence of early child marriage before age 18 in the Rohingya community recorded the highest rate at 63% followed by the host community at 54%. Overall, 10% of women were found to have entered marriage before reaching the age of 15. Incidents of abduction and kidnapping were also a prevalent concern in 2024 for 38% of the households. According to the joint protection monitoring report there was a total of 157 cases of abduction/kidnapping registered in quarter 3 which includes 31 boys and 5 girls. Abductions/kidnappings are usually perpetrated by organized groups who demand a ransom for their release.The October 2024 Child Protection Report highlights rising substance abuse and online gambling among youth in camps, fueling domestic violence. Increased crime, theft, and armed group control exacerbate protection risks. Urgent life-saving child protection services are needed to address these challenges in both refugee and host communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection is the core of all Save the Children's programs. We unwaveringly adhere to a set of core protection principles, which guide our approach at every phase of our projects. These principles include Prioritizing Safety and Dignity, Avoiding Causing Harm, Ensuring Meaningful Access, Upholding Accountability, Promoting Participation, and Fostering Empowerment.To effectively implement these principles, Save the Children follows a rigorous process that includes risk mapping, mitigation planning, and regular risk monitoring for each of our projects. Risk mapping and mitigation planning are not just optional but are considered mandatory steps in our project implementation.In our quest to ensure safety and prevent further harm, we actively engage adolescents in safety mapping exercises. This child-friendly approach enables us to identify safe and unsafe places, along with the underlying reasons for these designations. Moreover, adolescents collaborate to develop strategies for mitigating risks, contributing to a safer environment.Child participation is a central tenet of our approach, and we strive to set exemplary standards for meaningful child involvement in our programs. Some of our successful initiatives include child-led projects and initiatives, periodic discussion sessions involving Camp In charge (CIC) and children, and regular feedback sessions with children. Importantly, we take this feedback seriously and actively address their concerns and recommendations to enhance our projects further.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Save the Children remains deeply committed to delivering child protection services that are meticulously tailored to meet the unique and evolving needs of children across different age groups. SC will continue to employ finely crafted, age-appropriate interventions to address the multifaceted requirements of infants, young children, school-aged children, and adolescents. This unwavering commitment is at the core of our mission, ensuring that every child we serve will receive services and support that not only align with their developmental stage but also respect their distinctive individual needs.Aligned with our steadfast dedication to inclusivity, SC's child protection program conducted surveys using the Washington Set questionnaires, a comprehensive tool specifically tailored to identify individuals, with a focus on children with disabilities. SC has integrated this approach in its ongoing child protection projects and plans to continue its application in future initiatives. This diligent approach is specifically crafted to ensure their active inclusion as integral project participants. SC has also established partnerships with specialized organizations dedicated to promoting inclusion, including Handicap International, the Centre for Disability in Development (CDD), and BANDHU.Beyond our regular activities, Save the Children will take decisive measures to address the specific needs of children with disabilities which will include providing comprehensive support for assistive devices at all our working camps enhancing overall accessibility, and implementing a comprehensive range of actions aimed at nurturing an inclusive environment where every child can thrive and reach their full potential.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Save the Children is dedicated to enhancing child participation at all levels, ensuring the voices of both boys and girls across diverse age groups are amplified. This empowerment is evident through their active involvement in designing, executing, and monitoring program interventions, adopting a holistic approach to minimize harm and address the distinctive needs of girls, boys, adolescents, women, and men. Children's participation, including adolescents, is facilitated through methods such as FGDs and participatory approaches. Transparency is maintained by sharing assessment and consultation findings, accompanied by clear explanations for decisions made. SC takes a proactive role in empowering adolescents by facilitating club activities where consultations occur, allowing them to voice their rights. Adolescents play a key role in designing activities, leading meetings, and executing action plans. They actively communicate with and engage Child and Adolescents, Community-Based Child Protection Committees (CBCPC) members to address concerns and participate in activities simultaneously. In community-level consultation and decision-making processes, committees, including children, assume pivotal roles in each protection space. SC adheres to child and adolescent-friendly versions of quality benchmarks for every Child Protection service, incorporating child-friendly response mechanisms. The organization has well established Child-Friendly Feedback and Response Mechanism guidelines encompassing eight child-friendly feedback tools, such as dot voting, risk mapping, and youth focal points. To foster active engagement and open dialogue, SC maintains a toll-free hotline and helpdesk, diligently collecting feedback through regular group discussions and mechanisms like door-to-door visits, exit interviews, and post-distribution monitoring. This comprehensive approach reflects SC's commitment to excellence in child protection services and active participation.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) stands as one of the cornerstones of Save the Children's core policies. We place a paramount emphasis on maintaining a robust internal mechanism that encompasses a comprehensive range of measures. These measures include the implementation of a Code of Conduct, compulsory induction training for staff, volunteers, and partners, and periodic refresher training. In addition, we prioritize the development and vigilant monitoring of risk registers and the continuous capacity building of PSEA and Child Safeguarding (CSG) focal points.As a manifestation of our zero-tolerance stance on PSEA, Save the Children ensures that PSEA and CSG visibility materials are readily available in all project locations. These materials constantly remind us of our commitment to the safety and well-being of all involved.To maintain a transparent and accessible reporting mechanism, Save the Children operates a toll-free hotline number, prominently included in the visibility materials. In terms of community engagement, SC also conducts regular campaigns and awareness sessions, providing children, caregivers, and community people with a spectrum of avenues to report incidents, provide feedback, and receive a response. When a PSEA complaint/issue is identified/registered/documented, SC's Safeguarding team works on the issue while maintaining utmost confidentiality. A TOR is developed for this purpose, including objectives, process, child-friendly or age-specific questions, a timeline, and investigation member names. According to the policy, a complaint has to be resolved within 90 days, and after resolving, the survivor is informed about the result.Furthermore, SC conducts project-based risk assessments and sector-wise PSEA assessments yearly. Besides having PSEA focal points, SC is also a member of the PSEA Network, hence maintaining an alliance with ISCG.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Save the Children (SC) integrates climate action into its Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Disaster Risk Management (DRM), and anticipatory actions as a part of strategic approach to climate change adaptation and mitigation. A key element of SC’s Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan is incorporating message on climate action to all the Child protection activities aiming to reduce the impact of climate-related disaster on vulnerable communities. SC prioritizes green initiatives, including community and child-led tree-plantation activities, reduce the usage of plastic materials, promoting upcycle and recycling to promote environmental stewardship. In alignment with CPSS guidelines and the Cyclone Preparedness Program (CPP), SC facilitate early warning distribution, while community-based committees trained in DRR, act as first responders. These committees prevent protection risks, identify child protection needs, and make referrals. SC is prioritizing child-focused interventions, emphasizing proactive climate action and community resilience-building to protect vulnerable populations against the growing challenges of climate change.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project will embed comprehensive Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures to ensure readiness and continuity during disasters including business continuation plan, maintaining the emergency stock and emergency response standard operating procedures to guide Child Protection activities in all phases of emergencies. Contingency funds also set aside to facilitate rapid response actions. SC will prioritize the widespread dissemination of Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) messages across the community and conduct pre-, during-, and post-scenario analyses to guide anticipatory actions that minimize disaster impacts. Staff will be prepared to participate actively in the Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU) within camps. CP focal points in camps will maintain updated referral pathways and share emergency updates, linking community committees, volunteers, CP staff, and emergency focal points. Child Protection staff are volunteers are trained on emergency response including PFA, Family tracing and Reunification process during an emergency, safe referral which allow us to respond without delaying during emergency. SC operate Mobile Child friendly Spaces to ensure that children received PFA and PSS support, child friendly help desk to support the child and family members with necessary information for their required support. Our comprehensive approach will strengthen child-focused interventions, ensuring robust preparedness and response measures are in place to safeguard the children and community.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Save the Children will engage youth (15-18 years) as key participants in child protection initiatives, empowering them through targeted, participatory activities that build on their strengths and unique perspectives. Youth will take on active roles in planning and executing child protection activities, fostering greater awareness within their communities. The youth above 18 years old will mostly lead awareness-building initiatives and serve as peer Para Social Workers supporting case management efforts and provide support to vulnerable children in terms of identification and referral especially during the time when child protection workers are not available in the field. In their roles as advocates and facilitators, these youth will address critical issues such as preventing child marriage, combatting child labor, and creating and promoting safe spaces for children. Comprehensive training sessions will focus on life skills, protective behavior, leadership, communication, and core child protection principles, equipping these young people with the tools needed to serve as role models and positive influences within their communities.Additionally, Save the Children will support youth-led initiatives, such as sports programs and creative platforms that enable youth to express themselves, build resilience, and champion safer, more inclusive environments. By fostering these youth-driven activities, Save the Children will cultivate a generation of empowered young leaders who actively contribute to a protective community network. This approach will not only amplify the voices of young people but also strengthen the overall impact and sustainability of the child protection project, building a community-wide commitment to safeguarding children in both the Rohingya camps and adjacent host communities.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Save the Children empowers refugee adolescents and youth in Cox's Bazar by fostering self-reliance and resilience through its targeted child protection initiatives. Life skills and protective behavior training equip adolescents with essential skills for a brighter future, while Multi-Purpose Child and Adolescent Centers (MPCAC) offer safe spaces for mental health support and confidence-building activities. SC strengthens family and community structures with parenting workshops, and community-led initiatives, creating nurturing environments for children and youth to thrive. By collaborating with caregivers and local stakeholders, including para-social workers, SC cultivates a sense of ownership and collective responsibility, empowering communities to address challenges independently and build lasting resilience.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Save the Children supports the district development priorities of Cox’s Bazar through its development projects in other upazilas. SC has established 15 Child Protection Community Centers across Ukhiya, Cox’s Bazar Sadar, and Eidgoan upazilas to support vulnerable children in collaboration with the Department of Social Services (DSS). SC support to strengthen and operationalize the Child Welfare Board and Child Affairs Police Desk of Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila. Additionally, SC provides capacity-building training to government actors focused on child protection. SC has also supported the government in developing a capacity-building module on child protection and standard operating procedures for Child Protection Case Management, in collaboration with the Department of Social Services, through a project implemented in Cox’s Bazar.
