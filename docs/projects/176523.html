Project Summary

Through the proposed humanitarian responses, International Medical Corps (IMC) will provide integrated lifesaving nutrition service to conflict affected and displaced communities in Juba IDP Camps, Panyijar, Malakal and Canal Pigi Counties. With the aim to reduce suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable populations, IMC will increase equitable access and utilization of comprehensive treatment and preventive packages, which are essential for addressing key drivers of acute malnutrition affecting children and women in the aforesaid project locations. The proposed response will include all components of Community Management of Acute Malnutrition, including Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP), Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP), Stabilization Center (SC) and community mobilization activities.
International Medical Corps proposes to operate 22 OTPs, 22 supplementary feeding programs (SFPs), and 2 SCs across the project sites. IMC will also continue to address prevention of malnutrition in children under two years of age through promotion of appropriate Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices. The service will be delivered through static and outreach/mobile facilities for remote and hard to reach areas in the targeted project locations. IMC will work with the Ministry of Health (MoH), UNICEF and WFP to secure timely provision of nutrition supplies, which are necessary for nutrition service delivery. IMC will reach approximately 54,675 individuals, of which 26,357 are IDPs. All project activities will be implemented in line with adapted guidelines for nutrition services delivery in the context of COVID-19 provided by the Nutrition Cluster.
Additionally, IMC will reinforce and promote access to integrated nutrition, health, WASH and FSL services provision in the project target areas with critical levels of acute malnutrition, through close coordination and joint programming with other Clusters (Health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection). This project aims to enhance nutrition-sensitive interventions that will ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services; to further decrease the prevalence of acute malnutrition, and ultimately, to contribute to the prevention and reduction of morbidity and mortality associated to malnutrition in South Sudan. IMC will also support hygiene interventions to improve nutrition outcomes and reduce risks of hygiene-related morbidities.
IMC will monitor the nutrition status of the population and the nutrition response through routine nutrition program data, quarterly MUAC screening campaigns and population-based SMART Surveys. IMC, with its already established Nutrition Surveillance Team will undertake nutrition assessments in priority Counties and its project locations to provide robust information on the nutrition situation of a given population, this information will be key in responding effectively to nutrition needs in the project area.
IMC will continue to support effective Coordination of emergency nutrition activities at the national level through the Nutrition Cluster co-lead role This will ensure a nationwide adequate and coherent nutrition response capacity, and enhanced leadership and accountability in Nutrition sector response.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition needs are greatest in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Lakes, Warrap and Unity states, where a number of acutely malnourished children under-five and PLW caseloads are highest, also with more than 50% of their respective population facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Panyijar County, with 86,121 population, continues to face numerous challenges, including insecurity due to cattle raids and revenge killings; the negative impact of COVID-19; high prevalence of malnutrition and chronic food insecurity; increasing morbidity levels particularly malaria, watery diarrhea and respiratory tract infection diseases; high incidence of gender-based violence, and yearly flooding from the Nile river and heavy rains. Accdg. to the IPC released in January of 2021, Panyijiar as one of the 11 counties in the country of great concern facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and expected to persist during the projected period April 2021 - Aug 2021. Findings of the IMC’s SMART survey in Dec 2019 in Greater Panyijar, estimated GAM prevalence at 15.4% (12.4-18.9 95% CI) and SAM prevalence at 2.4% (1.4- 4.1 95% CI) based on W4H, with 0.35 death/10,000/day and 0.16 deaths/10,0000 for CMR and U5MR, respectively. MIYCN care practices continued to be sub-optimal in this location. The same population-level survey indicated an exclusive breastfeeding rate of 61.4%; MDD among 6-23-month children of 37.9%, and MAD of 32.7%. In July and Dec 2019, IMC and Concern World Wide conducted a SMART survey in Malakal PoC and Juba IDP camps, respectively. In Malakal PoC, the prevalence of GAM among the surveyed population was estimated at 16.9 % (13.2↔21.3 95% C.I), and the prevalence of SAM was 3.0 % (1.6↔5.5 95% C.I) Therefore, the current nutritional status among the IDPs in Malakal PoC is critical. Similarly, the overall GAM prevalence in Juba IDP camps is indicative of critical nutritional status based on the WHO standards. The GAM prevalence in Juba IDP camps based on weight for height z scores and oedema was 18.3% [14.6  22.6, 95% C.I.], and SAM prevalence was 3.3% [2.0  5.2, 95% CI]. In the two camps, children aged 6-17 months and 18-29 months are more affected by SAM, an average of 8.4% and 2.4%, respectively. Similarly, for IYCF indicators – prevalence in Malakal was 90.0% of exclusive breastfeeding, while early initiation was practiced by 91.8%. The current level of acute wasting is associated with poor dietary intake, high morbidity burden, poor feeding practices, inadequate sanitation conditions, and hygiene practices in the IDP camps. As of November 2020, Canal/Pigi County in Jonglei State has been classified by IPC to be experiencing Level 4 ‘acute’ food insecurity. In total, 92,000 people or 61% are estimated to be in need of food assistance with 31,000 people experiencing IPC 3 ‘crisis’, 46,000 in IPC 4 ‘emergency’, and 10,000 in IPC 5 ‘catastrophe’ conditions. IMC’s 2019 SMART survey report in Canal/Pigi indicated a prevalence of GAM of 24.3% (19.5-29.8 95% CI), and SAM prevalence of 6.7% (4.6- 9.7 95% CI). The CMR from the survey was 0.86 deaths/10,000 persons/day [0.42–1.75, 95% C.I.], while U5MR was 0.74 deaths per 10,000 persons/day [0.29-2.17 95% CI]. The critical nutritional status among the children under-five is mainly attributed to the limited access to quality and diversified food, and high morbidity burden. At the time of the survey, 43.8% of the children aged 6-59 months were reported to have been sick two weeks prior to the survey. MIYCN care practices in the county continued to be sub-optimal. IMC currently implements nutrition programs in Panyijar, Malakal and Canal/Pigi counties. Although, IMC’s nutrition interventions have minimized morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition, sustained interventions are needed to be done to ultimately reduce the number of children and PLW suffering from acute malnutrition.

IMC has a well-established monitoring and evaluation system. At the field level, the Program Managers use the developed work plan that will guide implementation of the program. In addition to the formal reporting system, field program team will conduct quarterly joint supportive supervision, as well as review meetings to review the project’s progress against planned activities, as well as share best practices and experiences. The MEAL Coordinator will carry out quarterly monitoring visits to support Data Quality Assessments (DQA), with an aim to improve the data collected and used to assess program performance. The DQA process will include the participation of MEAL officers and program staff at the field. Findings will be discussed with program teams during the project quarterly review and planning meetings. During the monitoring visits, Program staff will continue to receive on the job training on collection of quality data, and how to analyze and use the information to produce positive changes during implementation period. Data collected will be disaggregated according to location, gender, age and target group (male, female, girls, boys), including disability; analysis will look at how different population groups are affected, involved and /participating in the program.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IMC in South Sudan has developed country-specific guidance to protect beneficiaries from exploitation and abuse in humanitarian crisis. This Guidance complements IMC’s global safeguarding policies. All IMC employee has to undergo a training on PSEA, and sign a pre-employment commitment against sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as report any suspected cases. IMC will conduct regular awareness raising sessions with the community on PSEA through QAAPCs CNVs and CHWs. The project team will incorporate PSEA messages into CBFRM activities, especially during monthly AAP meetings with the community. The project team will also ensure posting/distribution of IEC materials at strategic locations in the community, including how to report PSEA cases. Regular sessions at the health facilities and other services point will be done on a monthly basis. Community focal points will be empowered through trainings to be able to pass on key PSEA messages, jointly with IMC project teams. For sensitive cases that arise, communities will be sensitized to report to the field site manager/designated focal point. The field site manager is responsible to log in the complaints into IMC reporting system, Ethics point, for further investigation. Communities will be sensitized to report any sensitive cases directly to Ethics point reporting systems for investigation. Ethics point contacts will be shared during community sensitizations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IMC will further strengthen and/or put in place various CBFRM mechanisms that will ensure the most vulnerable members of the community are catered for. IMC ensures accountability to affected population is incorporated from the design phase by consulting affected communities, with equal representation from women, girls, PWDs, elderly, etc., on their specific needs and vulnerabilities, that should be adapted by the project. During project implementation, the project will use different channels, such as AAP monthly meetings, suggestion boxes, hotlines, house-to-house visits, etc., to inform affected communities on their rights and entitlements, and mechanisms available to them to share feedback and complaints. The project will also consult communities on their preferred feedback modalities, and support set up of these channels where they are not functional. The mechanisms will be robust to ensure collection of all forms of complaints including cases of PSEA. Feedback and complaints coming from the various channels will be analyzed, and patterns identified to inform program adaptation where necessary. In addition, regular community consultations will be conducted to ensure participation of communities in the ongoing implementation and M&E activities. These will include assessing level of satisfaction with services provided, unmet needs, identification of lesson learned and recommendations for project improvement. Finally, IMC will monitor community’s perception of and satisfaction with service provision through integration of a set of AAP indicators into the SMART and KAP surveys.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project is going to provide beneficiaries with information about the assistance to be provided. IMC nutrition response is designed to work closely with the communities from project inception through implementation and monitoring of results. IMC will ensure active consultations with the beneficiaries or affected populations during onsite monitoring, which allows generation of feedback from the beneficiaries. At the onset of the project, IMC will conduct community consultations and sensitizations on the types of nutrition intervention needed to implement, which takes into consideration gender and age group needs. During the nutrition treatment days, mothers will receive explanation about the program, its importance, the distribution process of supplies, and the importance of using therapeutic supplies for its sole purpose. Beneficiary feedback will be received through already existing Quality Accountability to the Affected Population Committees (QAAPC) in all the project sites. Similarly, IMC will conduct beneficiary feedback assessments at the household level that will gauge how beneficiary feel about the project, and their overall satisfaction on the services they received from IMC. IMC will also involve the County Health Department in field visits, giving them chance to meet with beneficiaries and directly get feedback regarding the program. IMC will also organize meeting with Payam leaders on a quarterly basis to inform them on program achievements and challenges, and seek support in community mobilization and awareness raising on nutrition-related matters. Additionally, IMC utilizes community-based feedback and response mechanisms (CBFRM) to solicit feedback and receive complaints. IMC’s CBFRM procedures provides guidance on: (1) effective community consultations – building upon community preferences on how to raise concerns and provide feedback; (2) major steps on managing feedback and complaints and how to explain our actions and decisions to the communities (including vulnerable and marginalised people); (3) Clear informed consent - IMC will provide all necessary information on how to manage feedback and complaint process, explain people’s rights and entitlements, as well as key steps that IMC will take to keep confidentiality of sensitive complaints, and (4) Routing and management of sensitive complaints, including SEA and safeguarding, including, protection measures and investigation pathways. CBFRM is aligned closely with the organization’s broader safeguarding and accountability policies; together, these procedures help ensure the systematic use and management of the feedback and complaint handling process across all field locations and programs. Building on the existing feedback and complaints mechanism, IMC will ultimately promote community participation on management and use of the feedback and complaints through community learning meetings, community Help desks, Post distribution Monitoring, AAP meetings and field registers as a safe and user friendly channels to collect, process and respond to the complaints according to IMC and community’ preferences, as well as disseminate information about the project. During program implementation, IMC will continue to enhance these already existing community accountability structures in the interventions guided by IMC South Sudan CBFRM country local procedures. IMC will share relevant program information including information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure meaningful participation. Regular information on the program performance and monitoring results, staff code of conduct and PSEA reporting lines staff will also be shared. Communities will be sensitized on existing AAP structures as a means of sharing complaints and feedback. During these sensitizations, communities will also be consulted on the most context-appropriate and safe avenue to share complaints and feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Through this response, IMC will engage the affected population to participate fully in the project design and implementation to ensure communities have clarity about the project, and have their say on the project’s objectives, activities, location, and their entitlements. Feedback and Complaints arising from the communities will be processed, analyzed and discussed during program management meetings. The meetings will develop responses and action plans to address the complaints and feedback raised. These responses will be provided through the AAP committees in each project location. In addition, the information will be keyed into a complaints and feedback response database for analysis and learning. During monthly/quarterly program review meetings, the information from the database will form part of the meeting reflections and will inform necessary programmatic corrections.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

IMC incorporates protection principles in its all its programs and its daily work by prioritizing the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries served. Consultations and other activities will take place in secure areas that are also easily accessible for groups who are extremely vulnerable and/or with limited mobility. IMC healthcare and nutrition workers will be trained with a focus on gender-based violence prevention and survivor-centered treatment core concepts (clinical management of rape), considering the challenging environment that poses high barriers to vulnerable people, especially women and girls, in terms of reporting GBV cases, as well to counter the general lack of information regarding this topic. IMC’s multi-sectoral health, nutrition and protection project will integrate GBV medical and MHPSS services to survivors, including counselling, management and proper referral for survivors of GBV, including rape survivors. All staff will be trained on protection, GBV Core Concepts and safe referrals. Service mapping and medical referral protocols are in place across IMC’s health facilities and project locations to ensure meaningful and safe referrals, including ensuring patient information shared with third-party service providers is only done so after informed consent of the patient is secured. Furthermore, IMC provides integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, including psychiatric emergency services for both in- and out-patients in all its supported health facilities. Other PSS interventions that IMC provides include psychoeducation, support groups and peer support sessions for the most vulnerable groups, including community leaders, women, children, GBV survivors and PWDs of the displaced and conflict-affected population in the target sites. These are conducted through small group discussions, tea sessions and community outreaches with the aim of bringing vulnerable individuals and group of people together, create social opportunities for their families and care givers of persons with psychosocial disabilities, and the wider community structures, which are tailored to ensure promotion of psychosocial wellbeing is achieved and the human rights of socially disadvantaged vulnerable persons are promoted. In addition, IMC will implement/pilot Self-help -plus (SH+) in all its project locations. SH+ is a stress management intervention based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) principles, for use by persons and group of people experiencing psychosocial distress and other stress-related conditions.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Through an integrated and comprehensive health, nutrition and protection services delivery, and through working with partners, IMC will ensure optimal and sustainable service quality for acute malnutrition management, as well as improving IYCF practices through nutrition education, counseling, facilitation of support groups, complementary feeding and kitchen gardening. A sustained delivery of such interventions will then significantly contribute to the decrease of proportion of populations facing IPC 3, 4 and 5.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

IMC will conduct Gender Analysis to understand the needs, roles and dynamics of women, girls, boys and men in different age groups. The project scope will also include coverage surveys and bottleneck analysis to identify barriers to accessing the different nutrition interventions, and identify solution to overcome these barriers. IMC will work closely with protection actors to incorporate community awareness to address inequalities or protection threats. For example, preferential feeding practices where women do not always have the same access to food as men and children. Sensitization will be carried out during project activities to address cultural inequalities where men may be fed first and receive the biggest and best portions. Similarly, women themselves may choose to give food to their children, in such instances pregnant and lactating women are especially at considerable high risk of malnutrition. Sometimes women/adolescent mothers who have been raped may discontinue breast-feeding. Some of these inequalities will be addressed through mother support group’s sessions. Equality on gender (male and female) participation will be encouraged and most especially the women in the nutrition program will be provided with information that will lead to adoption of healthy practices, influencing a healthy family. Mother support groups will play a key role on the involvement of the affected population in infant and young child feeding program, supporting them to make right decision pertaining childcare practices. IMC will put in place safe and friendly systems for women to respond to their needs. IMC will also mainstream gender equality in program review and planning through deep analysis of the program results to assess impact of health activities to target beneficiaries, particularly women and girls. This proposed project is also designed and will be implemented to account for the challenges that may be faced by PWDs and Older People. IMC will promote accessibility of the project’s services by defining cross-impairment indicators for monitoring and ensuring that support is given to people with different impairments. All IMC staff and volunteers will receive protection training that highlights the special needs of these groups. Risk communication activities will also emphasize the role of the community on ensuring protection for PWDs and older people, and their immediate referrals to health facilities in case of any illnesses. IMC will use the IASC Guidelines on the inclusions of PWDs in Humanitarian Actions.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

IMC will adopt a multi-sectoral approach in services delivery in all its target locations, integrating health, nutrition and protection services. IMC directly implements nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive interventions, as well as strengthening the enabling environment for nutrition to address the immediate, underlying and basic causes of undernutrition through provision of both curative and preventive nutrition services. In areas/facilities where IMC does not implement nutrition programs, IMC will closely work with nutrition partners on the ground to establish a well-coordinated and coherent referral systems. Through this, IMC will contribute to decreasing the incidence and prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 and PLWs.
