Project Summary

In response to the huge needs resulting from the ongoing conflict in Syria as well as the recent escalation of conflict and displacement in NWS, SAMS through this project aims to ensure conflict-affected Syrians have access to quality, integrated primary health services in Idleb Governorate. Through this project, SAMS will support the provision of quality, standardized primary and reproductive care, following the Essential Package of Primary Health Services (EPHS) including treatment for communicable (CDs) and non-communicable diseases (NCDs), Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) and including improved GBV response in addition to the community health workers (CHWs), in four primary health clinics in Northwest Syria.
This project aims to both fill critical service gaps and improve the quality of services, through achieving the following objectives:
1. Conflict-affected people, including GBV survivors, receive quality primary health care services.
2. Health systems in implementation area are strengthened

Needs

Close to eight years into crisis, the overall scale and complexity of humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain staggering in terms of magnitude and severity with an estimated 13.2 million people who are in need of health assistance in Syria. Females make up 72 per cent of the PiN. Vulnerable groups include 5.2 million women of reproductive age, 2.2 million children under 5 years old, and 2.3 million people aged over 50, (HNO 2019). Across Syria health-related needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient access to healthcare due to a combination of damage and destruction of health facilities, insufficient human resources, and imposed access constraints. According to the WHO Surveillance System of Attacks on Healthcare (SSA), 137 targeted attacks on health care took place in Syria during the first 10 months of 2018, resulting in a reported 97 deaths and 169 injuries, (World Health Organization. Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care. See ssa.who.int for the Public Dashboard), compared to 122 verified attacks in 2017, reportedly resulting in 73 deaths and 149 injuries, (Violence against Health Care report by the Turkey Health Cluster). Attacks and resource constraints impact the functionality of health facilities. September 2018 Health Resource Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) data indicated that across Syria 46 per cent of health facilities were either non-functional or partially functional, with 167 facilities completely destroyed, and a further 419 facilities partially destroyed and in need of rehabilitation. The national ratio of fully functional Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCs) of 2.28 per 50,000 populations is above Sphere Standards, but the distribution is highly unequal, with 101 of 270 surveyed sub-districts reporting having no fully functional PHC, and an additional 9 sub-districts not meeting minimum standards. Nationally, less than one functional hospital bed is available per 1,000 populations, which barely below Sphere standards and very low when measured against a global average of 3 beds per 1,000 populations. As for PHCs, availability of and access to hospital services is not distributed evenly across the country, (Whole of Syria Q3 2018 consolidated HeRAMS).UNICEF/WHO estimates that in 2019, about 577,219 newborns will require routine immunization across Syria, with an estimated 320,000 children between 13 and 59 months of age not fully vaccinated during their first years of life. Protection concerns are similarly widespread and disproportionately affect vulnerable women and children. The 2018 Voices report (Voices from Syria 2018, Assessment Findings of the Humanitarian Needs Overview) confirmed that gender-based violence (GBV) continues to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria at home and in public. In the recent Global Protection Cluster assessment of Syria, women report being least safe at home, experiencing marital rape, being locked up by their husbands and even homicide. The main types of GBV affecting women are verbal harassment, domestic violence, child marriages and the fear of sexual violence including sexual harassment. Survivors face significant challenges in accessing GBV response services, and in NWS, the needs are vastly higher than the current services can address. Access is also affected by barriers attributed to safety, lack of awareness and availability of gender-sensitive services (particularly for RH), and restrictions on the movement of women and girls. Due to various reasons, including stigma, GBV survivors often resort to non-disclosure, a common negative coping mechanism.The Voices report expressed the importance for better integration of GBV programming with health programming to improve access and availability of gender and culturally sensitive health services.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Sexual Exploitation and abuse by Facilities staff toward beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: high. Mitigation measures: 1. Set PSEA Policy and effectively implement the policy in Hiring the staff and oriented them on the policy, training them, and make sure every staff sign the policy, 2. Effective complaint mechanism in place, 3. Awareness raising for beneficiaries on PSEA, 4. Clear complaints channels for beneficiaries and distribute brochures and leaflets in all places in the facilities (waiting rooms, entrance, Etc.).Risk: Sexual and Gender Based Violence in the facilities between beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Put cameras in the entrance and every floor of the facilities and corridors, 2. Use or establish separated toilets for men and women, 3. Establish spacious waiting rooms to host huge number of patient. 4. Put keys for the empty rooms if any. Risk: Facilities are inaccessible for the disabled patient and elderly persons. Possibility: moderate. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Build ramp for disabled people at the facility entrance, 2. Make sure of the availability of logistics supplies needed for elderly persons and people with disabilities. Risk: impure or spoiled medications and supplies that could be harmful to beneficiaries. Possibility: moderate. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Procure medication with long time validity (taking into consideration the transportation time for these medication and deliver it to the facilities then to beneficiaries to be used), 2. Procure the medications from reliable sources, 3. ensure cold-chain transportation for drugs requiring low temperatures, 4. Regular monitoring of the storage conditions of the medications and the validity date.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Control by unaccepted groups / entities or GoS. Mitigation measures: Anti-Terrorism Check (ATC), Pause / Cessation; Information security SOP; Strengthening the relations with the local communities; Relocating the activities locations.Risk: Attack by GoS and its allies / others.Mitigation measures: Selecting fortified locations; Security assessment (site & building); Evacuation SOP; Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation.Risk: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salariesMitigation measure: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries; Repeating the trainings regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it; Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible.Risk: Abduction (kidnapping)Mitigation measure: Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Security Briefing; Counter Abduction; Abduction Management training for staff and partners.Risk: TheftMitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for SAMS facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records during monitoring visits; Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Quarterly physical inventory of warehouses; review of monthly consumption reports; Security Briefing for staff and partners.
