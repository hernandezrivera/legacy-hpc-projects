Project Summary

The establishment of quality learning opportunities is urgently required to ensure children and youth can exercise their right to education and retain a sense of hope. UNHCR seeks to facilitate access to equitable quality education for children aged 3-14 yrs, adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years through the existing learning centres ensuring rentention of learners and creating opportunities for transition from one level to the next per the Guidline for informal Education Program (GIEP). Additionally, through technology enable education, UNHCR and its partners will enhance the learning experience for the adolescents and youth. Recruit, train and deploy teachers, procure and distribute adequate teaching and learning materials. Enable recruitment and training of adequate National Non-Govermental organization staff to facilitate appropriate education service delivery. Maintain existing learning centres and shared spaces, construct additional resilient learning facilities. Create opportunity for girls only learning spaces/sessions to guarantee access to female adolescents and youth. Equip and operationalize the satellite teachers training facility in Ukhiya aimed at enhancing the processes of teacher professional development. Strengthen community based language training programs led by young refugees. Strengthen the mechanism to measure learning outcomes and institute a reliable Refugee Education Management Information System (REMIS).
Strengthen the existing partnership refining and identifying strategic focus based on comparative advantages. Seek and involve non-traditional humanitarian actors; engage institutions of higher learning, facilitate policy dialogues aimed at enhancing access to meaningful education for refugees in Bangladesh. Continue the ongoing constructive engagement with distric education institutions to create more opportunities for Host community adolescent and youth living in Ukhiya and Teknaf. Initiate certifiable teachers training packages in response to the refugee program in Bangladesh. Promote community led language and skills hub development centres.

Needs

Per the Education Sector gap analysis in July 2019, about 16% of the children age 6-14 and 96% of adolescent and youth aged 15-24 are not accessing any form of learning. Moreover, over 113,643 children enrolled in Community based ECD ought to progress to level II. The major obstacle to access to meaningful education for the refugees remain a lack of endorsed curriculum with clear pathway to progression, accreditation and certification. While the endorsement of LCFA level 1 and 2 is a positive step, a lack of clarity on the next steps remains an issue of concern to the community as well as the education stakeholders. Moreover, the implementation of the LCFA/GIEP remains a complex process of developing and delivering the teaching and learning materials on time negatively impacting learning. Further, due to a lack of trained teachers, teaching is largely rote, slowing pace of learning significantly. For the learners to thrive, they must acquire essential skills in academic as well as life skills such as Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), psychosocial wellbeing, health and hygiene promotion, as well as awareness on vital elements on environmental protection, individual development and collective resilience. As such, in addition to subject matter mastery, all the teachers require training on DRR, psycho-social support (PSS), Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Sanitation and Hygiene promotion and Child safe guard principles.

Activities or outputs

UNHCR seeks to strengthen and expand equitable quality lifelong learning opportunities aligned with the education sector, Ministry of Primary and Mass Education (MoPME) as well as Ministry of Education (MoE) standards. UNHCR will continue extending technical support and contribute towards the overall improvement of education services for refugees and host community children, co-leading the Strategic Advocacy Group (SAG), facilitating retention of learners already enrolled and expanding access to reach a total of 88,400 children and youth aged 3-24 years in 16 different camps. UNHCR will expand physical access through renovation and maintenance of an existing 442 classrooms and 1235 community-based learning facilities and constructing of 96 new learning center (66 double story and 30 single story) and 30 community-based learning facilities with gender segregated and adequate Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) facilities. Additionally, UNHCR will recruit 150 teachers and facilitators for new learning facilities. Support host community schools by renovating classrooms, toilets and continuing support for ongoing Community Technology Access (CTA) centers to benefit 6,600 students. Procure school, teachers, and students kits for 1,850 learning spaces. The project will engage the community in overall project cycle through organizing training on MHPSS, DRR and SGBV and organizing join monitoring visit, FGDs and KII. Well trained and supported teachers are motivated and better prepared to support learners. Due to the traumatic experiences, many refugee children present the most dynamic and complex classroom of learners. As such, teachers must be equipped beyond the subject matter content to enable them to navigate the complex classrooms and ensure learning takes place. The project shall target 1000 teachers, 70 technical officers and school supervisors. Moreover, UNHCR will also ensure that the newly recruited staff develop their aptitude effectively managed their classrooms through the foundational training. Additionally, Efforts to provide training to understand and help mainstream gender-sensitive and inclusive education principles for all teachers and educator is a priority. The project targets; 1,500 teachers and facilitators; 70 technical officers and school supervisors for training programs aimed at sensitization on life-saving interventions that includes; DRR, PSS, PSEA, Sanitation and Hygiene promotion and the tenets of “Do No-Harm” including child safeguarding principles. To mitigate the scarcity of space in the camps, enhance durability and guarantee the safety and security of learners and educators, UNHCR is planning to upgrade the existing bamboo facilities to double floor classrooms with gender segregated WASH facilities. The structure will also end the need for routine repairs and reduce the maintenance cost over time. The proposed structure can withstand winds of up to 125 km/h, enhancing the safety of learners and educators while guaranteeing access to twice the number of learners within the same physical space. Additionally, the learning centers will serve as better temporary emergency shelters if needed as has been the case during cyclones and monsoon. UNHCR seeks to collaborate with various partners to promote skills development and self-reliance through the establishment of adolescent and Youth clubs as well upgrading existing adolescent clubs. The adolescent and youth clubs will provide a platform for the development of foundational skills that support lifelong learning, ensuring that youth can live and work in dignity. The program targets the youth and adolescents aged 15-24 with an opportunity to bridge the learning gap that currently exists for the age cohort. The main component of the learning program seeks to provide learners with an integrated curriculum comprising life skills training combined with basic literacy and numeracy and psycho-social support.
