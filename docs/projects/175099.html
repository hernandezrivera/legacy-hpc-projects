Project Summary

Pakistan is a disaster-prone country mostly faced with floods, droughts, earthquakes and is currently passing through the second wave of COVID-19 pandemic. The ongoing COVID19 Pandemic has further deteriorated the situation as the country is faced with both direct and indirect morbidities and mortalities due to COVID pandemic. COVID-19 cases are the highest in Sindh and Punjab provinces and certain urban areas of Balochistan followed by KP.To date, the total number of cases notified since the beginning of the outbreak is 488,529 with 10,350 deaths (CFR: 2.12%).
Most recently, heavy rains in Pakistan in august 2020 resulted in devastating floods affected many districts in Sindh and Balochistan province resulting damages to infrastructure and properties and population displacement. Sindh is the highly affected province followed by Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa respectively. Literature reveals that global warming, unpredicted heavy rains and floods, population displacement due to natural and man-made disasters result in outbreaks of water and vector borne diseases resulting in high morbidity and mortality. Most districts of Balochistan, Sindh and KP are more prone to natural emergencies, already show poor development indicators including health. The country has suffered approximately $18 billion in damages and losses from natural disasters over the past decade. It has been estimated that nearly 3 million people are affected by disasters in Pakistan each year; the annual economic impact of floods – which affects 77% of all Pakistanis affected by disasters – is estimated between $1.2 billion to $1.8 billion, equivalent to 0.5% to 0.8% of Pakistan’s GDP.
Due to the unexpected flooding situation in Sindh, on 25th august 2020 emergency was declared in 20 of the 29 districts in the province. In Sindh 2.2 million people have been directly or indirectly been affected by the flooding with 77,337 houses fully damaged and 137,007 partially damaged in 15,233 villages.
The second most affected province is Balochistan where torrential rains lashed 22 districts of Baluchistan province on Saturday 7 August 2020 caused flooding and damaged bridges and highways, cutting off highways Gwadar-Karachi, Quetta-Jacobabad from main cities. Initially, it has been reported that Jhal Magsi, Jafferabad, Sibi, Harnai, Naseerabad and Kachi Districts areas are badly affected by the floods.
Health related effects in flood affected areas of Sindh revealed that 15% of the health facilities are nonfunctional. The nonfunctional facilities are more based in the rural settings, compared to urban. The provision of round the clock (24/7) services in the functional health facilities of the flood affected areas is 57%, while 43% have services availability only at morning times. Reproductive Maternal and Neonatal Child Health (RMNCH) services for pregnant women have also been affected in various districts, while 43% of the pregnant women have no access to the health facilities. The average time required to reach the nearest health facility including RMNCH services has almost doubled in both the urban and rural flood affected areas due to damage to infrastructure; roads, bridges and limited public transportation which has also increased the monetary costs for the households while some residents are expected to delay seeking health care due to financial limitations, which would negatively impact the communities.
Alongside with this, other diseases continue to impact the health status of the affected populations. As of 1st October 2020, a total number of 1,964 confirmed dengue cases has been reported out of 81,379 suspects from all four provinces and Capital city (ICT) of Pakistan.
