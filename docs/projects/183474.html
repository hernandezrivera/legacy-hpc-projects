1 - Needs

The tragic May 2021 military conflict which occurred in the Gaza Strip was only the latest in a long line of violent escalations which has underscored the critical need for conflict preparedness and protection activities in Palestine. The Gaza Strip remains in a state of protracted low intensity conflict in which Israeli military incursions and airstrikes are common place and high-intensity periods of conflict can materialize without warning. Armed conflict in Gaza is characterized by the widespread use of explosive weapons in populated areas (EWIPA), which has had a devastating impact on Gaza’s civilian population. From 2010 through to the end of 2020, Action On Armed Violence (AOAV) recorded a total of 782 confirmed attacks using explosive weapons in Gaza which resulted in at least 1317 civilian deaths and over 3800 of civilian injuries. During the recent May 2021 Gaza conflict alone, 128 Palestinian civilians were killed and nearly 2000 injured in Gaza according to UN OCHA. Facing ongoing instability and the continuous possibility of military threats, residents and communities across the Gaza Strip must be prepared for this eventuality and pre-emptively implement safety measures to mitigate the risks associated with armed conflict. By far the greatest conflict related risk threatening civilians in Gaza is the use of explosive weapons in populated areas. Explosive weapons such as aerial bombs, missiles, projectiles, etc., have wide-area blast and fragmentation effects which can kill and injure people within broad proximity to the detonation. Safety and preparedness measures exist which can help civilians mitigate the risks associated with explosive threats and help to improve civilian survivability during and/or after an attack occurs. Knowledge of safety measures, risk mitigation techniques, proper evacuation procedures, basic fire safety, and basic First Aid can help civilians prevent loss of life either during or in the aftermath of an attack. Public awareness regarding Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) also plays a critical role in protecting civilians against ERW which can litter populated areas during a conflict and pose a threat long after an attack occurs. So far in 2021, there have been several accidents involving ERW in Gaza resulting in 60 casualties (16 women, 16 men, 10 girls, 18 boys), which is dramatically higher than the previous year owing to increased ERW contamination. The presence of ERW contamination in Gaza and the ever-present possibility of renewed high-intensity conflict underscores the need to educate civilians about the threats posed by explosive weapons and ERW prior to any future bouts of armed conflict. Despite the clear and present need to raise awareness about emergency preparedness and protection in Gaza, concerted efforts focused on improving individual and household risk mitigation measures have been limited. A field survey conducted in 2015-16 by NPA with 1,689 head-of-households (465 women, 1224 men) in vulnerable areas of Gaza showed that the vast majority of civilians (99.98%) in these areas had never received emergency preparedness and risk education training, many civilians had little to no first aid (84%) or fire safety (91%) experience, and the presence of ERW was still a perceived threat (93%). Civilians surveyed generally held many misconceptions about what constituted safe behaviour during a conflict and lacked basic information that could help improve the safety of themselves and their loved ones in the event of an attack. The implementation of NPA’s Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) risk education training sessions in communities in 2016-21 has proven effective at improving public awareness about conflict-related risks and informing people about the safety measures they can implement to improve their safety during an emergency. NPA’s pre-/post-training assessments of adult CPP beneficiaries conducted in Dec 2019-Jan 2020 and with children in August 2020 have demonstrated both groups retain the information taught and are far more knowledgeable than the baseline (ie. untrained) on the training content. Tests show that trained beneficiaries are significantly more aware of the safest actions and positions to take during an attack, how to protect one’s head and body, breathe control, how to safely evacuate a building, and what they should do if they encounter ERW. By emphasizing that there are indeed actions which civilians can take to better protect themselves during conflict, emergency preparedness training is helping to empower civilians, motivate them to seek adequate protection, and combat feelings of indifference or helplessness.

2 - Activities

This project represents the third year of a four-year intervention in the Gaza Strip seeking to provide CPP training and support to Gaza’s vulnerable civilian population, funded by the NMFA. As a leading global advocate against the use of EWIPA, Norwegian People’s Aid and the NMFA aim to remedy the existing gap regarding emergency preparedness risk education in Palestine as one of the world’s countries most affected by explosive weapons through CPP programming. NPA’s CPP initiative empowers civilians to mitigate risks associated with armed conflict and explosive weapons. This project will utilize NPA’s existing CPP framework, methodologies, and educational materials which have been effectively employed in Gaza since 2015 to expand the provision of CPP risk education training to adult heads of household and school aged children. To date, NPA has provided CPP risk education training to over 131,000 direct beneficiaries across the Gaza Strip in cooperation with the school system and NPA’s network of local partners. Participants of NPA's CPP risk education trainings receive instruction on the following topics: home preparation and blast protection safety measures, conflict risk mitigation measures, safe evacuation procedures, basic fire safety, ERW awareness, and improvised First Aid solutions. NPA’s CPP framework utilizes separate curriculums for adults and children, designed to convey safety messaging in a manner which is appropriate and effective for each age group. All training sessions incorporate high quality training aids, educational materials, and media to enhance the learning experience. This project aims to achieve its objective via the following activities conducted in partnership with up to three CBO partners (TBD based on needs assessments; NPA’s 2021 CPP implementation partners are the Palestinian Save the Children Foundation, Save Youth Future Society, and Youth Without Borders Forum): Activity 1: Capacity development of CBO partners and delivery of CPP training sessions for adult direct beneficiaries in prioritized areas of the Gaza Strip. 3 - Indicators

Monitoring & Evaluation: While CPP training operations are carried out in the field by NPA’s cooperating partners, training sessions are continually monitored by the NPA Palestine CPP team which consists of two national staff based in Gaza: 1) NPA’s CPP Area Coordinator, and 2) the CPP Project Officer. During the start-up phase of each project, the CPP Area Coordinator and Project Officer are responsible for training NPA’s local implementing partners as CPP trainers and practitioners – usually conducted via 2-3 day training-of-trainers workshops – to ensure that the partners have sufficient capacity to deliver the project. Partner procurement processes during the start-up phase are closely supported and monitored by the CPP team to ensure that the quality and quantities of the materials, training aids, and equipment procured are adequate for the proper implementation of the project. During the training phase of the project, the CPP programme team deploys on a daily basis to conduct monitoring visits at partner training sites across Gaza. These monitoring visits allow NPA to directly QA the delivery of the training in the field, provide guidance and assistance to the partner training team, understand and help address the challenges faced by the partner in the implementation of the activity, gather beneficiary feedback, and collect photography and video records. Partner reporting is conducted directly from the field via NPA’s Survey123/ArcGIS-based electronic reporting system which enables the individual training teams to submit standardized training reports in-situ via mobile device. Electronic reports submitted in this manner capture all key training session data such as location, date, start time, end time, number and types of beneficiaries (disaggregated by gender, age, and disability status), materials used, issues/challenges, etc, and include photos for purposes of remote verification and QA. Teams complete and submit their reports in the field immediately following the completion of a training session, which are then automatically logged and collated on NPA’s online Information Management (IM) dashboard system. This IM dashboard visualizes the aggregated report data as charts and key statistics, allowing both NPA and the implementing partner to track operations and progress against project indicators at a glance, in real time. The system allows for close coordination between NPA’s project monitors and the partner project team, enabling increased situational awareness and the ability to make rapid adjustments in the targeting of project activities. NPA will report on the project's indicators to UNMAS and the Mine Action Working Group of the Protection Cluster on a regular basis. NPA will also report project indicators through the HRP periodic reporting framework and update the Financial Tracking System as funding for project activities is secured. Results will be measured via pre- and post-training tests for individuals to determine changes in knowledge, attitudes, and practices of beneficiaries as a result of the intervention. Evaluation is a key aspect of NPA Palestine’s CPP intervention approach in Gaza which is conducted with the aim of measuring the impact and effectiveness of a CPP project’s activities. As a Protection activity focused on improving the safety and preparedness of civilians living in Gaza through the provision of training, CPP training activities are evaluated by how they impact the knowledge, attitudes, and practices of civilians living in at-risk, conflict-affected communities. Pre-/post-testing of beneficiaries is conducted throughout the training phase of the project to gauge baseline knowledge and attitudes regarding the training content. NPA evaluates the baseline (pre-training test) data and compares it with the endline (post-training test) data to determine changes knowledge and attitudes as a result of the training. Beneficiary feedback is collected continuously throughout the project’s training phase to help the CPP programme team understand how well the beneficiaries are receiving the information, which content they find most/least useful, and which aspects of the training can be improved. While changes in knowledge and attitudes can be tracked relatively easily through periodic evaluation, determining changes in practices has proven far more difficult. NPA will implement a project evaluation over the final months of 2021 and again in 2022 to assess the impact of its CPP interventions in the previously targeted communities. The aim of this assessment will be to reach out to and interview individuals who have previously received CPP training about how they have implemented the safety information in their everyday lives, especially in the wake of the May 2021 military conflict. This data will help NPA further refine its CPP framework to better address existing protection needs and gaps. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

NPA has been implementing CPP programming in Gaza for over 6 years and over this time has worked to develop its methodologies, approaches, and content by engaging with affected populations and incorporating their feedback. NPA works to collect feedback from all relevant groups (gender, age, disability, geographic location, etc.) on a routine basis so that their needs and perspectives are taken into account. Each year, NPA uses evaluation data and beneficiary feedback from these groups to make adjustments to its training approach/curricula, realign project targeting, and update CPP safety message content. NPA draws on the experience and connections of its wide network of local cooperating partners to foster cross-communication and connect with the communities we aim to assist. By doing so, NPA ensures that affected populations are heard and included at all stages of our projects, and that CPP activities are implemented in line with NPA’s core ethos of “Solidarity in Action”. NPA’s approach to its development and humanitarian programming is rooted in empowering the most vulnerable to create societies where power and resources are distributed more fairly, and their rights and protection are ensured. NPA uses its experience and interventions in Palestine to help champion the rights of Palestinians and to help amplify their voices on the international stage.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Preventing harm to civilians is the core of NPA’s CPP programme and, as such,ensuring the safety and dignity of targeted communities is critical to our project plan.NPA is committed to the humanitarian principle of ‘do no harm’ and has designed thisproject’s activities to empower beneficiaries and mitigate threats against them withoutjeopardizing their wellbeing. Special considerations regarding age, gender, disability,and displacement, among others, will be considered at all stages of the project. CPP training provides beneficiaries with the knowledge they need to provide for their physical protection and for the protection of others during periods of armed conflict. CPP safety messaging is designed to help prevent people from taking risks during conflict scenarios and to feel empowered to take active decisions for one’s own protection before, during, and after conflict. Training content for children is specifically designed to be child-friendly and promote conflict safety principles in a way that is accessible and sensitive to their wellbeing. NPA’s Code of Conduct and Safeguarding Policies help prevent and mitigate risks for project beneficiaries and staff. Complaint mechanisms will be put in place to help NPA and its partners respond to issues and complaints. Routine QA and monitoring in the field to ensure that activities are being conducted in line with the humanitarian principle of Do No Harm, while beneficiary feedback will help inform the CPP programme team of any potential risks observed by the beneficiaries themselves.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NPA’s CPP framework has been designed to meet the needs of those most vulnerableto the direct and indirect effects of armed conflict. NPA will prioritize targetingaccessible at-risk areas and communities most severely affected by past conflict, aswell as vulnerable populations such as women, children, people with disabilities (PwD)and caregivers, and refugees where possible. NPA will continue to work with its network of local partners with specialization in reaching specific disability groups and adapt its CPP its messagingand methodologies to better meet the needs of PwD under our 2022 programmeactivities. CPP risk education activities will be disability mainstreamed in order to better meet the needs of PwD and guarantee their participation in the project. NPA will work with its implementing partners to specifically target beneficiaries with disabilities knowing that PwD may have reduced mobility and/or access to risk education trainings. NPA will work to ensure its CPP risk education video content posted to YouTube will include subtitles for the benefit of people with hearing disabilities. NPA actively consult and seek out feedback from beneficiaries with disabilities to better inform the project design, lesson plans, and make continuous improvements with regard to accessibility.
