Project Summary

UNRWA estimates that 418,000 Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance in 2021. In a context of rising inflation and additional economic hardship caused by COVID-19, Palestine refugees are more and more affected by food insecurity and therefore increasingly depend on UNRWA assistance to meet their minimum food needs. Due to the vulnerability of Palestine refugees in the current socio-economic crisis, compounded by COVID-19, in 2021 UNRWA will expand its food programming and provide all 418,000 Palestine refugees with in-kind food assistance through three distribution rounds. The Agency will continue to monitor the availability of food on the markets and will retain operational flexibility to adjust to a changing context as appropriate.
The composition of the food basket was developed based on WFP guidelines and in view of meeting ~ 50% of daily nutritional needs for 4 months.
UNRWA's original plan is to distribute 3 rounds of in-kind food assistance, each round covering a period of 4 months. However, this depends on the availability of funds. Until May 2021, UNRWA received or confirmed pledges are only sufficient to cover the first two rounds of 2021. The Agency will continue to mobilize resources to ensure funding for the third round is also secured.
In 2021, the number of people estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance including for food needs is 418,000 Palestine refugees to strengthen their resilience and prevent further deterioration into acute food insecurity. As per its emergency planning, UNRWA aims to provide food assistance as well as multipurpose cash assistance, and protection interventions at both household and community levels, as well as the coordination of these activities across the different Sectors framework.
UNRWA has been providing life-saving humanitarian aid to Palestine refugees since the start of the crisis in Syria. The in-kind food assistance program remains one of the most important forms of UNRWA emergency support to stabilize the food security status for Palestine refugees in Syria, as it helps them meet part of their basic nutritional and survival requirements (~50% of kcal/person/day) which will increase resilience, and reduce humanitarian needs and the spiraling food insecurity levels.

4 - Needs

Syria hosts a significant number of Palestinian refugees, and the majority of them continue to require assistance. Of the 438,000 Palestinian refugees in Syria, 60% have been displaced at least once. In addition to their refugee status, Palestinian refugees remain equally affected by the broader macro-economic, Covid-19 and social conditions affecting Syria as a country. Even before the current crisis, 91% of Palestine refugees were living in absolute poverty. There is a growing lack of food or money to buy food. The rapidly deteriorating economy and near hyperinflation are increasing the vulnerability of the entire population. The drastic loss of purchasing power during this economic crisis renewed fears about meeting basic needs in particular food. National average food basket price in February 2021 was 241% higher compared to February 2020.The impact of COVID-19 is also very concerning. According to an assessment conducted by UNRWA in July 2020 on the Socio-Economic impact of COVID-19 on Palestine refugees, 79% of Palestine refugees reported having reduced the number of meals or quantity of food they have been consuming since March 2020. More than 90% of Palestine refugee households stated that they had to resort to consuming cheaper food and/or food of lower nutritious value. Furthermore, Palestine refugees’ ability to secure their income has faced a devastating setback due to limited livelihood opportunities. An estimated 99% of Palestine refugee households surveyed reported that they struggled to purchase food and other basic items due to the increasing market prices, with many going into debt to meet their basic needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNRWA will provide in-kind food assistance to 418,000 Palestine refugees through three distribution rounds. Each round will cover 4 months of food assistance. The Agency will continue to monitor the availability of food on the markets and will retain operational flexibility to adjust to a changing context as appropriate. Each food ration for one person will be composed of the following items: W/Flour (25 kg), Rice (2 kg), S.F. oil (2 lt), Sugar (1 kg), Chick Peas (2 kg), Lentils (2 kg), Milk (0.4 kg). The composition of the food basket was developed based on WFP guidelines. Wheat flour will be included in the basket in 2021 for the first time, following WFP and Agency-wide guidelines. Distribution will be reorganized to adjust to the important quantities of wheat that will be distributed and ensure a smooth distribution process without overcrowding. Procurement will be either internationally or locally depending on price competitiveness’s and timely availability. UNRWA will distribute the food baskets through its distribution centres in targeted governorates. All precautions measures will be taken to limit COVID-19 contamination risks during the distributions. UNRWA staff will wear personal protective equipment, and social distancing will be ensured in all distribution centres. Monitoring and Evaluation: UNRWA has a dedicated Area Support Team and a SFO based Programme Support Team. Monitoring will be guaranteed through regular field visits and feedback from implementing staff based in the field and their respective programme. The Programme Support Office has a Monitoring and Evaluation system in place that closely follows up on and provides feedback and donor reports for all projects.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK 1 Beneﬁciaries face exploitation or abuse travelling to and from distribution points (Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium)• Mitigating measures: 1. In order to minimize problems in accessing distribution points, flexibility allowed within the families as to who collects the assistance. A delegation system allows individuals to nominate a person to collect the assistance on their behalf. 2. Lists of what beneﬁciaries received provided to facilitate their travel while carrying in-kind assistance. 3. Focus on community engagement and participation enhanced, and feedback responded to.RISK 2 Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of GBV and sexual harassment, particularly at locations with high numbers of beneﬁciaries (Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium)• Mitigating measures: 1. Vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and PwDs prioritized in order to reduce their waiting time. 2. Training provided on protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion, ensuring principles such as safety and dignity and access are incorporated. 3. Protection audits conducted on a biennial basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve services and optimize compliance to the main protection principles. 4. Focus on community engagement and participation enhanced, and feedback responded to.RISK 3 Explosive remnants of war (ERW) risk for Palestine refugees when accessing UNWRA services(Likelihood: low, Impact: high)• Mitigating measures: 1. Awareness raising and training to staff, community members, including children (awareness sessions and activities, awareness material, social media campaigns). 2. Text messages sent to beneﬁciaries advising on changes in order to minimize unnecessary movements.

UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is responded to.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. RISK 1 UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. The RBM approach to programming ensures evidence-based decision-making and accountability. Risks are also mitigated by ensuring a sound and solid monitoring of implementation. RISK 2: UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. RISK 3: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization materials will be made available at offices and distribution centres.
