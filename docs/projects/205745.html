Project Summary

The recurrent instabilities in most parts of South Sudan both political and inter-communal conflicts in addition to the effect of floods including the limited food security and livelihood opportunities result in the displacement of communities and the economic regression exerting much stress on already limited WASH services across South Sudan.
PAH's experience in successfully implementing both static and rapid responses in various sectors is using its strong WASH expertise and responding with emergency lifesaving assistance to vulnerable communities across South Sudan by improving access to potable water, sanitation services, and the knowledge and capacity for the affected communities for better wellbeing.
PAH prioritizes the vulnerable people affected by high level of WASH vulnerability especially in locations faced with IPC levels 3, 4 and 5, displaced people due to a variety of factors, returnees and host communities with over-stretched resources due to influx of IDPs in locations with outbreaks waterborne disease such as cholera, HEV, and where refugees and returnees/refugees have settled or in transit to their resettlement destinations. To improve access to clean and safe water (drinking, cooking and personal hygiene plus other domestic uses) PAH conducts repair/rehabilitation and construction of water points mainly boreholes, water yards, shallow wells, rainwater harvesting systems in the affected communities as well as in institutions, especially nutrition and health facilities for integration of the response. PAH prioritizes sanitation at the institutional level. PAH encourages a community participatory approach at community and household levels for sustainability as well as exit strategy. To enable a positive behavior change of the affected population and the sustainability of the operation and maintenance of the WASH infrastructure, PAH designs a community-led hygiene and health promotion messaging, combined with protection, health, nutrition and other cross-sectoral topics integrated with the messaging. PAH chose from the affected population people who are being trained as hygiene promoters, water management committees, sanitation committees, pump mechanics etc.
PAH ensures accountability to the affected population (AAP) and meaningful access to services for men and women, including people with disabilities and other special needs, the elderly, and children. PAH team encourages community participation, consultation, establishes safe and accessible feedback mechanisms, and promotes resilient approaches. The safety and dignity of the beneficiaries are at the center of interventions.

1. Needs

The precarious situation in South Sudan is very fluid resulting in deterioration of services especially for water supply and sanitation. This is further derails by outbreak of waterborne diseases such as the March 2023 outbreak of Cholera in Fangak and Malakal counties, the October 2023 outbreak of HEV virus in Fangak and Pibor (Marowa payam) and the Jiggers outbreak in Magwi. Armed violence involving cattle herders in most locations across South Sudan result in continuous displacements as well as loss of lives and properties while compromising the coping mechanisms of the IDPS, returning populations both internal and returnees from Sudan due to the ongoing conflict increasing the humanitarian situation across sectors including livelihood, emergency shelter services, WASH among others.The outbreak of civil unrest in neighboring Sudan in the north since April 2023 worsened the humanitarian situation across all sectors in South Sudan. This conflict that led to influx of people both refugees and South Sudanese returnees has put additional stress on the already scarce resources in South Sudan.Another factor that strains the humanitarian situation is heavy rainfalls causing in the low-land areas especially along the major rivers Nile, Sobat, Kiir, Jur and the flood plains including around the flood plains of Shamble in the South Sudan Great lakes region. In November and December, forecasts for above-average rainfalls could result to above normal flooding levels which will negatively exacerbate the already dire situation.PAH has been responding in the area of Canal Pigi and Pibor since 2022 and conducted needs assessments as well a baseline survey in March 2023. According to the findings, Pigi was heavily affected by flooding, people displaced, 75% of the people having limited access to clean drinking water, limited or no boreholes in the area. The majority of the households have broken water collection and storage containers, knowledge on household water treatment and storage was at 16% only. Almost 90% of the nutrition facilities were noted to - having no protected water points. In Atar, only one non-functional surface treatment plant was seen rendered un-operational due to a lack of fuel for generators. In Khorfulus, the surface treatment plant is submerged by the floods, community were displaced to higher ground. There is a high number of AWD among children under 5 years. 98% of HHs do not have latrines, open defecation is common. Hand washing facilities in nutrition facilities are not available. In Boma drivers for increased GAM rates and IPC levels ranges from floods to dry spell in some areas like Kassengor (IRNA/spot check August 2022), conflict induced displacement like in Nyat (IRNA Feb 2023). Following December 2022 intercommunal conflict in Gumuruk and Verteth 40-50KM away from Boma – more than 2000 individuals crossed to Boma between December 2022 and May 2023. This resulted in food insecurity and pressured (limited) WASH infrastructures.

2. Project activities

The program aims to reduce WASH-related morbidity, and mortality in Canal Pigi and Pibor in South Sudan by improving access to WASH services. The primary objectives include improving access to clean water, sanitation, and hygiene practices. Proposed approach/methodology: WASH NFIs Kit Distribution: The program addresses this by distributing hygiene kits, that include soap and water treatment materials t. This ensures access to essential hygiene products. The WASH NFIs will be provided to 3500 people. PAH will access the WASH NFIs from the cluster co-pipeline. Construction/Rehabilitation of Water Points: PAH will assess, repair, construct, and rehabilitate 29 water points, ensuring a sustainable clean water supply. This addresses the issue of contaminated surface water. Conversion of productive 2 boreholes into Solar Powered Water system- Mini Yard in Nutrition centers.Hygiene Promotion: Community hygiene promoters will educate the community about safe water practices, sanitation, and hygiene, thereby instilling sustainable behaviors to address the root causes of WASH issues. Capacity Building: PAH will conduct the capacity building of WASH management committees for sustainability of operation and maintenance and train pump mechanics.Sanitation Infrastructure Construction: The program will construct sanitation infrastructure in institutions on the community level. Latrine blocks of two stances will be constructed at the institutions depending on assessment findings. Safety Audits and Protection: All activities will be supported by safety audits by the Protection Officer to reduce risks to beneficiaries and improve the (AAP) approach. Monitoring and Evaluation: The program will incorporate MEAL activities related to complaint and feedback mechanisms, baseline, mid-term, and end-line surveys to determine the impact of the project.
