Project Summary

Through the proposed action, REACH (a joint initiative of ACTED, IMPACT Initiatives, and UNOSAT) will first undertake the technical generation and coordination of a comprehensive meta dataset, capable of providing triangulated information for application by humanitarian and development actors. The meta dataset will be composed of the most relevant datasets and information resources, determined through a participatory process with key actors on the basis of indicators available. It will also include key gender indicators and analysis. The purpose of the dataset will be to serve as a tool that combines the key information resources of relevance for the actors noted above and help inform programming and related activities, in particular the application of the triple nexus.
In support of accompanying elements of the nexus, REACH will also conduct an area-based data collection exercise in three communities in the West Bank. This activity will generate findings on key indicators, topics, and themes that are not sufficiently covered by the more generalized MSNA, and therefore feeding into and complementing the 2022 MSNA. Data collection will be limited to qualitative methods, primarily key informant interviews (KIIs). This qualitative data collection will supplement and complement existing data sources, allowing for a more in- depth follow-up on identified themes and communities of concern/interest. This activity will be situated within broader efforts to operationalize the triple nexus approach, principally through a focus on localization and identifying linkages between humanitarian and development activities, which can be applied in the response of relevant actors.
The proposed activities will aim to achieve the following objectives:
1. Directly inform joint humanitarian-development response planning based on the nexus approach that the United Nations Country Team (UNCT) adopted in 2022 through the provision of a triangulated meta dataset, consisting of key humanitarian and development resources, that can provide key multi-sectoral information at varying levels of analysis (e.g. community, household, individual) in a single resource.
2. Support humanitarian-development response planning on a localized level through the provision of area-specific qualitative data that can supplement MSNA findings and those of related datasets.
3. Develop informative outputs around key themes of areas of concern for response actors.
REACH will produce the following key outputs:
1 validated, comprehensive meta dataset
1 key findings and familiarization presentation 1 familiarization toolkit
3 area-specific briefs

1 - Needs

The oPt currently faces complex, multi-faceted challenges, which are predicated on decades of political and humanitarian crises. These have stunted development and limited opportunities for lasting peace. According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 2.1 million Palestinians out of a total population of 5.3 million, were estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance, 39% of whom were classified as being in severe need. Fifty-five years of occupation of the West Bank and the Gaza Strip, cyclical escalations in tensions, and recurring conflict between Israeli forces and Palestinian armed groups have eroded the protection of human rights and cultivated an environment of persistent insecurity. Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza are also experiencing the compounding effects of a diminishing economy, limited access to employment, and an overall decline in socioeconomic development opportunities. The COVID-19 pandemic and recurrent escalations have caused further economic contraction and exacerbated ongoing humanitarian needs, whilst inhibiting opportunities for growth in the long term. Vulnerable Palestinian households (HHs) are at continual risk of violence, forced displacement, food insecurity, and poverty, requiring a robust humanitarian response during a time of record-low funding levels for the provision of critical assistance and services. This is especially true for female-headed households, who have been proven to experience food insecurity, poverty, and limited access to services at greater rates than male-headed households. Other vulnerable groups, such as persons with disabilities, child laborers (boys and girls), and the elderly, are also deserving of additional attention, owing to their distinct circumstances and heightened vulnerabilities and humanitarian needs. In this context of humanitarian crisis and widespread need, including key variations across distinct population groups, publicly available information resources, and coordination between them, is critical for response actors. Though a number of datasets and related resources are currently available, existing efforts to unify them into a common, meta dataset have been scarce. Owing to diverging methodological characteristics (e.g., level of analysis, sampling), differing rationales and applications, limited coordination between actors of varying provenance (e.g. governmental, UN agencies, INGOs, etc.), and limited technical capacity / availability, these efforts have not been undertaken. Nonetheless, there is a clear need to develop a triangulated data resource which would, if done successfully, decrease over- reliance on a few datasets and create a more holistic overview of contemporary circumstances in Palestine with relevance from a humanitarian, development, and peace perspective. In this respect, the meta dataset aims to be an important resource in nexus planning and information needs. In addition to a need for triangulated data on the national and regional (e.g., West Bank and Gaza Strip) levels, there is also a need for localized information in the West Bank. The fragmented nature of this region, stemming in large part from the presence and policies of the Occupation, limits the ability of West Bank-wide data to accurately inform response programming. Communities in the region have wide-ranging and often distinct circumstances as a result of their geography and demography, their locality type (e.g., urban, rural, refugee camp), and their circumstances related to the Occupation (e.g., presence of military bases and settlements, access to land / resources, governance under the Oslo Accords). As a result, area-based data represents a valuable supplement for existing more generalized resources, such as the MSNA. Such assessments will also serve to advance the localization element that constitutes a key pillar of nexus thinking and application.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 - Coordination and Information Gathering: REACH intends to undertake the generation of the meta dataset under the auspices of official approval from key actors in the response. For the creation of the dataset, REACH will seek the mandate to conduct these activities from the relevant bodies and groups that support and guide the humanitarian response, principally those responsible for the creation and coordination of information resources. This may include the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the Inter- Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG), or the Assessment Working Group (AWG). In addition to seeking the mandate to carry out this activity, the first activity will also be devoted to soliciting the input of key partners, particularly those who are owners of potential datasets or placed in a central position in terms of information management and related coordination. This will include identifying the most valuable datasets for inclusion into the meta dataset, emphasizing those that cover a range of humanitarian and development indicators, as well as the compatibility of different datasets and optimal ways to present and socialize key actors with the data. A significant element of this activity will be devoted to ensuring that a sufficient number of datasets from the humanitarian and development realms are sufficiently incorporated, allowing the final dataset to provide information that bridges both dimensions and contributes to nexus application. As much as possible, REACH will incorporate datasets that include sex, age, and disability disaggregated data (SADD) and will specifically solicit such resources from partners in the inception phase.This phase will also include the identification of key milestones in the activities of the response (e.g. the HPC) and how REACH activities can best align and support in the process. Ahead of area-based data collection activities, REACH will similarly engage with key actors in the relevant areas of the response. Chief among these are those actors pursuing a localized approach to their programming and experts and others with experience in area-based research. The purpose of these activities will be to ensure that the research design is aligned with the information needs of partners, supplements existing information resources, avoids duplication of existing area- based research, and incorporates lessons learned from related assessments. It will also serve to identify those communities where area-based information does not exist and is most critical for upcoming response programming. The relevant methodological information (e.g. indicators, sampling, etc.), as well as the timeline, will be compiled in a research ToR published at the completion of this activity. Activity 2 - Creation of Meta Dataset: Following the identification of key datasets and information resources to form the inputs of the meta dataset, REACH will begin the process of constructing the new dataset. It is expected that in combination with data from a number of sources (including but not limited to data from OCHA, Humanitarian Atlas, UNSCO Atlas of Sustainable Development, PCBS Census and Labor Force Survey etc.), the 2022 MSNA will form the core of the final dataset, as will the 2021 MSNA. In creating the meta dataset, REACH will ensure that the final resource conforms with the technical and ethical standards delineated by IMPACT headquarters in Geneva. REACH will also prepare a series of outputs to familiarize potential partners and relevant stakeholders with the dataset including instructional presentations and trainings. Activity 3 - Conducting of Area-Based Research: REACH will conduct three area-based data collection exercises in the communities identified through the research design. These exercises will be defined by the conducting of KIIs with key stakeholders in the community and supplemented by relevant secondary data, including geographic resources and related research. In selecting experts, REACH will attempt to include representatives from key cross sections of society, including participants from different genders and experts in the circumstances of vulnerable groups within society. The Research Officers will also conduct field visits in the targeted communities. Key PSEA messages as well as information about ACTED’s complaint response mechanism (CRM) will be shared with all interviewees and officers will receive training on ethics in data collection, as explained further below. Following data collection, all information will be analyzed and presented in the form of area-based briefs, which will include this primary data as well as MSNA and related datasets as secondary resources. The area-based briefs will be responsible for presenting the overall needs of the assessed area, while also identifying distinct circumstances and needs according to key measurements of vulnerability, including sex, age, and disability (SADD).

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

ACTED and IMPACT will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and co-applicants’ work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that co-applicants learn from the feedback provided through this process. Any feedback received through the CRM will be taken into account for future and ongoing assessments, and will be incorporated into the research methodology if determined to be necessary.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

ACTED’s Complaint Response Mechanism also utilizes SMS and WhatsApp for potential complaints and affected population reporting.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Informed consent will be a central principle guiding the data collection activities, which includes informing all research participants that their participation may be withdrawn at any time and informing them on all CRM channels that are available to them and how to access them. In addition, all enumerators will undergo training in CRM awareness modules to ensure they are sufficiently informed of how to guarantee access and support research participants. In addition, all staff will undergo mandatory PSEA training and familiarize themselves with and sign ACTED’s Code of Conduct. As indicated above, the full range of ACTED’s CRM includes multiple modalities for complaints to be submitted, allowing maximum access to participants.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

To begin, REACH will only use datasets and related information resources that can affirmatively prove their data collection methods complied with the fundamental standards of ethical research. This includes, among other standards, explicit mention that respondents gave informed consent, data was sufficiently anonymized, and relevant considerations (e.g. training) were taken for data collection with minors and respondents with the potential for re-traumatization. In addition, before publishing the final meta dataset, REACH will ensure it conforms with the standards for anonymization of personal identifiable information (PII), as established by IMPACT headquarters in Geneva. Regarding primary data collection, informed consent will be the central principle guiding the data collection process. Data collectors will inform all participants of the purpose and objectives of the assessment and confirm their affirmative willingness to participate before beginning any interviews. Participants will also be informed of their right to withdraw from the interview at any point. Further, while REACH will collect contact information (likely phone numbers) in advance of doing the interviews, none of these will be included in any public resources. The same is true of all publicly identifiable information (PII). In addition, only adults will participate in the research; no persons under the age of 18 years old will be interviewed.
