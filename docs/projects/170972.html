Project Summary

The proposed project aims to enhance resilience and improve food security of 1475 farmer's HHs with capita of 7078 (1655 men, 1590 women, 3612 children and 219 elderly). This will be achieved through land rehabilitation of 650 dunums to enhance productivity and livelihoods. The project will rehabilitate 27 km of agricultural roads to enhance access to lands, rehabilitate 12 water cisterns to provide farmers with water for Agriculture, installing 6 km of water carries for crops irrigation and do better water management , rehabilitate 20 dunums of greenhouses to enhance productivity and livelihoods, planting of 500 dunums of fodder seeds that will serve farmers with livestock fodder and protect the land from settler’s attacks and confiscation. In addition to distributing agricultural inputs for 260 farmers such as (olive harvest tools, grass cutters, water tanks), these inputs will facilitate the work of the farmers and minimize the confrontation with Israeli's settlers, and distributing protection and hygiene materials (gloves, Masks and disinfecting gel) for 900 farmers to protect from COVID-19 outbreak. Last, rehabilitate 40 livestock barns/ animal shelter to protect the livestock from diseases and therefore ensure better production and livelihoods. This project directly respond to the 3rd strategic objective of HRP: ”The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. The proposed project will target 20 communities; in Ramallah,Salfit and Qalqeliya in area C, even in Farkha that is considered area A & B, the project will target a land that has a forfeiture order from Israeli Civil administration since 1983 called “Al-Baten” The targeted locations in Ramallah governorate suffers the threat of confiscation of the agricultural lands by settlersAs for Qalqelia governorate especially the western Izab struggle with access to land and movement restriction due to the fact that part of their agricultural lands are on the western side of the Apartheid wall (has water sources) were farmers are forced to access their lands through gates. Additionally, the (MoA) advised PARC to target locations in Salfit as they have not had sufficient support in the previous years, settler's attacks and occupation unhumanitarian measures. PARC team will announce and formulate Local Committees (LC) in each location. which will ensure beneficiaries have access to information and therefore the provided aid. The announcement will take place in the local village council, Mosques and PARC's social media platforms, women will be encourage to participate, where a separate session will be conducted for women in conservative localities. Then the formulation of LC will take place which will have representatives from; (village council) VC, Municipalities, CBOs, MoA, women clubs and they will participate and steer the project in order to ensure participation of local community, accountability & transparency in selection and reflect needs of the local community. The LC will be constituted of both men and women. The LC will ensure neutrality in accessing the provided humanitarian aid, giving high priority to women, children, elderly and people with disability. Furthermore, PARC gives 10 extra marks to Women headed households in the selection criteria, people with disabilities, elderlies with diseases. Then, tendering and contracting, where PARC and the local committees will ensure the best value of money for different services and quality. Also, all field officers will be made aware and will abide by PSEA practices to ensure beneficiaries protection. All activities implemented will be ensured to abide by protection of COVID-19 standard of Operation (SoPs). PARC will implement the project based on a well formulated exit strategy. Furthermore, the Project is designed based on (HRBA)focusing on the actual needs.

1 - Needs

PARC in cooperation with ACPP conducted a humanitarian need assessment in 2018-2019 and was recently verified over 40 locations in the West bank in August-September 2020 to document and respond to the needs of most vulnerable population in Ramallah, Salfit and Qalqiliya. The need assessment ensured participatory and human rights based approach in its methodology which was carried out via desk review, structured and semi structured interviews, and focus groups. The focus group engaged all vulnerable groups in the area from women, elderly and people with disabilities.This was carried out in coordination with key actors in the area and relevant stakeholders (Governorate bodies, MoA, OCHA field office, food security and protection clusters, Village councils’ members, and mukhtars). The need assessment aimed to determine the needs of all vulnerable groups with special attention to women, children, elderly and people with Disability. Through conducting activities to ensure safe access to agricultural lands and support production which will affect the food security of HH including women and children. The need assessment study was in line with OCHA reports that states the vulnerability of targeted communities in Ramallah, Qalqiliya and Salfit to add to that, the Jerusalem Aid & Human Rights Centre report stated that 18 communities in Salfit are suffering from the enormous expansion of settlements in the nearby area.18 communities in Salfit are surrounded with 22 settlements with a total of 43,478 dunums under Israeli jurisdiction. This threatens the humanitarian living conditions and rights of Palestinians living in Salfit. The conducted humanitarian need assessment and the meetings with MoA prioritized the most vulnerable locations as follows in Salfit (Biddya, Farkha, Marda, Mas-ha, Qarawat Bani Hassan, and Sarta) Qalqiliya (Al Mudawwar, Hajja , Immatin, Izbat al Ashqar, Izbat Jalud, Izbat Salman, Jayyus and Jit) and Ramallah (Abud, Al Mazraa al Qibliya, An Nabi Salih, Deir Nidham, Kobar, Umm Safa ). The proposed project aims to address the needs of the targeted location to ensure that their food security, livelihood assets is protected and enhanced through land, cistern, green house and barns rehabilitation, seeds distribution, provision of agriculture inputs in addition to hygiene and safety materials to protect the targeted population against COVID-19 outbreak. Consequently, the proposed intervention will respond directly to the Vulnerability measures identified by OCHA which categorize the Qalqiliya targeted communities (Jayyus, Hajja, Izbat Salman, Izbat Al Ashqar, Al Mudawwar and Izbat Jalud) to be high/ Medium vulnerability in regards to access to land, and protection. Noting that a high percentage of the farmers are females and parents, hence enhancing their access to land will ensure their protection, easier access, and therefore food security which will be reflected on female farmers living conditions/ food security and their children. another reflected need was seen in the, eastern side outside the wall and Western side inside the wall that is separated by the Apartheid wall. The humanitarian need assessment verified that there is a need in Qalqiliya to do land rehabilitation and rehabilitation and provision of water carries and the targeted locations are known to be the richest in water sources with existing water cisterns hence an opportunity to enhance land productivity. The land rehabilitation will highly prioritize female farmers, elderly farmers, farmers who support children and or have people with disabilities in their families. As for Ramallah Governorate and as advised by the MoA to target such communities who have not had the needed humanitarian support and are endangered with the constant settler's attacks, human rights violations, and confiscation. Ramallah targeted locations (Abud, Al Mazraa al Qibliya, An Nabi Salih, Deir Nidham, Kobar, and Umm Safa) are surrounded with Halamish settlement that is expanding and taking more of the targeted locations lands, making them highly vulnerable and in need of humanitarian interventions to protect their land and livelihood assets.

2 - Activities

In response to the needs of the targeted localities and in line with the HRP strategic objective number three: "The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued". The proposed project defined it's overall objective to: " To reduce vulnerability and protect the livelihoods of food insecure farmers and marginalized people to strengthen their resilience against man-made crises. " in order to achieve the overall project objective, the following activities will be implemented. In Salfit governorate the proposed project will: 1. Rehabilitate 250 dunums of Agricultural Lands to serve 63 farmers' HH in which Different sub activities will be implemented for each beneficiary depending on the needs: Light machinery work for leveling, rock removal, rehabilitation of the stony retaining walls to protect from soil degradation and to increase water retention capacity, fencing to protect land from wild and livestock animals 2. Rehabilitate/ open 10 km of Agricultural roads to serve 300 farmers' HH which will include several sub activities; machinery work, base course, culvert, and construction of stony retaining walls 3. Rehabilitate 6 water cisterns to serve 6 farmers' HH including all sub activities from cleaning to plastering covering the cost of the workers and materials. 4. Rehabilitate 20 livestock barns to serve 20 Farmer's HH and providing them with all needed equipment depending on the need of the barn. 5. Distribute fodder seeds to plant 250 dunums to serve 25 farmers' HH 6. Provision of Agricultural/production inputs such as (olive piking tool, grass cutter... etc) to serve 80 farmers. 7. Distribute protection materials (masks, gloves, disinfecting gel) to 300 farmers In Qalqelia Governorate the proposed project will: 1. Rehabilitation of 180 dunums of Agricultural lands to serve 46 farmers' HH which will include several sub activities; machinery work, base course, culvert, and construction of stony retaining walls 2. Rehabilitation of 6 Km of Agricultural roads to serve 180 farmers' HH which will include several sub activities; machinery work, base course, culvert, and construction of stony retaining walls 3. Rehabilitate 20 dunums of Green houses to serve 20 farmers' HH through the provision of needed materials for rehabilitation such as: wood, plastic sheets, internal water network and nets. The installation of material provided will be covered by the farmers 4. Installing 6 km of water carriers to serve 120 farmers' HH covering the cost of all materials, agriculture engineer from PARC and the installation will be covered by targeted farmers. 5. Provision of Agricultural/production inputs such as (olive piking tool, grass cutter... etc) to serve 80 farmers 6. Distribute protection materials (masks, gloves, disinfecting gel ) to 300 farmers. In Ramallah Governorate the proposed project will: 1. Rehabilitation of 220 dunums of Agricultural lands to serve 55 farmers' HH in which Different sub activities will be implemented for each beneficiary depending on the needs: Light machinery work for leveling, rock removal, rehabilitation of the stony retaining walls to protect from soil degradation and to increase water retention capacity, fencing to protect land from wild and livestock animals 2. Rehabilitation/opening of 11 km of Agricultural roads serving 330 farmers' HH including different sub activities: machinery work, base course, culvert, and construction of stony retaining walls 3. Rehabilitation of 6 rain water cisterns serving 6 farmers' HH including all sub activities from cleaning to plastering covering the cost of the workers and materials. 4. Rehabilitation of 20 livestock barns to serve 20 Farmer's HH and providing them with all needed equipment depending on the need of the barn. 5. Provision of fodder seeds to plant 250 dunums for 25 farmers’ HH 6. Provision of Agricultural/ production inputs such as (olive piking tool, grass cutter... etc) to serve 100 farmers 7. Distribute protection materials (masks, gloves, disinfecting gel ) to 300 farmers. It is worth mentioning that all targeted agricultural lands in which the activities will be implemented in are classified as area C while the communities live in area B.PARC has developed Standard of Operation SoPs for the implementation of the different activities field visit, M&E, complaint and feedback mechanism. The SoP was developed in partnership with Premier Urgence International as lead partner (a previous action). All activities implemented will be ensured to abide by protection of COVID-19 SoPs. PARC is also using its social media, channels, CBOs partners and community protection committees to sensitize community and raise awareness of community of the prevention measures of COVID 9 since its outbreak. The project seeks to mitigate the risk of additional potential harmful coping strategies due to COVID19, by targeting the most vulnerable households. PARC will be working to formulate PSEA policy to ensure beneficiaries protection. Field team will be trained on prevention of PSEA and ensure that the proper PSEA practices are well practiced to ensure beneficiaries protection. Last, all suggested activities will be implemented after developing a selection targeting criteria that highly prioritize female farmers, women headed households, Families who have members with disabilities, family members with heart dieases, families with children. As for the community activities like land and road rehabilitation high priority will be given to most vulnerable lands depending on sensitivity of its locations in addition to the previously mentioned social criteria.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

PARC has a mechanism regarding accountability and receiving feedback from beneficiaries; Handling complains and ensuring accountability measures are implemented since the design phase of the project until the handing over of all activities. Feedback is ensured via HAP mechanism that is adopted by PARC which ensures to provide a secure space for targeted population to express their satisfaction and dissatisfaction. In 2015, PARC developed its guidance to HAP policy systems in support and collaboration with Christian Aid Britain, which includes participation, information sharing, and complaint and response policies. This mechanism includes a well-designed complaint form that will be tracked through PARC’s MEAL officer and documented through the MEAL documentation system. All project activities will be followed by the MEAL officer who collects data to develop the lessons learned on two levels; during the implementation and after achieving the project, which PARC builds on for future similar designed interventions. A HAP complaint mechanism will be elaborated at targeted communities and audience will be encouraged to claim their rights. The mechanism allows beneficiaries and community members to provide their feedback and report to PARC in a formal and confidential manner. This is promoted and implemented in coordination with local committees (LCs). A project LC is formulated (women and men) representing community stakeholders and target groups (village councils (VC), CBOs, activists, farmers’ committees, women clubs, etc) in which they are given the opportunity to participate effectively in overseeing project implementation. Hence, a complaint and feedback box will be introduced to VCs, placed in the targeted communities in addition to a generic e-mail, and is easily accessible to community members (males and females). PARC team will ensure that health and safety measures are taken into consideration throughout the project life cycle.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No Harm" as seen to be part of ensuring accountability towards the local communities used and monitored during any intervention implemented by PARC including participation, information sharing, enabling complaints and "do no harm”, environment protection and gender mainstreaming. The designed project interventions and implementation strategies adheres to the social, political, and economic context of targeted populations and is based on comprehensive understanding of the targeted contexts through the conducted participatory needs assessment and PARC do-no-harm approaches. In regards to social sensitivity, PARC understands the conservative local Palestinian context and act on that in all project phases from the announcement that takes place in a wide, easily accessed area where women can feel comfortable to join and share their needs. For the political side, all proposed interventions will take into consideration the sensitivity of its location in which a low-profile working approach will take place. This will happen by supporting the local community to document the Israeli violations. In addition to that a group of the targeted community will follow up to document all Israeli violations and report back to PARC’s team, other relevant acting organizations and OCHA field office. Finally, at economic level the project interventions aid to support local population to empower their production abilities and access to their assets. Continued consultations will be conducted with the project target groups/stakeholders and HHs to analyze potential risks that the Project team will take into consideration while progressing/implementing the project. PARC team adheres to the developed Standard of Operation SoPs for the implementation of the different activities, field visits, M&E, complaint and feedback mechanism to ensure all safety and security measures are taken against COVID-19. The project seeks to mitigate the risk of additional potential harmful coping strategies due to COVID-19, by targeting the most vulnerable households .PARC believes in equal opportunities for all beneficiaries; ensuring equal access of information, neutrality and assistance through the project cycle starting with the inclusion of all relevant stakeholder’s in the need assessment. Coordination with the local authorities, CBOs, and community leaders in the project planning and selection process to ensure optimal community participation, outreach, and transparency in service provision. PARC ensures a transparent selection process which avoids social conflict during the implementation of the project.The project is designed based on Human-Rights Based Approaches (HRBA) including women, youth and Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). HRBA; focusing on the actual needs of people and integrating principles of accountability, transparency, meaningful participation, nondiscrimination and linkage to global, regional and national human rights instruments are an integral part of PARC work.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PARC ensures that aid and impartial access reaches the most vulnerable since the design phase when conducting a need assessment at communities. Gender mainstreaming and protection will be integrated within the project activities. In need assessment PARC ensures to engage local community members, VCs and other relevant stakeholders to have a comprehensive understanding of the targeted communities needs. The findings of the need assessment define the proposed interventions. This is then followed at the implementation phase of the proposed project, in which PARC starts its intervention with formulating a Project Local Committee that represents the targeted population and include key actors from the area and representatives of Community Based Organizations which ensures optimal outreach to vulnerable community groups. The project Local Committee and PARC team plays a role in ensuring the access to information to all community members through a clear project announcement in the mosques, village councils, and PARC’s social media platforms. PARC team develops a well-defined selection criteria that responds to the needs of the targeted population and ensures accessing the most vulnerable. This will be hand on hand with the project local committee. An open call of applications will also ensure the meaningful access, empowerment and transparent process in selecting final beneficiaries which will also be in participation with Project Local Committee. This participatory approach emphasizes ownership, transparency, good governance at beneficiary and community level. Monthly meetings will be held between the municipality, project local committee, community members and PARC field officer for each community. PARC accountability system, which follows a clear mechanism of complaint registration, reporting and solving, is accessible by the municipality and local community members. Regular follow-up of complaints received from communities, VCs, and Municipality are performed with PARC’s M&E unit, Including when necessary addressing OCHA for larger external complaints concerning security and overall occupation related to protection risks. Meaningful access, participation, and empowerment: through the established LCs which ensure optimal outreach to affected and vulnerable target groups, and documentation of Human Rights violations, which PARC will refer to the related clusters. PARC believes in equal opportunities for all beneficiaries; ensuring equal access of information and assistance through the project cycle starting with the inclusion of all relevant stakeholder’s in the need assessment. Special attention will also be given to people’s access to services under restrictions due to Covid19 outbreak, and online consultations and virtual meetings will be available to targeted beneficiaries to communicate their challenges and voice these needs/rights, which PARC will ensure sharing with relevant stakeholders.
