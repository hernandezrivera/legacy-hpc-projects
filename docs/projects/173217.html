Project Summary

This project is designed to improve equitable access to protective education for 12,884 emergency affected school school-age children in Khartoum Umbada and Algezira (almanagil-janub algezira) and river nile at almatama (6570 girls and 6314 boys); from the vulnerable residents and refugees.
The project will provide access to education for 12,884 children through learning spaces by rehabilitation of 17classrooms in school s for girls and boys (7 classrooms in algezira, 4 in umbada and 6 at almatama primary schools), and enhancing access to WASH facilities in these schools through construction of 25 semi-permanent gender sensitive school latrines (2 latrines each). Additionally, Sadagaat will distribute learning/teaching tool kits and supplies to 8,000 to targeted school-aged children and 300 teachers and conduct awareness raising campaign to boost school enrollment in all targeted localities.
The project will contribute in improving the quality of basic education through increasing the capacity of school teachers, PTA members, school head masters and social workers on teaching methodologies and core subjects, life skills in emergencies, EiE, psychosocial support and hygiene and health education by train 300 teachers and 300 PTAs members.There has been an increase of emphasis on community engagement to achieve sustainability goals. There is a focus on community participation. Yet much of the research looking at the success of community engagement in achieving sustainability outcomes is inconclusive.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan in general remains complex. Chronic under development and widespread poverty, exacerbated by various shocks, are also negatively impacting on vulnerable populations’ coping capacities. Accesses to basic services remain largely insufficient. According to Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey MICS 2014, the average of the overall school attendance in Sudan was very low. The situations of the disasters and economic affected states have lower access to education for both boys and girls in the country.The displaced and refugees moved people aged children in umbada are having difficulties in accessing existing and overcrowded learning facilities that ranges between 75 to 110 students per class with a high pupil to teacher ratio that can reach up to 54:1.The overcrowded condition in school in term of classroom, supplies, seating, and WASH facilities are resulting in an environment that is non-conducive for learning, unhygienic practice and increased drop-out of displaced children, particularly girls. . Girls are the most affected by such decision as culturally are deprived from education. This urge then needs of teachers training and PTAs capacity development to both improve the quality of education especially for English and Math and encourage child education especially for girls, he dropout rate, families are not able to pay the school fees; children are able to remain on school despite the fact that they often spend several hours day collecting firewood, fetching water and working in the fields.
