Project Summary

Emergency situations and exposure to adversity can lead to lifelong learning impairment, behavior, and physical and mental health The years 2020 and 2021 have been exceptionally challenging; Lebanon’s economic situation continues to severely worsen, spread of COVID-19 pandemic, prevalence of substantial stressors and lack in access to specialized services.
These crises had multiple and worsening impacts including increasing unemployment and underemployment, reducing access to and quality of basic services; increasing multidimensional poverty and inequality; and decrease in social stability. The most vulnerable groups, including children, are worse hit, particularly in the continued absence of a sustained social assistance programme to vulnerable households. Given this fragile context, the education in Lebanon has been also interrupted several times resulting significant increase in the number of out of school children; over 1.2 million school-aged children, with 400,000 children being left out of school. The situation will further exacerbate by the anticipated increase of 100,000 – 120,000 students transferring from private to already overburdened public schools puts the continuity of quality and inclusive education at risk. Aside from missing out on learning, children out of school are at a higher risk to fall victim to child labour, child marriage and other forms of abuse and exploitation. Children with disabilities, as well, are gravely impacted as they miss out on the developmental benefits of going to school and interacting with other children.
That said, urgent actions should be taken for addressing the emerging inequalities within the education space, as well as rethinking what makes quality education available to all children. Accordingly, RMF and FCA throughout this project, seek securing sustainable solutions to continuing education, especially for those living in low or no-connectivity areas and in turn leaving no one behind. This will be achieved through the pyramid model intervention where different types of support are needed and response is designed in a way that facilitates affected population’s efforts to retain their education, and thereby enabling them to bounce back to a sense of normalcy.
To address these dire needs and respond to the economic crisis and its impact on the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant households, RMF and FCA are planning to scale up their ongoing joint project in Beirut (Bourj Hammoud, Chiyeh and Sed El Bawchrieh) and in North Lebanon (Batroun, Koura, Bcharre, Minnieh-Dennieh and Tripoli) with an aim to ensure access to quality education for crisis-affected children in a safe and protective learning environment in schools estimated to have the least capacity to support transferring students from the private schools.
In line with the ERP priorities, the project focuses on the first specific objective through the provision of direct community support measures to most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children.
To achieve this objective, an inclusive education approach is designed including series of integrated activities targeting 2,500 children crisis-affected (school aged) Lebanese and migrant girls and boys, who are at risk of dropping out of schools; or who have suffered a learning loss in the last two years; or who have moved or will be moving from private to public schools and their caregivers. Activities to reach the objective include: Identifying, outreach, awareness raising and referrals on OOEC to basic services and education; provision of assistive technologies for most vulnerable children with disabilities; cash for education support to the most vulnerable families to enable access to education for their children; remote learning support through homeschooling support to parents/caregivers; PSS-SEL programs; GBV risk reduction in learning programs and raising awareness on violence and bullying; support with low cost remote learning solutions and food packages for learners.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

RMF & FCA focus on SO1. An inclusive integrated approach addressing the most vulnerable communities in terms of education, is the most effective solution to reach a high number of PoC. The only way to avoid increase in OOEC numbers is to conduct outreach & awareness raising in vulnerable communities targeting all cohorts of population building on existing structures. This approach enables partners identify people in need & refer them through the existing referral channels to the appropriate services. Due to the existing economic situation, most caregivers are de-prioritizing education in comparison to securing food & medicines. For this, cash for education & provision of devices to PwDs contribute to the removal of barriers preventing children from accessing education & preventing drop-outs. Undeniably there is prevalence of violence, abuse, neglect, bullying and other forms of violence in local communities. That said, partners will be supporting caregivers in being more responsive in caregiving through enhancing their transferable skills & digital literacy in addition to supporting them with low cost remote learning solution. Reducing suffering & enhancing one’s well-being is vital for the implementing partners, children as well as their caregivers will be participating in PSS/SEL activities & awareness raising activities. Finally, securing basic needs remain the core concern for all families for this partners will be delivering food packages to all targeted households.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Protection, gender equality and social inclusion are mainstreamed in partners’ response to meet the needs of women, men, girls and boys. Given the significant risk women and girls face in fragile communities; partners count on the community itself through building its capacity and increase its commitment and willingness to support in education retention and CP/GBV prevention and response services. Following that, community dialogue engaging children, parents/caregivers, key stakeholders, and municipalities is initiated for identifying risks and suggesting solutions to address rights violations in order to prevent and mitigate risks especially those related to early marriage and child labour. Partners’ team will ensure prevention and response to SEA and the potential for other forms of misconduct are fully considered and integrated in the response for example ensuring that distribution of food packages take place in daylight hours and placed in public points in the neighborhood.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Setting-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints is a key contributor for ensuring accountability for affected populations. A feedback/complaint age and gender appropriate mechanisms will be put in place to make sure that affected populations are able to reflect their feedback as well as report on any type of violence, neglect, or exploitation. That said, partners will be putting in place a combination of channels for collecting feedback and complaints; (1) active channels where affected populations are deliberately and systematically solicited (such as focus groups); (2) reactive channels, where affected populations have more control over when and how they share feedback/complaint (such as feedback/complaint box/hotline); and (3) open channels where feedback is solicited in an ad hoc manner, through ongoing interaction during the implementation of the activities.
