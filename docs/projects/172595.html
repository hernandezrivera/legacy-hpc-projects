Project Summary

Shelter and NFI assistance is required for Internally Displaced People (IDPs), returnees and hosting community in new or protracted displacement and in newly accessible locations. Pregnant and lactating women, chronically ill people, elderly, unaccompanied minors, and physically disabled are particularly vulnerable amongst all affected populations - in addition to NFIs, their shelter needs are particularly high for protection related issues (i.e. elements, dignity, accessibility) and for exposure to further health risks. South Kordofan and White Nile states are among the ones with highest concentration of IDPs and refugees across Sudan. The displaced populations face complex protection risks, including issues related to land rights that often prevents them from constructing more permanent shelters and achieving durable solutions.
Overcrowded shelters and congested camps remain a serious concern, with all the sites currently hosting refugee populations beyond the initial capacity. In such context, ensuring that adequate space and basic services are available to absorb new arrivals, while sustaining sufficient services provision to the existing caseloads, especially in camps, remains a challenge. This is particularly problematic in camps hosting South Sudanese in White Nile were dynamics around land rights with private landowners and host communities are complex. Vast majority of the existing shelters need rehabilitation/repairs to ensure minimum protection against the elements. New shelters are needed in those settlements/camps accommodating newly displaced in the existing shelters. Replenishment and new Essential NFI kits are needed for the most vulnerable groups. Communal infrastructures such as community centers facilitating cohesion and social integration is highly demanded and needed by target communities. The need for adequate lighting in refugee camps and informal settlements, such as solar lanterns and streetlights, is an important factor that supports the protection and physical safety of vulnerable refugees. Environmental and energy related issues are as well paramount.
The project includes three pillars:
1. Emergency and Hard-to-Reach (H2R) Response, w NRC will address acute humanitarian needs and enhance protection through the provision of timely assistance to vulnerable, people affected by displacement, including IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities, with focus on under-served and hard to-reach areas. Household-level emergency Shelter and essential NFI assistance
2. Facilitating Durable Solutions NRC will strengthen the resilience of vulnerable households through the provision of transitional/permanent shelters. Interventions will support the realisation of the right to adequate housing through an integrated package of Shelter, WASH and HLP assistance to households. The Shelter component of this result will focus on household-level light rehabilitation works to ensure climatic protection, safety, security, privacy and dignity. Through synergy with the ICLA programme, NRC will strengthen security of tenure and improve access to services.
3. Community-level Support. NRC will strengthen the resilience of vulnerable communities through the provision of transitional or permanent upgrades/repair of basic and social infrastructure improving social cohesion among displaced and hosting population. NRC Shelter programme will support communal infrastructures like youth centers and both formal and informal education infrastructures for children and youth in synergy with the Education programme. In addition, NRC will support communities through the upgrade/repair of productive assets that support recovery and access to livelihoods (e.g. marketplaces).

1 - Needs

Except for the recent Ethiopian refugee crisis in Eastern Sudan, new displacement across the country reduced in past years - mainly, people continued to be displaced due to localized armed clashes and intercommunal violence, as well as hazards such as floods and disease outbreaks.Localized tribal conflicts are still occurring in South Kordofan, causing displacement to safer areas. The situation is further exacerbated by the ongoing macro-economic challenges, with hyperinflation leading to shortages of fuel and purchasing power loss that, combined with COVID19 implications, has determined in the second half of 2020 a spike in prices of key commodities and medicines.IDPs and refugees need shelter and essential NFIs. These items help restore dignity and protection against the elements, mitigating health risks and providing enhanced privacy and security to those in need. Displaced returning to areas of origin, resettling in stable places or integrating within host communities, are also in need of shelter and NFI assistance. Since the items provided are not durable, annual replenishment of key items to the most vulnerable is required.NRC team has confirmed those needs in Khartoum Open Areas, White Nile, South Kordofan and Gedaref states conducting multisector RNAs. The lack of durable solutions for displaced populations is a key challenge. This is due to the lack of sustainable funding for early recovery activities, lack of basic services and lack of land tenure which is often a source of conflict and tension among populations.In NRC target locations, despite the humanitarian support, the vast majority of the shelters are in very poor conditions, with heavy damages in roofing and walling unable to meet the minimum sectoral standards. On the environmental and energy sub-components, needs are as well significant. Fuel for cooking is a major issue - women are in charge to collect firewood from the camp/settlement surroundings. Protection incidents are often experienced by women and girls, including stealing, beating and sexual abuses. The latter in particular generate serious problems to the victims, not only in relation to the physical and psychological damage, but also at cultural level in terms of acceptance and stigma. Majority of IDP/refugee sites don't have electrical/lighting system. Generally, private generators are used in the market area, providing electricity service to the population. The most needed NFIs include bedding items (sleeping mats, blankets), cooking materials, light sources, cooking gas stoves, floor mats. Communal clubs, with access to basic amenities (TV, radios, etc.) are requested by the community as priority infrastructure, as way to socialize and connect/receive information and news from outside the area.As reported by all IDP groups interviewed and observed walking through the settlements, shelter conditions are extremely poor and basic NFIs are lacking in all assessed sites. Plastic sheets, jerry cans, blankets, sleeping mats, solar lamps and kitchen sets are the most needed items. Basic commodities are available in the nearby markets; however, they are too expensive for the low-income capacity of the IDPs. These substandard conditions affect primarily elderly, women HoH and disabled.In White Nile camps, due to the continued influx of refugees, all camps and shelters are overcrowded (up to 20 persons/shelter), with inadequate distance between shelters and insufficient privacy and physical protection.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Output 1. Emergency and Hard-to-Reach (H2R) ResponseNRC will address acute humanitarian needs and enhance protection through the provision of timely assistance to vulnerable, displaced conflict affected populations. It will focus on under-served and hard-to-reach areas. Household-level emergency Shelter and essential NFI assistance will be delivered as part of an integrated multi-sector package of assistance in close coordination with humanitarian partners (including WASH and Food Security components). The Shelter component of the emergency response will include the provision repair/sealing-off kits (shelter kits) and NFIs. Assistance will be delivered through the most appropriate transfer modality – preferably cash/voucher modalities - tailored to needs, preferences and market conditions. Output 2- Facilitating Durable SolutionsNRC will strengthen the resilience of vulnerable households through the provision of transitional/permanent shelters. Interventions will support the realisation of the right to adequate housing through an integrated package of Shelter, WASH and HLP assistance to households. The Shelter component of this result will focus on household-level light rehabilitation works to ensure climatic protection, safety, security, privacy and dignity. Through synergy with the ICLA programme, NRC will strengthen security of tenure and improve access to services. This result will be delivered as a beneficiary-led package of assistance. As such, NRC will explore and pilot self-build through conditional-cash assistance, where context allows, or contractor led processes as secondary option. Where the housing unit is occupied by a tenant, the upgrades will be implemented in exchange for a rental reduction. Where relevant, this result will link with Community-level Support (see below) and support improved connection to community-level servicesOutput 3 - Community-level Support NRC will strengthen the resilience of vulnerable communities through the provision of transitional or permanent upgrades/repair of basic and social infrastructure improving social cohesion among displaced and hosting population. NRC Shelter programme will support communal infrastructures like youth centers and both formal and informal education infrastructures for children and youth in synergy with the Education programme. In addition, NRC will support communities through the upgrade/repair of productive assets that support recovery and access to livelihoods (e.g. marketplaces). This will be delivered in synergy with other NRC programming (e.g. Food Security).All activities will follow sector program cycle, considering Age, Gender and People with disabilities consideration and robust monitoring and evaluation system in place along all activities.
