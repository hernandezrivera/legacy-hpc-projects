Project Summary

The project focuses on continuous effort to contribute to the reduction of suffering and life loss due to WASH-related diseases through consolidating efforts to improved access to adequate safe water, sanitation services and public health promotion, support to local WASH agencies in knowledge transfer and to establish conditions that allow Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar to live with good health, dignity and better safety.

Objectives

1. To ensure regular, sufficient, equitable and dignified access for all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities to sufficient and safe water for drinking and domestic needs. 2. To ensure all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities have adequate, appropriate and acceptable toilets to allow rapid, safe and secure access at all times.3. To ensure the change of potentially dangerous behaviors through access and exposure to innovative hygiene promotion (including hygiene items) for all refugees living in camps and affected host communities. 4. To ensure local capacity developed at the WASH sector and community level and platforms are created for knowledge sharing and skills development

Needs

Improved Water Services: After two years in the response only 19% of the refugees in the camps have access to chlorinated drinking water from piped water supply network; the remaining 81% of the refugee population in the camps still use water from shallow and deep tube wells with high possibility of contamination during and after collection of water. The challenge is even more in the Teknaf camps during the dry season, due to long waiting times, where 54% of households reported taking more than 30 minutes to collect water and in some most cases not sufficient to cover the daily minimum as per the sector standards.Access to Sanitation: Despite 99% of refugees reporting using latrines in the camps; 59% of females in the refugee camps express “as not feeling safe” accessing the sanitation facilities due to location and appropriateness of design. In addition: the fecal sludge and solid waste management is a part of the major challenges in the camps that bring significant public health risks both for the refugees and host communities. Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: appropriate hygiene practices for specific risk behaviors remain low with, for example, handwashing after handling children’s faeces at 15%, and appropriate safe water handling at household level where contamination of stored water is reported at over 80%. (Oxfam internal study). The recent “WASH Sector Operational Partners map – 20th of August 2019” indicates that out of 19 local and national NGOs active in WASH response in the camps and nearby host communities only 4 are taking the leadership role as camp Focal agencies representing 13 camps out of total 34 camps of which two organizations cover 85% of the 13 camps, which inadequate representation of local actors have created a gap in the localized leadership and coordination role in the WASH sector. In addition, at Cox’s WASH sector level there is only one local agency in the coordination and technical (TWiG) leadership role out of 8 chair and co-chair roles existed in the WASH sector and subsector structure.

Activities or outputs

1. Consolidate pipeline water supply system camp 22, all design of water points and locations will be based on community consultation including women, men, adolescence girls, elderly and people with special needs groups2. Construction and upgrading of sanitation facilities using Social architecture approach at camps and host community, all design and location of sanitation facilities are based on the agreement with women, men, adolescence girls, children, elderly and people with special needs groups following social architecture approach3. Installation of intermediate sludge transfer tanks and pipeline 4. Public health promotion and community engagement with women, men, adolescence girls, children and elderly groups for behavior change at camps and host community 5. Provide training for 25 women and 25 men WASH experts from local government agencies and national NGOs.

Key project/performance indicators

1. % of women and girls from the total targeted beneficiaries report that they have equal access to clean drinking water and sanitation facilities 2. % of female from the refugees reporting improved safety and comfort accessing WASH facilities3. % of households from the total targeted beneficiaries with the improved practice of safe hygiene and sanitation4. % of targeted communities develop and implement community action plans for environmental health and hygiene management5. % of the WASH experts that participated in training for WASH capacity development who are female
