Project Summary

The project will provide protection assistance in regions of Dana including widows’ camps, through CP services through CFSs targeting and mobile teams (Women: 4000, Girls: 8000, Boy: 10000, Men: 3000).
There is a huge need for protection services, especially CP in the targeted areas, and till now a big gap in the coverage of the CFS due to many reasons related to funds and lack of capacities, and most of the established centers are suffering from the cutting in the fund,
MF will target Atma, Aqrabat, Qah, Deir Hassan, and Salwa and expand to provide CP services through mobile teams and CFSs.
This project will benefit these high-priority areas by strengthening and enhancing child protection (CP) services,
MF will consider Do No harm principles and guidelines and match with the widows' camps context to prevent any further harm. In light of the spread of COVID-19, MF during implementation will take into consideration all prevention procedures, guidelines of COVID-19
The project will be implemented in stages:
• coordinating with the camp administration to secure a place for the project activities that consider the confidentiality & safety of the activities.
• MF will ensure the participation of all relevant stakeholders at all stages of the operations management cycle.
• An elected committee of BNFs including PWDs & elderly women (in each camp), is going to participate in decision-making to determine the identity of the place, the type of activities.
• MF will select project staff to match the BNFs types considering the culture and situation on the ground and try to balance between males and female staff as much as possible. mobile teams in each location will be implementing the project’s activities, each team consists of a case manager (CP) who will provide CM both in-person and remotely, a PSS worker and female facilitator for each camp, and another male facilitator will be targeting men & boys as well, and local authorities.
• Then Conducting training for MF field staff who are working in this project on (PFA), CP, referrals pathways, and case management
• Establish friendly spaces for boys and girls taking into consideration gender needs and capacities, If no secured place, MF will rent a tent as a CFS in camps.
• Securing safe spaces for interviews, intake, and case management for extremely vulnerable boys and girls in targeted camps
• Enhanced awareness and organize recreational activities in friendly spaces, ensuring that they are age-appropriate and accessible for beneficiaries
• parents will learn how to deal with children during crisis through a parenting program
• All project activities will be adapted to match gender guidelines including (PWD, Men, women, girls, boy, and elders)
• For boys, girls, women, elders, and people with disabilities through mobile teams,
• the community will be targeted through capacity-building activities, including humanitarian workers within different sectors to ensure integration of protection considerations and better overall quality and integration of services provided within safe areas. MF will be responsible for the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation (M&E), and closure phases. MF will manage the project, provide the contractual services, ensure the required coordination among clusters, and ensure high-quality results and efficient progress through the provision of capable programmatic specialized

4 - Needs

Grave violations against children remain a significant concern including the risk of being killed, injured, recruited, used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted, and sexually abused. Girls and boys continue to experience persisting violence including physical, emotional, sexual, and domestic violence. Insecurity, economic hardship, and Covid-19 exacerbate child protection concerns. These fueled harmful coping mechanisms, and increased child labor and child marriage, putting adolescent girls at particular risk. Child Protection issues are spread across the region at generally increased levels, based on the CHILD PROTECTION SITUATION MONITORING, at Aldana 1.1 % of KIs reported children in need of focused non-specialized services, 1.2 % of KIs reported children in need of specialized services, 1.3 % of KIs reported parents (or other caregivers) in need of specialized services, 2.1 % of KIs reported children engaged in the type of work that prevents them from going to school, 2.3 % of KIs that reported children living in households with persons with specific needs engaged in child labor, 3.1 % of KIs reported children being separated from their usual caregivers, 3.4 % of KIs reported newly established institutions for children without their usual caregivers, Numerous unaccompanied and separated children or children living with older caregivers, or living in camps are at increased risk of exposure to violence

5 - Implementation of activities

MF will establish CFS and the mobile teams at Al-Dana region, then hire the staff taking into consideration the gender balance, there will be male and female facilitators who consider the context and gender differences between children Also, we will conduct awareness sessions that will focus on mitigating the risks of child labor and encouraging them to return to school, also targeting caregivers through awareness sessions parenting skills. This project intends to incorporate protection principles by supporting vulnerable people including women girls, boys, and men through CP, raising awareness, and PSS sessions. This project will promote girls and boys to participate in the CFS and provide them with opportunities to safely talk about CP concerns. MF will enhance the ability of CP staff to detect and support specific vulnerabilities amongst girls and boys through comprehensive case management and referrals, in addition to the reporting process with the CP sub-cluster (4Ws, CM reporting tool). Then it will provide training on core CP and some related topics to the local community and form social networks in local authorities to respond to any child protection concerns. MF is keen to grant a quality assurance in accordance with the CPMS while implementing this project through continuous coordination with the M&E department and a feedback system with a complaints mechanism will be activated. M&E team will meet every two weeks to review project progress and learning.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Preventing women, girls, and boys from reaching centers to receive CP services within the center Possibility: Medium Impact: high Mitigation Strategy: Mitigation Measures: Ensuring the provision of CP services for males and family members of the families and couples during the implementation of the project activities and in particular, CP awareness activities in order to mitigate and reduce the risk of preventing the access of beneﬁciaries to CP services Risk: Lack of acceptance of society and the forces controlling the content of the CP activities provided they contain highly sensitive topics Possibility: High Impact: High Mitigation Strategy: The beneﬁciaries and local staff will be consulted about the possibility of implementing the sessions of the CP program and work on structuring the sessions in a contextual and speciﬁc manner when there is a need to address the CP topics indirectly and depends on the resources within the group and supports activities. Risk: Category of beneﬁciaries Risk: Disability of persons with disabilities, older persons, and areas away from CFS. Possibility: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: Mobile teams will be allocated to reach beneﬁciaries who cannot access the CFS to participate in CP activities for people with severe disabilities and elderly people with mobility diﬃculties in remote areas. Beneﬁciaries will be facilitated by providing transportation as needed to ensure their effective participation


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: the populations will be hesitant or unwilling to engage on this topic. Possible increased reporting of SEA with increased awareness about beneficiary rights and reporting mechanisms. Risk: the potential for the very difficult working environment in integrating the SOPs and best practices of PSEA due to the sensitive nature of the topic in the culture, and/or other risks include facing increased reporting of SEA without adequate capacity to respond. Maram foundation team will be conducted PSEA Awareness-raising and information-sharing efforts and scaled up where possible, by respective PSEA focal points supported by the PSEA Networks program and by Maram foundation with the presence inside Syria to communicate information on PSEA. At the least, The following are standards for the affected communities will be informed on: I. The definition of sexual exploitation and abuse. II. The standards of conduct for humanitarian workers. III. Humanitarian workers have a right to humanitarian assistance without being subjected to SEA IV. Where to report SEA incidents (all possible options/channels). V. What services are available, including complaint confidentiality assessments, and how to access them. VI. What to expect after making a complaint, including potential referrals, timeframes, and the roles, responsibilities, and any limitations of actors involved. VII. What steps the Maram and the concerned agency will take to ensure safety and confidentiality.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project areas have been relatively safe and far from clashes, therefore they are a destination for many IDPs. MF safety and security field team are already and will continue to be monitoring the security situation CP project areas daily. In addition, MF teams in Syria are aware of safety controls during emergencies and familiar with project areas, as we implemented former projects in the Dana sub-district. MF will take all precautionary measures to avoid exposing our teams and project beneficiaries, especially the children to danger. Key roles involved in this include the Safety and Security Manager (SSM) who is responsible for approval of the security plan, leads and participates in the risk management process, and takes ownership of risk mitigation/contingency planning and execution. Regarding the security within the targeted camps, MF staff has to send their movement plan in advance, which will be approved by the safety and security department in case of any event that triggers the contingency plan. project stakeholders assist in monitoring risk action effectiveness and participate in risk management, as necessary. MF will ensure best practices in Keeping child safeguarding, Children's Safe standards as well as better accountability to those who are using or beneﬁting from the services of an organization. Each standard outlines the key elements that should be put in place to keep children safe and lists the requirements needed to meet these standards.
