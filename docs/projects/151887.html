Project Summary

The Somalia Humanitarian Fund (SHF) is a multi-donor country-based pooled fund that was established in 2010 to support the timely allocation and disbursement of donor resources to address the most urgent humanitarian needs in Somalia. The SHF has been essential in enabling timely, coordinated and effective humanitarian action due to its distinct comparative advantages – the unearmarked nature of the Fund; the established and functioning accountability systems; integration within the existing coordination systems; and flexibility.

Needs

Despite improvements in food security in the 2016-2017 drought-affected areas, high levels of vulnerability persist in Somalia that require sustained levels of assistance. Improvements were largely due to an above average Gu rainy season and large-scale humanitarian assistance that led to a reduction in the number of people in need to an estimated 4.6 million, including 1.5 million reported to be in Emergency and Crisis. There have been marginal improvements in the nutrition status of children aged below five with an estimated 294,000 acutely malnourished, 55,000 of them severely. Urgent support is required to holistically address the contributory factors to the persistently high levels of malnutrition. Current levels of assistance are not enough to meet the costs of ongoing humanitarian response. The challenging operational environment characterized by forced evictions, restricted humanitarian access, and ongoing conflict also continues to inhibit effective service delivery. Communities of concern include the 2.7 million internally displaced persons (IDP) in Somalia who constitute 58 per cent of the food insecure and face the gravest protection violations, and poor urban and rural households who have not recovered from the impact of the 2016/2017 drought. Children are particularly vulnerable - making up 1.85 million of those currently food insecure, and numbering one million out of the 2.6 million IDPs. With only 1.5 million out of 4.5 million school aged children in protective school environments, they risk forced recruitment by armed groups and other child related violations. The assurance of a protective environment for IDPs, children and the marginalized is hindered by persistently poor funding across its complementary clusters of camp coordination and management, education, protection and shelter.

Activities

1. Advocate for system wide resource mobilization: Conduct regular bilateral and multilateral donor briefings and outreach to ensure that the HRP and SHF are well resourced2. Produce and disseminate appropriate information products providing updates on analysis of humanitarian financing for Somalia for decision making3. Increase donor confidence through effective fund management including implementation of SHF accountability framework for effective risk management4. Provide guidance and support to the Humanitarian Coordinator to ensure the strategic and efficient use of the fund in line with allocation principles taking into consideration complementary donor and CERF funding. 5. Make pooled funding decisions in consultation with the Advisory Board, ICCG and HCT to support strategic response to high priority humanitarian needs and in alignment with HRP 6. Strategic management of SHF reserve to ensure t is responsive to emergencies 5. Support localization in humanitarian response through progressive increments in fund allocations to local NGOs6. Provide of guidance and support to partners and clusters throughout all segments of the allocation cycle – strategic prioritization, project selection, disbursements, implementation of activities and accountability measures, project closure – in close collaboration with all relevant stakeholders.
