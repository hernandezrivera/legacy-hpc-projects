Project Summary

Aligned with the Syria HRP 2021 objectives and according to the Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme (HNAP), the proposed project aims to provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people affected by the escalation of violence in southern Idleb and western countryside of Aleppo, Most IDPs (in camps, informal settlements and among host community members in villages) continue to face critical WaSH needs and their Shelter locations lack water tanks and sustainable solid waste solutions (garbage bins, sewage networks). This is particularly problematic in light of the COVID pandemic. Findings from the CCCM Cluster confirm that, despite the humanitarian presence, WaSH needs are still not fully covered in Azaz. In fact, according to Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme (HNAP) report of Dec 2020, 40 % of IDPs in NWS report that water is their first priority. In terms of infrastructure, at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated, while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional and 26% of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities (HNO 2020), with 64% of water stations not functional, according to the Syrian Water Resources Platform.
SI addresses identified needs with a dual approach:
1. Provision of comprehensive WaSH package for IDPs in Mare, Aza, Suran and Akhtarin camps and Informal Settlements
• Daily supply of safe, sufficient and treated water in the form of water points for an average of 35 Litter per person per day through water trucking and pumping (running cost). Water distribution and quality monitoring will be implemented in the full respect of the WASH cluster technical recommendations;
• Procurement, distribution and installation of water storage containers for water domestic use and hygiene behaviors
• Solid waste collection and management through provision of adequate solid containers
• Life-saving hygiene promotion awareness will be ensured within and beyond recently displaced people to prevent water and vector borne diseases.
• Distribution of 3000 comprehensive Hygiene-COVID kits in alliance with COVID response for 3,000 HHs (15,442 individuals)
• Water network rehabilitation in Suran village.
2. Shelter rehabilitation and gravelling
• SI will rehabilitate 100 partially damaged shelters based on a technical assessment in all of the locations where we provide WaSH services. This work consists of gravelling works for vulnerable damaged shelters, totaling an average of 350 USD.
• Gravelling works for roads, Specifically, the activity the gravelling of the roads in Azaz area, covering 1,463 beneficiaries in the targeted locations. Please refer to the design
Targeting will be based on needs analysis, inter-agency coordination, and SI ability to maintain humanitarian safe and principled access. Beneficiary selection criteria for individual activities will vary, but always be based on needs and vulnerabilities. Gender equality will be prioritized and beneficiary engagement throughout the project cycle will ensure transparency and awareness of project activities.

4 - Needs

Most IDPs continue to face critical WaSH needs and their Shelter locations lack water tanks and sustainable solid waste solutions. This is particularly problematic in light of the COVID pandemic. Findings from the CCCM Cluster confirm that, despite the humanitarian presence, WaSH needs are still not fully covered in Azaz. In fact, according to Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme (HNAP) report of Dec 2020, 40 % of IDPs in NWS report that water is their first priority. In terms of infrastructure, at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated, while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional and 26% of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities (HNO 2020), with 64% of water stations not functional, according to the Syrian Water Resources Platform.Based on the latest available information gathered through SI’s registration team in January 2021, there are approximately 79,667 residents, 11,763 HHs (around 40,166 females, 39,501 males in 30 ISs, camps and villages in Azaz area (Annex 06). The population is expected to increase due to the current escalation in conflict in Northwest Syria and further deteriorating of the security context. In late 2020, SI conducted focus group discussions with key stakeholders at the targeted communities. A key finding was that many camp residents are still in need of WaSH services, water tanks, garbage bins to provide a healthy environment in the camps, amongst other needs. Furthermore, recent floods in December 2020 and January 2021 affected many camps, ISs in Azaz causing flooding and damage of tents, caravans, soil erosion and mudslides, highlighting the need for site improvement activities including gravelling for the main roads and shelter rehabilitation.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks include:• Inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across locations• Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees due to varied assistance packages• Abuse of power by staff distributing goods or delivering services to vulnerable populations. • Road accidents, physical harm• Restricted access (due to insecurity of COVID-19 lockdowns)• COVID-19 public health concerns Mitigation measures for the identified risks are integrated in SI’s implementation strategy, starting with transparent selection criteria agreed upon in coordination with the relevant clusters. The risk of abuse of power is mitigated by thorough vetting of locally recruited staff, who all abide by the SI Code of Conduct. SI also has a Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM) in place that allows for real-time feedback from beneficiaries and other stakeholders. The FCRM is an accountability tool that simultaneously facilitates the collection of lessons learnt that will be used to improve future interventions.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access to vulnerable population and vendors is not further reduced by additional border-crossings restrictions;• Operations are not further limited by regulatory restrictions inside Syria;• Safety and security of SI and partner staff is not compromised • SI is able to appropriately manage the risks of aid diversion to designated terrorist groups or through corruption and bribery;• Exchange rates and material/service costs remain reasonably stable throughout implementation period of the project; • Sufficient materials and skills required for the output are available in the targeted geographic areas. The assumption, based on past experiences and rapid markets surveys is that markets are functional and quality material is available• Construction companies with sufficient capacity are available inside Syria.• Labour with sufficient technical skills is available inside Syria.• Security situation allows regular sharing of information between stakeholders • Communication channels remain open and enable relevant monitoring methods, as appropriate.• Payment mechanisms remain open for the reimbursement of suppliers, SI relies on money transfer system (hawala) for its operations in North Syria including for the payment of its staff and contractors. The assumption is that this system will continue to work throughout the project with acceptable fees.• Inter-agency coordination mechanisms minimize the risk of duplication and variations in levels of assistance.Cooperation with Turkish authorities and capacity to work Cross-Border from Turkey: Whilst all activities for this program will be implemented in Syria, the SI mission maintains a representation in Gaziantep in Turkey with its main coordination office in Amman, Jordan
