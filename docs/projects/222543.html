Project Summary

In acknowledgement of the effect of the disease burden as a result of consumption of unsafe water and poor behaviour related to sanitation and hygiene, CIDO in this 2025 Response Plan, proposes to step up and necessitate equitable access to safe, clean drinking-water, basic sanitation and promote hygiene while enhancing survival of the affected population to address morbidity and mortality related to WASH through integration of WASH intervention with FSL, Protection, NFI, Health and Nutrition. The response targets 11,625 (Akobo-7768 and Twic – 3857) persons with much needed WASH services in line with the following WASH specific objectives for the 2025. The locations targeted falls under Priority 1 and 2 as established by the cluster targeting guidance running 2025. This response attracts a budget of USD 476,625 to support 2 counties within a period of one year. During the period, CIDO expects to address SAM cases of children and their care takers as well as Pregnant and lactating women through nutrition facilities with WASH services (WASH Kits) to reduce disease transmission at household level. The Nutrition facilities such as OTP sites, TSFP and SC including health facilities will be reached with basic water and sanitation package to reduce risk of HAIs transmission due to poor WASH conditions. In addition, communities showing high malnutrition prevalence combined with limited access to safe water will be targeted with lasting solutions for water supply and hygiene. Throughout the response, CIDO will as well mainstream protection and mitigate WASH Gender Based Violence to guarantee that beneficiaries of WASH services, in particular women and children access facilities in a safe manner. In some specific contexts, CIDO will also ensure that water points are not a trigger for conflict by ensuring that water user management committees are well trained to discharge their mandate without biasness. CIDO will exploit its vast experience in implementation of multisector project including Health, Nutrition, WASH, NFI, Protection, and FSL to enhance integration by providing inter sectoral aid for the most vulnerable population with WASH services. Realizing the cross-cutting nature of WASH, CIDO will combine the described activities with other sectors by providing dignity kits and NFIs as a mitigation to improve protection status of women and girls. While improving access to water, the response will take care of irrigation needs for farmers and water for livestock, which contributes to improvement of food production and contain elements of diseases prevention as well as improving sanitation and water access in nutrition sites, health centers and schools

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire, characterized by a protracted humanitarian crisis, widespread displacement, food insecurity, and limited access to basic services, including healthcare (United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs [OCHA], 2023). The country has experienced multiple emergencies, including disease outbreaks, floods, and conflict-related displacement. In 2024, an estimated 9 million people need humanitarian assistance. The Sudan crisis, Influx of returnees as a result of suspension of humanitarian aid in Gambella Refugee camps in Ethiopia, Intercomunal violence, food insecurity, and the severe impact of climate change present enormous challenges. Disease burden caused by unsafe water, sanitation, and hygiene. The drivers of WASH outcomes and needs cut across various sectors including food, nutrition, shelter, protection and health. Generally, poor infrastructure including road network, and loss of agricultural livelihoods due to flooding, drought, and sub-national conflict translates to inability of households to meet their WASH and related intersectoral needs and therefore rely on humanitarian assistance. The destruction of WASH facilities, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities and limited access to clean drinking water contribute to poor health conditions with communities at risk of water-borne diseases such diarrheal diseases, cholera and Hepatitis E. combined with the presence of flood waters vector-borne diseases such as malaria, lymphatic filariasis, schistosomiasis with likelihood is to increase.According to the IPC Acute food insecurity and malnutrition analysis September 2024 - July 2025, from July to September 2024, 10 out of 11 counties, Akobo and Twic counties being among them are classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical). Major drivers are poor WASH practices which include poor access to improved drinking water (<80 percent in 9 counties), poor access to improved sanitation facilities (<20% in 10 counties) and high prevalence of open defecation (>60%). Adolescent school girls are likely to be inconsistent in school attendance or drop out due to lack of menstrual management supplies. This increases their vulnerability and puts them at risk of early marriages (Protection Cluster – UNHCR, 2019). Whereas, the 2024 Intercluster Severity Scales for Humanitarian Needs Overview indicates the vast majority of the country (67 out of 78 counties) under extreme severity apparently CIDO target locations included. This is attributed to endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. Protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. Despite humanitarian support witnessed in 2024, still very minimal improvement or nil has been recorded in targeted counties. In 2025, the precarious situation is expected to be further exacerbated by the country’s increasing humanitarian caseload, the expected increase in the duration of needs in the most affected areas and the projected further reduction in humanitarian assistance across the country. The current economic crisis is impacting people’s ability to access markets, while conflict, flooding and seasonal effects are limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and service as such, and there is an urgent need to address WASH gaps which would optimally reduce WASH related morbidity & mortality
