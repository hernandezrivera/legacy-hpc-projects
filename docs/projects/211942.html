Project Summary

Over 14.9 million people in Syria are estimated to be in need of life-saving primary and secondary care health assistance in 2024(Health Sector HNO 2024), especially after the earthquake that hit Syria in last Feb, which led to increase the burden on vulnerable groups.
It was reported in 2023 HNO that assistive devices are key needs for communities. Due to expensive costs or their unavailability in local markets, it became a major barrier to assisting survivors of explosive ordnance incidents and those with disabilities.
The HELP project will respond to the needs of 400 PWDs in Deir AL Zor, Lattakia and Aleppo by providing assistive devices to this most vulnerable group of people during 2024, under objective 1, activity 1.1, indicator: 1.1.4 of Sector logframe.
The above-mentioned governorates have high number PIN and severity scales (3 and above) according to Health Sector severity and PiN 2024 where services, resources and assistance are limited, especially for Aleppo and Lattakia after the Earthquake.
ADRA will prioritize areas which include largest number of children under five years, women of reproductive age, older persons, displaced persons –including refugees, IDPs and returnees, PWDs and chronic diseases, and sub-districts with highest health severity scale
The activity will be advertised through local partners such as SARC and local charities inviting the PWDs to apply for the required device to be included in the beneficiary list. ADRA will review any existing lists of PWDs that can be obtained from the local partners. Once the procurement is complete, devices will be delivered to each beneficiary’s house with basic instruction on the use of the device provided during the delivery. For devices such as hearing aids, the beneficiary will be connected with local clinic for support with setting up and adjusting the device
Protection risk and Mitigation measure
Violence against Health Facilities/providers: ADRA will assure the safety of intervene areas and report any attacks through the Surveillance System for Attacks (SSA)
Tensions between beneficiaries and/or community members : ADRA will work closely with local partners to ensure that the planned interventions are culturally appropriate and harmonized with other forms of assistance to ‘do no harm’ and minimize the risks to beneficiaries
Sensitive data is leaked to unauthorized sources : ADRA has a data protection policy that will be implemented to ensure the protection of sensitive beneficiary information.
An increase in GBV if the abuser finds out about the reported cases : ADRA will ensure that the staff are aware of the risk and know the safe referral pathways in the area, and also trained on communication with persons with diverse disabilities.
Risk/ assumption, mitigation
1 Delays in obtaining the necessary approvals to implement activities in a timely manner from relevant authorities. ADRA will maintain strong connections with all stakeholders and follow-up on necessary approvals.
2 Changes in the security situation status in Syria, which might impact ADRA, partners and the community particularly in project locations Regular monitoring of the security situation with UNDSS, INGOs, sectors, stakeholders and staff. ADRA has an established and proven safety and security plan which is shared with all staff.
3 Extended delays in fund transfers to Syria or devaluation of SYP may place project activities outside the planned budget. Closely monitor exchange rates, use competitive tender processes. ADRA has opened a variety of different channels to money transfer funds into Syria.
4 Qualified staff may not be available Staff are recruited through a clear and transparent recruitment process, and provided with the necessary training including on PSEA.
5 The procurement of supplies could be subject to corruption or conflict of interest. ADRA Syria has developed and follows a clear procurement policy to help prevent fraud, corruption, and conflict of interest.
