Project Summary

WFP’s programme focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with focus on the first 1000days of life. The Programme targets all children aged 6-23 months and pregnant and lactating women in vulnerable households also targeted for general food assistance.
key project activities include distribution os specialized nutritious food, screening and referral of cases of acute malnutrition to relevant treatment programmes , social behavior change communication on infant and young child feeding practices. WFP ensures that its programmes take into account the needs of men, women, girls and boys, as well as people with special needs. Distribution points are located in locations that do not pose a particular risk to women's access. Practical procedures are taken to reduce queues at distribution points, and to give priority to pregnant women, people with disabilities and the elderly. WFP’s programmes are designed and implemented with due consideration of population protection concerns or barriers for people with specific needs for safe and dignified access to Nutrition services.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The consequences of the various humanitarian crises in Cameroon are aggravating factors for the deterioration of the already fragile nutritional status of affected populations. Indeed, the prevalence of stunting in children under 5 is above the WHO serious threshold of 30% in all regions affected by humanitarian crises and rates of acute malnutrition do not show significant changes for several years and remain on the poor thresholds. Children under the age of 5 with acute malnutrition are more likely to die than their healthy peers. Pregnant and lactating women are also particularly affected because they have limited access to adequate quality and quantity of food in a context of limited access to basic social services. In a context of massive displacement of people (internally displaced persons or refugees), women and young children are particularly exposed to poor living conditions and the accumulation of trauma that can affect practices feeding infants and young children, in particular the ability of mothers to ensure optimal breastfeeding, further weakening the nutritional status of the already most vulnerable populations. The level of acute malnutrition in the North West and southwest regions are not well known as access constraints have hampered the realization of a formal nutrition survey. Nutrition indicators has been integrated into emergency food security assessment (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 and revealed a proxy Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates of5.1% and 7.4% for the NW and SW regions respectively. The nutrition situation is likely to deteriorate in the coming months since acute malnutrition in children aged 6-59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in the nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, health, and WASH situations among others considering access challenges and the very low coverage of emergency nutrition response activities in 2021. WFP’s programme focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with focus on the first 1000 days of life. The programme targets all children aged 6-23 months and pregnant and lactating women and girls in a food-insecure household with a package of activities including, distribution of specialized nutritious food, screening and referral of malnourished cases to relevant programmes for treatment and social behavior change communication in favor of appropriate IYCF practices in emergency. WFP in 2022 intends to target about 27300 beneficiaries in the NWSW regions
