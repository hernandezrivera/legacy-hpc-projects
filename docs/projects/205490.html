Project Summary

The project has been designed to address the deteriorating food security in the project locations. The proposed locations include the 12 Counties of Uror, Twic East, Duk, Pochalla,Aweil East, Aweil South, Aweil Centre, Mayendit, Rubkona,Kajo Keji, Yei, Lainya of the 5 States of Jonglei, Unity, Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) and Central Equatoria, Northern Bahr el Ghazal respectively. According to the IPC for South Sudan, 2023 the high levels of acute food insecurity in South Sudan remain worrying, with the most severely acute food insecure populations significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance. The latest data shows that 5.83 million people (46 percent of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), with 1.64 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). An estimated 35,000 people are classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in the Duk (3,000) and Nyirol (3,000) counties of Jonglei State; and the Rubkona County (15,000) of Unity State. An estimated 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).
The project has been designed to address the deteriorating food security in the project locations. The proposed intervention will achieve the HRP Strategic and Cluster objectives in line with the sector objectives: SO1: Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe, and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs,SO3: Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand the impact of shock is increased, and nexus opportunities are optimized. CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in IPC 4 and 5, CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock, CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. This will be done through the provision of lifesaving assistance to conflict affected communities by providing agricultural inputs and providing training to vulnerable households to increase food production through good agricultural and fisheries practices to improve food security. Emergency livelihood kits distribution, and increasing access to local markets through seed fairs will be organised for local farmers and the project will provide direct distribution of food rations to the most vulnerable communities. Tearfund will also look at ways of resilience-building that reduce recipient’s future risk and vulnerability to negative shocks.
The proposed project activities will enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, and fishing support. The distribution of vegetable and crop seeds will provide the households with a variety of food for their consumption in a span of 3-4 months, in addition, surplus harvest will be sold in the market for income or preserved for future consumption during the lean season. Cereals such as lowland rice will be grown in flood prone counties such as Twic East and Aweil Centre. The Fishing kit’s will help the households get the much needed proteins almost immediately after they receive them and engage in fishing activities, the households will be encouraged to preserve and or sell surplus fish for future use or earn extra income for the household to address other household needs.

1. Needs

There have been several stressors and shocks ranging from drought, floods and conflicts in South Sudan and these have prolonged in the areas of Unity, Jonglei, GPAA, Central Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. In 2023, the latest data shows that 5.83 million people (46 percent of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), with 1.64 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). An estimated 35,000 people are classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe), 3000 of these are in Duk and 15,000 are in Rubkona Counties of Jonglei and Unity state respectively. Continued frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict,widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. The key drivers are the South Sudan’s economy is experiencing a macroeconomic crisis caused by the depreciation of the local currency and high food prices. The spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan as well as sub national and localized conflict are disrupting livelihoods and forcing the displacement of people. Multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance delivery is severely hampered by incidents of insecurity and conflict, the country is highly prone to natural disasters, particularly floods and dry spells that affect agriculture, disrupt livelihoods and hamper humanitarian access. The primary challenge driving needs is food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. At the same time, limited improvements in some areas have prompted some people to spontaneously return. In addition, the government, with the support of some humanitarian agencies - has facilitated returns to certain areas. Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. Women and girls continue to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial distress. People’s coping mechanisms has weakened because of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labour and child marriage. Besides the high levels of acute food insecurity there is declined crop production, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, limited access to basic services,and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. The current key livelihood opportunities include cultivation of vegetable and field crops, rearing of cattle, goats and sheep. They provide the most needed vegetables, grains, milk and meat for family meals and some are sold for income for other family needs like clothing, medical and foodstuff. Key crops grown in the areas of Uror, Twic East, Duk, Pochalla, Mayendit, Rubkona, Kajo Keji, Yei, Lainya, Aweil East, Aweil Centre, and Aweil South include sorghum, maize, Rice, Cassava, Kudra (Jute mallow), Cow peas and other vegetables are grown for household consumption. Rice will be grown in IPC 3 Counties such as Twic East and Aweil Centre. Based on the findings of a seed security assessment conducted by Tearfund in December 2023, it was observed that seed sources for most farmers are reserved from their harvest. To a large extent except in cases where one depletes their whole produce, the farmers always find comfort in using their own seeds reserved from the preceding season. Alternatively, they may purchase from markets or obtain through social networks.

2. Project activities

Tearfund will focus on providing holistic programmes in an integrated approach factoring in sectors and resilience building of communities. All the proposed activities will work to establish a nexus between Humanitarian response, Peace building (conflict sensitivity) and Resilience programming. The main activities will include the emergency food and cash distribution, including distribution of complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, and fishing support . The distribution of vegetable seeds will provide the households with a variety of vegetable food for their consumption in a span of 3-4 months, in addition, surplus harvest will be sold in the market for income or preserved for future consumption during the lean season. The Fishing kit’s will help the households get the much needed proteins almost immediately after they receive them and engage in fishing activities, the households will be encouraged to preserve and or sell surplus fish for future use or earn extra income for the household to address other household needs. The distribution of kits will be conducted across the Bomas in agreed upon distribution sites with local community leaders and local government actors. Project staff, community volunteers and leaders will facilitate distribution and ensure crowd control. Post distribution monitoring meetings will also be implemented in all the selected Boma’s to identify key learnings, record statistical distribution data and confirm receipt on behalf of the communities. To ensure efficient utilization of kits by individual recipients, appropriate training on vegetable production, agronomy, post-harvest and sustainable fishing and conservation methods will be delivered. Training sessions will be facilitated by Tearfund’s Food Security and Livelihoods Project officers in collaboration with Government extension workers. Lead farmers will also be targeted during vegetable kit and seeds training so as to disseminate key learnings to communities. The project will be implemented by a team of staff who are already established and currently implementing crop kit distribution food security programmes, supported by an Advisor with specialist technical knowledge. Tearfund staff in Juba, such as Grants Information Coordinator, will support report writing, monitoring visits and financial transactions throughout this project.The community and other stakeholders will be sensitized on the project and informed of the beneficiary selection criteria, which will be agreed by community members. During community sensitization the importance of prioritizing women and vulnerable groups will be emphasized in order to gain community acceptance of the selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs will be prioritized for assistance, including female, elderly and child headed HHs, those with malnourished children and people living with disabilities. Beneficiaries will be asked their preferences regarding seeds through FGDs, which will be held separately for men, women and specific vulnerable groups to ensure that vulnerable groups are able to fully participate in and contribute to the discussions. Community members, including representatives of vulnerable groups, will be involved in the selection and verification of beneficiaries.As part of Tearfund’s continued risk mitigation approach, a comprehensive analysis will be conducted and periodically updated in all the project sites. Tearfund considers seasonal risks such as access during the rainy season and the security risks associated with the dry season. In order to mitigate this, items will be propositioned where possible, and procurement will be done in the dry season. Appropriate measures will be taken to secure warehouses. Conversely, environmental risks such as flooding, disease outbreak/crop interventions have also been considered. Tearfund will work closely with FAO and other NGOs to ensure timely detection and reporting to the appropriate focal points.
