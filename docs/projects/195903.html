Project Summary

IOM will undertake a wide range of activities to create enabling environment for the Rohingya refugees, aiming to support eventual sustainable voluntary return and reintegration in Myanmar when conditions permit.
Rohingya refugees will be provided with training on on-farm demonstration programs such as homestead gardening, composting programs for agricultural support to crops and help them become resilient in terms of food security. They will receive training on aquaculture, livestock rearing, and livestock input support to build their resilience in fisheries and livestock support activities. Disaster preparedness and response concepts and activities, including early warning and early action, will be integrated into trainings with the aim of protecting food stores and sources, preserving livelihoods, and protecting productive assets such as livestock and the tools necessary for livelihoods activities. The project will create opportunities for targeted Rohingya beneficiaries through skilled and semi-skilled volunteers from among the Rohingya community by means of Cash for Work and vendors who are engaged in small-scale infrastructure projects (e.g., lighting, water-wells, fixing roads etc.) in the camps. Rohingya beneficiaries will also receive awareness raising sessions on food security, environment conservation, etc.
Beneficiaries from the host communities will be given training on aquaculture, livestock rearing and inputs to build resilience. Beneficiaries will also be given training on organic dry fish processing under the food processing activity.
IOM will contribute to expanding market linkages with the private sector engagement for host community beneficiaries to sell their products to individual consumers as well as wholesalers. Additionally, IOM will conduct labor market assessment, and value chain assessment to understand the underlying gaps and solutions for creating effective market linkages. Additionally, IOM will work on empowering young people by helping them find decent work/employment options through supporting life and vocational skills and job matching opportunities. Host community beneficiaries will be given scope for market linkage such as product fairs to make their income generation opportunity a sustainable one.
IOM will integrate various cross cutting issues in project design by identifying and including the most vulnerable beneficiaries through gender and disability sensitive beneficiary selection criteria and provide tailored assistance for the livelihoods and self-reliance opportunities. Women empowerment, leadership, decision making will be promoted through incorporating income opportunities and entrepreneurship opportunities, business development training, financial literacy etc., for women in the Host community and self-reliance opportunities for women in the camps to ensure sustainable development, return and reintegration in Myanmar.
To enhance overall disaster resilience of host communities and support food security initiatives, IOM will provide several disaster risk reduction and risk management activities. Reconstruction of roads and installation of drainage systems will provide pathways and critical access for food security project beneficiaries and other community members pathways, connecting them to markets, health centers, water points and to evacuation centers. Reconstruction or renovation of multi-purpose cyclone shelters will be conducted to provide safe, dignified and equitable access for emergency sheltering of the most disaster-vulnerable groups in host communities. Further, activation, training and capacity building of local institutions and Cyclone Preparedness Program volunteers for management of evacuation shelters will be included. These will focus on shelter management with emphasis on GBV and social protection of evacuees. Cyclone Preparedness Program volunteers will receive new and refresher disaster response.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The socioeconomic condition of Bangladesh was able to withstand the shock of the COVID-19 pandemic through its diversified agricultural and skills-based income generation activities (Tackling the Covid-19 Social and Economic Crisis in Bangladesh, UNICEF, 2021). However, the price hike of daily commodities created another pressure on the day-to-day life of lower- and middle-income families to meet their household needs. The current food inflation rate is down to 8.2% in July 2022 from the highest of 8.6 in June 2022 (WFP VAM report, July 2022). However, the aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic is still observed in many communities due to the unstable market chain causing price hike of daily commodities. Access to foods, shelters, and income generation activities are three main priorities according to the Joint Multipurpose Needs Assessment in December 2021.Despite many humanitarian and development activities in Teknaf and Ukhiya, the resilience and capacity index of these two Upazila are the lowest compared to Cox’s Bazar Sadar and Ramu (FAO Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis, 2022). The women are the most vulnerable observed in the assessment. In Bangladesh, the provision of the social safety net is very limited. Around 33% of the host community population had access to social safety net according to the findings. The support were mostly dedicated during the COVID-19 pandemic, however, the trend declined during the price hike of daily commodities when this safety net was also a requirement for the sustainability of the vulnerable population. Bangladesh has a long history of sheltering Rohingya refugees in Myanmar. In 2017, millions of Rohingya were forced to seek refuge in Bangladesh after the brutal ethnic violence initiated by Myanmar. These displaced Rohingyas are currently living in different makeshift camps in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, waiting to return to their homeland. The total number of Rohingya in Bangladesh has increased from 767,324 (154,264 households) after the August 2017 influx to 901,703 (UNHCR population data, November 2021) in December 2021. These additional populations have a social, natural, environmental, legal, and financial impact on Bangladesh. The skills development support for the Rohingyas is mostly dependent on the humanitarian assistance provided by national and international donor organizations. The purpose of the support is to provide tailored vocational skills so that they can utilize the skills once they return to Myanmar. The overall vulnerability of the Rohingya population has increased since 2017 (Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment 2022). The prevailing dual crisis of the COVID-19 pandemic and the war between Russia and Ukraine leading to instability of global markets and reducing the purchasing power of both communities are some of the causes for increasing vulnerabilities. The study finds that food is still the highest number of expenditures incurred for both the host and Rohingya community. The food expenditure for the Rohingya community has grown at an alarming rate due to the increased population and lower resources. Besides, the income opportunity in the camp is very limited. Only 30 per cent of income came from work-based income sources in the Rohingya camps compared to 91 per cent for the host community.Nevertheless, the resilience of women is also low. According to the latest report from FAO (Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis, 2022), women have the lowest resilience capacity index compared to men in both the Host and Rohingya community. To ensure equal opportunity for women and increase their empowerment, IOM will take wide ranges of activities focusing on women both in the camps and host communities. Persons with disabilities will also be included to enable them to contribute to society and family so that they can be considered as resources rather than burdens for their families.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

IOM will implement and plan the activities in close coordination with the relevant sector and other implementing partners to reduce duplication and overlapping of activities. This will help to harmonize the activities for the maximum benefit of the vulnerable people. The project implementation information (location, achievement, target, finding status) will be regularly shared with the sector for making a consolidated information management system. The project locations will be identified in consultation with the sectors so that IOM activities are not duplicated with other implementing partners. IOM and the partners will strictly adhere to the local standards and policies implemented by the government and the sector. IOM will ensure the partners have required policies related to PSEA, AAP, and Gender engagement before identifying the partners. While designing the project, IOM will prioritize the sustainability of the activities. Only the sustainable activities will be identified through the needs assessment and secondary analysis. IOM will ensure the continuation of the project through a regular funding channel beyond the year 2023 so that the activities are not suddenly stopped creating a void in the community needs.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

IOM will ensure mainstreaming of protection throughout the implementation of the activities. “Do no harm” is the guiding principle for implementing the activities for the vulnerable people. Privacy and confidentiality of the beneficiaries will be considered while designing and implementing the project. Beneficiaries will also receive awareness raising session to protect them from any unwanted incidents. IOM will deliver services and assistance in ways that preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities, that are culturally appropriate and that avoid any unintended negative consequences. IOM will provide assistance and services according to needs and not on the basis of age, sex, gender, nationality, race, ethnic allegiance and so on. Services and assistance are provided in an adequate scale, within safe and easy reach, are known by the affected individuals and accessible by all groups including medical cases, Persons with Disability, discriminated groups and the like.

3.2 - Gender

IOM activities will be targeted for all gender in the targeted camps irrespective of their race and ethnicity. The needs assessments will identify the specific needs of different gender and provide tailored support to them. IOM will institutionalize and integrate gender into the entire spectrum of activities -projects, programs, and staff policies. For gender issues to be fully integrated into the Organization’s vision, all staff need to understand and implement the Strategy in their work. This mainstreaming process is not exclusively related to women and their specific needs and concerns. It also includes initiatives for men when needed, provided they promote overall gender equality. IOM will target 50% men and 50% women in the program activities. Gender-sensitive training centers, equipment, and trades will be provided to the beneficiaries. Since women are left behind in many societies, IOM will especially focus to increase women's empowerment and leadership throughout the program.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

IOMs' beneficiaries are mostly aged 18+ years and above and does not include child beneficiaries IOM plans to include youth and the adult community as the direct beneficiary of the self-reliance and livelihoods activities. However, the activities will indirectly benefit all the age groups of the benefited household. The interventions will be designed in a manner considering the safeguarding of the children of the female beneficiaries. The training centers will be equipped with breastfeeding corners, and child-friendly spaces so that their mother can take the training courses while the children can safely stay with them.

3.4 - Diversity

All the activities planned for the year will consider people from different races and ethnicity. The activities will try to benefit all the beneficiaries in need. IOM will specially consider the minority people in the community to mainstream them into the society. The activities will be tailored to their specific needs and skills based on the outcome of the needs assessment.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability remains a vital and integral component of this proposed project. During the needs assessment, the affected populations in all the targeted locations will be consulted for their needs and priorities. They will also be given the opportunity to contribute to the design and planning of trade selection, opportunities, market analysis, with support from IOM. The affected people will take a lead in making decisions on their roles, local resources mobilization, and livelihoods/ self reliance skills considering safety, security, and gender dynamics. Affected women and girls will be consulted, in addition to and separately from men and boys, about trades and skills. IOM and the partners will establish a complaint feedback mechanism at the project sites to receive regular feedback from the beneficiaries regarding the activities. The privacy of the beneficiaries will be ensured and the response to their feedback will be delivered regularly.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project integrates DRR in the activities. Basic Disaster management trainings will be provided to the beneficiaries for protecting their foods and livestock/ livelihoods tools during disaster. Most of the families are familiars to keep their food grains and seedling safe in disaster and as a result they lose their agricultural inputs at that time. IOM will also provide training on disaster resilient food grains and seedling for sustainable agriculture. Besides that, the Cyclone Preparedness Volunteers will receive training on Gender Based Violence to protect the vulnerable community from sexual harassment/ abuse during disaster. Moreover, Cyclone Shelters will be renovated to meet the basic needs of the evacuated people in emergency and create education friendly environment for the school students. The roads, culverts, pathways,, drains will be reconstructed inside the community to ensure proper access during disaster and help reduce the water logging during heavy rain/ flood.
