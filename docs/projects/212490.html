Project Summary

Needs:
After over a decade of conflict, repeated displacements, and economic decline, the humanitarian crisis in Northern Syria affects every sector, area, and population group. Needs remain high and widespread, with almost three-quarters of households reporting they were unable to secure basic necessities. people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023.
In 2024, 16.7M people are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3M in 2023. Of the 16.7M people in need, 5.5M are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites.
The erosion of basic service capacity has continued, in a context of barely any development investment.
The inability to cover essential needs was mainly attributed to the escalating prices of basic goods, unaffordable services, and lack of employment opportunities, prompting more than 90% of assessed households to resort to negative coping strategies.
Recurrent food insecurity is contributing to rising mortality and morbidity, including increased rates of malnutrition and increased humanitarian needs.
By October 2023, the cost of the food basket had doubled compared to January and had quadrupled in two years.
The food situation remained difficult in NWS with KIs reporting challenges to access sufficient food. In January, KIs increasingly reported that households relied on food stored previously.
In addition, KIs in 91% of assessed communities reported that households experienced barriers to access sufficient food. It is worth adding that the Nutrition Cluster indicated that the nutrition situation in NWS has been worsening in the past year.
More than 50% of children under 6 months of age were being reportedly fed infant formula, while experts recommend breastfeeding during the first half year of life. In 35% of these cases, mothers did not have enough breastmilk, which could be associated with heightened malnutrition risks.
The project will target 1500 families with regular (monthly) food assistance through two appropriate modalities:
1- Cash for food: GAN will target 750 most vulnerable families in village and camps with cash for food, the cash intervention is feasible in these camps as they all have access to different main markets in the targeted communities, eight rounds of cash for food will be distributed, each round consists of 20 days for distribution and 10 days for validation and PDM, 14$/person will be allocated as recommended by FSL cluster and CWG, the data gained from the FSL cluster indicate these areas as high priority ranking and no partner plan to target the same vulnerable and underserved camps, according to the needs assessment.
2- E-voucher for Food: GAN will target 750 most vulnerable families in the village with food voucher with the aim of saving lives and ensuring continued and regular access to food for 8 months for most vulnerable groups, beneficiaries could replace the monthly top-up assistance (14$ per person monthly) with the main food items from the markets contracted with GAN and will be able to buy among 40 specified items with restricted amounts that allowed us to fulfill the calories min standard set by the WFP.
The project will target the IDPs with emergency/short term food assistance through:
1- Providing cooked meals to 750 families of newly IDPs in the targeted locations.
2- Providing RTERs to 750 families of newly IDPs.
3- Providing food baskets to the beneficiaries (750 HHs) who have received Cooked meals/RTERs as a next step toward securing their food security which will last for 3 months.
4- Bread bundles: provide bread to 500 HHs in the targeted camps for 30 days frequencies.
GAN will use beneficiary selection criteria that prioritize vulnerable HHs, including female or children-headed households, those with elderly or disabled family members, chronic ill family members, pregnant, and households with more than six persons.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

GAN is a member of PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from SEA in the project, GAN have developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a workplan to implement the policy in place, GAN will ensure all staff members are signed and adhered to a code of conduct policy, PSEA and will support the members to access relevant pieces of training (PSEA, GBV), ensuring that non-humanitarian stakeholders who will be in touch with beneficiaries such as (suppliers) will be informed and adhere to the humanitarian principles and code of conduct, PSEA posters will be available at DPs. GAN recognizes the rights of all stakeholders, particularly the vulnerable, to access humanitarian programs without the risk of SEA. GAN intends to prevent abuse by implementing procedures to protect beneficiaries through best recruitment practices, staff induction, creating an open and aware culture and assigning clear management responsibilities, ensuring that victims are supported, and perpetrators held to account. Information on the referral pathway will be circulated through posters and banners at the DPs that will be inside the camps itself, during daylight hours, and safeguard that there is appropriate segregation for women to create a culturally sensitive and safe operational environment. GAN prioritizes equal and safe access to life-saving assistance for all, without discrimination, including women, girls, people with disabilities, and marginalized groups, within this context, the "Do No Harm" principle is crucial to avoid worsening existing tensions and conflicts over limited resources, beneficiaries face risks such as sexual assault and robbery, especially at checkpoints. To mitigate this, training halls will be located within communities, during daylight hours, with appropriate segregation for women, GAN team will include female members to ensure women feel comfortable discussing their needs. Feedback mechanisms will be accessible to all, including those with disabilities, with various modes of communication provided, procedures to prevent abuse will include best recruitment practices, staff induction, fostering an open culture, and clear management responsibilities. Support for victims and accountability for perpetrators will be prioritized. GAN will monitor the distribution on daily basis, GAN will conduct PDM assessment after each redeem process/distribution, GAN will choose a sample of beneficiaries (5-10%) to gauge beneficiary feedback in relation to timeliness of the provision of assistance, quality and quantity of assistance provided, the distribution process, behavior of the distribution staff, satisfaction with the project.The feedback mechanism will ensure accurate information about the implementation mechanism and the beneficiary's satisfaction.
