Project Summary

In response to the growing humanitarian crisis in southern Somalia, Dialog Forening is proposing the following interventions to expand lifesaving GBV and child protection services to vulnerable populations in Gedo and Lower Juba regions.
On GBV response, the project will provide comprehensive case management to GBV survivors ;face-to-face individual PSS & legal consultations, adding to economic empowerment through cash assistance for GBV survivors to mitigate the psychosocial & economic stresses facing them during Covid-19 pandemic. Case management & referral approach will also be followed to provide mental health services & legal assistance the project will also capacity build local authorities and community structures on GBV , and create awareness at the household level about GBV
The project will also raise the awareness of women, girls, men & boys in multiple psychosocial, legal & health topics, such as PSEA, prevention of Covid-19 infection, GBV, women's rights, self-care mechanisms & other topics to promote women & girls rights & enable them to cope with the current situation & face challenges. The project beneficiaries also will be provided with dignity kits to meet their special needs during home quarantine, the kits will include PPE to prevent them from Covid-19 infection.
On child protection, the project seeks to strengthen case management through direct service provision to children at risk ,specifically the project will target High-risk groups such as unaccompanied and separated children, children with disabilities, those exhibiting severe signs of distress, and children associated with armed forces and groups (CAAFAG). The interventions will offer direct services like family tracing of the primary duty-bearers if in the best interest of each individual child, reintegration into the community for children formerly associated with armed groups (including psychosocial first aid- PFA) and Psychosocial support to both children, caregivers in addition to victim support and referral services for where a holistic approach is necessary. Project beneficiaries will receive comprehensive case management and psycho-social support (PSS) – including emergency care, referral to other actors for services not offered by Dialog Forening, access tailored material assistance, then follow up on all services (both directly provided and those received during/after referrals). Capacity building of duty bearers (including formal and informal authorities) and child protection committees to strengthen and improve identification and provision of child protection services. Considering current approaches, the emphasis is on scaling up prevention and response services to protection-related risk for children (including vulnerable caregivers to improve the protective environment and the quality of care).
A project implementing committee (PIC) will be established comprising of community structures (women and youth), local authorities, community leaders and organizations representing PWDs. The PIC will be involved in selection of beneficiaries to ensure that the most vulnerable people (PWDs , women and children ) receive immediate protection services . The project will also address barriers that hinder PWD access protection services

Project Activities

1.1. Provision of Comprehensive Case management services to GBV survivors (sexual violence, recruitment, abduction, and maiming), including referral to specialized clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS), will be provided within 72 hours.1.2 Provisional of psychosocial support, basic emotional support, and mental health services. Psychosocial support to GBV survivors will be provided by trained counselors and caseworkers who will offer to support if the survivor experiences any post-traumatic incidents.1.3. Provision of cash and voucher assistance through the distribution of dignity, hygiene kits, and solar lanterns as an emergency response targeting women at risk of GBV. This project will provide dignity hygiene kits and solar lanterns to 2000 women and girls of reproductive age within the project locations.1.4. Provide capacity building to 400 members from GBV AoR members and other NGO staff, service providers (e.g; social workers), and duty-bearers (e.g from the line ministry) particularly, those supporting emergency response teams1.5. Adopt the harmonized GBV AoR IEC messaging, print, and distribute through teams of GBV Outreach Workers in the target locations with a target population of 11,700HH combined. Through door-to-door awareness-raising and weekly community outreach, the community caseworkers each will be able to distribute over 12,000 IEC materials on GBV prevention and response for GBV, referral pathways, and available services in the local language1.6. Establishment of Women and Girls safe space in each of the target locations according to UNFPA/IRC global standards. 3000 direct beneficiaries will regularly access the safe spaces through service provisions, 4000 HH will be reached indirectly through access to awareness services through the women and girls safe spaces constructed in the vicinity of their camps. The established safe space will provide a space where women and girls can go to at any time to feel safer and empowered and have access to information, skills building activities, support and service2.1. Improve the quality of CP services for boys and girls at risk most (including child-survivors of violence, exploitation, and abuse). Children of concern for protection from child labor (including hazardous forms). High-risk groups such as unaccompanied and separated children, children with disabilities, those exhibiting severe signs of distress, and children associated with armed forces and groups (CAAFAG) prioritizing protection enhanced by direct support to children at risk, child survivors including unaccompanied and separated through providing case management services that meet their unique needs, which include family tracing and reunification services, alternative care, of inclusive community reintegration (including victim assistance where applicable2.2. Provision of structured MHPSS activities including individual and group counseling focus on children in need, caregivers, and families (including positive parenting and in-kind of support) in order to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their own children. Supporting tailored material support to at-risk children (including but not limited to UASC or CAAFAG)2.3. Strengthening community-based protection mechanisms to prevent and mitigate risks faced by boys, and girls (as well as caregivers or those at high risk of abuse/exploitation) through multi-layered, community-based psychosocial support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks by building a protective environment at family and community levels. 2.4. Systematizing efforts to develop sustainable local capacities through facilitating workshops/seminars/training with civil society members and authorities on protection principles and best interests’ practices for children (including meaningful participation of children). Equip traditional duty bearers as well as medical personnel, and humanitarian staff with relevant information and approaches on CP

Mitigation Measures

The emergency response will (as far as local conditions allow) meet minimum SPHERE standards, work within the framework of Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) and Do No Harm (DNH) to ensure beneficiaries wholesomely participate in project planning and implementation. A project implementing committee (PIC) will be established at each target location, the Project implementing committee (PIC) will comprise of community and clan leaders, representatives of marginalized groups and local authority.DF will maintain up to date financial management systems including books of accounts and payment vouchers to eliminate the risk on mismanagement of funds. Payments and accounting will be segregated where roles will be assigned to different persons. Proper tendering procedure will be followed for purchase of tools and materials. Proper lists of trainees and participants shall be provided after each training. The lists shall contain the names and telephone contact details of the participants. All the lists shall be properly signed by the attendees and participants
