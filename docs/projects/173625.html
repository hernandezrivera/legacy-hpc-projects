Project Summary

The proposed project for 2021 will contribute to the WASH sector 2019-2021 strategy. Project will focus on providing non food item (NFI) and emergency shelter kits for the recently displace people due to insurgency, flood and fire outbreak. A multi sectorial rapid assessment will be conducted with in 72 hours from the alert received to identify emergency needs of women, girls, boys and men including disable people. 35,000 individuals (9100 men, 9800 women, 7000 boys and 9100 girls) will be benefiting from NFI and emergency shelter.

1 - Needs

Nigeria faces multiple crises, with a protracted conflict in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states. In the absence of a viable peace and reconciliation process, the most likely scenario for 2021 is that the situation in north-east Nigeria’s BAY states that have suffered from ten years of conflict remains static at best, and a deteriorating security environment at worst. Continuous threats to safety and security, forced recruitment, abductions, and killings and rape remain major protection concerns for the physical and mental wellbeing, living standards, and coping mechanisms of IDPs, returnees and host communities. According to Round 33 of IOM DTM report, 2,118,550 IDPs or 436,058 households were recorded as displaced out of which 53% are female, 47% male, 28% children under 6, 78% women and children. A total of 1,714,682 returnees were recorded out of which 54% female, 46% male, 18% children under 6 Y, 82% women and children. Borno State continues to host the highest number of IDPs at 1,566,011. The continuing trend of increasing returns continued in this round of assessment with 1,714,682 (276,479 households) returnees recorded. Furthermore, windstorm and rainfall(flooding) had adverse effects in camps and host communities since June 2020 till date. IOM, SEMA and UNOCHA reported that about 6,799 households (29,243 individuals), 57 sites, 2,780 shelters, 180 toilets, 81 shelters were affected and damaged during this period in 17 LGAs in Yobe and more than 50 camps in Borno State.

2 - Activities

Distribution of NFIs and Shelter kits will be done together with hygiene kits proposed under WASH sector to help women, men, boys and girls maintain their dignity during humanitarian crises. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, which is important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence also assists them in providing care and protection to their children. Generic hygiene kits will help people improve cleanliness (with items such as jerry can, soap, sanitary materials) and whilst NFIs and Shelter kits are similar to get access of shade and daily routine kitchen utensils hygiene in hostile emergency. All kits are designed in partnership with RRM operational group with affected communities to ensure the most appropriate items are included, and distribution is carried out in a manner that minimizes the risk of GBV against women and girls. Kits will be distributed in kind considering facts that IDPs and returnees will not be in position to buy, secondly proposed NFI/Shelter and Hygiene Kit’s items will be not available at local market also limited market accessibility in remote areas. Kits help target group by: • Contributing to the psychosocial and physical wellbeing of women and girls, by including items such as intimate wash and pads, and improving mobility of women and girls by providing specific sanitary items.• Contributing to the protection of women and girls, by including items such as flashlights, so that women and girls do not have to walk in the dark. • NFI/Shelter kits will enable HHs to build shade and to cook food for family, and household items for use such as mates for sleeping will be helpful.Activity 01: Distribution of emergency shelter kits Emergency shelter kits will be distributed to the recently displaced IDPs to ensure their safety and security. Project team will coordinate with shelter and NFI cluster to support the affected population. A total of 500 shelter kits will be distributed only for targeted most affected HHs in a location where shelter and NFI partners are not able to immediately access the locations. The shelter kits will consist of two tarpaulin sheets, one rope, umbrella nail packs, hammer, and wood. At the same time, AAH will coordinate with shelter cluster partners to support long term solutions for shelter. Activity 02: Distribution of emergency NFI kitsThe NFI kits will be distributed to the recently displaced or returnee IDPs to ensure that they have access to basic non-food items. A total of 3000 CCCM/NFI kits will be distributed, and the kit includes three plates, three cups, four cooking pots, one kitchen knife, three tablespoons, three blankets, three sleeping mats, foldable mattress, seven laundry soaps, seven bathing soaps, four reusable pads, two 10l and one 20litres jerry cans, one 2l kettle, two 10litres buckets, one 10litre basin, two mosquito nets, two serving spoons, one carrying bag and one solar flashlight.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The target group will be involved in the planning and implementation of the project. The multisectorial assessment will be conducted with community leaders and female beneficiaries’ members and the outcome will be discussed with leaders. Location for water points and latrines will be selected according the recommendation from women and girls to reduce the risk of gender based violence. Special needs of the individuals will be addressed, ensuring that different points of view and groups have their say during the implementation phase. In addition, as described below, AAH has a comprehensive feedback and complaint response mechanism (FCM) with well-trained accountability personnel to ensure responsiveness of feedback and complaints to affected population, transparency to project stakeholders and information-sharing with different groups.The mechanims is structured to integrate the people affected by the crisis into key decisions and processes and to have effective communication and feedback channels. When RRM is triggered in any community/location, the team will hold community discussions to ensure communities/beneficiaries understand the purpose and intended impact of the project, to agree on two-way accountability measures and responsibilities and to determine community obligations and participation in project implementation and monitoring. The team will place banners showing access to the feedback mechanism toll free phone lines, complaint boxes, complaint booths and feedback forms that are administered during feedback days at the community level
