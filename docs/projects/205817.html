Project Summary

South Sudan is still going through some of the worst humanitarian crises driven by a number of factors, which includes, extreme weather conditions fueled by climate change, a deepening economic crisis, as well as sub-national violence among others. Considerable internal and cross-border displacement has further strained scarce resources, livelihoods, and basic services, increased protection risks, and inter-communal tensions, with the biggest impacts felt by children and women. In 2022, 8.9 million people, including 4.7 million children, and 1.3 million people with disabilities required humanitarian assistance. All these factors are contributing to malnutrition.
Malnutrition, in particular under-nutrition, continues to pose as one of the major challenges affecting the health sector across the Republic of South Sudan and in particular Jonglei State. According to the 2022 FSNMS surveys, the prevalence of GAM rate at national level was at 16.0% which is above the WHO estimated emergency threshold of >15%. More so, during the IPC report of 2022, the most food insecure states where more than 50% of their populations were facing crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity were Jonglei (68.0%) on top followed by Unity (66.7%) and last by Lakes (56.9%).
Due to recent shocks, the country will continue to face one of the world’s worst food security emergencies as these recurrent shocks have driven a reduction in food availability, regularly cut off household access to food, and limited households’ capacity to recover from past shocks. Continued conflict and displacement are contributing to high levels of food insecurity and poor WASH infrastructure and practices, and increasing rates of global acute malnutrition (GAM) and morbidity in Uror, Twic East and Pochalla Counties. The floods make the roads impassable, destroy critical infrastructure, washing away acres of cropland and increasing the risk of contamination of water sources and post-flooding disease outbreaks. The recent needs assessment in these Counties have shown that the livelihoods of community members have suffered greatly, leaving thousands of people in need of humanitarian assistance. The recent surge violence in Pochalla had eroded the gains in the management of acute malnutrition and this will take another time to improve the nutritional status of under-five children, pregnant and lactating women.
Poor Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices, low food production and consequently low food reserves are also preventing children from receiving nutritionally dense foods. Therefore, this project will provide a multi-sectoral response to effectively address malnutrition, through addressing gaps in WASH infrastructure and poor hygiene practices, food security and emergency nutrition needs. Tearfund is the sole organization supporting nutrition interventions in Uror, Twic East and Pochalla, in 27 Feeding Centres and 4 Stabilization Centres.
In the first 9 month of this year across the three locations of Uror, Twic East and Pochalla, 33,170 under-five children were reached through Nutrition specific intervention such as Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) key massages, health and hygiene massages, Vitamin A supplementation and Deworming, 10,948 pregnant women were reached through Nutrition specific interventions such as the screening and referrals of pregnant women at the community to TSFP sites. 41,299 admitted and managed with acute malnutrition. Out of these cases, 94.9% in OTP and 95.4% U5 children in TSFP were discharged cured.

1. Needs

In South Sudan, high levels of food insecurity drive humanitarian needs in most parts of the country. In 2022, an estimated 8.9 million people (60% of the total population), including refugees, were expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May-July) as shocks appear to be intensifying. The level of severe food insecurity has seen an increase in 2023 compared to 2022. Among the counties with extreme levels of food insecurity in 2023, Uror County is among them (HPC data Oct 2023). In addition, an estimated 2.52 million people, including under-five children, pregnant and lactating women, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2024. The high numbers are attributed to increased food insecurity and the decrease in coverage and of nutrition services due to conflict, flooding and impacts of extreme weather. Acute malnutrition has been protracted problem in South Sudan and one that requires sustained investment for life-saving nutrition service delivery. Its key drivers are multifaceted, including severe acute food insecurity, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease, inadequate sanitation conditions and hygiene practices, and limited access to health and nutrition services driven by the elements noted above. Those who are malnourished are more vulnerable to common and infectious diseases such as cholera, malaria, diarrhoea diseases, acute respiratory infection and measles. Jonglei is one of the states where Counties of extreme concern are found. According to the food security analysis Feb 2022, Counties of extreme and severe concern will need immediate emergency food assistance complemented with emergency livelihood and livestock support across farming, agricultural and pastoral communities, for the medium- and longer-term to build both absorptive and adaptive capacity. Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. Heavy rainfall over the past three years, has led to overflows of the Nile and other rivers as well as inland flooding, mainly in the eastern parts of South Sudan. This has caused large-scale displacement of people, some of whom moved with their cattle and Crops and property damages were widespread in the affected areas. Jonglei has been the worst affected according to South Sudan flooding situation report. Over the past three years, local authorities in Twic East estimate that as much as 85% of Twic East was affected by flooding with as many as 80,000 people having fled the area for safer, drier ground. Communities find shelter in churches, schools, and outdoor spaces (Seasonal Flood Report 2021). Severe food insecurity is expected at the height of the lean season of 2022 due to the impact of conflict, climatic and economic shocks. According to the subsequent SMART surveys, the high rates of malnutrition in Tearfund operational areas were attributed to several factors including high levels of food insecurity, poor infant and young child feeding practices as well as high morbidity due to a weak health system. Furthermore, poor access to nutrition services in all the three locations during the rainy season due to insecurity along the roads and poor road infrastructure have significantly affected service delivery. In Uror, the findings from the June 2021 SMART survey revealed a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM)prevalence of 14.4 % (11.1 - 18.5 95% C.I.) and a Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM)prevalence of 2.8 % (1.7 - 4.7 95% C.I.) indicating a ‘high concern’ nutrition situation according to WHO cut off values (SMART survey, June 2021). Tearfund is currently implementing UNICEF, WFP and BHA funded integrated Emergency nutrition, WASH and FSL projects. Each county has well equipped storage facilities and full time Nutrition staff as well as a network of IYCF groups, Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and community-based staff.

2. Project activities

Tearfund will continue its nutrition interventions primarily focusing on provision of quality preventative nutrition-specific services to children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women and adolescents in Twic East, Pochalla and Uror Counties. Activities will include: Community mobilization, training of CNVs, active case finding, mass screening campaigns, TSFP services, and provision of deworming tablets and vitamin A supplements to under five years old children and PLWs OTP Treatment: All children with a MUAC of<11.5 cm will be admitted into the OTP and treated with Ready to Use therapeutic food (RUTF) and routine medicines according to the South Sudan CMAM guideline. Community Nutrition Workers (CNVs) will deliver health and nutrition related messages at the community level through individual counselling, group discussion and encouraging other women to share their experiences with the group. SC Treatment: Medical and nutritional treatment for severely malnourished children and infants in the stabilization Centre will be conducted according to the standard inpatient treatment protocols. Admissions to the SC will be children who are severely malnourished and who have bilateral pitting oedema grade +++ or any grade with medical complication. Children will be discharged when the medical condition is stabilized and they are able to eat well or have passed the appetite test. All discharges will be through the OTP where they will continue with treatment and follow-up. All children discharged from the SC will be given a priority for outreach follow-up during their first week in the OTP. Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP): Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months, children meeting the criteria (MUAC >=11.5 cm - <12.5cm) will be admitted in TSFP and treated with Ready to Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) and pregnant and lactating mothers who meet the criteria (MUAC <23 cm) will be admitted and treated with super cereal plus (CSB++). Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN): provision of quality MIYCN and micronutrient supplementation (VitA and deworming) services in nutrition sites and community level. PLWs attending the feeding centres and within the community will be targeted for individual and group counselling (mother-to-mother support groups) on IYCF practices, following the UNICEF recommended approach Multi-sectoral intervention. Specific multi-sectoral activities are to increase access to safe water and sanitation, promote critical WASH messages, and integrate early childhood development strategies in nutrition treatment sites. Kitchen gardening, screening and treating of malaria, managing diarrhoea, and mainstreaming child protection and gender-based violence risk mitigation will also be promoted at the nutrition sites. In addition, advocacy efforts will be undertaken to integrate inpatient care of severe acute malnutrition in the health system. The use of community platforms through the community nutrition volunteers will be supported as a main vehicle for delivering multi-sectoral services at community and household levels. Tearfund is currently implementing UNICEF, WFP and BHA funded Integrated Lifesaving Support Project (ILSP) to IDPs and Returnees in South Sudan supporting nutrition, WASH and FSL projects in Pochalla, Uror and Twic East Counties of Jonglei state. Each county has well equipped storage facilities and full time Nutrition staff as well as a network of IYCF groups, Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and community-based staff. It has been supporting respective County Health Departments (CHD) in identifying and treating acutely malnourished under five years old children, and pregnant and lactating women as an immediate life saving measure while at the same time tackling barriers that cause poor utilization of health and nutrition services.
