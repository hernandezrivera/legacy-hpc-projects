Project Summary

“SALAAM SUDAN" – is a One UN Initiative to Enhance Coordination of Feedback and Response to communities, to strengthen centrality of protection and accountability to affected populations in Sudan.
The project is reaching out to communities with humanitarian and/or development actions in their communities, the project is closely designed to support and build the capacity support the Ministry of Labor and Social Development (MoLSD) to be able to manage and maintain the operation of the call center in the future.

1 - Needs

In a humanitarian crisis, access to critical information is vital, and the ability to stay connected isoften a lifeline for families. Hundreds of thousands of people remain affected by conflict andnatural disasters, and accessing these communities and meeting their needs remains a majorconcern. During inter-agency consultations, it was noted that the people of Sudan had limitedaccess and incomplete information regarding the provision of and access to basic services andassistance that significantly reduced their ability to cope with the crisis. In 2013, OCHA in WestDarfur rolled out a telephone hotline in an attempt to improve communication and to allowdisplaced people across 38 camps to share their needs and concerns. The Darfur Hotline was atool designed to identify and respond to gaps in camp services by bridging communicationchannels between the camp population and humanitarian organisations through mobile phones.it functioned well for a few years, but ceased due to lack of adequate financialand human resources. With information being increasingly recognized as a vital form of aid – with conflict anddisaster-affected people requiring life-saving information on services as much as protection,water, food, medicine or shelter, particularly in areas where there is restricted access –humanitarian agencies are being asked to increase information exchange, improve dialogue withaffected communities, and strengthen their ability to listen and respond to the needs andfeedback of people. This feedback can strengthen preparedness of programmes, and caninfluence how protection and humanitarian assistance is organised and delivered while ensuringthe response modality is appropriate to the needs of affected communities. Also with the recent changes in the political situation in Sudan and the set-up of the transitional government, this opens door for more transparency and communities to share their concerns and feedback on developmental activities implemented in their communities.The UN Country Team and the Humanitarian Country Team in Sudan proposes to establish anational toll-free hotline called “Salaam Sudan” to enhance a two-way communication withSALAAM SUDAN – A National Toll-Free Call Center with affected communities, protect and provide early warning, strengthen accountability and transparency, and support better coordination between humanitarian and development agencies. As a telephone hotline for humanitarian operations and development programmes, Salaam Sudan will support the GoS in its efforts to work with national and international partners in providing people with accurate and up-to-date information on how to access humanitarian and social services, and report beneficiary feedback and complaints, while maintainingconfidentiality. Through established communication and reporting channels, Salaam Sudan will circulate callerfeedback, complaints and data to the humanitarian community, allowing stakeholders tocoordinate, focus, and prioritize thematic and geographic areas of support, especially inunder-served areas and areas facing displacement and return. This information flow will help theGoS and the humanitarian and development community to identify gaps in aid and obstacles to access it, convey the needs and priorities of affected communities, improve the way information is shared, andshape how humanitarian and development assistance is organised and delivered. Salaam Sudan will help monitor and assess impact and relevance of programming throughremote post-distribution/post-return monitoring. It will complement the work of conflict anddisaster preparedness and response and recovery programmes by quickly sharing informationon needs gathered from callers. This information can be used by the GoS and UN partners tobuild preparedness plans and prepare mitigation/prevention activities and do no harm analysis,for both short-term individual projects and long-term comprehensive programmes.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Objective: To strengthen centrality of protection and accountability to affected populations in Sudan.Output: Support the GoS to establish and manage a nationwide toll-free, confidential hotline centerthrough which critical feedback and complaints can be collected, from anyone with a mobile phone, and submitted to humanitarians to influence programming and delivery of assistance.Outcomes:1) Affected populations are empowered with information that allows access to services in aninformed manner Salaam Sudan will enhance accountability to affected populations and communications withcommunities by improving community access to two-way information exchange platforms onhumanitarian services as well as complaints mechanisms via the hotline and its referral andfeedback mechanisms. Activities will include: Improving accountability on behalf of the GoS andhumanitarian agencies by closing the loop on complaints and providing responses to callers. 2) Humanitarian partners are more accountable for ensuring an effective and appropriateresponse to unmet needs identified by callers By sharing data on needs and priorities in real time, Salaam Sudan will help to strengthen thecapacity of the GoS and partners to provide a faster and more relevant response to affectedpopulations. Key activities under this will include: Sharing relevant and useful data and trendanalysis to donors and clusters that can be used to identify gaps in assistance, support ongoinginformation exchange, influence how information is shared, shape how humanitarian assistanceis organised and delivered, and help improve Disaster Risk Management and mitigation. 3) Protection of the affected population is enhanced through protection risks, gender and dono harm analysis that will inform advocacy actions as well as through provision ofpsychosocial counselling and referrals. Salaam Sudan will participate in collecting, documenting and referring protection issues and earlywarning signs in order to prevent, mitigate, and respond to protection risks. The project willpotentially provide psychosocial service to alleviate stress and discharge affected populations.The national hotline will inform a gender-based approach through analysis of informationprovided by callers. The activities include:● Analyse information provided by callers that can inform do no harm analysis;● Refer protection cases to protection agencies;● Provide dis-aggregated data and analysis of gender specific needs analysis; and● Share early warning signs and Protection of Civilian alert with the Protection Cluster.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

A) Do no HarmThe project will integrate protection principles throughout the project cycle, including in itsday-to-day work. The project will be informed by the ‘do no harm’ principles, adhering tohumanitarian principles and promoting the application of international standards that advanceprotection and the resilience of Sudan’s most vulnerable. Information received will help tocapture and understand protection risks related to the delivery of humanitarian assistance andbetter develop conflict-sensitive programming. B) Effective access to humanitarian assistance without discriminationThe project will capture information related to obstacles to humanitarian assistance eitherrelated to security, operational processes (long distance), geographical or natural hazard barriers.This will inform humanitarian actors in adjusting response modalities and operational processesand inform advocacy action and post-response monitoring.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

UNOPS will implement the project Salaam Sudan on behalf of UN agencies, local andinternational NGOS, the project donors, and affected populations. The project will be built on thesuccess of Iraq and Afghanistan Call Centers that are managed by UNOPS with partner agencies.With more than 15 years of experience in Sudan gained through several projects, UNOPS Sudanbrings the agility and technical expertise needed to get the project done – on cost, on scheduleand meeting quality requirements. The project will be delivered in line with the principles, rulesand regulations of the UN system, to support the response to the current needs and nationalpriorities in the country. The project will be managed by an international project manager, who oversees the design,planning, execution, and monitoring of the and is the point of contact for donors, governmentrepresentatives, and other key stakeholders. A national project supervisor will carry outday-to-day management of the project, and national operators will be in charge of answeringcalls, disseminating information, and recording complaints. For calls requiring immediateattention or related to psychosocial distress, it may be possible for a Sudan psychosocial expertto attend to the calls. An Information Officer will manage the data and information flows. Governance, and Roles and ResponsibilitiesUNOPS proposes the project structure below, based on the PRINCE2 project governance model.PRINCE2 project management methodology encompasses the management, control andSALAAM SUDAN – A National Toll-Free Call Center 16 organization of the project, and is recognized as global best practice. Given the security situationand constraints, the governance structure needs to be flexible and able to rapidly respond to achanging environment. UNOPS will facilitate the appointment of the members of the Project Board. The Project Boardwill be appointed during the initial set-up of the project and will meet on a regular basis (every 3months) or more frequently, including on an ad hoc basis, as may be deemed appropriate. It willbe convened by the Project Executive. Formal meeting minutes to be taken, finalized andaccepted for the project record. The project will be Governed by the following structure, in a very participatory manner:- Project Executive: RC- Senior Users: GoS, UN & INGOs/NNGOS partners- Senior suppliers: UNOPS/Donors- Project Assurance: UNOPS Sudan office- Project Manager: UNOPS
