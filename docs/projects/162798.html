Project Summary

This project aims to achieve sustainable improvement in access to quality water supply, sanitation condition and hygiene practices among IDPs and host communities in East and South Darfur states of Sudan. The project will focus on subsectors of water supply, sanitation, hygiene promotion, environmental health and WASH Non-food Items (NFIs) with the aim to achieve the following objectives:
o Maintain and improve access to safe and clean water in both in the target communities and health and nutrition facilities through new water points, and upgrade and maintenance of existing water infrastructure and services to meet minimum SPHERE standards communities.
o Continue improvement of the hygiene and sanitation conditions for the targeted beneficiaries in the rural communities and IDPs camps. This will be done through promotion and support in construction of emergency latrines for most vulnerable families, and improved household shared latrines with hand washing points, and promote good hygiene practices among the targeted beneficiaries.
o Reduce the disease prevalence and health risks of communicable diseases by sustaining water safety and quality through testing and treatment.
o Promote improved hygiene and sanitation in conjunction with the health and nutrition messaging in target IDP and host communities.
o Mainstream protection in all WASH activities to protect the population from all potential risks associated with WASH services.
In order to improve the water supplies in targeted area of operation, environmentally friendly technologies will be used namely water provision through installation of solar pumping systems, monitoring water quality on daily and bi-weekly bases through chlorination and bacteria tests for ensuring safe drinking water provided. community based operation and maintenance approach will be applied to ensure long lasting water service provision. In design and locating of water sources, more consideration will be given ensure safe and easy access to the water points by women and girls, as they are in most cases the ones tasked with fetching water. Such considerations will include: distance to water points, time for water distribution, waiting time at water points, and safety and protection of users.
In Sanitation and Environmental Health: Latrines will be provided at household level with considerable community contribution in terms of labor for ensuring easy access, enhanced dignity and protection especially for women, girls, children and old-aged. Solid waste management and vector control activities planned to integrate with other sanitation services to cut short sanitation transmission chain and improve health status of refugees and host community, voluntary hygiene promotors and community women groups will be assigned to supervise these activities.
In regard to hygiene promotion: Community hygiene promotion volunteers and the Community Relief and Development Committees (CRDC)s will mobilize men and women from the community to participate in community level hygiene awareness events focused on cleanliness of water points, and water safety at household level through testing of the free residual chlorine in water at household water storage using user-friendly pool testers.
Vulnerable individuals to receive the proposed hygiene kits will be identified through a community co-creative process, including HHs with disabled people, elderly and female- and child headed HHs. WASH NFI provision will be implemented with the objective of reducing incidence of water borne diseases through improved quality of collection and storage of drinking water; contributing to the psychosocial and physical wellbeing of women and girls, by including items such as intimate hygiene items and pads ensuring dignified living conditions of women and adolescent girls.

1 - Needs

For many years now, Sudan has been under a protracted crisis driven by conflict, natural disasters, poor governance and insecurity. The gaps and needs of the communities are still not adequately or efficiently met. Adequate access to safe water: Based on WASH strategy 7.6 million people in need for WASH intervention despite 73 % of the Sudanese population have access to basic water services as per the revised S3MII . 50% of those people who are without access to basic water services are considered in severe need. Access to water has increased in some villages and camps to approximate the minimum Sphere standards of 7.5 to 15 liters per person per day (l/p/d). However, in Kalma IDPs barely have access to the minimal water quantities, particularly in congested areas. (ARC WASH Survey, 2019). IDPs still depend largely on ARC to run, maintain and rehabilitate water systems. Despite hard efforts from ARC side, there is still no effective community-driven cost-recovery and sustainable structure established in the camps, which is partly due to limited resources and capacity. The demand for water continues to grow each year, and is more acutely felt in high population density areas like IDP camps where at times the water supply does not match the demand and is way under the standard, not exceeding 4 or 5 l/p/d. This leads to overcrowding at water-points, and constant breakdown of pumping equipment and infrastructure from overuse. Water, Environment and Sanitation (WES) and State Water Corporation (SWC) have exerted some effort to rehabilitate, operate and maintain water points, but they have stated clearly that the gaps in water supply and O&M (operation and maintenance) were beyond their capacity. In both East and South Darfur, communities purchase water from private water tankers or water vendors who mostly use donkey carts. The host communities in particular are in danger of water insecurity caused by huge numbers of displaced persons and refugees they are hosting. This requires continuous expansion and upgrading of all WASH services to ensure adequate services. Sanitation & hygiene still need improvement, the latrine coverage for both IDPs and host communities is very low, and open defecation is a common practice. According to the WES and SMoH data (2019). Although latrine coverage in Kalma camps is above 80%, flooding damages HHs latrines each year and causes collapsed and need for replacement, including the hand washing facilities and water containers that get lost or damaged, preventing people to fetch, transport and store water safely, as well as from using clean water to wash hands. In Kalma and Gereida IDP camps the ARC WASH coverage assessment carried out in July 2018 showed that in Kalma, 74.88% of the population drink clean water from protected sources. About 76.82% of HHs spend fewer than 30 minutes to fetch clean water and 75.3% of HHs surveyed have latrines with slab, while 8.2% of HHs do not have latrines and are practicing open defecation. 75.54% of HHs surveyed wash their hands after using the toilet. For Gereida the household visit report indicated that 60% have access to sanitation facility.

2 - Activities / Outputs

The aim of this intervention is to implement lasting solutions within the IDPs camps and rural areas through construction, upkeep, operation and maintenance. This includes:- Running of 9 solar water-pumping systems and 9 hand pumps in Kalma IDPs camp,- Construction of 4 water points construction and rehabilitation of water distribution network in Kalma camp, - Support Operation and maintenance of PHCC water supply systems in IDP camps (Kalma, Gereida, El Neem)- Upgrade of thee high yield sources operated by hand pumps hand pump to solar/ hybrid systems in accordance with the best WASH Cluster/ OFDA standards ( in Bulbul, Dalaengar and El Safia) - Conduct water treatment and quality testing at all water systems and targeted household in IDP camps (Kalma, Gereida, El Neem),- Replacement of 150 HH latrine in Kalma and Gereida IDPs camp, - Support community to construct 100 household shared latrine in Kalma and Gereida IDPs camp, emphasizing gender-separated dignified access, separate hand washing points and lockable latrine doors for user privacy. - Carry out monthly debris clean-up Campaigns in South and East Darfur (IDPs camps), - Conduct quarterly clean-up campaign in 10 villages hosting IDPs, - Open/ rehabilitate drainage canals for flood prevention & clear stagnant water in Kalma camp, - Desludging and rehabilitation of 6 PHCC institutional latrines in IDP camps (Kalma, Gereida, El Neem), emphasizing gender-separated dignified access, separate hand washing points and lockable latrine doors for user privacy, adequate intimate / menstrual hygiene practices through safe disposals and water for cleaning, and solar lanterns for safety.- Sanitation Social Marketing co-creation meetings and prototypes to encourage people to maintain and upgrade their latrines- Conduct refresher training of community relief and development committees (CRDCs)- Conduct five refresher trainings for integrated HNW Volunteers in IDP camp and Rural Areas in ttC/Baby WASH, - Conduct three refresher PHAST trainings for Community Hygiene volunteers and conduct CHAST training in schools in Kalma and Gereida IDPs camp.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ARC will continue its use of environmentally friendly approaches such as use of solar energy instead of fossil fuels to pump water. ARC has converted a number of water systems into hybrid solar/ diesel pumping systems with diesel pump being used only as standby alternative in the event of a breakdown in solar system. This has lowered the use of carbon-based fuels like diesel and reduced the cost of operation and maintenance by 50%. ARC has developed latrine designs which use concrete slabs and beams instead of wooden poles to minimize cutting down trees. For super structure bamboo sticks will be used because bamboo is easily renewable and has less environmental impact. ARC does not use asbestos or lead based paints in its projects, does not use fire bricks or strives to replace them with the stabilized soil blocks (SSBs) whenever possible and practices responsible sourcing of materials.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

This project address protection and safety concern by ensuring that proposed WASH services are meeting the needs of especially women and girls. Gender-differentiated approaches will be considered e.g. ensuring short distances to water points, establishment of latrine door in HHs, separate school latrines facilities for boys and girls.
