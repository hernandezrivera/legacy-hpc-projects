Project Summary

The proposed intervention is aimed at providing immediate lifesaving assistance to the most vulnerable affected populations in Lower Juba region specifically 3 ditricts; Kismayu, Afmadow and Badhade. through 3 fixed sites and 2 mobile teams (working in 5 mobile sites) focusing on the hard to reach areas and internally displaced and host communities. The three fixed sites are; Diif town, Burgabo of coastal town and Qam qaam along the riverine. While mobile sites will be conducted in the outskirt of Diif town; Dardaar and Gura Arba and suburbs of Qamqaam; Istanbul, New Gobwein and Daif respectively. Burgabo will not have mobiles teams due to access challenges i.e security. The project will target 11,000 beneficiaries overall; consisting of MAM <5 boys & girls- 6,200, MAM PLWs- 2,000 and SAM <5 boys & girls- 2,800.
WASDA additionally proposes to provide conditional cash transfers to severely malnourished children (SAM) upon discharge to avoid relapse. Nutrition, Hygiene and Health Education will be provided as well as IYCF individual and group counselling sessions. IMAM training for 17 nutrition project staff and protection mainstreaming & PSEA training will be conducted too. Wasda will also put more emphasis on COVID-19 mitigation measure at all times during screening, registration and at distributing points as well as social distancing.
Somalia’s population is currently experiencing multiple shocks including the desert locust upsurge that continues to threaten food security and livelihoods of pastoralists and farmers in many parts of the country; riverine and flash floods during the recent Gu (April-June) season that affected the food security, livelihoods and safety of farmers and people living in flood-prone and the novel coronavirus pandemic is having severe health and socio-economic impacts both in Somalia and globally. The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on Somalia economy in general and the food security situation in particular are more visible, driven by the global economic downturn, supply chain disruptions in the Arabian Peninsula, and social distancing measures. International and local flight restrictions and port closures in Dubai disrupted import and export trade activities, while money vendors reported steep declines in both external and internal remittances. These declines in business activities have affected the airline, construction, road transport, hospitality, petty trade, miraa/khat trade, and education and health services sectors. In the South, declines in business activity have affected labour demand. In Kismayo, where wages have fallen most significantly, the daily labour wage fell to 30-40 percent below May 2019 and by 27-33 percent compared to the five-year average. Cross-border livestock market closures in Kenya have also contributed to changing livestock export dynamics. Overall, the impact of the pandemic on food security is most significant in urban areas, where poor urban, returnees and IDP households face declining daily labour wages, internal remittances, and social support. With the reduction of imports as result of the COVID 19 restrictions, the retails prices of staple food items risen by 8-20 percent above 2019 and roughly up to 40 percent above the five-year average. Due to the combined effects of these multiple shocks, current and projected levels of acute food insecurity and malnutrition remain high although both the magnitude and severity are lower compared to earlier projections. Currently an estimated 849,900 children under the age of five face acute malnutrition over the next 12 months (Sep 2020-Aug 2021), including 143,400 likely to be severely malnourished. These high levels of acute malnutrition tend to persist across Somalia due to several factors, including high morbidity, low immunization and Vitamin-A supplementation, poor care practices and acute food insecurity.

Project Activities

1.1: Community mobilization and sensitization: Communities, stakeholders, local authorities, community leaders & beneficiaries will be mobilized at the start and during project implementation period. 1.2: Identification, Screening & Referral of SAM and MAM cases: Early detection of malnutrition will be emphasized through facility and community level screening. Screening will be done by community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) and screeners. To mitigate the spread of Covid-19 child anthropometric measurements will be reduced to use of MUAC only in places with large gatherings. Additionally, mother to mother support groups (mother MUACs) will be trained on how to refer malnourished children from the community. 1.3: Admission and Treatment of MAM cases: Following the screening process, all children aged 6-59 months and PLWs meeting the admission criteria based on the Somalia Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM) guidelines will be admitted to TSFP. In addition, all admitted children and PLW will also receive systematic treatments, such as Albendazole for deworming, and micronutrients supplementation. 1.4: Admission and Treatment of SAM cases (without Complications): All children presenting with SAM upon screening will be admitted into OTP for treatment for maximum of 4 months according to the Somalia IMAM guidelines. All children with medical complication, no appetite or with severe Oedema grade 3 (+++) will be transferred to the nearest stabilization center for inpatient treatment. 1.5: Routine medications & immunization: WASDA will provide routine medics including vitamin A, Albendazole, folic acid and multiple micro nutrient tab to <5 children and PLWs. WASDA will liaise with MOH/EPI team or village EPI cold chain to get vaccines at the nutrition centers. 1.6: Nutrition, Hygiene and Health Education: Nutrition, hygiene and health (NHHP) promotion sessions will be conducted by CNVs both on site as well as inside the community. The CNVs will be trained on NHHP using the UNICEF developed Manuals. Hand washing facilities with no contact will be put in place at nutrition centers. Social distancing will be observed at waiting shade, screening, registration and distributing points. COVID-19 stickers/IEC materials will be used at the centers during nutrition counselling/education sessions while observing social distancing. 1.7: IYCF: Individual and Group IYCF counseling: WASDA will provide IYCF counselling both to individual mothers with specific feeding problems and caregivers in groups in fixed nutrition facilities and in mobile teams. The individual counselling will be conducted by a trained IYCF counsellor who will meet the mothers with key feeding problems either identified through the group counselling session by staff or by the mother herself. Key messages will be covered systematically (1-2 messages each visit) through group counseling sessions. Mothers who have received individual counseling will also be consulted again during the follow up visits to ensure their challenges are addressed. Mother to mothers support groups will be established, each group will be trained on MUAC & counselling for a period of 3 months after which they will graduate and given MUAC to identify and refer malnourished children. IEC materials developed by UNICEF/MOH will be disseminated to IYCF counsellors as well as mother to mother support group leaders at the community level and during campaigns and ensure there is good visibility in all supported nutrition facilities. Social distancing will be observed during counselling session at all centers. 2.0: IMAM training for 17 nutrition project staff: 5 Nutrition Nurse, 6 screeners and 6 Community nutrition volunteers (CNV) will be trained on IMAM Somalia guidelines. 2.1: Protection mainstreaming & PSEA training: A total 26 project staff will be trained on PM and PSEA. 2.2: Joint monitoring supervision: Monitoring will be done in mid of project, by WASDA team, Jubaland MOH/Nutrition focal.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The recurrent drought, locust invasion, floods, insecurity and COVID-19 pandemics across the country has intensified humanitarian needs. Food security conditions and livelihood activities among vulnerable populations have also been disrupted. According to the Somalia Operational Plan for Pre-Famine Scale Up of Humanitarian Assistance, cash-based assistance will be one of the main response modalities for the drought. In places with functional markets, cash or vouchers can play a stabilizing role on local markets by maintaining demand from existing retailers. The provision of income through conditional cash transfers will help diminish negative coping mechanisms, such as the sale of productive assets among drought-affected households. Cash-based programming is well suited to the monetized Somali economy. The primary benefit of cash transfers is their efficiency. Cash transfers are easy to complete. Cash transfers also allow those in need to meet their needs more specifically. Need is personal and varies from one person to the next, and cash transfers offer beneficiaries the freedom to choose how to use their benefits. The theory here is that you know your needs best, and cash lets you fulfill them in the best way for you.The Cash-based interventions will improve households’ purchasing power which in turn contributes to improvements in the local economy. In addition, support economic activities and contribute to strengthened resilience to future shocks. WASDA therefore proposes to provide conditional cash transfer to OTP beneficiaries upon discharge in the target locations to avoid relapse. The key objective is to increase purchasing power for vulnerable malnourished communities so they can meet their daily needs. While the proposed conditional cash transfer interventions are temporary in nature, they will go some way in ensuring that households are better able to meet their basic needs including debt repayment. WASDA proposes to provide $40 per beneficiary for OTP beneficiaries upon discharge. WASDA will ensure that only the most vulnerable malnourished beneficiaries participate in the cash transfer activities while preventing the distortion of local labor markets and discouraging reliance on temporary income. With the existence of a robust telecommunication system across project sites, WASDA believes that cash can be delivered to beneficiaries safely and effectively. WASDA have worked with reputable money transfer in the past on cash transfer interventions. WASDA for this activity will engage Hormuud telecom where beneficiaries will receive cash through mobile money transfer system (MMT) or so called EVC (electronic voucher currency). Prior to implementation, MoUs will be signed with these vendors setting out operating procedures that include the protection of beneficiaries during cash payments and strict guidelines/training for payment procedures. WASDA will conduct a Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) survey at least two weeks after payment. The PDM will among others, provide key information on beneficiary perception regarding appropriateness of cash payment cycles, security at cash payment centers, cash utilization at household level, decision making patterns on the use of cash, adequacy of the cash value and any possible fluctuation on the exchange rate. Beneficiaries will also utilize the Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) which provides a safe, accessible and effective channel for project beneficiaries to exercise their right to give feedback and raise complaints for a response or redress to be given.

Mitigation Measures

Protection: In WASDA’s Somalia’s programming, ensuring the protection of every individual is a requirement, not an option. The protection aspect is not limited to stand-alone activities. It is an inherent part of our strategy and approach. In addition to targeting women and children as groups in need of protection, WASDA recognizes and includes other vulnerable groups such as minorities, people with disabilities, older persons and people with special needs in to the protection response. WASDA will ensure that protection is mainstreamed in the project with focus on access to aid, safety and security. Through this WASDA will train project staff and volunteers on protection mainstreaming & prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA)training, so that they can play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalized groups is represented. Further all nutrition staff and volunteers will have to understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients. Disability: Through the community engagement, WASDA will make sure that persons with disability and older persons are consulted and have access to information about the project. Additionally, WASDA will also make sure that nutrition centers are able to provide conducive working environment to all so that it is accessible to persons with impaired mobility (e.g. persons with physical disabilities and older persons). The services include separate shade, and toilets for male and female. WASDA will ensure awareness on recognizing cultural norms and traditions that may discriminate against particular groups, such as women and clan minorities. Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM): WASDA has an FCRM system (hotline), which provides a safe, accessible and effective channel for project beneficiaries to exercise their right to give feedback and raise complaints for a response or redress to be given. This mechanism has worked well particularly in WASDA’s past projects in Lower Juba. WASDA staffs have been trained on the use of the FCRM mechanism. The Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) will provide an opportunity for beneficiaries to interact with the project management team. Do no harm policy: The spirit of the global movement to improve and not cause harm to communities during project implementation will be upheld.
