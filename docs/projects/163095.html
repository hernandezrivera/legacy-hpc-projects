Project Summary

Over the past decade, communities in project target areas increasingly suffered from climatic shocks such as erratic rainfall, chronic dry spells coupled with poor water, sanitation and health services. Communities within these areas also face challenges to sanitation- open defaecation is high as well as diarrheal incidence especially amongst children under 5 years, and because of scarce availability of safe water, unsafe water sources are used. The situation is compounded by the rapid economic deterioration experienced in recent months. According to the Rural WASH Information Management System (RWIMS) of July 2019, 28.8 per cent of the water points nationally are seasonal and only 12.74 per cent are good yielding and perennial. The water shortages will further increase the burden of water collection for domestic use among women and girls. As of the June 2019 ZIMVAC Assessment report, showed that 16 per cent of households travelled more than 1km to fetch water from the nearest main water source and 32 per cent over 500m, still above the Sphere standard. This travel distance will increase as more water sources dry up. A further drought forecasted for Southern Africa during the 2019/2020 season will further accelerate the dire situation as access to water deteriorates in both rural and urban areas. This will compel people to scramble for water at the few water sources. Access to water for women and people with impairment becomes very limited under such circumstances. In the absence of urgent humanitarian intervention, WASH related diseases are likely going to increase and these include cholera and typhoid which are already a high risk in Zimbabwe given they have been recurrent outbreaks.
CAFOD and its Caritas partners (Caritas Harare, Caritas Gokwe and Caritas Hwange) will implement the project in worst drought hit rural wards of Mudzi, Uzumba-Maramba-Pfungwe, Gokwe North, Gokwe South and Binga districts in collaboration with governmental partners. A total of 215,000 vulnerable people that are affected by the humanitarian situation will benefit from increased access to safe water, a means to practice good hygiene and improve health and hygiene practices. Determinations of vulnerability criteria will be done in close coordination with the affected communities and cross-referenced to data available at district level.
The project will rehabilitate multiple-use water infrastructure for targeted institutions and households, especially female -headed households by identifying/ rehabilitating/ constructing water points (boreholes, wells, solar piped-water schemes). High yielding water points, most water stressed areas and waterpoints that can be completed within the project period will be prioritized in consultation with communities and local leaders. Rehabilitated water points with high water yields will also be used for productive use (irrigation and livestock) thus supporting nutrition and livelihoods. Water quality will be monitored and treatment done where necessary. Aqua tabs will be distributed in high cholera risk areas to prevent incidences of waterborne diseases in project areas. The capacities of local communities to sustainably manage and improve water facilities will be built at household, community, institutional and local government levels. To improve sanitation, health and hygiene knowledge, attitudes and practices for vulnerable women, men, girls and boys, the project will train communities using gender sensitive participatory health and hygiene education messaging, complemented by awareness-raising campaigns, specifically addressing open defecation, cholera awareness and response. The project will establish and train school health and hygiene clubs, targeting girls and boys, including addressing negative gender norms and providing gender sensitive training, e.g. menstrual hygiene management. In addition, the project will distribute hygiene and MHM materials.
