Project Summary

Action Against Hunger (AAH)’s proposed intervention, together with partners Volontariato Internazionale per lo Sviluppo (VIS) and their sub-partner Guidance and Training Center for the Child and Family’s (GTC), will focus on safe and equitable access to quality education for children in Area C and H2 area of Hebron city in the West Bank. Specifically, the proposed project aims to provide emergency response to the most vulnerable schools in Area C through integrated and targeted activities, aiming to increase the protection of 1,186 students and teachers (students; girls: 600, boys: 490, teachers; 67 females, 29 males). The intervention will also be responsive to the negative impacts that COVID-19 has had and is continuing to have on education. The response will be implemented by upgrading the educational environment in schools through the rehabilitation of classrooms, and providing key support to enhance healthy educational environments. It will also supply and construct new classrooms in overcrowded schools to facilitate separate spaces for students from different grades. Activities will also target the schools’ staff and parents to improve their capacities in delivering knowledge as well as dealing with risk reduction; risk mitigation (man-made (settlers attacks, clashes) and natural hazards, including COVID-19); and to give the students the needed support in the face of these risks and hazards (evacuation procedures, first aid, etc.). Simultaneously, the project will respond to psychosocial issues faced by students and teachers alike as a result of the coercive environment they are subjected to. The proposed psychosocial response will be innovative; various artistic and recreational tools targeting students, teacher and parents, will be adopted to ensure the maximum effect on the targeted students. The proposed interventions are in line with Education Cluster 2021 Strategic Objective 1 for the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) “Strengthen advocacy and reporting on education-related violations across Gaza and the West Bank and ensure that school staff and students affected by the education-related violations and COVID-19 pandemic benefit from protective environment”, the Strategic Objective 2 "Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children” and the Strategic Objective 3 “Children and school staff in vulnerable schools are supported in preparing and responding to emergencies”, and aligns with the 3 Strategic Objectives of the oPt Humanitarian Response Plan. The project uses a community-based approach, with students (girls and boys) and teachers (women and men) participating in all stages of the project including in the assessment, design and implementation stages. Hence, the students, teachers as well as parents’ fears, needs and ideas will be discussed and analysed to inform effective technical and psychosocial activities considering gender and age disaggregated data, resulting in adapted assistance. As outlined, the proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability to affected population as well as participation and empowerment.

1 - Needs

The ongoing protracted conflict in the oPt has had a significant impact on all aspects of people’s lives, particularly on the right to access to basic needs. Access to education in the oPt continues to be compromised due to the occupation and recurrent conflicts. Education related violations include attacks on schools, military use of schools, threats of attacks against school staff and students and interferences with access to education. According to the 2021 HNO, 503,927 people need Education in Emergencies humanitarian assistance across the oPt. These include 489,009 children (254,493 M; 234,516 F) of whom 9,780 are children living with a disability (CWD), who face challenges in accessing quality education in a safe, child-friendly environment. In the West Bank including Jerusalem, checkpoints and the Barrier impede access to education for children living in the “Seam Zone” and in East Jerusalem communities isolated by the Barrier. Based on the Education Cluster, recent years have witnessed an increased number of Israeli attacks on schools located in Area C and Hebron H2. In 2019, 328 education related incidents were reported affecting 19,913 (12,968 males and 6945 females) students of which 33% (109 cases) involved firing tear gas canisters on schools’ premises and/or students while commuting to schools affecting 6,653 (5920 males, 733 females) students. Many students are forced to travel long distances, exposed to delays at military checkpoints, harassment and violence, military activity in or around the school, or have to cross military areas and Firing Zones in order to access schools. Moreover, school children face additional challenges that negatively affect educational attainment and a sense of safety and security, due to the poor educational facilities, unsafe and unfriendly overall educational environment, and the lack and inadequacy of school facilities. Schools remain at the front line of facing demolishing orders, 5 incidents of demolition orders and/or stop-work orders served in 2019 in addition to 2 orders in 2020, and 3 schools partially or fully demolished in Tubas, Hebron and East Jerusalem governorates. The negative impacts of COVID-19 have pushed more families into poverty, undermining the affordability of basic education supplies, transportation costs, adequate internet or IT equipment for distance learning and increasing the risk of school dropout and anxiety. Despite the adaptation of distance and blended learning, education in Area C remains a big challenge. At least one third of the schools in Area C and H2 area of Hebron lack the minimum standards for safe learning environment (ECW Baseline Assessment, 2020). The Palestinian Authority has been unable to significantly intervene in these communities as they are in Area C, i.e. under Israeli occupation. Additionally, all the existing school structures have been implemented under emergency conditions following minimum standards aiming to ensure an access to education and keeping space for further upgrading works in future. Consequently, all the schools have become uncomfortable spaces for students who are already struggling to reach them due to access restraints, particularly during winter. Poor school conditions and access have driven many families to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as withdrawing their children from school or displacing part of the family. In August and September 2019, AAH updated a multi sectorial needs assessment and protection analysis involving 76 communities in Area C using a community-based approach. The ongoing assessment of AAH’s interventions in 2020 that complete the 2019 assessment indicated that communities in the E1 zone, Firing Zone 918, Jordan Valley and Road 317 clusters are the most affected by Israeli restriction policies, affecting the educational sector in the targeted communities. Girls’ school dropout, access to schools and schools’ unsafe environment were the main concerns for the children in these communities. The intervention is coordinated with different organisations (including Medecins du Monde, UNICEF and NRC) acting in the area to support these students to access and enjoy school spaces.

2 - Activities

The continuous assessment of AAH’s interventions in 2020 in addition to the previous field assessments provide a full picture of the required actions to address vulnerable community’s needs. In September 2019, AAH, VIS and GTC conducted needs and technical assessments in 13 schools adopting a participatory methodology targeting the students, the school teachers and households. This was developed with a comprehensive understanding of the problems schools face due to the unsuitability and inadequacy of school facilities, unsafe educational environment and the associated psychosocial problems faced by students and their families. Therefore, based on its experience in implementing over 40 educational interventions in Area C, AAH has proposed the needed technical solutions for each school in order to create a more healthy, safe, and friendly educational spaces for students and considering ICA construction restrictions in Area C. It will do so by rehabilitating the existing infrastructures to ensure the basic and humanitarian needs that will thereby support students in continuing their education. To complement, VIS, who has extensive experience in conducting psychosocial activities at both community and school level, and its local partner GTC, who has more than 24 years’ experience working on child and families psychosocial and mental health related issues, has conducted different focus group sessions with students, teachers and parents from different Area C communities. Throughout these sessions VIS and GTC’s social experts identified the psychosocial problems that targeted groups suffer due to school access and environment; out of which they were able to propose technical solutions and share them with relevant stakeholders (Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MoEHE)) and school headmasters, etc. AAH and VIS have experience in implementing educational activities in coordination with (MoEHE). For this project the coordination with MoEHE will be on two levels, Ministry level: to ensure that all the project activities will be implemented according to the Ministry strategies and priorities, and on Directorates level: to ensure that the education Directorates in each area have been consulted regarding the assessment, design and even during the implementation of the intervention. Furthermore, the activities will be handed over to the MoEHE as the service provider of the educational sector. The following activities will ensure meaningful access to education and psychosocial support, prioritizing safety and dignity: 1) Provision of emergency education supplies: targeting schools that suffer overcrowding or merging students from different grades into one classroom; schools that face a deficit in power supplies, affecting the safety, health and educational attainment for students. Under this activity simple classrooms will be added, and power supply solutions will be developed (including solar systems for isolated communities). 2) Basic emergency rehabilitation to schools: to enhance the students’ learning environment, classrooms and other facilities will be rehabilitated/upgraded, creating a friendlier, more enjoyable and safer space for children. The upgrading of sub-standard structures in the most vulnerable communities will ensure a more protective educational space and the improvement of facilities in schools. 3) Provision of school-based psychosocial support to vulnerable school children and staff: the activity will target the most vulnerable schools that suffer from a variety of threats including settler violence, natural hazards, and those with a generally unsafe and unhealthy environment. Detailed psychosocial assessments will be conducted in each school and consequently VIS and GTC’s psychosocial support teams will identify the appropriate psychosocial intervention in each school. They will use various artistic and recreational events and sessions with students, teachers and parents, including activities focusing on stress management, trauma and class communication. 4) Provision of hygiene equipment to assist schools and children in facing COVID-19, including hygiene awareness raising and campaigns, and the distribution of hygiene kits.

3 - Indicators

Technical and psychosocial baseline surveys will be conducted by VIS, GTC and AAH to identify the target groups’ needs; the survey will facilitate the supervision, monitoring and evaluation of the activities. Teachers and students (girls, boys and persons with disabilities) will be involved in the design, monitoring and evaluation of the activities to ensure the full participation of all groups. A post line and KAP surveys will be conducted to assess and evaluate the satisfaction and impacts of the activities on all groups, including an evaluation of improved access to schools and the students’ psychosocial status. Project activities will be monitored by 1) Working field staff; 2) Community representatives; 3) MoEHE visits and participation in the activities; 4) Baseline and post line reports; 5) Donors and cluster monitoring visits; 6) Project progress and impact reports. The communities’ representatives and village councils will support AAH, VIS and GTC to provide ongoing field monitoring of the rehabilitated schools and advise AAH on ongoing security status to determine optimal timing of field operations. In order to ensure accountability, AAH has a Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM), which allows beneficiaries to provide their comments and complaints on the activities and is designed to increase their protection, in compliance with sensitive data management and adhering to the “Do No Harm” principle. The knowledge of this system has been promoted through several field assessments and monitoring visits. In addition, a service phone number will be distributed to the population in the proposed target communities enabling them to report feedback or complaints relating to project implementation by AAH and its partners. AAH will process and treat all information collected through an ad-hoc committee composed of project staff to understand the situation, identify appropriate solutions and include technical adjustment or modifications if needed. The intervention will target a total of 12 schools in Area C in 4 different governorates and one school in Hebron H2 area (Bethlehem: 1, Ramallah: 1, Jerusalem: 2, Hebron: 8, and H2: 1 school). A total of 1186 (students; girls: 504, boys: 586, teachers; 56 females, 40 males) will benefit from the project, of whom 1000 are in Area C (915 students; girls: 504, boys: 411, teachers: 56 females, 29 males) and 186 located in H2 area (students; only boys 175, 11 male teachers). All targeted schools are elementary/basic schools (students age between 5 and 16 years old, teachers age between 25 and 50 years). In Area C, the geographic distribution of the targeted beneficiaries will be as follows; Hebron: 640 students (352 girls, 288 boys), Bethlehem: 85 students (47 girls, 38 boys), Jerusalem: 140 students (77 girls, 63 boys), Ramallah: 50 students (28 girls, 22 boys). Output 1: 8 schools; Hebron: 5, H2: 1, Jerusalem: 1, and Ramallah: 1 school (731 students and teachers, of whom 672 are students and 59 are teachers; 370 girls, 302 boys, 41 female teachers, and 18 are male teachers) benefited of the emergency education supplies (caravans, solar systems, educational tools). Output 2: 5 schools; Hebron 3, Jerusalem 1, and Bethlehem 1 school (455 students and teachers, of whom 418 are students and 37 are teachers; 230 girls, 188 boys, 26 female teachers, and 11 are males) received emergency rehabilitation for the school building and facilities. Output 3: Psychosocial support through using various artistic and recreational tools (26 events) provided to 1186 (students; girls: 504, boys: 586, teachers; 56 females, 40 males) students, parents and teachers received. Output 4: All targeted schools received awareness training and hygiene kits. The expected objective verifiable indicators and targets from the project are as follows: Outcome1: 50% of the targeted students reported that the schools became safer. Outcome 2: the total educational attainment improved by 20% in the targeted schools. Outcome 3: the average absenteeism rate decreased by 30%. To further ensure protection is mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, the indicator “% of communities/project beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” is added and will be measured through an end line survey of the beneficiaries.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming is guided by the protection principles of Do No Harm. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. As a specific protection mainstreaming component, AAH is in the process of developing its own protection mainstreaming toolkit and to start working with its own sector specific gender and protection mainstreaming checklists. Up to date, AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection Mainstreaming Tip Sheets. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. AAH in oPt prioritises this also through context specific (risk) analyses as well as the protection coordinator position stirring questions and discussion. Overall AAH seeks to complement its own actions with for instance legal support to inform risk mitigation measures and consequently to increase complementary humanitarian action. In terms of doing no harm, particular attention in designing this project has been paid to the exclusion of girls, children with disabilities, and other children belonging to disadvantaged or marginalized groups. The project will also ensure that schools, classrooms, child friendly spaces, playgrounds and surrounding areas are rehabilitated in a way that poses no safety risks to children and teachers. In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH continuously works on understanding gender roles. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff has the obligation to adhere to the organization code of conduct and to AAH Child Protection Policy. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. AAH also has a gender and protection manager supporting the project teams to include those issues in education project design and implementation. To ensure the safety of beneficiaries, rehabilitation activities will be done during the holidays in order not to expose the students to construction risks. Students will be involved in the design of the rehabilitation of school spaces, with the intent for the rehabilitations to address all sources of fears and discomfort, privacy issues, ensuring dignity of the beneficiaries.To further ensure that no harm is done through this project, AAH will follow internal and international guidelines to protect their staff and beneficiaries through adopting adequate behaviors to prevent the spread of COVID-19 and wearing protective equipment during interventions.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH endeavors to include all vulnerable groups in its work to reflect on their needs and adapt project implementation, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments with students and teachers alike, making sure that those with specific needs are heard. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through individual and community consultations and focus group discussions that are accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate, especially for children and girls in particular. Building on a number of ongoing and past successful education projects in the West Bank, AAH in its assessments considers lessons learned from previous interventions including ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs. AAH has safe and accessible beneficiary feedback mechanisms in place which allow beneficiaries to provide their inputs, complaints, and satisfaction regarding the activities implemented. They are designed to increase beneficiaries’ protection and comply with sensitive data management guidelines to uphold the principle of ‘Do No Harm’. Beneficiary knowledge of this system are promoted during field assessments and monitoring visits during which AAH and partners staff explain how the mechanisms work, made child friendly. The AAH beneficiary feedback mechanisms which is accessible by phone, email, or in person (AAH or partners field staff or base members), according to AAH protocols. A service phone number will be distributed to the beneficiaries in the proposed target communities, enabling them to report feedback or complaints relating to project implementation or AAH employees’ behaviour. AAH will process and treat confidentially all information collected with ad-hoc committee composed of project staff to understand the situation, identify appropriate solutions and include technical adjustments or modifications if needed. With regards to access in this specific project, AAH tries to achieve equal and impartial access through for instance working with children, teachers, community representatives and parents to support equal and safe access to education and training opportunities without discriminations based on gender, age, capacities or background. Through the project, AAH discusses with representatives and children of the targeted schools’ communities (including men, women, girls, boys, people with disabilities) to ensure all benefit from project intended outcomes and have access to the services provided. Activity 4 particularly will ensure that all students and school staff members benefit from hygiene material and trainings with regards to addressing the pandemic.
