Project Summary

The main purpose of the project is to reduce the vulnerability, increase and maintain human dignity (especially for women and girls), and vulnerable lives saved of displaced populations affected by the hunger crisis in Ethiopia. In addition to the Nutrition outcomes, Improved access to essential gender responsive, life-saving water and sanitation services is believed to be contributing to the purpose of the project. The WASH sector of the project will be benefitting 81,462 beneficiaries including 23,846 women,18,514 girls, 17,286 boys, 21,816 men, through provision of safe water supply, improved sanitation facilities, behavior change hygiene promotion and distribution of basic WASH NFIs. Communities targeted for CMAM intervention are prioritized with provision of this improved WASH services. Health care facilities which are providing such nutrition services will also be prioritized for the WASH intervention according to the need identified during assessment.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

To ensure sustainability and maximize efficiency of resource utilization, the project will implement all activities in partnership and consultation with existing government structures, including the zonal and woreda offices; international and local NGOs; and UN agencies responding. To avoid duplication of efforts, the project will coordinate with other NGOs involved in the WASH sector in the project areas. Reports will be shared among all stakeholders, and lessons learned will immediately be incorporated to improve performance. The situation in Tigray Region is different than most other regions of Ethiopia, where there is limited government structure or systems to coordinate emergency assistance with, worsened by the fragile security condition of the woredas. World Vision will keep working with the available government offices when possible, and work build capacity among them. WV will also work to build capacity among community-based structures and leaders, including religious leaders, youth, and community leaders, to integrate the humanitarian work. World Vision will also closely work with the UN and other humanitarian organizations in information sharing and coordination of other activities. WActive participation of boys, girls, men and women, people with disabilities, institutional leaders, faith based leaders, community elders, and key informants is a foundational part of the planning, monitoring and evaluation of emergency interventions. Selected community members will be part of the selection of WASH facility construction sites, establishment of wash committees, selection of volunteers, and participation in selecting NFI beneficiaries.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Community feedback is important to learn about the community’s views on the project. This tool is very important to identify the gaps observed by the community during emergency interventions. Appropriate feedback mechanisms will be implemented such as suggestion boxes, suggestion books, and review meetings. Social accountability refers to measuring the quality of the services provided by the government or other service providers and promoting appropriate corrective measures by service providers. World Vision’s social accountability tool is called Citizen Voice Action (CVA). World Vision will focus on improving partners’ capacity to form CVA groups that will be responsible for monitoring and assessing the different social service providers in the area and the quality of their services.
