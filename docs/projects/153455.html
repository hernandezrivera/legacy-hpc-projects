Project Summary

Prevailing social norms, gender roles, national conflict, a highly militarized environment, and the frequent cattle raiding and revenge killings among the different clans and tribes exacerbate the risk of GBV in South Sudan. Protection concerns such as generalized violence and insecurity, access to food, water and firewood, expose women to a high risk of GBV. There is, therefore, an urgent need to enhance GBV prevention efforts with additional strategies to mitigate risks, such as fuel strategies and protective accompaniments. Furthermore, lack of recognition of GBV as violation of fundamental human rights continues a culture of impunity throughout the country. Therefore, nowtihstanding the advancing of the peace process, it is important to continue to raise awareness, reduce prevalent stigmas in the communities and increase identification and documentation of GBV cases as well as providing survivors access to psychosocial support and other services for their recovery and healing. INTERSOS GBV interventions therefore aim to strengthen prevention and response to GBV. INTERSOS will provide women and girls a safe and confidential environment in Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) where they will access psychosocial support services in form of peer-to-peer support, individual case management and recreational activities. All activities will focus on overcoming trauma and psychosocial distress by strengthening their individual coping mechanism, resilience and social network. Cases will be identified through the Protection Monitoring Officers, community based protection networks and Community Mobilizers and will be referred to trained and skilled GBV Caseworkers. Furthermore, INTERSOS will continue to support the economic empowerment of women through income generating activities that support their livelihood. A selected group of women that is interested in starting their own business will attend regular business skills training and be assisted in developing a business plan.
In order to confront social norms that drive GBV, INTERSOS will specifically focus on engaging men through its programming to become change agents for shifting gender roles. The discussion around gender and social norms will be facilitated in regular workshops, including influential community leaders to reflect on masculinity and the reasons behind their violence towards women, gender, sexuality, and substance abuse. In addition, INTERSOS will conduct awareness campaigns addressing specific problems affecting girls such as early and forced marriage and the low inclusion of girls in school. There will be specific awareness campaigns in primary and secondary schools for the promotion of girls’ enrolment and to reduce the drop out from schools.
INTERSOS has played an important role in strengthening coordination among humanitarian actors present in in its areas. INTERSOS in Bor will lead the GBV Sub Cluster and is strengthening coordination with the GBV Working Group in Akobo, as well as coordinating with the Protection Working Group in Lankien, which is led by INTERSOS. In 2019, INTERSOS will also take over leadership of the GBV subcluster in Jonglei State. The coordination is deemed fundamental to elaborate a common strategy for the GBV sector, including partners’ joint activities and to prevent duplication or overlapping. INTERSOS GBV interventions will be aligned with the GBV Sub-cluster Strategy. To effectively respond to GBV, INTERSOS will collaborate with strategic partners to contribute to the provision of the basic package of multi-sectoral GBV services at community level and build capacity of service providers and communities to deliver quality GBV services in line with best practices and minimum standards for humanitarian settings. INTERSOS will strengthen GBV prevention and risk mitigation across its interventions through mainstreaming GBV in Education, WASH and Health sectors.

Needs

In recent times, the political, security and economic situation in South Sudan has deteriorated with a corresponding increase in violence, a significantly worsened humanitarian situation and massive violations of women and child rights. According to UNDP (2017), the current conflict has made South Sudan a truly dangerous place to be a woman or a girl. Gender-based violence - including rape, sexual assault, domestic violence, forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, abduction, discriminatory practices within the legal system and harmful traditional practices – is a persistent and serious problem in South Sudan. (South Sudan GBV Subcluster, 2017). A 2016 analysis by UNICEF documented serious gender inequalities, rooted in traditional social norms. Roughly 41 per cent of survey respondents reported experiencing gender-based violence, within the past year (UNICEF, 2018). Gender based violence (GBV) is grossly underreported due to fear of retaliation and social stigma. Prevailing social norms, gender roles, national conflict, a highly militarized environment, and the frequent cattle raiding and revenge killings among the different clans and tribes exacerbate the risk of GBV in South Sudan. Protection concerns such as generalized violence and insecurity, access to food, water and firewood, expose women to a high risk of GBV. Unity State, particularly central and southern Unity continue to experience significant protection violations which exacerbate humanitarian needs. According to Intersos monitoring report of the first quarter of 2018, 67% of the respondents reported that their girl child dropped out of school. About 52% reported that girls that dropped out of school were assisting in the household chores and, While 22% reported that their girl child married early. Significant number of respondents (22%) said that girls who were not in school were idle. The reason given by the majority for not sending children to school were the needed for them to work (50%), schools were not reachable in the area in the area (21%), and the schools were far (10%). Another challenge reported was the security concern (16%) particularly in Akobo which is a hotspot for revenge killing incidents and clan fights. Conflict in Jonglei State has deep roots. Historically, Nuer, Dinka and Murle pastoralists all participated in cyclical cattle raiding and child abduction. Cattle are central not only to all three communities livelihoods but also to their social and cultural systems. Conflict and violence often disrupt agricultural activities. As a result, food insecurity has increased dramatically across much of the state, with particularly severe effects in Akobo and Pibor. Food insecurity and poverty are the major drivers of early marriage Due to poverty, parents often allow their daughters to get married. Idleness and lack of proper school functioning to engage young girls in studies makes them very vulnerable to early marriage. Child marriage is also a key concern affecting adolescent girls living on the islands. From participant observation, nearly all girls are married at the age of 15 or much earlier. A focused group discussion with adolescent girls indicated that parents from these communities send their daughters to live in the islands so as to get married to men believed to be wealthier in terms of cows.

Activities

Firstly, INTERSOS will carry out rapid protection assessments (including risk assessments) to determine the factors that drive GBV and to enable proper planning of GBV interventions in each of the target areas. INTERSOS will aim to provide comprehensive case management services for women and girls survivors of GBV but will pay special attention to manifestations of GBV across genders. INTERSOS will also provide, through the Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS), a safe and confidential environment in which they will access psychosocial support services in form of peer-to-peer support, individual case management and recreational activities. All activities will focus on overcoming trauma and psychosocial distress by strengthening their individual coping mechanism, resilience and social network. INTERSOS will deploy Protection Monitoring Officers, and Community Mobilizers who will work with community-based protection mechanisms to identify cases, and refer them to INTERSOS trained GBV Caseworkers. INTERSOS will establish and support referral mechanisms for provision of key services by local service providers. Furthermore, INTERSOS will continue to support the recovery and economic empowerment of women through IGA and livelihood for protective purposes. A selected group of women that is interested in starting their own business will attend regular business skills training and be assisted in developing a business plan. In order to confront social norms that drive GBV, INTERSOS will specifically focus on engaging men through its programming to become change agents for shifting gender roles. The discussion around gender and social norms will be facilitated in regular workshops, including influential community leaders to reflect on masculinity and the reasons behind their violence towards women, gender, sexuality, and substance abuse. In addition, INTERSOS will conduct awareness campaigns addressing specific problems affecting girls such as early and forced marriage and the low inclusion of girls in school. There will be specific awareness campaigns in primary and secondary schools for the promotion of girls’ enrolment and to reduce the drop out from schools. INTERSOS will also implement community engagements aimed at promoting peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence. INTERSOS has played an important role in strengthening coordination among humanitarian actors present in its target areas. INTERSOS in Bor is co-chairing the GBV Sub Cluster and is strengthening coordination with the GBV Working Group in Akobo, as well as coordinating with the Protection Working Group in Lankien, which is led by INTERSOS. In 2019, INTERSOS will also take over leadership of the GBV subcluster in Jonglei State. The coordination is deemed fundamental to elaborating a common strategy for the GBV sector, including partners’ joint activities and to prevent duplication or overlapping. INTERSOS GBV interventions will be aligned with the GBV Sub-cluster Strategy. To effectively respond to GBV, INTERSOS will collaborate with strategic partners to contribute to the provision of the basic package of multi-sectoral GBV services at community level and build capacity of service providers and communities to deliver quality GBV services in line with best practices and minimum standards for humanitarian settings. INTERSOS will strengthen GBV prevention and risk mitigation across its interventions through mainstreaming GBV in Education, WASH and Health sectors.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Through community engagement meetings, awareness and social mobilisation campaigns.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context specific and tailored for gender and age groups. INTERSOS uses facilitation guides for children, adolescents and women which are age appropriate for facilitating sessions with children in the Women and Girls Friendly Spaces. Specific tailored tools are also used during community engagement and awareness raising activities.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

INTERSOS staff who come into contact with beneficiaries sign a Prevention of SEA Code of Conduct to ensure that they prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of project beneficiaries. Internal trainings are also conducted to refresh staff on their obligations to prevent SEA by timely reporting cases.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Consultations were conducted at community level through focus group discussions, key informant interviews and observation. The consultations covered men, women, boys and girls as well as traditional and local leaders.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

INTERSOS ensures that complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place during the implementation period. This is achieved through the installation of a mobile complaint box in each location where INTERSOS deploys as well as regular FGDs with beneficiaries about INTERSOS service delivery to understand how they are perceived and what can be improved. Furthermore, there will be monthly staff meetings to understand the community feedback on existing services and additional needs identified by those who closely work with members of the community. INTERSOS will use its internal monitoring and evaluation and control project appraisal tool (PAT) as well as field visits to ensure accountability and guarantee corrective measures are taken timely whenever necessary. The PSS intervention will follow a flexible work-plan, which is constantly revised upon collection of beneficiaries’ inputs. During case management, all case management principles will be respected, particularly the confidentiality of survivors will be respected at all stages in field locations. Women will be informed about confidentiality and can agree to what extent they wish to give consent about the release of information in case of referrals to third parties. All case files will be kept in a lockable cabinet and no identifiable information will be recorded.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Communities are engaged in the monitoring and evaluation processes through use of participatory methodologies such as focus group discussions, community dialogues and interviews.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project conducts an initial risk analysis as part of the safety audits and needs assessment conducted at the beginning of the project. The risk analysis focuses on protection needs, available services and gaps and allows for determining the needs or special needs groups.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project identifies barriers to access during the needs assessment and also during the routine monitoring process. Major barriers to access identified included stigma and discrimination for survivors of GBV, and gender inequalities. The project will address these barriers through community level advocacy and social mobilisation aimed at reducing stigma and discrimination and empowerment of women to mitigate gender inequalities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project seeks to address gender based inequalities and protection threats through addressing the drivers of gender based violence, particularly social norms, gender roles, community conflicts, exclusion and deprivation. Violence will be addressed through community engagement and provision of services to survivors of GBV.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

To respond to inequalities, INTERSOS will engage communities to promote the rights of women and girls. INTERSOS will also implement a series of empowerment interventions for women and girls through the Youth Friendly Spaces and the Women and Girls Friendly Spaces aimed at increasing women and girls’ resilience against GBV. In that regard, INTERSOS will promote women and girl’s economic empowerment through livelihoods programs for more equitable roles and income generation. Actually, the Life Skills program supported by UNFPA has been highly appreciated by women and girls in Bor and Pibor since 2017. Protection threats will be addressed through awareness campaigns agsinst GBV. These will include door-to-door visits, focus group discussions and community dialogue sessions with the engagement of men and boys.

Access to services will be measured through tracking the number of children provided with GBV services through the GBVMIS and other monitoring processes.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring process is participatory, age and gender specific, ensuring the dignity, confidentiality and protection of beneficiaries.
