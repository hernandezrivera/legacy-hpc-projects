Project Summary

This project will reduce child protection concerns including violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation by improving the access of at-risk girls and boys to responsive and preventive services. Those services include the provision of focused and non-focused psychosocial support activities, awareness raising, parenting skills sessions, case management, and individual support, especially for children living with disabilities, through static child friendly spaces and integrated CP activities in safe spaces within schools/learning spaces. In the same way, this project aims to enhance local capacity to provide child protection services through building the capacity of child protection teams and school personnel on Child Protection themes. In addition, the project includes the establishment of local child protection committees/ networks and building their capacity on Child Protection.
The project targets about 18,300 affected girls, boys, women and men in 20 locations in both Ar Raqqa and Deir azzor governorates through child protection services. Syria Relief SR will provide secure and supportive environment. In addition, SR will build on existing structures and capacities within the local communities. The safe spaces will be within or next to the learning spaces. Syria Relief CP teams will implement the child protection activities directly and will receive technical, logistic and financial support from SR field offices in Raqqa and Deir.

Needs

The prolonged conflict in Syria significantly influenced the smallest unit of community in Syria, leading to normalizing negative coping mechanisms affecting the lives of children. Child rights continued to be violated, where thousands of children were killed and injured due to the increasing level of violence. According to Child Protection Needs in Syria (in HNO 2019 Initial Analysis) and An Overview of Children’s Protection Needs in Syria 2018, child labor, family separation, early marriage, child recruitment, death and injuries, harassment, and sexual violence were the most common coping mechanisms followed, during conflict, risking the lives of boys and girls. According to a rapid need assessment conducted by SR in June 2018, there is a continuing need to provide child protection services, where there are thousands of boys and girls, living at risk of different types of protection concerns, including lack/loss of personal documents, child labor, early marriage domestic violence, harassment, and the lack of psychosocial support services. Moreover, SR Case Workers in the current SR Child Protection Program have identified tens of most vulnerable children including children who have got physical disabilities that have been preventing them to access into different CP services. Overall, those risks affected the safety and well-being of girls, boys, women, and men in all areas of their lives from their homes to the wider community. Syria Relief proposes to provide CP services based onidentified needs, gaps in the proposed governments in NES are Ar Raqqa and Deir Azoor, with the aim of reducing CP concerns, through continuous support to 20 safe spaces within/next to 20 learning spaces. The girls and boys will be protected from violence and will do well socially and emotionally. Finally, it is very important to integrate the education activities with PSS since there are a lot of children who have psychological issues like trauma and these issues have prevented them to attend regularly in the schools.

Implementation of activities

Syria Relief will provide support to safe spaces within schools/TLSs and static child friendly spaces which will carry out structured, non-focused, interactive and supervised, SR will building life skills and resilience to protect children, Life skills and spiritual development can build children’s capacity to make good decisions, influence their peers and environment positively, and build resilience to difficult situations. - Providing quality support to vulnerable families because positive parenting supports to their children will also create an environment that promotes a child’s healthy physical, cognitive, social and emotional growth, providing them with critical protection in vulnerable situations.psycho-social activities for children from 6 to 14 years old. Those activities will be conducted directly by trained Syria Relief PSS workers. Those spaces will be a community hub where parents, caregivers, children, as well as other service providers can gather and provide targeted services. SR will rehabilitate those Safe Spaces and CFSs in the first implementation period. Rehabilitation will include fixing the infrastructure of the spaces including WASH facilities, and painting. In addition, SR will make sure those spaces are accessible by children living with disabilities. The activities that will be conducted in the SSs and CFSs include the provision of “I Deal” curriculum for Psycho-social Support activities and life skills, play and recreational activities for children; awareness raising on preventing violence and separation; how to recognize and avoid unexploded ordinance. The spaces will be managed technically and administratively by SR CFS supervisors. Syria Relief will be utilizing the teams operating the child friendly spaces, to provide community awareness raising sessions for parents, community members, etc., on child protection related concerns including but not limited to child labor, early marriage, school dropout, lack of personal documentation, domestic violence, and risks of explosive remnants of war. Syria Relief will provide parents/caregivers with parenting sessions dedicated to provide them with the skills they need to interact with their children, communication skills, problem-solving and decision making, non-violence discipline strategies, psychosocial support skills, children’s rights, children’s concerns, and positive parenting. Moreover, Syria Relief will support establishing child protection community committees/ networks. The community committees will inform members about available services and enabling access by liaising with relevant actions. Syria Relief will work with local communities organizing child protection awareness events with children and adults. These community committees will support the prevention and response mechanisms for children at risks in targeted locations. SR will be working with school personnel, community leaders and community members, local councils and other service providers, etc., to establish and strengthen the capacity of these committees in each location. Additionally, Syria Relief will identify and support cases identified through its caseworkers or referred through the committees and existing mechanisms in the communities and will address the most prevalent CP concerns including early marriage, child labour, psychosocial/psychological problems, school dropping out, violence, abuse and neglect as well as related child protection in emergencies, including family tracing and reunification for separated and unaccompanied children. This will allow Syria Relief to directly implement case management in the targeted location. In terms of capacity building, Syria Relief aims to strengthen the Child Protection Intervention, through conducting capacity building of frontline service providers including school personnel and CFSs personnel, strengthening the coordination among different sectors in child protection, and raising awareness.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Girls, boys, women and men’s physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated armed parties; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access, and lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure during and directly after service provision.Mitigation 1: Consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed-site service provision. Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (route and location) by boys, girls, women and men, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical, visual/hearing deficits, and psychological issues). Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community.Risk 2: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups.Mitigation 2: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what servicesare available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for Code of Conduct and PSEA

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: the security situation is stable and helps to deliver the project services. Risks: Security situation deteriorates (airstrikes, shelling, inter-fighting between NSAGs) and control over the area of operation changes which might cause a delay in implementing the activities: Mitigation: SR may suspend some activities till the situation is stable. The staff is trained in and adhere to security protocols. SR employs program and monitoring stafffrom the area so they can move more freely. Strong community support will be maintained. If the control changes, Syria Relief will negotiate access for other areas.Considering the need of CP services for children across northern Syria, the service provided can be easily targeted to another location and SR may move to CP mobile units instead of static CFSs. All Syria Relief’s offices and field personnel implementing the activities have emergency contacts and receive updates on the security situation. SR informs donors and partners of relevant activities and security incidents. Risk: Staff and beneficiaries might be attacked or injured causing attrition or limits to the implementation of activities. Mitigation: All the activities that Syria Relief implements are based on the skills and competencies of the field staff inside Syria along with strengthened support from Senior staff based in Turkey. Access: Syria Relief maintains a good relationship with the communities it operates in allowing access and movement of staff from and to project location easily. Coordination: Syria Relief coordinates its project activities with NGOs, through UN-led clusters as well as through direct coordination with NGOs and local authorities. Assumption: Case Management teams are well trained on Case Management procedures and tools. Risk: risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequencesto girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of lowquality Mitigation: SR will take the informed consent from the parents to include their children in Case Management service. Caseworkers will be trained on communicationskills, CP SOPs related to case management, and PFA.
