Project Summary

Structural gender inequality and unequal power relationships between men and women are the root causes of GBV. Gender equality efforts and women’s empowerment are at the heart of preventing and addressing gender-based violence. Primary GBV prevention programs will be implemented to address harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality and power imbalances. Essential GBV actions will be integrated and undertaken across multiple clusters to save lives and mitigate GBV risks. GBV response interventions will be also implemented in a coordinated manner to ensure the provision lifesaving specialized GBV response services.
This project will provide integrated GBV mobile and static services through an integrated approach with Health in remote and hard to reach locations in Longechuk, Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut Counties of Upper Nile State, where static facilities are not accessible. A target of 22,857 (men 1600, Boys 2286, women 9371,Girls 9600) beneficiaries will be reached. Of this, RI is targeting 15% to be people with disabilities (PWDs). RI has been providing life-saving services in UNS since 2006 in Health, Nutrition, Food Security and Livelihoods, Water Sanitation and Health and Protection. Since September 2020, RI piloted an integrated model comprising Medical Mobile Units and Mobile Protection Units to deliver life-saving services in Longechuk and Nasir in Health and Protection/GBV response. So far, RI is running a total of 7 MPU teams out of which 2 are in static locations with funding from BHA however, gaps still exist. Both MMUs and MPUs follow the same schedule and rotation. Despite numerous constraints, lesson learned shows that mobile service delivery is the most appropriate approach to deliver services to communities that have been displaced or are unable to access the static services due to a myriad of barriers and risks that hinder safe access to humanitarian services.
Maiwut is split into two administrative areas, Jekow is IO controlled while Maiwut Centre is controlled by the government. It is important to ensure that both areas receive similar services to manage local political dynamics. A total of 12 MPUs will be formed, 3 in each projection site comprising of 1 static team and 2 mobile teams. GBV prevention priorities will include community engagement, awareness-raising activities on risk factors to GBV, GBV response services, and sensitization on importance of seeking GBV response services within 72 hours of the incidents happening. RI prevention activities will include primary prevention interventions such as Engaging Men in Accountable Practice (EMAP), SASA! and Community Cares programming that transform harmful social norms contributing to gender inequality. The GBV risk mitigation interventions will prioritize integrating GBV risk mitigation and survivor support across different sectors, dignity kit programming, distribution of fuel-efficient stoves, and conducting safety audits. RI’s GBV response priority interventions include providing psychosocial support, GBV case management services, establishing and strengthening GBV referral systems, and implementing Women and Girls safe spaces and providing capacity building training to frontline service providers. RI is committed to providing equal employment opportunities to all qualified people, regardless of race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, age, disability, marital status, national origin, military status, gender identity, pregnancy, or any other basis. Considering the local context, RI will, where possible, recruit female staff to ensure privacy for beneficiaries during service provision, collect sex and age disaggregated data to assess utilization of services, and strive to achieve a gender balance in its capacity building opportunities. Gender appropriate services will be mainstreamed across all RI Sectors.

1 - Needs

Gender-based violence remains pervasive in South Sudan due to structural gender inequality and unequal gender power relationships, women and girls and especially those who are internally displaced or living near cantonment sites, or with a disability continue to suffer from GBV. The population in need (PiN) for GBV interventions increased by 25% in 2022 compared to 2021, an estimated 2.6 million people faced GBV risks in 2022. This demonstrates an increased need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response services. The increased need for GBV interventions has been demonstrated by an alarming reports of rape incidents from different parts of the country especially following the flood and conflict affected hot spot areas. Further, Children in need (CIN)in 2022 increased by 25% compared to 2021. Increased reports of children recruitment, family separation, and children survivors of GBV require expansion of CP services. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 contributed to a rise in GBV incidents, coupled with gaps in response services which necessitates an urgent need for strengthened primary GBV prevention, integration of GBV risk mitigation in other sectors, and scaling up GBV response services, according to HNO. Upper Nile continues to experience multiple shocks in addition to protracted humanitarian crisis experienced in South Sudan, further deepening the levels of vulnerabilities. Relief International is among few humanitarian organizations operating in UNS-in IO Counties of Longechuk, Nasir, Maiwut, Ulang, Maiwut Jekow as well as inMaban since 2006. In 2020, RI piloted mobile and static GBV services in Longechuk and Nasir through an integrated approach with Health (Mobile Medical Units) and GBV (Mobile Protection Units (MPUs) to provide services in remote locations where centralized static services are not feasible. An MPU comprises of 4 core GBV staff namely: MPU team Leader, two GBV/PSS Caseworkers and local protection mobiliser- Volunteer. Each MPU covers between two and three locations a week and follows the same schedule as RI’s Mobile Medical Units (MMUs), enabling RI to provide tailored, comprehensive support to GBV survivors that addresses health, psychosocial, and protection needs. To better understand the needs and gaps, RI conducted a baseline assessment in Longechuk and Nasir, in September 2020, and a rapid assessment in Maiwut in February, 2021 and an endline assessment in March 2022. These assessments affirmed the prevalent of GBV in the community among other protection concerns which exacerbates the situation for women and girls. Additionally, huge gaps were identified in the provision of CP services for children residing in all three proposed counties. Some factors were noted to hinder survivor’s access to services-such as while travelling to access health services, collecting firewood or accessing markets. Violence within domestic setting is also prevalent and most perpetrators are male family members questioning the safe of women and girls at home. Gaps in service provision were; limited access to GBV specialized case management and PSS services including lack of safe spaces where women and girls can safely seek support, services and information. Generally, there are few partners operating in Sobat corridor and access to services is a major gap. There is need to strengthen GBV response and prevention programming across UNS to continue providing services to vulnerable populations in hard-to reach areas through mobile response. RI will replicate lessons learnt from piloting the integrated approach in Longechuk and Nasir by forming additional 3 Mobile Protection Units (1static and 2 mobile teams) in Longechuk, Nasir, Maiwut and Jekow) to work alongside MMUs to complement BHA by expanding the scope of MPU services to areas not covered by the MMUs, and integrate CP into this approach to ensure the needs of children are alsomet.

2 - Project activities

Strengthening GBV case management and referral pathways: Comprehensive assessment on health, safety, psychosocial, Legal and other relevant needs and determine a course of action to address them through an action plan. Training of staff on GBV basic concepts and guiding principles emphasizing on importance of beneficiary confidentiality and data protection. RI will guarantee confidentiality, respect survivor rights, wishes and their dignity when making any decisions on the most appropriate course of action to respond to an incident of GBV. Referrals of survivors who require medical care. Strengthening Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group: Culturally appropriate individual and group PSS services to women and girl survivors of GBV and those at risk to foster their physical and mental well-being. The activities will be tailored for women and girls separately. A rapid assessment will be conduct with women, men, boys, girls and PWDs to establish reactional activities and skills needed by different cohorts. Protection team will work closely with MHPSS working group strengthen referral for specialized PSS cases. Strengthening GBV case management and referral pathways: RI will establish and/or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support; RI will participate in service mapping exercise to review and update existing referral pathways and development of one where none is available. Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group Strengthening GBV awareness and risk mitigation: RI will establish Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment. Establishment of 3 static WGFS in designated locations in Nasir, Longechuk and Maiwut Counties (Jekwo and Maiwut). Temporary safe spaces will be identified through a mapping exercise with the community and in consultation with women and girls. Two temporary safe spaces will be established in sites not supported by the static WGFS.Strengthening GBV awareness interventions and risk mitigation measures Awareness creation on GBV basic concepts, services available, and dissemination of key messages and referral pathways within MPU catchment areas. Protection Mobilizers will support the mobile team in disseminating GBV messages, mobilizing the community and will act as focal points in identifying and referring cases to the community as well as supporting survivors get re-integrated back to the community. RI will use community engagement strategies including EMAP and SASA! to engage men and boys in GBV prevention awareness raising. RI will also conduct regular multisectoral safety audits to establish protection concerns for men women, women, boys and PWD. Dignity kit distribution and programming as part of GBV risk mitigation: Distribution of 5,000 dignity kits in Longechuk, Nasir, Maiwut and Jekow. The contents will be per UNFPA guidelines. Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted to get feedback on their usefulness and any proposals for improvement /changes. Fuel efficient stoves and charcoal briquette making: 100 women trained on production of Fuel Efficient Stoves (disaggregated by age) as part of GBV risk mitigation measure. 132 women and girls trained across project sites in making fuel efficient stove and charcoal briquettes and those trained will receive fuel efficient stoves. Capacity building trainings to GBV actors and frontline service providers: 100 staff/frontline workers trained on GBV minimum Standards, GBV Guiding principles basic concepts. Case workers will be trained on Case management and PSS. Frontline health workers will be trained on clinical management of rape and PFA RI staff/volunteers will be trained on the 16 GBV minimum standards and the guidelines distributed to ensure all GBV activities are aligned to the set standards.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project is designed to meet the needs of some of the most marginalized and vulnerable members of communities in Upper Nile - women, girls, boys, men, and PWDs survivors of GBV. RI's Protection Team and MEAL team will have equal representation of both sexes, to ensure that women/girls feel comfortable sharing their feedback. RI's feedback mechanisms for this cohort are based on an underlying assumption that most beneficiaries will be Illiterate. As such RI has embedded multiple layers of verbal feedback including monthly FGDs and KIIs, intake and exit interviews, and weekly monitoring by RI's Protection coordinator, Senior Management and MEAL team, to ensure that all inclusive feedback is obtain throughout the lifecycle.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

RI will conduct community sensitization and mobilization prior to start of project, through consultations with community members and leaders, both formally and informally. RI’s will conduct baseline interviews with community members, particularly women and girls and PWD – but also men and boys. This will enhance acceptance of the project and proposed activities as a whole and will be crucial to the success of the project during implementation. Project introductory meetings will be held in all project areas and will include an explanation of the entitlements under this project as well as how the community members can access the Complaints, Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) in order to give feedback and make complaints. Responses to the beneficiaries will be managed within the CFRM loop to ensure that all feedback and complaints are dealt with internally within RI or referred/ redirected to partners who can assist to resolve. Moreover; the RI staff and local authority staff will be trained on how to orient, collect, record beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints received, as well as how and when to respond. All staff will be trained on core data protection and confidentiality principals as well to ensure that client data is kept confidential.RI will use the beneficiaries’ feedbacks to shape and adapt into project design and implementation. RI will conduct post distribution monitoring to get beneficiaries feedback on beneficiaries’ satisfaction on content, quantity and quality of the dignity kits and will change / adjust the kit content, quantity and quality based on the feedback. Ongoing monthly FGDs with community members to gain feedback on project activities will be conducted by RIs trained MEAL team. RI's response to feedback will be shared back with community leader. Action plans will be developed with RI's project team and where appropriate actions will be followed up to improve the implementation of the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All RI staff and volunteers who are based in the four locations will be trained on RI's PSEA and safeguarding policies, and ways to report incidents. Each RI field office has safeguarding Focal point who are given more intensive training on RI's safeguarding and PSEA approach. During project kick-off meetings with communities RI will inform communities of the PSEA policy, who the safeguarding Focal Point are, what constitutes PSEA, emphasize that all project activities are delivered free of charge and that beneficiaries should not be asked to accept anything in exchange for any goods or services. RI will place posters and visuals outlining RI's policy and where community members can report at distribution sites. Additionally, RI will have a help desk manned by RI staff at each distribution site, to collect complaints and feedback and to oversee distributions. Finally, RI's independent MEAL team and project management staff from Juba will conduct ad-hoc monitoring visits to project sites, meeting with community members and will reiterate the training and RI's position on PSEA. RI has engaged National Safe guarding specialist to receive and report safeguarding incidences to Global support office as well as support investigation/ fact findings on safeguarding incident reported as well as training all staff and communities on PSEA, child protection policy and code of conduct.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RI will remain adaptable and factor in feedback into the implementation and will use lessons learned from RI’s previous and ongoing projects to continuously improve on service delivery. RI will use the beneficiaries’ feedback to shape and adapt the project design and implementation. RI will undertake Project kickoff and close meetings with Community Leaders, and women leaders, local authorities, Monthly FGDs with disaggregated cohorts of the target population, baseline and end line surveys- in-depth interviews with sample beneficiaries, Interview beneficiaries to document case studies. PDM for dignity kit distributions to get beneficiaries feedback on satisfaction levels for the services they receive. RI will then address feedback received and adapt its programme to address any concerns or gaps highlighted. Additionally, RI's MEAL team will conduct quarterly FGDs with community members to gain feedback on RI's GBV response project activities. During the monthly consultation meetings, RI will then provide feedback to the community leaders and beneficiaries on any changes made to the project based on feedback received. Where necessary action plans will be developed with RI's project team and actions points followed up to improve the implementation of the project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

8.1 Community engagement and sensitisation will be done through out the programming as well as safety audits to ensure that beneficiaries are protected from possible risks during distributions. Where necessary RI will have volunteers and staff doing crowd control.8.2 All RI staff and volunteers who are based in the four locations are trained on RI's PSEA and safeguarding policies, and ways to report incidents. Each RI field office has safeguarding champions who are given more intensive training on RI's safeguarding and PSEA approach. A safeguarding focal point based in Juba has been recruited to support PSEA risk mitigation.8.4 This protection intervention will work closely with the health, nutrition and WASH program to ensure that all beneficiaries are fully aware of the referral pathways and have full access to GBV response services. The CMR services offered under the RI run health interventions will also be vital in supporting this aspect of programming.8.5/8.6 RI will have in place mechanisms ( FCM) to ensure that all vulnerable groups participate in the design, implementation and monitoring of the project and have adequate space to give feedback through multiple channels. Community engagement and consultation will be done throughout the program.8.7 RI staff and community workers are trained on data privacy and will be reminded on the importance of confidentiality of beneficiary data.8.8 RI will adhere to the 'Do no Harm' approach in this intervention and ensure that the program is conflict sensitive. Relying on the community acceptance RI will continue to engage positively with the local administration to enlist their support for the program thereby reducing the possibility of external interference.
