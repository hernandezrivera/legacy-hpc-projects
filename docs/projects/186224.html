Project Summary

This project will bridge life-threatening gaps and bring health care closer to hard-to-reach populations through the mobile clinics. At present, there is only a single health facility functional in this area, and a mobile clinic will allow for primary health care, antenatal care and malnutrition services to be available to those who otherwise would not have access. Mobile clinics, composed of one doctor, 3 nurses, a driver and an access officer will offer free primary health care to the conflict affected population of Mbonge.
Since the beginning of the crisis, there have been disease outbreaks in the area including measles, monkey pox, and cholera. However, there was no alert from within Mbonge health district due to a break down in the facility based surveillance system upon with the health district depends. The mobile clinics will offer an opportunity to sensitize the population on outbreak prone diseases as well as on family planning, GBV, HIV, STIs and non-communicable diseases, as well as carrying out direct surveillance.
Summary of Activities:
1) Access mediation
Introductory meetings will take place at community level to ensure acceptance for Reach Out’s activities and respect for neutrality and safety during outreach clinics.
2) Medical outreach through mobile clinics
A mobile clinic will be set up in the targeted health district and conduct curative consultations within displaced and affected communities on a weekly basis. The mobile clinic will provide essential medical services including antenatal care, malaria treatment, non-communicable disease management, sexual and reproductive health and malnutrition services as well as surveillance for outbreak prone diseases. In addition they will ensure continued access to ARVs for people living with HIV, deliver psychosocial first aid and treat physical trauma. A total of 11,000 curative consultations will be done in 12 months with 1 mobile clinic. 100 Women identified in there 3rd trimester, they will be given a delivery kit in order to reduce the cost of the delivery.
3) Outbreak surveillance and response
In addition, active searches for outbreak prone diseases by the mobile clinic and passively by community members sensitized on epidemic prone diseases by using a hotline to report any strange health events of epidemic potential. Any reported outbreak prone disease will be investigated by the mobile clinic, in collaboration with the district, and any necessary intervention will occur.
4) Sensitization campaigns
Sensitization on outbreak prone diseases, HIV, family planning, GBV and non-communicable diseases will be carried out by the team at each mobile clinic site. 20,000 people will be sensitized.
5) Malnutrition screening and case-management
Mobile clinics will conduct MUAC screening on all children who attend the clinic aged 6 – 59 months. MAM cases will receive nutritional counselling and SAM cases will be treated as an outpatient or referred. A total of 1000 children will be screened for malnutrition.
6) Accountability to affected populations (AAP)
Communities will be empowered to provide oversight of services being rendered to them. A hotline will be set up and the number shared with all beneficiaries as well as a quarter heads and traditional leaders. This will receive complaints and feedback from community members.
7) GBV reporting
The hotline for AAP will double as a GBV hotline. Communities will be sensitised on GBV and encouraged to contact the hotline for support if needed. These will then be treated and referred via the GBV sub-cluster.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The Anglophone crisis that started in 2016 as political struggle for equality and justice has spiraled into a full-blown humanitarian crisis with forced displacements in the affected areas. Most displaced persons reside in remote forests and farmlands where they have no access to basic health care. Several risk factors, which promote transmittable diseases work in synergy during displacement, with significant negative impacts on public health and the well-being of these populations. Displacement to remote forest and hard to reach areas has created a gap for affected communities to access essential health services and weakened the epidemiological surveillance system, increasing overall mortality and the likelihood of epidemics in these areas. Since 2019, there have been epidemics of Measles, monkey pox, and cholera (twice) in Ekondo-Titi and Mbonge. Although interventions in 2019, 2020, and 2021 have touched Ekondo-Titi, Kumba, Muyuka, or Konye significantly, Mbonge remained neglected. Mbonge Sub-Division is a conflict zone with daily confrontations between Non-State Armed Groups and the state military. For this reason, data regarding healthcare and GBV is limited coming from this health district.Reach Out has worked with survivors of GBV from Mbonge, including children, and women who were ganged-raped by armed groups. About 40% of the female adult population is illiterate, as a result of the prioritization of male education in the past. This conflict has further put women, young girls, children and people with disabilities at a much vulnerable position of harassment, coerced sex, rape and abuse. Young girls of school going age getting unwanted pregnancies from rape and coerced sex and are not economically able to pay for transportation to the nearest clinic for antennal consultations. More so, with the bad nature of the roads and closure of most community clinics, persons living with disabilities are unable to access health care when in need. In addition, people living with HIV have limited access to care. The same is true for people suffering from chronic diseases such as high blood pressure, diabetes, kidney failure and respiratory problems.
