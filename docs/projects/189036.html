Project Summary

Given the current state of flooding in Rubkona County and much of Unity State, many families and households have been displaced, instability and fear within communities has settled, and increasingly smaller spaces of dry land are available to accommodate the competing priorities. Under such conditions, Mercy Corps proposes to leverage school infrastructure and institutional standing to enhance quality and inclusive education while expanding child protective factors of schools through intentional interventions and mainstreaming.
Mercy Corps will work to improve the equitable access to safe and protective education and community-based child protection services for 34,427 school-age children (18,049 boys and 16,378 girls), and raise the awareness and capacity of their parents and caregivers, teachers and school personnel, and other frontline and community workers who currently live in the Bentiu IDP Camp (data provided by IOM DTM population count, January 2022).
Under the Education arm of the proposed project, Mercy Corps will support schools in enrolment of school-age children while continuing to accelerate enrolment and attendance of female pupils and children living with disabilities. To better ensure that this, Mercy Corps will work with school and community leadership to identify key barriers to education and work to remove them through targeted back to learning campaigns and events while also making schools safer and more accessible through rehabilitation of TLS blocks and school-based WASH services. Based on prior activities and community feedback, Mercy Corps will procure and distribute age-appropriate teaching and learning material, ECD kits, and recreational equipment to school locations to benefit learning and wellness outcomes. Mercy Corps will train teachers on pedagogy and inclusive teaching methodologies as well as child wellbeing and child protection mainstreaming. Mercy Corps will work with P8 school leavers through life skills and intermediary vocational development. Finally, Mercy Corps will work with school leadership structures, community partners, and education officials to strengthen emergency response capacity and mitigate the impact of emergencies on learners, teachers and communities.
Under the Child Protection arm of the proposed project, Mercy Corps will seek to strengthen community-based child protection case management and referral services while continuing to increase engagement and capacity at the school level to identify and direct possible cases and through updated Bentiu IDP Camp referral pathways for child protective—and other protection—services. Mercy Corps will also continue to scale up Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services in schools to ensure school spaces are protected and child friendly. Mercy Corps will work with school leadership to fit schools for multi-purpose functionality as child friendly and recreational spaces outside of the school day and as centers to enable children to enjoy the fullness of childhood. As such, Mercy Corps will tap into the resources of PTA committee members and community leaders to train them on CPiE and activate awareness campaigns and events to highlight the need for MHPSS and child wellbeing. Mercy Corps will similarly train select teachers and other frontline workers in MHPSS focused and non-focused approaches to enact low-level interventions at the school and community level while referring or elevating more specialized cases to appropriate partners. Working with local community members, Mercy Corps will continue to explore and support the revitalization of traditional games and activities to compliment the organization’s evidence based and tested MHPSS and life skills curriculum for children and adolescents. Mercy Corps will train select staff in case identification and management for children tailored to their specific needs, including the needs of unaccompanied and separated children, or child headed households.

1 - Needs

South Sudan ranks high globally as one of the most vulnerable to climate impact, including flooding. According to the HNO 2022, an estimated 835,000 people were affected by severe flooding, and UNOCHA estimates that 200,000 of those affected are in Rubkona County, roughly 60% school age children. Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return across the state and within Rubkona County, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. The limited land which remains dry lays behind a labyrinth of loose soil dams withstanding water two meters deep. In these conditions of standing floodwater and compromised WASH services, people are at constant risk of an outbreak of infection; In the Bentiu IDP camp, the Hepatitis E virus continues to circulate, particularly amongst children. The situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges (including but not limited to COVID-19), which have a severe impact on access to education and child protection services.According to the HNO, more than 3.5 million people, of whom 2.9 million are children and adolescents, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse and exploitation and need critical child protection services. Exacerbated by flooding, women and girls are exposed to risks within their daily routines; women and girls who rely on gathering firewood to take to market are exposed to potentially dangerous waters, poisonous snakes and other wild animal attacks, and heightened GBV prevalence. Child protection concerns are also heightened and, as static child protective services closed in response to COVID-19, many services, centers, and child friendly spaces have been impacted and/or cut off due to flooding. Increased cases of child drownings across Rubkona have been reported since October 2021 as many activities have become water-based; recreation, household and personal cleaning, and livelihoods activities all now take place within the same waters. Women, children, and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and exposed to risks of exploitation. As communities and households continue to struggle to adapt, many children are vulnerable to meet changing labor demands and generate income to overcome adverse impacts of COVID-19, decreases in humanitarian aid, and now unprecedented flooding. Specifically, girls are further at risk of early marriage for the bride price without consent as a manner of coping for families, and boys are increasingly targeted by local defense groups and other forms of abuses, including abduction and potential trafficking, as political factions grapple to capitalize on potential instabilities. 3.6 million people, 97 per cent children, are estimated to need education services, according to the HNO 2022. Despite the national school re-opening in May 2021, school functionality has been severely impacted by insecurity, especially in Unity State, according to the Education Needs Assessment. The state has been one of the worst impacted by floods as state education officials estimate that more than 190 schools have been closed due to flooding. Under the threat of flooding throughout 2022, school spaces offer potential refuge for displaced individuals. As has been demonstrated in other counties, school shelters interrupt children’s education for months at a time, further threatening the limited child friendly spaces. Flooding, conflict, and food insecurity exacerbate existing barriers, including inadequate school infrastructure, costs associated with accessing quality education, untrained education personnel, and irregular and insufficient teacher payments threatens South Sudan’s commitment of free and compulsory education. The limited provision of education opportunities due to this insecurity and flooding has led to increased cost of operations, a prohibitive cost which disproportionately falls to vulnerable households.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CARM was developed as part of Mercy Corps’ global commitment to accountability to affected populations and the prevention of all forms of exploitation and abuse. CARM is a channel for any and all community members to provide feedback, suggestions, complaints, and concerns, in a manner that is safe, confidential, transparent, and accessible, enabling Mercy Corps to respond and make any necessary programmatic or safeguarding adaptations and to ensure the safety, security, and empowerment of program participants.Each Mercy Corps Country or equivalent will designate a country CARM focal point, who has overall responsibility for the management and quality of CARM for the Country or equivalent and who is responsible for leading the design and implementation of its CARM. These responsibilities will be reflected in the team member’s position description. The CARM country focal point is encouraged to sit on the Country Senior Management Team to help ensure the continued prioritization of CARM. Each program will designate a program CARM focal point, who will be responsible for reporting any identified gaps in the program’s CARM to the country CARM focal point and the program manager. Every program is required to have a minimum of three feedback channels to collect unsolicited feedback within 90 days of program start-up (90 days from the first day of the grant agreement): 1. Face-to-face feedback; 2. A channel that allows feedback to be submitted anonymously; and, 3. An additional context-appropriate channel. All CARM feedback, regardless of whether it is received via an official CARM feedback channel or if it is received via a different channel, must be documented in a CARM database. Teams can adapt the available CARM database template as needed. Access to the database will be restricted to the team members who are responsible for documenting feedback in order to protect the identity of informants. The required CARM data protection protocols outlined in the CARM SOP will be followed. View the CARM data storage and protection guidance for additional information on CARM databases and secure use.After a Grade is assigned to feedback, appropriate action must be taken based on that Grade. See the taking appropriate action guidance for additional information. Appropriate action and segregation of duties will be outlined in the CARM SOP. The person who shared the feedback must be informed of the resolution of their case for 100% of feedback that is not submitted anonymously. If feedback is submitted anonymously, it may be appropriate to report on programmatic changes that resulted from the feedback to the larger community. This minimum standard applies to all feedback Grades, and the HQ Ethics team will provide guidance on how to best close the feedback loop for Grade 5 and 6 feedback. View the closing the feedback loop guidance for more information.

In determining which feedback channels are context-appropriate for a specific program, communities must be engaged to ensure that channels are chosen in an inclusive manner, and that they are accessible to people of different age, sex, ability, and people from minority or marginalized groups.Inclusive sensitization is conducted to inform community members of the feedback channels available to them and their purpose. Community members must be informed of the availability and purpose of feedback channels in an appropriate and inclusive way, which can be achieved through community sensitization. Community sensitization should relay key messages about the availability of individual feedback channels and how the CARM works as a whole. In order to ensure that community sensitization is inclusive, teams will work to identify who the minority, majority, and marginalized groups are, and where these individuals are located. It’s also important to learn where and how each of thee groups receive new information and communication.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Mercy Corps is committed to ensuring that all individuals we come into contact with through our work, whether Team Members, community members, Program Participants or others, are treated with respect and dignity. We expect Team Members, Visitors and Partners to put the well-being and best interests of its Program Participants as the highest priority, to treat all Program Participants and host community members with respect and dignity, and to never engage in conduct that could be perceived as abusive, exploitative or harassing. We expect management and all Team Members to fully foster a culture that supports this standard. We will not tolerate sexual exploitation, abuse, or harassment by our Team Members, Visitors or anyone else receiving Mercy Corps resources. We are committed to the core principles regarding prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse laid out by the United Nations Secretary General in 2003 and Mercy Corps is a signatory to the “Statement of Commitment on Eliminating Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by UN and non-UN Personnel.” This policy sets forth our expectations of personal and professional behavior related to sexual exploitation and/or abuse of Program Participants and community members and the required procedures to ensure we uphold our commitment to preventing such conduct.Mercy Corps ensures compliance with this policy through global HR teams working with field teams and through appropriate processes and procedures designed to ensure to the extent possible that the required procedures are in place across all teams and programs. All potential new Team Members undergo screening designed to ensure that they have not previously engaged in sexual exploitation, abuse, or harassment. All Team Members are informed and trained on what conduct this policy requires (via code of conduct training) and their responsibilities under this Policy, including what sexual exploitation and abuse are and how to report them, and all Team Members certify that they have understood this policy and agree to abide by it. All Mercy Corps offices display awareness posters on this policy, including all available reporting mechanisms, translated into the primary language of the office and in conspicuous locations where all Team Members will see them. Each country office has a designated focal point who can act as a local resource for team members, Program Participants and community members who wish to report or discuss sexual misconduct. For each program, the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse of Program Participants is assessed and reasonable risk mitigation measures are incorporated into the design and operation of the program (a risk-based approach). Program participants and host communities are informed about Mercy Corps’ commitments under this Policy and how to report any suspected violations by Mercy Corps Team Members, Partners or Visitors.Additionally, Mercy Corps has a special obligation to ensure the safety and dignity of all children it comes into contact with through its work. This policy reinforces our commitment to the best interests of children. This policy states Mercy Corps' expectations regarding team member conduct with children and what conduct is prohibited. It also states the process and procedures Mercy Corps will maintain to ensure that team members uphold its standards and how cases of misconduct are identified, reported, and addressed in an appropriate and timely manner.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Under the proposed project, Mercy Corps will work with camp management (IOM CCCM), camp leadership (Community High Committee), and camp administration (Bentiu IDP Camp RRC Office) to raise awareness amongst key community and camp stakeholders to disseminate project goals, objectives, and activities. This will also ensure that Mercy Corps receives critical feedback from these partners at initial stages of project implementation to onboard any feedback or concerns regarding the intended interventions and supports. Additionally, Mercy Corps will continue to work closely with line ministries, namely the State Ministry of Gender Child and Social Welfare and the State Ministry of General Education and Instruction. Mercy Corps maintains a strong working relationship, built up over the years, with school personnel and leadership in the community, including the teachers, school management committee members, and parent teacher association representatives. Mercy Corps will also continue to build on recent success in intentionally partnering and coordinating with community-based organizations, the Community with Disabilities Committee (CDC) as one example, to ensure that information is being shared in channels where they may have previously been missed. Finally, as a actively engaged organization, Mercy Corps maintains regular engagement with cluster, inter-cluster, and joint-cluster partnerships in order to amplify messages, planning, and interventions, particularly in the emergency context of flooding. By coordinating with partners in Education, Health, Protection, and the like, key communication and messages will better reach community members, direct beneficiaries, end users, and others associated to the project, its goals and objectives, and activities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Mercy Corps fully recognizes the context and risks surrounding its programs in South Sudan. Diversion of aid intended to reach the civilian population is always a risk, whether that diversion be to a sanctioned group or due to any form of corruption. Over many years of working in high-risk environments, Mercy Corps has honed and continues to hone its system of formal and informal controls and means of verifying that its resources are not diverted and its programs have the maximum impact possible. This document, the policies and procedures it summarizes, as well as related communication with donors and donor representatives are considered confidential information to Mercy Corps. Their disclosure to the general public could jeopardize the perception of Mercy Corps as an impartial humanitarian aid organization and affect the safety and security of Mercy Corps staff. Accordingly, Mercy Corps requests that this document be kept confidential and not released without Mercy Corps’ consent.Mercy Corps dedicates significant effort to developing systems for monitoring and ensuring that its programs in South Sudan achieve their intended goals and do not result in any form of direct or indirect support to sanctioned groups or individuals. Our system in South Sudan is based on Mercy Corps’ standard, global policies and procedures which were developed based on research about all available systems for due diligence, significant sharing and cross-checking with peer organizations, as well as consultation with subject matter experts around the world.Mercy Corps assesses the context and risks in any geographical area where it operates or plans to operate. During its mission, Mercy Corps continuously monitors the security situation and the context and analyzes the political power players in the area. Based on the analysis Mercy Corps identifies the risks to programs, its programs participants, staff, assets and resources.Mercy Corps South Sudan is supported by the Global and Regional Security Advisor and the Donor Compliance Department who guide the local team to undertake regular security assessments of our operating areas, paying special attention to how the ongoing or potential hostilities affect the areas of operation. Based on risk assessment, mitigation measures are built to ensure that our activities do not put our program participants at risk or provide support to sanctioned groups or individuals. Mercy Corps also establishes and maintains a robust network with communities and local stakeholders. This approach enables Mercy Corps to quickly receive information, analyze and take action and/or adjust programs and staff movements in order to minimize threat impacts.Mercy Corps uses a community acceptance strategy to both ensure the safety of Mercy Corps staff and to mitigate the risk of diversion of aid intended for program participants. Mercy Corps is transparent with the communities where it works, sharing its operational modalities and humanitarian principles with community members. Mercy Corps also creates a stable link to communities and local authorities through consultations during staff recruitment, in this way building trust and ensuring acceptance. Mercy Corps is well known for being an impartial and neutral organization and communities and other partners are aware that any aid diversion in their area may potentially lead to suspension of the support. Our partners, staff and volunteers are carefully selected before we begin implementation, and by having a strong understanding of the dynamics and context in these communities, the humanitarian nature of Mercy Corps activities are clearly understood by the communities. Mercy Corps will not directly engage in negotiations with any sanctioned groups or individuals unless it is purely for humanitarian access, as defined by International Humanitarian Law. Mercy Corps staff are educated on these principles and are also instructed to inform senior management of any such interaction
