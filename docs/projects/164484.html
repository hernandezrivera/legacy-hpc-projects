Project Summary

Nine years into the crisis, the food security situation in Syria in 2020 has deteriorated compared to 2019. The number of food insecure people has increased from 6.5 million (2019) to 7.9 million (2020); approximately a 20% increase with an additional 1.9 million people at risk of food insecurity. AAH Food Security and Livelihood project is a combination of food assistance, food security and livelihoods support. The main target group for the provision of supplementary food items as a one-off action include recently displaced households and returnees; the priority target will be pregnant or lactating women and children under five, elderly and people with special needs in terms of nutrition and care. A second target group are host communities, which often have similar needs. Beneficiaries will receive food parcels or Ready to Eat Rations. Food aid will be delivered in collaboration with SARC and/or other local partners. In order to reduce dependence on food assistance, host communities will be specifically targeted by activities aimed at improving their food security and/or strengthening their livelihood opportunities. AAH will place increased attention on longer-term drivers of vulnerability and capitalize on opportunities to rejuvenate social and economic dynamics, while continuing to focus on humanitarian needs. Based on needs identified, the distribution of FSL inputs (including seeds, seedlings, tools, fertilizer, livestock fodder, irrigation equipment) together with technical capacity building and pilot projects on novel agricultural techniques will be implemented to improve the household food security status and/or support the restoration of previous Income Generating Activities or the establishment of a new initiative to achieve sustainable livelihoods. In order to mitigate protection risks and to ensure safe programming, beneﬁciary selection committees will be established, including female representation – this body will be prepared relating to beneficiary selection criteria and the selection process. Mechanisms to receive feedback from the population will be promoted and all distributions planned to take place close to beneficiary locations; beneficiaries will be also timely informed about the planned date and time for distributions. The livestock sector is a significant livelihood activity – especially in areas where there is limited rainfall and poor land quality. The predominant focus for the livestock sector will, therefore, be the provision of fodder (as feed barley, high-protein pellets or grazing) to retain females of reproductive age within the flock, ensure ideal reproductive levels and prevent animal deaths by ensuring the animals are fed an adequate diet. Access to veterinary services will be promoted to reduce animal mortality and avoid the rapid spread of disease (distribution of para-vet kits); coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform (MAAR) will also be conducted to support the ministry to implement a livestock vaccination. As complementary component, access to water for irrigation of field crops will be increased by rehabilitation of existing irrigation. AAH will target the most vulnerable households based on a set of defined selection criteria. Priority will be given to women headed households, families with children under 5, PLWs and elderly family members. The deﬁnitive areas of intervention has been deﬁned based on needs assessments.
Baseline and endline surveys of specific activities will enable AAH to assess the degree of change associated with the project. Regular onsite monitoring will be conducted: Post Distribution Monitoring of inputs provided; FGD to assess in quantity and quality of the inputs; market prices monitoring; post-harvest monitoring.
AAH will ensure that relevant age/sex groups are considered with all data sex disaggregated throughout the project cycle from assessment and implementation to monitoring and evaluation.

4 - Needs

Nine years into the crisis, the food security situation in Syria in 2020 has deteriorated compared to 2019. The number of food insecure people has increased from 6.5 million (2019) to 7.9 million (2020). The national average price of a standard reference food basket is USD 77 (more than 8 times higher than pre-crisis levels). Food insecurity in Syria is defined by loss and lack of livelihood opportunities contributing to high poverty levels, and reduced production capacity hampering financial and physical access to food. Many community members (in particular the more vulnerable - including female headed households) are not in a position to earn a minimum income to address their families’ basic needs. Although all population groups have been affected by the protracted crisis, children under 5 and PLWs are facing the highest risk in terms of deteriorated physical and mental well-being as a result of inadequate food and poor dietary diversity. According to AAH assessments, HHs are resorting to harmful coping strategies; the most common of which are: relying on humanitarian assistance, renting their own land as they are unable to cultivate it themselves, multiple jobs (Rural Damascus), decreasing the amount of food consumed per meal or per day, reducing the amount of food consumed by an adult in order to allow the children to eat (Dar’a); purchasing food on credit, borrowing from relatives or other families, selling animals or assets, excluding some food groups, taking their children out of school so they can provide another source of income (Aleppo and Hassakeh). Approximately 216,000 hectares of wheat and other crops such as barley, fruit trees, vegetables and grazing areas (17% of national areas planted) were affected by a number of shocks including floods, fires, frost, hailstorms, high temperatures, plant diseases and pests. The governorates estimated to have the highest levels of food insecurity are Aleppo, Idleb, Hassakeh, Deir ez Zor, and Dar’a.

5 - Implementation of activities

AAH Food Security and Livelihood project is a combination of food assistance, food security and livelihoods support. Food Assistance (as food parcels or Read to Eat Meals dependent on context) will mainly target pregnant or lactating women and children under five, elderly and people with special needs in terms of nutrition and care. Communities will be specifically targeted by activities aimed at improving their food security and/or strengthening their livelihood opportunities. Based on needs identified, the distribution of FSL inputs to cover winter and spring planting seasons (including vegetable seeds, seedlings, tools, fertilizer, livestock fodder, irrigation equipment) and drought resistant seed wheat planted in a crop-rotation with beans; technical capacity building and pilot projects on novel agricultural techniques will be implemented to improve the household food security status. The income generated will be used to meet household expenditure and potentially to be reinvested in the IGA, thus their dependency on humanitarian aid will be reduced. Protection risks will be mitigated through beneficiary selection committees being fully aware of beneficiary selection criteria. Mechanisms to receive feedback from the population will be promoted. The predominant focus for the livestock sector will be the provision of fodder (as feed barley, high-protein pellets or grazing) to ensure ideal reproductive levels. Access to veterinary services will be promoted to reduce animal mortality and avoid the rapid spread of disease; coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform (MAAR) will also be conducted to support the ministry to implement a livestock vaccination. Access to water for irrigation of field crops will be increased by rehabilitation of existing irrigation systems focusing primarily on infrastructure and channel repair. In collaboration with the partner, technical surveys will be conducted, to assess the impact of the project.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Failure to correctly identify beneﬁciaries in need of humanitarian assistance (community tension arising from the beneficiaries selection). Likelihood: Low, Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Ensure the beneﬁciary selection criteria are widely publicized; Conduct community meetings to clarify the beneﬁciary selection process. Beneﬁciary selection committee members (responsible for beneﬁciary selection) understand the criteria, which they rigorously apply, have female representation. Mechanism established to receive feedback from the population. Protection Risk: Beneﬁciaries not aware of distribution date and location. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Ensure beneﬁciaries who have been selected are advised of the distribution date and location at least 24 hours before the proposed distribution event. Conﬁrm that transport facilities are available for beneﬁciaries to be able to travel. Protection risk: Inappropriate and unsafe distribution location resulting in large crowds, slow distribution process, tension between men and women. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures: Correct identiﬁcation of distribution location to ensure security of beneﬁciaries. Recruit enough trained skilled staff (male and female) to ensure an adequate ﬂow rate of beneﬁciaries through the distribution process; the recruited staff will also respond appropriately to instances of tension or sexual intimidation against female beneﬁciaries. Recruit enough skilled and trained crowd control staff (male and female) to ensure safety of beneﬁciaries. Have separate distribution lines for male and female beneﬁciaries. Provide adequate and gender speciﬁc toilets and refreshment facilities for male and female beneﬁciaries.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: security situation allows safe response; access to target areas and beneficiaries is granted; partners are willing to collaborate on timely manner; availability of suppliers and items in the market, prices remain stable; no major displacements occur. Risk: security situation is unstable and/or worsens, AAH has no access; there are additional administrative procedures imposed on D-INGOs, and restrictions on access to beneﬁciaries; lengthy processes to obtain approvals. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: AAH coordinates with local authorities, Ministries, and stakeholders to monitor the situation, to maintain access to target areas and beneﬁciaries, to develop relationships and avoid or mitigate restrictions. Security rules and plans are in place and regularly reviewed. Re-planning of activities to recover eventual delays. Advocacy and coordination with the humanitarian community. Risk: significant devaluation of EURO/ USD against SYP; International bank transfers are not ﬂuid and timely. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: signiﬁcant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be monitored and, if materialized, communicated to donors and options discussed. Risk: aid diversion, financial fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: strict policies and procedures in place for procurement, financial management and risk management; internal controls set up. Monthly monitoring of financial spending. Risk: markets and services are not functional for planned deliveries; procurement of equipment/materials are delayed. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: diversify procurement according to AAH guidelines. Launch framework agreements or tenders for medium time frames. Risk: any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc.). Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: compulsory trainings on organizational ethical policies. AAH mission has a policy for PSEA and a reference person against harassment at country level.
