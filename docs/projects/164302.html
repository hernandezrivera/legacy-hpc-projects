Project Summary

According to HNO 2019, the total number of people in need of education services has reached 6 million including 5.9 million children in the age between (5-17) and 0.1 million are teachers from both genders. Male gender numbers have reached 3.2 million and females are2.9 million.
The proposed project aims to provide equitable, quality and protective education services in Northern Syria/ Afrin District for a duration of 12 months, through running and rehabilitating 12 primary schools in Afrin district according to Education cluster approved protocol. Children and teachers with disabilities from both genders will be under a special focus of the project activities in regard to access to schools and accessibilities to education services.
The proposed project aims to apply the following activities:
1.Provision of children and youth with formal education;
2.Provision of non-formal education
3.Provision of Psychosocial Support (PSS) and life-skills;
4.Reparation and improvement of classrooms and schools.
5.Provision of school supplies, teaching and learning supplies, school furniture and textbooks
6.An incentive to volunteer teachers
7.The orientation of teacher and education personnel;
8. Establishing Parent-Teacher Associations.
SKT will apply these services serving 1353 students ( 679 girls and 674 boys), and 60 teachers from both gender through 12 schools located in Afrin district/ Aleppo governorate. The total catchment population will be covered are approximately 58067 according to OCHA population figures for August 2018 (52% male and 48% female) and ( 43% IDPs and 57% host community). Among this population; 7% are people with disabilities.
According to the need assessment done by SKT for the target area, 12 schools need rehabilitation activities including desk and infrastructure in order to expand the capacity of the educational facilities to host more children, provide fair services for both females and males, and children with disabilities and provide better education.
In order to aligned with the academic year, this project will be applied during one cycle of non-formal education for out of school children from June to August and transition these children to formal education with the start of the academic year in September. ASER tests will be conducted to students in order to allow SKT for better planning of educational activities. Distribution of needed educational materials for both teachers and students are covered by these project activities.
Non-formal education is tailored by age and needs in order to fulfill the gaps for each targeted age and gender, to provide literacy and numeracy along with PSS sessions. 1 NEF center in each school to provide Remedial program
PSS and life-skills; SKT will establish one child-friendly space in each school (total 12) to conduct the PSS activities one session a week per class. Ant potential dropped out of students will be followed up by the specialized protection staff and they will try to get them back to schools.
Furthermore, in order to enhance the quality of the education system, building the capacity of 60 volunteering teachers and educational actors on;
1.pedagogy,
2.Modern Teaching Methods
3.DO-NO-HARM and child protection policy principles
The special focus on school protection supervisors and protection coordinators through special training.
Population accountability is also a key role in this project; PTA meetings to make sure a good level of coordination and engagement with parents. SKT will monitor the mentioned above activities and take corrective measures according to PTA feedback.
The proposed project will cover the rehabilitation activities of schools, back-to-learning campaign costs, PSS services, school kits, for both teachers and students. Furthermore, running costs by providing fuel for heating purposes for 5 months and incentives to volunteer teachers.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structuresLikelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigating Measure: Communication with donors to accept flexibility and support the relocation of activities to safer spaces whenever needed.Risk 2: Delay of the printing and timely delivery.Likelihood: MediumImpact: MediumMitigating Measure: Print books with and without logos depending on the location served.Risk 3: Limited number of beneficiaries in the program, which might cause inter-community tension and pressure on implementing staffLikelihood: HighImpact: MediumMitigating Measure: Set a clear cut selection criteria and inform the community of limited resources and the selection criteriaRisk 4: Attacks on schoolsLikelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigating Measure: Training of partners and local teams on Child safety, development of schools specific childRisk_4: Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/ kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, a direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff/students or military use of the school. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: All attacks will be reported to the Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism. If possible, learning facilities would be structurally strengthened to better withstand the type of attack they are most likely to be exposed to. Learning facilities will be provided with safety protocols in place besides safety resources like; walkie-talkies, firefighting equipment, and exit signs.
