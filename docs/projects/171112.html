Project Summary

Somalia Country faces a protracted humanitarian crisis, with more than 2.1 million people acutely food insecure, and 849 900 children under the age of five including 143,400 SAM are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021 (Fsnau Sept 2020). Current high levels of food insecurity are driven by the combined effects of widespread insecurity, severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19, and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks making Somalis increasingly vulnerable to food insecurity and loss of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC3 & IPC4). 2020Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to the average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation. In Hirshabelle State, there are more than 100,000 people in crisis (IPC3) while 19,000 people are in Emergency (IPC4) and most of the riverine communities are under humanitarian emergency, hence this means that riverine communities will be food insecure until the next harvest of Gu season 2021. Decades of conflict have destroyed infrastructure, on which lives, and livelihoods depend, such as water, soil, land, and forest resources, making Hirshabeelle state increasingly vulnerable to recurrent climatic shocks. In the project area of Beletweyne, Buloburte, Jalalaqsi, and Jowhar districts, food security, and nutrition among riverine communities are critical and considered to be hotspots in need of urgent food and nutrition interventions. The Gam and Sam of Shabelle river communities are 16.0 and 2.4respectively,
Restoring the farmland productive capacity of Hirshabeelle state to better prepare for, respond to and recover from shocks is a precursor to improving food security and nutrition in the riverine communities. Besides diversification of crops and the introduction of nutritious crops such as orange-fleshed sweet potatoes (Ipomoea batatas) may alleviate hunger and malnutrition conditions of the riverine communities in Hirshabelle State. This project is therefore aimed at enhancing the capacity of riverine farmers to rebuild their productive assets and reconstruct their capacity to absorb the recurrent climatic shocks by applying crop diversification, soil amendment techniques, and access to food by distributing food vouchers to the needy people in the target areas during the lean time of the growing season. The project provides conditional farm inputs such as seedling of orange-fleshed sweet potato (OFSP), water pumps, and extension services to the affected farmers as well as food vouchers in order to facilitate access food during the lean time. It also provides training on soil fertility amendments and good farming practices for ensuring sustainable food production in riverine livelihood zones of Beletweyne, Buloburte, Jalalaqsi, and jowhar districts. Direct target beneficiaries of project 4500 riverine communities in four target districts and will be provided farm inputs such as orange-fleshed sweet potatoes to selected farmers from the four districts and food vouchers during the lean time. It will also provide crop management training, composting techniques, and water harvesting techniques for enhancing farmland productivity and restoration of farmland ecosystems. SOADO has been conducting trials of a selection of OFSP varieties since 2018 and has gained knowledge of two orange-fleshed sweet potatoes varieties which are ready to be scaled-up, and believe that the scale-up program will increase food production along with the riverine communities and alleviate hunger and malnutrition in under age five children very significantly. The project will support indirectly pastoral communities and Livestock within the target communities will have access to crop residues and pasture of the restored ecosystems. SOADO will address the underlying causes of food insecurity and restoring farmland the productivity of target areas for building the adaptive capacity

Project Activities

1. Restore productive capacity of the riverine community through the provision of farm inputs to 4500 HHs of riverine (seedling of OFSP)to selected farmers in the project target areas (1,125 HHs for each district)2. Train farmers of 450 riverine farmers on OFSP crop management, manure composting & water harvesting techniques for improvement of soil fertility management and ecosystem restoration. 3. Provide single-piston water pumps to 50 HHs in the riverine area of the project target areas.4. Provide 2500 HHs food vouchers during the lean time of the growing season (625 HHs for each district) 5. Set-up small Lab for micro-propagation for ensuring seedling are clean and quickly multiplied
