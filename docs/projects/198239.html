1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project responds to urgent needs of particularly girls and boys and adolescents and will reach at least 21,503 affected people and provide protection services through access to CFS activities, provision of MPHSS, case management, CP information dissemination, MHM support and strengthening CP referral systems, community-based CP mechanisms and formal systems. The project also provides services such as identification, documentation and case management support to UASC, including provision of family-based alternative care and referral for FTR services. PLAN will establish and reinforce internal complaints, reporting and feedback mechanisms by establishing age, gender, and culturally appropriate mechanisms suggested by children, adolescents and community members.The project key target is 21,503 direct beneficiaries in conflict affected children, adolescent boys and girls, women and men in crisis affected communities including IDPs and host community members. The targets are divided by districts per service a) Case Management (Chiure 450, Mecufi 217 ,Montepuez 394, Mueda 311, Erati 220, Meconta 435, Monapo 167)b) MHPSS (Chiure 2,533 , Mecufi 1,222 ,Montepuez 2,216, Mueda 1,747, Erati 1,236, Meconta 2,445, Monapo 942)c) UASC Chiure 23, Mecufi 11 ,Montepuez 20, Mueda 16, Erati 11, Meconta 22, Monapo 8)d) GBV Chiure 1,404, Mecufi 679 ,Montepuez 1,231, Mueda 971, Erati 687, Meconta 1,359, Monapo 523)

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Chiure, Montepeuz, Mecufi and Meconta, Erati Monapo districts in IDP and host communities shall have similar activities. Focus will be on identifying and addressing child protection risks of unaccompanied and separated children, children associated with armed groups or armed forces, conflict related sexual violence and GBV, and the dire need of MHPSS for children, adolescents and caregivers• Provision psychosocial activities for children in child friendly spaces (CFS) and other safe spaces. Project will establish and equip CFS in target communities to ensure girls and boys are provided psychosocial interventions. CFS animators will be identified and trained from the community to manage age- and gender-appropriate activities in the spaces. Plan International will also work on building the capacity of community structures to provide Mental Health and Psychosocial support. Synergies will be created with like minded organizations like The Regional Psychosocial Support initiative on the use of tools that have been proven to be useful in providing PSS including MHPSS. The target group will also include youth, caregivers and young mothers. Apart from this Plan International will support the functionality of the multipurpose centers also observing disability issues. Sessions will also be conducted my trained mentors in the safe spaces • Provision of quality case management and support UASC; including identification, documentation and FTR, provided by trained Caseworkers to vulnerable children including UASC, CAAFAG and child survivors of SGBV. Alternative care will be provided through identification and training of foster families and Caregivers also following the Ministry of Gender Guidelines including other work that has been done by the partners in Cabo Delgado. To ensure quality on the interventions, Plan International will invest time to build the skills and competencies of community volunteers to support informal care arrangements. Priority during implementation will be provided to new displacements. • Establishment of and strengthening community-based CP mechanisms. The project will map existing community support structures in target communities and where necessary establish and build capacity of community-based child protection committees in collaboration with relevant government departments. The committees will be supported to led on prevention of and response to child protection concerns. • Strengthening capacity of formal CP systems to effectively prevent and respond to child protection concerns and needs. The project will work with the SDSMAS and CP District Reference Groups to ensure capacity strengthening of Social workers in CPiE. Plan will also be involved in the response to CAFAAG issues in collaboration with other partners. • Dissemination of protection information to children, adolescents, parents and other community members on CP concerns implemented through community awareness sessions, meetings, during CFS activities with children, child’s clubs in schools and community radio programming as well as the Protection Focal Persons. • Provision of MHM support to vulnerable adolescent girls and young women in IDP settlements and host communities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project aims to strengthen protective environment and promote access to supported services for children and adolescents (girls and boys) affected by the crisis, prevent SGBV and provide appropriate gender and age support to child survivors of SGBV, neglect and exploitation. Girls and boys become vulnerable particularly when in humanitarian crisis like the risk of GBV, SEA, violence and child labour is exacerbated. To prevent these, the project will include a series of child protection activities, ensuring that crisis affected girls and boys will access psycho-social support and referral responses, including gender-based violence and comprehensive CP case management in order to prevent and respond to protection risks in the affected population Furthermore, the project will conduct a child-centered baseline risk mapping and ensure child protection concerns are seeing from children’s perspectives and ensure meaningful child participation according with the CP Principles and CPMS. The mapping will also take into consideration existing CP structures and community resources that support and address children’s issues. Results will feel into the project to improve implementation and performance. Capacity development of children’s groups and adolescents will help enhance their meaningful participation in their own protection and ensure they lead on protection activities in their respective communities. Plan International is committed to combating Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. In regards, protocols on PSEA are set up within the organisations. Recruitments of personnel are well vetted and candidates are scrutinized to ensure they are not risk to children and communities they serve. Orientation and training is provided on PSEA and staff signed the PSEA code of conduct. PSEA focal points are identified, trained and available in all field offices and projects at field level. SEA reporting mechanisms are in place and are safe and confidential. Reporting can be made through focal points, to designated personnel, to regional or headquarters. Specific numbers and channels are in place to ensure this. Community awareness and sensitization on SEA are carried out by all projects implemented by PLAN in target communities’ populations. Also, training to partners and counterparts include PSEA policy as component part to ensure that the risk of sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse of women and children is acknowledged combated. PLAN has in place modalities to provide immediate and appropriate assistance to survivors of PSEA based on their specific needs. PlAN works closely with survivor and their family to ensure this, respecting their safety, dignity, choices, confidentiality and rights.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project design is based on an analysis of different identified barriers for girls and boys, women and men using the IASC Gender & Age Marker tool. PLAN also integrates considerations of power distribution between women, men, girls and boys, as well as dependent factors in the development of this project PLAN will engage with boys and men, focusing on changing dominant masculinities and promoting girls’ empowerment through a Gender Transformative approach ensuring community and stakeholders’ participation throughout the project. We aim to have girls make up 60% of the beneficiaries, ensuring gender sensitive community-based child protection mechanisms, integrating local community members as part of monitoring. Specific responses are developed to address obstacles faced by girls in claiming their rights. Adolescent girls will be consulted to ensure that child protection activities and access to services respond to their specific needs. For case management services, for example, girls will be supported by female case workers and boys will have a choice based on who they feel most comfortable with. In training sessions, awareness and psychosocial activities with girls and boys, gender roles and dynamics will be considered, while at the same time being cognisant as not to reinforce harmful gender norms.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

PLAN is committed to disability inclusion in all its programming. This project is also designed to include all children including child with disabilities. PLAN makes explicit the need to include children with disabilities and promotes children with disabilities to be afforded equal access to protection activities; upholding the principles of non-discrimination, participation and inclusion rights. PLAN will work to include children with disabilities in baseline mapping, project activities design, implementation and monitoring and will seek to identifiy and address the barriers children with disabilities facing to participate in project activities in order to increase access to activities and services. Project staff will be provided orientation and training on disability inclusion so that they consciously adopt inclusive principles at outset of project implementation, so that their personal attitudes and practices are more aligned with humanitarian principles, inclusion principles and child protection standards. Consultation with children with disabilities will be critical and the project team will adopt activities that tend to empower and increase children with disabilities inclusion in project activities, i.e. attention to access issues-CFS locations, training venues, sites for PSS support activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The majority of the project will be a person to person intervention with minimum environmental impact. To ensure there is limited waste produced by the project, reusable supplies will be purchased whenever possible and limited consumables, particularly single use plastic goods, will be purchased throughout the activities. For the construction of child friendly spaces, environment assessment will be conducted to ensure that materials used for CFS construction do not result into contributing to deforestation of local communities. Basic community friendly materials shall be use in construction child friendly spaces. Most staff of the project and volunteers will be recruited locally and in proximity communities in which they will work, to minimize the number of times vehicles are plying in IDP settlement centers and host communities.
