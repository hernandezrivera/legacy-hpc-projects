Project Summary

In 2020, Samaritan’s Purse (SP) will implement Nutrition programming under the modality of Institutional Feeding Programs (IFP) in Maban County, Greater Upper Nile State, reaching a total of 1,608 individuals as well as a Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) in Pariang Country, Unity/Ruweng State reaching 1,189 individuals. The assumed approach is deemed to provide an immediate reduction in the malnutrition rates and help stabilize food security among the severely food insecure HHs who are chronically ill. Such programming will also help mitigate conflict-overstrained resources that are currently shared with the HH, thus also positively addressing gender and protection issues.

1 - Needs

A total of 1.5 million South Sudanese were estimated to require emergency nutrition support in 2019, with approximately 0.9 million children under the age of five (U5) anticipated to be acutely malnourished and “in need of life-saving services”. Aproximatey 1.2 million children U5 in South Sudan are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2020. According to the IPC Acute Malnutrition projection analysis of August 2019, 39% of the country will be facing Crisis, IPC Phase 3. This includes Upper Nile State that estimates 845,000 people to experience Crisis. Earlier 2019 IPC classifications projected that Maban County would be under Crisis (Phase 3) classification by July 2019. In Unity State, only 12.5% of children aged 6-23 months met the recommended Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) while the Minimum Meal Frequency was at 32.6%. Overall, the major drivers of acute malnutrition across South Sudan include poor dietary intake, critical food insecurity and conflict; in addition to high disease burden, and poor childcare and feeding practices, which are aggravated by reduced access to quality health and nutrition services in the country. Overall the 2019 nutrition situation is worse compared to that of the same period in 2018 for the lean season.

2 - Activities

In Maban County, activities include IFP, which serves the moderately acute malnourished, HIV, TB, and Kala-azar patients within Bunj Hospital. In Pariang County, TSFP programming will serve the moderately acute malnourished children aged 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women. They will also receive nutrition counseling to further improve their nutritional status.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Samaritan’s Purse works closely with the other non-governmental organizations (NGOs) in the area under the umbrella of the United Nations (UN) Cluster System to ensure that there is no geographic overlap in programming and that the most urgent needs of the population in each locality are being met appropriately. Throughout the project, SP will have representation at all levels of WFP coordination meetings, including camp coordination meetings, camp sectoral meetings, and bi-weekly multi-cluster coordination meetings, as well as monthly Health and Nutrition Cluster meetings, to ensure emerging issues in the humanitarian response are addressed in a timely manner. At the field level, SP will work closely with WFP and other partners to ensure that there is no duplication of efforts. In addition, SP will coordinate with the community and government leadership, to guarantee the involvement of all relevant stakeholders.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?
6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Generally, the security condition in South Sudan is improving and the Revitalized Peace Agreement has resulted in a calm atmosphere in the country. However, due to sporadic communal conflict, security remains a concern throughout the project implementation period. Samaritan´s Purse will work closely with the local administration and the community to ensure the safety of the warehouses and the food commodities stored in them. Samaritan´s Purse has also put in place comprehensive security management procedures that guide security operation plans for all moderate to high-risk operation areas.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The nutrition programs in the proposed areas is designed to be a temporary intervention and focuses on emergency response. It is anticipated, however, that the prevalence of GAM will remain high within the project locations, as long as the people are displaced and cannot grow food due to persistence of the conflict. The lack of systems in place to strengthen FSL is a fueling factor for food insecurity which is followed by malnutrition. Other factors like seasonal malaria and diarrhea and an unsafe and inadequate water supply will continue to exacerbate levels of malnutrition. Thus, SP intends to continue working in South Sudan beyond the life of this project to ensure that suffering is alleviated and resilience is built in these vulnerable regions. The project will build the capacity of local staff to enable them to carry out these activities in the event that an evacuation of international field staff is required. Samaritan’s Purse will also work closely with the nutrition partners in the county to strengthen referral links for the moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases found during community screening.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.14: Samaritan’s Purse has designed this project to use a conflict-sensitive lens developed from lessons learned from the previous programming so as to incorporate a Do-No-Harm approach. By combining facility-based and outreach site services, beneficiaries will be able to receive adequate nutrition services so as to avoid potential tensions between populations with and without easy access to health care facilities. Samaritan’s Purse will also ensure staff, partners, and communities involved in the project are aware of and take into consideration actions that have the potential to cause conflicts within and between community and social groups. All implementing staff will be trained in the Do-No-Harm approach that will enable them to perceive dividers and connectors within communities that can serve to harm or help in situations of conflict. Understanding the unique challenges and opportunities in each target area will allow staff to avoid negative impacts and emphasize peace-building initiatives.8.15: Samaritan’s Purse is committed to the core humanitarian standards (CHS) of accountability, which empower beneficiaries to provide feedback on program deliverables and ways of working. To encourage routine feedback from the beneficiaries on services provided and to improve accountability to conflict-affected populations, regular meetings will be held between SP and community leaders in the target camps, as well as with special interest groups such as women. Feedback received in these meetings will inform SP’s operations by quickly identifying and addressing issues. Samaritan’s Purse will work with existing settlement complaints mechanisms to ensure that any distribution-related queries are addressed as soon as possible. 8.17: Nutrition centers will be located at points close to the participating HHs, in order to minimize walking time to the centers and traveling after dark, thereby reducing vulnerability.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Gender-sensitive complaints desks will be established at every nutrition service delivery site; beneficiaries and staff will be encouraged to make use of these desks to report any PSEA related concerns in addition to other issues that affect their rights and entitlements. Professional program team members and senior management in the locations of intervention and within the Country Office will handle reported cases to ensure that confidentiality is maintained and safety of the affected persons assured. Also, staff and beneficiaries will be enlightened on other available external reporting channels such as the WFP hotline (hotline@wfp.org/ +390665133663) and the South Sudan PSEA Taskforce reporting line (pseass@unfpa.org/ 0912100012/ 0920111333/888), while emphasizing the need to observe the existing communication policy within SP.
