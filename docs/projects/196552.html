Project Summary

For 2023, UNHCR will continue to support returnees’ areas, enhancing resiliency, and early recovery for the targeted HHs through applying approaches that are strongly aligned with the Sector Objective 1, Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria.
The project aims to assist 490,000 individual / 98,000 households disaggregated as following:
Women (Over 18 years) 142,100, Men (Over 18 years) 122,500, Boys (0-17 years) 122,500, Girls (0-17 years) 102,900
mainly in the areas where shelter assistance provided. To ensure that area-based return support approach has been applied providing integrated assistance to the People of Concern.
Due consideration will be given to ensure that WASH facilities are equally accessible to both returnees and affected host communities. Through provided support to target populations the project will reduce the number of persons exposed to the risks of displacement, reduce risks of GBV and PSEA. For community engagement and consultations on project implementation, various participatory methods including FGDs, and community/household visits will be used to consult with PoC.
UNHCR works on raising awareness among UNHCR and Partners’ staff on PSEA, through basic PSEA trainings organized by UNHCR periodically.
UNHCR is working to enhance capacity for the country and field offices to provide regular and timely inputs to the WASH sector 4Ws. Since UNHCR is working through partnership modality to implement most of WASH interventions, partners will be strongly encouraged to provide inputs to the WASH sector 4Ws.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, power crisis dramatically affecting water supply systems functionality, there is a need to support water supply systems with alternative energies. WASH infrastructure requires significant support and investments, water supply systems and water networks are damaged and in need of rehabilitation, 52% of the population are relying on unsafe, uncontrolled alternatives to piped water. Investment in sanitation infrastructure is critical, as at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional and in needs for rehabilitation or maintenance, increasing public health risks, including the new rise of AWD/cholera, malnutrition, and COVID-19. Over 30% of garbage is inappropriately disposed or collected with low frequency. Since UNHCR is supporting voluntary return, almost all locations at the sub-district /community level (even locations with a low severity score) considered in HRP 2023, that is attributed to the unpredicted dynamic of the potential return movement that would changing the needs accordingly. Following are the locations where UNHCR is prioritizing in 2023: Aleppo, Al Bab - Menbej - Jabal Saman- As-safira for water/sewage networks rehabilitation. Lattakia, Al Haffa, Maysaloun for water supply system rehabilitation. Lattakia "Salma-Almrej or kfareeh" for SWBs. Rural Damascus for sewage system & boreholes rehabilitation. Further locations might be considered based on the needs and fund availability.

4 - Implementation of activities

To support areas that showed spontaneous return, UNHCR will work with partners on implementing a simple WASH programme in returnees’ areas according to the needs and where shelter assistance will be provided. This programme will include replacing broken pipes, providing manhole covers and enhance capacity of sanitation systems. Increase water supply by rehabilitating boreholes and solarizing them where possible.Improve solid waste management systems by providing the necessary materials.Coordination will be maintained with all actors to performintegrated projects to support shelter objectives in coordination with the local authoritiesand the WASH Sector.For 2023, comprehensively, UNHCR will support a total of 240,000 persons of concern through thedistribution of around 1,000 solid waste bins and the repair of 13,000 linear meters ofsewage networks in addition to support 20 communities with storm water systems.250,000 persons will benefit from interventions to improveaccess to water through the rehabilitation of damaged water networks and repair water boreholes in addition to support them with solar power where possible. Post Intervention Monitoring (PIM) which aims to ensure a high quality of implementation as well as measuring the satisfactory level of the beneficiaries will be applied to all WASH implemented projects in 2023. In parallel, UNHCRs’ partners are highly encouraged to regularly conduct monitoring and evaluation for the implemented projects.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network to ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to mandatory trainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the following year.During implementation of WASH activities, UNHCR will ensure that all partners/UNHCR staff on the ground will be consisted of female and male while dealing with the beneficiaries. All WASH contracts which will be under direct implementation of UNHCR will include the ‘’Code of Conduct’’, similarly to the UNHCR partners’ contracts. GBV Safety Audit and Risk Migration Workshop will take place in January 2023 to train all UNHCR staff working on WASH activities. UNHCR partners are committed to conduct briefing and or training for volunteers on the purpose of the assessment and how to communicate clearly and honestly and how to approach the beneficiaries.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation, such as price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply. Mitigating measures: Early release of resources to partners to allow for more time for implementation. Flexibilities in planning and implementation in response to market changes. Risk 2: Delay in providing WASH services for people in need due to the lengthy approval processes. Mitigating measures: close coordination with relevant governmental authorities, follow up with UNHCR partners for early submission of projects’ approvals, consider approvals’ potential delay within projects’ design, clear selection criteria focusing on the most vulnerable groups and areas with high WASH severity scale. Risk 3: Escalation of cholera cases due to poor sanitation. MMs: Close coordination with WASH sector partners and Health actors, focusing on sewage system rehabilitation in areas of lower sewer connection rate (Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor, Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo). Risk 4: Current power supply crisis which effect the functionality of rehabilitated water supply systems. MMs: Solarization of water boreholes where possible.
