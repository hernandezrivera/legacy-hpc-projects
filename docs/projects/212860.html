Project Summary

Within this framework, the intervention aims to consolidate and expand the strategies implemented in the last years, on one side expanding the health waste management (HWM) system to health facilities that are still not involved and continuing the work initiated for the safe disposal of expired pharmaceuticals, and on the other side ensuring the capacity building of the local authorities involved – Department of Local Administration and Environment, Municipalities, Department of Health, Committees of Health – in the target Governorates of Hasakeh and Raqqa to consolidate their role as main actors responsible of the system.
The intervention is developed along the following three axes:
- Strengthening the capacities and means of the local health actors to ensure proper HWM and environmental health standards in the health settings.
Work is done for the provision of materials and training to new health facilities in Hasakeh and Raqqa Governorates in order to involve them in the system. The coordination with the health authorities is strengthened to ensure a proper supervision of the system, and training, coaching and mentoring related to environmental health standards is provided to the local actors.
- Strengthening the capacity and means of municipalities to ensure proper transportation and disposal of medical waste.
Work is done to improve the management of waste transportation and disposal and processes follow up, and municipalities are supported with the provision of equipment and the maintenance of the municipal fleet to improve collection of HW.
- Supporting Local Authorities (LAs) in improving the disposal of expired pharmaceuticals. Technical support is provided to ensure safe disposal of expired pharmaceuticals while a study to assess the feasibility of more sustainable methods is developed. The 2,642,219 inhabitants of Qamishli, Amuda, Derek, Hasakeh, Tell Tamer, Derbasiya, Suwar, Raqqa, Tabqa and Menbij are the right holders, while civil society associations and local NGOs are the responsibility bearers.
while specifically in Kobane district, several water infrastructures – water stations, water towers, water networks – reported damages due to the earthquake, worsening a situation in which Kobane Water Directorate – the local authority in charge – was already facing different challenges in ensuring water access for the population of the area. Different WASH INGOs have mobilized funds to answer to the immediate rehabilitation needs required after the earthquake, but severe needs remain. The NES WASH Working Group highlighted the necessity to support t Kobane Water Directorate in the improvement of the water networks in the area, whose conditions were already critical and in need of maintenance before the earthquake and got even worst after. The same need was raised by Kobane Water Directorate itself, that does not have the equipment and machineries needed to implement such maintenance works (ex. Welding machines for polyethylene pipes, valves, jackhammers, leakages detectors, bobcats, etc.). At the same time, the Water Directorate also highlighted other critical gaps in the provision of safe water, pointing out the needs to work to provide sustainable solutions alternative to water trucking, that result expensive for large part of the population contributing to economic vulnerability. For a detailed list of needs identified by Water Directorate with the involvement of the target population. The proposed intervention aims at building on the existing infrastructure in order to improve access to water for the target population. 0 - Project Summary (extended)

In this context, local institutions seek assistance in the organization of services, technical training sessions and capacity building, in order to continue their programs based on the principles of ecology, environment and health preservation. The DoLAE of Jazeera Region joined several initiatives, including in-person and remote meetings and knowledge exchanges with the European actors, proving to be a solid and engaged institution. In this regard, AMB support and involvement have been extremely valuable, it enabled UPP and KRC to respond quickly to a health waste emergency and set up the first steps for the safe management of health waste in some of the biggest cities in the region. A pivotal component of AMB supported program is the partnership with KRC, which is a well-established NGO with whom UPP has been collaborating on various programs for the past 8 years. KRC is one of the main health care providers in NES, offering emergency, primary and specialized medical care services to IDPs, refugees and local populations in close coordination with the local institutions in charge of public health in the region.moreover, due to the lack of piped water, the population living in the target area currently buys water from private vendors, adding a financial burden on households whose livelihoods are often not enough to cover basic needs. Across all NES, in fact, many families report costs that are bigger then their capacities to create incomes, with 87% of families reporting that monthly incomes are insufficient to meet basic needs (HNAP, Socioeconomic and Shelter Conditions, 2022). Moreover, those families that are not able to cover the costs related to water may depend on uncontrolled, alternative water sources, with potential high heath risks as the current cholera outbreak shows. As for the main difficulties the project might face related to the project implementation team, the following risks were identified: • border closure due to growing political instability, hindering the capacity of international staff to travel to NES and carry out relevant activities in the field;• escalation of the military conflict which might impact the capacity of the staff members to safely move across the regions to implement the activities;• decrease in international funding for UPP-KRC organizations with consequent impact on the capacities of the respective Departments to maintain the current structure and allocate the same resources to the activities;• diversion of the LA’s resources to respond to emergency situations or to cope with the economic crisis, undermining the capacity to consolidate their interventions and further expanding it, both in terms of human resources and equipmentFurthermore, UPP has an internal monitoring system for all its staff for the respect of its fundamental policies, such as anti-fraud, PSEA, anti-corruption, anti-terrorism and Ensure that effective, safe, and accessible feedback and response systems are in place.
