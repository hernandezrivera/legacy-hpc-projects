Project Summary

The project responds to Shelter/NFIs SO3: “Increase capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner” through capacity building of local NGO’s, community committees, municipal forums, and volunteers, by training and tools for emergency response, self-recovery, or protection measures to be able to support IDPs and affected people during and post emergencies and improving urban displacement settings to host IDPs in case of emergency.
The overarching goal of this project is to equip the most vulnerable communities in Gaza to respond to emerging needs and displacement. NRC, will contribute to HRP Strategic Objective 3 to build and increase capacity of five major municipalities through its Urban Displacement Caseload Management package. This training will further be disseminated at community level creating close coordination between the community, municipality and Ministry, for streamlined communication, information sharing and disbursement of urgent resources. This project will be carried out in close coordination with the shelter cluster and it will link municipal fora to the MoSD and humanitarian actors’ community outreach structure. The project consists of two main pillars:
1. Increasing local communities, CBOs and community groups’ capacities to be able to respond to emergencies, including in settings of urban displacement
2. Enabling vulnerable families and IDPs to access proper humanitarian services through municipal fora.
The project will build the capacities of 150 beneficiaries, which include vulnerable households, municipal fora members, CBOs members, and MoSD & municipalities’ staff involved in emergency responses measures and emergency preparedness.
In addition, 400 vulnerable HH living in the surroundings of the designated emergency shelters (DES) at the on-set of an emergency will be provided with multi-purpose cash that will help them supporting the additional burden coming from hosting displaced families arriving from the affected areas.
NRC staff will prioritize the urban displacement guidelines, which is currently being finalized, and will use these guidelines to support training to community focal points and CBOs.

1 - Needs

A mapping on humanitarian services, conducted by NRC in Gaza strip during September 2018, emphasized lack of knowledge among the potential affected communities about emergency services and mitigation measures. Gaps in communication, information sharing and field coordination were identified by displaced and vulnerable population as major barriers for recovery and restoration. NRC has invested in filling these gaps through the introduction of Urban Displacement and Outside Camps “UDOC” approach in the Gaza Strip. UDOC is component of the Camp Management (CM) Core Competency aiming to facilitate the access of the affected communities to protection and basic services. The UDOC team established representation and field coordination structures, municipal fora, and commenced the development of the Urban Displacement Response guidelines and tools early in 2019. These efforts are ongoing, as there are continuous needs to strengthen the established municipal fora, as well as adapt and mainstream the Urban Displacement Response guidelines and tools. Municipal fora are representation structures established through the UDOC intervention by NRC. These fora include youth, women, community leaders, local CBOs, the municipality, and IDPs representatives. Municipal fora have agreed to endorse Termes of Reference (TORs) from the municipality of reference. Municipal fora act as an area-based coordination structure that support the two-way communication between the affected population and stakeholders involved in the humanitarian services. Linking Municipal fora with clusters focal points in different governorates will reflect communities’ needs and priorities in clusters coordinated efforts to respond to these needs and priorities. Municipal fora can also support in mapping the services present in their areas. NRC UDOC succeeded to develop collective centers management tool box and exchanged this experience with UNRWA and MoSD through ToT program for 27 staff members from both main stakeholders who manages designated emergency shelters (DES) during conflict and at times of displacement. More over UDOC established and sustained municipal fora in 17 municipalities in Gaza strip including the big five municipalities as part of community engagement approach adopted by NRC UDOC. NRC UDOC team succeeded to conduct wide range of assessments and IDP’s re-registration exercise, as well development of IEC materials and supported the creation of IDPs registration platform managed by OCHA. More over NRC UDOC in past two years developed small scale emergency response mechanisms in coordination with OCHA and cluster system. The project has available budgets to respond immediately for small shocks such as the flooding happened in early 2020 and escalation in May 2019 as NRC responded to affected families in those incidents. Recent vulnerability assessment conducted by NRC UDOC team for 4300 vulnerable households and concluded in September 2020 showed, 39% report damage to housing unit in past conflicts among them 8% report full damage / destroyed, 89% of HH report inability or partially inability to meet shelter needs, 6% of HH has PWD, 9% of households reported their children exposed to violence and 2% dropout schools and 91% of households have debts. Cash is the most urgent need, followed by work (food in North Gaza), food, debt repayment, household items, and either shelter repairs or healthcare as reported by HHs.In a series of Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) conducted in December 2019 with communities at risk of displacement, participants stated that eastern localities of Gaza strip are more at risk of displacement in case of conflicts’ escalation. In a similar FGD conducted by NRC in October 2019 clearly showed that vulnerable households, when affected by small and medium scale emergency, find refuge in nearby CBOs. This finding clearly shows the linkage and importance of training of CBOs and community focal points in disaster management and response. Moreover, the FGD participants also highlighted the high need for NFI’s and shelter repairs which NRC is proposing to support the multi-purpose cash intervention. This clearly shows that the host community’s ability to receive and accommodate IDPs will be jeopardized in case of displacement. Therefore, community representative structures (municipal fora, CBOs & committees including women & youth) in the receiving areas need to be strengthened through the proposed trainings and cash support to be able to accommodate IDP families and provide adequate referrals. Building the capacities of municipalities and local communities to respond to humanitarian needs during and post emergencies, whether inside collective centers or in urban settings, is a clear need. Communities and local authorities need to better understand humanitarian disaster response mechanisms in Gaza including how to reach the displaced population, services and capacities of the DES and how they can support displaced population in host communities. While there an absolute necessity in light of the escalating risks in Gaza Strip and such instability may lead to a new devastating escalation and the displacement of large numbers of the population, estimated at 600,000 . As the capacities of DESs will not be able to host such numbers, there is a need to increase and improve the capacities of IDPs hosting in communities. More over as per UDOC previous assessments which showed that IDPs who are not in DES usually remains in areas surrounding DESs with host families.

2 - Activities

NRC is currently leading the development of tools and guidelines needed to respond to urban displacement, in close cooperation with MoSD and OCHA. To build up on this expertise, NRC proposes to expand coverage through providing tailored training on the Urban Displacement Caseload Management to the staff of the big 5 municipalities in the Gaza Strip and MoSD staff at governorate level. This training will also be tailored and provided to Municipal fora and community focal points. Afterwards, the trained staff of the big five municipalities and MoSD will be linked with municipal fora, neighborhoods’ committees, CBOs and community groups at different receiving areas. These links will be established through a series of workshops conducted by municipalities with support from NRC and attended by their staff and community representatives. These links will enable municipalities and MoSD staff to easily access and respond to information on the needs of IDPs in urban settings as they can contact community focal points in certain areas. In addition, trainings for local authorities and CBOs will strengthen capacity to support host communities through the provision of awareness sessions and trainings on how to collect and raise vulnerability information during emergencies; finalization of management guidelines for Urban Displaced; and wide dissemination of the developed management toolkit. NRC UDOC is proposing to provide municipal fora and community groups with training on humanitarian disaster response mechanisms in Gaza, including clusters’ coordinated services to identify vulnerable cases and link them to service providers and/or cluster members. These trainings will also jointly target MoSD staff at governorate level in order to link them with their respective communities and CBOs. Activities of knowledge sharing and capacity building will create a roster of community groups that can be easily accessed by the humanitarian actors to seek information about the affected population, or to convey certain messages to the affected communities. UDOC will share this roster with clusters’ members based on their geographical area of work. Trainings will be followed up by awareness sessions to the host communities, where 400 vulnerable households in the potential host communities will be prioritized for multi-purpose cash assistance based on shelter cluster adopted selection criteria with emphasis on socio-economic vulnerabilities, shelter, characteristics of head of household (age, disability, female headed, health etc..) in addition to location of households in the recipient areas of displacement in the five big municipalities. The 265$ one payment multi-purpose cash will provide flexible way for families to cope with hosting of IDPs in their homes, thus it will give them the freedom to meet their most prioritized items or services in such times NRC’s staff will provide coaching and mentoring to ensure that these capacities are utilized and sustained. Additionally, NRC will support the big five municipalities to design and print guidelines and maps illustrating safe shelters and procedures during emergencies. Moreover, focus will be given to areas adjacent to the currently identified DESs, as these neighborhoods will be seen as safe refuge by the affected population and will potentially receive a higher proportion of IDPs. Furthermore, due to the deteriorating socio-economic conditions in the Gaza Strip, it is expected that host communities will be overwhelmed by this influx. Therefore, this intervention will provide host communities with the relevant trainings and MCP to adequately assist the IDPs during emergencies. At the onset of an emergency, the multi-purpose cash assistance will be provided to the receiving families to purchase the needed items, to increase their capacity to host IDPs. Community committees’ members and focal points will be trained on referral pathways, PSEA and humanitarian disaster response mechanisms and such members will be linked with clusters to enhance protection aspects of their communities and displaced population in their areas. This will be done in collaboration with the shelter cluster and communication with communities working group (CWC WG). All activities will be in close coordination with OCHA and support of MoSD.At the last quarter of the project, if there is no large scale emergency, and the multi-purpose cash will not be disbursed to said households, the activity budget will be used within current NRC emergency rapid response mechanisms (RRM) to respond to small scale emergencies such as winterization. NRC RRM will cash support for affected vulnerable families in different areas of Gaza strip with the same amount to enable them to cope emerging needs due to emergency. NRC’s M&E framework ensures that relevant M&E tools are used to meet planned targets with quality. The monitoring tools include an indicator matrix with definition and project M&E plan, post implementation assessments, and post distribution monitoring with targeted households which will provide quantitative and qualitative data. Lessons learnt will be captured in the grants closure meetings and used for informing NRC’s following year interventions and strategy. Project staff, in collaboration with M&E unit, ensures that a tracking and reporting system is in place to document project progress toward desired outcomes. An indicator matrix records data from the field on a monthly basis to measure progress and provide corrective measures where needed. NRC supports quality control measures and accountability towards beneficiaries. In addition, the M&E unit follow up the NRC complaint and feedback mechanism.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The response will be designed together with the targeted households. Specific protection and other special needs will be identified and addressed during the intervention. Field staffs are sufficiently trained in identifying protection concerns of vulnerable households. As part of the baseline/endline assessments, target households will be asked protection-oriented questions to assess their feeling regarding safety and dignity improvement. The self-assessment of the level of safety and dignity is not only asked in relation to shelter as a product but also to shelter as a process e.g. during the event when contracts are signed, shelter/WASH responses are designed, implemented, and monitored. A confidential referral process is in place to ensure support to identified cases encountering protection risks. The strong community involvement and the feedback mechanism in the project will prevent harm in the first place and will allow project management to quickly react in case of concerns of beneficiaries throughout the project duration. The dignity of target households will be also reflected in using the cash modality (when applicable) and a self-help approach that empowers households to resume their capacity in leading the solution to their concerns rather than being considered as a mere recipient of support. NRC respects confidentiality and safeguards personal and other sensitive information, and apply tight protocol when sharing beneficiary’s information or during the referral of protection cases to the related cluster. NRC will train the CBOs on the socio-vulnerability and protection criteria throughout the year, so the response capacity and beneficiary awareness is greater during an emergency response and in particular the multi-purpose cash distribution.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NRC programs are designed to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestinians in hard-to-reach areas have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, to reduce their vulnerability and build resilience. The community engagement at the design and implementation stage of the project will minimize the risk of impartial access to aid, as NRC will define the vulnerable groups in accordance and coordination with all local stakeholders and vulnerable groups (youth, elderly, people with special needs and women representatives) regardless of age, social status and gender.
