Project Summary

Please note:
- duration of the project as submitted to the donor is 39 months, all budgetary figures, targets, beneficiary data given here apply to 3 month, = Jan. - March 2021 (extension phase of the on-going project)
Needs: In an evaluation of studies on mental health in conflict contexts, WHO concludes that at any given time (of the conflict) about 22% of those affected suffer from depression, anxiety, post-traumatic disorders, bipolar disorder or schizophrenia. 13% suffer from mild forms of depression, anxiety and PTSD, 4% from moderate forms and over 5% from severe forms.
In a policy brief, the UN points out that people with serious mental illnesses live 10 to 20 years shorter than the respective average life expectancy of the population.
The Ukraine Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 (HRP 2020) estimates that almost 40% of the inhabitants of the Donetsk and Lugansk regions have had traumatic experiences that can result in stress, depression, anxiety and PTSD. A study by the University of Birmingham in 2018 already concluded that 20.2% of IDPs and 12.2% of the general population have moderately severe or severe anxiety attacks. The prevalence of depression was 25% for IDPs and 14% for the general population.
Against this background, the project contributes to improving the psychological condition of those affected - measurable according to standard psychological indicators. The measures planned for this project focus on individual specialized psychosocial support measures. Further measures will be implemented in the area of community-based psychosocial support/psycho-education as well as further training and supervision of professionals. All services are harmonised with the international standards of the UN Inter-Agency Working Group on Mental Health.
Beneficiaries: It is planned to reach 1530 project beneficiaries in Donetsk and Lugansk oblasts. At least 75% of them live within 20 km on both sides of the contact line. The remaining 25% reside beyond the 20km line and are people in need particularly affected by the conflict (ex-combatants with problems of domestic violence, IDPs etc.). By Washington Group Criteria (long list of questions) people living with (severe) depression, fatigue, PTSD or anxiety are considered persons living with a disability. A further target group are the health workforce in order to protect them from exhaustion and mental distress.
Objectives:
The project contributes to all 3 Health Cluster Objectives.
1) Reduce infectious disease transmission and hospitalization rate by supporting the healthcare system,
2) Improve access of conflict-affected population to essential healthcare services, incl. MHPSS
and
3) Improve capacity, sustainability and quality of healthcare services.

1 - Needs

In a systematic evaluation of studies on mental health in conflict contexts, WHO concludes that at any given time (of the conflict) about 22% of those affected suffer from depression, anxiety, post-traumatic disorders, bipolar disorder or schizophrenia. 13% suffer from mild forms of depression, anxiety and PTSD, 4% from moderate forms and over 5% from severe forms. In a policy brief, the UN points out that people with serious mental illnesses live 10 to 20 years shorter than the respective average life expectancy of the population. The Ukraine Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 (HRP 2020) estimates that almost 40% of the inhabitants of the Donetsk and Luhansk regions have had traumatic experiences that can result in stress, depression, anxiety and PTSD. A study by the University of Birmingham in 2018 already concluded that 20.2% of IDPs and 12.2% of the general population have moderately severe or severe anxiety attacks. The prevalence of depression was 25% for IDPs and 14% for the general population. 16% of IDPs and 8% of the general population suffered from both anxiety and depression at the time of the study. Mental health problems and stress management mechanisms are understood as a continuum, where the categories of ill and healthy do not exist, but rather the variation between light, temporary stress and severe mental illness. The humanitarian needs analysis for HRP 2021 (as of Oct 2020) states that there is an increase in mental stress in Ukraine. (The ability to cope with stress decreases in all age groups, which is due to a variety of factors.) The COVID 19 pandemic is another stress factor that leads to a deterioration in mental health. While the ability to cope with stress decreases in all age groups and among both women and men, external signs of this stress can vary. For example, older people often complain about feelings of isolation. Children are increasingly conspicuous in schools through aggressiveness or lack of concentration. Problems with domestic violence occur more frequently and affect women and children in particular. Men use destructive coping strategies with above-average frequency, such as increased alcohol consumption. They are generally more difficult to motivate to take advantage of psychosocial support services. Women accept direct offers of individual and group therapy more easily. Regarding geographical zones and priority setting, please see section indicators and targets! The needs assessment is based on the following sources: 1) Partner assessments during the current project phase 2020: - Round tables, discussions with and needs assessment of local authorities as well as with civil society (health office, employment office, etc.) - Ongoing cooperation with governmental and non-governmental psychosocial support actors in Donetsk and Luhansk, regular co-ordination at the centers location with all stakeholders, incl. decentral Health Cluster meetings - Individual needs assessment on a client basis (initial questionnaire), which also shows trends/needs cumulatively per centre 2) UN, Cluster & Scientific Surveys: - Humanitarian Needs Analysis and related documents for HRP 2021 - UN Policy Brief: COVID-19 and the Need for Action on Mental Health 2020 - Ukraine Humanitarian Response Plan 2020, ink. Covid19 update - WHO 2019: Prevalence estimates of mental disorders in conflict settings: a systematic review and meta-analysis - REACH: UKRAINE Government Controlled Areas of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts within 20 km of the Line of Contact Analysis of Humanitarian Trends July 2019 (unpublished presentation) - University of Birmingham 2019: The Mental Health of Internally Displaced People and the General Population in Ukraine - Kyiv International Institute of Sociology: Mental health in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts - 2018

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Activity Cluster 1 ➢ Activity 1.1: Group and family services for MHPSS (various formats, incl. first psychological and medical aid) ➢ Activity 1.2.: Individual psychotherapeutic and counseling services (including telephone counselling on Covid19) ➢ Activity 1.3.: Indiv. & group EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitisation and Reprocessing) ➢ Activity 1.4.: Indiv. psychiatric telemedicine ➢ Activity 1.5.: Support to Health Workforce Implementation: 1) Mobile Teams/ Online work due to Covid19: Isolated people and places in the grey zone have so far been mainly reached by mobile teams. The mobile teams usually work together with local contact persons on site (per village). Often these are school directors, teachers and the few available school psychologists, sometimes also doctors or simply particularly committed local representatives. Due to Covid19 , this direct work on site was temporarily switched to online formats. As of April 21 we plan to react flexibly to the Covid19 development. We assume that mixed forms of mobile teams and online/telephone services can be used. In case the infection situation does not allow a continuous use of the mobile teams, they will be active in the spring and summer months and offer outdoor activities (where the infection risks can be managed much easier). Provided that the infection risks continue to exist as "new normal" in autumn 2021, from that time on more online-based activities will be implemented again for the winter months. 2) In the centres telephone and online-based activities will be implemented and other target groups affected by the conflict will be reached directly (e.g. ex-combatants and their families, internally displaced persons and people who have witnessed fighting and are experiencing traumatic consequences). Further the support to the health workforce is led by the centers staff. Composition of activities 1.1. to 1.4.: The choice of the specific methodology depends primarily on the needs of the client. Elderly people often state that they suffer from isolation and are therefore easily reached in group offers; EMDR is often an effective (and one of the fastest) methods to counteract traumatisation; family offers presuppose that several family members are willing to participate; psychiatric telemedicine is only indicated if the psychological professionals come to the conclusion that other methods are not sufficient and need to be accompanied by medication, for example. Activity Cluster 2 ➢ Activity 2.1.: Integrative public events of psychoeducation and promotion of the project offers (subject to COVID19 further development on-site events might have to be dropped). ➢ Activity 2.2.: Information materials on psychoeducation in print and online form (websites and interactive social media of the centres) These community-based activities serve to raise awareness, provide basic knowledge and de-stigmatise mental illness and mental stress reactions, as non-hospitalised treatment of mental illness was not practised in Ukraine under the previous system. Activity Cluster 3 Cross-cutting activities in support of activity cluster 1 and 2 (IASC Standard: general activities to support MHPSS) ➢ Activity 3.1.: Supervisions for lay counsellors and project staff / networking of lay counsellors. Supervision serves the quality assurance of the services provided to project beneficiaries and the maintenance of the manpower of project staff and volunteers (burn-out prevention etc.). ➢ Activity 3.2.: Training measures for lay counsellors, psychosocial professionals, representatives of public authorities and doctors in private practice as potential first contact points for people in need. ➢ Activity 3.3.: Specialist conference for knowledge bundling and networking ➢ Activity 3.4.: Capacity building measures for the partner organisations to support the exit strategy (strategy building workshops, specialist training outside the mental health sector, e.g. on fundraising etc.)
