Project Summary

South Sudan continues to experience the cumulative effects of the prolonged years of conflict, high levels of acute food insecurity, approximately 6.6 million people, or over half of South Sudan’s population (54% of the population), are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity driven by the impact of the economic meltdown, climate-related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells), conflict and insecurity and low agriculture production. Tribal conflicts and cattle rustling are among other factors causing displacement of many people across the Country. Over 25,807 people in Leer & Koch and 9,521 people in Fangak were displaced as a result of conflict among armed groups as of April and August 2022.
Against this background, SPEDP programs a 12 months HRP project aimed at reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality and GBV risks among the displaced and flood-affected populations of Fangak, Pigi Canal, Leer and Koch Counties. The approach is through WASH integrated programming with nutrition, health and mitigation of gender base violence (GBV) risks in order for women and children to access WASH facilities in a safe environment. This project targets 62500 vulnerable people (5905boys-U5yrs, 5448girls-U5yrs, 11315boys-6-17yrs, 10473girls-6-17yrs, 11942men-18-59yrs, 11756women-18-59yrs, 2071men-above 60yrs and 3590women) among IDPs, returnees and host communities in Fangak, Pigi Canal, Leer and Koch Counties with minimum WASH packages.
According to the cluster strategic objectives, the following major activities will be executed: -
I) SPEDP will distribute standard kits to 6,000 SAM children/caretakers and pregnant lactating women (PLW) paired with hygiene promotion (hand, food and domestic hygiene) and water household treatment and safety.
II) SPEDP will drill four (4) new hand pumps; rehabilitate 32 hand pumps and 4 water yards in Fangak, Leer and Koch Counties based on assessed numbers in nutrition facilities and community level after safety audits through a gender and AAP lens. About 16 nutrition facilities will be targeted for the hand pump rehabilitation and construction. These water points will provide 22,000 people with access to safe water supply at walkable distance of 500m and queuing time of 15mins at both nutrition facilities and Community level. Out of the 32 hand pumps rehabs, 10 hand pumps will be rehabilitated with resilient flood design. Steps, ramps, rails etc constructed for easy access for children, elderly or disabled people.
III) SPEDP will construct 5blocks of 5stances and rehabilitate 20blocks of institutional latrines in 16 nutrition facilities in Fangak, Leer and Koch Counties after safety audits through a gender and AAP lens. These latrines will serve 6,300 people with access to safe excreta disposal. Out of the 25 blocks of latrines, 10 blocks rehabilitated and constructed with resilient flood design. The latrines are gender separated according to patient, staff and visitors. Women and other at-risk groups will be involved in the siting, design, construction and maintenance of the facilities. Steps, ramps, rails etc constructed for easy access for children, elderly or disabled people.
IV) Recruitment and training of community health promoters to disseminate key hygiene messages such as the link between disease prevention and how proper hand washing can save lives, and how families can use basic sanitation techniques to reduce sickness and death in conflict and flood settings. About 62,500 people in both nutrition facilities and community who received and adopted good hygiene practices. Install functional handwashing facility at each key area filled with water and soap available to ensure regular hand washing at critical times.
V) Conduct safety audits of WASH facilities through women and girls for suitable siting, design, construction and maintenance of the water and sanitation facilities in order to ensure that women and girls have improved access to water and/or sanitation.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to experience the cumulative effects of the prolonged years of conflict, high levels of acute food insecurity, approximately 6.6 million people, or over half of South Sudan’s population (54% of the population), are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity driven by the impact of the economic meltdown, climate-related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells), conflict, insecurity and low agriculture production. Overall, 2.2 million people are experiencing worse conditions in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and an estimated 61,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Fangakand Canal/Pigiamong of Jonglei State others . In the post-harvest projection period of December 2022 to March 2023, an estimated 6.31 million people (51% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 33,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. An estimated 1.4 million children under the age of five in South Sudan will likely suffer from acute malnutrition over the course of 2023, including 345,000 children who will be severely malnourished and in need of treatment. The acute malnutrition burden will be highly concentrated in the four states of Jonglei,, Unity, Upper Nile Upper Nile and Warrap in, about 61% of the total burden and unlike in the past, not much improvement is expected during the post-harvest season. On another note, tribal conflicts and cattle rustling are among other factors causing displacement of many people across the Country. Over 25,807 people in Leer & Koch as of April 2022 and 9,521 people in Fangak were displaced as a result of conflict among armed groups (August 2022 IRNA report ). Public health challenges which include indirect effects of COVID-19 pandemic and outbreak of Ebola in Uganda is putting the Country in high alert. The felt needs in Fangak, Pigi Canal, Leer and Koch Counties include - limited or no access to clean, and safe water, and low access to latrines resulting to high open defecation levels that pollutes the environment and water sources. Many displaced families are exposed to high rates of domestic violence, harmful coping mechanisms, health related challenges and recurrent adverse climatic conditions. The main activities of the proposed intervention will seek to reduce morbidity and mortality rates, high levels of malnutrition and GBV related issues among the displaced and flood-affected populations of Fangak, Pigi Canal, Leer, Koch and Koch Counties.

2 - Project activities

The key interventions SPEDP will be addressing in Fangak, Pigi Canal, Leer and Koch Counties are as follows:– 1) SPEDP will distribute standard kits to 6,000 SAM children/caretakers and pregnant lactating women (PLW) paired with hygiene promotion (hand, food and domestic hygiene) and water household treatment and safety. The contents of the standard kits include: - One 20l bucket with tap; one 20lt bucket without tap or one jerrycan (10 or 20l); chlorine tablets for 3 months (e.g. Aquatabs 67 mg) – Sedimentation/Disinfection powder for 3 months (e.g. PUR) with one piece of filter clothes and soap, 250 grs per person per month for 3 months obtained from the Core pipeline. 2) SPEDP will drill four (4) new hand pumps; rehabilitate 32 hand pumps and 4 water yards in Fangak, Leer and Koch Counties based on assessed numbers in nutrition facilities and community level after safety audits through a gender and AAP lens. About 16 nutrition facilities will be targeted for the hand pump rehabilitation and construction. These water points will provide 20,000 people with access to safe water supply at walkable distance of 500m and queueing time of 15mins at both nutrition facilities and Community level. Out of the 32 hand pumps rehabs, 10 hand pumps will be rehabilitated with resilient flood design. Steps, ramps, rails etc constructed for easy access for children, elderly or disabled people. 3) SPEDP will construct 2blocks of 5stances and rehabilitate 20blocks of institutional latrines in 16 nutrition facilities in Fangak, Pigi Canal, Leer and Koch Counties after safety audits through a gender and AAP lens. These latrines will serve up to 5,544 people with access to safe excreta disposal. Out of the 22 blocks of latrines, 7 blocks rehabilitated and constructed with resilient flood design. The latrines are gender separated according to patient, staff and visitors. Women and other at-risk groups will be involved in the siting, design, construction and maintenance of the facilities. 4) Recruitment and training of community health promoters to disseminate key hygiene messages such as the link between disease prevention and how proper hand washing can save lives, and how families can use basic sanitation techniques to reduce sickness and death in conflict and flood settings. About 62,500 people in both nutrition facilities and community who received and adopted good hygiene practices. Functional handwashing facility at each key area filled with chlorinated water and soap available to ensure regular hand washing at critical times as a remedy for diarrhea, covid-19 and Ebola Virus Disease (EVD). 5) Conduct safety audits of WASH facilities through women and girls for suitable siting, design, construction and maintenance of the water and sanitation facilities in order to ensure that women and girls have improved access to water and/or sanitation. The criteria for the selection of the sites will be facilitated by SPEDP protection (GBV) female staff.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will be set up and made functional and accessible to the project beneficiaries. The CFRM will deliver a mechanism that enables the beneficiaries and community members to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of this response and other development actors in their communities, in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible. The initiative will promote beneficiary accountability and feedback and response mechanism to other development actors. The project will measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints of most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls). Access barriers could be at following level i.e. within IDP, returnees and host communities and age and sex. SPEDP has deployed beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism and operates complaints desks in the proposed project locations to enable community members to report concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. Beneficiaries will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or raise issues during the provision of services, whereby the hygiene promoters and water point attendants will gather information daily and report to the Project Manager. Additionally, SPEDP will gather complaints and feedback through the weekly FGD. The SPEDP gender focal person will be dedicated to conduct this activity once in a week and work with protection colleagues and the Protection Cluster to provide further support that improves dignity of females. Through its activities, SPEDP will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the project, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols. Complaints and feedback submitted to the project managers will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the project for improvement.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community engagement modalities will continue to form a critical part of all protection interventions, incorporating such approaches as media campaigns, community consultations on feedback, complain boxes, hotlines and institutionalization of complaint and feedback mechanisms. Efforts will be made to ensure safe and accessible reporting on PSEA, quality and accessible survivor assistance and accountability to affected populations (AAP). To demonstrate SPEDP's commitment to AAP mechanism, SPEDP will ensure strong accountability in its responses toward affected communities directly affected by disasters and conflicts by involving them directly in project design and encouraging them to provide feedback on how our interventions are influencing them. SPEDP will train all staff implementing the project to ensure that they understand and have the capacity to implement the AAP mechanism. During project inception, SPEDP teams will establish complaint and feedback mechanism in its project locations in order to register complaints from community and give immediate feedback to the complainers where necessary and this will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committees with support from project staff. The project will utilize Community Based Response Mechanism (CBRM) as a community management mechanism that delivers collaborative livelihoods and income generating opportunities as a means of building peace and stability. The CBRM will address issues that may arise in a credible manner. CBRM will also facilitate community-based dispute resolution process to receive feedback, deal with complaints or disputes. The project is expected to receive and proactively respond to issues with the potential to impact community relationships. It will increase transparency and accountability of local authorities for fair, inclusive and quality service delivery to the affected populations. The project will obtain informed consent from participants and ensure confidentiality for project activities that involve any probing into participants’ experiences in project activities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPEDP staff have signed a code of conduct which includes zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse policy. SPEDP commits to continuously educate beneciaries on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and other violations and to inform them on reporting mechanisms in place when such cases occur. SPEDP will strictly adhere to the protection principles throughout the project period across the proposed locations. All project staff will be trained on PSEA. WGFSs are used as entry points for reporting PSEA cases for women and girls. SPEDP will support survivors of PSEA to access support in a manner that does not compromise their dignity and privacy and confidentiality. PSEA messaging will be incorporated in the community awareness raising activities to ensure the community members are aware of what PSEA entails, the available reporting mechanisms and the importance of reporting. SPEDP will build the capacity of PSEA networks or focal point(s) who will be included in the community sensitization activities to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms. IEC materials with PSEA messages will be developed, translated into the local language, and distributed to the communities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback information will be fed back to Project Management Team who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly on real time. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC via scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing two-way communication. PMCs will also publicize and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints. PMCs will use project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to complaints.
