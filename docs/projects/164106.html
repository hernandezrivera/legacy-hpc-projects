Project Summary

Starting from the long experience of AVSI Foundation on psycho-social support, which brought to create its specific Manual on Phychosocial Aproach and Toolikit together with UNICEF Lebanon (available in Arabic), we would like to develop community-based PSS and protection risks prevention in favor of children and women and PwDs in order to promote social cohesion and community development in Syria. The project intends to promote family well-being community centers where to perform with children, women, PwDs - mainly IDPs or returnees - a PSS and social cohesion programme, promoting resilience, family development, gender and disability equality, social networking and life skills for the development of the community. The pilot initiative will be in three different areas of Syria to better monitor and evaluate the results of the project in different contexts and so learning from field experience. Protection needs assessments and monitoring by protection officers will be pursued during the entire project to better identify needs and inform a strategic response (according to HRP 2019).

4 - Needs

Psycho-social support is a natural and necessary need for societies, especially children, which have been suffering from protracted crisis, such as the Syrians. By UNICEF data (Press Release 13 December 2018), 4 million of children (half of the children population) grew up only witnessing violence and they are in need of psycho-social support. Psycho-social projects are therefore important to enhance equitable access to quality child protection services, including for children with disabilities (UNICEF 2020). Disruption of traditional safety nets and of livelihood opportunities, in addition to sudden displacement, put moreover many women and girls at risk too, aggravating protection concerns such as psycho-social stress. In addition the protracted crisis may also cause situation of marginalization and neglect for groups with specific needs like disable persons (HRP 2019, p.34). The project is in line with objective 1, 2 and 5 of Protection Cluster WoS (HRP 2019 and 2020 Protection Cluster Response Framework).

5 - Implementation of activities

The project would like to reinforce and to establish in three communities – areas with different social characteristics, and where the number of IDPs (or returnees) is high - a family well-being center. The activities will be develop together with our partner SCFAP: they will provide the access to the community, the management of the center and the volunteers to develop the activities together with AVSI specialists in the different sectors. In particular, the programme will be developed in favor of local children (6-17 yrs old) and women (18-60 yrs old) and PWDs (all age). The programme will cover trainings to social workers, Child Friendly Spaces (CFS), PSS programme sessions for different categories of PINs, mobile unit service on the local territory (outreach mechanism), GBV awareness, life skills and recreational activities, social cohesion activities and events, PwDs social inclusion. In particular, the CFS will provide informal learning opportunities for kids, life skills and recreational activities, hygiene sessions and parents supporting as primary care givers with parenting programs and finally specific support (like educative support, material support, psychological support) to children and young adults with disability.We will organize sessions of PSS, as main activity, for boys, girls, and women (integrating PWDs in those sessions) implemented in the center itself, but even directly in the surrounding community. The PSS activities will be specifically monitored and evaluated (by a specific M&E programme) in the three different contexts/areas selected to better evaluate why and where they work better in order to create valid models for future interventions. Through the center AVSI staff and SCFAP’s volunteers will conduct awareness campaigns on protection, child protection, GBV. The center will organize finally community focus groups and initiative on how to ameliorate the community(and relative interventions and incentives to do it like public events).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project is in line with the principle of Protection of the Sphere Standards in Humanitarian Aid, especially to assist people in psycho-social recovery. All staff involved will received a specific training on CP and PSS according to AVSI international Psycho-social Approach Manual and its toolkit and they will sign and adhere to the AVSI Code of Ethics and Child Protection Policy. A specialized psychologist will be available in order to help staff, volunteers, women, disabled persons, children with their parents in their problems and complains, in collaboration with SCFAP. All activities will be tailored to be inclusive and accessible for girls and children with disabilities and specific structured interventions are planned to allow this. All beneficiaries identified for need of specialized services that require referrals will be asked to sign an information release consent form. This would also apply in regards to field staff sharing information among each other. The discretion of beneficiary information will be protected, as it will only be disclosed upon consent of the beneficiaries. Continuous activities' monitoring and whistle-blowing procedures will prevent misconduct and potential harassment.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The situation of the area selected is relative stable and the partner of the project guarantees the access. AVSI is in continuous contact with its main partner and UNDSS and it receives periodic specific reports about the situations in the country. AVSI has specific Security Guidelines and Procedures for Syria (updated in August 2019) and specific measures are in place to prevent security accidents. The identified areas are selected according to needs of the population and to AVSI capacity to reach them directly or through the implementing partner. Consultation with partner, local population, community leaders and other stakeholders and coordination with Protection cluster and local protection actors will be continuous in order to mitigate the risk of misunderstanding, of excluding some groups as well as of choosing a not appropriate location within the community. HR is managed by a specific AVSI HR officer to avoid delays and to face internal queries. Finally, from a financial point of view, all the expenditures related to the project are sent on a monthly base to HQ and to the regional office to enable the revision of the accounting in order to avoid financial delays or problems.
