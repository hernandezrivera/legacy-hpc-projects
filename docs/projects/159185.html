Project Summary

Lower Shabelle and Galgudud, project’s target areas, are examples of regions where enrollment and retention of school age children is very low. Displacement, poverty and protection related issues hinder school age children’s access of education. According to Education Cluster’s matrix of school age children in need (PiN), Lower Shabelle region has 78480 children of whom 41589 are girls while that of Galgudud is 57491 of whom 26981 are school age girls.
The project targets to support 3778 (1852 girls and 1926 boys) out of school children in the age bracket of 6 to 14 enrolled and retained in 10 schools in four districts of Lower Shabelle (Afgoye, Marka, Barawe and Kuntuwarey) and one district in Galgudud ( Elbur). The project aims at enrolling out of school children from IDP camps, people living in extreme poverty, minority and marginalized groups with special consideration to children with disabilities, into public schools in operation. The target schools are in operation but enroll only those whose families afford to pay fees. The project targets children from the most vulnerable households in the enrollments and will be exempted from paying fees and instead 160 teachers in these schools will be given monthly emergency incentives for ten months. At least one teacher with disability will be employed in each school. To ensure pupil’s retention in schools, children will be given one hot meal during school days. Learning materials will also be offered to these disadvantaged children. Adolescent girls will have access to free sanitary materials to prevent absenteeism and at the same time promote hygiene. Families whose children are already in the school paying fees can afford to provide for their children in terms of food, learning materials and sanitary for girls.
With increased enrollment schools will have to employ additional teachers to help meet teaching demands of children. The project will contribute incentives to the new and some of the teachers already working in the target schools as newly enrolled pupils will not be subjected to pay any fees.
Enough gender segregated latrines with hand wash facilities will be constructed in each of the 10 schools. Clean drinking water and recreational facilities will also be installed in the 10 schools. Water kiosks will be installed in each school for each school and will be used by the entire school pupils and staff.
Protection issues will be given at most consideration and teachers, CEC members and influential persons in the school locality will have their capacities and awareness raised on children’s rights, violent free learning environment with zero tolerance to all kinds of child abuses including bullying in and out of school. Teachers will be trained on INEE Inclusive Education, Protection and Socio-Emotional learning. Two protection focal points (one male and one female) with additional PSS First Aid training and knowledge of referral pathways to specific child protection will be appointed in each school. Psycho-social support will be offered in each school. Children with protection issues will be referred to child protection service centers.
The project targets the accessible areas of the target regions and is linked to the three strategic objectives of the Educ. cluster.
NWO has an outstanding experience in working with communities. To ensure the sustainability of the project after the expiry date, NWO will build the capacity of the CECs and engage with local authorities, including the ministry of education. Conduct regular monthly meetings to discuss the role of the community in supporting the school development plans and how the community can mobilise resources to fund the project, especially teacher incentives. Equally, engage with parents who are paying teacher incentives of the teachers teaching in the public schools and urge them to consider supporting the marginalised children. Also, NWO will start lobbying for fundraising from diaspora communities.

Project Activities

The project aims at executing the following activities:1. Distribution of scholastic materials to 3778 (1926 boys and 1852 girls) including children with disabilities in 10 schools in 5 districts. Materials to be distributed include: (exercise books, pens, pencils, erasers, sharpeners and solar lights) to IDPs and poor host communities' children in ten schools (2 schools in each of the 5 districts targeted.2. Provision of school feeding programme in 10 schools for 10 months. 3778 (1926 boys and 1852 girls) including children with disabilities targeted3. Provision of emergency teacher incentives to 160 teachers ( 40 female and 120 male) who will be teaching and supporting learners at the schools. The incentive will be 100 USD per month for 10 months. Since the target beneficiaries are children affected by drought and conflict and have been out of school for long.4. Provision of sanitary materials to girls for ten months. 1000 adolescent school girls targeted5. Provision of clean drinking water to the 10 target schools6. 160 teachers (120 male and 40 female) will be provided with training on life-skills training such as PSS, mine risk reduction, and multi-age child centered teaching methodology, school management and communicating with children.7. Provision of teacher training in the INEE Inclusive Education, Protection and Socio-Emotional learning (4-days training) . 160 (40 female and 120 male) teachers targeted8. Conduct awareness sessions for teachers on the Teachers’ Code of Conduct (TCOC) and sign it for accountability. 160 (40 female and 120 male) teachers targeted 9. Awareness raising for CECs, teachers and community on children’s rights and a violent free learning environment with zero tolerance to all kinds of child abuses including bullying in and out of school. The project targets 280 (160 teachers, 100 CEC members and 20 focal points) persons.10. Appoint Child Protection focal points at school (1 male and 1 female) with additional PSS First Aid training and knowledge of referral pathways to specific child protection. 20 (10 female and 10 male) focal points appointed.11. Facilitate structured gender sensitive recreational and PSS activities at the schools for both boys and girls. Twenty recreational facilities (two for each school) to be installed.12. Establish community dialogues to raise awareness on mitigating risks and all kinds of child abuse practices to enhance children’s access and retention in education. Ten locations targeted13. Provision of safe, dignified and gender friendly wash facilities including adequate gender segregated latrines (targets 40 latrines in 10 target schools)14. Mapping of marginalization dynamics in the targeted community (targets 10 locations)

Mitigation Measures

The project will have strategies to mitigate the possible protection concerns the project and beneficiaries might encounter which can negatively impact the project out come. Below are possible risks and their proposed mitigation:1a) Protection of civilians1. Avoidance of placing education facilities near military installations and conflict front-lines2. Considering risks associated with land mines, explosive remnants of war, busy roads, exposed wires and dangerous materials on the ground.3. Keeping in mind the distance children have to travel and their mode of travel.4. Ensuring the timing of classes is safe for children; classes will be set for the day time with sufficient to reach home before nightfall. 5. Situating play areas in clearly visible and safe locations. 2a) Exclusion and marginalization1. Collect and use data disaggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming. 2. Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups.3. Advocate for equal access to education for girls and sensitize the community about the value and benefits of girls’ education. 4. Identify and address barriers children face accessing education services.5. Ensure that affected populations know their right to education and where/how to access education services.2b) Exclusion and marginalization1. Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to education needs. Different criteria may affect the power dynamics. 2. Consult women, men, boys, girls, adolescents, children with disabilities, teachers and parents to understand their needs and preferences for school location, design and education services. 3. Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response 4. Talk to girls and boys separately about the barriers they face and hold discussions with groups of children that could face added difficulties. 5. Establish participatory student associations and parent-teacher committees and engage them in the design of education programs. Such associations can boost the confidence and self-protection capacities of those involved whilst benefiting others. 6. Ensure student, teacher, parent and school management committees are democratic and inclusive and members represent all groups within the community (e.g. gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities). 7. Reinforce the capacity of parents and teachers to act as a protective layer for children against violations. 4d) Protection mainstreaming1. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at learning centres, phone lines2. Consider the gender balance of education staff - ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for female teachers and students.3. Ensure that all staff and committee representatives receive protection mainstreaming training.4. Work with education authorities and school management to establish school rules and a code of conduct that includes a child protection policy. Ensure all teachers and volunteers working in the education facility understand, sign and adhere to its content.5. In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of teachers to monitor, report and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures.
