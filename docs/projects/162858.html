Project Summary

This project aims to provide life-saving support to contribute to the reduction of morbidity associated with malnutrition and prevent livelihood depletion of vulnerable communities through responding to immediate food security & livelihood and protection needs of the target population in Abugubeiha, Aldalang, Rashad and Talodi localities of South Kordofan State, through improved access to agricultural and livestock inputs and provision of livelihoods support for 18,482 conflict-affected HHs (110,892 beneficiaries) including: 8502 IDPs HHs, 5914 Returnees HHs and 4,066 HHs from destitute host communities. The intervention aims to address immediate FSL needs through direct provision of agricultural and livestock inputs including: provision of community-based veterinary services including vaccination and vouchetreatment of 250,000 heads of livestock belonging to 10,000 HHs in the 3 localities, to contribute to the beneficiaries’ livestock assets protection and reduce morbidity and mortality rates. Increased animals movement due to large scale displacement places affected populations both animals and humans at risk of diseases, hence 30 CAHWs will be provided with refresher training on disease protection, outbreak prevention and control methods, CAHWs play a key role in treating animals and reporting on outbreaks, and other concerns regarding pastures, water resources and livestock problems in remote locations. In this activity: 500,000 doses of animal vaccines will be provided, including: sheep pox, PPR, HS, anthrax and BQ, CAHWs kits and veterinary drugs will be provided to CAHWs for animals’ treatment. Provision of quality assorted seeds mainly legumes (27.968 mt) including: chick pea, pigeon pea and vegetable seeds (1.189 mt) including: okra, watermelon and tomatoes to 6,992 vulnerable HHs, in addition to provision of hand tools including: hoes and rakes (5,594 pieces) to 40% of beneficiaries (4,894 HHs), this activity aims to enable beneficiaries ensure self-reliance, increase their dietary intake and diversification and will place special focus on women who are involved in farming activities in targeted areas. In addition to 300 farmers targeted by extension training in recommended agricultural practices. To enable the targeted communities to withstand current crises and improve their resilience; restorative livelihood activities and Income Generating Activities (IGAs) will be undertaken including: capacity building in small scale business management and provision of start-up packages whilst mainstreaming gender and environmental protection. IGAs target 1,490 vulnerable HHs to create self-employment and improve their income including: Provision of a small stock of goats (2,700) to 540 vulnerable female headed HHs with the necessary animal husbandry extension training required for the success of the intervention. To further contribute to women empowerment; 300 women will be targeted by training on manufacturing of fuel efficient stoves (FES) and provision of start-up kits, FES decrease firewood consumption hence reduce the time spent by women collecting firewood and their exposure to abuse and SGBV in remote areas, this activity also promotes environmental conservation as a result of reduced firewood extraction, and reduces health and safety risks associated with using traditional three stone fire. 650 men will be trained in manufacturing of soil stabilized blocks (SSB) and provided with hand press machines; this activity aims to facilitate environmentally friendly production, marketing and application of affordable building materials that can enhance construction and rehabilitation of settlements and basic services for returnees, IDPs and other vulnerable communities. All of the project activities will be conducted with technical support from line ministries at the state level. VSFG will incorporate best practices in humanitarian response including DRR, NRM and "Do No Harm" approaches in this project.

1 - Needs

This project proposal is based on different needs assessments conducted by the consortium members including a needs assessment mission carried out in May 2019 covering Talodi and Abu Jubeiha Localities. As well as the inter-agency assessment carried out by UN agencies and NGOs including the consortium partners (June 2019)- attached. The assessments conducted have indicated that the humanitarian situation particularly food and nutrition security has worsened due to soaring of food- and also other commodities- prices which affect poor households food consumption and nutrition. Water supply infrastructure is inadequate especially, people are forced to use unsafe sources, several water points have been identified as being malfunctioning and in need of rehabilitation /maintenance. Open defecation is practiced and other indicators of poor hygiene and sanitation knowledge were observed in the assessed areas. This is in addition to the severe shortage of health and nutrition services in all of the targeted localities. The protracted displacement in South Kordofan state creates a conducive environment for the occurrence of varying forms of gender-based violence, poverty, food insecurity, inadequate shelter and WASH service and low level of education, all of these factors contribute to the vulnerability of women and girls to GBV, in addition to that the cultural attitudes towards GBV in the state are worrying. Though the accessibility to reliable data and information of reported incidence on gender-based violence is very limited due to cultural hindrance, gender-based violence in such contexts can intuitively be perceived and expected, especially during the farming season. The proposed activities in this project directly correspond with the identified needs across the five sectors in the four targeted localities for approximately 40,000 IDPs, 90,000 returnees and 40,000 of the affected host communities. According to the assessments’ key findings and recommendations in addition to covering identified gaps and needs in the above mentioned sectors, GBV and child protection components are much advisable to be integrated with the project activities as well as components of natural resources management focusing on integrated water resource management program (IWRM), implemented closely with livelihood programs in addition to peacebuilding and cohabitation. Further, IGAs programs targeting girls and women were also recommended.According to the HNO 2019 document, malnutrition remains deemed critical across the country. Malnutrition prevalence in 11 states have reached beyond WHO emergency threshold of 15%. There are some factors contributing to the high level including increases in food costs, localized insecurities, displacement and lean seasons. With limited data available, OCHA reported that in South Kordofan malnutrition is prevalence among under five children. Aside from the overall economic and political contexts, specific determinants such as poor child-feeding practices, poverty, lack of proper nutrition during pregnancy, limited access to safe drinking water and sanitation services contribute directly to malnutrition in children.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The proposed grant will enhance improved resource management and equal access to available resources. Furthermore, VSF G has considered seriously the ‘environmental marker across all the project designed activities. ’The project will protect and restore the natural resource base in the livelihoods systems of the poor and vulnerable households through redressing environmental degradation, increasing efficiency and promoting conservation of soil, water and biomass resources. Due to potential waste generation through medical supplies (vaccination and Treatment campaigns) VSF G will ensure mitigation measures are in place such as proper waste management of the disposable elements of animal vaccination and treatment. The waste will be incinerated safely and each training will include element of environment protection.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

VSF-G adopts do no harm approach in its interventions, hence incorporates protection principles into its programming through promoting inclusive and meaningful access to services provided, considers the safety and dignity of the project beneficiaries and target communities in designing implementation modalities. VSF-G will ensure participation, consultation and inclusion of all community ethnic, age and gender groups to guarantee that unintended negative effects that might increase the vulnerability of targeted people are prevented and duly mitigated. Risks will be assessed at the beginning of this action, and then regularly to ensure access and participation of especially marginalized and or at-risk groups (i.e. elderly, disabled) is guaranteed. Through implementing accountability to the affected population with effective feedback and response mechanisms to measure the adequacy of the services provided and satisfaction of the target population and to effectively and timely address valid complaints. Empowering the communities through involving them in decision-making, promotion of participation and capacity building of the communities with regard to their rights and responsibilities. This will be ensured by establishing dialogue and orientation in community meetings. GBV is a cross-cutting issue and therefore will be well integrated into this response. In this project VSF-G aims to empower women and girls through awareness creation on GBV through women multipurpose centers which will be established/rehabilitated by the project and introduction of IGAs, to increase their food and livelihood security. community-based protection committees will be strengthened through the centers which will represent a place for women to meet and discuss their concerns, exchange views and learn from each other. Additionally, among the training for IGA groups, training sessions on GBV will be incorporated including topics such as FGM, early marriage, domestic violence, women rights, reproductive health, and HIV/AIDS.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

VSF-G adopts Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in setting standards, building capacity, communication, participation, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve the quality of partnership on principles of accountability. IASC commitments with regard to AAP are incorporated in this plan including governance and integration of feedback and response mechanisms into program design, maintaining transparency and dialogue between the organization and the affected population, participation, and involvement of the target population in project design, monitoring, and evaluation of this project. All the while mainstreaming protection and considering the safety and confidentiality of the target population. VSF-G focuses on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities, including men, women, boys, girls, elders, persons with disabilities among other vulnerable and or marginalized groups. Community consultation meetings and orientation sessions will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure that the project is well understood by target communities and raise the communities’ awareness with regard to their participatory roles and responsibilities, and the responsibilities of the project partners towards them. Participation will be promoted through the involvement of beneficiaries in the formation of community committees and planning the implementation schedule. The community committees will be created to organize and mobilize the beneficiaries, participate in the development of beneficiary selection criteria, distribution of inputs and monitoring and evaluation of the intervention. Representatives from a cross-section (with respect to age, gender, and diversity) will be ensured to participate as community committee members and in the monitoring and evaluation of the project. The most appropriate mechanisms will be established in consultation with community members, taking into consideration literacy rates, accessibility to communication devices and the presence of suitable sites for establishment of complaint/suggestion boxes; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of timely response. Good internal procedures and confidential reporting systems will be implemented to ensure the confidentiality and safety of all community members. The communities’ feedback will be acquired and verified through different approaches including regular monitoring activities and feedback, complaints and response mechanisms including focus group discussions, semi-structured interviews, key informant interviews, community meetings and communicating the partners’ contacts and addresses with community members and committees.
