Project Summary

Needs : In Syria, 15,4 million persons are expected to need early recovery and livelihoods assistance in 2024, as per the 2024 HNO. Across Syria, a general deterioration in access to livelihoods, ability to meet basic needs, availability and affordability of infrastructure and services is observed, threatening the resilience and social cohesion of households. Inflationary trends on key goods and services, widespread low productivity of inputs, low wages, and lack of employment, among other factors, continue to impact households’ priority needs and exacerbate the income-expenditure gapacross the country. According to REACH, early recovery livelihoods is regularly reported within the top (3) priority needs of households across the country. The economic crisis has significantly worsened in certain areas, leading to a substantial increase in the cost of the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) by 86% over 12 months. With 99% of the population in Northeast Syria (NES) living below the extreme poverty line of USD 1.90 per person per day, urgent investments in economic recovery are crucial. Ongoing drought in NES has led to a loss of daily work opportunities, particularly in agriculture, the main source of livelihood in the region. Agricultural value chains, particularly wheat and barley production, have been severely affected, causing a decline in employment opportunities.

Activities : The proposed project by ACTED focuses on the Early Recovery Livelihoods (ERL) sector with the objective of restoring urban livelihoods in conflict and earthquake-affected communities across Northern Syria. The plan involves a multifaceted approach, incorporating short-term income opportunities through Cash-for-Work schemes, sustainable restoration of basic services, and value chain improvement. This includes the repair, installation, and rehabilitation of key assets like water infrastructure and schools. Also, livelihoods support and market recovery efforts will be done through grants and training for small-to-mid-scale businesses.
ACTED will continue its Area-Based Approach, providing multi-sectoral assistance and building the capacity of local stakeholders for community-driven response plans. The organization aims to reach 254,677 individuals in 2024 and contribute to limiting the migration of affected populations to Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) camps.
In terms of business support, ACTED plans to implement Cash for Work schemes in specific areas, targeting vulnerable households to provide temporary income opportunities. These schemes will focus on unskilled or low-skilled work, determined through a participatory process involving community input. Activities may include tasks like compost industry, poly tunnel construction support, school repairs, public garden rehabilitation, and fencing. The goal is to ensure community acceptance and accessibility for various population groups, including women, the elderly, and individuals with disabilities. Participants are expected to work for three months, receiving a daily wage of approximately 4.5 USD.
In the context of the ERL sector, ACTED is actively contributing to household and community resilience by offering increased opportunities for sustainable livelihoods, inclusive access to basic public services, and enhancing the capacity of local institutions and civil society to support early recovery efforts. Additionally, the organization has provided solar systems to strengthen resilience and improve access to services.
PSEA : All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project.
