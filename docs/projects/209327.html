Project Summary

Darfur region with it is five states witnessed all forms of violence against the civilian population including GBV this has happened in the context of war together with historical insecurity and characterizing social norms to target community that resulted in distraction of infrastructure including health, water and financial institutions, farms and every livelihoods means. The huge gap in service delivery and protection of civilians have endangered women, children and other vulnerable groups. This includes none availability of vaccination for newly born babies, the necessary vitamins and health care for pregnant and lactating women total absence of assisted delivery to jeopardize the whole reproductive health and collapse GBV response and mitigation measures as the internal displacement journey has no halt and the people has to leave the area once the fighting reached it, with it is rapid rhythm during the displacement survivors of GBV has shared their experience with SUDO that varied from killing to rape, abduction, sex slavery forcible work,use of rape as a war tactic, looting, denial of medical care or work and harassment including sexual harassment and in some cases to allow imprisoned husbands or sons set free or visited by families or return confiscated valuables and belongings. Although there is no evidence SUDO has information of human trafficking networks active in Malit North Darfur, Omdafook, Rodom, in which the less resourced IDPs fleeing from Nyala, Fasher, Zalingei and Elganina are accumulated in huge numbers are subject to become victims. The information gathering and analyses to GBV Issues are consisted as follow:-
1. Absence of medical care for the GBV survivors
2. Absence of referral pathways
3. Lack of drugs and medical care for the aftermath short medium and long term support
4. Absence of PNC and ANC
5. Reproductive health’s endangerment, childhood diseases, unassisted delivery / pregnancy and impossible ANC, PNC increasing mortalities among vulnerable groups
6. Likelihood of child diseases for none availability of all kind of vaccines during the pregnancy for newly born babies
7. Livelihoods is jeopardized and all means of production and income generation activities
8. In the absence or destruction of the primary health care centers the reproductive health rests on untrained/ watched Traditional Birth attendants TBAs
9. Collapse of the Mother to mother supporting groups MtMSG
10. Collapse of the GBV SOP
With the aim of reactivating GBV response within Darfur region that has been disturbed by the conflict and specifically to:- a-Reducing the risk of GBV and b- engaging community, actors and stakeholders on the conflict environment related likelihood of exposure to risk of violence , c- support of vulnerable groups
SUDO will engage in a constructive partnership with the community and social/ Health officials and actors to plan and implement.
The project aims to:
- Raising awareness of service providers and communities about cases of violations
- Engage the community and increase knowledge of gender-based violence
- Improving the work environment and providing aids and supplies
- Support GBV surviors with income generation activities/start-up fund
This will be through:
-establishment of women center/ rehabilitation/maintenance and equip
- Training of the TBAs, MtMSG , ANC,PNC and F/M type and group
- GBV awareness and service provision
-In service Training of psychosocial, Social workers health care practitioners on GBV case management and medical intervention on aid of GBV survivors
- Economic empowerment for GBV survivors and venerable groups
The project targets all community groups particularly girls and women of reproductive age.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project is adopting protection approach throughout its cycle based on the fact that SUDO prioritizes the safety and well-being of both its beneficiaries and staff through the implementation of a robust safeguarding policy. This policy is designed to ensure that appropriate measures are in place to protect individuals involved in its programs. Moreover, SUDO has developed specific technical guidance tailored to working in conflict areas, taking into account the unique context of Sudan. For example, when staff member or SUDO volunteers confronted with armed groups, a negotiation and de-escalation techniques will be used by remaining calm and non-threatening while avoiding aggressive or provocative behavior and engaging in respectful dialogue, if possible, and demonstrating peaceful intentions. As one of the safeguarding commitment, SUDO staff is aware about their commitment for protection of beneficiaries from abuse, harassment and exploitation. We are aware that, in the current situation, the regular reporting and feedback mechanisms are not functional; however, SUDO will be working to find a practical mean for beneficiaries to report any misconduct from staff or its associates.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Feedback from communities whether through face to face discussion, phone calls, complaints box will be collected and analyzed by the MEAL team to advice on corrections measures. when there is complaint, the Head of Office will work with the project staff to suggest modification to the activities or to the implementation approach. When the complaint is related to sexual exploitation and abuse, the organization policy on this regard will be applied.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

SUDO has a safeguarding policy with the objectives to protect beneficiaries of SUDO projects from any form of abuse and exploitation as well as protect staff from the same and any potential false allegation of misconduct. Safeguarding Policy applies to; full time and part time SUDO staff, members of SUDO, Board of Directors, Volunteers and interns, consultants, visitors, people acting on behalf of SUDO and – to the relevant extent – to SUDO suppliers and partner organizations. SUDO take all reasonable steps to protect beneficiaries from any harassment, abuse and exploitation potentially perpetrated by SUDO staff or associates. All SUDO staff and associates are required to RESPECT the rights and dignity of individuals, families and communities with whom SUDO work. likewise, SUDO is committed to protecting the rights of the SUDO staff and to maintaining its dignified working environment free of any potential harassment, abuse and exploitation. SUDO ensures that safe, appropriate and accessible means of reporting safeguarding concerns are made available to staff and the communities. In the response SUDO follow up on safeguarding reports and concerns according to principles and obligations described in the SUDO Code of Conduct and Community Response Mechanism (CRM). The reaction to a complaint or report shall result in a confidential, sensitive, thorough and prompt investigation. Such investigation either may result in the reduction of concern or in further action which includes disciplinary action up to dismissal and/or reporting to relevant authorities.
