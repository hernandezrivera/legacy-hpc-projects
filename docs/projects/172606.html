Project Summary

The project will seek to address portable water access challenges, poor hygiene and low sanitation coverage targeting vulnerable communities facing perennial challenges of access to suffient quality water sources and with low sanitation coverage Mwenezi and Zaka districts of Masvingo province. High yielding perennial boreholes with potential to service big number of households will be targeted for rehabilitation while some community managed piped water schemes are going to rehabilitated/constructed. All rehabilitated water sources will be quality tested. All projected supported water points/sources will have trained and functional water point/asset committees.
Project will also Conduct gender and child sensitive hygiene promotion sessions through Sanitation Focused Participatory Health and Hygiene Education (SaFPHHE) in communities, Refresher training of Environmental Health Technicians (EHTs), Village Health Workers (VHWs) and Community Health Workers (CHWs) supporting community-based counselling of caregivers on infant and young child feeding practices on critical lifesaving WASH messages / hygiene practices will also be done. The 12 months project with budget of US$940 932 will benefit a population of 52 303 women, girls, men and boys.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Project is addressing challenges identified by communities through consultative processes such as ZIMVAC. Implementation will be done through and with the community WASH structures as mandated by Government. CARE is committed to meeting international standards of equality and accountability as guided by its accountability framework and the CARE Gender Equality Policy and CARE International Safeguarding policy among others. CARE has also put in place open communication channels that allow for people to come forward with sexual harassment, exploitation or abuse claims using multiple reporting channels that include suggestion box, help desks (manned by both community represenattives and care staff), walk-ins, Deloitte hotline and community meetings. CARE also has a single global CARE Line for receiving reports with support in over 100 languages, online or phone options, and anonymous reporting options. CARE will hold quarterly review meeting with the communities, to assess project outcomes, and community satisfaction with services provided.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

All project activities will be implemented by strictly adhering to all the COVId 19 protocols ensuring no one is harmed in the process. The safety of women and children will take center stage ensuring they will not be abused in the process. Project will take an inclusive approach by ensuring that all people participate including those with disabilities. Appropriate training will be conducted based on the identified gaps inorder to empower the communities.The project will do due diligence during recruitment. Staff, partner organizations, government will receive training on CARE’s Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse policy, and the Inter Agency Steering Committee code of conduct for the prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Capacities of staff and partners will be built around PSEA Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).
