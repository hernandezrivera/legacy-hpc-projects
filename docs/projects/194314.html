Project Summary

South Sudan remains one of the most food insecure countries in the world. Its acute food insecurity situation is caused by multiple and interconnected factors such as prolonged conflict, natural disasters (successive years of flooding and localized droughts), high food prices, dry spells affecting crop and pasture, Covid-19 consequences and displacements among others. According to the IPC report released in November (2022), 6.31M people (51.0% of the population) will be facing severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+) during the post-harvest period of December 2022 to March 2023 and are in need of urgent action. This is expected to increase to during the lean season projection period of April to July 2023 to 7.76M people (62.7% of the population) will be facing severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+) and are in need of urgent action. The targeted areas of this project are some of the worst affected in terms of food insecurity. Currently, people facing severe acute food insecurity in the project target areas: in Tonj North (145,000), in Wau (112,000), in Yei (106,000), in Kajokeji (94,000), in Kapoeta East (93,000), and in Kapoeta North (79,000). It is estimated that all current crisis IPC levels will deteriorate significantly if emergency assistance is not provided. In the lean season projection period of April to July 2023 the food security situation will significantly deteriorate/worsen. in 2023. In Tonj North (171,000), in Wau (144,000), in Kajokeji (140,000), in Yei (136,000), in Kapoeta East (118,000), and in Kapoeta North (94,000).
The Project Enhancing Food Security and Resilient livelihood to Conflict and Drought affected Population in Warrap, Eastern Equatoria, Central Equatoria and Western Bahr el Ghazal States aims at saving life and building resilient livelihood of 48,180 vulnerable Population (6,263 Boys, 12,045 Girls, 13,009 men, and 16,863 women) at Warrap state (Tonj North), Eastern Equatoria (Kaoeta East and Kapoeta North), Central Equatoria (Yei and Kajokeji) and Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau). The project will among others provide food parcels; agricultural inputs, training on farming, crop disease prevention, harvest and storage; livestock disease prevention and animal husbandry training and provision of start-up vet-kits. These interventions will contribute to improving agricultural/livestock production thereby improving food security
The project shall contribute to providing food assistance and emergency food production to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations by employing Accountability to the affected population (AAP) approach and making sure that activities are implemented in line with core humanitarian standards (CHS). All the responds shall be integrated with strategies that bridges gender in equality gaps and addresses issues of GBV that happens to be a major contributing factor for food insecurity and compromise livelihood of men, women and girls. The respond shall aim at addressing risk factors of covid-19 transmission and employ risk mitigation measures during activities implementation. As part of Monitoring Evaluation and learning. IRSS shall conduct Project progress and Midterm review workshop to serve both learning and accountability purpose and will seek to identify any problems and constraints encountered by the project to date and formulate appropriate recommendations for corrective actions for effective implementation of the remaining period of the project implementation. Also ensure the project indicators that are monitored are on track and lesson learnt are drawn for future improvement. The workshop shall involve key stake holders of the projects especially representatives of women, people living with disability, youth, government line ministries, representatives of implementing partners, FSL cluster members, etc.

1 - Needs

According to South Sudan HNO 2023, an estimated 8.2 million people are likely to experience severe food insecurity during lean season (May – July 2023). This is comparable with the situation of FSL sectoral analysis (December 2021) and IPC analysis (March 2022) where food security needs remained large and severe. According to IRSS rapid needs assessment 2022, 90,000 people are reported displaced across the county and lacks most of the basic necessities. Moreover, Food insecurity is the third highest Warrap (70%) with the highest levels of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) at the peak of the lean season 2023. South Sudan is also highly prone to climate-related shocks as a consecutive fourth year of extreme flooding has impacted almost one million people so far in 2022, destroying homes, schools, health facilities, water sources, crop fields, pasture and trade routes The main drivers are climatic shocks, conflicts and economic shocks; and household stressors. Food insecurity is greatest when these shocks and stressors are compounded by the persistent high levels of vulnerability and poverty associated with the protracted crisis. This situation will be further exacerbated by the progressive reductions in funding against increasing humanitarian needs in recent years which has forced humanitarian agencies to reduce entitlements and prioritize the most vulnerable households. According to IPC Acute Food Insecurity & Acute Malnutrition Analysis Report issued in November 2022, it is estimated that all current crisis IPC levels will deteriorate significantly if emergency assistance is not provided. During the period of December 2022 to March 2023, Tonj North (158,000), in Kapoeta East (110,000), in Wau (96,000), in Yei (91,000), in Kapoeta North (86,000), and in Kajokeji (82,000) will face food insecurity. During the period of April to July 2023 the food security situation will worsen in Tonj North (171,000), in Wau (144,000), in Kajokeji (140,000), in Yei (136,000), in Kapoeta East (118,000), and in Kapoeta North (94,000). According to IRW independent assessment conducted in August 2022 indicated that the situation will worsen as WFP and other partners have significantly reduced food assistance. Many of the assessed households reported that 79.9% in Tonj North, and 85.6% in Yei will exhaust their food reserves within one week while 96.9% in Tonj North, and 99.7% in Yei will exhaust their food reserves within 2 weeks (IRSS RNA 2022). It also reveals that 79.7% of the population rely on humanitarian food assistance. The market functionality is a big challenge in the proposed project area due to poor road network. The market is very far with 58.2% spending more than one hour to reach the next market. Being a pastoral community, 31.3% of the respondents have small ruminants, 28.4% large ruminants and 80% keep poultry. 90% of the population rely on pastoralism as their main source of income while 61.8% of the population are engaged in some IGAs. 54.5% of bread winners are women as they are the main work force within Toposa society, the main tribe in the region. Gender equality and GBV profile of Kapoeta is similar to that of Tonj north because these are both pastoral communities. In the proposed action area high levels of food insecurity, violence and cattle raids affected vulnerable people. The violence severely impacted response operations in the area. People’s homes and humanitarian facilities were looted and burnt down, this included warehouses of relief commodities and vehicles. The restriction in movement by security forces affect access to farmland by the civil population and compromise on delivery of humanitarian services. All these have contributed negatively to the IPC levels as indicated in the paragraph above. Therefore, IRSS shall contribute to addressing the needs of 48,180 food insecure vulnerable populations through FSL cluster objectives in Tonj Nortn,Wau, Kajokeji, Kapoeta East and North.

2 - Project activities

Given the current level of food insecurity and projected trends in 2023, IRSS proposes to provide lifesaving support and to safeguard livelihoods to the most vulnerable in the target areas. In line with South Sudan’s HRP strategy, this project aims to enhance the food security for community members through diverse intervention including resilient agriculture among the vulnerable households. There are several proposed activities designed to support the capacity of target groups in meeting their basic needs, reducing vulnerability, and sustaining livelihoods and dignity. The proposed interventions include: a) 4,030 vulnerable households will benefit from distribution of in-kind food assistance during lean season covering three months of food needs to prevent famine and improve food consumption and dietary diversity. The food packages will be in line with Sphere Standards and minimum expenditure basket and based on FSL cluster recommendations for a six-member household. b) To further strengthen and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop production. 4,000 vulnerable households will be provided with inputs necessary to engage in crop and vegetable production, including distribution of livelihood kits, crop seeds, and quick maturing vegetable seeds. All kits are based on FAO and cluster recommendations, including tested, drought resistant and improved varieties. c) Basic impact training will be given for the same 4000 households on agronomic- and post-harvest practices and emerging threats such as trans-boundary crop diseases. d) Fishing kits will be provided to a total of 200 households who live along the river Nile and river Kaya. e) The project shall train 40 agricultural extension workers (CAEWs) to carry on the extension work.f) Training of modern livestock management to 300 households. 25 incentivized CAHWs will be trained and provided start-up vet kits for management of livestock diseases, whom thereafter will be able perform veterinary tasks and promote husbandry methods to optimize animal production. g) The trained community animal health workers (CAHWs) will be deployed to conduct mass vaccination Campaign targeting 40,000 livestock. This will help to address the issues of animal health and prevent zoonotic diseases. This is particularly important as the crisis has significantly compromised livestock production.
