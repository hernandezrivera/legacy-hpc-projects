Project Summary

PAH will implement both static and mobile WASH and multi-sectoral responses. As an organization active in 5 sectors - WASH, NFI, Protection, FSL and Education PAH will provide cross sectorial aid for the most vulnerable population of South Sudan. PAH will cooperate with partners in Nutrition and Health to provide the synergy of the actions. PAH will use its strong expertise in WASH to provide access to water and improve sanitation.
PAH will mainly target people facing high level of WASH vulnerability, those in locations if WASH Severity Classification 4 and 3, displaced or returnees and host communities with over-stretched resources.
PAH is an experienced organization with broad portfolio of successfully implemented projects in various sectors. PAH will use is strongest expertise in WASH to provide assistance to vulnerable communities across the country by providing accessing to water, improving and sanitation both as single approach as well as in multi-sectoral responses PAH always seeks the synergies with other actions, as WASH sector covers the needs crucial for other aspects of life too.
PAH will mainly target people facing high level of WASH vulnerability, those in severity 4, displaced or returnees and host communities with over-stretched resources. In case of need PAH will respond in mobile modality in other locations, according to the needs. PAH will focus on rehabilitation and building water sources in institutions and those prioritized by community members. Sanitation will be provided on institutional level. Household level latrines will be promoted and sample ones constructed. PAH provides hygiene promotion, combined with protection, health, nutrition elements, with cross-sectoral topics adjusted to the needs of the groups. Strong cooperation is implemented especially with the sector of Nutrition, by improving sanitation and water access in nutrition sites and training of the Nutrition personnel. The capacity building is always based on community members chosen to be promoters, water user committees and other engaged people within the community. PAH holds itself accountable to affected population and ensures its ownership of project results.
Recognizing the cross-cutting nature of WASH, PAH combine the described activities with other sectors by pro PAH will focus on rehabilitation and building water sources in institutions and those prioritized by community members. 1 - Needs

The ongoing instabilities in most parts of the country cause the displacement of communities and the economic downturn, both have put a further strain on already limited WASH services. Conflicts, extremely high levels of violence and annual consecutive heavy flooding in the country cause displacements, the disruption of water services and access to sanitation facilities that result in high reliance on humanitarian assistance. Large-scale flooding for two consecutive years resulted in low yields and deterioration of food security, access to water and hygiene. Additional aspect are the Protection concerns as 12% of women and girls report GBV concerns while reaching the water points. The prevalence of open defecation is high, as approximately 80% of the population does not have access to sanitation facilities. In the states of Warrap, Unity, Jonglei and Nothern Bahr el Gazal this figure is below 10%. Personal hygiene practice are challenged as approximately only16% of households have access to soap. Across the country in 2022 6.1 million people are projected as population in need in terms of water, sanitation and hygiene services. As PAH responses in both static and mobile modality the submitted proposal refers to the general needs of the country due to possibility of mobile responses in multiple locations. According to WASH Severity Classification 2021 and FSNMS+ data South Sudan has 52 severity 4 counties, which are a priority for WASH interventions for 2022. 15 of them were clasified as priority with over 70% of population in critical phase with 1.8 mln people to be targeted for the response. These are Tonj East, Fangak, Panyikang, Canal Pigi, Twic East, Cueibet, Baliet, Malakal, Maiwut, Rubkona, Pibor, Ayod, Guit, Kapoeta East, Leer. Next group are 17 counties: Fashoda, Ulang, Longochuk, Manyo, Luapkini/Nasir, Terekeka, Koch, Akobo, Panyijar, Budi, Mayendit, Awerial, Duk, Lafon, Kapoeta North, Tonj North with 950 000 people to be targeted. Following priority group within severity 4 locations are : Aweil East, Mayom, Pariang, Rumbek North, Mayom, pariang, Yirol East, Yei, Ikotos, Bor South, Uror, Pochalla, Nyirol, Maban, Renk, Melut with 450 000 beneficiries to be targeted and last priority locations group is Aweil Center, Aweil South, Twic, Gogrial East, Tonj South, Nagero, Ezo, Nzara, Yambio, Wulu, Mvolo, Yirol West, Mundri East, Juba, Kajo-Keji, Magwi, Torit, Kapoeta South with 200 000 people as target. Additional 26 is classified as level 3.

2 - Project activities

1. Rehabilitation of existing water points. PAH will target water points at the community level, with special attention to point in Nutrition facilities. In grazing areas water points will be designed in a manner to ensure safety for people and animals. In areas with high food insecurity PAH will assess the irrigation practices and possibilities and may give instructions and encourage people for sustainable and safe solutions for watering the crops. This will provide synergy between WASH and food access. 2. Establishment of new water points. New water points development will be considered in case of communities, whose water points are overcrowded, there is no water point, however the community is willing to stay in this place. New water points will also be established in permanent/semi-permanent IDP location depending on the need and population. The water points may be developed/upgraded to solar powered water yards. For both new and rehabilitated points the water user committees will be trained and equipped to ensure sustainability and maintenance cost will be instituted. Pump mechanics will be trained locally to ensure urgent repairs in case of break downs.. The water points will be raised to avoid inflow of contaminated water from floods. 3. PAH will consider construction of new or improving existing sanitation facilities at local institutions, at crowded settings. PAH will establish gender and disability segregated sanitation facilities provided with proper doors and inside locks to enable privacy and mitigate risk of GBV. For a sustainable sanitation approach in IDP, host community and returnee settings, PAH will form and train sanitation committees selected from across the villages. The provision of the sanitation kits will be accompanied with awareness on the importance of latrine use, the dangers of open defecation etc. The sanitation facilities will be handed over to the institutions management and monitored to ensure sustainability.4. Flood resilient constructions solutions will be implemented for both new/rehabilitated boreholes and sanitation facilities. PAH has designed mentioned facilities, including household level latrines to be constructed with a use of local material. 5. Where possible, the cash based intervention modality will be implemented. As a member of CASH working group and JMMI initiative PAH will monitor closely the market situation. Provision of solar lamps, dignity/hygiene kits and soap will be ensured for those most at risk of GBV. PAH will provide vouchers for soap which will cover three months’ supply. Depending on availability, items will be procured from the local markets in the project locations. The market and risks assessment will be conducted to later decide if vouchers modality can be used. 5. Community awareness on hygiene, sanitation, domestic hygiene, COVID19 mitigation etc. will be delivered to the community at the HH level, including monitoring of implementing the measures, with special focus on mother and care-taker of severely malnourished child. For sustainability of water treatment. PAH will provide protection training and awareness raising to community hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committees’ members, women groups and others. PAH will work closely with Nutrition and FSL partners to combine the project with their activities. Pah will provide trainings for Nutrition staff. 6. Distribution of WASH NFIs. PAH, relying on core pipeline and local market for cash voucher assistance will distribute core WASH NFIs. ensuring meaningful access - separate queues for elders, disable and pregnant women. Carriers will be considered for people with limited mobility. PAH will ensure the distribution will be safe in terms of Covid-19 protection. 7. PAH will conduct WASH assessments with protection analysis using risk equation. in cooperation with clusters and NGOs to provide platform for gap analysis and needs.

3 - Project indicators

MEAL plan will be developed for the project to determine the type of data that need to be collected, frequency of data collection, tools used for data collections, responsible person and track the progress against key results and output indicators and the information captured in different databases. PAH MEAL team will support the development of common data collection methodologies and takes part in joint needs assessments to ensure common standards. Periodic data analysis and interpretation will be made at different levels (at project implementation, location and country program level) and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be employed for collecting data against key indicators and the findings are incorporated into these reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. PAH will utilise sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the project progress towards the end of the project. Quantitative data collection methods will be used for this purpose. Post intervention monitoring (PIM) and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas at the end of the interventions and/or after 6 to 8 weeks of each distribution to assess the short-term impact of the interventions and perception of people on the quality of items distributed, convenience of distribution location, safety of people traveling to and going back from distribution locations, etc. and inform subsequent distributions. HH Interviews, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and observations will be the major methodologies during PIM/PDM. All survey data collection will be supported through mobile-based systems. Some of indicators used will be: # of water points rehabilitated at nutrition sites and schools; # of institutions with functioning, safe and dignified sanitation facilities; # of people (disaggregated by sex and age) received WASH related NFI, including dignity kits; # of people trained and sensitized thanks to hygiene promotion or protection combined with other cross-sectoral topics. Despite of quantitative indicators, PAH will collect qualitative data about assistance, satisfaction, changes resulting from the project. PAH conducts periodic Lessons Learnt reviews which fuels the process of constant improvement. All reports will be shared between relevant stakeholders, including clusters.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CVA will be used only after careful assessment of the market functionality and availability of the products. Protection risk analysis will be conducted as well. Due to high inflation, the market assessment will give information on cost-effectiveness of the activity and determine delivery mechanism. CVA supports local economy and empower the beneficiaries to purchase items of their choice WASH non-food items. The vouchers will be used as part of MHM for women and girls.In frame of multi-sector responses PAH will consider multi-purpose cash assistance, according to Inter-agency Cash working group recommendation.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

In an effort to inform cash-based interventions and better understand market dynamics PAH will monthly conduct the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) in accessible locations and will use the data to inform the project activities. Additionally PAH will conduct monthly price monitoring using cluster unified tools. PAH will conduct protection risk analysis as part of market functionality assessment. PAH is a member of the Inter-agency Cash working group.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback is kept and regularly analysed by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. The implementing programme team, under supervision of Senior Management, if necessary, conduct regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

For the people with special needs, whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited, PAH will try to adjust mechanism paths. In case of beneficiaries with disabilities, with limited mobility, PAH will visit households with the help of the hygiene promoters to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, community hygiene promoters, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to PAH program staff or monitoring team. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committee enumerators who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback personally or via trusted persons.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The PSEA information is part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation. The messages will be given by PAH officers (including women) to groups of beneficiaries during FGD baseline studies, KII, education campaigns. PSEA will be important part of training of community members engaged in the project - volunteers.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Data for assessment, baseline and monitoring will be collected through institutions and HH survey, focus group discussions with representatives of various groups disaggregated by gender and age, people with disabilities, interviews with key informants, authorities and other NGOs present in the area. Each time PAH and/or enumerators will inform about the scope and aim of data collection. Collected data will be a base for designing activities, adjusting to the needs or external circumstances. Special attention will be given to women and girls’ voices, PWD and members of minority groups. As the GBV cases are often unreported, PAH officers will engage the caseworkers from the community and will be present in the community as often as possible to build trust and encourage effective psychosocial support. TOR for all activities will be presented to the authorities, plans are discussed with the community. People are engaged as community-based hygiene promoters, water user commitees' members, translators, enumerators. Regular meetings with the community representatives during intervention implementation will be organised to present plans of the activities and receive feedback on the implementation process, as well as suggestions for improvement. At all phases of the intervention, PAH staff will always consult and engage with the beneficiaries and local authorities to ensure that the decisions are made jointly with their prior knowledge. To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be mitigated by ensuring that they are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. For example, eligibility criteria for identifying beneficiaries for WASH NFI distribution will be used and the following categories of beneficiaries will be considered: people in areas of IPC 4 and 5 and WASH vulnerability 4 and 5; female-headed households, elderly-headed households, child-headed households, unaccompanied children, people with less or no community link, people living with disabilities, elderly and people with chronic sicknesses, pregnant and lactating women with little support, disease-affected population, children and women, responsible for fetching water. During the registration for distribution, PAH will endure door to door registration to ensure that even those who are not able to come to the registration points are registered including their vulnerability status. Women and girls will be consulted separately on their needs. PAH will ensure that decisions pertaining location of WASH facilities are made in consultation with women and girls. Separate focus group discussions will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff or protection officer so that they can open up During registration/distribution, PAH do registration from household to household and will ensure that order is maintained by involving community leaders, crowed controllers and creating separate ques for men and women, children and different categories of people. Protection risk assessment will be conducted before deciding the distribution site to ensure that distribution sites are safe and accessible to the beneficiaries. PAH will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear age gender and disability (AGD) data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level for each location. PAH will achieve it through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and HH surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, the host community and returnee population. PAH works closely with local authorities, institutions and NGOs to clearly understand the cultural norms, inter-communal dynamics and potential risks. PAH will involve local actors in their mitigation. Thanks to comprehensive cooperation PAH can avoid aid diversion, ensure ownership of project results and involvement of the community in monitoring and control of the results. PAH will coordinate closely with partners of different sectors or will put efforts to join multi-sectoral responses to ensure synergies and comprehensive responses. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, including logistic and security challenges. The analysis is regularly repeated and the security situation is monitored continuously. Ongoing coordination and communication with the log cluster are done to support the delivery of project items. NGO forum and OCHA are focal points for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. Thanks to close coordination with clusters, PAH has diversified sources of information, between others about explosive hazards, local history of cooperation with stakeholders, local regulations that may try to include taxation or influence beneficiary selections. Experienced team of PAH officers, supported by the humanitarian community, can deal with different stakeholders and solve complex problems without compromising humanitarian values and highest standards. Protection assessments and analysis capturing information on access, safety, dignity, participation and power dynamics will be the key methodology where needs are analysed, prioritized with clear recommendations captured from the beneficiaries.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Accessing water and other WASH facilities is a significant factor influencing GBV in the country, indicated as one of the key moments imposing risk of GBV on women and girls. Therefore, providing safe water sources in short distances from the settlements reduces the risk of GBV. Additionally, gender disaggregated sanitation facilities and access to MHM kits can significantly support women and girls. Mainly, thanks to community mobilization, PAH officers, water user committees, hygiene promoters can spread the protection messaging and encourage seeking for help. As PAH provides multi-sector responses, including WASH and Protection, the GBV cases will be referred to PAH Protection unit.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

PAH has designed and construct flood resilient WASH facilities, including household level latrines to be constructed with a use of local material. These will be implemented in the flood prone locations where feasible. Such solution will provide people access to water in the flooded areas and decrease the need of displacement.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The household safe, hygienic environment and safely prepared food with clean water, sanitation and healthy daily practices in terms of hand-washing may significantly prevent malnutrition in the family. In the case of domestic food production, storage and sales on the local market, access to water, soap and the proper hygienic practices are crucial for safety of the food. Moreover, if the safe water access is ensured, women can limit the time used for collecting water and take care of livelihood activities instead. Moreover, access to water an development of irrigation system will support food production. PAH provides multi-sector responses, including WASH and FSL.
