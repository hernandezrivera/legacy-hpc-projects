Project Summary

The proposed HRP 2021 intervention contributes towards the enhanced protective environment and protection of women, men, girls and boys in IDP, returnee and host communities in Pibor Administrative Area and Akobo East, South Sudan. To achieve this, the project has the following specific objectives:
1. To undertake effective and timely protection assessments and monitoring in targeted areas for timely and robust humanitarian and protection decisions, influence and actions
2. To strengthen community based protection mechanisms for risk reduction and promotion of local peace and security for affected population
3. To provide individual protection assistance to address priority protection needs for vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in targeted areas
4. To support ‘safe programming’ (protection mainstreaming) in other sectors including WASH, Food Security, Education in Emergencies, and Gender.
The geographical targeted area is Pibor, Jonglei State which is a critical area for populations in IPC Phase 4 and 5. The proposed action will significantly contribute to the Protection Cluster (General Protection) Cluster Objectives, particularly: Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks.

1 - Needs

The multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted by Oxfam and partners found that protection needs were stemming from the devastating intercommunal violence, cattle raiding and age set fighting being experienced in GPAA. The age set fighting has led to loss of lives and displacement, and these conflicts have aggravated the prevalence of GBV, women and child abductions and killings. According to the survey, respondents in all locations listed many risks affecting their community; among those were physical violence (52% female and 47% male), sexual violence against women and girls (54% female and 35% male), early and forced marriage (55% female and 34% male), domestic violence (46% female and 37% male) and child labour (44% female and 27% male) as the major protection threats reported. Other risks identified were inability to access services, lack of basic services and forced labour. The respondents were also asked about their actions if they found a GBV survivor or people with disabilities and 58% across all locations said they would take them to a nearby health facility or report to local police or local authorities. A significant proportion of respondents said they did not know what to do. The survey also found that 67% of people across all locations reported that they have never participated in any awareness raising event related to protection and gender issues. According to Oxfam's internal protection reports, project reports, security updates, market assessments, Gender Analysis, research on 'negative masculinity' and 'Community self-protection and coping strategies for accessing water, livelihoods, and food’ and context updates for Pibor, the following protection threats and risks are observed in the target location: multiple displacement of women, men, and children by inter-communal violence, revenge killing, age set violence, prevalence of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV), violence against women (VAW), limited access to basic services due to rain and inaccessible road networks, increasing use of negative coping strategies such as early and forced marriages for girls, closure of markets during age set violence and other forms of public violence. Ultimately, women, men and children have limited access to their required services, rights, and goods. Besides limited freedoms, people living in 'fear of violence' and the prevalence of violent cattle raids, there is a need to improve the protection of civilians in Pibor. The relative prevalence of peace also brings opportunities for the potential return of IDPs to rural homes. Supporting safe return and 'durable solutions' for IDPs is therefore critical. The HRP project therefore aims to improve the protective environment and protection of civilians. An estimated 1,042,000 people have been affected by floods in South Sudan. Jonglei State and the Greater Pibor Administrative Area are the worst affected (with a total of 495,000 people affected). Flood-affected people’s urgent needs include food and livelihood support, emergency shelter and non-food items (ES/NFI), and water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), health and protection services.

2 - Project activities

Proposed activities intended to contribute to the Protection Cluster and HRP objectives include: 1. Protection assessments and reporting. This will also include trend analysis and situational analysis. 2. Protection advocacy through local, state, national, and international level influencing towards enhancing the protective environment for women, men, girls and boys in South Sudan. 3. Strengthening and supporting Community Based Protection: CPCs and Water and Accountability Committees (under WASH Sector in Oxfam) will have their knowledge and community protection skills appropriately and contextually enhanced towards identifying, mitigating, preventing and even responding to protection threats. Attention will be given to youth, women and 'spear masters' (traditional religious leaders) based on the critical role they play in risk mitigation in communities. 4. Information dissemination, awareness raising and campaigns on protection messages including GBV will carried out. This will cover information on services available, access, appropriate rights and entitlements (as informed by the protection risk analysis), Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), among others. This activity will include information dissemination on protection messages including GBV. Besides increasing awareness of rights, entitlements, the expected result of this activity is help seeking behaviour, improved access to services (i.e. affected beneficiaries will know how, where and when to access which services). This activity is vital for AAP purposes.5. Referrals and Individual Protection Assistance. Referrals will be strengthened through revitalizing and communicating service maps and towards encouraging 'help seeking behaviour' among women, men, girls and boys affected by the risks including GBV. Guided by existing SOPs and the Cluster, and informed by the protection analysis, Oxfam will provide individual protection assistance through cash and NFIs to support individuals for protective purposes. This project seeks to strengthen community-based protection for risk mitigation and improved protective environment for women, girls, men, and boys in Pibor and Akobo. For close monitoring, both a static and mobile approach will be adopted at the field though a roving response to enhance protection monitoring (of trends) and assessment. The proposed project seeks to sustain the localized and previously established community-based protection mechanisms that have been active. These mechanisms include Community Protection Committees (CPCs), 4 in Pibor (Verther, Pibor, Lokuruyanga, Lekuangule), and 6 in Akobo (Bilkey, Mareng, & Pultoak denjok and Nyadith). This will complement existing and ongoing interventions including emergency food assistance. Complementary will be enhanced through supported risk mitigation, supported mechanisms, and avoidance of duplication. In addition, Oxfam’s team will be utilized in areas outside Jonglei with high needs. The proposed project is in line with key strategies for humanitarian response that includes protection and allocation priorities. Through activities which strengthen community-based protection and improved protective environment, the grant will promote the purpose of the allocation strategy: supporting populations facing greatest needs from armed conflict, displacement, hunger and natural disasters of Jonglei and supporting populations facing the greatest risk through information dissemination and individual cash for protection. It will support local initiatives though strengthening existing community-based protection mechanisms (50/50 male and female) ie. Community Protection Committees, Disability Committees, Youth, women association, WASH committees, Accountability and Protection Committees among others. This will help promotion of active participation of women and youth in strengthening local peace and security through community-based platforms, dialogues, and information dissemination on risk mitigation.

Experience has shown that while needs for referrals affect the whole population, the greatest needs relate to GBV affecting women and girls, which is why Oxfam anticipates that most target beneficiaries for referrals and Individual Protection Assistance will be women and girls. The selection of individuals for protection assistance will be based on the analysis and referrals from other protection agencies. Information dissemination, awareness raising and campaigns targets approximately a total of 12,000 (5000 men, 4613 women, 1077 boys, and 1410 girls). This is inclusive of all individuals reached through other protection activities proposed, without double counting. This project estimates that each participant in the training Oxfam will conduct and leaders reached through direct engagement with Oxfam will benefit approximately 10 more beneficiaries. Targeted beneficiaries from the host communities and IDPs and returnee will be identified through close collaboration and proactive engagement with targeted populations, community-based protection structures and local authorities. Regular assessments and protection analysis within targeted areas will be done continuously in order to identify risks facing women,men, boys and girls. The project intends to address identified risks through referrals, information dissemination to enhance help seeking behavior, individual protection assistance, local advocacy and strengthening community-based structures for risk reduction.

Oxfam has ongoing market-based programming in the proposed locations, and will build on established networks and capacity and ensure that assistance is provided where markets are functional and responsive to an increase in demand.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The Protection team will identify, verify, and distribute cash for protection. Taking into consideration the SOP for Cash for Protection of Oxfam, protection mainstreaming principles through the risk reduction approach, referrals and protection analysis for specific areas, the identified beneficiaries will gain access to cash which will contribute to protection outcomes. For example, there might be children who may be forced to marriage and denied access to education or survivors of SGBV who might be using negative coping mechanisms and 'transport for protection' or legal assistance is required for those at risk. Referrals for accessing 'cash for protection' will be evaluated according to the criteria of contribution to the protection outcome and not merely that an individual is vulnerable.Following the protection analysis supported by the protection risk assessment, to augment risk reductions regarding the protection activities, and referrals. Regarding IPA programming, IPA will address an actual and specific protection need for a specific individual or household, and not merely target “populations of concerns”. IPA will support referrals in the targeted locations. Oxfam will build on existing practice of ‘Cash for Protection’ and ‘NFI’s for Protection’. The target for individuals who are at risk or exposed to protection risks. For example, in the past, Oxfam has learnt that some beneficiaries may not allow children to go to school simply because they do not have school fees, hence exposing children to be involved in armed activities or exposed to early marriages; some individuals fleeing violence may not have the transport say because they are physically challenged, and may need transport for fleeing violence; at times there are women and girls who have been exposed to GBV and may need transport to access GBV health services, legal services, or even transport. Other examples, also include assistance reduce or stop the use of negative coping mechanisms with protection risks – with the hope to improve the protection outcome. The kind of assistance, the target, and amount is determined by the protection risk analysis conducted in a specific area. This will explicitly address a specific protection risk or need. The purpose is to have a protective outcome. The protection risk analysis will inform individuals, homes, and referrals will inform the protection team of specific individuals exposed with risks including SBGV risks. In addition to SOP for Cash for Protection, the IPA will also take into consideration vulnerability criteria of Inter-Agency SOPs on Identification and Referral Persons with Specific Needs, therefore the protection assistance on cash for protection is conditional.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Oxfam has a zero tolerance policy on PSEA and ensures all staff andvolunteers are trained on PSEA and sign up to its PSEA policy. A PSEA communication strategy, and appropriate feedback and complaints/reporting mechanisms will be in place from the inception of the project with beneficiaries and stakeholders informed of the available mechanisms via multiples means from inception meeting onwards.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will ensure equality of representation of vulnerable groups during assessments and all monitoring and evaluation processes. Project staff will be locally employed from the field which will support with translation of messaging into local languages to maximise accessibility. Members of vulnerable groups will be invited to participate in community consultation sessions, surveys etc.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will conduct a quarterly Project Review Reflection with Community, CPCs, Local Leaders, Youth and Women. This will ensure ownership, one way of getting feedback and complaints from the primary local actors. In addition, the reflection will draw lessons which will be used inform local community engagement and project implementation. The result of this activity will be a review reflection report. The project will reflect on the progress, challenges and opportunities in relation to project implementation. Isolated exit interviews will be done soon after distribution of cash or NFIs to beneficiaries. This will be done to solicit opinion and satisfaction of the services and process done with the beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Internally, Oxfam has a system of documenting internal monthly protection reports. The monthly contextual updates will be fed to the Protection Cluster towards monitoring of trends. The analysis is also shared and used by other sectors so as to inform 'safe programming'. The project will include a quarterly Project Review Reflection with Community, CPCs, Local Leaders, Youth and Women. This activity, with the support of the MEAL, will ensure ownership, one way of getting feedback and complaints from the primary local actors. In addition, the reflection will draw lessons which will be used inform local community engagement and project implementation. The result of this activity will be a review reflection report. The project will reflect on the progress, challenges and opportunities in relation to project implementation. In addition, the activity will go on to inform and update the conflict and context analysis of the project location.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Oxfam will work closely with the affected population, local authorities, and host communities to ensure that potential risks to the successful implementation of the project such as safety and security) are identified on a timely basis with appropriated mitigating measures adopted accordingly. Furthermore, Oxfam will undertake GBV safety audit and mitigation analysis on protection project interventions in the project to ensure that the intervention promotes safe and dignified access to assistance among women and girls, boys and men. The GBV risk mitigation analysis will be carried out on livelihoods kits to be distributed to comply with ‘Do No Harm’ principle. Protection partner will establish links with GBV actors on the ground and protection partners to deliver project activities. The strengthening of protection and GBV referral pathways will be undertaken, and pathways made known to the population to enable for easy reporting of protection and GBV concerns. Also, on spot checks will be conducted with beneficiaries through the help desks to identify and report protection concerns for attentions and risk mitigation measures strengthened.
