Project Summary

Skills development is critical for poverty reduction, existing gender inequalities make it harder for women everywhere, including in refugee contexts. Cultural practices such as early marriages and early child bearing deny women access to education hence hinder them from accessing decent employment opportunities. One important strategy to address this barrier is to increase access to community-based technical skills training. However, it is important that these measures are not excluding male beneficiaries. If the project focus will be on young women exclusively that could easily increase protection risks and GBV cases with intra and extra household violence due to male community members who already feel disempowered and robbed of their role as family providers.
Therefore, an equal number of adolescent and young women and men from carefully selected most vulnerable households will be selected. The beneficiaries will receive gender- and context-appropriate training on income generating activities in line with local commodity chains and market opportunities. Examples include agricultural processing, soap making, embroidery, and animal breeding, welding, carpentry, hairdressing. To do this, an assessment will be carried out in Maradi and Tillabery and the surrounding villages for the choice of trainings according to their profitability and for the identification of potential beneficiaries. 200 Beneficiaries will be put into age and culturally appropriate small groups that will be learning together. They will meet 2 to 3 times a week to learn technical skills, business and saving skills.
Upon completion of their skills training, adolescent boys and girls will be beneficiaries of the small business kits. These are start-up kits to be given to allow each of them to carry out the activity which they have learned in the skills training.
The main economic activities of displaced people living in Maradi and Tillaberi, before the crisis, was agriculture, animal husbandry (pastoralism). The crisis disrupted the lives of many by forcing them to abandon their livestock and stop their economic activities. The project will equip 400 women affected in Maradi and Tillaberi, with livestock in order to reduce the risk of child malnutrition and help them to build resilience. The project will serve as a complementary activity to other livelihood activities in the target area, by allowing vulnerable women affected by the crisis to generate income and build resilience.

Besoins

The security crises continue to worsen access to food and livelihoods in Maradi and Tillaberi for both refugee and host population. The humanitarian space is quickly shrinking as tensions within communities (farmers and herders) and attacks and threats against the civilian population increase. In Maradi the number of Nigerian refugees fleeing Nigeria because of these threats now stands at 50,000 (up to December 2019), the vast proportion of which only made the crossing over the past six months. In the Tillaberi region 138,000 people are displaced including 100.000 IDPs, 38.000 refugees.Indeed, there is an evident pressure on the existing and available food resources in the welcome villages. Refugee when fleeing from their homes in Nigeria, Mali, did not have time or thought to carry any livestock or personal goods because the urgent need was to save their own lives. Supplies in most households throughout the rest of the area remain not sufficient to cover consumption demand. The recent needs assessment conducted by WV in the area shows that 63% of the households have a HDDS weak and 57% of households have a food stock which cannot exceed on week to finish and most of them don’t have another source of income after harvest. An estimated 50,000 people is food insecure and in need of humanitarian assistance. Access to livelihood and food has worsened as people flee for safety first before considering their basic needs. World Vision seeks additional funding and support to respond to this most fundamental of needs – food – so that gradually vulnerable communities can be assisted and helped to improve access to livelihood and then hence food access for the most vulnerable children.
