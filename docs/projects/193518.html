Project Summary

Project Objective:
To enhance the capacity of agricultural assets of vulnerable farmers in the middle area, Khan Younis and Rafah governorate of the Gaza Strip who are affected by humanitarian crises to restore
livelihoods.
Special objectives:
1. To rehabilitated 80 Dunums of open-land field 80 Dunums belong to 40 farmers (at least 4 female farmers their ages between 20-65 years and 36 male farmers headed households their age between 20-65-year-old).
2. To rehabilitated 60 greenhouses belong to 60 farmers in the targeted area ( at least 6 female
farmers their ages between 20 -65-year-old and 95 male farmers headed households their ages between 20-65-year-old).
3. To rehabilitated 125 poultry farms and install 125 kW solar power belong to 125 farmers in the targeted area ( at least 10 female farmers their age between 20 -65-year-old and 115 male farmers headed households their ages between 20-65-year-old).
4. To rehabilitated 100 Livestock farms in the targeted area belong to 100 farmers ( at least 10 female farmers their ages between 20 -65-year-old and 90 male farmers headed households their ages between 20-65-year-old).
5. To rehabilitate agricultural five water wells (25 PH) and install 25 kW renewable for each water wells in the targeted area to improve water access to 400 dunums to benefit at least 50 farmers.

The project activities will be implemented in Rafah, Khan Younis, and the Middle Area.
All project activities will be implemented by the parallel in all regions and completely coordinated with the Ministry of Agriculture and the Food Security Sector.
The estimated number of direct beneficiaries is 325 farmers (At least 30 female farmers and 295 males farmers headed households their ages between 20-65-year-old) while the total number of the farmers and their families is 1820 members.(478 male, 460 female, 450 boys, 432 girls) with 35 PWDs.
The project is designed to urgent support to protect and restore the livelihoods of vulnerable farmers who are affected by humanitarian crises, blockades, and recent hostility in Gaza.
The project will be based on the Do No Harm framework with a gender transformative approach and all targeted households will be selected according to protection-related and socio-economic vulnerability criteria.
The project's objectives match the Food Security Sector (FSS) objective #(1) The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security, and productive capacity restored/enhanced. Moreover, project objectives aligned to the HRP 2023 oPt, Strategic objective country #( 3): "The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued".
through the Implementation of project activities that will contribute to protecting and restoring livelihoods for vulnerable farmers who are affected by humanitarian crises.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip faces a chronic humanitarian crisis that made most of the persons in need of some form of urgent humanitarian assistance, This crisis has been driven by a longstanding Israeli blockade and an internal Palestinian political divide and exacerbated by recurrent escalations in Gaza, this had extremely increased poverty & unemployment rates where the overall unemployment rate in Gaza reached 44.1 per cent during the second quarter of 2022 according to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), Furthermore “one-third of the Palestinian population, 1.79 million people, suffer from food insecurity. Of those 1.1 million Palestinians are severely food insecure. Most of them (90 per cent) live in the Gaza Strip” According to (the WFP Palestine Country Brief August 2022), the (FSS-HNO2023) indicate that an estimated 1.8 million individuals (33.6% of the population) suffer from moderate to severe food insecurity4. In 2022, approximately 1 out of 3 people experienced some form of food insecurity, 84% (1.54 million people) of which are expected to need humanitarian assistance, On the other hand, according to the Ministry Of Agriculture (MoA) in the Gaza Strip, the last hostilities in August 2022 hit tens of agricultural lands, greenhouses, livestock, and poultry farms, causing damages and losses estimated at One Million dollars. Al Nakheel has investigated humanitarian needs using several methods in KhanYouis, the Middle area, and Rafah five focus groups discussions were conducted (14 female farmers, and 61 male farmers) in the three targeted areas Their age was between 20 to 65 years with different marital statuses and Six Semi-structured interviews executed during the field visits in the affected area were to some owners of the greenhouse, open-field land farmers, poultry farmers, livestock farmers, and water wells owners. With female and male farmers (random samples). The main finding (1)- 100% of the attendee's farmers stated that they are affected directly by Gaza humanitarian crisises. (2)-92% of the attendee's farmers stated that they suffer from the high cost of agro-input as well as they suffer from the fluctuation in prices produced crops. (3)- 86 % of the attendees determined that there is a lack of irrigation water as well as a lack of power to operate water wells. (4)-100 % of the attendees stated that they are in need of cash assistance to help them maintain their livelihood the next season. (5)-100 % of the attendee's farmers stated that high cost of raising poultry and fluctuations in selling prices.(6)- 85 % of the attendee's livestock farmers stated the need to rehabilitate their farms to beat the vagaries of the weather to protect their sheep from death.(7)-100 %of attendees' female farmers illustrated their need to maintain their livelihoods to reactivate their production. (8). The people with disabilities indicate that they are actually in a bad economic situation and their need for the source of income for secure food for their families while most of the income is spent on healthcare and they could not invest in the rehabilitation of their farms. (9). Females focused on their rights to benefit from the agricultural assets of their fathers or grandfathers - equal with the males. Additionally accordantly to the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2022, the (MSNA) key findings indicate the number of people in need (PIN), in the Gaza strip in 2023 is 1,188,989 (53.4% of the total population) and 91.8% of the respondent households expressed their need for any form of humanitarian aid or assistance in the future; 99.2% among them are female-headed households. Women in the oPt are particularly disadvantaged with regard to their limited share of agricultural holdings and the limited economic opportunities available for women to generate income. In conclusion, there are no differences between the reported needs of female and male farmers. And the farmers have horrible damage and their needs have never been changed since 2019, and there increase in the number of affected and vulnerable farmers. The intervention will be built on the understanding of gender-specific needs and expectations as well as socio-economic factors and cultural sensitivities and raised PSEA awareness as well as.Finally, the farmers need immediate support to maintain their livelihood.

2 - Activities

This intervention aims to improve food security and livelihoods for vulnerable farmers and households in the Gaza strip through protecting and restoring the agricultural assets held by male and female farmers who are vulnerable to humanitarian crises in Rafah, Khan Younis, and the Middle area and improving food security for vulnerable households in Rafah, Khan Younis, and the Middle area. The activities are designed in coordination with MoA and FSS to eliminate overlapping with other actors. , The selection beneficiaries criteria are based on the vulnerability criteria such as (the farmer is registered in MoA, the farmer from small-scale farmers, The land in the targeted area and affected by humanitarian crises, Agriculture is the only source of income, the farmer is the breadwinner of the family, the farmer who has not received similar assistance since 12 months ago, the additional criteria and priority will be given to Female-headed households, Farmers with disabilities in their households, Farmers who has a large number of families, and High dependency ratio ) and the identified needs, gender equity, sensitivity without any bias, and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. to ensuring equal benefit. Although traditionally most farmers are male, attention will be paid to targeting farms headed by women or those that employ women, this project will target women farmers who are the head of households, hold agriculture assets, and actually practice agriculture by themselves. 5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The cash modality is the most suitable modality to address the identified needs for open land and greenhouses farmers, it's the fastest and safest way to ensure the intervention is successful and the follow-up methods are easy due to the different needs of each farmer to network water, type seeding, tilling, plastic sheet, etc.. furthermore, farmers have the full experience to do this without any help, However, the farmers will receive the cash in three installments and after each instalment, the project staff including the agricultural engineer will check the progress and achieve the farmer into the rehabilitation of their open land or the greenhouses to recommending the next installments. On another hand, Service delivery modalities will be addressing the rehabilitation of the water wells, and livestock and poultry farms due to this type of intervention needs experts and specialists in determining the technical specifications for the rehabilitation of wells, farms, and installation of renewable to ensure the intervention is done efficiently and effectively, where the farmers lack the experience and ability to do this by themself. moreover, project staff including the agricultural engineer and electrical engineer will follow up and monitor the rehabilitation works of farms and water wells including the installation of renewable for the water wells and poultry farms.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is a key driver to maximising positive impact. The proposed project mainstreams protection, including Al Nakheel, following the Code of Ethics and Conduct ensuring that the beneficiaries are treated with respect and dignity, and preventing abuse. The Partners through the nature of work understand the concept of "do no harm" and consecrate their effort to provide the received an agricultural assets to the local community to strengthen peace among them. Therefore, the staff is highly recommended not to use their power during the project implementing stages and not to be an element of the conflict. And not to use this agricultural input to support their sides in the conflict to prevent harm to the community. Partners follow the Rights-Based Approach Manual as it deals with beneficiaries as rights holders rather than service recipients and empowering them to protect and claim their rights. Partners use an effective complaint system so that beneficiaries can give feedback on their treatment and quality of services Partners follow the humanitarian principle of DO Not Harm through preventing and activities that may cause negative outcomes on the target beneficiaries. The project will be adequately announced in the targeted communities using various outreach tools to give equal opportunities to benefit from the project. The targeted groups will be selected based on transparent selection criteria that ensure humanitarian assistance is delivered without bias, and with a view to alleviating existing inequalities and vulnerabilities. The selection of beneficiaries will fulfill the transparent and clear criteria for the project to be reducing suffering, maintaining human dignity, and reach the most valuable people. Gender has been strongly incorporated into the design of the project. the partners adopted “Zero Tolerance” regarding any action causing GBV, and all the staff members signed and trained on GBV and harassment prevention policies PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse). The gender focal point in Al Nakheel will be responsible for monitoring and following up on any Gender-related complaints. The gender risks facing project implementation are protection risks associated with cash programming in Gaza including an increase in domestic violence and/or intra-household power imbalances (i.e. who controls and spends the cash) and the exclusion of women or individuals with specific needs in the process of awarding the cash (e.g. due to secondary social status and restrictions on movement) Mitigation measures include: - use of female staff members to make female household members more comfortable. - in-person visits if households are not able to collect cash. - a beneficiary orientation that informs male and female household members about the potential risks, especially for female-headed households as well as an introduction to the Community Accountability and Reporting. - on a case-by-case basis, staff will conduct household visits for orientation. - training staff on the detection of potential protection risks.
