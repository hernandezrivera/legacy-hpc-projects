Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to contribute to the sustainable improvement of food security and nutritional status for vulnerable households in Nara district, Mali. This will be achieved through the achievement of two specific objectives:
Specific objective 1: Strengthen the capacity of vulnerable households to meet their basic food and nutritional needs, avoid negative coping strategies, and prevent acute malnutrition in children under five and pregnant and lactating women;
Specific objective 2: Strengthen coverage of malnutrition prevention services for children under five in vulnerable food insecure households.

Needs

The population of Nara district is facing an early onset of the 2018 lean season following a poor harvest in late 2017. The long lean season is already impacting vulnerable households’ resilience, and the months ahead are likely to see reduced food consumption, increased recourse to negative coping strategies, and higher levels of acute malnutrition.Nara district is projected to remain in IPC Phase 2 (Stressed) through September 2018, with 22% of households in either Phase 2 or Phase 3 (Crisis). During a needs assessment conducted by the IRC in Nara district in February 2018, 80% of households surveyed judged the 2017–18 growing season to have been poor or very poor. The most commonly cited reason was poor rainfall. Rainfall in Nara district in 2017 was below the 20 year average, and was particularly poor in July, when farmers typically sow their crops, at 62% of average. The average land area cultivated was 32% smaller than in a typical year, and the average amounts of millet and sorghum harvested were 69% and 82% lower, respectively. The average duration of household food stocks was three months, as compared to seven months in a typical year. Low food stocks directly affect food consumption: on average, 80% of food consumed by households in Nara comes from their own agropastoral production. As a result of the poor harvest in 2017, households are now more dependent on markets to meet their food needs. As of February, only two months after the harvest, purchase of cereals at the main markets in Nara district was up between 21% and 35% as compared to a typical year. This increased reliance on markets is already causing price increases – prices for millet and sorghum are 25% and 27% higher, respectively, than in a typical year – further restricting vulnerable households’ access to food. Vulnerable households widely report resorting to negative coping strategies that risk reducing their resilience when faced with future shortages. Currently, pastoral households’ average herd size is 29, as compared to 38 in a typical year. The most common reason given for this is increased sale of livestock in order to meet food needs; average annual income from livestock sale is 45% higher than normal. Many households reported bartering their stocks of peanuts for cereals in order to meet their food needs. In typical years, peanuts serve as a form of savings and are used to buy livestock, meaning that depletion of peanut stocks will likely impact households’ ability to recover from the current lean season and resist future shocks. Another widely reported negative coping strategy is increased seasonal migration. Youth in Nara district typically migrate within Mali or to neighboring countries to find work after the harvest, but households reported that, in anticipation of a poor harvest, many youth migrated before the harvest. The overall proportion of youth migrating nearly tripled as compared to a typical year, from 25% to 71%. In the absence of many youth, reliance on child labor during the harvest increased. This worrying food security situation may contribute to a deterioration of the nutritional status of under five (U5) children in Nara district. As of August 2017, prevalence of GAM in Koulikoro region was at 7.8% while prevalence of SAM was at 1.3%. While district level data on GAM and SAM prevalence is not available, it is likely that prevalence in Nara district is significantly higher than the mean rates in Koulikoro region. Over the past three years, the number of SAM patients admitted to health centers in Nara has been on average 171% of the number that would be expected per the SAM prevalence in Koulikoro, indicating that actual SAM prevalence in Nara is likely higher. The number of admissions in the first quarter of 2018 was 33% higher than in the first quarter of 2017, an increase from 483 to 641 admissions, indicating that need for screening and treatment services is likely to remain high for the foreseeable future.

Activities

Households will receive food assistance through vouchers with a total per household value of 120,000 FCFA (approximately $218). The IRC estimates that this transfer amount will enable households to meet 55% of their caloric needs for 9 months during the project period. The transfer amount is also aligned with the recommendations of Jigisemejiri, Mali’s national social safety net program. Vouchers will be distributed three times on a quarterly basis. Each transfer will cover 55% of 3 months of food needs. Should the project start on June 1, as planned, the first distribution will take place in August 2018, at the height of the agropastoral lean season, and will help households meet their food needs and avoid recourse to negative coping strategies during this difficult time. The second distribution will take place in November 2018, after the harvest, and is intended to allow households to use recently harvested foodstuffs to replenish their food stocks, rather than immediately consuming them. The third and final distribution will take place in February 2019, at the start of the pastoral lean season and a few months before the start of the 2019 agricultural lean season. This final distribution is intended to shield households’ food stocks from early depletion, effectively delaying the start of the 2019 lean season for assisted households.during the 2nd semester, IRC will also distributed 40 000 FCFA additional in August 2019The project will include a limited scale pilot of electronic voucher use. Most households will receive paper vouchers, a modality that the IRC has previously used to provide food assistance in Mali. However, 200 households (approximately 4% of total beneficiaries) will receive electronic vouchers via the MasterCard Aid platform, a platform that has not yet been used by any humanitarian actor in Mali. , Improving delivery of cash and voucher assistance is a key priority for the IRC globally, and the IRC has successfully used MasterCard Aid to deliver food vouchers in other countries, including Côte d’Ivoire, Niger, and Somalia. In the context of Mali, use of electronic vouchers rather than paper vouchers can improve the timeliness and efficiency of aid delivery, and provide beneficiaries with greater flexibility and discretion. By piloting this modality in Mali, the IRC aims to demonstrate its relevance in the context of Mali and to identify any context specific obstacles and lessons learned to apply to future cash and voucher programming in Mali.In response to the major gaps in prevention, screening and referral of acute malnutrition, the project will include complementary nutrition programming linked to nutrition activities already being implemented by the IRC in the targeted communes with funding from ECHO. Mothers of U5 children from households receiving food assistance will be provided with measurement of upper arm circumference (MUAC) tape and trained using the Mother MUAC approach, enabling them to screen their children at home. A community based monitoring system will be put in place to facilitate referral of children suffering from SAM to local health facilities, where the IRC already supports treatment services. The IRC will further use a counseling based approach to improve mothers’ knowledge of good infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices. Complementary activities will not comprise more than 20% of the total project budget.
