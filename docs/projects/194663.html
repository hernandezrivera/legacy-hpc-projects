Project Summary

For sustainability, the Project will continue partnering with national agencies, Local Authorities and the communities to build the capacities of partners and Community beneficiaries in order to deliver quality and friendly services to the key affected populations. Capacity building will be in the areas of Physical, institutional strengthening, Behavioral/attitudinal (Addressing 3 Key Barriers to WASH; in accordance to the millennium development goal no. (4) on poverty reduction, improved health, and access to safe water which will never be equitably met unless the disabled people are included, Monitoring and Evaluation, Group development, management of GBV and Disability, HIV technical capacity development, networking and referrals during service delivery.
TITI Foundation has been actively involved in the WASH Cluster activities and 5 Ws reporting for over 4 years with interventions in the targeted locations. The project will build the capacity of the WASH user Committees to develop community action plans on CLTS and sub-committees comprising of the women, PWD, youth, men and the elderly to monitor implementation. These structures will use community dialogue days and community outreaches to reach out to community members.
TITI continues to support hygiene promotion activities through public health campaigns and the distribution of essential supplies, such as buckets, soap, dignity hygiene kits (ensure the safe distribution of dignity kits to survivors of GBV and this will be based on gender, age and disabilities in attention to other relevant aspects of diversity. )and strengthening referral pathways in Lakes, Central Equatoria states. TF will adopt the WASH Cluster county prioritization by focusing its interventions through strategic partnerships, we will continue to capitalize on existing opportunities to make a lasting contribution to improved health and WaSH outcomes with full community ownership focusing on: building local skills and supporting systems for sustainability; improving WASH service demand, access, and for most vulnerable groups, and leveraging social determinants of health to improve health outcomes. The Project will continue this relationship with the states, counties and Payams but engage even much more with each of them in a dialogue that ensures that project resources will achieve the highest level of efficiency and effectiveness in contributing towards agreed goals and objectives.

1 - Needs

Climate change continues to impact different sectors, particularly agriculture, which is one of the most climate-sensitive economic sectors. Above-normal rainfall for the third consecutive year in 2021 led to prolonged flooding, which impacted areas that had not flooded in previous years. An estimated 835,000 people were affected by severe flooding between May and December 2021, and 80% of those affected were from Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states. Many flood-displaced people, including those who were displaced by the 2020 flood were unable to return to their homes until early 2022, if at all. The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage to water facilities, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, and intensified existing vulnerabilities of affected people, which include high poverty rates, limited access to basic services including health and education, high prevalence of disease outbreaks, and widespread displacement. Covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks, as well as other household-level stressors, for example, gender-based violence (GBV) and poor water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, are some of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the country. In addition, there are 6 million vulnerable host community/non-displaced people, of whom some are older persons or infirm, or persons with disabilities. According to the intersectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs are most concerning in Fangak, Duk and Pibor counties in Jonglei and Cueibet and Rumbek East counties in Lakes, which were classified as in catastrophic need. An estimated 6 million people’s living standards and their well-being will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. WASH conditions were found to be most severe in the Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. The counties classified with the greatest needs are Tonj East, Fangak, Panyikang, Canal-Pigi and Twic East. Despite the fact that major waterways were replenished by the long rainy season, only 39% of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide, dropping to 34% in rural areas. In certain states, such as Unity, Warrap, Jonglei and Upper Nile, 33% of households travel for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities. In addition, difficulty in securing enough water is only part of the issue; extremely low coverage of water infrastructure explains that 49% of households reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their main water source. The situation is particularly concerning in Upper Nile whereas where as many as 71% of households reportedly use rivers or puddles as their main water source, while in certain counties, such as Canal/Pigi, Ayod, and Manyo virtually all households relied on surface water. Surface water is often untreated prior to consumption and this, along with the widespread practice of open defecation, creates a substantial public health risk, especially during floods, when latrine pits seep into waterways. Limited access to water is also compounded by only 14% of households reporting having at least one jerrycan and/or bucket to safely store water. (HNO 2022).According to REACH, January 2022; anticipates that by 2022 and 2023 with the decreased levels of access to resources, logistical constraints due to rainy season road blockage, limited access and the uncertainty in the availability and prices of key WASH items in the local markets has impacted the ability for humanitarian agencies to respond to humanitarian needs in an efficient, effective and timely. Titi Foundation will line up an integrated response to reduce the burden on WaSH-affected targeted locations.

2 - Project activities

The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites.TITI Foundation will provide permanent access to water and sanitation services for targeted IDP, returnees, host communities affected by floods and (pre-)famine conditions; it will deliver specific activities to mitigate GBV risk for users of WASH facilities and services, especially women, children, PWDs through the following aspects:WASH severity: the severity of population needs to water and sanitation will be monitored through the WASH Severity Classification (WSC) that will be conducted on a yearly basis. The trend analysis will be conducted at the county level to measure the impact of sectoral response, in particular those involving sustainable solutions that should lead to a decrease of percentages of people in phases 4 (extreme) and 5 (catastrophic) in prioritized counties. Safety audit; outcomes of the WASH safety audits will be monitored with qualitative data (in addition to related quantitative sources). Feedback of women and children on their perception of safety at WASH facilities before and after safety audit will be monitored with Focus Group Discussions and interviews through several assessments at national or subnational levels. Closing the gender gap; women's participation in WASH programming will be monitored in a pilot project with the support of the volunteer WASH Cluster partners.Strengthened community management and delivery: Supporting WASH data sharing forums at the community Unit level to promote the use of data to inform WASH interventions. Implement water quality tests for functional boreholes.Strengthened Human Resources Management: TF will work with the local community structures to engage and train different cadres in the community to take up the initiatives both as employees, hygiene promoters, Volunteers and Community Committees. Strengthened Infrastructure Maintenance: The Project will map out Water sources, Public Latrines and Boreholes in the community, assess the condition/status including vulnerability inclusion. Major disability inclusion renovations and Construction will be undertaken. Repair boreholes with rusted pipes; chlorination of wells and other water points; Each Household or clan will be encouraged to construct a simple sanitary structure with a model Integrated design disseminated by TF.Strengthened supplies and Health Products Management: To promote effective public commodity supply management, the project will continue to work with MOH for COVID-19 Adherence, commodities for immunization, RTK, filter clothes, Dignity packs (Panties and Sanitary pads) and ORS and the project will support them where needed.Strengthened joint quarterly supportive supervision: The Project will support States, counties in building stronger supportive supervision systems through orientation sessions, OJT and coaching, adaptation and development of supervision tools. Strengthened referral care: Linkages between the different levels of care will be strengthened by supporting facility and community management committees to strengthen and improve established community desks at high volume facilities. Recruitment and training of hygiene promoters: TF will recruit hygiene promoters through consultation with the local authorities. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM): TF with support from other static partners will conduct both mini (at end of each distribution) and comprehensive (after 1-2months of distribution) PDM. Water quality monitoring at the household level: Water quality testing at the household level will be carried out by the TF WASH team to ensure that FRC (free residual chlorine) levels are still sufficient in the households that have benefitted from the distribution of household water treatment and safe storage products.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

WASH Cluster and partners are facing mounting pressure to be more accountable in their humanitarian actions and in this regard the South Sudan WASH Cluster has developed a checklist to be accountable not only to donors but also, to the beneficiaries we help. The checklist will be adopted to ensure that beneficiaries of all ages, sex and diverse background, affected by a crisis have equitable and meaningful access to appropriate, relevant and timely information before, during and after receiving assistance. TF WaSH program will ensure the beneficiaries have two-way communication channels that facilitate feedback/ complaints and provide redress for complaints, are committed to ensuring affected population participate in decisions with fair and transparent systems of representation across the diverse communities including women-headed households, ethnic minorities and persons with disabilities (PwD).All TF's stakeholders can give feedback and make complaints, including direct and indirect beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, group of persons, organizations, project staff or any person who can provide feedback about the TF program, activities, staff, implementing partners and WaSH related suppliers. Complaints are collected through a paper-based system on the field level, phones calls, emails, complaint boxes, during monitoring visits, established help desk, coordination/review meetings, recipient/complaint committees. Possible courses of action for resolving disputes and complaints include: Apologize and accept the complaints when genuine and consider all relevant information and arguments; Refer/forward to alternative agencies if the complaint/issue raised is not within TF mandate and is unable to assist with services; Explain the complaint process and the steps that will be followed after complaint submission, if possible, resolve the complaint immediately or commit to a timeframe for investigating the complaint further; Refer if the complaint requires additional information and further investigation/analysis by management for decision making; Referral to government body if the complaint requires government intervention.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Accountability to affected populations and community engagement will be considered at all stages of the project. A community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach will be employed in all locations to ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. As outlined above, the CBPP will ensure the transparency, accountability, acceptance and ownership of the project for the targeted community. This involves consistent, continuous and wide consultations with the beneficiaries during project design, planning, implementation and monitoring. In this project, TF has engaged people, including the marginalized, individuals with no community links, women, girls, boys, men, persons living with disability, severely sick persons, elderly men and women, local authorities, community leaders and chiefs in order to correctly identify their needs, flag problems on time, obtain their views, suggestions, and preferences in a respectful and dignified manner while paying attention to beneficiaries choice, rights and freedoms.In addition, post-distribution monitoring/ rapid assessments will also be conducted over the course of the project and will serve as another form of AAP by evaluating the effectiveness of the project in addressing the beneficiaries’ most immediate needs, according to the beneficiaries themselves, TF will link communities and the response teams to maximize community influence to reduce public health risks, provide appropriate, accessible services, improve programme quality and strengthen accountability. Girls, women, boys, men and PwD will be engaged during the registration of beneficiaries, CFM process, Training, community pump mechanics and as members of the water user committees. Inclusion will be prioritised in all the sanitation facilities and points of water use to enable mobility of PwD. The project will ensure a selection of caregivers who will represent and or receive WaSH NFI kits on behalf of the Elderly and people with disability. The distribution sites will be accessed within 30 min walking range to ensure safety. TF will explore the capacity and willingness of the community to manage and maintain the WASH facilities for purpose of sustainability. With more female-headed households in dire conditions and a growing number of persons with disabilities, the project will engage women and persons with disabilities to enhance community engagement and contribute to localization to strengthen the development humanitarian nexus. “Engaging with the community will create an essential understanding of perceptions, needs, coping mechanisms, capacities, existing norms, leadership structures and priorities, as well as the appropriate actions to take.” To ensure effective records of feedback/ complaints from the beneficiaries for follow up Titi teams will establish CRFMs such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on an ongoing basis and will be used to adjust project activities. the records of complaints and feedback are kept in the kobo system and regularly analyzed by the MEAL teams in juba whose officers will track each case and support the program teams. the implementing program team under the supervision of the senior management if necessary will conduct regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback. where necessary Titi will close the complaint and feedback cycle by giving answers to the beneficiaries on the raised complaints or making referrals to other service providers

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Titi Foundation has a comprehensive PSEA policy that all staff have signed on as part of our code of conduct and are trained on, including those located in the various field locations. Additionally, whenever services or projects are implemented, communities are provided with information on the PSEA policy, awareness of what PSEA is, and the process for them to report any incidents. Beneficiaries are encouraged to come forward with any SEA concerns or information they have but will then intern be provided with information on referral pathways for each community. Throughout the project, basic PSEA training will be given to our targeted beneficiaries and community volunteers during our baseline surveys, focus group discussions, protection training and campaigns. Titi will work in collaboration with other protection partners on the ground to ensure that our beneficiaries are included in ongoing PSEA training. Also, integrated IEC materials on WaSH and PSEA will be sourced, translated and distributed in all the project locations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure effective records of feedback/ complaints from the beneficiaries for follow up Titi teams will establish CRFMs such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on an ongoing basis and will be used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback will be keyed in the kobo system and regularly analyzed by the MEAL teams in juba whose officers will track each case and support the program teams. The implementing program team under the supervision of the senior management if necessary will conduct regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback. Where necessary, Titi will close the complaint and feedback cycle by giving answers to the beneficiaries on the raised complaints or making referrals to other service providers engaged in WaSH programming.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The community’s leaders and Hygiene Promoters will be trained to ensure water-safety plans at households are adhered to by the beneficiaries. Each household is expected to receive 24 sachets of PUR per month throughout the project period. One roll of filter cloth will be shared among 100 households, and each household will receive one bucket with a lid and tap and one bucket with a lid to be distributed once during the project period. Hygiene kits will be distributed to the most vulnerable women and girls among the Returnees and HCs to restore and protect their dignity. WASH NFIs (PUR, filter clothes, and two buckets, one with tap and lid and the other only with lid for storage, and soap) and hygiene kits will be obtained through the WASH Core Pipeline. Titi Foundation will maintain strong ties to community leaders and members in all of the locations it operates in, reducing the risks of tensions around the distribution and at point of use (Boreholes) services. Titi Foundation has a comprehensive PSEA policy which all staff are trained on in all locations. The organization’s management works closely with field-based staff to ensure that they have the resources and support needed to address any issues that may arise in this area. Communities are also provided information at the beginning of each project to ensure that they are familiar with the mechanisms within Titi Foundation that are available to support them if they raise any concerns or complaints.
