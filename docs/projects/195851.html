Project Summary

Tdh will continue to ensure access to quality health services at two PHCs in camp 26 and 27, with a focus on infants, children, adolescents, and their mothers. Across both PHCs, the PRM funded program will ensure 150,000 OPD consultations each year. Given the PHC is one of the first entry points with an established service delivery infrastructure in the community, Tdh will continue to support capacity building of health care service providers in both PHCs, enabling them to adequately identify and refer vulnerable children and caregivers to child protection services. All health services will continue to be delivered with utmost adherence to the COVID-19 Standard Operating Procedures. An Electronic Medical Record ( EMR) system will continue to be used, with the aim of enhancing data capture, validation, analysis, timely reporting, and informed decision making. Tdh will continue providing the full Essential Package of Health Services at the two PHCs, including services for Mental Health, Basic Emergency Obstetric and New-born Care (BEmONC), Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), Non-Communicable Diseases and nutrition. MHPSS support will be done by Psychologist. This will ensure a continuum of quality care by improving patient diagnosis and treatment, reducing redundancy in diagnostic testing, and supporting more efficient and effective decision-making and care. Both PHCs have a dedicated pharmacy with an automated logistics management system and an adequate level of diagnostic facilities to address primary health care services with a provision of immunization services. Tdh PHCs will continue to support COVID-19 testing and vaccination as well as maintain triage and infection prevention protocol. Integrated community outreach activities will continue by a multi-skilled community outreach team reaching an estimated 15,000 beneficiaries. Sessions will focus on disseminating information on health, child protection and hygiene awareness in the camps, and host communities. To reduce home delivery, Traditional Birthing Attendants (TBAs) will be engaged to promote facility-based births. The project will further increase the capacity of maternity services to ensure deliveries under a high-quality standards framework, and that antenatal and postnatal care processes and results are in place. This includes strengthening Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services such as 24/7 BEmoNC, ANC, PNC, and family planning within both PHCs. Tdh will follow key protocols such as Helping Babies Survive, Helping Mothers Survive, Helping Babies Breathe, and will provide training to physicians on new-born treatment protocols. Tdh will continue sharing its expert knowledge to other organizations, especially NNGOs, of Simulation of Essential Skills in Obstetrical and Neonatal care (SIMESON), which trains midwives and healthcare workers through the use of Laerdal mannequins.
Through the strengthening of counselling activities to mothers in the Breastfeeding corner equipped with an IYCF Counsellor, and through outreach activities, Tdh will continue increasing number of children under 6 months who are exclusively breastfeed. Due to availability of a SAM/MAM) centre adjacent to the PHC, Tdh will focus on identifying the children suffering from malnutrition, referring them, and following up after they received nutritional services.
Through nutrition screening and outreach team support, Tdh will be able to ensure 100% referral, engage Traditional Birth Attendant to improve referral for facility delivery, support drop out for EPI vaccination and promote family planning services. Through multi skill community volunteer support with PNGO work for integration of health, WASH and protection activities. Tdh will roll out EMR system and improve capacity building of other actors. Tdh will increase coordination with local actors for transitioning two PHCs through a detailed handover plan, selecting a local partner with capacity development.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Ensuring adherence to quality and respectful implementation of the minimum standards for services in health facilities in the camps continues to be a challenge, in part due to the large caseload. The JRP 2020 identified critical gaps including essential health services; comprehensive emergency obstetric care; new-born care; family planning and timely access to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management of rape as well as first line support of Gender Based Violence (GBV) survivors. Needs and service gaps were further exacerbated by COVID-19 and its containment measures, with health-seeking behaviour dropping notably as well as access to basic services. As per 2022 JRP, Health care remains one of the most commonly reported need in the host communities. By end of September 2019, the proportion of women accessing facility-based deliveries had improved to 47% as compared with 37% at the start of the year, but this remains below target. Risks for communicable disease persist due to living conditions in the camps. Immunization coverage among children under 1 year old improved from 67% at the start of 2019 to 80% by end of September 2022, but substantive improvements are needed to achieve 95%. Capacities for non-communicable disease management, laboratory diagnostics; mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS), limited access of referral pathways from community health facilities to higher levels of care, and gender inequalities in access to health outreach, remains a major impediment to adequate treatment as observed by Tdh outreach team and indicated in J-MSNA 2021. Additionally, the proportion of children aged 6-59 months enrolled in nutrition programming remains low with only 15% of host communities and 60% of refugee respondents enrolled. TDH got approval from CIC to work in block C in Camp 27 and Block B,C,D,E and F in Camp 26.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project is designed to address health strategic objectives of supporting the health sector response for FDMN and host communities in Cox's Bazar to implement quality primary health care services. The project supporting to the Government of Bangladesh contributing to reach Sustainable Development Goals (Goal 3: Good health and wellbeing, Goal 5: Gender equality, and Goal 10: Reduced inequalities). The main objective of the project is to improve access to essential and quality health and nutrition services through integrated, community-based mechanisms, focused on women and children under 5. This is an integrated project focused on Child Protection, Health, and WASH. Through ensuring EPHS in PHC of Camp 27 (C) and 26 (B,C,D,E and F block) project will ensure adequate 1) 24/7 access to primary health services, 2) Integrated community-based messaging for beneficiaries is in place, capacity of maternity services ensures deliveries done under a high-quality standards framework, 3) children under 6 months are exclusively breastfeed by women who benefit from the program, 4) children under 5 involved in the program are referred to services for recovery from severe malnutrition, 5) capacity of National NGOs, Government Health Facilities, and similar INGOs are strengthened. Project will provide 150000 OPD Consultation services from both PHC. The project will be implemented following the guidelines of RRRC and CiC through approval of projects from NGOAB and regular consultation with the authorities. This project will bring positive changes in health status with improved access to essential healthcare services through integrated community-based mechanism focused on women and children. Project will ensure gender responsive healthcare for women and children for FDMN and host community population. During implementation, project will follow core Humanitarian Standard and the ‘do no harm’ approach, and sector’s health strategy, national strategy in the project interventions.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Targeted beneficiaries will receive services from all the project components as well as the outreach team will engage both communities (Rohingya and host) to implement activities and increase their meaningful participation which will help them in decision making during project and facility design, planning, and implementation. For example, same beneficiaries will get WASH, CP and health services, WASH project will construct gender sensitive, age and disability friendly structures, hygiene promotion (including NFI), messaging (including handwashing, sanitation, MHM, waste management, water safety plan, AWD, Dengue and COVID-19) and will receive their feedback and complain from CFRM system and address them, and will follow do no harm and CHS, all service directly contribute to protection and save lives. TDH will cover population in Camp 27 block C and Camp 26 in block B,C,D,E and F. TDH will not restrict people who are coming from other blocks of camp 26 and camp 27.

3.2 - Gender

In the proposed 2023 project, Tdh will address gender equality, especially during the all-project activity’s implementation period, as like in any meeting/session same number/percentage beneficiaries will be considered along with gender friendly primary health care services. Staff hiring time we will give more importance to hire equal numbers of male and female staff member, project will ensure equitable gender sensitive health care access at both community (Rohingya and host).

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Essential Health Package services offered by the PHC will continue to include different age, gender and disable persons in seeking health care services with access to drugs and vaccination. Project ensured access to COVID screening and vaccination services as well as in collaboration with Upazila Health Complex. Project will continue to serve BEmONC services 24/7 and ensuring EPI vaccination as well. In addition, well structured referral services is in place for nutrition, disability and other emergency health care services. Also, any child protection issue detected will be immediately reported to the protection team which will ensure child protection.

3.4 - Diversity

The project will ensure access to quality Essential Health Package Services including supply of drugs and consumables to the transgender / LGBTQ community within the camp. Through psychologist Tdh will provide MHPSS services both in Rohingya and host community. Considering limited accessibility and stigma, they face a lot of harassment and humiliating situations when they want to take psychosocial support. So, it can be said that this assistance will be diversification activities among the targeted beneficiaries, and it will help them to reduce social inequalities and access to services and counselling.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Under the AAP IASC and Tdh framework, Tdh will implement activities following three main phases. (I) Taking account: the project team will engage with beneficiaries from the very beginning to update the baseline data and share all relevant project information and to involve them in the decision-making process. (2) Giving account: Tdh will organize a series of consultations focused on sharing information with communities related to the progress of the project activities, especially during multisectoral community outreach sessions. (3) Being held to account: Tdh will organize formal and informal meetings at key moments of the project (start, annually and end line evaluation) to provide communities with the opportunity to assess the project and to give feedback on the project performances. Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism that is already established at the camp level will continue getting direct information from the beneficiaries and addressing them through proper channel.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will contribute to disaster risk reduction during floods/flash floods, and AWD outbreaks. Project will ensure stock availability of essential supply of drugs and consumables, as well as will manage the ANC follow up, 24/7 BEmONC services and EPI vaccinations. The project will organize mobile medical team in ensuring emergency assistance for health care needs for mitigating sufferings of the Rohingya and host communities. Project will ensure adolescent friendly services and availability of Family Planning commodities as per need of the population.
