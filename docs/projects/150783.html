Project Summary

This project is aligned to HNO 2018-2020 Cluster Strategic Objectives 1 & 2. UNFPA and its and implementing partners (IPs); Ministry of Health (MOH) and Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS) aim to save women’s lives and ensure that, women, men and girls living in Bethlehem and Hebron (H2 and Area C), have access to essential maternal and reproductive health (RH) services, counselling and information, including on well-being and protection from gender based violence (GBV).
This will be achieved through filling gaps in RH services, including access to essential maternal health care and RH service provision and preventing excess mortality and morbidity by;
1) Ensuring a continuum of care and provision of life sustaining RH services, counselling and information including strengthening community preparedness.
2) Provision of essential life-saving maternal health equipment and supplies.
3) Implementation of MISP and training on emergency obstetric care, safe birth etc.
4) Actively engaging communities, strengthening their resilience, supporting coping mechanisms, sensitization, awareness raising and outreach of life saving approaches for RH, including maternal health.
5) Support linking and informing communities to available services with a special focus on marginalized communities and groups including the disabled, elderly etc.
This project is primarily meant to address the immediate response to urgent and critical needs of pregnant and lactating women (around 4,216), especially those living in areas recognized by OCHA as high and medium vulnerable communities (60,310 in area C of Bethlehem, H2 and Hebron). It will also support women of reproductive age (7,245), men (5,175 adults aged 22 years and above), and other vulnerable adolescents; boys (3,060 aged 11-18 years) and girls (5,220 aged 11-18 years), and strengthen the preparedness and resilience of the community and health system for absorbing shocks, saving lives and recovering from crises.
MOH shall serve as the backbone and main partner in establishing and maintaining referral pathways between vulnerable communities and its health facilities. MOH primary care facilities will refer to specialized service provision, particularly secondary health care, including emergency obstetric care, safe birth, and high risk pregnancy management and follow up. PMRS, in close collaboration with Village Council Committees, will use mobile clinics (MCs) to conduct community based RH interventions through service provision and awareness raising in the target areas.
UNFPA will strengthen RH care and complement UNICEF and WHO interventions targeting <5 and neonates, including nutrition activities. UNFPA will synergize activities for maximum benefit for pregnant and lactating women, women in reproductive age, men and adolescents, to utilize the complete package of life sustaining interventions available. UNFPA will coordinate closely with UNICEF and WHO through the Health Cluster and bilateral coordination meetings that already occur on a regular basis to ensure synergies and avoid duplication. This entails sharing information, coordinating activities for target communities & facilities, and reviewing and verifying equipment and supplies to be procured; to ensure alignment with common frameworks and cooperation plans i.e. Every Newborn Action Plan (ENAP 2017-2020).
The project objectives and activities are aligned with the 2019 health cluster HRP strategy, including responsive and remedial components and is guided by the UNFPA Arab States regional framework for resilience and the UN strategy for Hebron and latest OCHA multi-sectoral needs assessment. UNFPA aims to improve access to RH services and information, through a strengthened continuum of care, MISP implementation at the primary health care level, support to mobile clinics, reinforcement of referral pathways, increase of community awareness and preparedness for emergencies, and increase of resilience for women, men and girls in these communities.

Needs

The challenging situation in Palestine is well recognized as a protracted crisis with humanitarian consequences, driven by a lack of respect for international law by the Occupation and its measures to prevent Palestinians from attaining quality standards of living and access to resources, including health care. Palestinian women, men, girls and boys in the West Bank, especially in seam zones, H2, Area C of Hebron and Bethlehem, face a range of serious protection and humanitarian threats including threats to life, settler violence, house demolitions, and liberty and security. The effects of the occupation are far reaching and include severe repercussions on RH. GBV, as an integral component of RH, continues to be a key protection and health concern in Palestine, as it exacerbates the already existing vulnerabilities of women and girls’ and increases the pressure on already limited health resources, including life sustaining RH services and information. According to OCHA’s vulnerability analysis, there are 532 Palestinian communities (300,000 people) located in Area C. The OCHA report highlights lack of health infrastructure and adequately trained staff, long distances to health facilities, limited opening hours, and high cost of transportation to facilities as the key barriers to address in order to ensure access to healthcare. In this regard, the geographic distribution of communities in southern West Bank, with Bethlehem, Hebron and south Hebron governorates being most affected, indicates high vulnerability. This includes 98 communities (60,310 people) with a medium or high vulnerability profile living in Bethlehem and Hebron (H2 and area C). Women and mothers in remote locations, especially in Area C, H2, East Jerusalem and outside the wall have particular difficulties in accessing essential health care, including maternal health care and information and protection services. A study by the Palestinian Family Planning and Protection Association (PFPPA) found that while the national antenatal care coverage is 99%, antenatal care coverage in south Hebron is only 70%. In addition, prevalence of anemia is high among pregnant women (38%) and lactating mothers (28%), which poses serious short and long term threats to the health and well-being of the mother and child. Previously, many communities received assistance from mobile clinics supported by MOH and humanitarian actors. However, according to WHO, 164 out of 189 communities no longer receive services due to lack of funding. This leaves women, children and people living with disabilities at increased risk, particularly in health emergencies, including childbirth, heart attacks, and accidents. Access to quality emergency obstetric care is also hindered, as hospitals in Hebron, particularly Alia Hospital, Muhtaseb in H2, and Yatta hospitals, suffer from significantly restricted capacity to cope with high caseloads and report occupancy rates beyond 100%. In most cases, mothers and infants are not systematically assessed, nor is the mother properly counselled, partly due to lack of essential ICU and maternity ward equipment. The need to strengthen the linkage between communities, primary care, and hospitals to ensure adequate lifesaving obstetric care is crucial. Without intervention, the lack of access to quality RH services and information, including maternal health care, nutrition, protection from GBV, and other RH needs, in the West Bank, a protracted humanitarian crisis, not only negatively impacts the health and wellbeing of women and children, but causes significant short and long term financial and social strain on families and health systems leading to decreased resilience to crises.

Activities

This project will strengthen the preparedness and capacity of the National Health System, UNFPA, and IPs will target the most vulnerable women in marginalized areas in Bethlehem and Hebron (H2 and area C). The project will equip health service providers with basic emergency skills, life- saving supplies, and commodities to strengthen the response and resilience to life threatening conditions. The project, will fill gaps in the continuum of care using holistic approach, focusing on strengthening linkages between the target facilities and the communities, as to increase access and utilization of services by the target group, through the following domains; -Strengthen access of people with focus on “pregnant and lactating women, and girls” to life saving reproductive and maternal health services, counselling and information, through a well-defined continuum of care linking communities to primary health care centers to maternities/ hospitals in target areas in H2 and Area C of Bethlehem, Hebron. (Response) -Improve Health facilities capacities, equipment/supplies, operationalize mobile clinics services and training of staff on life saving skills (the services shall be coordinated/installed with IPs and local actors, villages councils in target communities or annexed to ongoing mobile clinics by other actors etc. UNFPA is not planning to purchase vehicles for mobile clinics. (Response) -Support networks of community activists and implement community based reproductive health interventions “ campaigns” including health education/awareness component, that will target the issues from community and women empowerment perspective via educating families and girls on basic gender and human rights issues, including women/girls with disability and their personal hygiene and RH needs, risks “health and social” of early marriage and promotion of healthy outcomes and reproduction, strengthening resilience and positive coping mechanisms, supporting positive participation on relief and preparedness to emergencies. (Remedial) Activities:-Provision of basic essential life-saving drugs, medical equipment, and supplies to hospitals and primary health care facilities in the targeted Area C communities in Bethlehem, Hebron/H2. UNFPA shall focus on facilities that would work as referral points, recommended by MOH and those were supported by UNFPA before" i.e. Alia, Yatta, Beit Jala and others (i.e. Muhtaseb). -Operationalization of MCs providing ANC, PNC, FP services, counselling and other RH, maternal health care in the targeted communities. -Implementation of minimal initial service package for RH (MISP) at primary health care clinics and targeted communities.-Supporting referral pathways linking communities, operating MCs to PHC and maternities in the catchment areas. (Reducing 2nd delay)-Training of health professionals providers on (MISP), emergency preparedness, emergency obstetric and maternal care and referral. (Reducing 3rd delay) -Community awareness sessions targeting women, men, young boys and girls on RH issues, danger signs of pregnancy, breast cancer and self-breast exam, GBV protection and emergency preparedness. (Reducing 1st delay) UNFPA shall coordinate with active actors in the filed i.e. WHO, UNICEF, other organizations as to enhance the coordination in targeting selected communities, operations and scope of MCs, where possible, other providers running MCs and offering other services i.e. non communicable diseases, new born and children <5 care. and others, could be coordinated with the RH services offered by this project as to maximize the benefit for the target communities, this would also encounter annexing RH services to ongoing MC, as UNFPA is not aiming at affording vehicles for MCs. Note: final list of communities, facilities to be targeted, scope of support shall be endorsed by MOH after coordination with UNICEF, WHO, upon implementation, to accommodate emerging needs and changes and ensure complentarity.

Indicators

Monitoring and Evaluation: The selection of target facilities have been discussed with and recommended by MOH technical directorates including; Women’s Health Directorate (WHDD), Hospital Directorate, and verified by international Cooperation Department (ICD), in addition to detailed discussion with health providers on the spot, i.e. Dr. Jumaa Abu Snenieh/Director of Muhtaseb Hospital etc.Upon implementation, UNFPA shall integrate the project components/activities into its annual work plans with implementing partners (MOH and PMRS etc.) and will develop a detailed monitoring and evaluation plan, in line with UNFPA’s M&E system and framework, which includes monitoring activities and appropriate tools that will be used to track project performance indicators, beneficiaries’ feedback and achievements of results. The monitoring framework shall track progress against achieving key deliverables and millstones both on partners and UNFPA directly implemented activities. UNFPA jointly with partners (MOH and PMRS) will conduct focus groups discussions with beneficiaries and regular field monitoring visits to the implementation sites, to assess and track progress towards planned results, and address challenges that may affect implementation. UNFPA and IPs will be ensuring/advocating vulnerable beneficiaries i.e. marginalized people, GBV survivors and women & girls with disabilities are reached and mechanisms to reach them and facilitate their access to services are considered by providers. Also, field monitoring visits may be planned and done jointly with the donors upon their request.
