Project Summary

This project supports children in Idleb(Harim, Idelp) and Aleppo(Afrin, AlBab, Azaz) from the host community and IDPs, which includes the most vulnerable groups9,000 children(4,500 boys, 4,500 girls) from 7 to 15 age, IDP children, and children with disabilities to provide equitable access to quality learning opportunities for girls and boys in both primary and secondary school level in formal education in 23 schools and formal education with the summer program in 9 learning spaces and 23 schools )
The project’s activities revolve around the following components:
1stCOMPONENT :
Targeting 7,000 children (3,500 girls, 3,500 boys, ~4% CwD, 5-17 years old) in 23 Formal Schools in NW of Syria
The activities are:
1)Conduct awareness sessions and preventive measures on Cholera to increase awareness among parents and local communities on the importance of education and how to be protected from epidemics
2)Conduction of back to learning (BTL) campaigns:conduct awareness-raising campaigns to sensitize the students’ parents and local communities in the target areas.Campaign messages will be focused on the value of schooling and safety of targeted learning facilities.Moreover, students’ parents will be informed of all the operational, educational and recreational supports that are provided by the project to the targeted schools. As part of the BTL campaigns, parents and local communities will also be sensitized about Cholera outbreak and precautionary sanitary and hygiene measures to prevent infection .
3) Deliver classes and exams as per the academic calendar, using SiG curriculum.
Each school will be provided with required Operational Supplies: Recreational kits, Supplies for PSS Activities, Stationary, Supplementary Materials, and Winterization for classrooms heating. Children will undergo pre and post learning assessment to measure their improvement.
4) Conduct structured PSS and Social and Emotional Learning programs via daily sessions using War Child’s “I DEAL”.
5) Promote community engagement by activating the role of PTAs in improving and monitoring the project activities.
6) Conduct Capacity Building programs, to enhance quality of education, benefiting 450 teachers and staff (225 Men, 225 Women).
7) Conduct an in-school integration activities targeting CwD via CwD-inclusion-officers.
All 23 schools will have an established AAP/PSEA reporting channel as per UNICEF Guidelines. The channels will be introduced to children and adults (parents and teachers) to be more familiar with and confident using it.
2ndCOMPONENT: Conduction of non-formal education program for out-of-school children: under this component, the project will design and facilitate a remedial learning program in order to ensure that children and youth who have missed out on part of their schooling can catch up and sucessfully enroll in formal education.
ONSUR has planned to carry out a catch-up/remedial learning program with an aim to provide out-of-school children with an opportutnity to integrate into the formal education system.
The program is of an intensive nature and seeks to enable children with little or no schooling to overcome their educational shortcommings within a relatively short period of time.
Emphasis is given to language and mathematics, but other subjects like hygiene, environment, life skills, sports and culture are also included.
ONSUR will coordinate the catch-up/remedial learning program
with the authorties at the seleted schools in order to facilitate the link and esnure easy transfer of target bene¦ciaries from informal to the formal education system.
In total 2000 children [including 1000 boys & 600 girls] will be selected by the project to undergo the catch-up/remedial learning program.
Before commencing the learning program, the project will administer a placement test to determine childrens eductional levels and accordingly design NFE progroms .
The catch-up/remedial learning program will be conducted for a period of 4 month.

3 - Needs

6.9 million people (97% children, about 7% with disability) need humanitarian education assistance in the whole of Syria with approximately1.9million in northwest Syria(HNO 2023 ).As per education cluster statistics,more than 800,000 out-of-school children(44% of all school age children in NWS)among them155,000 children have never been in school. The education services that exist are often low in quality.Teachers are often underqualified and one in three were unpaid as of October 2022 according to Education Directorate (ED) data. Curricula are not always appropriate for students’ learning needs and long-term prospects.One quarter of schools is under-resourced, partly a result of decreasing donor funding for education, leaving teachers and students under-equipped for learning and schools at risk of closure.The weakness of the school system is exacerbating common protection issues including high dropout rates (especially among girls),early marriage,and child labor.Also,the harsh winter in northwest Syria presents challenges for children to access education. Children are required to continue their studies during winter,yet most schools are not equipped for heating throughout the winter months. Severe winter conditions including flooding and snowstorms affect displaced people the most given camp vulnerabilities.The decrease of funding for Education in general and for winterization,in particular, is a challenge. More often, winterization support is not seen as a priority need.

4 - Implementation of activities

This project supports Education Objective1&2and3: Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth through :1- Support the provision of Non-formal & formal education to 9,000 children (4,500 girls, 4,500 boys, ~4% CwD, 5-17 years old) in 23 Formal Schools in Idleb(Harim, Idelp).2- Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns to 9,000 children (4,500 girls, 4,500 boys, ~4% CwD, 5-17 years old) in 23 Formal Schools in Idleb(Harim, Idelp).3- Conduct parent-teacher associations (PTA) in 23 Formal Schools in Idleb(Harim, Idelp).4-Conducting Payment of incentives for 450 teachers and staff (225 Men, 225 Women) in 23 schools.5- Conducting training 450 teachers and staff (225 Men, 225 Women) on PSS, educational subjects, and child-protection principles and practices.6- Provision of stationary supplies for 23 schools on monthly basis targeting 9,000 children (4,500 girls, 4,500 boys, ~4% CwD, 5-17 years old).7-Hygiene promotion and hygiene awareness campaign using agreed UNICEF-WHO messages to preventive measures of Cholera to increase awareness among parents and local communities on the importance of education and how to be protected from epidemics.8- Establishing PSEA/Feedback Reporting ChannelsTargeting 23 Schools.9-Conducting MEAL Activities :include monitoring and evaluation activities both operational and educational, to monitor the achievement of project.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Security (MEDIUM-HIGH) Changes in the security situation in the target areas, including the deterioration of safety and mobility conditions, as well as the displacement of staff, in a manner that delays or hinders the implementation of the project. MitigationWe will monitor the security situation in Northern Syria, and establish emergency response and contingency plans to deal with changes in stability and access. These plans include relocating program sites if necessary [These plans are available with the Security and Safety Department]. We are also open to using alternative modalities of service delivery, such as home-schooling. Risk 2: COVID-19 & Cholera RiskWe will strictly adhere to the cluster and WHO recommendations on preventive measures related to COVID-19. As for Cholera, arrangements will be made with the Directorate of Health to address and handle any potential Cholera cases in the targeted schools. Risk 3: Currency Fluctuation (medium)Mitigation: high fluctuation of currency and market prices inside Syria can affect the exchange value of project goods and salaries. We will monitor exchange rates and market dynamics to anticipate and plan for such changes and adapt budgeting and planning accordingly. Where possible, we set fixed prices with contractors and supply providers.
