Project Summary

The objective of this project is to enhance access for services for GBV survivors through improving capacity of service providers, strengthening of referral pathways and provision of material and livelihood opportunities for women and girls.
The specific objectives of this project are:
a. Facilitate access to comprehensive multi sectoral GBV services (medical, legal, psychosocial) for 6000 women and girls
b. Enhance the capacity/and mobilize 200 PSS counsellors; 150 health workers to provide timely, quality and confidential PSS and rape treatment
c. Promote mobilization of women and girls to access services through the establishment of 15 safe spaces for women and girls (WGSS) to reach 3000 women and girls
d. Strengthen referral pathways for dissemination and access to services reach 2000 women, girls, boys and men.
e. Provide 2,000 dignity kits and 36 post rape kits dignity protection for women and girls and treatment of rape
f. Support 200 women and girls’ survivors of GBV with skills and grants for livelihood

Needs

Needs The increased displacement caused by droughts and floods, evictions and conflicts presents significant protection challenges, particularly for women and children, as households lose traditional socio-economic safety nets and frequently experience extremely poor living conditions in Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) sites. IDPs lived in make-shift structures built from sticks, clothing and plastic sheets . The event of the Cyclone Sagal in 2018 affected most of Somaliland and Puntland and recent conflict in Tukarak Puntland and Somaliland also contributed to increased displacements, loss of livelihoods and wide ranging evictions. According to Evictions Trend Analysis produced by HLP Sub Cluster leads, UN Habitat and NRC, 158,669 people were evicted between January and 6 May 2018. 70 per cent of the IDPs were women and children. Most households were headed by women as men migrated with livestock leaving behind household members. The GBVIMS Jan-July 2018 indicate that most incidents were reported by the displaced persons, accounting for 83%, while 15% were from the host communities and the remaining 2% are returnees, refugees and asylum seekers. 70% of the GBV incidents were reported to service providers by women and girls who are married or cohabitating, whereas 15% are single, 9% are divorced or separated and 6% are widowed. Physical assault (59%) continues to be the most commonly experienced GBV incident followed by sexual assault and rape at 11%. Married and co-habiting women/girls; single and divorced women are the most at risk groups for GBV as indicated by the GBVIMS reports of 2018 while widows are reported to be the least vulnerable. Within IDP sites, lack of privacy in poor quality shelters, adequate lighting at night and firewood gathering or taking animals to graze activities are major GBV risks for women and girls (Joint Partner assessments – April 2018). The GBVIMS (2018) also indicate that most of reported incidents took place in the evening/

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The GBVIMS (2018) also indicate that most of reported incidents took place in the evening/night due to the low lighting in the settlements, which exposes women and girls to GBV accounting for 43%. Where latrines are not available women and girls go to host community areas to defecate, further exposing them to GBV risks. Journey to and from IDPS sites to gather firewood, fetch water and seek livelihood opportunities increase their vulnerability to sexual assault and rape. Women are increasingly taking on the role of breadwinner due the livelihood loss for men which contributes to the increase in intimate partner violence as reported by the GBVIMS Jan – July 2018 report. While majority of IDPs in 2018 are women and girls with limited access to protective shelter, safe water and sanitation facilities and other basic need; men and boys often migrate with livestock in search of pasture and water during drought conditions which further weakened family protection systems and exposed men and boys to attacks and women and girls and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Families are adopting negative coping mechanisms to escape GBV, poverty and provide protection for their adolescent girls and women. Early marriage has become more common since the start of the drought as families seek to access dowry as an additional income source. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Findings from joint rapid assessment indicate a growing population of traumatized women and girls in need of psychosocial counselling and support, livelihoods support, and rape services. Increased trauma from violent experience as a results of displacement situation arising from droughts, floods and conflicts for women and girls as well as boys and girls in 2018 is identified as a major consequence and form of GBV in joint partner assessment referenced in this document. GBV survivors often decline legal assistance due to fear of retaliation from the known perpetrators, lack of trust and confidence in a fair trial and pressure from the families to withdraw cases and resort to the informal justice system that is often male dominated and decisions may not be in the best interest of the survivor.
