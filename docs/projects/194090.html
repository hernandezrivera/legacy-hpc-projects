Project Summary

This project aims to provide life saving integrated multi-sectoral health, nutrition and WASH assistance to the refugees and host communities in ND and BN states. A)Health Sector: Considering the health needs of the refugees, this project will provide quality emergency health services to the refugees and host communities in North Darfur where 38,257 refugees and host communities will be reached in two localities: 1) In Elfashir, 1,305 (536 F; 142 G; 486 M; 141 B) refugees will be reached, and 2) In El Laiet 30,792 (12,649 F; 3,363 G; 11,676 M; 3,104 B) refugees and 6,160 (2,531 F; 672 G; 2,337 M; 620 B) host community will have access to health services. RI will ensure that protection and SGBV is mainstreamed in RI’s health, nutrition and WASH interventions. To reduce and mitigate SGBV risks, the project will engage with community protection groups and will ensure the existence of a well coordinated referral mechanism. RI will ensure that SGBV survivors identified in RI programmes have access to SGBV services, including clinical management of rape and psycho social support. The cost of provision of the proposed emergency health intervention is estimated at US$600,000. B) Nutrition Sector: RI will aim to improve access to basic nutrition services and will contribute to the reduction of the risk of morbidity and mortality among the refugee populations in ND by targeting 30,792 refugees through 7 nutrition sites integrated in static health facilities (HFs) in El-Laiet locality. In Elfashir, 1,305 (536 F; 142 G; 486 M; 141 B) refugees will be targeted through both health and nutrition services. RI’s nutrition strategy aims at preventing and treating acute malnutrition by using a holistic approach that includes curative and promotion-oriented preventive interventions. The nutrition intervention will be done in line with Sudan CMAM and IYCF guidelines. To ensure quality nutrition activities, RI will provided CMAM and IYCF interventions and will build the capacity of health and nutrition workers, and will procurement of supplies as well as support referrals to the stabilization centers for management of SAM with medical complications. The cost for provision of nutrition services is estimated at US$ 900,000. C) WASH Sector: The proposed action will reach 45,916 individuals (33,899 Refugees and 12,017 host communities) with WASH intervention. In ND, 34,560 individuals (25,543 Refugees and 9,017 host communities) while in BLNS (11,356 individuals -8,356 Refugees and 3000host communities) will be provided comprehensive WASH services. The estimated cost of the intervention is US$ 577,253. Through WASH, RI will aim to address the pressing needs of water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion for the refugees at the refugee camps of Wad Almahi- camp 6/village 6 in BN states, while in ND, RI will reach the refugees and host communities with comprehensive WASH services in Elfashir (Abushouck, Alsalam) and Alleit camps. RI’s WASH intervention will focus on rehabilitation rather than new constructions of WASH facilities. Renewable energy sources like solarization of water yards will be prioritized. Where feasible and needed, RI will prioritize water supply pipeline extensions and scale up the distribution systems in the camps in order to scale down water trucking. Strengthening and capacity building of the Refugees and host community Water user committees (WUCs) will be core part of the intervention and will be provided through formal training and on job mentor-ship. In both states, RI will provide hygiene promotion (including MHM management and dignity kits distribution) which will be delivered to the beneficiaries. Awareness raising campaigns on hygiene and sanitation will be delivered to the refugees and host community through facilitation of cleaning and waste management interventions.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview, there are around 1.9 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, refugees and vulnerable residents in North Darfur. Conflict, food insecurity, dry spells and pests that affected the 2021 harvest, the economic crisis and poor services are the main drivers of humanitarian needs in North Darfur. Based on UNHCR’s figures for 2022, Sudan hosts over 1million refugees that are living at different parts of the country. A) Health Sector: ND is ranked as second worst in the country in terms of access to healthcare and cost of services. A December 2021 RI needs assessment, conducted by RI, confirmed considerable barriers to accessing services, including distance from HFs, prohibitive costs of accessing healthcare, lack of essential medicines and medical equipment, insufficient services, and a lack of personnel. RI’s most recent needs assessment (March 2022) also highlighted a lack of staff for basic, but essential care and the prohibitive cost of transportation to referral hospitals. The availability of critical health services in the assessed HFs are as follows: Sixty-six percent of the HFs were providing OPD services. EPI service delivery was at 33%, exposing children under 5 to morbidity and mortality from vaccine preventable diseases. ANC/PNC 55% and safe delivery services were at 22% respectively, showing the limited access women have for reproduction health services. Communicable diseases were the top causes of morbidity in these HFs. Outbreaks such as malaria, dengue, and hepatitis E occur almost yearly and exert debilitating effects on the health of the population and over-burdening the health system. The COVID-19 pandemic has also contributed to exacerbating the challenges faced by the strained health care system. for example, 50% of the needed staff capacity was available and there was a gap in human resources for health. Clinical management of rape cases and mental health and psycho- social support (MHPSS) services remain critical gaps. B) Nutrition Sector: According to the IPC report released in June 2022, with significant increase in food and other commodity prices, a reduced harvest, and continued conflict, acute food insecurity in Sudan continues to worsen rapidly. Urgent and timely interventions are needed to prevent these populations, including the refugees, from slipping into worse phases. Through this action, RI will increase the prevention, detection and treatment of moderate and severe acute malnutrition among under-fives (U5) children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in El Laiet locality and will integrated nutrition services in health services in Elfashir. C) WASH Sector: The main WASH challenges in the refugee settings in ND and BN in the refugee camps are lack of access to adequate safe water supply - and not meeting the UNHCR desired standard of 20lpd, thus there is need of Water Trucking until completion of water points rehabilitation work or in the absence of other options, need of water yards solarization to decrease high operation and maintenance costs, need of pipeline extension especially for Wad Almahi camps in BNS; Sanitation wise, high Open Defecation prevalence is reported (63.9%) because of quite low latrine coverage, which brings the need of households (HH) latrines and communal latrines construction. In addition, there is a need of provision of WASH NFIs/hygiene kits including hand washing soaps and women dignity kits. Moreover, hygiene promotion and improvement of the hygienic environment with regular cleaning campaigns, maintenance of latrines, and provision/installation of sufficient hand washing facilities, waste management and waste disposal points establishments are other critical needs among the refugees.

2 - Activities/Outputs

A)Health: RI will support 7 health facilities in two localities within North Darfur. These clinics will provide integrated timely life-saving basic primary health care services in line with the HRP 2023 objectives. RI will; (i) Support and sustain the 7 existing HFs to ensure provision of primary health care, including maternal, neonatal and child care for refugees and host communities; (ii) rehabilitate, equip, staff with qualified health workers and procure essential equipment and medical supplies including clean delivery kits (CDKs) for pregnant women, PPEs for infection prevention and control and PEP kits for clinical management of rape (CMR) for 7 health facilities; (iii) Establish and maintain an effective drug management system in the selected 7 HFs to ensure accountability, rational use of medicine and medical supplies and avoid stock-outs; (iv) Support and train 45 workers (25 Male and 20 Female), 14 (7 Female and 7 male) community health workers (CHWs) and 14 female village trained midwives to ensure access to quality health services for the refugees and host communities; (v) Support the 7 selected HFs with basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric care; (vi) Integrate infection prevention and control (IPC) and COVID-19 prevention and response interventions in the 7 selected health facilities. At secondary level of care, RI will provide multi-layered mental health and psychosocial support and referral support using the WHOmhGAP guideline at community level. B) Nutrition: The proposed nutrition intervention will be done through specific nutrition activities including screening of 6,652 fives and 2,957 PLW and referring them to appropriate services. RI will support the treatment of 475 under-fives with severe acute malnutrition without medical complications; treatment of 47 SAM cases with medical complications in the SC and also provide MAM treatment to 2,619 under-fives and 443 PLW. In addition, RI will support weekly community mobilization through key messages through community outreach workers, community leaders and mothers to improve awareness about signs and dangers of severe acute malnutrition and objectives of CMAM services. RI will provide vitamin A supplementation to 6,652 children aged 6-59 months; provide de-worming to 6,652 children aged 1-5 years; provide iron/folate supplementation to 2,957; provide maternal infant and child feeding (MIYCN) counseling to 9,609 PLW and provide BSFP for 2,661 children 6-23 months and 2,957 PLW. C) WASH: RI will ensure the availability and continuity of WASH services to refugee populations and their closest host communities that are in urgent need of WASH services. RI will prioritize rehabilitation, upgrading and sustainability of existing water points rather than new water points constructions. Therefore, this project's contribution to safer water access will revolve around ensuring adequate water quantity (20l/p/d), rehabilitation and upgrading of water points as well as supporting and building capacity of the water users committees for the refugees and host communities. RI will rehabilitate 17 water points (4water yard rehabilitation, 8 water yard solarization, and 5 Handpump rehabilitations) and Water supply by water trucking for refugees in Wad Almahi Camp-6 of BNS and Abushouk, Alsalam and Allait camps of NDS. To improve the access to sanitation services, RI will also intervene in the same locations with the construction of 450 individual emergency household latrines, 5 communal latrines and 83 rehabilitation/dislodging of communal latrines. RI will conduct regular hygiene promotions including MHM sensitization, construction/installation of 65 Hand washing facilities, support disposal of solid waste through construction of 29 waste disposal sites, facilitate 54 clean-up campaigns and distribution of 2,500 WASH NFIs/Hygiene kits and distribution of women dignity kit for 7,000 women.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

RI will focus on Ground water monitoring, sanitary survey, water quality monitoring and surveillances with the framework of integrated water resource management to avoid any impact on Environment and Natural resource. In addition, RI strategy will focus on Rehabilitation and upgrading works rather than new water points construction, which will avoid any Environmental impact. Before any intervention, RI will do appropriate assessments to avoid any risk of contamination due to latrines or other wastes near the water points. RI’s Water supply strategy focuses on renewable Energy (solar) than an approach that is dependent on fuel for generators which have eventual impact on the Environment. All waste management disposal facilities (like solid waste disposal pits) will be fenced and all essential measures will be taken to avoid pollution to the Environment and Natural resources.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

RI aims at promoting equitable access to health services which ensures that the project reaches the most deprived members of the targeted communities. Nearly all the 2023 health indicators are gender-disaggregated, and it is expected that over 50% of beneficiaries will be women. Special attention will be paid to mothers and PLWs through provision of reproductive health services. Privacy and confidentiality of patients will be respected and shall be ensured at all times of service delivery. RI will deploy health workers of both genders who will be able to attend to the unique needs of both women and men in a comfortable, private and confidential way. Skilled village based midwives will ensure that women’s reproductive health needs of the target population, especially safe home delivery, are addressed and documented. Community Health Workers (CHW) and village health committees (VHC) will identify people with disabilities and/or the elderly who require home visits for treatment. VHCs will have both men and women representatives; the proposed project shall aim at a 50:50 gender representation. Additionally, there will be posters in the HFs discouraging any form of abuse. RI will use nutrition workers seconded from the MoH who have already been trained to work with populations with unique needs e.g. persons with disabilities, women and children. RI will mainstream minimum protection standards and will ensure equitable and safe access to WASH programs and facilities. Safety and dignity are key considerations for sanitation activities, and each refugee household will be involved in the construction of their IHHL with technical support from RI WASH team to account for the unique needs of its members, and the hygiene kits will incorporate MHM items. All beneficiaries of the different interventions will be selected according to set criteria and in consultation with the camp management, refugee community representatives and UNHCR. The hygiene promoters, CHWs and CNWs will receive hygiene promotion training that considers how to tailor messaging to diverse groups. Priority will be given to the elderly, the disabled, female-headed households, and beneficiaries with cases of acute malnutrition. In the training of WUCs, RI will ensure that there is representation from vulnerable community groups and that the WUCs develop processes to ensure that vulnerable community members are able to access water easily. Gender Based Violence (GBV), a lack of AAP, and disability have detrimental effect on the nutritional status of under-five children and PLW most often contributing to the deterioration of the nutritional status as well as impacting their mental and psycho-social wellbeing. Lastly, COVID-19 precautionary measures will be taken into consideration when defining the response plans, particularly in high concentration zones like reception centers.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the proper implementation and management of the accountability to community principles including safeguarding issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working to strengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of the program recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment, designing, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI and therefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for beneficiaries including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receives mandatory training on this policy. RI will continue to strengthen its accountability system, putting a mechanism which enables inter agency coordination and facilitate learning of best practices. As part of the existing accountability system, RI will conduct an annual satisfaction survey on services provided to the refugees and host communities, to enable informed decisions on program design, improvement and strategic allocation of resources.
