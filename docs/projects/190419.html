Project Summary

Ethiopia just like other Horn African countries has been hit by an exceptionally severe drought that was occasioned by the failure of the last three consecutive rains. Poor rains in October-December 2020 and March-May 2021 have been followed by an extremely dry October- December 2021. Current forecasts point to a fourth below-normal rainfall season during March-May 2022, a sequence that was never seen since 1981 in the region, which if it happens as predicted could further exacerbate the current humanitarian challenges in the region. The Somali region of Ethiopia is one of the regions hard–hit by the ongoing drought. The Post- deyr rains assessment and regional drought response plan, prepared in December 2021, confirmed that the whole Somali region is affected by the current drought and 2,279.606 people in the region are reported to be in need of water trucking, at 657 sites across 85 Woredas. Water resources are increasingly being strained as a result of the drought conditions while most surface water sources have largely dried up in the region. The humanitarian situation in Northern Ethiopia has been worsening since the onset of the war in November 2020 in the region which has affected more than 6 million people, displaced more than 2.2 million people displaced from their homes including almost one million women and girls as the fighting crossed into the Neighboring Amhara and Afar region. In Tigray and Amhara region particularly in the war-affected areas, the conflict has rendered a large number of water points dysfunctional due to damage to water supplies systems, looting of water points, the collapse of water governing structures, disruptions in the Supply chains of Essential WASH equipment in the region. A recent OCHA update reported that there are about 418,000 IDPs in 131 sites in Tigray that require water trucking supports and that about 9300 water points (more than 54%) are not functional in Tigray affecting access of more than 3.5 million people to safe drinking water.
According to the WASH cluster situation update from the Amhara region (issued 1st march 2022). There are over 256, 491 IDPs in the region, of which the majority are newly displaced. - IDPs are mainly found in North Shewa, North Wollo, Waghemra, and North Gondar zones - There are sheltered in 20 Collective sites across the Amhara region. In view of the above, Alight proposes emergency WASH Services provisions to the conflict and drought-affected IDPs and vulnerable host communities including Schools and health facilities that are also in need of basic WASH services from affected Woredas in Tigray, Amhara and the Somali region with the aim of saving lives and reducing morbidities associated with WASH-related diseases. In this proposal Alight will:
• Prioritize emergency Water trucking supports to IDPs and vulnerable host community members for 2 months benefiting approximately 30,000 beneficiaries.
• Restore the functionality of 10 strategic boreholes and shallow wells by rehabilitating and providing critical pumping and power systems installations to support durable access to water for the targeted beneficiaries
• Construct 400 communal emergency latrines and bathing cubicles for IDPs populations
• Distribute environmental sanitation kits to IDPs especially newly arrived IDPs to help them manage waste.
• Provide WASH Supports to 14 Institutions (8 schools and 6 Health facilities.
• Hygiene promotion will remain a key component of the intervention to prevent the spread of Covid 19 and other WASH-related diseases through risk communications and community engagement promoting behaviour changes.
• Where feasible, Alight will use Market based programming approach in the delivery of the planned WASH Interventions ( e.g use of commodity and cash vouchers). The project plans to reach a total of 90,000 beneficiaries ( 20700 men, 24300 women,19800 boys and 25200 girls).

1. - Projects activities

vulnerable host communities living in affected woredas from Tigfrey, Amhara and the Somali region for a period of two months. Alight will Provide 10 Strategic boreholes and shallow wells ( motorized) in the targeted regions with critical equipment such as Generators, submersible pumps, and control panels and will facilitate their installations to restore functionality and service provisions to the drought and conflict-affected people. In addition and depending on needs, Alight will provide these strategic water points with storage facilities, repair/construct water kiosks and improve plumbing works. Where feasible, Alight solarize critical water points to relieve these facilities from costly fuel operating costs. In IDP camps especially new arrivals, Pipeline water extensions including the construction of elevated water storage tanks and water kiosks with a minimum of 6 taps will be done to increase water. To ensure access to safe water, Alight will chlorinate water sources at supply points through bulk chlorination and to provide secondary barriers against contaminations, Alight will distribute aquatabs to the targeted beneficiaries at the household level. ARC will establish Gender balanced water managementcommittees for the 10 targeted water points to ensure continuity of services, appropriate operation, and maintenance as well as strengthening women's participation in the WASH project. On Sanitation, Alight will target IDPs and poor host communities with the construction of 400 gender designated and disability-friendly communal latrines and bathing cubicles, complete with their handwashing facilities will be provided benefiting approximately 20,000 people. Alight will mobilize active sanitation groups in the targeted IDP camps and will provide environmental sanitation kits to help them safely manage their solid wastes. Since Schools and Health facilities are critical for the targeted communities and were equally affected by the ongoing conflict, Alight will rehabilitate and construct/install new WASH facilities for 14 Institutions ( 8 schools and 6 Health facilities). Priority will be given to those Schools that were hosting IDPs and those health facilities that were damaged during the conflict in the Tigray and Amhara regions. Alight will consult men, boys, girls, the elderly and people with disabilities on their needs and preference regarding the type, Design and location of the water and sanitation facilities to address barriers relating to access.Cholera and AWD focused hygiene promotions will be an integral component of the project to prevent the spread of WASH-related diseases including Covid-19 through risk communications and community engagement promoting behaviourchanges. A total of 70,000 people will be reached with hygiene promotion messages. Part of these people will be reached emergency WASH NFIs to support and enable hygiene promotions. Where feasible, Alight will use a Market-based programming approach in the delivery of the planned WASH Interventions ( e.g use of commodity and cash vouchers). The project plans to reach a total of 90,000 beneficiaries ( 20700 men, 24300 women,19800 boys and 25200 girls).

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Alight will use a mix of approaches ( use of cash or vouchers or in-kind) depending on the context especially the conditions of the local markets in the targeted woredas. In the Somali region, which is stable and markets are functional, Alight will use commodity vouchers to deliver hygiene and sanitation construction materials to the targeted beneficiaries. In the Amhara and Tigray, where access is constrained, where feasible Cash or vouchers will be used or In-kind depending on the local market conditions and security concerns.The justification for the use of cash over in-kind is that cash will stimulate demand and supply at the local market level. By giving cash to the beneficiaries to solely purchase the hygiene supplies, beneficiaries will get the chance to link directly with the local traders, have a choice of who to buy from, and encourage the local traders to stock more of the demand of cash directly delivered to the beneficiaries via their mobile phones is faster and logistically easier to do, less prone to diversion than in Kind distributions. Alight is experienced in the use of Market- based programming approach

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

ALIGHT will involve community leaders and members of the affected populations in all planning and preparation related to the activities proposed. ALIGHT will maintain a strong relationship with the government and relevant stakeholders in the Tigray, Amhara and Somali regions, which will allow us to approach the community with acceptance from local leaders in order to gain the trust of the local community and provide effective services. In addition, Alight will establish feedback mechanisms involving targeted beneficiaries in target communities through visiting the project sites and hearing the views of the beneficiaries on services provided to them and recommendations for future programming. ALIGHT will ensure accountability of the interventions provided by setting up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. These mechanisms will include following up and assessing the effectiveness of the established Complaint Mechanism including placing a complaint box in all service centres and informing beneficiaries, creating a hotline for complaints and feedback. Further, the WASH sector will work with the Health and WASH sectors including national and regional level coordination platforms to share information, reports, learnings and harmonise response in the operational areas Staff will be sensitized on how to identify and ensure that members from marginalized groups are accepted and treated with the dignity they deserve; providing infrastructure that caters to their wellbeings, such as constructing ramps at entrances to the health facilities and at the latrines for those with mobility issues; promoting recruitment of persons from these groups by ensuring that all recruitment advertisements bear clear information encouraging the receipt of applications from qualified persons including those with disabilities; and developing IEC/BCC materials that promote community understanding and inclusion.
