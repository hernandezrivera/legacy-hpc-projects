Project Summary

According to the HNO for 2020, the humanitarian crisis in Somalia continues to expose 3.2 million Somalia to violence, abuse and neglect. More than half of those with Protection needs (1.9 million), are living in displacement while a further 1.2 million people are urban and rural host communities and returnees. Civilians are exposed to armed conflicts or violence from various groups including the State, non-state Armed actors, inter-clan conflicts. This further compounds the devastation of frequent climatic shocks that impact on physical safety and integrity. A lack of a protective legal framework means often affected communities cannot access their rights. Protection needs across Somalia have been on the increase since 2018 with growing protection risks being recorded. Funding for Protection activities does not match the needs. The Protection Cluster reports that as of September 2019, 84% of Protection needs in the 2019 HRP were unmet. Of these, 63% of GBV funding needs were unmet.
Needs are recorded across gender, age and ethnic groupings. 64 percent of persons in need are children, facing risk of forced conscription, child labour and exploitation, early/forced marriage, family separation, all in addition to displacement in overcrowded IDP camps.
48 percent of estimated persons in need in 2020 are women and girls. Women and girls who are already the most vulnerable in times of crisis experience heightened risks and have compromised capacity to protect themselves from GBV. Gender Based Violence (GBV) continues to be a key Protection concern, affecting mostly the displaced women and girls, who are at risk of Rape, Intimate Partner Violence, FGM/C, Early and Forced Marriage. According to the GBVIMS data Second quarter 2019, Women and girls are at risk of GBV accounting for 96% of the reported incidents and also the report indicated that the most of the GBV incidents are reported by the displaced persons, accounting for 77% of the reported incidents in Somalia.
A Safety Audit assessment conducted by IMC in April 2019 in Baidao and Mogadishu indicated Gender Based Violence remains a daily occurrence with poor access to gender segregated and lockable latrine and bath facilities. IDPs settlements of Bay, Middle Shabelle, Banadir and Mudug, face some of the most serious gender-related problems in the whole country. In March and April 2019, numerous rapes, including of under aged girls, torture and killings were committed in IDP in camps in Baidoa, Buulaburte and Galkayo. The particularly low awareness of the extremely high rates of violence, low reporting rates and effective limited of treatment facilities as well as insufficient infrastructure, poor WASH facilities, limited access to markets, water points, and health facilities often equate to inadequate protection capacities
Through this project, IMC aims to support vulnerable populations in drought-affected areas in Banadir (Mogadishu), Bay(Baidao), Middle shabelle (Jowhar)Mudug regions (Galkayo), who are exposed to Gender Based Violence (GBV). This includes child survivors of violence. The project tackles both prevention and response through a combination of activities which rely on IMC’s combined experience in the sector together with long-term presence in the geographical areas of intervention.

Project Activities

1. Provide Psychosocial Support, Case Management, Material assistance, Medical support, Clinical Management of Rape for 1600 survivors in project locations (Mogadishu, Baidoa, Jowhar and Galkayo (85% women and girls 15% men and boys). 2. Procure and distribute 4100 dignity kits and 4100 solar lanterns to 4000 most vulnerable persons who are at risk of GBV particularly at night in Mogadishu, Jowhar, Baidoa and Galkayo. (70% will be Women and Girls and 30% men and boys). 3. Support 4 Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) based on the four regions of the project location the beneficiaries of the WGSS will be 800 women and girls (500 women and 300 girls). 4. Organize and conduct commemoration of 16 Days Activism, Zero tolerance of FGM for 1,000 participants from community members, civil society organizations, religious leaders, traditional leaders, women groups and local authority in the project location. (650 women and girls and 350 men and boys). 5. Conducting community sensitization and awareness raising sessions regarding GBV Prevention and Response our target will be 10,000 from the different community levels. 7000 will be women and girls and 3000 will be men and boys).

Mitigation Measures

1b. • IMC will ensure there is adequate integration and linkages with Health/WASH/Nutrition services in the targeted areas to minimize distance travelled to access complementary humanitarian services. 2a. • Marginalized minorities, single/child headed households and Women/children/PLWs with disabilities will be consulted on the Protection risks they face and activities that would make their communities safer for them. • During distribution of dignity kits, beneficiaries with disabilities & other special needs, will be identified and given priority. • Outreach messaging will be respectful and inclusive to ensure that beneficiaries from marginalised groups know how to access timely, confidential Protection response services. Messages will be disseminated through household visits, community gatherings and videos at heath facilities, in consideration of literacy, disability and mobility levels. • Protection teams will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, socio-demographic groups, disability). 2b. • IMC will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. • Protection specific awareness messaging will involve both girls, boys, women and men and will be timed to fit around women and men’s other work schedules. 3a. • IMC will engage with local IDP and community leaders and community representatives to play an active role in identifying Protection risk mitigation solutions and the decision-making processes so as to reduce GBV stigma, promote survivor centred care and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. 3b. • All IMC Protection staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, will sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. • All IMC Protection staff in direct contact with beneficiaries will receive training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) 3c. • IMC will provide solar powered lamps as part of dignity kit distribution to GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls in high risk areas. • Working in coordination with WASH and Nutrition sectors, IMC will put in place measures to prevent physical/sexual violence arising from distributions and poor WASH infrastructure in camp settings. • Through establishment of community risk mitigation mechanisms, IMC will advocate for community watch and buddy systems particularly for activities that have heightened GBV risk such as fetching firewood or water. • Protection staff will be trained on provision of safe, survivor centred case management, including provision of PSS and safe data storage. • In areas where IMC has Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) teams will refer beneficiaries to WGSS group PSS and basic life skills training. The location and the timing of WGSS activities will endeavor to achieve safe proximity to the beneficiaries a well as keeping to hours that allow women and girls to return to their homes before nightfall. • All IMC health centres will have confidential and private spaces, with at least one trained female health worker on duty at all times to ensure 24/7 access to clinical management of rape and GBV services. • IMC will roll out a safe-word based referral protocol at all implementation sites to ensure direct referral of survivors from Health to the GBV teams, without the Survivor needing to repeat her/his story potentially exposing her/him to further trauma. 4b. • IMC maintains an emergency GBV response number that allows confidential reporting of GBV cases and ambulance callout when necessary. • Through a Somalia wide complaints and feedback line, IMC teams will receive and make timely corrective responses.• Throughout the project life cycle, IMC will ensure that it communicates with affected populations in languages, formats and media that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate.
