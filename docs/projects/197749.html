Project Summary

Many years of conflict have filled Al Hol Camp in northeast Syria to double capacity — with thousands returning to already unsettled and divided areas of return. An assessment carried out by the partner Khotoat identified that returnee women from Al Hol camp that have returned to Baguz - the last IS stronghold to be liberated in 2019 - are marginalised for their association with the camp, and have severe immediate needs such as shelter, livelihoods and food that minimise their ability to reintegrate into society. Recent needs assessments have identified that lack of civil documentation has limited their access to basic services and freedom of movement.
The purpose of this two-year project is to reintegrate women and children that have been released from Al Hol Camp into society in Baguz, DZ by providing immediate in-kind assistance, psychosocial assistance, education, and vocational skills training to the most vulnerable women in Baguz – both returnee from Al Hol as well as local vulnerable women to mitigate community tensions. To ensure inclusion and representation of all sections of Baguz, the project will independently engage with all heads of tribes and clans who serve as “guarantors” of the returning families and prominent local professionals. An open and productive partnership with the commins, and PWG/CPWG/ CAAFAG TF to streamline the effort of assistance to returnees from Al Hol camp will also be integral to the reintegration focus of the project.
An assessment will be conducted by 6 outreach officers who will be influential and highly respected women in Baguz society that will be selected by the partner. They will reach out to 150 families in Baguz (both returnee and residents) and provide a list of urgent needs of each family. Khotoat will respond to the urgent needs of 100 families by allocating an average of $200 for each family covering the cost of urgent legal assistance, medical care, living arrangements etc.
14 case managers (CM) will develop reintegration support plans with each family under supervision of two psychologists. This will be run from a community centre which will be identified by Khotoat. As most buildings are houses, a big house or two connected house rentals to ensure enough space will be selected. To safeguard the beneficiaries and reduce community tensions, there will be no branding of the organisations implementing the project at the centre. PSS support will also be open to local women who suffer from severe trauma.
The centre will be a safe space with two psychologists who will provide the PSS therapy to a maximum of 100 cases of women and children. They will provide weekly reports for protection and monitoring the implementation of activities, supervising cases and case managers and the psychosocial training for the centre’s staff. At the centre, both returnee and local women will attend a literacy and numeracy programme for adults, a computer literacy and IT skills training open to children too, and vocational training involving basic book-keeping and administration training and marketable skills (cooking, tailoring). The centre will hold recreational children's sporting events, social, and cultural events to improve community cohesion.
Through their established relationship, the municipality of Baguz have expressed interest to Khotoat in opening a similar community centre aimed at targeting returnees for reintegration. It should be recognized that any project that aims to directly promote and support the reintegration of returnees with exposure or affiliation to Al Hol Camp, is extremely sensitive. The project will therefore focus on PSS and reintegration, rather than ideological rehabilitation. Khotoat will implement the project, take the lead on engagement and liaison with the Baguz municipality, and the day-to-day implementation of the project. CAID will be the overall project manager exercising financial and technical control and oversight of the entire programme.

3 - Needs

The mass release and dispersal of a vulnerable population poses heightened risks for a resurgence of IS activity seeking to capitalize on and exacerbate the situation for raising recruitment and intimidation campaigns against returnees. According to an assessment undertaken by our implementing partner among 5 key members of the Bagouz authorities and 25 returnee women, there are currently no reintegration facilities for camp returnees or ex-IS affiliates in Syria NES. In Baguz, an updated CAID needs analysis of returnees from Al Hol Camp (implemented by Khotoat with the commendation of the Baguz council with whom we have an established relationship and signed permission and support), identified that their living conditions were so poor, access to food, and basic services was so restricted due to a combination of fear of the community, not going outside, and no income. Over half (15) Al hol Returnee women reported that their access to food was so bad that they wished they were still at the camp. There were also reports of domestic servitude and returnee children unable to access education due to discrimination and violence from other pupils.The organization will coordinate with the CP sWG and PWG through weekly/monthly correspondence.M&E will be completed in parallel to the caseload with data collected each month following the improvement of the participant's situation, monitoring access to shelter, food and livelihoods. A pre and post assessment will also be included.

4 - Implementation of activities

Objective 1• Up to 80 families consisting of women and children returning from Al Hol to Baguz and suffering from trauma and barriers to reintegration.• Up to 20 families consisting of women and children who have remained in Baguz whose needs mirror returnee families will also be included.Objective 2• Up to 50 adult women drawn from families targeted from Ob. 1 (returnee and local). The first referral of women who appear to struggle particularly hard with their transition from Al Hol or are local with severe trauma will be made by the outreach officers implementing Objective 1. • Up to 150 children between the ages of 6 and 15 drawn from families under Objective 1 (returnee and local). Children needing specialized psychosocial support will be enrolled following the same process as adult women (see above). Particular attention will be paid to those who have been unable to successfully reintegrate into formal education as a result of their experience at Al Hol. (See Project XNP-188548-1 'Improved access to Education for Returnee children from Al Hol Camp in Baghouz').Beneficiaries will include around 1500 people including the targeted women that will receive the assistance, dependants of targeted women; Khotoat staff and national CAID field officers. The population of Baguz is approx 4000 people who will benefit from the centre's organised social, cultural and sporting events to improve social cohesion. The total number of direct beneficiaries for both years is 11,000.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of community tensions. Mitigating measures: PSS support will also be open to local women who suffer from severe trauma. To safeguard the beneficiaries and further reduce community tensions, there will also be no branding of the organisations implementing the project at the centre. Risk of discrimination/perceptions of discrimination if referral services are provided to specific groups/individuals at the exclusion of others. Mitigating measures: 1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed which will include vulnerable local women in the assistance too; 2. Ensure consultation with local councils, imams, commins and guarantors of the returnees understand the individual community perspectives of the returnees; 3. Carry out safety audits for beneficiaries’ access to locations of services. Risk of abuse and assault from host community. Mitigating measures: Ensure women are never alone when seeking services or travelling to and from the centre; Consultations with women and men should be organized among the project and center staff (which will be staffed by women outreach officers) and councillors. Psychological brain drain of case managers: Mitigating measures: Cases will be equally divided for each CM to help ease the burden so that each CM does not “deal” with too many high-risk cases. CMs will handle 15 caseloads each, with 10 CMs for children and four CMs for women.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project has a Community Response Mechanism (CRM) where participants can directly provide feedback or complaints about the centre and activities and staff and systems via email, whatsapp messaging, telephone and designated complaints boxes.Continually over the two year project, the psychologists and Khotoat staff will be responsible for disseminating CRM cards to beneficiaries and their household's and give clear guidelines on why and how all feedback should be reported. CAID and Khotoat's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are in line with the principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Khotoat safeguarding and child protection codes of conduct are vetted by CAID HQ DD team. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate risk analysis and mitigation measures identified together with the partner which includes the promotion of female staff at the centre to ensure a space of safety. PSEA focal points will be trained by specialist staff on safeguarding and PSEA measures and appointed to the centre who will engage with the wider PSEA network in NES in liaison with the PWG. They will be responsible for enhancing prevention strategies. Khotoat staff will be trained on checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in activities. Further, all personnel will adhere to and sign the CAID PSEA code of conduct prior to contracting.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse among staff towards beneficiaries. Mitigating measures: CAID Best Practice Guidelines agreed by all stakeholders; Clear anti-fraud, corruption, abuse and harassment SOPs and training; bribery and corruption clauses included in contractual commitments and policies; ; Sensitize stakeholders and community about PSEA and how to report incidents of harassment SEA, as well as abuse. Lack of buy-in from stakeholders - Identify linkages with other humanitarian community whereby support can be scaled up for referrals and sustainability of the centre. Identify new sources of services, build relationships between service providers, insititutions and the community. Explain to stakeholders importance of reintegration in target area.Difficulty targeting women - Initial outreach will be important to ensure buy-in and the targeted participant groups are included in the program. This will be done through enlisting the wives of social notables (teachers, doctors) to do the outreach of participants.Women attending the centre will be mostly returnee, however vulnerable resident women will also be included to increase community buy-in, acceptance of returnees and establish reintegration. The center will be limited to women and children, with caseload managers, outreach officers, caseload managers, and othe staff also women. Risk of open conflict - Security plan developed and used as a basis for operations. Staff adhere to strict security measures in place.
