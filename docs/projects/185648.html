Project Summary

The world’s largest congested Rohingya camps and surrounding host communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf of Cox’s Bazar have become food unsecured due to loss of economic opportunities in pandemic COVID-19. Approximately half of the assessed households in camps not having an acceptable food consumption score, and reporting challenges related to their food assistance; The most worried issues are that still 10% HH are poor in FCS (Ref: J-MSNA, 17 Oct’2021).
The same study shows that in camps, 70% of households reported having adopted livelihoods-based coping strategies due to a lack of money to meet basic needs. Though, Host are in better position, yet 89% of households reported engaging in coping mechanisms due to a lack of money to meet basic needs. However, 20% of households, reporting this strategy not to be available to them or to already have exhausted it.
In addition, % of Host HH reporting to the need of access to food rises from 55% to 60% compared to last reporting year. Also, the needs to access to income generating activities/employment increases 7% compared to the last JMSNA.
Due to COVID-19, Rising prices of vegetables, eggs, fish, and broiler meat have had a devastating effect on poor households who struggle to afford the basic food basket. Prices have risen to 55% due to a shortage of supply. To manage rising food prices, poor households are eating cheaper and less nutritious foods. Poor households seek the cheapest vegetables and sacrifice on quality. Project focusing on economic crisis recovery due to COVID-19 by adopting on-farm, off-farm and restoration environment by improvement of soil for land capability and erosion prevention by plantation.
The project will support 3605 (Female 2125, Male 1480) Rohingya and host HH for economic recovery from COVID-19.
Under the objective 2; 1650 (Female 1095, Male 555) and 25 differently abled persons from the refugee households will receive self-reliance support. 1195 Women headed HH will receive cash grants support for Skill development on homemade marketable ready-made food for targeting local markets, and men will be supported on Hygienic food corner for diversified food catering business at market.
Under objective 3; 1955 (F 1030 M 925) and 10 differently abled persons from the host community HH will be supported for kitchen garden, vertical garden for subsistence by adapt climate smart technology and will be trained on handicrafts, Hygienic food preparation, vertical gardening for self-reliance’s & additional income for economic recovery.
The Project planned to boost up food consumption, increase household income that reduced selling of productive assets by creating option of additional homemade income for women, boost up agricultural productivity and enhance food storage for hard times. These will be done by improving contributing Factors of production and income by supporting on-farm and off-farm, facilitating terms of trade for market actors and increase Food Expenditure.
Moreover, the project aims to build social cohesion by establishing market linkage among FDMN and host community. Proper sensitization and coordination meeting will be organized with relevant government and other stakeholders to incorporate the findings and learnings and to adopt those learnings accordingly. The project is focussed on accountability mainstreaming to ensure that the needs of the affected population are at the centre of every intervention. Additionally, projects will be implemented through local partners, with adequate capacity building and mentoring that promote localization.
In addition, CAID has an in-house accountability tool called COMPASS to ensure community’s feedback is systematically recorded and resolved. Moreover, CAID is an active member of the PSEA network and regularly receives training and learning materials from the network to further disseminate in the community. Besides, CAID adheres to its own accountability and safeguarding policies at all levels.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar specially Ukhiya and Teknaf are food deficient zone, almost 70% vegetables and other necessities comes from other districts (FAO Study,2019). Moreover, due to COVID-19, Rising prices of vegetables, eggs, fish, and broiler meat have had a devastating effect on poor households who struggle to afford the basic food basket. Prices have risen to 55% (Scoping Study Potential Livelihood and Enterprise Options for Host and Refugee Communities in Cox’s Bazar) due to a shortage of supply. To manage rising food prices, poor households are choosing cheaper and less nutritious foods. Poor households seek the cheapest vegetables and sacrifice quality. Bangladeshi people of Ukhiya and Teknaf believe that their livelihood opportunities-historically dependent on agriculture, livestock and fisheries have been significantly hampered since the 2017 influx. And historically, Ukhiya and Teknaf are climate change impacted areas. Year-round production is not possible due to a lack of climate-adaptive technologies and inputs. COVID 19 and preventive measures have resulted in an increase in Multi-Dimensional Deprivation or multi-dimensional poverty: driven by deprivation in education due to school closures, deprivation on health, income, and food access. The disruption on livelihoods and self-reliance activities, combined with increased food prices, have reduced household purchasing power and capacity to cover their basic needs (REVA-4)Rising prices and falling wages – recognized as the most common challenges for both host and Rohingya communities often hurt the poorest and most vulnerable groups the most. Prices likely to vary with distance to camps. Hosts living closer to camps are more likely to compete with the displaced population in markets for natural resources like land and fuelwood, labor and goods, compared to those living further away from camps.Due to the COVID-19 market becoming non-functional and to make the markets normal functioning markets needs favourable conditions for production inputs which can reduce the negative coping strategies reportedly being exercised by both guest and host communities. Cash for Work at host and camp through Portable skill development training can ensure cash interventions in the host community and Rohingya community for local market dynamics.As there are shortage of cultivable land due to the accommodation of Rohingya community, loss of Livestock, distortion of Terms of Trade, loss of Household Income and decreased Percentage of Household Food Expenditure. During CAID’s need assessment FGD with handicraft entrepreneurs who were mostly female, pointed out their limitation and lack of support while working from home for some earning. These includes lack of electricity, able to bear the cost of solar panel, failing to open a saving account in the bank and so on. The need for support to cater based on these priorities is crucial.With the reduction of activities in the camps during the peak of COVID-19, some cash-for-work and self-reliance programming have halted increasing the added vulnerability of the community. For refugees, opportunities for generating an income in the camps tend to be limited, ad hoc and small-scale which often are distributed disproportionately within the community which often leaves out women-headed households, persons with disabilities and transgenders. While male members of this community have consistently voiced their preference for reliance on work over aid, voices of the women and left behind groups are not raised. As a result, women, a person with a disability, transgender finds less opportunity for building capacity as a step towards self-reliance.CAID's need assessment link is https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/12SyPGeJtoUIG3fZGw9Wrm-m_dzRIukPf?usp=sharing
