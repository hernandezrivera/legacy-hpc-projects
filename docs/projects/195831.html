Project Summary

Overall, the Food Security Sector (FSS) estimates that 12 million people in Syria are facing acute food insecurity, which is at least 54.4% of Syria population. While the food insecure figures have slightly declined by at least 4% compared to last year’s record increase, the number of people facing acute food insecurity remains extremely high.,. Drought-like conditions water scarcity and very low availability of energy supplies in Syria are affecting agriculture production capacity, thus affecting farmer’s livelihoods and local food production. In this context, FAO has designed a programme to assist 92,903 small-scale farming households (equivalent to 557,419 people) in Syria, with the objective of protecting and enhancing the livelihoods of vulnerable farmers and IDPs through the restoration of critical irrigation infrastructures and improve water use efficiency to increase household food production and food security.
. The project outputs include:
• Output 1: Critical small-scale irrigation infrastructures in the targeted governorates are assessed and light rehabilitation undertaken.
• Output 2: Capacity of technical staff and relevant stakeholders including farmers strengthened to increase water use efficiency and establishment of Water Users Associations (WUAs)
A training programme on enhancing irrigation management, water use efficiency in small-scale irrigation, agricultural land management will be based on FAO standards and approaches. It is expected that stakeholders and farmers will participate in the proposed trainings.
This programme is expected to contribute to increasing access to irrigation water for crisis-affected farmers and their families in the targeted areas through the light rehabilitation of critical small scale irrigation infrastructure. Crop cultivation and other agricultural practices, including livestock and animal feed production, will consequently benefit from the improved water supply plans as an emergency response to the prevailing crisis and drought-like conditions. The proposed intervention will strengthen household food production capacities, skills and knowledge and address immediate food security and nutrition needs, through increasing local availability and supply of food. This may in turn promote flourishing of agricultural based livelihoods in the targeted rural communities, which would contribute to social cohesion, peace and stability. The programme seeks to utilize intensive labour in order to maximize community participation and inject cash into the local community.

3 - Needs

Agricultural infrastructure suffered extensive damage, during the years of Syrian crisis. Resulted to disruption of large sections of the agriculture system and substantial weakening of its components, including farmers who rely on irrigation systems. Ministry of Water Resources estimates that the average annual deficit water (1.533 billion m3) increased to 5 billion recently. Moreover, it is estimate that 25% of the irrigation infrastructures damaged. The low production of wheat and other crops during the 2022 harvest season has resulted in a significant gap in supply and demand. Farmers’ livelihoods and stability are greatly compromised by the many challenges they face including the high cost of irrigation, damage to pumping stations, irrigation infrastructure and equipment, many farmers have abandoned irrigation and turned to rain fed production, which is prone to climate- risks. Light rehabilitation of critical infrastructures to support crop production has become an urgent concern that calls for immediate actions to save the lives and protect livelihoods of farmers. Since the beginning of the crisis, FAO has restored irrigation to more than 10,000 ha belonging to about 5,500 smallholder HH, while studies shown that benefits also accrue to the extended HH, and create opportunities for paid agricultural work. In addition, FAO interventions have enhanced equitable access to irrigation water and communities management of water resources through establishing WAU.

4 - Implementation of activities

Output 1: Critical small-scale irrigation infrastructures in the targeted governorates are assessed and light rehabilitation undertaken. Activities; 1)Conduct field missions the main accessible irrigation projects in the targeted Governorates and assess the irrigation infrastructure damage, as well as water resource availability. 2)Agree with counterparts on a priority list for urgent interventions to rehabilitate. 3)Development of beneficiary selection criteria, and Formalize, finalize agreement with selected IP. 4)Priority irrigation infrastructure rehabilitation: -Rehabilitation of the main irrigation channels in the selected sites, Rehabilitation of some collective irrigation wells by operating pumps, Maintenance of some closed and pressurized irrigation networks, Maintenance of sewage canals in selected locations, Maintenance and rehabilitation of irrigation canals siphons, pilot fields for modern irrigation. 5)Monitor and evaluate the project activities. Output 2: Capacity of technical staff and relevant stakeholders including farmers strengthened to increase water use efficiency and establishment of WUAs. Activities: 1)Prepare training programme. 2)Select beneficiaries upon criteria. 3) training on good agricultural practices, water use efficiency based on water–crop requirement. 4) practical field training on installation and operation of drip irrigation systems, solar panels, practical support for operating and maintaining of small-scale irrigation systems. 5) WAU

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiary selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (for example: IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. Mitigation measures: Establish a community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do no harm principles, including GBV and PSEA risk mitigation measures. Measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted because of the being selected for the project. Women and youth will be prioritized, inputs and trainings will take account of the different requirements of women and men, boys and girls. Returnees will also be targeted so that re-settlement is supported. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well to limit possible friction between the two
