Project Summary

WFP aims to contribute to a reduction in acute and chronic malnutrition rates, and an improvement in nutritional status among refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar through the provision of curative and preventive nutrition services for children below 5 years and pregnant and lactating women (PLW).
WFP will implement its project interventions across 45 integrated in nutrition facilities in all 34 camps: and 129 sites in the host community in partnership with UNICEF, UNHCR and implementing partners.
WFP will provide targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) for moderate acutely malnourished children 6-59 months and PLW to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition in both the refugee camps and in the host community.
An enhanced prevention of malnutrition package will be put in place. This includes provision of the blanket supplementary feeding program (BSFP) for children 6-59 months and PLW to mitigate the increase in prevalence of acute and chronic malnutrition and its associated risks for mortality, morbidity, and child development; a nutrition sensitive cash transfer to a proportion of older children (24-59 months) to enable them access more fresh fruits and vegetables and enhance their dietary diversity in the refugee camps; Social Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) activities and utilization of tools that positively influence behaviour, knowledge and attitudes around nutrition, hygiene and social protection interventions to improve dietary practices and ensure sustained changes for cases of malnutrition.
Prevention of stunting among children 6 – 23 months (the 1000 days window of opportunity) through promotion of recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices (breastfeeding for children below 2 years and complementary feeding for children 6 – 23 months) and appropriate maternal nutrition will also be part of the project interventions.
Active case finding through community screening, referral and follow up of malnourished children under five and pregnant and lactating women will be done. Additionally, growth monitoring and promotion for children 6-59 months will be part of the project interventions.
The underlying causes of malnutrition will be addressed through integration of nutrition sensitive approaches in ongoing general food assistance, school feeding, self-reliance, and livelihood interventions.
WFP will promote the prevention of micronutrient deficiencies particularly anaemia through the promotion of dietary diversification.
WFP will conduct capacity strengthening for partners and stakeholders to improve capacity for implementation of nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive programming.
WFP will support quantitative and qualitative nutrition assessments to ensure that nutrition programmes are tailormade according to the needs of the targeted populations.
Project interventions will be adapted to ensure an inclusive approach to promote participation of women, men, older people, people who have chronic health conditions and persons with disabilities. Additionally, protection mechanisms will be put in place including complaints and feedback mechanisms and help desks to ensure accountability to affected populations.

1 - Identified Needs

According to preliminary results of the SENS November 2021, there was a deterioration of the nutrition situation with GAM rates increasing from 11.3% in 2020 to 13.7% in 2021. Chronic malnutrition is high according to WHO thresholds with stunting at 30.2% and underweight at 31.7%. There was an increase in anaemia rates among children and women of reproductive age at 50.5% and 40.3% respectively indicating a problem of public health significance. The COVID-19 situation and its consequent restrictions has significantly hampered the delivery of services. Several services like nutrition education and growth monitoring and promotion which are required to enhance utilization of nutrition services and products and identify those at risk of acute and chronic forms of malnutrition respectively, were suspended. Nutrition sensitization and awareness aimed at improving household level dietary practices was not regularly conducted. These factors could have contributed to the poor nutrition outcomes observed. Moreover, community mobilization has been difficult meaning that there are could be more children who are not being reached with services and efforts to prevent and treat malnutrition. Ongoing assessments have highlighted sub-optimal IYCF practices, inadequate dietary diversity, poor care practices, high disease morbidity, low birth spacing, inadequate WASH practices as the key underlying factors to the high prevalence rates of malnutrition observed in the camps. There is therefore need for multisectoral approaches that integrate nutrition sensitive actions for the prevention of malnutrition.
