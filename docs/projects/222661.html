Project Summary


Cordaid employs an integrated approach in implementing food security and livelihoods response to meet the critical needs, particularly food needs and building resilience of vulnerable communities in targeted location; Magwi, Malakal, Renk The project seeks to address food insecurity, malnutrition and other basic needs driven by multifaceted factors, conflict, climatic and economic shocks. The project is aligned to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HRP) objectives and the Food Security and livelihood Cluster Objective 1,2 and 3. The project targets a total of 15,000 households , who will receive food assistance, emergency livelihood support; food production (crop, vegetables) and fishing kits coupled with the training on climate smart agriculture, post harvest handling and nutrition education. In additions households in IPC3 will be supported through skills development and business start up to engage in nutrition sensitive value chains, off farm income generation activities. Under the emergency food assistance to prevent famine and improve food security and reduce reliance on negative coping mechanism; Cordaid will provide food assistance using the cash modality and in-kind food rations in accordance with the FSL cluster standards to a total of 9,000 individuals. The distribution will be done alongside key messaging on nutrition, hygiene, gender equality, conflict sensitivity and referrals to nutrition, health and protection services. The target will prioritize the most vulnerable particularly the children, pregnant and lactating women, people with disability, elderly, the chronical ill, female headed households drawn from the internally displaced person, returnees, refugees and the host communities. The conditional will target the labor endowed households in both IPC 3 and 4 locations to enable households to meet the food needs and other basic needs whilst creating assets that promote food production, community infrastructure that improve access to basic services, assets that reduce disasters and are climate resilient. The project will support improved access to emergency livelihood through complementary vegetable and crop kits, agricultural tools, fishing kits and livestock inputs to enhance food production. The project will target a total of 2,500 households. The training on agronomic practices, fish processing and post-harvest management will be done to reduce post-harvest losses. Further, the community-based extension services will be strengthened by training of community animal health workers and extension agents to support livestock vaccination and provide extension services. In IPC 3 locations, the project will build community resilience and foster social cohesion to reduce dependency on humanitarian assistance by strengthening the adsorptive and adaptive capacities of households to shocks. A total of 2000HHs will benefit from this intervention. This will be done through supporting increased diversified production, post-harvest handling, processing and nutrition and gender sensitive value chain development. In addition, the households will receive training on climate smart agriculture, farming as a business using the farmer field school approach and linked to the markets to improve household economy. Off-farm income generating activities will be promoted through skills and entrepreneurship development. To enhance access to finance the farmer groups will be supported to form village saving groups. Climate mitigation and adaptation approaches will be strengthened by implementing community driven disaster risk management, community based early warning systems.

1. Needs

Conflict and insecurity: South Sudan is a fragile state, with ongoing subnational intercommunal conflicts, insecurity, resulting in frequent displacements in a protracted crisis. This has further been compounded by the Sudan crisis that has led to 822,534 people, 51% women and girls crossing to South Sudan putting additional pressure on social amenities that are already in deplorable state thus worsening an already dire situation. Vulnerability to climate change, characterized by consecutive flooding that has led to displacements of 735,000 people alongside droughts combined with a deepening economic crisis, currency depreciation, low agricultural production, and sharp rising market prices, high unemployment have exacerbated food insecurity. Food insecurity and malnutrition levels are high, with 6.3million people facing acute hunger and malnutrition-IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) (SSD IPC report, November 2024). An estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). Malnutrition rates are high among children and women due to limited access to healthy diets, poor water and hygiene practices, GBV concerns, and poor child feeding and caring practices. Agricultural production is hindered by flooding, dry spells, poor agricultural practices, lack of improved climate resilient crops or vegetable varieties and mechanized farming and post-harvest technology. Gender disparity/inequality and protection risks: Conflict and climate shocks have exacerbated exploitation, abuse, sexual violence and gender-based violence. In some instances, they are forced to participate in underage employment and negative coping mechanisms to support their HHs livelihoods. In addition, Socio-cultural norms have marginalized women, limiting their access to resources, decision-making processes and economic empowerment Limited public infrastructure and weakened local structures to implement DRR prepared response and early warning systems. This creates a potential conflict, as host communities, refugees, returnees, and IDPs compete for limited resources, putting additional strain on local systems. The upper Nile state particularly Malakal and Renk counties, fall in the main transit or settling zones for returnees coming from Sudan, they have a high number of returnees and Internal displaced persons (IDPs) integrating within the local communities straining local resources and systems and creating potential for conflict. The region faces significant problems, including food insecurity, a high number of returnees, recurrent intercommunal tensions, susceptibility to natural disasters resulting in flooding or droughts, and limited capacities of subnational and community-based structures to implement effective disaster preparedness and prevention measures to protect livelihoods. Further, these locations are in IPC3+; Renk and Malakal facing acute food insecurity. These areas are characterized by high food insecurity, malnutrition, depleted assets base and are resorting to crisis or emergency coping mechanism. Renk has recently experienced a cholera outbreak in October 2024, in the transit zone which might spread to the surrounding host communities posing a significant health threat to the vulnerable populations that if not mitigated could result to fatalities. Magwi counties have been targeted as they have some population who are food insecure, and a need to enhance livelihoods to prevent the population sliding back to IPC4.
