Project Summary

Medair’s proposed intervention will provide life-saving WASH interventions at community and household-level to increase water supply that currently barely reaches or in most cases are under the Sphere Standard and address a dire situation in community sanitation. The proposed response will reach approximately 95,175 beneficiaries through:
i. Water network rehabilitation:
Medair will rehabilitate up to 6 community level water systems. Medair’s activities will include the upgrade/installation of water pumping equipment, equipping wells, replacing generators, repairing distribution lines and installing new components for safe drinking water systems. Alongside these activities, Medair will promote water conservation, and also teach proper operation and maintenance (O&M) techniques to Water Economic Unit (WEU) water system operators responsible for systems rehabilitated by Medair.
ii. Water storage solution:
As part of FLER particularly for recently displaced HHs in hard to reach (HTR) and newly accessible areas, or as COVID-19 response for HHs particularly vulnerable for COVID-19, Medair will provide household water tanks (HHWT) to approximately 540 households for domestic use.
iii. Sewage network rehabilitation:
Medair will rehabilitate one sewage network, the aim of the intervention is to ensure that waste can be adequately transported away from the HH and public spaces where it may pose health risks through a functioning piped network free from leaks and blockages. Medair’s planned sewage network rehabilitation will focus on Deir-ez-Zor city to prevent further discharge of raw sewage into the open and to restore the previous sewage network and connectivity to the main sewage line.
iv. Distribution of CovHPs
Medair will distribute 2,900 COVID-19 hygiene packs for families particularly vulnerable for COVID-19 infection. Packs include hygiene items as well as hygiene awareness posters.
v. Distribution of FHKs
Medair will distribute 2,355 Female Hygiene Kits (FHKs) to women of reproductive age in order to meet their hygiene and protection needs. Women are identified during multi-sectoral household assessments conducted by Medair.
Medair’s proposed intervention will target vulnerable households and communities in Government Controlled areas within Homs, Hama, Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor City, Quneitra or Rural Damascus, where Medair already has presence, subject to approvals and identified needs. Ar-Raqqa is also included as a potential area of intervention (areas under GoS control), pending context development and access.
Medair will conduct post-project and post-distribution monitoring, as well as have site engineers and Medair staff present during distributions and rehabilitations to monitor the process.

4 - Needs

WASH infrastructure is severely damaged due to sustained hostilities, especially in HTR or newly accessible locations. WASH provision is now more important than ever with COVID-19. Most communities assessed by Medair past year did not meet Sphere standards for safe drinking water (15 l/p/d), let alone the covered minimum needs of 35 l/p/d based on Medair’s previous experience. RNAs conducted by Medair in Khashniyyeh and Khan Arnaba found that 50% of people do not have access to enough water, and had to resort to watertrucking, while 100% stated there was no access to hygiene items such as water containers and disinfectant. Assessments in Taldu found that none of the communities had access to enough water. In Duma, RNAs showed that there was a lack of hygiene items available, with 50% of people needing to share toilets with other HHs. In all communities assessed in RAQ, 100% stated there was a lack of hygiene items, as well as water containers.80 to 85% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of sewage systems do not function. Locations of special concern include areas high in IDPs/returnees putting pressure on existing WASH infrastructure, areas with limited electricity access, severe issues with sewage disposal and lack of garbage removal services. In DEZ, these factors contribute to significant health risks confirmed by Medair’s health programming incl. talks with DoH and MoH.Deteriorating economic conditions remain a challenge for vulnerable communities and HHs in accessing safe, affordable, and equitable WASH services. WASH infrastructure requires significant repair and operational support, including training for O&M of rehabilitated infrastructure and on-grid electricity supply in areas of high need.

5 - Implementation of activities

Medair will rehab 6 water systems to restore adequate water supply to communities. After rehabilitations, projects are handed back to the WEU who is responsible for water quality and testing on regular basis. O&M training is provided to WEUs to ensure sustainability. Where possible, Community Awareness-Raising workshops on water conservation and safety are organized for key local leaders. Targeted sub-districts are Khashniyyeh, Khan Arnaba, and Taldu, with potential added locations in RAQ, Homs, and ALP.Medair will rehabilitate one community level sewage network in DEZ, assumed to serve 12,500. Selection of the project will happen based on strict vulnerability criteria, considering the public health impact. Post-rehab, the system is handed back to the sewage company and local Municipality.Medair will distribute 540 HHWTs (incl. hygiene awareness materials) serving an up to 2,700 beneficiaries to meet urgent water storage needs. Installations are carried out for select vulnerable beneficiaries (e.g. FHH, PwD) who may not be able to do so themselves. Targeted sub-districts are Duma and DEZ.Medair will provide 2,900 CovHPs where the HH is considered at higher COVID-19 vulnerability, to protect them from infection. The CovHP consists of a.o. hygiene materials such as soap, liquid chlorine disinfectant, house cleaning kit, and Hygiene posters. Targeted sub-districts are Duma and DEZ.Medair will provide 2,355 FHKs to women of reproductive age to address their hygiene and protection needs. Targeted subdistricts are Haritan, Duma, DEZ, and RAQ.Medair will conduct PPMs/PDM after the activities, during distributions AAP focal points will be present, and during rehabs, a Medair site-engineer will be present as focal point.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: - No significant change to the political and security situation inside Syria - Market systems remain relatively stable - Any inflation/deflation of the SYP will be able to be offset against hard currency (USD) - Acceptance and participation of authorities, local leaders and communities during interventions - Equipment in specifications listed by Ministries will be available at the time of procurement RISKS: - Medair cannot secure approvals from ministries within the timeline, delaying project delivery - Significant injury to team members within Syria due to security incidents - Central Bank imposes regulations which stop or constrain Medair’s ability to maintain adequate cash flow to pay programme expenses - Medair staff or contractors commit fraud or do not comply with procurement guidelines.- Widespread COVID-19 infection occurs, affecting staff, beneficiaries, and the project.MITIGATIONS: - keep security planning up to date and training a crisis management team for each location - Daily, careful monitoring of context, security situation and trends including close liaison with local stakeholders on security and a clear safety briefing to all staff and volunteers - Maintain good contact with SARC, Ministries, local authorities, communities, UN agencies and other stakeholders - Balance activities for IDPs, returnees and host population to avoid unnecessary tensions, noting that lead factor for beneficiary selection is vulnerability, not status - Complete training for new staff on procurement guidelines, conduct systematic internal and external audits to assess adherence to financial and logistic guidelines and spot check for any fraudulent activity.- have COVID contingency plans in place.
