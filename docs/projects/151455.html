Project Summary

The Nutrition and health emergency project provide life-saving nutrition and health interventions for Guban Agropastoral and pastoral community IPD, Urban and rural in Baki, Lughaya and Saylac districts in Awdal region. The project will be done through the adoption of the necessary nutrition services package (BNSP). And as well as the corresponding essential vital areas for maternal and child health interventions namely: management of acute malnutrition, micronutrient supplementation, immunisation, de-worming, promotion and support for IYCF/IYCF-E and maternal nutrition and care, prevention and management of common illnesses, monitoring and surveillance.
The Nutrition intervention is to support approximately 32,821 moderately malnourished children, 24,615PLW in project target areas of Awdal region. The OTP beneficiaries, in the case of the mobile sites and the villages within the coverage access Community Nutrition Workers will conduct mass screening, defaulter tracing and follow up and refer malnourished individuals to the nearest service delivery point.
SOLNARDO has a good relationship with the host communities and government and thus will continue to work closely with them. In order in engaging the community, SOLNARDO will conduct community needs assessment, sensitisation and orientation meetings. SOLNARDO is an active member of the Somaliland Nutrition Working group, and will continue actively participating in the forum where it will share achievements, challenges and lessons learnt. SOLNARDO will also continue to conduct joint monitoring visits to project sites with MoH and UNICEF. This joint monitoring will not only improve coordination but will contribute to improving the quality of health service provision. Community-based monitoring of the project will be promoted through liaison with district health boards, Community development committees, local CBOs and other community structures. The project will capacitate community development committees on disaster and emergency preparedness and response.

Needs

Malnutrition is one of the most persistence humanitarian challenges in Somalia despite concerted efforts to address it. Acute malnutrition in children between 6-59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, WASH and health situations among others. The significant deterioration of the malnutrition situation seen among the IPDs can be associated to high morbidity ( disease incidence, e.g. AWD, measles, ....etc.), low humanitarian support, poor child feeding and caring practices. Similarly, partners on the ground generally felt that food insecurity, limited, health services availability, like poor EPI coverage, and increased morbidity, poor health-seeking behaviour and difficulty accessing clean water supply are driving factors for the current situation. Malnutrition is an outcome indicator highlighting needs some sectors and the vulnerability of communities. Displacement of the population out of the rural area due to conflict or/ and climate hazard and growing trends of evictions of IDPs: The growing number of IDPs due to evacuation, conflict and natural hazards (cyclone) is another humanitarian challenge that requires a sustained humanitarian response in 2019. The coupled with expected deteriorations in some parts of the country will make the overall population in need of emergency nutrition support to about 2.6 million community including those in need of preventive, and therapeutic nutrition services. Similarly, as per Somalia 2016 IYCF Study, Somali children are at higher risk of nutritional deprivation including those living in a poor household headed by a male, under the care of a young mother. IDP populations face considerable challenges where high pervasiveness of women-headed households is chronically combating high levels of acute malnutrition in their households.

Activities

SOLNARDO in partnership with the MoHD will address the nutrition and health challenges in project target community in Agro-pastoral, postal, and IDP community in Awdal region, Baki, Lughaya, and Saylac districts. The community-based project will provide integrated emergency health and nutrition live-saving services in a selected community. The project activities include OTP and mobile health clinic services for Acute Malnourished children. Mass screening for children 6-59 Months for early case identification, defaulter tracing and follow up. Optimal IYCF/IYCF-E interventions, monitoring Code of MBS volitions, orientation/training and coordinating with the other partners. In IDPs, Breastfeeding Corners establish to support breastfeeding mothers, and mass social awareness and advocacy. The project implementation approach is ‘bottom-up,’ which hinges on involving the community at every level of the project to build the community trust and acceptance propose solutions to improve maternally and infant and young child practices. The project will conduct community needs assessment and identified the priority needs and the status of optimal IYCF/IYCF-E practices. The result of the assessment provides project baseline data which assisted in the development of community action plans and communication strategies and approaches to address identified barriers and build community assets that support those behaviours. The emergency health and nutrition project addresses existing community barriers on Maternal and infant and young child nutrition in the emergency by conducting activities at a variety of levels including household, community and health facility. At community level trained community Nutrition workers/CHW with the IYCF counselling package, interpersonal communication skills and Nutrition Health and Hygiene Promotion, and screening children 6-59 months. The project will train Community development committees on Emergency preparedness and responses.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Community-based integrated emergency Nutrition and health project have linked with the creation of sustainable optimal IYCF practices through changing behaviour and practices of mothers, child care providers and community. The project will improve the knowledge, skills, and practices of the target community and capacity community nutrition workers on the promotion, protection and support of optimal IYCF practices, promotion of hygiene, situation and health-seeking behaviour practices. Similarly, the project will establish a sustainable community resource through training community nutrtion workers. The project will engage and involved local district authority, community development committee, women and youth organisations on the participation of project interventions from the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages. Also, a project will capacitate a community development committee for disaster and emergency preparation and mitigation. The emergency integrated nutrition and health project is targeting to capacitate and the establishment of functioning community emergency preparedness and planning system at the community level. The rural communities suffer a lot than the other people living in semi-urban or urban communities in the towns. The rural communities are not capacitated to respond and plan those emergency situations affecting their lives, and property. Capacitating the village development committees through training them on Disaster RR reduction will have helped them to be better prepared for the cycle effect on human and assists ( livestock and Properties ) emergency and achieving early recovery.Apart from the recent cyclone, the community of Lughaya has been experiencing a frequent drought for the past five years. Due to receiving low average rainfall. In 2017 the people of lochia lost around 70-80% of their livestock which they economically depend on which increased their vulnerability for food impurity, increased malnutrition rate among children, disease outbreak and water shortage. In this aspect, the community committee is to capacitate in the emergency prepares and response plan to mitigate the effects of the emergency drought and cyclone.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main risks facing affected people are to be marginalized by the host community. The project mitigation is to ensure the inclusiveness of all the population, including disability, and minority groups to be part of the process of the project. The project staff will be trained on the monitoring and risk analysis, identification and reporting.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The main risk facing the target population is the marginalization based in gender inequality and elder members of the community, and the mitigation process is to analys and ensure inclusiveness of gender balance and all relevant age groups in the process of the project interventions.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project target locations are secure and do not involve in armed conflicts and violence.
