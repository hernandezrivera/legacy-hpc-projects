Project Summary

More than 2.7 million are Internally Displaced People (IDPs), many of whom are living in over-crowded locations with limited access to essential services. As of February 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1,385 camps or informal sites (including 24,800 people displaced in January and February 2021 alone). Women and children represent 80 per cent of this caseload while more than 22,000 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs. This must also be considered against the lingering effects of the massive displacement of nearly 1 million people in early 2020.
The expanding impact of the COVID-19 pandemic has further complicated the lives of people in humanitarian settings
in northwest Syria. While the number of positive COVID-19 cases has steadily decreased, there is a continued need to focus on treating and mitigating the spread of the virus and its impacts on displaced people and other vulnerable communities, including the healthcare workforce, and supporting scale-up of the public health response

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A lack of food and NFIs, WASH facilities and shelter create a myriad of protectionconcerns, especially for vulnerable groups, such as the elderly, women and adolescents.SoP will continue to monitor protection risks, working with protection colleagues if specific issues arise and will ensure that any activities including targeting, distribution and monitoring do not increase these risks. Due to security threats from the conflict such as most relief assistance is being delivered in a low-profile manner through the door-to-door processor in public buildings where IDPs currently reside and in Camps This distribution modality helps to limit the exposure of beneficiaries and the local association members .Where it is deemed appropriate by the local associations, there are distribution points where beneficiaries convene to collect distributions. In some areas, public distributions are unavoidable butcare is taken to arrange them at what is considered the safest possible location and time of day When distributions are done in a more centralized manner, utilizing information solicited from beneficiaries communities, SoP will ensure that they are done at times and in places that are safe and accessible to women and girls, as well as the elderly and people with disabilities and that any barriers to access as a result of gender norms or physicalability are taken into account when planning for the distribution. As needed, extrameasures will be taken to minimize risk, such as buddy systems for going to and fromdistribution points. Community input will be solicited as to which measures would bemost feasible and preferred, and support will be provided by SoP to ensure a sustainable ,community-driven approach is adopted.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: The security situation remains stable and the center remains accessible.The acceptance of SOP and its center by the local community and the local authorities issustained.The need for GBV program in the area persists. Risks:1-military escalation erupts in the targeted location mitigation: we will train our staff on emergencypreparedness and GBV closure SOPs. the situation will be monitored closely with the field staff and local authorities.2- the confidentiality of the GBV is breached.mitigation: we will put in place strict disciplinary measures and train the staff on the code of conduct. Our staff have been working in this center for several years and they are aware of the sensitivity of GBV cases. Risk 3: Beneficiaries will not attend the vocational training regularly mitigation 3: Beneficiaries will be consulted on the topics, the pace of the course and they will be able to give feedback. the reasons for their lack of commitment will be analyzed and addressed accordingly.
