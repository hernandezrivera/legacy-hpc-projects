Project Summary

The goal of the project is to reduce WASH related public health and protection risks among 65 000 conflict affected men and women through the provision of safe and adequate drinking water, sanitation facilities and public health promotion in affected IDPs and host communities in Mudug,Banadir (Mogadishu/Afgooye Corridor), Baidoa, Lower Juba and Galmudug regions of South Central Somalia. One of the outputs is for the targeted men, women and children to have improved access to at least 20 litres of drinking water per day as per the Sphere standards during and after the project period. This will be achieved through trucking of sufficient quantity of safe water to designated water points to meet the needs of the displaced populations in newly established IDP settlements which do not have any source of water. The water points will be installed at strategic locations of not more than 500 metres away from the furthest beneficiary settlements. The water tanks will also have a high water output to ensure that the queuing time does not exceed 30 minutes. To address water needs in host communities receiving IDPs, the project will rehabilitate shallow wells across four regions in Somalia (Galmudug, lower juba, Banadir and Bay including installation of new hand pumps, raising the well mouth and fencing the perimeter to ensure animals and children are kept away. This is expected to provide 10,000 individuals with safe water at 7.5 litres per person per day. 500 persons per hand pump at flow rate of 17 liters/minute. Furthermore, the project will also address water needs in schools by installing of a water pipe system supported by a 5000 litres plastic water tank in each school. This will serve 6000 plus pupils where majority of them are IDP children. Mercy Corps will also conduct water quality testing to ensure quality of water. To improve the practice of good hygiene, the project will also provide vulnerable households with adequate and appropriate NFIs such as water collection and storage jerricans accompanied with hygiene promotion sessions spearheaded by trained CHWs. To address sanitation needs, the project will support the construction of gender sensitive latrines. The project will address protection issues especially for women and girls through awareness raising. To ensure accountability to beneficiaries, Mercy Corps have sound accountability mechanisms which includes a dedicated mobile telephone number which can be used by beneficiaries to complain in case of violence/ harassment and other related complaints. To ensure sustainability, the project will adopt participatory and empowering approaches. Also Mercy Corps has technical staff who can guarantee the quality of the project outcomes, also through various trainings and participation, the community will be empowered to withstand future shocks.

Needs

Somali people urgently need support to access WASH services owing to insecurity related challenges main in parts of central Somalia. An increase in the influx of IDPs has significantly strained existing water sources, sanitation facilities and compromised hygiene practices especially in IDPS camps. Recent assessments by Mercy Corps in Galmudug, lower Juba, Banadir and Bay regions more than half of the population are with no access to improved water sources with about 35% of the boreholes not functioning. Access to WASH services has been compromised due to repeated displacement, insecurity and damage to infrastructure hence exposing individuals to diseases. As a result, almost half of assessed households across the four regions (47%) indicated insufficient water access, with heavy reliance on unprotected sources (either a river, unprotected well or a berkad) and salinity of ground water is also a major concern. Surface water sources are seasonal and usually contaminated, while protected groundwater supplies are either difficult to access to due to distance and/or not affordable due the high costs of extraction and the dominance of private sector entrepreneurs in their operation. Long water collection distances further limit access and water consumption for many people. Most rural populations are reliant on adhoc water supply such as water trucking for both human and livestock consumption. Open defecation still remains rampant in these areas hosting IDPs and returnees leading to frequent outbreaks of AWDs. Overcrowding in IDPs has compromised access to proper sanitation. In many IDP settlements there are no health promoters and populations are practicing risk behavior which exposes them to WASH related diseases.

Activities

Mercy corps plans to rehabilitate and protect 50 shallow wells across four regions in Somalia (Galmudug, lower juba, Banadir and Bay including installation of new hand pumps, protection of the wells will include raising the well mouth and fencing the perimeter to ensure animals and children are kept away. To improve water supply in schools Mercy Corps will install 9 water piping systems in selected schools. The challenges of water salinity of ground water and continuous displacement and movement of people necessitated the need for water trucking of 10 000 litres per each delivery and Mercy Corps will target those IDP camps in critical situations. In addition to water provision, MC will conduct bacteriological testing weekly. Mercy corps plans to construct 100 disludgeable, gender friendly latrines, equipped with hand wash facilities and provision of soap for hand washing and other related NFIs such as jerry cans. To address sanitation gaps in the targeted 20 schools located in the four target regions, Mercy corps proposes to build a total of 10 blocks twin latrines of each two stances. Each of the 20 schools will get two blocks, one for girls and another for boys with hand wash facilities and sited away from each other. Mercy corps will adopt the UNICEF institutional latrine design. Mercy Corps will train 350 male and female CHWs to spearhead hygiene promotion in communities including distributing IEC materials. Mercy Corps will also train at least 3 teachers in supported schools on hygiene promotion.Output 1.1 Targeted men, women and children have safe and improved access to clean drinking water during and after project period.Output 2.1 Targeted households and school children benefit from sanitary facilities.Output 3.1 Improved access to public health information to conflict affected populationOutput 4.1 Targeted men, women and children living in areas accessible to humanitarian workers have information about protection and referral services available.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project will address the humanitarian needs of the displaced people and vulnerable hosts’ community in a manner that drives beyond service delivery, to one that builds resilience and the ability to withstand future shocks; preserves safety and dignity in order to improve the lives and self-reliance of displaced populations while simultaneously addressing the impact on host communities. Mercy Corps will work with all respective stakeholders starting with the participation of affected persons, the WASH, Protection and Health Cluster Coordination team at local and national level and the relevant Government Ministries. The participation of all stakeholders will build a strong foundation for disaster risk reduction through empowerment, synergies of approaches and complimentary programing across sectors. Hygiene kits beneficiaries and beneficiaries of sanitation and water assets will be identified jointly with IDP Leaders and local authorities; this will ensure effectiveness in addressing real needs which is necessary for sustainability. In line with Mercy corps principles, the implementation will be anchored on beneficiary buy-in and ownership mechanisms accountable to the beneficiary, the donor, other fellow implementing partners and the local administration. This will help identify and assign roles and responsibilities at local community level, activation of local structures is necessary for sustainability. Also MC has in place accountability mechanisms to ensure the beneficiary and stakeholders get detailed and accurate information relevant to the project and they can also provide feedback. Mercy corps has a team of technical and administrative managers and officers at various levels who can ensure the quality of the intended outputs guided by a set of standards including the SPHERE. Training of CHWs, teachers and local authorities in various subjects including DRR will improve capacity to sustain the interventions and reduce their risks to future shocks.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project will directly target areas receiving or hosting displaced households in order to mitigate the risk of exclusion based on displacement. The project seeks to address the needs of the households displaced and the selection criteria will target those households displaced. To respond to mobile vulnerable populations, the project is designed in a flexible manner to meet to needs of ever displaced people including a specific design of a temporary latrine to cater for displacements. Water trucking is also in part meant to address the needs of displaced people. During the targeting process local authority might influence on the selection of locations due to clan nepotism or particular interest and this way risk of exclusion of selection of the most disaster/displacement affected locations may be experienced. To mitigate this risk, Mercy Corps will adopt vulnerability-based targeting with community input approach. In addition, at the onset of the project a comprehensive community mobilization and sensitization activities will be undertaken focusing on the three pillars of; Information sharing, participation and complaint & response mechanism. This process will enable all community members including displaced communities to be fully aware of the project which will be implemented in their community. During targeting priority will be given to female, orphan headed households, minority and marginalized groups, those with many children, elderly and disabled amongst displaced communities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Mercy Corps places vulnerability due to gender and age at the centre of all its emergency assistance. To mitigate the risk of exclusion, during the project design stage, the targeted women ,men and children of different age groups were directly engaged through focus group discussions (FGD) and Key informant interviews for them to articulate their core problems and rank their key priorities that are relevant and appropriate to their needs. Mercy Corps organized separate interactive sessions for people with special interests and needs mainly women, elderly and people with disabilities in order to capture their special needs and design activities that meet their needs as well. Representatives of these core groups were later involved in the final prioritization and ranking of the key needs and the preferred interventions. In all the FGDs and Key informant interviews conducted with key stakeholders and other special groups, improving access to clean drinking was ranked as the main priority for the majority of the conflict-affected households. MC has global procedures and standards for gender incorporated in design, start up and implementation and monitoring and evaluation processes to help teams integrate gender into their program. These have been rolled out agency wide and will be shared with programme staff to help them keep gender issues at the forefront of their development, implementation, and monitoring of the program. With regards to this particular project, there is a deliberate approach to gender based targeting. Women and girls in Somalia remain marginalized and most of the beneficiaries will be women. In addition, the project will take into consideration different needs for both men and women including sex sanitation needs. Culturally men are not active in WASH especially hygiene promotion and the project will target men, encouraging them to participate as CHWs. Mercy Corps staff will mainstream gender throughout the project implementation.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The target populations remain concerned with insecurity which caused their displacement and it continue to affect them in different ways include the risk of exclusion from accessing humanitarian assistance due to limited access and perceived risk by aid agencies. To mitigate this, MC has updated security and safety plans in place to prevent and respond to threats. MC works closely with UN agencies, local partner organizations, and stakeholders to share security information, coordination, and joint security emergency response including evacuation and relocation. Furthermore, MC has strong community networks at the locality level and in their respective work areas. MC also has a well-established field office and a strong community network across the regions for many years which can be instrumental in ensuring the safety and security of staff and project assets, as well as the overall coordination hub for activities. Additionally, MC will instruct field staff to take the requisite field security trainings.This project will put deliberate efforts to increase most vulnerable groups participation and influence in contexts of peace, conflicts and other humanitarian crisis. To mitigate impact of armed conflict and violence particularly on the most vulnerable groups (women, minority clans etc.) the project will seek to collaborate with communities to foster peaceful co-existence and advocate for behavior change during community gatherings. The project will map out safe and protected distribution centers for project entitlements to mitigate harm and ensure protection and dignity of beneficiaries is observed. Mercy Corps will also designate female representatives at field level to receive beneficiary feedback and complains from women and girls as this is more culturally acceptable. Random calls will establish and address issues of discrimination, abuse and exploitation against women and girls.
