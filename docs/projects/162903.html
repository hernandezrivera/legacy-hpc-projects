Project Summary

this project is designed to reduce the critical educational gaps and needs for 18 schools in the Northern and Southern parts of kutum Locality in North Darfur state, by tackling the needs of 5,154 people including IDPs, Returnees, and the hosting community. Out of which 4,992 are in school age segregated as (1,924 Boys, and 3,068 Girls) , and 435 adults (230 Males, and 115 Females) encompassing teachers, and parents, in Bairi, biraidik, Maryood azrag, Amray, Ango, Anka, Alhilf alshamali, biraidik, Um teen, Gora, Um sidir, Hahsab, Radma, Hamra albeer, kasa, Naina, Kurbe alhamra, Jadeeda baneen, jadeeda banat, and Um Dilaika schools in Rural Kutum, and other 6 schools in southern Kutum).
FPDO will increase and sustains access to basic education through reaching out the communities residing in the targeted areas by enrollment campaigns in order to promote the importance of children enrollment, and the importance of girls’ education, construction of 18 permanent classrooms and rehabilitation of 38 permanent classrooms, construction of 26 gender sensitive latrines with 6 drop holes, and rehabilitation of 8 permanent latrines. Also FPDO will intend to provide 600 seating units for 2400 students (1200 boy & 1200 girl).
The improvement of educational environment would be through: Distribution of school Uniform for 3000 students (1500 girls and 1500 boys), teaching and learning materials for 3000 students (1500 girls and 1500 boys).
The action will also contribute in improving resilience of local actors through capacity building (PTAs and teachers) in mitigating the bad effects of education emergencies. FPDO will organize 8 teacher trainings for 165 teachers on core school subjects and life skills targeting and train 270 PTA members on leadership, school management, child protection, and school monitoring, gender equal representation and resources mobilization.

1 - Needs

The 2019 HNO has stated that almost 1.6 M all-over Sudan’s states are in need of Education services , and that Darfur alone is a host of 930,585 Persons, 286,175 of them in North Darfur state. Kutum locality houses 221,282 capita, the 2019 HNO confirmed that 49,900 are in need of humanitarian assistance, divided as 25,919 IDPs, almost one thousand returnees, and 22,664 vulnerable residents. The Education sector’s target is about 15,917 persons 99% are of school age. displaced people are registered as living in camps. The majority of children in need of emergency. Due to the mass influx of IDPs into host communities there is a strain on education services in these communities. The situation of people living in protracted IDPs camps has recently been strongly affected by influx of newly displaced people adding more pressure on existing services and by a deteriorating food security situation. The school environment is unfavorable condition, the students are overcrowded at the classrooms which not help students to reach education benefits. That situation wouldn't help the teachers either to instruct and communicate properly with students. The primary education offices stated that: girls are noticed to be neglected of catching schools and being educated mostly to be engaged in home work or send them to work to increase household sources, putting them in a position to face harmful situation and lost their chances in education and protection from exploitation, abuse and harassment. Evidenced by a rapid assessment steered by FPDO in November 2018, reflected that schools of (Hamra albeer, kasa (290, , Naina, Kurbe alhamra, Jadeeda baneen, jadeeda banat, Bairi, biraidik, Maryood azrag, Amray, Ango, Anka, Alhilf alsani, Um teen, Gora, Um sidir, Hahsab, Radma, and Um Dilaika) accommodating 7,325 students (3,036 boys, and 4,289 girls) in Rural Kutum educational section; are in favor for rehabilitation of existing class rooms and latrines, construction of new latrines and classrooms and teachers’ offices, training of teachers and PTAs, fencing, provision of seatings, office furniture, teaching and learning materials providing schools with Water tanks (1000 liter capacity), installment of washing facilities, and soap for hands washing, provision of rubbish trash cans, in addition to a gap in construction and rehabilitation of latrines. It’s worth mentioning that among the 18 targeted schools; only 3 permanent latrines are existing the rest are semi fixed and most of them needs rehabilitated and some are not functioning.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The designation of this project activities are devoted to promote gender equity in stage of planning, assessment and implementation stages. Planning incorporates separate focus group discussions with women, men, boys and girls as well as a breakdown of daily activities by gender to ensure access by key groups. FPDO will provide services in a manner which addresses any gender inequalities in the beneficiary population, without exacerbating tensions. In North Darfur Kutum locality, where women are at serious risk if they leave the confines of IDP camps,Missing of education among girls are restrict access to better opportunities and best chances to ensure gender equality which will negatively effect on the community. In these localities, SMOE present 52% of enrolled children at basic school are girls for 2016 -2017 schooling year. FPDO took into account all relevant gender aspects as following: In the assessment stage FPDO collected the information from both men and women regarding their mobility patterns due to their roles and responsibilities. As women and girls are mostly affected by conflict, the project will allow more access to live saving education facilities to these group. Access to local resources and facilities will be improved significantly for men, women, boys and girls in places where FPDO works.Also awareness raising sessions and encouragement to increase female participation in project activities, especially as members of the PTAs. FPDO will work among the community to increase girl’s attendance to schools. Female teachers and girl students will be considered to enjoy WASH facilities in consultation of latrines locations in the schools. Provision of teaching and learning materials, access to WASH facilities and using semi-permanent classrooms rehabilitated would be utilize by girls, women as similar to boys and men. In addition FPDO will encourage women to participate fully in PTAs and all established activities. FPDO will sensitize community through PTAs and awareness sessions on the importance of girl’s education in order to increase the attendance of girls. The siting and layout of water points and sanitation facilities will be subject of particular attention, in addition to encouragement of girl education among the parents, and enhancing of the school environment as a crucial component for their protection. overall, FPDO will apply the Do No Harm approach when implementing different projects components.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

During planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation phases, FPDO will adhere to the principles of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) adopted by Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership guidelines (HAP) and Inter-agency Standard Committees (IASC). FPDO mainstreaming approach will focus on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities including men, women, boy, elderly those living with disabilities by increasing their participation through consultation, sharing the information about the project in a timely, accessible and inclusive manner. Moreover, during the implementation of project activities FPDO will ensure that people receive information relating to the project start and end dates, time and place of training, campaigns and other relevant facts. Affected population will be consulted during the whole project design steps, communication will be applied before and during the assessment stages to facilitate mission to Kutum locality. The beneficiaries will be identified with their needs clearly. As a result of this action affected population will contribute positively to the project design and will continue to participate in the implementation. the project will also promote the community ownership and leadership through their active participation in PTAs, students' hygiene clubs, school cleaning campaigns, and any other activities where community members might be engaged, and their efforts could be contributed. Kutum education offices, Teachers and PTAs are very cooperative and welling to work hand in hand in collaboration with FPDO to implement the activities. The community is very helpful and motivated to work on facilitating access to equal gender and live saving and quality education among affected population. ‎FPDO ‎will ensure consistent two way ‎‎of communication getting the community feed back during focus group discussions (FGD) with the community. Advocacy and sensitization ‎meetings will be ‎conducted at Kutum with stakeholders (including ‎beneficiaries) and ‎community leaders and will further share the information to the communities. complaints mechanisms will be em-placed through the complaints boxes, and assigning a dedicated staff member only to receive issues and concerns reported from community members, considering people's privacy, and protection.
