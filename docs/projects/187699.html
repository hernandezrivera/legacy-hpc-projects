4 - Needs

The crisis in NES is characterized by severe protection, economic livelihoods, and food security concerns (FS). By mid- 2021 an additional segment of the population became food insecure, totalling 12.8 million people meaning 2 of 3 Syrians needs food assistance, with an additional 1.8 million risk falling into food security (HNO 2022). According to MSNA conducted by DCA on Food Security in 2021, vulnerable households spend large proportion of their monthly income on basic food needs. Only 18.4% of households have ‘acceptable’ food consumption scores, and over 60% have poor FCS. Al-Thawrah and Menbij have particularly high proportions of households in the poor FCS category. Female-headed households are more likely than male-headed households to have poor FCS, and IDP households are more likely than residents to have poor FCS. The reduced coping strategy index is high among the respondents, with 27.2 as the average for the full sample. The average rCSI is the highest in Al-Thawrah and the lowest in Kobani. Female headed households have a higher average rCSI. IDP households also have a higher average rCSI than residents. The percentage of households with moderate or severe hunger is the highest in Menbij and the lowest in Al-Raqqa. Female-headed households are more likely to experience moderate or severe hunger than male-headed household. The two main issues with accessing food that recently displaced households face is that the food is too expensive and less money to buy.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will: Provide vulnerable HHs with regular food assistance in the form of Cash for Food grants for 8 rounds. In coordination with the FSL WG, DCA will apply the targeting criteria for FS partners in NES, to which DCA has contributed. This looks at socioeconomic status, family size, family head, family status (IDP, host or returnee) and FS indicators. First, beneficiaries are identified by local committees, community leaders/ Kumins who will support the development of a tentative list of HHs to be assessed to be 100% assessed by DCA teams. As mentioned, HHs will be able to request their HH be assessed; later, DCA will conduct a follow-up assessment of all HHs within target areas. Should vulnerable HHs still within the vulnerability criteria after 8 months of receiving grants, they may qualify for another cycle of assistance. The value of the monthly grants will be agreed with the FSWG and aim to provide 75% coverage of the previous month's SMEB food component and will be calculated per number of HH members. DCA will conduct market monitoring on monthly basis to check the availability of the food items and the price trends etc. to analyse the suitability of cash transfer programming. PDM will be conducted regularly throughout the project. In addition to C4F, DCA will distribute 3 rounds of MPC as an emergency response to HHs recently displaced, to cover 100% of the monthly SMEB.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA staff members or partner staff members. Likelihood: Low.Impact: HighMitigation measures: DCA Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about the provision of available services and who services are for. DCA PSEA focal point in coordination with HR will conduct training and refreshers to project staff and partner staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DCA will establish a complaint box that will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentiality. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by the member of complaints handling team or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. DCA follows principles of Do No Harm across its project activities.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project location impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas and threated the safety and security of DCA project staff and beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOP), and conducts regular security- related training for staffs. Project Management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation, and evacuation plans remain in place.Risk: Local authority- imposed COVID-19 curfews in NES prevent DCA project staff from moving and/ or working, and/ or local authority-imposed curfews in NES/ KRI prevent hawala agents from transferring money for distribution and salaries. Assumptions: Project locations will remain accessible and DCA will continue to have permission to operate (through modified activities to protect beneficiaries and staff) and Hawalas will be able to continue transferring money. Mitigation measures: DCA staff will follow local authority- imposed curfews and restrictions related to COVID-19. DCA will regularly communicate with hawala agents to ensure supply chains are operational. DCA will communicate with other NGOs and NES Forum should selected hawala agent is not able to transfer funds.
