Project Summary

1. Summary of intervention:
Providing multipurpose cash-based transfers to 10,000 people (about 1,666 families) in Fako, Meme, and Manyu divisions in the Southwest and Mezam division in the Northwest. region Each individual will receive 10,000 XAF (USD 16.37) per month for six months coupled with capacity strengthening for the rest of the year.
2. Purpose:
To enable project participants to meet their basic needs including buying preferred food, paying for their children’s education and health needs, and investing in income generating activities to later reduce their dependence on humanitarian assistance.

The cash-based programme will grant dignity and empowerment to the beneficiaries as they will have the freedom to make their own choices on how to spend the money.
This cash assistance will also serve as a safety net in times of emergency, allowing beneficiaries to cover basic needs. WFP will not dictate how the money should be utilized
Noting that the beneficiaries who will receive cash may want to invest part of their income in income generating activities, WFP will implement some resilience and livelihood activities including financial and digital literacy trainings, climate-smart crop production, post-harvest management, small business management, savings for change, etc. The capacity strengthening activities will allow beneficiaries to have the necessary skills to invest part of the cash they will be receiving into livelihood activities.
Targets:
Southwest: A caseload of 6,500 beneficiaries. Fako division 3,000 beneficiaries, Meme division 2,000 beneficiaries, and Manyu division 1,500 beneficiaries.
Northwest: A caseload of 3,500 beneficiaries (Mezam division 3,500 beneficiaries)
The targets exercise will be conducted through a consultative community-based participatory exercise that places beneficiaries, communities and local leadership at the center. This comprehensive community-based targeting approach will help to address inclusion and exclusion errors, while maintaining transparency, community involvement, and accountability throughout the process in identifying and ensuring the assistance reaches the most needy including vulnerable elderly, persons with disability, female-headed households, and displaced populations.
The targeting methodology will therefore be multi-stage process primarily focused on geographical and then household-level indicators.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Since November 2017, the socio-political crisis in the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions has progressively translated into insecurity and armed violence. 1. The escalation of tension and upsurge in hostilities between non-state armed groups (NSAGs) and government defense and security forces have triggered humanitarian needs across the two regions, marked by significant internal displacements. As of March 2023, the NWSW crisis led to the internal displacement of over 628,000 persons in the two regions, with a spillover into neighboring regions such as West, Centre and Littoral, and across the border into Nigeria.2. Humanitarian needs are compounded by structural development challenges (poverty, lack of employment for youth, rising prices for basic needs, weak investment in productive infrastructure and roads), outbreaks of pandemics and epidemics, malnutrition, the effects of the climate crisis, which severely impact households’ ability to access food and to engage in other livelihood activities. The situation is worsened by the Ukraine crisis, which has disrupted global supply chain of key commodities and led to increases in the prices of food, fuel, and farm inputs (particularly fertilizer).3. The humanitarian crises are exacerbating inequalities, increasing the vulnerability of girls and women. Boys and men are also affected by the conflicts, facing specific protection threats, including gender-based violence committed by other men, which remains an extremely sensitive and underreported matter.4. Evidence from the recent food security assessments indicate a worsening food insecurity in Cameroon in general and in South-West in particular. According to projections presented in the March 2023 Cadre Harmonisé, between June and August 2023, an estimated 420,591 people in the region were acutely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 and 4) and in need of humanitarian food assistance.
