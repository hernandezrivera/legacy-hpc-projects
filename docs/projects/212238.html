Project Summary

UNOPS proposes a project to support the Health Sector through the rehabilitation, refurbishment and provision of operational support to health facilities, including hospitals and PHCs. UNOPS will take an integrated sustainable approach, whereby the work will encompass the installation of renewable energy solutions and the repair of roads leading to the chosen facilities (Note 1) where possible, as well as the procurement of medical supplies and devices, including personal protective equipment (PPEs), as per the identified needs (Note: Repair of roads is planned under ERL Sector).
The proposed interventions will help address some of the most significant response gaps identified in the Health Sector in 2023 as part of the Defense of 2024-2025 Sector Strategies and Budgets. The targeted gaps include access and low functionality of health facilities and the lengthy prolonged procurement processes for medical equipment and supplies.
The project will target public health facilities in the locations with the highest needs (severity 3/4/5 level) in three Governorates: Aleppo, Homs and Hama for 2024. The target locations and priority needs have been identified based on the outcomes of UNOPS technical needs assessments in Aleppo, Homs and Hama, conducted in 2023.
UNOPS has extensive experience in the provision of operational capacity support and rehabilitation works to enhance the quality of services and conditions in health facilities in Syria and the region. UNOPS is currently implementing an infrastructure project focusing on rehabilitation works, supply and works of solar photovoltaic (PV) hybrid systems and provision of a medical waste management system to one of the Homs Grand Hospital buildings. In Jordan, Iraq and Yemen, UNOPS has implemented several projects to enhance the operational capacity of the public health system, with a focus on the rehabilitation of health facilities and the procurement of urgently required medical equipment in Jordan and Lebanon.
Procurement is one of UNOPS’ main areas of expertise and a critical component of its mandate. UNOPS activates its emergency procurement procedures (EPPs) for partners in urgent need of supplies, enabling the agency to rapidly respond to critical needs. The organization often incorporates a capacity building component within its procurement activities, whereby personnel are trained in the use of the procured equipment. In support of the COVID-19 response in Syria, UNOPS implemented a project whereby it procured medical equipment for COVID-19 case management in 40 health facilities including hospitals and healthcare facilities across 8 governorates.
UNOPS status as a leading organization in Sustainable Procurement (SP) within the UN, is well known and has been achieved through the progressive adoption of sustainability policies, initiatives, guidance and tools. This led to the release of the Sustainable Procurement Framework on 1st January 2020. The Sustainable Procurement Framework includes guidelines on how to apply sustainability to the procurement cycle, the mandatory requirements to be included during procurement, as well as details around UNOPS supplier sustainability and supplier diversity and inclusion programmes. UNOPS believes that sustainable, resilient and inclusive procurement practices support achieving its key procurement principles of best value for money; fairness, integrity and transparency as well as effective competition; and best interest of UNOPS and its partners.
UNOPS’ proposed plan will contribute to the Health Sector Objective 1: Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need and Objective 3: Strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements and changes in context.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection risks have been identified and mitigation measures have been developed. The main risk would be related to potential exposure to violence and abuse to the beneficiaries, particularly child/female patients and female health workers, when the UN staff and contractors commence the work on-site. The mitigation actions would be to conduct PSEAH awareness training to the project team/contractors, and design the work plan to avoid or reduce contacts with the beneficiary (e.g. patients of health facility units where the works will be taking place are transferred to other facilities). UNOPS will also put grievance mechanisms in place, including hotline, complain box in the project sites, and take remedial actions.In order to mitigate PSEAH risks, UNOPS plans to conduct gender-responsive needs assessments with female and male beneficiaries in order to identify their needs, as well as perceived SEAH risks, and to reflect these in the design and rehabilitation works. During the implementation, UNOPS will take measures to protect the security and safety of the beneficiaries, with particular attention to SEAH risk management through robust PSEAH training programmes to the project team and the selected contractors and strict adherence to UNOPS’ PSEAH safeguard. Given the focus of the project on marginalized populations including women, children and PWD, it is crucial to include PSEAH measures in the project. According to the Project Risk Analysis, the identified risks are as follows: 1) Fraudulent activities occur causing UNOPS operations to be suspended. 2) Delays in design works (if original drawings of the building structure are not available/provided to UNOPS). 3) Sanctions and lengthy approval procedures. 4) Lack of awareness of contractors on health and safety measures at the project site, which may trigger incidents on site. 5) Deteriorating security situation and kinetic environment that experiences frequent incidents which might hinder access to project sites. UNOPS takes mitigation actions against these risks, which are 1) Implementing a robust internal control framework with oversight mechanisms to mitigate the risk of fraudulent activities. Regular monitoring, quality assurance activities, reporting systems in place. Vendor due diligence and performance monitoring in place. 2) Coordination with the relevant authorities for their continuous support to provide the required technical information. 3) Proactive local assessment and availability for materials during the design phase and getting the required approvals ahead of time to avoid delays. 4) Mandatory HSSE training and inclusion of HSSE requirements in the tendering documents, and the selected contractors will be guided by the UNOPS team. 5) Close monitoring of the situation and coordination with UNDSS, and operational readiness and positioning staff to support alternate activities, in accordance with Business Continuity Plans.
