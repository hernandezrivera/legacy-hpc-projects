Project Summary

Under the leadership of the Government of Bangladesh and in close coordination with the RRRC and DC, the ISCG Secretariat will continue to support the overall humanitarian coordination structure and lead streamlining and rationalization efforts to ensure the coherence and cohesiveness of the response.
The ISCG Secretariat will enhance the work of the Sectors and provide clear and strategic linkages between the Inter-Sector Meeting, Refugee Operations and Coordination Team (ROCT), and the Strategic Executive Group (SEG), under the leadership of the SEG Co-Chairs in Dhaka. Sector Coordinators, staffed by the Sector Lead Agency, will play a neutral coordination role.
The ISCG Secretariat will manage the joint response planning cycle, covering activities from assessments and strategic planning to supporting resource mobilization, monitoring, and effective reporting. Supported assessments include the Inter-Sector Needs Assessment (ISNA), WFP-led Refugee Vulnerability Assessment (REVA), WHO-led assessments such as the Public Health Needs Assessment (PHNA), the Inter-Sector assessment (IS), and assessments for pharmaceuticals, emergency preparedness (EP), and outbreak readiness. Additionally, gender-sensitive analyses like the GiHA analyses, GAM review, and Gender Assessment, as well as the FAO-led Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis (RIMA), are integral to understanding the needs and vulnerabilities across sectors. These assessments are coordinated with the Office of the RRRC to ensure a comprehensive and cohesive approach.
The Secretariat will also deliver key information management services through a running IMS, as well as external relations and communication services. ISCG is actively engaging with development partners to promote complementarity between development efforts in the District and the humanitarian response.
The coordination system will also mainstream cross-cutting issues across all Sectors and humanitarian partners. This includes ensuring the Centrality of Protection as outlined in the Protection Framework. Protection mainstreaming involves designing and implementing programmes that consider protection risks and potential violations while maximizing the protective impact of aid programming.
Through the mainstreaming of Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) principles, the humanitarian community seeks to ensure equal access to rights for all individuals in affected communities. Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commits humanitarian partners to consider, account for, and be held accountable by Rohingya refugees and host communities.
Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and management, as well as Energy & Environment, are integrated across the response to support emergency preparedness, energy solutions, natural resource management, and environmental protection. Efforts are also underway to ensure Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), which is a form of gender-based violence constituting an abuse of power by humanitarian aid workers. All JRP partners, including UN and NGOs, are required to be members of the PSEA Network and adhere to its standards, including compliance with the PSEA Standard Operating Procedures in line with global guidelines.
