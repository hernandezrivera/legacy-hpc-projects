Project Summary

The Gaza Strip after 13 years of strict blockade is witnessing a deterioration of the socio-economic and mental health status of its population aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic and the related restrictions that have further isolated the area and decreased economic opportunities. The most impacted families include already vulnerable population groups such as economically vulnerable households on waiting list for the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), especially women headed households with chronic disease and disabilities and small and micro business owners (young women and young men) with limited benefit. Movements restrictions and reduction and loss of economic activities within the pandemic context led to a significant decrease in the psychological well-being of households in the Gaza Strip. The stress of losing income, combined with uncertainty of the situation, forced proximity within the household, children out of school and other factors conduced a rise of increase of violence against women, 1 out of 3 women reported abuse by intimate partner (UNWOMEN, 2020). To respond to this situation and cover the most pressing needs, Action Against Hunger in partnership with Women Affairs Center (WAC) and Culture Free Thoughts Association (CFTA) will support 2,475 individuals:
1. Support a total of 450 families within the two most impacted population groups described above with unconditional cash transfers to cover their basic needs;
2. Provide tailored case management and assistance plan to individuals in need of protection related services. Services will be provided for identified gender-based violence (GBV) survivors and specific attention to the needs of people with disabilities, referrals to community-based safe spaces and legal services will be ensured. Female headed households and households with members with chronic diseases and disabilities will be prioritized through the intervention with a comprehensive protection assistance based on needs identified to improve their psychological wellbeing. Financial wellbeing will be increased to ultimately improve food security through unconditional cash assistance, while psychological wellbeing will be improved and protection risks decreased through personalized case management, including emergency cash for protection for severe protection cases identified. This intervention will answer the second specific
objective of the Food Security Sector, namely supporting households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities to meet their basic food needs. It will also address the second objective of the Protection cluster namely: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence. The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment within all planned activities. Ultimately, the project aims to increase beneficiaries’ coverage of basic needs and GBV and protection cases management, for households particularly exposed to a wide range of protection issues.

1 - Needs

The COVID-19 pandemic declared in March 2020 in the occupied Palestinian territory added an additional layer of vulnerability in the Gaza Strip, already crippled by a protracted crisis resulting from the Israeli blockade of 13 years, previous wars, a quasi-stalling economy and an internal political division between the Palestinian Authority and the de-facto authority. As of October 14th 2020, 4,285 COVID-19 cases were recorded in the Gaza Strip both in quarantine centers and within the community (October 2020, OCHA) considering that people with pre-existing conditions and elderly are more exposed to risks of covid-10 (WHO, 2020). The governorates of Gaza City and Northern Gaza remained the epicenter of active cases, while movement restrictions established late August 2020 are being slowly lifted throughout October. In 2020, the unemployment rates among youth and young graduates (20-29 years old) in the Gaza Strip increased up to 69 percent (HNO, 2021). In addition to the 49.1% of unemployment rate recorded in the second quarter of 2020 in Gaza (PCBS, 2020). Employment among women decreased of 30% from the previous quarter, with an alarming rate of unemployment among young women reaching 92% compared to young men (63.2%). (Gisha, 2020). As of April 2020, the Ministry of Labor in Gaza recorded 158,611 individuals identifying as daily wage laborers affected by the pandemic (18.48 percent of them women) (Mezan. 2020). The main sectors affected include transports, education, tourism, industry, and commercial sectors (PNGO, 2020). About 62% of the population in the Gaza Strip is considered food insecure (FAO 2020). The main cause of food insecurity in the Gaza Strip is essentially the lack of access to food due to limited financial resources by chronically and newly vulnerable households. According to the 2021 Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO), it is anticipated that the percentage of people in need due to food insecurity will increase by 18% between 2020 and 2021. About 54% of female headed households are food insecure (WFP 2020) and more than two-thirds of female headed households reported high risks of food shortage, nearly ten percentage points above male-headed households (HNO, 2021). According to a UN Women flash survey (2020) of the crisis impact on women-owned businesses, 95% of women owners of micro, small and medium enterprises said their work had already been impacted by COVID-19. Moreover, almost 25% of females in the private sector work with no employment contract or pay protection. This reinforces the financial vulnerability that women headed households face and how recent pandemic measures have increased this vulnerability and contributed to lower financial access to basic needs. In addition to decreasing economic power, associated protection issues have increased within the Gaza Strip with 60.6% people in need for protection services in Gaza, including 79.3% at severe levels. Between 2020 and 2021, it is expected that people in need for protection services will increase of 15 % in the Gaza Strip, including 198,000 children and 274.000 adults (HNO, 2021). Measures of physical distancing, restricting the improvement of the socio-economic situation led to the deterioration of mental health status, increase of stress anxiety and depression, lower educational outcomes, increase in child labour and domestic violence as chronic and distressing issues among vulnerable households (HNO, 2021). A UN women study (2020) found that the lockdown and closures of schools has increased the burden of unpaid care work on women and girls. This is particularly stressful for women who were requested to work from home while also expected to take care of the children and housework. According to a survey by AWRAD, whose findings informed the gender analysis, women are more concerned than men about the increasing household burdens (62 per cent and 46 per cent respectively).An aggravating concern is the se of suicide attempts in 2020, with 44 recorded including children, compared to 8 in 2019 (HNO, 2021). Women as primary caregivers have been considerably affected by the increased mental load within households that recently lost their source of income. A report from UNWOMEN (2020) reports that women show higher stress levels (82%) than men (71%), while rise of GBV-related cases is worrying. A recent assessment conducted by IRPAL (October 2020) revealed that 81.5% of the respondents reported suffering from anxiety, tension, and stress since the Corona pandemic. This impact on the population’s wellbeing is further aggravated by the stress associated to loss of income and has led to increased domestic violence, GBV and child protection concerns (Care - OCHA, September 2020). Based on a previous AAH’s intervention supporting GMR affected households with cash assistance and psychosocial counselling, show that out of 400 individuals at least 80 needed specific mental health support, with many households observing increased violence towards women and children, along with suicidal thoughts for people suffering from the injury (data collected in March 2020). Based on a recent assessment conducted by CFTA, women and girls with disabilities indicated they lacked access to assistive devices, hygiene and dignity materials. Structured psychosocial support is identified as a major need, requested by all interviewed women and girls who also stressed the need for more engagement in the design and implementation of the activities. Women and men among respondents had little information about access to information regarding COVID and GBV services. In consideration of the elderly, it’s important to note that by mid-2020 about 5% of the population in Palestine was above 60 years old. Among them, also 22% have been suffering from medical negligence from other household members (24% of older females and 19% of older males) (PCBS, 2020). This reality further aggravates the lack of care to be received in a pandemic.

2 - Activities

1) Households selection: households to receive assistance will be among three profiles:i) 350 households who suffer from chronic poverty and food insecurity currently on the MoSD waiting list for the National Cash Transfer Program (NCTP) and suffering from lack of employment; ii) 100 households with an income generation activity stopped due to the COVID-19 restriction measures and at risk of sinking deeper into poverty without assistance. AAH is responsible for the household’s selection and will identify the 1st target group in coordination with the MoSD, crosschecking them with Ministry of Labor (MoL) for daily worker status. Households from the first target group will be selected and prioritized based on eligibility criteria (female headed household, chronic disease and disability, food security level, daily worker status), using criteria from the unified vulnerability assessment tool (UVAT) established by the Cash Working Group (CWG). The 2nd target group will be selected from AAH caseload from previous income-generation interventions but not exclusively. Female headed HHs will be prioritized, and in addition to the UVAT criteria, the status of the IGA will be part of the eligibility criteria: closure due to covid-19, IGA is the main source of income to household, IGA’s net profit before closure, number of family and external employees. 2) Unconditional cash support for basic food and protection needs: 6 months of unconditional cash assistance will be transferred to selected households to provide financial access to cover their basic needs. The transfer value of 255 USD corresponds to 80% of the MEB expenses calculated for food, education, water, communication and household items for cooking. The MEB review for the Gaza Strip was harmonized through the CWG and calculated for a household of an average of 6 members. Assistance will be received through Palpay ATM cards through Bank of Palestine ATM locations and direct purchase in accredited stores. Households from the 2nd target group with IGAs will also be supported with business coaching sessions as a support to prioritize spending between household needs and business needs and establish a financial recovery plan without adding further debts. Distribution of cards and coaching sessions will apply preventive measures towards the COVID-19, including facilitating online coaching sessions if feasible with participants. 3) Protection and GBV case management: in complementarity to the cash assistance for basic needs, selected households will benefit from protection services provided by WAC in North, Gaza and Middle area governorates and by CFTA in Khan Yunis and Rafah governorates. Both partners will conduct a diagnostic assessment of beneficiary households of unconditional cash assistance for basic needs with case managers to identify protection concerns and tailor activities according to needs observed and expressed by household members, followed by awareness sessions. Collection of situational protection information on risks and benefits of households’ individuals with an age and gender perspective will be key to identify tailored case management and modality. Assessment will be conducted through phone or through house visit if the context allows for an improved assessment. Mental health and psychosocial support will be provided along with services for identified GBV survivors, with specific attention to the needs of people with disabilities and referral to community-based safe spaces and legal services. Case management will be provided remotely when adequate (ensuring during the assessment available means and resources of the individual and household for remote services) or in person if the context allows for movements and in-person consultations. Emergency cash for protection concerns will be available for 120 severe cases. The cash for protection assistance will directly target the individual under case management and delivered according to the needed service to avoid any related risk to cash transfer within the household potentially causing further violence or protection concerns. Partners will also support 4 groups of women to lead women led initiatives that will support women in advocating for their GBV needs within the community and/or contribute to community social needs. This participatory activity will support women identifying the main priorities they wish to address within the needs of their community. Access to information about protection services through permanent hotlines and social media will be shared and accessible to participants during the intervention and beyond. Activities in person will apply COVID-19 preventive measures and childcare arrangements will be organized on need basis, taking in consideration needs of children with disabilities with trained staff, accessible and safe locations.

3 - Indicators

AAH will develop a comprehensive Monitoring and Evaluation Framework for the action collecting disaggregated data by sex, age and disability. AAH MEAL will supervise partner staff responsible for project monitoring to ensure the monitoring system relating to impartial beneficiary selection and effective implementation. Joint MEAL planning through the development of project tracking tool will be conducted through participatory approach including CFTA and WAC as local partners. In addition, AAH technical & MEAL teams will conduct regular meetings with partners to follow up the progress and ensure activities are implemented according to the project work plan developed in close collaboration with the local partners. AAH MEAL team will be responsible to collect data and monitor the desired outcomes and impact of the activities on households’ satisfaction, relevance and use of the cash assistance for basic needs, protection services and women led initiatives. The AAH robust monitoring and evaluation system, and beneficiary feedback system will ensure that beneficiary selection is conducted in a transparent and accountable manner, beneficiary selection prioritizing vulnerable families. The main MEAL activities will be a baseline and endline assessments of all households on both food security and protection dimensions, 2 Post Distribution Monitoring surveys for cash assistance to assess quality, safety and accessibility to basic food needs taking into account protection factors like safety and dignity. The 2nd PDM will be conducted after the last cash instalment to compare results between 1st one and 2nd one and assess improvement in families’ food consumption as well as reduce negative coping mechanisms like debts, additionally assess impact of cash assistance on minimizing protection threats on women and vulnerable household members. One additional PDM will be conducted towards the end of the action specifically for the cash for protection component to assess the relevance, adaptiveness, timeliness and safety of the assistance. 10 Most significant change stories will be collected for 10 women, elderly and youth cases to capture the story of change through being engaged in multi sectorial response and highlight significant protection/wellbeing changes on the lives of the beneficiaries. Onsite or remote monitoring (according to movement restrictions and current context) will be implemented by the MEAL team during the action to obtain beneficiaries’ feedback and collect observations on quality of services provided and BNFs satisfaction as well as draw learnings for activities. The following indicators will measure the intervention: Food security dimensions will be measured through the following indicators: # of individuals benefiting from unconditional cash Support (target: 2,475 – 542 men, 691 women, 587 boys, 655 girls); through the PDM surveys the satisfaction of assistance received will be also measured. At the end of the intervention the food security levels of households will be measured through the use of coping strategies and the proxy indicator FCS: % of households with decreased value of Coping Strategy Index (CSI) (target: 60%) % of households with increased Food Consumption Score (FCS) (target: 60%). To highlight results collected through the CSI indicator, the % of households that have changed intra-household food distribution (target: 60%) will be measured as well to identify any changes in food distribution patterns inside household as a coping strategy and potential protection concerns. Protection dimension will be measured through the following indicators: # of vulnerable men and women affected by conflict related violence and violence in households benefiting from structured psychosocial services (total target: 450 individuals – 200 men , 250 women), # of GBV survivors (women, men, girls and boys) received MHPSS services (total target: 180 - 5 men, 155 women, 5 boys, 15 girls), # of GBV survivors (women, girls, men and boys) receiving cash/voucher assistance (target: 120 – 10 men, 110 women), # of children affected by conflict related violence benefiting from specialized individual case management (target: 125 – 62 boys, 63 girls). Out of all targeted individuals, women, men, girls and boys, 10% of each group are expected to be individuals with disabilities. In order to evaluate the satisfaction of the beneficiaries of the interventions regarding their needs and wellbeing, the following indicators will be used: % of individuals satisfied by MHPSS activities received (target: 70%) % of women reporting feeling safer having access to new services (target: 60%). All indicators will be disaggregated by sex, age and disability.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles of Do No Harm. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance especially in a project that combines cash assistance and case management activities. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff have the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, a majority of AAH staff have received protection cluster facilitated trainings with the aim to know how to detect and refer potential cases of GBV and other protection concerns. As part of AAH accountability to affected populations, a feedback mechanism emphasizing fluid and transparent communication will be in place. More specifically for the project activities, AAH will lead a risk analysis while confirming the mechanism used to transfer cash to ensure safety and do no harm for targeted beneficiaries while commuting to the distribution place and while at it. AAH will also ensure confidentiality of beneficiaries to avoid stigma and possible pressure on the household if the surrounding is aware of a transfer. In its monitoring, AAH will ask about households’ dynamics, and pay specially attention to any tension arising after or because of the transfers to monitor do no harm. Questions about safety and dignity will also be tackled during the PDM and end line surveys. Change to the implementation modality will be made accordingly within available possibilities and current movement restrictions, while applying preventive measures against covid-19. The two partners WAC, CFTA, have core principles to ensure safety and do no harm considering the sensitivity of the work and activities they implement. Experienced case workers will ensure households members are at ease and services at tailored to their specific situation and the risks they might be exposed to. Women field staff and social workers will be communicating with female beneficiaries to ensure a safe and trustful exchange for the beneficiary. Strict confidentiality of all personal information will be ensured to protect beneficiaries through the data protection form explained to each beneficiary ensure safe handling of personal data. In this particular operational environment and in order to reducing the risk of transmission of COVID-19, AAH has developed strict SOPs for field and office work. COVID-19 protective measures and equipment will be applied throughout all interventions to further prevent harm being done to beneficiaries, communities and staff members.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH upholds the humanitarian principles of Humanity, Impartiality, Neutrality, and Independence. During the course of the project, AAH will ensure while confirming the cash transfer mechanisms that it is easily accessible and without risk to all the beneficiaries of the project including female headed household and people with disability who will be over represented in the targeted population. Alternative means of delivery (e.g. home distribution) could be set up in specific cases to ensure equal access to aid. . For the Cash for Basic Needs component, the assessment phase will be key to identify potentially vulnerable cases who will be supported during distributions. Delivery modalities will be adapted to avoid exposing beneficiaries to additional risks by collecting beneficiaries’ preferences and strengthening distribution procedures. For the Cash for Protection component, “cash in hand” modalities will be employed, with the assistance being handed over by the Case Manager who has been following the case. In order to further mitigate exposing vulnerable individuals to unnecessary risks, GBV survivors receiving Emergency cash instead of direct service delivery (120 individuals) will be selected on the basis of the beneficiaries’ informed consent to be served through this modality.The inclusion of a Protection component into the project is crucial to make identification& referral and Protection mainstreaming principle operational across all activities of the intervention. Information will be delivered on the cash assistance, its duration, . Tthe modality as well as on AAH feedback and complaints mechanism to ensure equal access to information and equal access to AAH for questions and complaints. Case management by CFTA and WAC will be lead with the full consent of the beneficiary household and for equally for all. The tailored assistance plan will be developed and explain to the household to ensure relevance and confirm consent. The option of delivering remote or in person protection services will be carefully evaluated according to the resources of each households to make sure that all participants will receive the intended aid.
