Project Summary

The project aims to distribute 100 Food Baskets for "food insecure people" displaced to Al-Bab sub-district (monthly distribution for 12 months), Priority will be given for households with specific vulnerability criteria (e.g. HH headed by women, People with special needs)
The Selection process will be coordinated with FSL Cluster and Al-Bab Local Council and will start in January 2023, Food baskets will be standardized according to FSL Cluster technical recommendations, it contains (Cereals without flour, Pulses/Protein, Fat, and Condiments)
• Sufficient for 6 persons HHs
• Provides 1550 Kcal (daily for each person)
• Weight 70 Kg (approximately)
• Estimated at 83.30 USD (approximately)
Al Bab sub-district was targeted by our project based on the HNO 2023 / FSA Chapter general assessment indicating that People in Need / PiN are (127,207 Acute / 10,309 at Risk) which means that severity category is extreme (4), additional primary needs Assessment was carried out for 100 Households in October 2022, which reached many neighborhoods inside Al-Bab city.
The targeted beneficiaries according to the needs assessment are:
- 94 IDPs / 6 HC (Total 100 families)
- 25 HHs headed by female / 58 HHs with PSN / 17 HHs under poverty line
- 98 Men / 106 Women / 146 Boys / 141 Girls (Total 513)
25% of the project fund is secured so far, 75% of the fund is planned (expected) to be secured in March 2023 (during Ramadan 1444 H)

3 - Needs

The project activities will address the following assessed needs: 1- Targeted households food consumption score 2- Targeted households coping strategy 3- Targeted households expenditure on food primary needs the assessment was carried out by the SOH MEAL team in October 2022, Seeds of Hope approved the following 3 segments based on comprehensive needs assessment in Al-Bab City: The targeted beneficiaries according to needs assessment are: - 94 IDPs / 6 HC (Total 100 families) - 25 HHs headed by female / 58 HHs with PSN / 17 HHs under poverty line - 98 Men / 106 Women / 146 Boys / 141 Girls (Total 513)

4 - Implementation of activities

SO 1.2 Provide the assessed food-insecure people with monthly food assistance through appropriate modalities Seeds of Hope will utilize its logistics capabilities in Al-Bab city (warehouse, transportation, work permit ...etc.), including a group of volunteers who will contribute to project implementation. Quality Control for Food Items Seeds of Hope will buy the Food Items from trusted suppliers (who are capable of providing attested health certificates), in addition, samples of the Food items purchased will be taken to a third party for analysis, to ensure no toxic material or any other harmful substances do exist in the shipment delivered to support food security of beneficiaries. Key Activities: We follow standard operating procedures in implementing highly standardized projects (such as Food Baskets distribution), the project starts by mobilization activities including the acquirement of logistics (facilities and vehicles) and human resources (project staff recruitment), and will be followed by final assessment for the most needy neighborhoods within Al Bab city in coordination with the Local Council (beneficiaries selection by name) and obtaining the work permit, thereafter the purchasing processes of the food items will start according to our policies and procedures and the food items will be delivered to our warehouses for packing, after agreement is being signed with the LC our project team will distribute every month one Food basket for each HHs selected previously.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat 1: The theft of specific food items (e.g. sugar) from the basket during temporary storage, or delivery to distribution sites, Likelihood: medium, Impact: high, Mitigating Measure: follow strict selection criteria for staff and services providers, enhancing monitoring and control over warehouses and transportation trips, Random check for food baskets contents just before handing over to beneficiaries, and inform beneficiaries about the existing feedback and accountability mechanisms to report any misconduct. Resources: Dedicated staff member and good storage management. Protection Risk/Threat 2: Reselling of food basket by beneficiary to third entity may expose them to risks (e.g. unbalanced bargain, harassment by buyers or drivers) Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium, Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the reasoning behind the sale/exchange of assistance and solicit their feedback with regards to solutions,


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA Risk/Threat 2: the attempt of sharing telephone number (by staff member) with female beneficiaries during registration or distribution, Likelihood: low, Impact: high, Mitigating Measures: provide training for SOH field staff, and avoid 1 to 1 connection with beneficiaries (SOH front-end team will consist of 1 male and 1 female at least), and ensure they commit to humanitarian principles and code of conduct signed after recruitment, and distribute brochures with Food baskets including hot-line numbers for reporting PSEA incidents PSEA Risk/Threat 1: beneficiaries sexually harassed or abused in exchange for Food baskets, Likelihood: low, Impact: high, Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the safest mechanism for handing over the food baskets, Monitoring: Enhance communication: (i) distribute brochures with Food baskets including hot-line numbers for reporting PSEA incidents (ii) avoid 1 to 1 connection with beneficiaries (SOH front-end team will consist of 1 male and 1 female at least), and (iii) provide training for SOH field staff, and ensure they commit to humanitarian principles and code of conduct signed after recruitment.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project took into consideration the following risks (beyond the project's control): 1- Mass displacement from Idlib to Aleppo based on conflict escalation (SOH contributes actively with local stakeholders during crises and has plan B to shift operation to Turkish/Syrian borderline). 2- Markets may be un-accessible because of insecurity (modalities will be changed if necessary according to local security conditions/challenges). 3- Some humanitarian actors may conduct uncoordinated distribution for households targeted by our project (coordination with the local council and FSL cluster will take place before implementation). 4- unability of the beneficearies to reach/access distribution sites (alternative modalities will be followed accordingly)
