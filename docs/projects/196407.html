Project Summary

The IDP camps in Northeast Syria are in a dire situation, especially in terms of education. Most camps don't provide formal schools, and due to their geographical placement, there are no schools available within a walking distance for the children inside the camp to attend. According to HNO 2022, 36% of the displaced school-aged children are less likely to attend any learning form compared to children outside the camp.
In Raqqa, Mahmudli camp is home to over 8800 IDPs, among them, 39% of the resident are school-aged children. The absence of formal schools and non-formal learning centres has left many children without proper education, they are either dropping out or not registered at schools at all, which further exposes them to protection risks, such as early marriage and child labour.
AFH is aiming to provide a school for children to create a positive, stimulating learning environment, empowering the students to participate in building their society with awareness and positive behaviour by providing them with a high-quality education.
Our project in Mahmudli camp is aiming to provide education and protection for 900 students (450 M- 450 F) more than 10% of which are PWD. Given the fact that most of these children did not get a chance to have an education due to the displacement of their families. AFH will advocate for the students and help them to develop an appreciation of responsibility to make the students aware of their rights and duties to achieve comprehensive development and provide them with practical human concepts in life of this age. The program will be in Mahmodli camp, Jurnneyah, Ath-Thawrah district in Raqqa, for nine (9) months divided into two terms with a preparation month.
Execution method: 1)The school contains 13 classes into two shifts (26 class tents) and the period of education session hours are from eight AM until 2 PM. Each shift contains 3 sessions of 45 duration time and a break of 30 minutes between the shifts and within each shift a mid-break for 15 minutes.
2) Maintenance works to maintain school tents and prepare them for winter and summer conditions.
3)Beneficiaries: 900 (450 males and 450 females) IDPs aged from 6 to 18 years old from first to sixth grade, more than 10% are PWD who will receive special treatment and wash facilities.
4)The executors will be the camp's teachers, social workers and case managers for the protection activities who are already trained by AFH experts on the methodology of education and child protection and GBV mitigation alongside the provision of a separate room for protection activities.
5) The Curriculum will be SLP and the materials include (Arabic- Mathematics-Science-English)in addition to PSS activities-awareness activities and Child protection and GBV case management provided by trained case managers, AFH is part of the child protection working subgroup and will join the GBV working subgroup as well when the program starts.
The project will focus on:
1- Providing children with non-formal education, teachers with monetary incentives and capacity-building training to ensure their readiness for giving lessons, providing children textbooks and stationery, personnel health awareness, special referrals to services, child protection, recreational activities, and In-kind materials to PWD.
2- FGD with parents.
3-Preparing a MEAL plan that will govern the whole activities implementation through field visits and weekly, monthly and 4Ws reports and ensure that IPTT is followed according to the plan and summing all results in quarterly and final reports to measure and ensure progress and success.
4-Sustainability will be the main target for all the projects and it will be ensured through monitoring and evaluation methods and ensuring accountability for all.
5-Participating in all EWG activities and sharing statistics, needs and suggestions.

3 - Needs

The situation in Mahmudli camp is emerging from conflict and there are not enough education sectors covering the age period of 6-12 years old where the camp administration and parents of students requested AFH to keep on supporting their children. IDP children who were living in camps were significantly more likely to be out of school than IDP children living outside of camps, 36% of IDPs in camps aren’t attending school according to 2022 HNO. This is most likely due to the camps having insufficient or no learning spaces for children to attend. 7% of children 5-17 years were reported having a health condition, or disability, this group was composed of more boys than girls (59% boys). They likely need specific interventions to enable them to access school. These interventions would be provided through specially trained teachers and school personnel, physical infrastructure and learning materials and teaching aids. Establishing an education centre (school) that attracts age groups between 6-12 who have not enrolled in schools and especially PWD with the aim of including them in the coming periods. It provides the opportunity for students to experience positive interactions with each other inside and outside the classroom for children who have not received an education appropriate to their age group, with a focus on children residing in Mahmudli camp and those with special needs and humanitarian cases.Ref,(Humanitarian Needs Assessment 2022- Syria Republic)

Developing and conducting awareness-raising activities for children, youth, caregivers, and key members of the community on child protection, education, available services, and prevention of family separation, in addition to PSEA and child safeguarding through centres and mobile teams.4- The monitoring plan, AFH will provide a weekly, and monthly quality checklist, quarterly progress and 4Ws report. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The main protection risks that might face the implementation of the project are as follows: 1- Child abuse, such as physical punishment, acting violently or mistreatment by teachers or educational personnel. In this regard, AFH will make sure to train all teachers and educational staff on safeguarding and child protection. AFH will make sure that all teachers and project staff will sign the Organization's Code of Conduct. 2- Acts of sexual harassment conducted by boys on young girls in latrines or unsafe places. AFH will ensure to rehabilitate of the sanitation facilities in a way that provides privacy and gender segregation to protect girls from any SG or harassment risk.3- Services not matching the needs of people with disabilities and the difficulty of people with disabilities going to distant schools, as well as the lack of health facilities (bathrooms, wheelchairs...). in this regard, AFH will make sure that all the facilities are PWD-friendly including classrooms and sanitation facilities.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The main risks that might affect the execution of the project are the following: 1- Parents' resistance to children's education and especially girls as a result of negative coping mechanisms such as child labour for economic reasons or forced and early marriage as a cultural and traditional reason. In this regard, the AFH team will conduct several awareness-raising sessions for caregivers about the importance of education to their children. Furthermore, the team will engage the community leaders and the elderly in advocating for children's access to education.2- Lack of qualified teachers in the area. This risk can be mitigated throughout the capacity building program that would be integrated into the project so that recruited teachers will undergo a package of training on PSEA, SEA and GBV cases. The teachers will also receive several training in active learning, child protection, methods of dealing with children, case management, as well as protection assessment.3- Possible theft of schools' assets and equipment. In order to avoid this risk, AFH will hire two security guards to ensure the protection of the school. Furthermore, the team will engage the community leaders and the well-respected individuals in the camp to increase their sense of ownership.4- Health risks such as cholera, and Covid-19 are mitigated through daily health awareness sessions, and focusing on spatial distancing and providing personal protective equipment such as sterilizers.
