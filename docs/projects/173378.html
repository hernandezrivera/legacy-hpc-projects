Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring access to quality GBV response services for survivors and strengthening GBV prevention and risk mitigation interventions in Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. BRAC will seek to enhance safety and dignity of survivors (do no harm), through its sector and partners according GBV Sub-Sector guidance.
The project targets a total of 37,895 individuals, of which 27,992 female, 9,870 male, 12,148 children and 458 disabled in the host and refugee community. BRAC will provide SGBV response and prevention services in 4 camps (Camps 8E, 15, 24 and 27) , Baharchara unions in Teknaf Upazilla and Palongkhali, Ratnapalong and Rajapalong unions in Ukhiya Upazilla.
The interventions will increase meaningful participation and community engagement through AGD and community-based approaches. Prevention of GBV incidents will be strengthened through sensitization and awareness-raising on GBV (including SEA) and life-saving information on available services. BRAC will also educate adolescent girls to claim and negotiate SRHR rights. Vocational skills training and livelihood opportunities for adolescent girls who are victims of early marriage, women, particularly women-headed households and GBV survivors. Case management and PSS services will be provided to SGBV survivors. BRAC will continue to mainstream SGBV interventions in emergency preparedness and response plans, through specialized capacity building for refugees, contributory stocks of 1000 dignity kits. 420 community activists will participate in SGBV prevention activities such as awareness raising campaigns. Safe identification and referral of survivors with disabilities, case management and PSS services for survivors with disabilities will be provided. BRAC will conduct daily monitoring on GBV in Rohingya and host communities in line with Accountability of the Affected People (AAP) and generate periodical reports. BRAC will engage relevant stakeholders, and organize evidence-based advocacy and capacity building sessions in PSEA and AGD for service providers in other sectors and relevant local government on protection mainstreaming.

1 - Identified Needs

According to the latest GBV Information Management System (GBV IMS) reports (September ,2020), GBV types reported include: rape, physical assault, sexual violence, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. Factors such as congested space, lack of privacy, inadequate lighting, and limited access to essential services for women and girls, are among the factors that increase the overall risks for their protection and are part of the conditions sustaining unequal power dynamics, which contribute to the high rates of IPV in the camps. The COVID-19 outbreak in the refugee camps in 2020, as predicted in the ISCG Gender analysis (May 2020), has disproportionately affected women and girls by limiting their mobility, decision-making and access to lifesaving services. Confinement, a rise in tensions, restrictions on services, and more limited access for humanitarian workers as a result of the government-issued lockdown has also increased levels of GBV, child abuse and neglect and sexual exploitation and abuse. This has set back the achievements of protection-centred interventions implemented in the target area for the last three years. According to the GBVIMS report (June 2020) for Cox’s Bazar, GBV has increased from 63% (March 2020) to 75% (April 2020). The majority of the GBV incidents are happening at survivors’ or perpetrators’ residences (96% of total). According to the GBVIMS data (GBVIMS report of September 2020), Intimate partner violence constitutes 84% of total reported SGBV cases. IPV has been identified as the overwhelmingly dominant form of sexual and gender-based violence reported in Cox’s Bazar. The October 2020 ISCG Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment found that households without men are more at risk. FGD mentioned women to be generally more vulnerable due to financial dependency on husbands and in addition less likely to receive justice as a result of corruption. In addition, single female-headed households are not able to meet their basic needs due to a lack of income compounded by interruptions in cash relief and lack of livelihoods support for people with disability. According to the community consultations in October 2020 conducted by BRAC, inadequate services for Rohingya men and boys’ survivors of GBV have also been identified a crucial gap in the response. Likewise, GBV service gaps have been identified for groups with other diversity of need including persons with disabilities and transgender groups. The inter-agency gender analysis (UNHCR, CARE and Action Aid, August 2020) confirm that men in both refugee and host communities remain the ultimate decision-makers. Furthermore, women have limited opportunities in the host community to participate in their community governance structures and influence decision-making processes. According to the report, Rohingya girls and young women are particularly vulnerable to child marriage, sexual abuse and trafficking. Women and girls, particularly those living with disabilities, and Hijras, are vulnerable to physical, psychological and sexual harassment and abuse. Women and girls in polygamous marriage situations are particularly at risk of experiencing IPV. In order to effectively prevent GBV, address gender stereotypes and support survivors' access to quality services in the Rohingya and host communities, there is a need to promote community-based approaches.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Establishment of 5 new SSWGs and efficient and effective use of 3 existing SSWGs for services related to GBV. These spaces will provide an opportunity for access to information, education, and recreational activities . In addition, case management, individual or group counseling, psychosocial support, safety planning and risk reduction, skills building, distribution of dignity kits will be provided through the SSWGs.2. The skill development and empowerment of women and girls will become more organized and structured. As role models, participants who have graduated from skill building activities will be engaged and enabled with coaching and capacity building in facilitation, basic counting , communication and management skills.3. In an integrated approach, meaningful engagement with men and boys includes working with individuals, family and communities to promote gender equality and equity and to transform gender norms.4. Provide life skills training through Girl Shine Program Model to improve their learning and development and adolescent sexual and reproductive health knowledge, essential to making healthy decisions.5. Prevention of SGBV incidents will be strengthened through sensitization and awareness-raising on SGBV (including sexual exploitation and abuse) and life-saving information on available services, COVID 19, DRR, PSEA. GBV risk mitigation measures will be integrated and mainstreamed across sectors and different areas of BRAC’s interventions. 6. BRAC will provide quality case management and PSS services for survivors including additional support aimed at addressing survivor needs7. BRAC will use Capability Assessment Tools to evaluate GBV and non-GBV actors' technical knowledge and skills. PSEA, GBV Core Principles, SGBV Case Management and Gender are included in capacity-development curriculum.8. Implementing GBVIMS Primero , integrating GBV with health and WASH and Inclusion of persons with disabilities in SGBV response 9. Ensure meaningful engagement Religious leaders and Women preachers to promote gender equality and equity for transformation. Involve Ministry of Religious Affairs Bangladesh for capacity building of the religious leaders in camps and host on prevention of violence against women and girls.10. BRAC will build capacity of refugee volunteers and the community as first responders to the GBV response for community level sustainability through the Solidarity Project.11. Identify women led local organizations and support them through capacity building on SGBV program design, implementation, monitoring and coordination. Engage and train CBO’s , Palli Samaj iwomen leaders and women led network on Women Leadership and GBV Prevention. 12. BRAC will continue to mainstream SGBV interventions in its emergency preparedness and response plans, through specialized capacity building for refugees, contributory stocks of 1000 dignity kits. "13. BRAC will work on Empowerment of women and girls with disabilities; support them to develop their capacities through Skill and Vocational training . Establish physical accessibility to persons with disabilities toSGBV infrastructure such as ramps, toilets, etc.""14. BRAC will work to create positive inter-community relationships in Refugee Camp and Host through interactions between the two communities to shape perceptions. BRAC will organize Friendship dialogue and sports activity between host community and Rohingya community. In addition We will work on strengthen engagement of religion preachers in social development and peacebuilding so that they can contribute in social cohesion.
