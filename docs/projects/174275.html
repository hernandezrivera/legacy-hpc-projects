Project Summary


The goal of this program is to facilitate evidence-based understanding of populations, needs and their conditions in hard to reach areas, and support evidence-based planning and programming for humanitarian actors in Northeast Nigeria through information management and coordination support, while addressing critical risks faced by IDPs in camps in Borno through CCCM and WASH support.

1 - Needs

The proposed action has been designed according to the needs identified in Nigeria by the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), and through communication with OCHA, sector leads and donors. The sections below summarize findings of an ongoing context analysis; introduce ACTED, IMPACT and their joint initiative REACH; and, outline our capacity and experiences to be able to deliver the proposed intervention in Northeast Nigeria. The ongoing decentralization of the humanitarian response in Northeast Nigeria, first relocating humanitarian architecture and international actors from Abuja to Maiduguri throughout late 2016 and early 2017, and now devolving coordination and response functions further from Maiduguri to enclaved LGA capitals with acute humanitarian needs, has fostered and promoted a better understanding of humanitarian needs and gaps across the response. ACTED and IMPACT’s REACH Initiative has engaged with this process extensively, working closely with OCHA, sector and working group leads, NGO fora, as well as with partner organizations more directly to establish a strong understanding of information needs and priorities, as well actively filling identified gaps through coordinated inter-agency assessments.REACH activities are implemented under the technical direction of IMPACT and with direct operational support from ACTED. REACH has been in operation in Nigeria since April 2017 and has rapidly expanded its activities to cover all accessible LGAs of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. All activities proposed under this intervention will be coordinated through joint offices in Maiduguri and supported by an existing ACTED field base in Monguno. The light-touch nature of REACH’s information management activities means interventions can be implemented in remote field locations with limited infrastructure.Through its engagement in Northeast Nigeria, REACH continues to contribute to data sharing with the objective of improving humanitarian partner capacities for response planning and evidence-based programming, while also strengthening collaboration and harmonization of approaches. In light of continued displacement trends, military operations, and offensives of armed opposition groups witnessed in 2019, continued access to reliable and up-to-date information on populations in H2R remains of critical need to the humanitarian community. To be able to appropriately plan and preposition resources to provide life-saving humanitarian aid to newly displaced populations in H2R areas, response actors require more accurate estimates of potential influx of new case-loads to existing IDP settlements, as well as the scale and scope of their needs. Currently, these data do not exist, preventing fact-based planning by partners and negatively impacting the appropriateness and efficacy of their response To facilitate decision-making and coordination among humanitarian actors, ACTED will partner with IMPACT, though their jointly implemented Initiative REACH to support multi-sectoral and area-based profiling of populations, their needs and conditions in H2R areas across Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. This will be implemented through iterative remote assessments using key informants with recent information of H2R areas in enclaved LGA capitals. This will enable humanitarian actors to understand accurate estimates of potential influx of new case-loads to existing IDP settlements, as well as the scale and scope of their needs, and begin planning responses in H2R areas once they become accessible. In Dikwa, the protracted conflict in the Northeast of Nigeria has led to the influx of over 60,000 displaced people, seeking refuge in chronically congested IDP sites, with significant unmet humanitarian needs. The garrison town setting demarcated by military trenches, hinders the expansion of camp settlements due to grave insecurity and high AOG operations on the outskirts of the town and surrounding region. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

• As CCCM Manager, ACTED will actively engage with GBV and protection partners to refer identified protection cases and ensure overall implementation of protection activities, including liaising with partners to address protection issues on site, as well as ensuring that older displaced persons are included in tracing and re-unification activities. This will be done in continuation with previous year’s activities, relying in particular on the CRM/CFM, on the Community-based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) for PSEA established by UNICEF and on outreach teams, which are effective tools that help identifying protection cases. On the one hand, the anonymity granted by the CRM/CFM and CBCM allows people to report any protection concerns to ACTED, which then refers them to the protection partner for action (the CBCM in particular will enable beneficiaries to report on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse). On the other hand, the presence of outreach teams roving across camps ensures that vulnerable people – especially those with limited mobility such as the elderly and people with disabilities – are able to report any protection concern, along with other encountered issues. Outreach teams guarantee total confidentiality of information shared. • The presence of outreach teams will also ensure that vulnerable people unable to participate in community meetings receive up-to-date information on activities, meetings, distributions, etc., implemented by ACTED and partners present in the camps. This service – compounded to radio messaging in the site – will reduce the risk of exclusion of the most vulnerable. • ACTED will continue to promote, monitor and advocate for safe and non‐discriminatory access to facilities and services by all site residents. Camp Management will ensure food distribution points are located in accessible locations; help for elder persons, people with disabilities and the chronically ill will be available to assist in collecting food, and NFI items will be distributed, should they be deemed necessary. Areas managed by ACTED have distribution points relatively close to IDP sites. Therefore, ACTED will be in the position to provide ‘door to door’ services through either community outreach workers helping directly, or casual labour hired during the day to assist with these vulnerable groups. ACTED will guarantee that information on services and facilities is widely distributed throughout the sites in ways that do not rely on literacy, and that facilities are not located in unsafe areas within the sites.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

Cognisant that displacement is experienced differently by men, women, boys, and girls, ACTED conducted a gendered assessment from 26 August-06 September 2019, looking specifically into the situation for women and girls living in camps, in Dikwa, Mafa, and Maiduguri. In Dikwa, the assessment covered 196 HH across 8 camps, with an all-female enumerator team seeking to better understand the concerns and priorities of women and girls living in camps. The results show that women and girls face fears within their resident camp settings; including 75%, 83% and 92% of respondents in Ajari, Shewari, and Ministry of Works camps respectively, reporting to feel most unsafe when using latrines. High rates of women also fear sexual assault inside the camp (over 60% in six out of eight assessed sites). Further, over 40% of respondents in Kamchijin Prayer Ground, Mohammed Kyarimi, Shehuri, Ajari, and Shehu Musta II Camps report not having access to torch light for accessing latrines at night. The integration of such findings into CCCM programming enables gender-nuanced response for improved overall protection within camp settings, promoting the safety and wellbeing of women and girl IDP residents.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED understands that individuals have particular needs, and that men, women, boys, and girls each face particular inequalities and risks in the context of living in displacement in an IDP camp. Part of ACTED’s role as camp management agency is to ensure that all activities and programmes taking place within ACTED’s CCCM sites are sensitive to the particular needs of vulnerable groups. To ensure that this is done objectively and accurately, ACTED CCCM team conducts regular safety audits in the target camps, as part of the site monitoring activities, and will provide regular feedback to partners active in the camp regarding protection concerns voiced by vulnerable groups, including women and girls. As CCCM Agency, ACTED ensures active advocacy for requisite interventions by implementing partners, and will pursue site improvement, operation and maintenance works, that directly address needed improvements in protection mainstreaming in camp activities.
