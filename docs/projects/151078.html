Project Summary

Enhancing the protective environment and increasing community resilience in villages along the line of contact in government-controlled areas (GCA) of eastern Ukraine through shelter repairs to 1) contribute to transitional solutions meeting minimal international and national shelter standards; and 2) provide durable housing for the most vulnerable conflict-affected population.

Needs

Today the conflict in eastern Ukraine is depicted to be in a frozen state - though regular escalations continue and overall shelter needs still exist in areas close to the contact line. DRC’s strategy for 2019 aims at addressing the remaining needs of persons of concerns (POC) and internally displaced people (IDP) along the contact line, keeping a minimal and necessary response capacity when shelling occurs. The area analysis conducted by REACH in February 2018 covering areas of GCA within 5 kilometers from the contact line indicated that nearly half (42%) of residents of those communities are over 60 years old. Since elderly households are the less likely to resettle and less likely to be able to manage repair and construction work, the project and the implementing methodology will be designed to address their specific needs, e.g. using contractor to implement the repair will be prioritised over distribution of building materials and/or cash grants to beneficiaries. Direct provision of materials with labor will ensure that the works are completed and in a timely manner. Light repair assistance to POCs and IDPs who have had their homes damaged in the conflict along the contact line continues to be a need for 2019. Along the line of contact, many INGOs have supported significant numbers of light and medium repair interventions. However, the Shelter Cluster’s damaged homes data-base shows that gaps remain where POC and IDP populations have not yet received assistance for this type of intervention. More specifically, DRC has homes in the towns/villages of Hranitne, Marinka, and Krasnogorivka that it has assessed but has been unable to assist with repairs to date due to funding limitations. It can also be expected that new damages will occur from the on-going, sporadic fighting. Based on past experience of DRC’s shelter intervention, a contractor driven approach provides a higher standard of assistance and gives the organisation more control over the quality of implementation, mitigating the risk in comparison with cash-driven approach. However, in line with DRCs needs and case-based approach, in certain exceptional cases a cash modality might be considered, especially in hard to reach areas where contractors may be hesitant/unable intervene.

Activities & outcomes

The proposed activities will allow DRC-DDG to ensure that high quality services are delivered to the most vulnerable persons in the government controlled areas of Donetsk Oblasts. DRC-DDG will directly implement the project utilising DRC’s tried and tested standard operating procedures, and in coordination with Shelter/NFI cluster recommendations. Result 1: Basic shelter needs are met for conflict-affected populations residing in private homes or apartments. Following our current implementation strategy, DRC is planning to repair damaged houses and apartments through contractors while DRC technical staff will ensure work quality standard and compliance with DRC shelter guidelines. In line with the Shelter Cluster guidelines, DRC will implement roof repairs, installation of windows, insulated ceiling, flooring, and internal electrical system repair according to each house’s needs, in order to ensure one or two warm rooms, depending on the size of the household. For heavy repairs houses, works will be more extensive, but will also seek to provide proper warm and safe environment for the targeted beneficiaries. Provision of furniture can be included as part of the heavy repair works – according to need. Activities• DRC field engineers conduct damage assessments in the pre-selected villages for further drafting BoQs to meet the Shelter Cluster standards in perspective of light/medium/heavy repairs (in many cases, this step was completed during previous projects);• Verification of the current shelter and protection assessment data to certify the relevance of the shelter interventions and the vulnerability level of the beneficiaries;• Tendering of the repair works to be implemented;• Work implementation through contractors;• DRC monitoring and approval of implemented works;• impact and satisfaction survey.
