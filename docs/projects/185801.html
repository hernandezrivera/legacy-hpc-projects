Project Summary

Save the Children (SC), through the project, will identify the non-functional water points and water supply systems in the camps and repair these to ensure safe water sources. This activity will also include repairs of accessibility path towards facilities and improvement of female friendly facilities, such as door locks, disposal buckets with lids for appropriate menstrual hygiene management. A database will be prepared for WASH facilities with GPS coordinates. The project will regularly de-sludge pits of latrines installed in the targeted areas to ensure its functionality. SC will identify the latrines and bathing units in the target camps and repair to ensure the functionality of the facilities. SC will work at individual household level to improve the hygiene practices. SC will engage hygiene promotion volunteers to carry out the hygiene promotion and COVID-19 messaging in the targeted camps. SC will continue solid waste management in camps that will minimize health and environmental hazards. SC will orient adolescent girls on Menstrual Hygiene Management and selected adolescent girls will be trained on production of reusable menstrual pads at home with materials that are easily available in the community. Solar lights will be installed/repaired around the constructed latrines, water points and access ways to WASH facilities to ensure safe and easy access for women, girls, children and people with disabilities. Existing WASH Committees will be strengthened for all facilities and training will be provided for skills building in operation and management of WASH infrastructure to take over their ownership. The project has provision for addressing the needs of people with disabilities through renovating the existing latrines into disability friendly and prepositioning the emergency latrine kits in case of any disasters and relocation.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya Refugees are residing in overcrowded camps in Cox’s Bazar and are highly vulnerable to COVID-19. The overcrowded and unhygienic conditions increase the potential for the rapid spread of disease. There is a critical need to ensure improved water service, sanitation, hygiene promotion, and community engagement. The provision of safe water, sanitation, hygienic living and environmental conditions are essential to protect human health during all infectious disease outbreaks, including the COVID-19 outbreak. To ensure smooth water supplies, sanitation and hygiene practices, repairs and maintenance of existing WASH structures along with distribution of hygiene and dignity kits are highly needed for the refugees to limit the outbreak. In Ukhia, lack of clean water is reported as a concern, while in Teknaf, lack of water is only perceived to be an issue by a few key informants, while generally, people report being able to meet even additional COVID-19-related water needs using rainwater (J-MSNA, Refugees, 2021). Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines during the day, while at night they risk of harassment and violence due to inadequate lighting. Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) has become more difficult during the pandemic due to delays in distribution of materials and increased difficulty for women and girls to wash and dry their menstrual cloths due to taboos around menstruation, resulting in an increased risk of infection. Rohingya refugees face a variety of problems when accessing water points, latrines, and bathing facilities. This shows that even if Sphere standards are met in terms of the number of WASH facilities, access to them is not guaranteed. For water points, access issues include waiting times, distance to water points, and water points not functioning. For latrines and bathing facilities, they include lack of lighting, lack of gender separation, and facilities not functioning. Access is a concern particularly for people with disabilities. Due to the excess generation of wastes in camps, there is a need for solid waste management in the camps. Solid waste is often dumped in narrow spaces between shelters and in landfill sites. Provision of appropriate solid waste management activities through active community participation is highly necessary for the camps. Based on the priorities, the following are the needs for WASH services in the camps: maintaining and continuing all institutional facilities (health, education, training centers, nutrition, food distribution centers), hygiene promotion, behavior change intervention, considering equitable access for vulnerable people (children, people with disabilities, PLW, old aged), durable solutions like water supply systems/network using renewable energy, fecal sludge management unit, female-friendly sanitation spaces, child participation in planning and monitoring program, and provision for menstrual hygiene management.
