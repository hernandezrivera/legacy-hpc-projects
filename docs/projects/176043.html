Project Summary

The project will be aligned to Nutrition Cluster specific objectives, namely 1. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women; 2 To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition; 3 To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information; 4 To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups. The project will complement the ongoing intervention in selected priority Counties, narrowing the nutrition care provision gaps in the most remote sites and for the most neglected groups for what concerns MAM/SAM treatment but also prevention (as per MIYCN principles) and timely detection of acute malnutrition. Nutrition services and related activities will be informed by the most recently updated guidelines, taking into account COVID19 prevention and containment measures. Different services delivery modalities will be considered, from static to mobile/community based, as proactive and articulated strategy to overcome the accessibility issues affecting current nutrition care coverage and to reach both host and displaced population. The proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities will facilitate the selection of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable option, while orientating the intervention on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries. Nutrition services will be provided at Health Facility level or through mobile team and community based initiatives in their catchment area and under their supervision and guide, resulting highly comprehensive, as addressing both health and nutrition conditions and the inter- related complications. In this way, the project will contribute enhancing the institutionalization of management of acute malnutrition into the health system as suggested by the Cluster. Corner stone of the proposed nutrition intervention will be its integration with other sectors as well. WASH, FSL, Education will be given high attention, with the result of the applied strategy to be wide enough to tackle some of the key acute malnutrition drivers, as poor hygiene and access to clean water, limited food availability, low knowledge. Being aware of the higher exposure to malnutrition of certain population groups, whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) makes them particularly vulnerable and their access to services more challenging, besides requiring specific response routine interventions are not used to provide, the project will guarantee adeguate integration with the Protection sector, through the opening and sustain of reciprocal referral channels for people to be offered both nutrition and clinical/psychosocial support. In general, people/right centered approach will be adopted and mainstreamed to guarantee project wide responsiveness and acceptability. The quality of provided care will be strenghten through technical assistance and supportive supervision and monitored through an enhanced data collection and analysis, functional to informed decision making process to improve the current situation. In an area where GAM overcomes the 15% emergency threshold and more than 50% of population are in IPC Phase 3, the project will contribute enhancing targeted communities capacity to cope with current and future crises.

1 - Needs

LAKES STATE can be considered priority humanitarian setting. It has been heavily affected by flooding, 5 over 8 Counties have been targeted by emergency interventions under the related SSHF Reserve Allocation. The adverse environment exacerbated its already poor situation, due to years of wide spread insecurity affecting essential services provision/access and resulting in high IDPs burden over hosting families (5.1% of these latter are in these conditions, versus 3.9% national average), whose resources are limited, because of raids/lootings and environmental factors. This is even more concerning when considering that, due to the continuous fightings, 72% households are headed by a woman, more exposed to violence when dealing with domestic tasks and likely to be food insecure. Lakes State is amongst those with the worst Food Security level; more than 50% of its population in IPC Phase 3 or above. Average GAM is above the 15% threshold, and almost the 40% of CBA women are stunting. Inadequate HFs number, distribution, conditions (staff number and skills, equipment, supplies), with insufficient funding for sustaining routine services, fail in offering the due preventive and curative functions and in guaranteeing effective integration with and provision of nutrition care. Nutrition services integration at HFs is still to be completed, especially in case of different IP; ie. growth monitoring is not part of routine OPD package (and in some Counties it has been suspended because of COVID19 prevention), resulting in missed opportunities for SAM/MAM identification. In the 5 Counties CUAMM is in charge of nutrition program support, all services saw a drop in admissions in comparison with 2019 (-7% SFP, -12% PLW, -16% OTP, -40% SC). While the reduction in SC patients might be due to improved knowledge and practices which promote earlier access to care and prevent further deterioration, what seen for the other services call for attention on their affective accessibility and perceived acceptability/responsiveness, although quality results to be above standard (cure rate above 90%). BHI launch resulted in almost 60000 people being reached by nutrition messages in the villages, but this means less than 15% of considered Counties overall population. Better results (95000 participants) have been reported under group counselling, although participation is still unbalanced, with only 20% male attendants. The few nutrition staff at the HFs, forcing CNVs to operate there instead of moving to the villages, result in zero MYCN activities at household. This implies nutrition care being not easily accessible for the most vulnerable groups, whose mobility is constrained and whose status put them even more at risk of developing malnutrition: people with disabilities and/or mental disorders, IDPs, women, children, elders, sick. The size of their problem is difficult to estimate. HMIS Nutrition indicators are not disaggregated by gender/age/vulnerability. NIS asks for detailed information but the double system implies under rather than improved reporting. This hampered local authorities capacities and possibilities of taking informed decision on nutrition services strenghten. Malnutrition drivers are not properly addressed. Besides the already mentioned Food Security, with 77% of population with poor food consumption, WASH is matter of concern: 96% households practice open defecation, 84% do not have soap. [2020DHIS2, IPCSouthSudanDecember2020, FSNMRRound25, 2020NIS]

2 - Project activities

The intervention will strengthen Acute Malnutrition management institutionalization into Health System with CHD and communities involvement. It will complement current intervention in 5 Lakes State Counties with 49 health providers offering OTP/TSFP and 5 SC (3 PHCCs,2 Hospitals). HFs will provide prevention/lifesaving treatment services as per updated GLs on CMAM and be referral site for Community Nutrition Volunteers. Combining static and outreach services delivery will be effective in reaching vulnerable/neglected groups, whose poor access to and utilization of care is affecting enrollment in and adherence to nutrition program. “To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women” (SO1), the project will invest on prevention, following the updated (COVID19 sensitive) GL on community nutrition and family MUAC. CNVs will cooperate with community representatives, involved in intervention planning, monitoring and realization. MIYCF practices (individual/group counselling, mothers to mothers support, social behavior change communication), Vit A Mass Campaign and defaulters tracking and referral will be widely implemented. “To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition” (SO2) HFs will be supported with staff recruitment/training/supervision, supplies/equipment (in kind or procured), infrastructures upgrade. Nutrition/Health integration will not be limited to site sharing. MUAC/Growth monitoring at EPI/ANC/OPD will make health services safe and accessible entry points for U5/PLW enrolment into Nutrition Program and malnutrition complications will be promptly treated. Health staff will be enabled to identify and handle the most common diseases in malnourished (as Malaria). The sustainable/effective integration of Nutrition interventions in County Health plan will be promoted through improved nutrition data collection, analysis, reporting for informed/evidence based decision making, as per SO3 (“To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information”). Nutrition/Health integration is just one side of the multidisciplinary approach to address malnutrition drivers, expanding to Education/FSL/Wash/Protection, coordinating various Stakeholders’ to maximize intervention outcomes as per SO4 (“To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups”). ECD principles will contribute through SAM children stimulation. Nutrition messages will be part of school health education. CNVs and Lead Mothers will involve families in kitchen gardens/cooking demonstrations to improve dietary variety. Nutrition sites will have improved WASH facilities (functioning latrines, hand wash points, clean water). Nutrition education sessions will include hygiene promotion, WASH kits for U5 SAM patients will be considered. Protection approach will be streamed, ensuring due attention to particularly vulnerable/neglected groups (SGBV survivors, people with disabilities/mental disorders). Nutrition services will be their entry point to specific care; they will be identified, handled and referred to specialized centers for psychosocial/legal support and follow up. Network with these facilities will be strengthen for concerned people adequate attendance. Staff will be trained/sensitized to respect their dignity and address their needs. CUAMM technical assistance/operational support on nutrition services delivery/management will guarantee actions quality, while services responsiveness and acceptability will be promoted engaging beneficiaries in all project phases, including M&E through community feedback mechanism

3 - Project indicators

The project will be monitored through the indicators proposed by the Nutrition Cluster. In particular the project will be monitored to confirm the achievement of 19640 men/women receiving MYINC individural counselling, 50245 U5 children getting Vit A supplementation through dedicated campaigns, 5141 U5 children treated for SAM and 7646 U5 children treated for MAM, 13539 PLW treated for MAM, 2570 severely malnourished children receiving WASH Kits and 2570 severely malnourished children being tested for Malaria, 150 GBV survivors accessing dedicated services through Nutrition program. Further, activities implementation will be monitored as well, to verify the realization of 196 cooking sessions, 196 programatin visits to nutrition sites, 4 dashboard with nutrition data at State level, 5 facility based play area and 5 facility based system for GBV audit. The related data will be collected as much as possible through the existing Health Management Information System (DHIS2) and through already adopted integrative system as Nutrition Information System (NIS), avoiding creating parallel channels and increasing health staff workload but contributing to nutrition program own monitoring and evaluation enhancement. At this regard, CUAMM Technical Assistance to targeted Counties authorities, staff and stakeholders will concern data collection/reporting/analysis, to promote prompt evidence based decision making process as per SO3. Supportive supervisions will be conducted to verify the completeness and reliability of available data and to feed nutrition staff back about their performance against the main indicators, as a a way to motivate sustainable and aware improvements. At this regard, CUAMM and CHD will propose State MoH a visual presentation of the main achieved results, to be widely disseminated (for instanca during quarterly meetings). For those indicators not found in the DHIS2/NIS, CUAMM and Cluster will agree on the specific tools to be used, choosing an user friendly design to facilitate field team and health staff work. When possible, CUAMM will encourage data disaggregation by age and gender, for being able indentifying eventual cases of unbalanced access and utilization of supported services. The proposed activities are expected to contribute to data production for each of the suggested indicators. Further, some of them implies the set up or strenghten of process for the collection of complementary information, both quantitative and qualitative

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CUAMM has recently approved and endorsed its own PSEA policy. The related communication strategy consists in integrating PSEA information dissemination into the ongoing activities aimed to sensitize people on Gender related matters, including Gender Equality, Gender Empowerment, GBV. These activities includes health staff and authorities training, community meetings, IEC materials production and events…activities which are part of CUAMM on going intervention and which will be included in the project as well, to reach a wider audience. Integrating PSEA information in this framework is expected to facilitate its understanding and to promote its acceptability. CUAMM PSEA policy is completed by a toolkit aimed to assist the field team in this process

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CUAMM and CHD are already working to ensure the above mentioned Health Committees (related to the same HFs providing nutrition services) gender balanced and inclusive composition and attendance, making them as participatory spaces women and other vulnerable individuals can be part of, an empowerment driver, offering public engagement opportunity, usually very limited for certain groups, and making these latter aware of their own value, to be enhanced through a virtuous circle of increasing participation. Similarly, Review Meetings and community based activities will be open to CSO representing women, PWD, and other vulnerable groups. Community Nutrition Volunteers or other community based health providers will bring services as much close as possible to beneficiaries, minimizing access constraints faced by who is not in condition to freely move because of physica, social and economic dependance, as women and PWD and offering these people the opportunity to provide their feedback and inputs. The anonimous Clients Satisfaction Survey has been designed and it is administered in a way that makes it accessible to all kind of HFs patients (including nutrition services clients), indipendently on their education background (usually poor for the most vulnerable and neglected as women and PWD) and without requiring time wasting and further movements.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information about the project will be driven through existing community consultation mechanism, to avoid overlapping and confusion and to contribute to strenghten them even further. The same mechanism will be used to get feedback and complaints, to make easier for the beneficiaries, already used to them, to give their contribute. This will be facilitated by the fact nutrition services will be integrated into the health system these mechanism had been designed for. CUAMM is used to maintain a constant dialogue with communities representatives, from those belonging to the Health Facilities related Health Committees to those from community based associations, which are invited to take part to the Quarterly Review Meetings held at County level and which ensure a wider perspective on community needs and expectations and can report on services responsiveness and acceptability. The recently introduced Client Satisfaction Survey offers the opportunity to get feedback on provided services by the direct beneficiaries. It is administered at service delivery site, meaning it is easily accessible, it is filled by literate volunteers so that it can be administered to any patient, without discrimination based on education level (which would have left behind the most vulnerable) and it has been piloted to confirm all questions being easy to understand and to be answered by people with different background. Finally, CUAMM is supporting local health authorities in managing County wide network of community based health providers (Boma Health Workers and Community Nutrition Volunteers), operating at grossroots level, directly in contact with beneficiaries and therefore in the position to inform them about the project and to get their feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback will be collected through the above described community consultation mechanism, already existing and regularly used to get population perspective and perceptions on services responsiveness and acceptability. This consultation mechanism has been designed to get continuous inputs on the situation on ground and to be enabled doing timely adaptations of the proposed activities/supported services. As already used to do, CUAMM field team will assist the local health authorities (who are in charge of nutrition services management as well) in collecting, consolidating and analyzing the various feedback and to translate them into action points to be integrated into proposed project work plan and overarching strategy, making them more tailored and focused to respond to the needs and to address the issues beneficiaries perceived as priorities in that specific moment. This is also functional to ensure project constant update and alignment to the reality, narrowing the gap created by the time passed from project writing and project implementation.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The project will support essential preventive and curative nutritions services, promoting them as entry point for protection ones, especially for women and children and people with disabilities. The project will enhance nutritions services accessibility, by providing them as much close as possible to beneficiaries, overcoming those geographical/economical/social barriers which are more penalizing for vulnerable people and groups; in this way, nutrition providers will be in the best position to capture these latter and to facilitate their entrance in the essensial services net. Nutrition staff will be trained to promptly identify patients with physical or intellectual impairment and/or who experienced GBV and provide them with the most suitable first aid, while arranging for their referral or for more structured treatment, according to facility/provider capacities and competencies. The staff will also learn how and will be given the means to make the sites where nutrition services will be provided as much accessible and acceptable as possible, starting from infrastructural barriers reduction up to developing a right based approach in receiving and treating patients, creating an enabling and comfortable environment for those who are used or fear to be discriminated/stigmatized because of their conditions. CUAMM and authorities will ensure the due coordination and cooperation with protection sector actors and stakeholders, creating structured and effective referral pathways for people reaching health facilities (where nutrition services will be provided)/being reached by nutrition providers to have prompt access to all other services they are in need of for a comprehensive management of their conditions

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project aims to benefit both host and displaced population and it will complement existing resources to strenghten and expand existing services, making these latter specifically responsive to the most vulnerable (women, children, PWD, elderlies) needs, usually neglected or not adequately addressed, especially during emergencies. For instance, one of the problems it will address is the inadequacy of routine nutritions services to respond to an increasing population, under IDPs influx, especially when IDPs prefer to locate in remote sites, not served by any health facilities or to reach those who cannot easily move to the few/dispersed ones. The adoption of outreach/community based service delivery modalities is functional to bring the needed assistance at the closest possible level to beneficiaries, reducing both the distance they would have covered to reach static providers and the risk they would have been exposed to while travelling. Nutrition staff as well as community nutrition volunteers will be trained and mentored to be able identifiying and handling patients with particular vulnerabilities, considering both clinical and psychosocial aspects, through the effective linkage and referral to specialized entities. CUAMM will work with local authorities to ensure nutrition services data being collected as per HMIS/NIS required gender/age/disabilities disaggregation, in order to get evidence for informed decision on more responsive system design/planning

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project directly and primarely address global acute malnutrition through the support to preventive and curative nutrition services at community and HF level, realizing the suggested institutionalization of health and nutrition program integration, as described in the Activities chapter.
