Project Summary

The proposed project will aim to target nearly 26000 beneﬁciaries in selected camps in provisioning safe and durable WASH services. While the main aim is to construct water network systems, operating vacutag to transfer fecal sludge from camp to lagoon and rehabilitate water points, latrines or bathing cubicles, hygiene promotion will also be carried out in these communities through effective community outreach methods. It is envisaged that targeted communities will then take on the responsibility of maintaining their hygiene including the upkeep of their WASH facilities with a sense of collective ownership. All activities proposed will also be done in an inclusive manner where targeted peopled will be consulted for their opinions and feedback which will inform the rehabilitation of infrastructure and tailoring of any hygiene and sanitation prop motion interventions. I.e, PWDs, elderly or other vulnerable populations will be considered and enraged in the project from its onset leaving for no discrimination.

Needs

A total of 29% of refugees in camps are now accessing chlorinated water through piped distribution networks which are being constructed under the Sector’s Water Network Plan. The remainder continue to access water from tubewells fitted with handpumps. Water quality surveillance continues to record that 52% of handpumps are showing signs of contamination from e coli. (WHO/UNICEF Water Quality Monitoring 12th Round August 2019) Refugees continue to face challenges in collecting water, especially in the southern Teknaf camps during the dry season, due to long waiting times, where 54% of households reported taking more than 30 minutes to collect water(REACH Household Survey May 2019) Despite 99% of households reporting using pit latrines in the camps , functionality of the latrines remains low at 78%. 32% of refugee households are facing problems accessing or using latrines due to low coverage (too many people using latrines), while 14% face a problem of distance between the latrines and their shelters( REACH WASH Household Dry Season Follow-up Assessment (October 2019): ) Solid Waste Management (SWM) remains a challenge in the camps, with only 33% of the refugee households reporting using household bins or shared garbage bins and 39% reporting the presence of garbage near their houses ( BGD_Joint MSNA_Analysis Tables_Refugee_September 2019_Final BGD_Joint MSNA_Analysis Tables_Refugee_September 2019_Final) appropriate hygiene practices for specific risk behaviors remain low with for example, rates of households reporting washing their hands after handling children’s faces at 32% , and appropriate safe water handling at household level where contamination of stored water is reported at over 90%. ( BGD_Joint MSNA_Analysis Tables_Refugee_September 2019_Final)
