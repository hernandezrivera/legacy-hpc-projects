Project Summary

Project Summary/ Резюме проекта/ Резюме проекту
(4,000 characters max) The aim of the project “Multipurpose Cash, Winterization Assistance and Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People along the ‘contact line’ in Donetska and Luhanska GCA” is to cover basic needs (food, health, hygiene, winterization, PSS) of conflict-affected populations zone along the 'contact line' in Eastern Ukraine (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts).
Whereas the project’s MPC and winterization components will be implemented by ASB Country Office Ukraine, the psychosocial support (PSS) will be provided by ASB’s local partner, Samaritan Union of Ukraine (SSU).
The MPC component of the project aims at addressing the most urgent needs of conflict-affected populations residing in the 20-km zone along the ‘contact line’ in Donetska and Luhanska GCA by providing multipurpose cash grants to 4000 vulnerable beneficiaries during July-December 2021. Thus, two caseloads of 2000 beneficiaries each will receive 3 months of assistance (either July-September 2021 or October-December 2021).
Since ASB Country Office Ukraine is already implementing cash programmes in the government-сontrolled areas along the ‘contact line’, the organization has immediate capacity and access to implement the proposed activities in 2021. The MPC activities will be coordinated with Cash Working Group (CWG) partners and follow the principles of do no harm, disability inclusion and accountability to affected populations (AAP). In the time of COVID-19 pandemic, all activities will follow the COVID-19 Ukraine Assistance Delivery Protocol.
The project activities contribute to the Cash Working Group Objective 1 “Increase the purchasing power of the targeted population to cover their immediate basic needs”.

1 - Needs

Needs/Нужды/Потреби(4,000 characters max) The conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues to have a negative impact on the socioeconomic situation in Ukraine, particularly in the areas adjacent to the ‘contact line’. Moreover, the socioeconomic situation all over the country has deteriorated because of COVID-19 pandemic. To illustrate, the research conducted by the M. Ptukha Institute for Demography and Social Studies of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine indicates that the poverty level may increase by 17% (compared to 2019) by the end of 2020. Particularly affected will be women, older people, and households with children. According to the needs assessment by HelpAge International conducted in rayons near the ‘contact line’ in the GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts (August 2020), 99,46% of older people rely on a pension as their main source of income. As demonstrated by the WASH Cluster Study 2019, the 20-km zone has a significantly higher proportion of households in the poorest category. For the 20-km zone, food is the main household expenditure, with the costs for heating and utilities being the second largest category of expenses (REACH Economic Security Assessment. Government-controlled areas of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts within 20 km of the contact line, March 2019). According to the REACH Economic Security Assessment, 74% of households in the 20-km zone reported having at least one vulnerability, with the proportion being even higher (76%) in the 5-km zone (REACH Economic Security Assessment, March 2019). According to the REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis (2020), 74% of households in the 5-km zone along the ‘contact line’ in the GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts are headed by women (in comparison to 69% in the 5-20-km zone). Within the 5-km zone, this proportion is even higher for rural settlements, where 76% of HHs are female-headed (in contrast to 73% of HHs in urban areas). Among the households of IDPs in the 5-km zone, 80% of them are headed by women. The same study shows that 9% of households residing in the 5-km zone along the ‘contact line’ in the GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts are headed by unemployed people (12% in case of IDPs).The food security situation in 2020 has deteriorated in comparison to 2019. Thus, whereas the proportion of moderately and severely food insecure households in the 5-km zone near the ‘contact line’ was estimated at 9% and 0% in 2019, now it is 13% and 1% respectively (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis, 2020). This study also shows that the food share became more prominent in the household expenditure of the residents of the 5-km zone along the ‘contact line’ (61%, in comparison to 49% in 2018 and 50% in 2019). According to ASB’s PDM data (March 2020), the top priorities for spending cash assistance for vulnerable households along the ‘contact line’ on the GCA side are food, medicines, and heating. Therefore, apart from the MPC grants, ASB plans to support vulnerable households along the ‘contact line’ with cash winterization grants, solid fuel and house insulation. So, both the primary and secondary data indicate a need in provision of multipurpose cash assistance in the areas close to the ‘contact line’, particularly during the COVID-19 pandemic.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Purpose: To cover immediate basic needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations in Eastern Ukraine.Output: vulnerable beneficiaries along the 'contact line' received multi-purpose cash assistance.Outcome: target beneficiaries increase their purchasing power.Activity 1. Beneficiary identification The beneficiary selection methodology will be based on general vulnerabilities, needs and household income. ASB Country Office Ukraine will involve communities in project design, implementation and monitoring, using a variety of methods to ensure that voices of affected people are heard. ASB plans to conduct key informant interviews as well as focus groups with community members to discuss the planned humanitarian intervention. All community consultations will be held remotely or in accordance with the COVID-19 protective measures. In terms of community engagement, ASB also widely uses quantitative data collected during PDMs to make relevant adjustments to the current and future programming. The beneficiary assessment will be undertaken in coordination with the Cash Working Group, local authorities and humanitarian agencies operating in the area. ASB will ensure that beneficiaries meet vulnerability criteria identified by HRP 2021. ASB will target primarily households of older men and women, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, persons aged 40-60 with no employment as well as men and women with compound vulnerabilities. The beneficiary assessment will take place in Avdiivska and Toretska municipalities, Bakhmutskyi and Yasynuvatskyi raions in Donetska oblast as well as in Novoaidarskyi, Popasnianskyi and Stanychno-Luhanskyi raions in Luhanska oblast. A complaints and response mechanism (CRM) is available to ask questions, request assistance, provide feedback and make complaints. All appeals are recorded in the CRM Database for follow-up. The complaints are dealt with in a timely manner, in line with the established CRM procedures. At the end of each distribution cycle, ASB will conduct the post-distribution monitoring (PDM). All assessments, including PDM, are designed to allow age, gender and disability disaggregation.Activity 2. Provision of timely MPC transfersThere will be 2 distribution cycles of cash assistance in 2021. In each caseload, up to 2000 beneficiaries will receive cash assistance to cover their immediate basic needs:• Caseload 1: July-September 2021 • Caseload 2: October-December 2021Cash transfers to beneficiaries will take place once or twice in 3 months, covering needs for the respective period. The proposed cash intervention will follow the June 2020 CWG’s recommendation (UAH 1150 per person per month), covering the income gap between actual subsistence level and the average income level of populations in the conflict zone. ASB may change a number of beneficiaries in case of significant exchange rate fluctuations.

3 - Indicators & targets

Indicators & targets / Индикаторы & Цели/ Индикатори & Цілі(4,000 characters max) The main indicators and targets are the following:• 85% of beneficiaries are satisfied with the aid provided;• 85% of beneficiaries report that the assistance was easily accessible;• 70% of beneficiaries report that they are aware of the complaint mechanisms;• 4000 people receiving emergency assistance in 2021;• 4000 grants distributed, by amount and month;• 60% of program beneficiaries are female;• 35% of beneficiaries are older people;• 15% of beneficiaries are persons with disabilities and/or homebound.Based on the interim monitoring data from the ASB’s ongoing project “Hygiene, Nutrition and Winterization as Cash as well as Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People in need along the Contact Line, Eastern Ukraine” (2019-2021), the above indicators are likely to be achieved also in 2021.As demonstrated by the results of the interim PDM on the project component of cash for food and hygiene (March 2020), the overall satisfaction of beneficiaries with the provided assistance is very high (99%). More than 95% of respondents said that the assistance was available, and it was safe for them to receive it. Also, 80,1% of PDM respondents said that they fully knew how to provide feedback to ASB through different channels (phone, e-mail or field staff). The project beneficiaries of cash assistance who were supported by ASB between 1 January and 30 September 2020 demonstrate significant vulnerabilities in terms of age, gender and disability. Thus, 56% of them are older people, 66% are women, 14% of the caseload are boys and girls, 23% are persons with disabilities, and 8% are IDPs. Since the markets are functioning in the 5-km zone along the ‘contact line’ (GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblast), ASB plans to provide cash assistance in 2021. The PDM study conducted by ASB (March 2020) confirmed that cash modality is in line with the preferences of affected populations: 95% of surveyed beneficiaries indicated cash as the preferred aid modality. For the population in the 5-km zone near the ‘contact line’ (GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts), the most preferred modes of delivery for cash assistance are bank account (48%) and post office (32%) (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2018). Thus, the modes of cash disbursement used by ASB (Oshchadbank and UkrPoshta) are also in line with the preferences of affected populations. Due to the home delivery of cash by Ukrposhta, older people and persons with disabilities do not need to travel to a bank or post office department to withdraw their cash grants, so the protection risks are minimized.The PDM findings and regular feedback from beneficiaries through ASB hotline and other complaint and response channels feed the redesign of ASB’s current and design of future project activities.
