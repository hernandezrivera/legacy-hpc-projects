Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to provide the most vulnerable people-returnees in targeted locations of Rural Damascus (Sbeineh) with quality, reliable access to water through the revitalization and rehabilitation of WASH facilities. As a result of the continuous conflict in the area, water and sewage mainlines have been neglected, damaged, or destroyed, causing a substantial hazard to the health and well-being of beneficiaries as well as a risk to the environment. Therefore, HELP will undertake a series of focused activities aimed at improving the sewage networks and the rehabilitation and maintenance of 5 wells in selected locations that have been identified as the most underserved and vulnerable by the Allocation Strategy Paper.

Needs

HELP's WASH engineering team conducted a needs assessment in targeted areas to uncover the most urgent priorities-this information was triangulated with the priorities set through the WASH cluster and Humanitarian Response Plan to confirm the locations to implement activities and most urgent needs of the affected populations. HELP will be implementing activities are in the most need as people begin to return to these areas as the conflict scales down and rebuilding begins. The assessment of the sewage networks in Sbeineh (25000 beneficiaries) indicated that there has been substantial damage to the 2 main sewage networks during the conflict. Furthermore, the maintenance of the mainlines has been neglected because of the ongoing conflict requiring rehabilitation of the mainline and manholes to accommodate the residents and returnees in these areas. The total number of beneficiaries reached for this portion of the project is 25000. Inadequate WASH facilities affect women and children more profoundly by increasing the possibility of water-born disease, requiring that women spend more time on WASH activities, and increasing women's vulnerability if they are required to travel distances to get water (WaterAid,2015). Furthermore, women adolescent girls are more susceptible to maintaining hygienic practices related to menstruation if they don't have access to adequate WASH facilities, therefore, the majority of beneficiaries for this intervention will consist of women and children. Specific information for the location is as follows: - Sbeineh - Rehabilitation and maintenance on residential area sewage mainlines that consists of 5200 Lm, 170 manholes. Women and children make up 70% of the beneficiaries for this portion of the project. The second project component will consist of the rehabilitation and maintenance of 5 wells targeting Sbeinah in rural Damascus. The needs assessment indicated that the wells have been substantially damaged due to the conflict and are not running optimally given the population of returnees. Maintenance and rehabilitation of the premises and equipment is required. A total of 5 wells will be maintained and rehabilitated in Sbeineh reaching a total of 35000 beneficiaries. 5 wells will be maintained and rehabilitated in the selected areas targeting 35,000 beneficiaries in total.- The main railway well - Water Flow 20 m3/h- Al Jala’ Street - Water Flow 15 m3/h- Al Sharqatly Area - Water Flow 20 m3/h- Qurtoba Area - Water Flow 20 m3/h- Hussein Fahed Mar’e Area – Water Flow 20 m3/h The two project components together will reach 60000 beneficiaries, with a majority of the beneficiaries as women and children.

Implementation of activities

The HELP team will directly implement all project activities. HELP's Syria team will coordinate and manage the activities on the ground in Sbeineh and report to the program management team. Project tolerances will be put into place to ensure that there is clarity as to which decisions the field team can make and when the changes or decisions need to be escalated to the program team. This helps to ensure that only authorized, assessed, and needed changes are made to the project and that decision making is done through appropriate channels. This method further enhances the accountability and transparency in the project. As for the areas of wells and sewage mainline in Sbeineh (rural Damascus), HELP detected and organized the assessment in cooperation with MoWR and the governorate of rural Damascus. Therefore, there will be no problems with access and supervision, as HELP will appoint a field engineer to be on site to follow the supervision on a daily basis in full coordination with MoWR, the Governorate of Rural Damascus and the Red Crescent Organization. Project management tools will be used to clearly articulate roles and responsibilities and to track progress against planned at the activity and output levels. Risk registers and issues logs will be regularly updated to reduce impact on project activities and better ensure a smooth implementation. Control procedures for operating are in place in the form of procurement and financial procedures and will ensure against fraud and corrupt actions. Monitoring and accountability mechanisms will also be incorporated to ensure that activities are completed within the agreed upon timeframe and to allow for stakeholders to provide feedback about the project and activities. Project activities will be closely aligned and coordinated with clusters and other agencies operating in rural Damascus by way of regular meetings and coordination in order to prevent duplication and better ensure maximum coverage of interventions for beneficiaries.

Protection Risk Analysis

The beneficiaries require protection. This applies to the resident population located in former conflict zones to those returning to their homes, safety, and protection.While the project does not explicitly contain protection activities, the design, implementation, and monitoring will incorporate protection mainstreaming into the project through the 7 principles of “do no harm” as outlined by UNICEF of: neutrality, impartiality, do less/no harm, accountability, and participation of affected populations-where possible. Monitoring will be conducted at specific intervals throughout the project to ensure that no harm is done to direct and indirect beneficiaries. Additionally, local authorities will be engaged with the project, working alongside the Help team to ensure the risk of harm is reduced for the local population and that inequities in the services provided in the project are mitigated through the transfer of knowledge to the local authorities and team on protection standards prior to implementation activities. Project activities will enhance beneficiary human rights through access to regular and safe water supply and proper sanitary hygiene and reduce the incidence of situations that put vulnerable population in harms way because they do not have access to WASH facilities, particularly for women and children.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation). Mitigating Measure: MOUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities. Risk: Extreme weather conditions have an affect on rehabiliation activities.Mitigating Measure: Plan rehabilitation for dry months to reduce the probability that weather will affect the implementation and timeline of activities. Risk: Project team turnoverMitigating Measure: Implement a comprehensive protocol for project documentation and handover procedures to mitigate the impact on the project timeline. Risk: Local authorities are uncooperative resulting in a delay of activities.Mitigating Measure: Engage authorities early and often through formal (MOUs) and informal means to ensure their buy-in and support for the project. Continue to engage with and work with the Ministry of Water Resources. Risk: Currency fluctuations in the local market affect the budget and procurement of materials.Mitigating Measure: Monitor the market prices and establish control mechanisms that indicate the procedures for escalation of the issue to management and donor.
