Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to reduction of infant morbidity and mortality through
improved access to screening, treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition; as well as
availing preventive micronutrient supplementation among some of the most vulnerable
communities and regions in South Central Somalia.
Based on results of the 2021 Post Gu assessment an estimated 1.2 million children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden) face acute malnutrition between August 2021 and July 2022, including 213 400 likely to be severely malnourished . According to the IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis, the results of 35 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2021, the overall median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Somalia remained Serious (11.1% weight-for-height z-score) Some of the aggravating factors are reported to be localized flooding, drought, and conflicts that contributed to increased vulnerabilities for malnutrition. In addition, limited access to and high prices of food, water shortages, limited access to basic services like primary healthcare, immunization, vitamin A supplementation, sub-optimal feeding practices and childcare, and safe drinking water & sanitation that increase in the prevalence of acute watery diarrhoea were also some risk factors for malnutrition.
Certain population groups such as Galkacyo, Mogadishu, Jowhar and Baidoa IDPs are of concern due to chronically high rates of acute malnutrition with GAM rates of 20.2%, 16.0%, 25.9% and 14.5% respectively. Some of the factors that are attributed to the high prevalence of acute malnutrition include high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices and food insecurity. IMC aims to contribute to reduction of malnutrition related morbidity and mortality through provision of quality nutrition services in four regions of South Central Somalia which have chronically high rates of acute malnutrition. IMC will focus on three main strategies i.e. lifesaving therapeutic interventions, preventive nutrition activities and capacity building. To enhance detection and regular identification of acutely malnourished children and PLWs, IMC Female Health Workers (FHWs) and CHWs will disseminate culturally. Appropriate promotion/prevention messaging and conduct community based screening and referral of malnourished children and PLWs using MUAC and oedema measurements. Family/mother lead MUAC strategy will be used as a simple and cost effective way of increasing coverage of case detection. It also ensures caregivers/mothers have knowledge to identify malnutrition early.
Children who are severely malnourished or at risk of being malnourished will be referred to OTP and TSFP treatment centres, and in the hard to reach areas, by mobile health and nutrition units. Children with severe acute malnutrition with medical complications will be referred to nearby stabilization centres in accordance with national guidelines while observing COVID 19 prevention measures. In addition to Individual and group IYCF counselling, IMC will promote optimal IYCF Practices and positive deviance through Mother to Mother Support Groups. Within these groups, nutritious recipes will be formulated and tested through cooking demonstrations. All these will observe COVID 19 preventive measures. Other high impact nutrition interventions will include multiple micronutrient supplementation for women of reproductive age; Vitamin A Supplementation; deworming for boys and girls aged 6-59 months; and Zinc for treatment of diarrhoea.
