Project Summary

The project will contributes to WASH cluster strategy with main objective to improve access to gender sensitive WASH services to reduce water and sanitation related morbidity and mortality as well as to reduce protection risks for women and girls. The project will be integrated within Concern on-going multi-sector emergency response in Aweil South, Aweil West and Aweil North Counties in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG); Rubkona and Guit counties in Unity; and Kajo-Keji County in Central Equatoria State.
Concern will continue to support routine operations and maintenance (O&M) of an existing borehole in Sector-5 of Bentiu IDP camp (hosting 27,000 people), continued support in waste management and repair & maintenance of sanitation facilities in IDP camp with continued efforts in implementation of WASH cluster transition strategy for IDP camp. Under Current SSHF funded Grant, Concern is hiring a consultant to formulate strategy on community based sanitation management in sector-5 of IDP camp. Concern will engage with the community as per WASH cluster transition strategy to ensure that the management and ownership of sanitation facilities is transferred to the community with formation of sanitation committee and explore potential for user fee structure for regular maintenance.
In Rubkona and Bentiu, Concern will continue the provision of safe water through operation of water treatment plant in Bentiu and the surface water treatment system in Rubkona with continued efforts to transfer water management responsibility to state authorities. Concern will also continue water and sanitation services in newly flood displaced camp in Bentiu/Rubkona in collaboration with WASH cluster and other partners. In Guit County, Concern will support repair of boreholes including enhancing resilience of hand pumps to recurrent flooding. In NBeG, Concern will engage with existing trained water management committee (WMC) to repair dysfunctional hand pumps in Aweil North, Aweil West and Aweil South Counties. Concern will continue to implement Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) in Aweil North and Aweil Counties. Given the influx of returnees in Kajo-Keji, Concern will repair dysfunctional hand pumps in the catchment areas of health facilities supported by Concern.
Context specific hygiene promotion based on best practices from international Behavior Change Communication (BCC) standards will be integrated with all sector level interventions to sensitize household on hygiene practices and COVID-19 awareness and prevention. The hygiene promotion will be integrated within nutrition and health interventions where the nutrition staff, CNVs, mother/fathers groups and health staff will be trained to promote hygiene awareness across all target locations. Similarly, the water and sanitation system in health and nutrition centers will be improved in Concern supported health and nutrition centers across all target locations.

2 - Project activities

The project will implement following activities to contribute to WASH cluster objectives. CO1: Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition: The prevalence of high level of malnutrition is often linked to poor access to water and sanitation services. The WASH activities will be implemented in areas with high GAM prevalence and will be integrated and linked to Concern nutrition and health centre. Concern will support access to safe water, sanitation and promote safe hygiene in communities with high malnutrition and food insecurity. Based on assessed needs, Concern will also repair and improve water and sanitation in nutrition and health centres. Concern will also implement Baby WASH activities integrated within nutrition programme. The SAM children form nutrition sites will receive hygiene kits (cluster stock). Through established network of the Mother/father groups and CNVs, the care takers will be sensitised on the hygiene including awareness on COVID-19. CO2: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties: Concern will continue to support routine operations and maintenance (O&M) of an existing borehole in Sector-5 of Bentiu IDP camp. The storage tanks and pumping hours will be increased to meet minimum sphere standards (15lpd). In Bentiu and Rubkona town, the state authorities will continue to receive O&M support for existing water treatment plants, with continued efforts for gradual hand over of the water systems to state authorities. In Rubkona and Guit Counties, Concern will support construction and repair of boreholes/hand pumps to improve access to safe water while consider necessary DRR measures. This activity will also support O&M of the existing temporary Surface Water Treatment (SWAT) systems at the new flood displaced sites (E&D) in Rubkona. In NBeG (Aweil North, West and South), Concern will assess the conduct repair of dysfunctional water points. Given the influx of returnees in Kajo-Keji, boreholes will be assessed for repair. Concern will continue to support the repair and maintenance of sanitation services in Bentiu IDP camp and implementation of WASH cluster transition Strategy. Sex-segregated semi-permanent latrines and bathing shelters will be constructed in the new flood induced displacement sites (E & D). Concern will continue implementing subsidised CLTS approach in target areas. In addition, Concern will continue to support waste management services in Sector-5 of IDP camp. The project will support WASH cluster transition strategy on the gradual transfer of sanitation management to the community. Context specific hygiene promotion will be conducted to sensitize HHs on hygiene practices and COVID-19 prevention. The main focus will be on hand washing practices, correct and consistent use of latrines for all defecations, safe water chain including HH water transport, storage and consumption. CO3: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation: Concern will conduct routine protection monitoring, gender+ and safety audits to adapt program design and approach accordingly. Prior to the construction of water points and latrines, Concern will conduct safety audit and use the WASH Cluster Protection Mainstreaming tip to ensure WASH specific GBV mitigations. The safety audit will analyse different needs, roles and dynamics to improve WASH interventions sensitivity to GBV risks. CO4: Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure: In flood prone areas, Concern has redesigned the apron and drainage, approved and adopted by the state ministry. Due to recurrent flooding and its impact on WASH facilities, Concern will engage with the community to ensure that WASH facilities to be built are not located in flood-prone corridors and design will incorporate DRR measures.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Concern engages with the target community and relevant state /local authorities during the needs assessment, project design and monitoring to seek their feedback and adapt project assistance/delivery accordingly. The broader consultations with the community during the assessment will inform about the project design, priority activities and modality of assistance. The project/villages committees including most vulnerable such as women, elderly, disable will be formed at inception stage to engage in project delivery and monitoring. The project/villages/boma committees from target beneficiaries share feedback to adapt the assistance accordingly. The regular monitoring and quarterly reviews will collate the feedback, lessons and inform about necessary adaptation to project assistance. Similarly, the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understand different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at distribution points/beneficiaries registration points will be used to avoid exclusion of access as for as possible considering the operational challenges in target locations. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through lead mothers and CNVs hired from local community.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. The safeguarding focal points each office ensure implementation and compliance with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitor are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stakeholders, Concern informs them about right to assistance, entitlements, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns which are referred to Protection partners. All recruitments of staff and trainings ensure that there is equal access to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Monitoring will collect data on any information on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Concern’s policies and operational guidelines are in line with IASC’s five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations. Prior to initiating project activities and delivery of assistance, Concern will conduct an inception meeting with the target beneficiaries and stakeholders to inform them about the project scope, target criteria and their entitlements. Similarly, during the inception meetings and during regular consultations with the community, Concern will inform the beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. Depending on type of assistance, context and target population profile, Concern ensure that the banners with information on complaint channel and feedback mechanism are displayed broadly in the target areas and at the distribution points. The monitoring and CRM (Dedicate staff) will conduct regular visit to refresh beneficiaries about the right to complaint, suggestion and how to make complaint.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with camp management, local authorities and Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. Village Health Committees will conduct hazard mapping of the main health risks for women, men, girls and boys as well as PSNs such as elderly and disabled, and identify solutions to improve hygiene practices that are relevant to their respective communities. Latrines and bathing shelters constructed will be gender segregated along with being child-friendly and accessible to disabled and the elderly, and be lockable to minimize protection risks. Water Management Committees will also aim to have equal representation to ensure women’s, men’s, boys’ and girls’ water needs are met and to identify mitigation measures to ensure the program does not have any negative impacts on conflict. Water Management Committees will be supported to analyze the roles of women and men in accessing water and decision-making, and trainings will target both male and female community members. The location of water points will be identified in consultation with communities to ensure locations are safe and secure. Concern will ensure that water sources located in visible, central locations and not more than 500 meters from settlement. Traditionally, it is primarily women and girls who are responsible for water collection, so the addition of water points will reduce the need to travel long distances and minimize protection risks. Concern have a participatory approach for designing water activities and will consult community members to identify conflict risks associated with use of water resources such as through conflict mapping. Focus will be made on involvement of female casual workers in WASH programming to ensure safeguarding measures are in place and disseminated at community level. Efforts will be put to ensure WASH programs are not reinforcing exploitative gender stereotypes and inequalities but rather that all partners thrive to implement sensitive and transformative WASH interventions. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in program to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.
