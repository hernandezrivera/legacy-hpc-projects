Project Summary

The main shocks that drive internal displacement in Somalia are conflict, drought, COVID-19, and flooding. As a result, approximately 2.9 million people have been internally displaced with the majority settling in over 2,400 sub-standard and unplanned IDP sites in urban, semi-urban and rural areas across the country. IDPs are exposed to poor living conditions and are subjected to constant displacements through forceful evictions by landlords and floods, the sites are also overcrowded, are inaccessible and lack proper planning to allow for efficient emergency services.
Inadequate shelter conditions, lack of long terms security of tenure and limited employment opportunities for internally displaced people are some of the factors contributing to increased vulnerabilities for IDPs. These factors in turn affect their ability to secure durable, safe, accessible, and decent shelter and settlements which exposes them to gender-based violence, increased spread of diseases especially COVID-19 and flooding due to poor drainage. While these challenges are experienced by all IDPs, the most vulnerable groups are women (where the majority head IDP households) households headed by girls, minority clans and person’s living with disabilities or old people who have no source of income. Persons with disability face several barriers such as separation from family members, loss of assistive devices, access to information and humanitarian assistance during displacement in informal and unplanned settlements.
To mitigate some of these challenges, SYPD aims to improve the living conditions and safe access to services to IDPs and strengthen the effectiveness of multi-sectoral interventions by establishing CCCM coordination mechanisms at the site level.
The project will target vulnerable IDPs focusing on persons living with disabilities and women living in sites that lack dignified living conditions and sufficient access to basic services. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data.
To ensure successful acceptance and implementation of the project, SYPD recently implemented CCM activities in the target locations and will continue to collaborate closely with various stakeholders including local authorities, host communities and IDPs.

1. - Projects activities

Project activities:1. Improve living conditions of displaced people through site development, care and maintenance and decongestion initiatives.To achieve this, SYPD will:- Provide training on-site maintenance and site planning to camp committees. Camp coordination and management will be strengthened in targeted locations through provision of trainings to camp committees on maintenance and planning of camp activities. The trainings will build the capacity of camp committees and will enhance continuous upkeep of sites in a bid to minimise protection risks and ensure safety for displaced people, particularly women, girls and people with disabilities.- Implement emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites (flood mitigation, fire prevention); these include street lighting, site layout and management, drainage systems, firebreaks, hand washing points.- Administer joint stakeholder site decongestion activities allowing for short-term improvements in settlement standards, minimizing protection threats and reducing COVID-19 transmission.- SYPD will conduct regular safety audits aimed at monitoring and addressing female risks in each target site.2. Strengthen safe access to multi-sectorial services at the site level through improved sitemanagement and coordination.- Gaps and capacity building needs within existing IDP governance structures will be identified and strengthened through capacity building exercises tailored to the identified gaps. - SYPD will support the governance structures through provision of community spaces for management committees and ensure the operation of timely and responsive feedback and complaint mechanisms.- In sites where SYPD is not yet involved, a mapping exercise of existing community governance structures will be conducted. SYPD will then oversee the establishment of Camp Management Committees (CMCs) and integration of new members while ensuring that the committees are inclusive.- In collaboration with other clusters, local authorities and partners, SYPD will coordinate efficient use of referral pathways for protection and service delivery to people with special needs.3. Strengthen community self-management and access to information for displacedpopulations.To ensure that governance structures serving the needs of displaced populations are in place, SYPD will:- Identify and support existing and/or new governance structures while ensuring involvement and participation of all populations, i.e., IDP and host communities- Establish/ support mechanisms for communication of multi-sector services available in IDP sites in Somali Project indicators:- Number of sites with established CCCM mechanisms; target – 110- Number of IDPs with an emphasis on women, persons with disabilities (PwDs) and individuals from minority clan affiliated groups benefiting from site-risk identification and site improvement activities; target -99,000- Number of people with equitable access to information on availability of services and inclusive CMC structures; target – 99,000

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1 Although the risk level is low, SYPD will ensure that all groups in the communities are engaged in various phases of project implementation from need identification, design, implementation, monitoring and handover to ensure inclusion. - SYPD will promote the participation of beneficiaries by establishing and strengthening project committees and building their capacity through training on gender mainstreaming and resources support. 4.2 - SYPD will identify areas in and around camps that could pose potential threats and monitor them regularly at different times of the day, these might include areas around showers, pit latrines, child-friendly spaces, schools and routes to schools, health facilities and water collection points. - SYPD will ensure that adequate sources of lighting such as solar lights are in areas leading to distribution points and common areas. Female-only FGDs will be carried out to enable women to identify areas that pose risks or areas where lighting will increase access and mitigate GBV threats.- SYPD will safeguard against protection risks against women and girls by ensuring that all services such as item distributions are carried out during the day, in locations close to IDPs residences and clearly guarded with crowd control mechanisms to avoid theft, intimidation and gender-based violence. Where men and women are all involved in the collection of items, SYPD will keep separate lines for women and men. 4.3 Care will also be taken to ensure children are not exposed to hard work or exploitation by having to carry heavy items. Each child-headed household will be assigned a guardian during the collection of supplies to avoid exclusion and abuse.- SYPD will always adapt and observe the Do-No-Harm principles to prevent unintended negative consequences.- To ensure safety and security measures are in place, SYPD will establish patrol groups to aid in the prevention of forced recruitment and GBV. 4.4 To minimize the risk of exposure to violence or indiscriminate attack from armed actors, SYPD will:- Monitor high-risk zones regularly and at different times of the day- Ensure access routes and locations to camps and camp-like settings are safe by installing adequate lighting or alternatives such as solar lamps and/or torches. 4.5 SYPD will also ensure to consult the host community, where applicable to identify any assistance needed and will consider providing the host community access to camp services and assistance if need be to avoid or reduce community tensions between camp and local/host communities. - Care will be taken to ensure that conflicting groups are not placed together during registration/distribution activities.- Mechanisms for dialogue will be between camp leaders and camp committees to ensure beneficiaries are fully involved in decision making relating to the development of campsites, site closure and management of the site.- Camp safety committee will be established to identify safety risks within the camp settlement for instance landmines, explosive remnants of war and busy roads.
