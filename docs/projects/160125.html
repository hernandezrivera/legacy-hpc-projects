Project Summary

This project aims at providing integrated lifesaving WASH facilities and services for vulnerable and affected populations in Duk County, Jonglei State. Interventions will include integrated WASH in health response to control WASH-related disease outbreaks at the wider community level; provision of timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for the IDPs, host community and returnees; and, mitigating WASH-related gender-based violence. The project is, therefore, in line with three WASH Cluster Objectives, CO2, CO3 and CO4. This project will be targeting 25,000 vulnerable beneficiaries (IDPs, returnees and host communities), that is, 6,250 women, 7,500 girls, 5,000 men and 6,250 boys in Duk County. The basic inter/multi-sectoral WASH needs of IDPs, new returnees and Host communities in this project will be met through an integrated lifesaving WASH response in a dignified and culturally appropriate manner to reduce the high WASH severity levels in Duk County. These will specifically be achieved through comprehensive assessment and rehabilitation of dysfunctional water points in locations with high needs and severity levels, facilitating construction of latrines for vulnerable households targeting high-density population areas with high risk of communicable diseases, prevalent open defecation and lowest ratios of HH/latrines using Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST), rehabilitation of institutional latrines especially in schools, health facilities, market places, and hygiene promotion – awareness raising to create good sanitation and hygiene practices through promotion of Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) where appropriate and PHAST methodologies through trained community hygiene promoters and facilitators, and provision of WASH NFIs, hygiene kits and menstrual hygiene management kits to vulnerable persons among the targeted groups. Gender equality and prevention of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) will be mainstreamed in all the project activities including facilitating the formation of gender-balanced Water Management Committees (WMCs) to manage water resources and participatory risk-reduction activities to mitigate WASH-related GBV risks. WWoH has a static presence in Duk County with field office located in Duk Padiet and has been operational in Duk County for over a decade with competent and experienced staff that will be rapidly deployed to respond to the needs. The organization currently has on-going Embassy of Japan-funded WASH project and FAO-funded Food Security and Livelihoods project in Duk County. WWoH has built very good rapport and excellent community goodwill, local state ministries linkages, strong local networks and coordination with the WASH Cluster. WWoH will conduct baseline KAP survey in the target location to identify the knowledge, attitude and practices of the communities while a detailed need assessment will be conducted to assess the access of the communities to safe water sources and sanitation facilities. The project implementation strategy will take into account specific gender needs during the surveys to ensure the equitable participation of all gender and age groups. The Sphere Protection Principles will be applied especially those related to Do No Harm and ensure people’s access to impartial assistance. WWoH will ensure a multi-sectoral approach through inter-cluster synergies, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including Gender Based Violence/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, disability inclusion, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability. The proposed intervention is, therefore, part of WWoH’s wider programme, which integrates WASH, Nutrition, Health and Food Security.

1 - Needs

South Sudan’s population continues to reel from the effects the protracted political and economic crisis. This situation, coupled with a longstanding environment of poverty, poor infrastructure and food insecurity, has caused mass displacement. This has occurred in an atmosphere where WASH coverage is also still very low at 41% coverage with basic water supply, 11% coverage with basic sanitation and with 63% of the population still practicing open defecation (UNICEF-WHO 2019 Joint Monitoring Programme). The situation is further compounded by the lack of sustainable operational and maintenance systems for existing WASH facilities and infrastructure as a result of governance challenges. About 170,000people (89%) in Duk County are predicted to be in crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in the first quarter of 2020 (Integrated Phase Classification, IPC Updates, August 2019). WASH Cluster Severity Analysis indicates that Duk County is in level 3 with 63,830 people in need in terms of poor wellbeing (affected by WASH related health issues including respiratory, Acute Water Diarrhoea (AWD), cholera, eye infection etc.) while 27,005 people are in need in terms of poor living standards (lack access to improved water source and WASH NFIs). Poor food security in Duk County, coupled with poor WASH practices is leading to high rates of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels( over 15%) among children under 5 years. Floods caused by heavy rains have worsened the living conditions of the communities in Jonglei State including Duk County in the past few months inundating shelters, water sources, crops and crucial productive assets in many areas (OCHA South Sudan Situation Report, October 2019). The critical needs resulting from flood effects include access to safe drinking water, anti-malarial and other basic drugs, and plastic sheets to be used as temporary shelters for families with damaged or destroyed houses. The degradation or loss of crops and other sources of livelihoods is likely to cause longer-term humanitarian needs. The high water level in some flooded areas is constraining vulnerable people’s physical access to basic services (OCHA: South Sudan Seasonal Flooding Snapshot, October 2019). The REACH WASH Assessment report released in August 2019 revealed that although there has been improved borehole coverage in the county (99.8% of HHs have borehole access), 20% still continue to collect water from unsafe sources, including surface water (river/pond/canal). 60% of HHs also regularly collect less than the SPHERE standard and 35% of HHs spend over 30 minutes round trip to fetch drinking water. Of those using unsafe water sources, 80% do not treat water before drinking. Knowledge on water treatment continues is very low as 60% of respondents lack knowledge about water treatment tablets, and 80% of respondents did not have the tablets at the time of the survey. While more than 50% of those surveyed had a trained pump mechanic in their village, approx. 70% of respondents reported that their local hand pump had broken down in the last 6months, and 85% mentioned that it can take between 1-3weeks for the hand pump repaired. 90% of the people practice open defecation in the bushes surrounding their homesteads due to lack of latrines. Hand washing and food hygiene practices in Duk County are low due to poor personal hygiene, prevailing social norms and a lack of awareness on importance of hand washing and food hygiene. All of these factors exacerbate the population’s vulnerability to cholera and diarrheal diseases passed through faecal routes. Lack of access to adequate sanitation in Duk County pose protection risks for women and girls with the search to find a safe place to defecate making them exposed to the threat of violence and harassment.

2 - Activities

OUTPUT1–Improved sustainable and equitable access to safe and sufficient water supply:1.1:Rehabilitation of 60 Hand pumps (HPs) including for schools and health units to improve access to safe water;detailed mapping of the functional and non-functional HPs in the area to be conducted to data used to strategize the rehabilitation of existing non-functional HPs.An inclusive community consultation will be held to ensure that protection considerations are included in the rehabilitation plan. Disinfection and shock chlorination conducted for Boreholes affected by floods.1.2: Support formation of WMCs (50% women) for all the rehabilitated HPs,trained in repair,operation & maintenance,and given a toolkit and spare parts to ensure effective and efficient operation and maintenance of the water points long after project phase out to ensure sustainability.OUTPUT2–Improved hygiene practice2.1:Conducting hygiene promotion training for 15 female and 10 male in each of the six payams of Duk County to serve as Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs); trained on participatory hygiene and sanitation promotion sessions, water quality monitoring and bucket chlorination.2.2:WASH in schools-teacher training and hygiene promotion will be carried out in12 schools to encourage behavioural change through child-to-child exchange.WASH friendly schools will be promoted by conducting activities and campaigns on the benefits of hand washing, the Safe Water Chain and proper waste disposal.Student Hygiene Clubs in 12 schools formed and trained to practice and promote the hygiene behaviour at school and community level.Children will work as agent of change while community elders will be included in the hygiene campaign through PHAST approach.2.3:WASH NFI Kits distributed during hygiene promotion activities. Key practices such as hand washing, safe water storage and treatment, food hygiene; the importance of latrine use and others will be emphasized by CHPs with the support of WMCs, including messaging at distribution sites. Messages on the use of items provided in the distributed hygiene kits will also be passed out. Participatory Rural Appraisal (PRA) tools will be used to motivate communities to adopt improved hygiene practices.Kits will be procured through the WASH Cluster pipeline and will focus on HH level water treatment and storage, as well as hygiene.2.4:Gender-sensitive WASH Kits (dignity kits) distributed to vulnerable women and girls with specific WASH needs,those identified as having protection risks,as well as SGBV survivors.2.5:Establishment and installation of hand washing facilities.OUTPUT3–Increased access to sanitation services3.1:Latrines in12 schools,12 health units and6 markets rehabilitated, segregated by gender including design improvements to ensure safety, security, privacy and dignity of users especially women and girls and provision of a separate room for Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM).The latrines will be accompanied by hand washing stands, soap, and a distribution of sanitary pads to girls of menstrual age.3.2:Facilitate construction of latrines for vulnerable households targeting high-density population areas with high risk of communicable diseases, prevalent open defecation and lowest ratios of HH/latrines using PHAST.Latrine slabs and technical support will be provided to the beneficiaries.Each selected HH will be responsible for providing local materials to increase sense of ownership.CHPs will sensitize households on use and maintenance of latrines,including decommissioning.A gender and protection ‘audit’ will be undertaken at the start of the project (and refreshed on a quarterly basis) to assess the impact that project activities are having on the lives of women and girls.All latrines will be constructed to include protection features including locks.3.3:As part of sanitation strategy,WWoH will launch Open Defecation Free (ODF) campaigns in Duk County,ensure to declare at least 6 ODF villages through the CLTS approach.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Wadeng Wing of Hope (WWoH) adopts human rights-based approach to all our programming and therefore fully recognizes the legality of the beneficiaries’ rights as enshrined in the human rights law and the international humanitarian law. All WWoH’s programs focus on the realization of the rights of all beneficiary categories particularly women and children, and are guided by the human rights and child rights principles. Our programs focus on developing the capacities of duty-bearers at all levels as well as the capacities of rights-holders to claim their rights. In addition, WWoH recognizes and applies, in all our humanitarian interventions, the Sphere Standard’s Humanitarian Charter which provides the ethical and legal backdrop to the Protection Principle, the Core Humanitarian Standard and the Minimum Standards. A special focus of the project will be on the rights of the women, children and the elderly to be protected from the worst forms of physical and sexual violence, child abuse and child labour. The project will keep in perspective a rights-based approach to social protection and fight exclusion and discrimination against marginalized groups. This means the project will focus community dialogues on protection risk factors and GBV prevention measures through community gatherings and meetings that are socially inclusive and engaging local leadership. WWoH takes a serious and proactive approach to ensure all of its activities are safe for community targets. This approach is outlined in its Beneficiary Protection Policy, which holds staff and volunteers to a high standard of responsibility for ensuring the safety and protection of the beneficiaries with whom they interact as well as upholding the rights of the beneficiaries in the implementation of project activities. The organization has a PSEA policy alongside a child safeguarding policy to prevent harm to women and children.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Internally displaced persons (IDPs) and Returnees are among the most vulnerable groups facing poor WASH and will be one of the priority target beneficiaries for this project. This project targets 11,100 IDPs and 2,700 returnees in Duk County which collectively form 46% of the project’s targeted beneficiaries. WWoH will involve the displacement-affected population to participate in project activities from inception to project close out, for instance, 40% of the members of WMCs or CHPs will be IDPs and/or Returnees while those with relevant skills to carry out certain activities including latrine construction will be given priority as long as they will exhibit ability and the skills to productively participate in direct implementation of the project activities. The project activities will be conducted near IDPs and Returnees settlement areas to ensure ease of access to training and subsequent rehabilitated water points and sanitation facilities. Project data will be disaggregated by sex, age, disability and residency in order to measure the level of participation of IDPs and returnees in every stage of the project.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

WWoH will implement this project in collaboration with the Ministry of Water and Irrigation at national level as well as work in collaboration and will support the line ministries and departments at the state and county level in ensuring that the target populations have access to safe and clean water, adequate sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion. WWoH will also work in collaboration with the Ministry of Agriculture, the Ministry of Health, etc. WWoH has on-going WASH project in Duk County which will enable the proposed project to benefit from progress already made especially in relation to linkages and collaboration with state and county government as well as local efforts. Work with state and county line ministries presents the project with an added advantage to utilize government extension capacity and other resources provided or supported by on-going projects. The community leadership is an important stakeholder in this project as it provides much needed political will to implementation of humanitarian activities. The leadership also supports in providing security and other requirements for conducive environment that allows smooth implementation of the project activities. The project will be linked with local community level structures including clan elders, youth groups and women groups. The community level protection committee and project implementation Committee will be engaged in the whole project cycle to get actual needs, priorities and actions for a long-term durable solution.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is aligned to the South Sudan HRP 2020 Strategic Objectives as well as the WASH Cluster Objectives CO2, CO3 and CO4. Under the United Nations Cooperation Framework (UNCF) 2019 – 2021 in South Sudan, the project will contribute to the Outcome 3 ” strengthening social services”. The proposed intervention is also aligned to the overall water and sanitation objectives of the Government of the Republic of South Sudan according to the Water Policy of 2007 which seeks to improve access to safe water supply and sanitation facilities and promoting hygiene education for all people in rural areas. The proposed intervention is complimentary to the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation and the Directorate of Rural Water and Sanitation on achieving increased access to water and sanitation coverage in a context of limited resources.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The current protection environment in Duk County is safe and stable, and this is largely attributed to the positive effect of the signing of R-ARCISS and the on-going efforts to form a transitional government of national unity. WWoH will conduct routine protection risk assessments and risk monitoring to understand and adapt to the evolving protection concerns during project implementation. The project will prioritize beneficiary safety and dignity in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’; non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, boys, youth, women and men. WWoH practices safe programming across all its projects through training of project staff to be able to engage with communities in identifying protection issues for the different gender and age groups. Awareness raising of communities is carried out in order to enable them to understand protection issues that can affect the different people by gender, age and displacement status. WWoH will incorporate protection in the programme including water points will only operate during daylight hours and locating latrines close to households, ensuring they are gender segregated and lockable and designed to ensure privacy. Safety audits will also be conducted for the WASH NFIs and hygiene kits that will be distributed to the beneficiaries.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

WWoH will put in place a number of mechanisms to ensure the overall sustainability of the result and project impact will go beyond the lifespan of the project. These mechanisms will include utilizing participatory approaches involving all stakeholders wherever possible – including local authorities, and the communities themselves. Beneficiary identification, sensitization, mobilization will be initiated through community participatory approaches, and project activities will be fine-tuned based on the results of community-based participatory planning. In order to increase ownership of the activities and the likelihood of replication and sustainability, the project will implement the WASH activities through PHAST, CHAST and CLTS approaches as well as undertake participatory monitoring and performance assessment which will be key for the success of the project and building of local capacity at community levels. Special efforts will be made to incorporate stakeholders at all levels to ensure that they contribute to and benefit from knowledge-sharing. The relevant line ministries and departments both at the state and county level will be involved to in key activities such as capacity building and training of community-based Water Management Committees (WMC). A dedicated monitoring, evaluation and learning component will be constituted to inform a learning agenda which will specifically target the uptake of knowledge and practices by relevant stakeholders. Special attention will be given to the participation of women in order to better their socio-economic conditions through improving their knowledge and skills improved WASH management and practices. This will also help in promoting a positive image of women in the community. Strong focus on institutionalization of tools, protocols and analysis, through awareness raising among decision-makers and practitioners, capacity development activities (e.g. blended learning), and technical support to relevant actors.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

WWoH will conduct routine gender and protection risk assessments and risk monitoring to understand and adapt to the evolving protection concerns during project implementation. 1) To mitigate the risk of non-inclusion of women and girls in consultations, the project will strictly ensure vulnerable groups are equally represented in community mobilization events and local committees such as WMCs and CHPs hence empowering women to influence local decision making on resource allocation. 2. To mitigate risk of exposure to sexual violence, the project will conduct: a) refresher training of WWoH staff on PSEA to enhance awareness b) risk analysis to identify factors that expose women and girls to sexual violence and take appropriate protection measures such as setting up rehabilitation of water points and latrines, and distribution sites for WASH NFI taking into consideration timing, security and centrality of the sites. c) put in place complaint and feedback mechanisms, in particular, help desk, at implementation sites to provide safe and secure avenue for women and girls to encourage them to report on any act of abuse and seek for assistance. 3. Targeting and beneficiary selection risk is highly likely, given the overall weak governance system in the targeted areas and the large size of the proposed beneficiary caseload. The project will ensure: a) targeting process is sufficiently decentralized down to Boma level and that targeting committees are established with fair representation across the community; b) targeting criteria are jointly developed with the community taking into account vulnerability and that the community is sufficiently sensitized on the criteria; c) the project conduct verification of the targeting process before approving the final list. 4. To mitigate non usage of complaint and feedback mechanisms risk, the project will conduct sensitization so beneficiaries are aware of the availability, location and reporting of complaint and feedback mechanisms.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed project has mechanisms by which target community members especially the most vulnerable including the elderly, PWDs, PLWs, children can voice their needs and preferences and demand inclusive processes. The project will facilitate community feedback mechanisms including setting up of help desks at distribution centers for WASH NFIs, AAP contact person and conducting monitoring and assessments to gather community views and concerns regarding project services and procedures. Community members will be encouraged to give feedback on various aspect of the project including beneficiary selection procedures, access to information, distribution sites and time, entitlements and conditions of rehabilitated water points and latrines , WASH NFIs distribution procedures, hygiene promotion as well as on issues related to sexual abuses. The complaints and feedback mechanism gives community a safe and effective avenue to express their views and find solutions for issues that affect their interest. The project will ensure all information communicated to the target community is appropriate and tailored to local specific circumstances. The information will be delivered in local languages and will be culturally and gender sensitive. Printed information will take into account the high level of illiteracy rate and will be presented in pictorial forms.
