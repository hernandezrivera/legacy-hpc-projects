Project Summary

Under the Outcome 2 of the national WaSH Sector, AVSI aims to provide access to minimal standards of water and critical hygiene items to vulnerable people in Nabatiyeh district. The project targets six vulnerable cadasters in Nabatiyeh district where 16,400 people live, including 13,285 host community members, 2,952 Syrians, and 164 PRL and PRS. The intervention will meet urgent and unmet WASH needs in the area where 23 schools operate and a total of 9,600 direct and 7,800 indirect beneficiaries will be targeted. The proposed intervention includes two main activities:
1- The installation of a 138.6 KWp and 130.9 KWp PV solar systems in two pumping stations (Habbouch Chreifeh well, Harouf Sekran well) in line with the SOP endorsed by the national WaSH sector and the specifications set by the LWE. The solarization of these two pumps is expected to ensure more consistent power supply and to increase the quantities of water available to households in the two municipalities of intervention, currently under-served. AVSI will carry out the installation of the PV systems through a national contractor being selected through a national tender and continuously monitored by a third party technical consultant. AVSI will also ensure community engagement by creating local committees in each municipality. The local committees will be engaged in the implementation process to exercise ownership over the PV systems after the handover.
2- The provision of Menstrual Higiene Kits containing locally produced reusable sanitary pads to adolescent girls and women in reproductive age in the targeted cadasters. The project also includes interactive awareness activities on the prevention of SGBV, PSEA, hygiene, menstrual health, and the best use of water, which will take place in women safe spaces in collaboration with Primary Health Care centers, schools, NFE, and social community centers. AVSI will also ensure linkages with other NFE centers and child protection actors to improve school readiness and support case management interventions.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Output 2.1: To ensure vulnerable populations have access to sufficient, reliable, and equitable water services, AVSI will install PV solar systems at the Habboush and Harouf pumping stations in accordance with SLWE standards. The municipalities of Habboush and Harouf have provided public land for the installation, and AVSI has collaborated with the SLWE and the responsible in Nabatiyeh and South Governorates to finalize the intervention plan. Output 2.3: To ensure that vulnerable girls and women have secured access to critical hygiene items and adopt sustainable and socially responsible WASH behaviors, AVSI will distribute menstrual hygiene kits to adolescent girls and women in reproductive age in the targeted areas. The kits will include reusable sanitary pads, as an economically sustainable alternative to potentially dangerous non-sanitary alternatives. Consultation with the GBV sector and UNFPA guidelines will ensure the kits contain key hygienic products, and 1000 females (including 4% PWD) will benefit from two rounds of distribution. AVSI will partner with Roof&Roots initiative to provide locally produced reusable pads and create a sustainable and inclusive distribution network. Awareness activities, coupled with hygiene kits distributions, will be held in women safe spaces in collaboration with healthcare centers, schools, and social community centers to promote SGBV and PSEA prevention, hygiene, menstrual health, and responsible water use.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

As mitigation strategy, AVSI was trained to include WaSH related conflict-sensitive programming to avoid discrimination in service provision, including intervention planned to ensure equitable access to water, transparency over beneficiaries’ selection criteria (demographically proportional and vulnerability-based) and dissemination of a clear Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM). The outreach includes a mapping of relevant gateways trusted by women and girls, a tailored strategy to reach out to the most vulnerable and excluded HHs, and a plan for discussing participation modalities with girls and women, highlighting that the activities are planned to favor inclusion and accessibility (location, timing, privacy). Implementation of SEAH/GBV prevention and mitigation strategies: PSEAH training to all staff, and stakeholders; Coordination with GBV service providers to ensure synergies/referrals pathways.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Under the supervision of the MEAL Manager and the Accountability Officer, AVSI will develop an AAP framework that includes a Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM) and several tools such as a hotline, field visits, focus group discussions, and referrals to ensure accountability and respect commitments in leadership/governance, transparency/information sharing, feedback and complaints, participation, and design, monitoring, and evaluation. The accountability officer will distribute flyers and cards with the hotline number to beneficiaries, conduct field visits and FGDs, engage beneficiaries in the design and evaluation stages, and follow up on feedback and referrals. AVSI will ensure "do no harm" principles are upheld by respecting privacy and dignity during the distribution of menstrual hygiene kits and coordinating with other partners in education and child protection sectors. A PDM will be conducted after the first round of distribution to capture beneficiaries' views.
