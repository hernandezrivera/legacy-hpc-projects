Project Summary

The proposed intervention will support farmers with farm inputs such and crop, vegetable, fisheries kits and livestock related interventions accompanied by related capacity building. The intervention will target the most vulnerable including women, single-headed households, sick and persons with disability in IPC 3+ locations where NRC has static presence such as Norther Bahr El Ghazel, Western Bahr El Ghazel, Warrap, Jonglei and Unity States. The input support will be through both FAO pipeline and NRC’s other funding mechanisms. Where markets are functional cash and market-based interventions such as direct purchases and or seed and tool fairs will be sought as informed by respective market assessments. These activities will be complemented by capacity building/training modules using various extension approaches including FFS and demo plots, farmer field days, lead farmer in the specific livelihoods opportunities so as to improve the targeted farmer production, productivity capacities and improve yields and returns. The fisheries training will enable target beneficiaries utilize their kits in the proper way and also improve their knowledge and skills in fish processing, preservation and marketing. The crops and vegetable production training will be based on the crop production seasonal calendar which includes land preparation, planting weeding, pest and disease management, harvesting, post-harvest management and marketing. Using the training of trainers (ToT) approach, the intervention will target 12,500 crop/vegetable farmers and 4,500 fisher folk for assorted livelihoods inputs support as well as training and engaging community based extension workers to provide the technical support to these farmers while they replicate the training to the other beneficiaries. The proposed interventions will also train targeted households with trainings on approved agronomic practices, pests and disease control, post-harvest handling, processing and value addition as well as linkages to markets and other value chain actors In addition, 7,500 HHs will be supported through unconditional/conditional cash transfer programming especially in flood and conflicted affected areas. The action will also target 1,500 HHs with small ruminant (goat/sheep) or poultry restocking through livestock voucher modality, 750 women, youth and men will be engaged in VSLAs/IGAs while 450 HHs will be engaged in natural resource management and climate adaptation activities.
To ensure integration the intervention will target nutrition & health centers, schools and mother garden’s locations conveniently with/without of access and proximity of a water source. Additional community gardens will be promoted at locations such as schools, health facilities where they will be jointly tended by students and community volunteers. The FSL interventions will be designed taking into consideration and incorporating mainstreaming of protection, safety, GBV prevention, Prevention of Sexual Abuse & Exploitation and conflict sensitivity analysis. The project teams will work closely with local authorities to identify persons with disabilities both men and women so that they are supported. This number is not expected to exceed 5% of the total households targeted.
All agricultural and fisheries activities will be implemented in collaboration with the State Ministry of Agriculture in order to ensure that government extension workers benefit from and also contribute to the technical components that will be offered by the project by so doing building capacities that will continue to be utilized after project closure. Community based extension workers such as lead farmers will be trained to continue supporting other farmers during and after project closure

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating as a result of the economic crisis, repeated climatic shocks – primarily widespread flooding – and conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation – putting additional pressure on an already fragile country.According to the current IPC report, between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season with a likelihood of an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analyzed) falling in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). According to the report, the nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025. The driver of food insecurity across South Sudan have been significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding, dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation, high food prices), conflict and insecurity, low agricultural production, and the effects of the ongoing conflict in Sudan. Low agricultural production is mainly attributed to unpredictable and extreme weather patterns, poor access to quality seeds and use of traditional farming methods negatively impacted agricultural production, resulting in increased humanitarian food assistance needs while economic crisis is as a result of South Sudan’s economy experiencing a macroeconomic crisis that is characterized by worsening currency depreciation, high food prices and declining household purchasing power. The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage to water facilities, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, and intensified existing vulnerabilities of affected people, which include high poverty rates, limited access to basic services including health and education, high prevalence of disease outbreaks, and widespread displacement. Priority needs include food assistance, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), livelihoods and health.

distribution or locally through seed.inputs fairs• The proposed activities for the 2025 lean Season response in priority 3 counties will be for 6 months only (April to September) and will revolve around capacity building on improved agronomic practices, farm inputs support (crop and vegetable seeds, tools) as well as fisheries activities.• Poultry & shoat distributions through livestock voucher/seed fairs to supplement household diets and improve nutrition status especially among the malnourished children and pregnant and lactating mothers • Assessments, market surveys and provision of regular food security and market information• Market monitoring – biweekly collection of market data on prices, supply and availability on key food and non-food commodities including terms of trade on cereal/livestock and labor/livestock/cereal. Additional food security information provided will be on rainfall data, livestock and crop disease outbreak, locusts and other pests outbreak information Cluster Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks;• Support community seed security through seed fairs using locally sourced, seed multiplication strengthening and market functionality support systems• Training & capacity building of farmers on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training, VSLAs, new business development skills etc. • Crop disease control including training of farmers and surveillance on Fall army worm (FAW) and Desert Locusts control such as establishing Community based surveillance and Early Warning mechanisms• Implementation of local climate-sensitive agricultural and natural resource and disaster risk reduction such as training of farmers on land, water, forest and range users in sustainable land and water management practices and introduce alternative resource-based conflict resolution mechanisms through APFSs, tree planting, fuel efficient stoves, agroforestry • Training farmers on postharvest losses and introduction of appropriate preservation technologies such as grain/seed harvesting, storage and support with seed storage using traditional granaries and hermitic bags, low cost cultivation techniques, post-harvest management and market linkages• Supporting small scale farmers on group formation, value addition to their produce and linking them to markets and agro-input dealers and other value chain actors • Cash for Work will aim to support productive community assets
