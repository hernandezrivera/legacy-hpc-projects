Project Summary

The project aims to: (i) provide continued access to safe, quality and inclusive formal basic educational services for girls and boys in Cabo Delgado and Nampula provinces; (ii) provide students and teachers with required infrastructures and resources for education continuity in a conflict-affected context.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project focuses on school renovations and improvements to ensure that girls and boys, including children with disabilities are able to access school, with the distribution of learning materials and the provision of relevant learning opportunities to out of school children (OOSC). Priority investments focus on conflict-affected schools or where school infrastructure is most damaged. Integrated Child Friendly Spaces (CFS), and Temporary Learning Spaces are constructed in IDP resettlement camps to enable a holistic education-protection approach. Cash+ for education offers learning opportunities for children most at risk of dropping out especially girls and children with disabilities. The project also aims to increase wider EiE capacity through dedicated efforts to improve coordination and information in the sector, including contextualizing the Mozambique INEE minimum standards to Cabo Delgado and Nampula, training EiE partners in the use of key assessment tools such as the Joint Education Needs Assessment, and Rapid Education Risk Analysis. 1) Total: 40,604Students: 40,604 (20,104 F; 20,500 M), of which 5% are CWD.School staff and members (teachers, school boards, directors : 324 (120 F, 204 M)

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Support to Back to School campaign: The campaign focuses on identifying IDPs school aged children and OOSC with disabilities or special needs to be enrolled, sensitizing communities on the right to education, with trained community members advancing the enrolment of girls and CwDs. 2. Provision of minor rehabilitations to ensure safe learning spaces are available to children, including CwDs: Construction and/or rehabilitation of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS), based on a pre-accessibility audit to school facilities. 3. Train teachers on EiE (TiCC and CSE), school-based protection mechanism, and psychological first aid including provision of focus sessions on VAC, GBV and PSEA prevention: improve the quality of teaching and the ability of teachers to deal with complex situations through a psychosocial approach supporting children and adolescents to express their feeling and practice relaxation from distressful and traumatic experiences through creative arts and crafts. Teachers are able to detect protection risks, increasing their skills in communicating with children on problems that they do not usually discuss with adults, reducing the effects and consequences of trauma and/or stressful events. 4. Implementation the of comprehensive school safety: teachers and school administrators undertake training on school safety planning, child rights, disability-inclusive child safeguarding, Emergency Preparedness Plan, risk management, gender norms, and Social Emotional Learning. 5. Provision of emergency learning and teaching materials: All students in the supported schools are provided with basic learning materials according to their grade level as defined by the Education Cluster. Teacher´s kits include blackboard, chalk, literacy and numeracy teaching aids, as well as self-learning manuals and guides on participatory methodologies, inclusive education approaches as well as gender-sensitive pedagogy.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Gender focal points and committees will work together on screening, supporting and referring cases of girls and boys at risk of dropping out of school, early marriages and other forms of vulnerability. Regular meetings will be promoted to address issues of potential vulnerability and/or child protection risks.Schools’ Councils will be constantly involved through the promotion of workshops about tracking mechanisms. Finally, at district/provincial level, the involvement of the referral system and the multi-sectorial team in the screening, support and referral of cases of different risks will be put in place with the collaboration of the different actors in the area.In general, the whole project is grounded in a meaningful involvement of all the principal actors (local and international) that will allow an easier and smoother access to the participation of beneficiaries in all proposed actions. In particular, institutional capacities of multi-sector teams in terms of mechanisms for identifying, supporting and following up cases will be strengthened; regular meetings for the improvement of synergies among multi-sectorial teams will be guaranteed (clusters, planning sessions, joint studies and research, etc.); finally, community members will lead the planning and decision-making process, through locally established structures. The project will consider gender issues according to the following:a) In terms of pedagogical support, GBV’s training for teachers, activists and schools’ directors will be developed as well as the revitalization and training of gender focal points at the level of the DPE and SDEJTs. In general, lessons will take into account the inclusion of gender aspects, with no discrimination in the selection of the beneficiaries. Finally, all school kits resources are gender-sensitive. Robust SC-developed gender analysis and gender mainstreaming tools and training materials are used throughout program implementation.b) In terms of participation of children, girls at risk of early marriage will be targeted for participation in protection activities but may also be targeted for education activities to ensure they enroll in or remain in school. Ongoing gender-responsive consultations with program beneficiaries and key stakeholders (with a focus on women and girls) take part to ensure that their voices and opinions are collected and accounted for within the whole program cycle. c) School councils and school management Committees with members from the school and the local community will be trained in child rights violations with special attention given to girls’ rights and the rights of children with disabilities involved in the project. Sensitization campaigns and training will be promoted about gender, rights and child protection.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Targeted community members participate and provide feedback at all stages of program design, implementation, and evaluation.Regarding design, Monitoring and Evaluation, all beneficiaries, including children, women, people with disabilities and school teachers' representatives, are involved during the project’s baseline and endline studies, providing key input on satisfaction and quality indicators. Through participatory assessments, SC gains a comprehensive overview of the situation and protection concerns and needs and hears solutions proposed by the community members. Assessments are conducted methods such as focus group discussions (FGDs) key informant interviews (KIIs), brief pop-up surveys. Accountability to Affected Persons (AAP) is a key mechanism of engaging and involvement of affected communities in the action activities that ensures feedback and response toissues raised by communities. Both male and female community members and girls and boys are included in thisaccountability work, with training conducted on Disability-inclusive child safeguarding, PSEA, Child protection principles, child abuse and response mechanism and accountability to Affected persons for teachers and school-based communities. To ensure report, response and effective accountability by all stakeholders, linkages are created between child safeguarding and APP through community referral pathways and other relevant mechanisms such as child helpline and Linha Verde.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project actively identifies children with disabilities (CwDs) that are either out of school or placed in institutions within the catchment areas of schools. This aligns with the initiative from the government's Ministry of Education – Department of Special Education of massively identifying out-of-school CwDs to enroll them in the education system.Identification is assisted by local communities’ leadership and the social action services, including sensitizations of parents of girls and boys with disabilities. The project collaborates with the schools’ management, social actions services and CBOs, including networks and Organizations of Persons with Disabilities (OPDs) to identify and enroll CwDs from the catchment areas of schools supported by the project. Parents of CwDs are encouraged to form self-help groups, and project implementing partners convey messages on the importance of group learning and counselling through Radio and IEC. Learning materials and resources are adapted for CwDs and teachers, community leaders, and case workers are trained in how to work with students with disabilities effectively. To ensure participation, girls, boys, and CwDs are engaged in establishing school clubs' activities through meetings in schools and community spaces, with activities focusing on access to education, MPHSS, Psychosocial support services, Case management, referral, and SGBV awareness.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The CO is in the process of developing a strategy to reduce our environmental impact, primarily through two approaches: Maximising the use of sustainable / natural resources, and Reducing waste, CO2 emissions, carbon footprintExamples, for example, are: To work as locally as possible – limit flights from Maputo to Pemba and instead, when possible, work with staff in Pemba office. All construction projects will be preceded by environmental assessments to understand the impacts that the project could have and mitigating measures for risks that are identified. Where possible, building materials will be procured through sustainable means and we will work with supply chain with framework agreements with suppliers that prioritize reducing their environmental impact, and use of more environmentally friendly materials for building will be prioritized.Minimise as much as possible the use of plastics in office, meetings and while on trips, as well as use of wrappings, packaging, plastic bags etc. Provide clearly marked and fenced storage for waste generated in schools or communal areas. Work with local authorities and service providers to integrate where possible the waste management into already existing systems and infrastructure. If not possible, organise a system to remove waste regularly from designated public collection points, and organise periodic or targeted solid waste clean-up activities within schools and school environments in collaboration with the beneficiary community. Including DRR in curriculum could have lasting impacts on how children continue to think about how their actions impact the environment around them. In the immediate aspects of this project, schools will have the opportunity to assess their own school environment and understand how they can reduce their environmental impacts.
