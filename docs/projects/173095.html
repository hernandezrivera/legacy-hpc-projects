Project Summary

Strong and effective GBV Sub-sector coordination mechanism is required to ensure that accessible and safe services are available to survivors, and that prevention and mitigation mechanisms are put in place to reduce incidents of GBV. The GBV sub-sector coordination will focus on strengthen interagency multi-sectoral GBV coordination in partnership with national authorities, relevant UN entities and national and international NGOs. The project will also facilitate training workshops, strengthening of referral pathways and support strategy development. Moreover, it will include high level advocacy, assessments, analysis, safe sharing of data and preparedness planning. Finally the project will support inter-agency leadership on GBV, and build capacity on GBV mainstreaming across humanitarian sectors and clusters.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Overview Need (HNO) estimated 153,000 people are most at risk of GBV, requiring sustained prevention and response services including 30,000 displaced, 16,000 returnees, 26,000 non-displaced Libyans, 63,000 migrants and 16,000 refugees. Of the total, 90,000 are women (59 per cent) and 41,000 are girls (26 per cent). The significant gaps in GBV services across Libya means that health consequences, including serious mental health consequences, sexually transmitted infections, unplanned pregnancies, and other possibly life-threatening health complications remain unaddressed. Scaling up and establishing comprehensive GBV prevention and response services are urgently required, especially in geographic locations that lack any specialized GBV services and are affected by increased conflict and displacement, such as Alkufra, Murzuq, Sirt. Advocacy on establishing protective legal mechanisms for women and girls, to end impunity for perpetrators due to the legal loopholes and unlimited humanitarian access to detention facilities needs to be strengthened. Discrimination and violence based on sex continues to have a serious effect on the rights of women and young girls. Ongoing conflict, insecurity and displacement continue to put people, particularly women and girls, at increased risk of GBV, including harmful practices, sexual violence, trafficking and forced prostitution, which is exacerbated by collapsing infrastructure and a lack of adequate protection systems and services. Mental health remains a neglected need with limited available services and there are growing needs for structured psychosocial support, particularly in light of the prolonged effect of COVID-related confinement measures on children.Weak governance has resulted in public institutions and facilities suffering from limited qualified personnel, resources and inadequate upkeep. This has been exacerbated by regular attacks by armed groups, particularly against electricity and water infrastructure, as well as on hospitals and schools.The lack of ability of public services to provide for the population is exemplified by the near collapse of the health system due to the COVID-19 pandemic.The GBV Sub-sector is aiming at an effective action to mitigate and prevent GBV and promote survivors’ access to multi-sector services. Supporting GBVIMS in order to ensure information are collected ethically and safely and contributing to improving the quality and accountability of GBV program. The intention of the GBVIMS support is both to assist service providers to better understand the GBV cases being reported as well as to enable actors to share data internally across project sites and externally with other organization to facilitate broader trends analysis and improved GBV coordination. Moreover, potential GBV risks and vulnerable groups are identified through quality, gender-sensitive assessments and risk mitigation measures are put in place. Additionally, GBV risk mitigation and survivor support are integrated across humanitarian sectors at every stage of the Programme cycle. Also, referral systems are established to connect women, girls and other at-risk groups to appropriate multi-sector GBV prevention and response services in a timely and safe manner. Finally the Sub-sector is aiming that GBV survivors, including women, girls, boys, people with disabilities, children and men, access quality, multispectral GBV services , with an emphasis on clinical management of rape.

2 - Activities

Planned activities aim to strengthen the GBV-Sub-Sector coordination to ensure; that the GBV protection concerns of people in-need are addressed in a comprehensive, timely and well-coordinated manner as well as improved access to safe, confidential and coordinated GBV services in accordance to a survivor-centered approach. The activities include :- •Building a common understanding of GBV among stakeholders and raise awareness on the need for addressing it•Mainstreaming GBV protection at all level and sectors of the humanitarian response•Advocating with State institutions for the elaboration of national normative and public policies that properly address GBV•Strengthening data collection on GBV through GBVIMS + and use such evidence for advocacy and awareness-raising activities according to information management protocols•Knowledge sharing: preparing printed and visual materials on GBV protection•Capacity-building: strengthening the skills of counterparts, communities and State institutions to prevent and respond to GBV•Service delivery: strengthen the Case Management mechanism and, thus, ensuring GBV service delivery•Regularly implement needs assessment to adjust strategies and activities •Implement communication campaigns with key messages on GBV prevention and response to be spread through social networks, radios, TV•Monitoring and reporting on activities and needs

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

In order to address risks related to disclosure of sensitive personal data of affected persons, including safety and confidentiality of case management conducted in detention centers, GBVSS coordination will roll out trainings on GBV guiding principles and issues related to confidentiality of data targeting the member of the GBVSS. The roll out of GBV IMS training will also contribute to support members to collect and share data in very secure means while upholding high standers of confidentiality and do no harm. Regarding Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of rights violations and other protection incidents (GBV, child protection, Explosive Hazards, eviction for example), The GBV SS will organize awareness raising activities through it is member to ensure that communities area aware about the available services as well as regularly update the referral pathway for the GBV Survivors. Additionally, the coordination mechanism will ensure that safety audit is regularly conducted and follow up were made on the action points. Moreover, we will set up multi-sectoral safety audit teams to encourage joint ownership and accountability of emerging recommendations and action plans. Finally, all elements of GBV prevention, response, coordination and advocacy should adhere to survivor-centered approach i.e at minimum to adhere to the following guiding principles: -1-Safety: The safety and security of the survivor and others, such as her/his children and people who have assisted her/him, must be the number one priority for all actors.2-Confidentiality: People have the right to choose to whom they will, or will not, tell their story. Maintaining confidentiality ensures the survivors, witnesses and information sources are protected, and informed consent is obtained before action is taken.3-Respect: All actions taken should be guided by respect for the choices, wishes, rights and dignity of the survivor, and be guided by the best interests of the child4-Non-discrimination: Survivors of violence should receive equal and fair treatment regardless of their age, gender, race, religion, nationality, ethnicity, sexual orientation or any other characteristic
