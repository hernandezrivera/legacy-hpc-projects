Project Summary

The project will enhance the food security of the most affected communities through integrated climate-smart agricultural interventions and improve food self-sufficiency in NW Syria. Strengthening the resilience of rural host and IDP communities through enhancing their food security and income generation is an urgent need to face both food insecurity and climate change impacts. Therefore, the project is designed to deliver outputs relevant to local needs, build on local practices, and apply regenerative approach. It is designed according to the best coping strategies that preserve assets, create new opportunities, and provide the best solutions for food security and livelihoods in short and long term through the following main interventions:
1. Vegetable seedling production and distribution: A seedling production nursery will be established to produce 40,000 seedlings of the most cultivated vegetables including tomatoes, eggplants and pepper. The seedlings will be distributed to the 400 families of the beneficiaries of the home and community gardens (see intervention 2). The nursery will provide also training opportunities and sustainable solutions for quality vegetable seedlings production throughout and beyond the project.
2. Promoting food self-sufficiency: and supporting the resilience of farmers and displaced communities by applying the regenerative. 400 families (200 families from host communities and 200 IDP families) will have access to and be able to produce vegetables in small spaces available in their homes (home gardens). This intervention will allow families to use low-cost and sustainable food production such as using greywater, compost, local seeds etc., and will promote social integration and generate income by selling surplus products.
3. Empowering olive farmers: 400 olive farmers will be supported with agricultural inputs and olive products marketing to withstand low income and low marketing. Main agricultural inputs such as fertilisers and pesticides will be provided to the olive farmers. In addition, 6000 kg of table olives and 4000 litres of olive oil will be purchased and distributed to 400 of the vulnerable IDP families (15 kg of table olives and 10 litres of olive oil per family).
4. Rationalization of water use and transformation to modern irrigation: 200 vegetable farmers will be provided with drip irrigation systems to cultivate about 0.1 hectares of summer vegetables.
5. Strengthening the preservation of livestock: 400 livestock breeders will be supported by concentrated fodder (200 kg per breeder) and 50 kits of Azolla production. The project will promote the production and using alternative fodders such as Azola in the correct way and with technical support.
6. Delivering professional training on grafting and pruning of olive trees (50 farmers), home gardening applying the regenerative approach (50 households), drip irrigation (50 farmers) and Azolla production (50 livestock breeders).
7. Participation of stakeholders in the project and production of resources to raise agricultural knowledge through 5 participatory workshops with stakeholders at the project start, 5 field days in the project area, 20 podcast episodes and 10 videos on agricultural activities and best practices applied/learned by the project, documentation and sharing lessons learned, and a participatory workshop with FSL partners in Gaziantep.
The project promotes a participatory approach, sharing lessons learned during its design and implementation stages with the project beneficiaries, local communities, and NGOs for sustainable benefit.
The beneficiaries will be selected from the most affected people according to specific criteria such as vulnerable families, gender (women 40%), age (young people 40%), agricultural background, access to land, small size of livestock etc. The project will target around 10,000 direct (2.500 men, 2500 women, and 2000 boys and 3000 girls), in addition to 200 people with disabilities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The PRA could refer to some of the following risks: - Conflict related to the beneficiary selection. - Unstable security situation. - Conflict with local authorities. – Beneficiaries are not satisfied with the interventions. SAE's monthly beneficiary satisfaction surveys are disaggregated by gender and displacement status. SEA will ensure ‘Do No Harm’ principles are applied to identify and mitigate against potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the FSL sector strategy and related activities. SAE has built relationships of trust between the affected community and local authorities. The SAE will get written approval from the local authorities to implement project activities according to Humanitarian Principles. SAE also will participate in sharing the access sites in the OCHA access group to de-conflict humanitarian facilities. SAE designed the project following the SOPs of protection clusters. SAE will ensure that W&G have access to programming and give feedback on provided services. SAE ensures gender, age and disability inclusion through all stages of the response. SAE ensures access for PWD to protection services on an equal basis with others. SAE will encourage the recruitment of PWD. The project will not discriminate between M&F, IDPs & HC, or PWD BNF’s. Also, we will ensure that all activities will target IDPs, returnees, and host communities. In addition to enhancing and ensuring the equal participation of both genders.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:The proposed locations became not safe or present high spread of Covid-19 cases.The households aren't motivated to cultivate vegetables in the home/community gardens.The olive farmers can't sell their products (green olives & olive oil) in the local markets.The households can’t join the training due to concerns related to transportation or Covid-19.There are no skilled media producers in Syria. Assumptions:The proposed locations are accessible without any concerns.The local authorities are cooperative and support the project. The agricultural inputs are available in the local markets. The households are able to attend the training.The households are motivated to use home/community gardens.The olive farmers can sell their products (green olives & olive oil) in the local markets.There are skilled staff in Syria to produce the podcast and the extension videos. Mitigation measures:SAE will monitor and assess the security situation in the field closely and share the risks.Unavailable agricultural inputs in the local markets will be purchased from Turkey.SAE team will explain to the benefits the importance of food production in the home/community gardens and will encourage them to decide what they want to grow and to work together. The project will support the olive value chain and purchase 10000 kg of green olives and olive oil from the farmers.SAE have strong experience and will contract with skilled Syrian media producers in Syria and Turkey.
