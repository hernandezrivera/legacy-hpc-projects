Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to provide access to basic WASH services over 2023 in order to bring about more profound change for 302,724 IDPs, returnees, and overburdened host community at-risk groups in the highly neglected sub-district of the below-mentioned areas than is possible with short-term projects through 2023:
Quneitra/ rehab of water network
Ash-Shajara, Abu Kamal, Ma'arrat An Nu'man, Jaramana, Nashabiyeh /rehab of sewage network
Mansura, Da'el, Abu Kamal, Ein Halaqim, Masyaf, Sabe Byar /hygiene kits
DayrHafir/garbage collection

The consequences of the conflict and the ongoing cholera are particularly severe, as many parts of various governorates have been transformed into hard-to-reach areas where people suffer from a lack of essential water, sanitation, and hygiene due to the massive destruction of washing and sanitation facilities. Loss of livelihood and income, high cost of basic food items, and severe currency devaluation. IDPs, returnees, and overwhelmed host communities face massive conflict as they try to provide basic water, sanitation, and food for their families. In addition, particularly in northern and northeastern Syria, insufficient rainfall combined with historically low water levels in the Euphrates River have not only limited access to water for drinking and domestic use for more than 4.3 million people, but also increased waterborne diseases and additional protection risks. The ongoing cholera worsening the situation where 5 million faces heavy financial burden of purchasing water or hygiene supplies. Through WASH support, water, sanitation and hygiene conditions will be improved for 302,723 people in areas with (45% high displacement rates with severity 4-5), hygiene awareness is raised through the distribution of hygiene kits, and water and sanitation networks will be rehabilitated. Garbage collection and education campaigns are also will be conducted. RSRP will adopt door-to-door visits delivering modality, because this method is used due to COVID19, to avoid overcrowding during delivery and ensure the safety of beneficiaries. Exit strategy and sustainability includes mobilize the municipality and the community and to make them responsible for the maintenance of the sewage and water network. via door-to-door visits and distribution brochures, banners on instructions for use of the water and sanitation network,the importance of regular cleaning of the network,and the efficiency of sanitation as well as the hygiene on their health conditions Following an assessment of the WASH gap,needs,and timeline for intervention through 2023by the RSRP team, and the coordination with the stakeholder taking into account all vulnerable and gender groups, including men, women, boys and girls, female HHS, vulnerable elderly, persons with disabilities, and households with limited access to hygiene items and safe drinking water, and considering sector results, the number of kits, kit components, damaged water and sanitation network, and target locations were determined. In addition, the available budget has not yet been confirmed by the donor. It should be noted that the project is consistent with the 2023 HNO and the Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene Sector Strategic Objectives (SO1 and SO3) and priorities: Strategic Objective 1: Provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable, focusing on areas of highest need.
Strategic Objective 3: Strengthen the resilience of affected communities by improving
access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, particularly for the most vulnerable households and communities. The intervention areas may be increased according to the increase in the areas that will be under control in the years 2023.Moreover, 19% of budget already secured for planned 2023 WASH Activities.

3 - Needs

The conflict has resulted in approximately 13.5 million people requiring WASH assistance. 52% of the population relies on unsafe water sources and alternatives to piped water to meet daily water needs. An estimated 70% of wastewater is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewage systems are non-functional.HNO2023. According to the field assessment conducted and in coordination with the communities, the current needs amount to 31717 hygiene kits distributed through mobile team (7210 in Dar'a, 7000 in Deir Ezzor, 5000 in Damascus rural, 7507 in Hama and 5000 in Ar-raqqa). RSRP estimates that inflation in the market is high due to the exchange rate and has reached a value of 5000SYP/US. 80% of the people do not have access to hygiene items due to remoteness and high transportation costs. Rehabilitation of 1 water network in Quneitra. The average daily water consumption per person is 21-liters and depends on water trucking from polluted source worsening the situation due to spread of cholera and costs for water trucking reached 35,000 SYP. Rehabilitation of 5 sewage networks in Deir Ezzor, Dar'a, Rural Damascus, Idleb, where 65% of sewers have not been renewed for 10 years. Bottlenecks in many sewers causing an environmental and health disaster by mixing sewage and drinking water, as there are many cases of cholera and typhoid. Garbage collection campaigns to be conducted in Aleppo where there is no access to public garbage disposal services

4 - Implementation of activities

RSRP responds to improve access to WASH services for affected households in targeted areas. In line with the WASH sector, the 2023 HNO framework up to the sub-district level, and in compliance with the HRP strategic objectives (SO1 and SO3),RSRP plans to carry out the activities as mentioned in the project cost section, the main pillars to achieve the proposed activities in coordination with DoWR, STD, WASH sector and other relevant stakeholders:-approval in coordination with STD.-Hiring process-Announcement of registration for hygiene kits through local community and social media.-Identification of beneficiaries by KOBO according to vulnerability selection criteria, considering female HHs,vulnerable elderly, people with disabilities,HHs living in overcrowded conditions,HHs without income sources -Procurement process-Distribution of 31717 hygiene kits will be done.a PDM survey and safety audit will be conducted through a questionnaire on KOBO to determine beneficiary satisfaction. All in consideration of safety measures related to Covid19.-Conduct cholera awareness campaigns and hygiene promotion activities including distribution of brochures and SMS, focus group discussions.-Remediation of 5km sewage networks and 1.5 water network-Removal of 30000m3 of garbage through garbage collection campaigns.-Regular site visits to ensure the quality of implementation.-Accountability is ensured through the establishment of a complaint's mechanism and regular evaluation feedback

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PR1: Misconduct by humanitarian workers while realizing the activities. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation Measure: 1. Inform and train humanitarian workers on all aspects related to protection and develop strategies to address the risks of GBV and PSEA during all project-stages. 2. Selection of competent and professional hygiene volunteers and signing of a code of conduct by all humanitarian workers. 3.Establishment of an effective feedback and complaint mechanism. 4.Assigning PSEA focal point to deal with all PSEA cases that may occurred. PR2: Children below 18 years old conduct works on behalf of humanitarian organization, its partners or contractors. Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigation Measure: 1. Ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ laboure’s. 2.add zero tolerance disclosure on child labor in the contracts. PR3: Targeted communities including victims, do not report allegations of SEA, which results in RSRP being unable to refer the allegation to investigation, hold personnel to account and assist victims.Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation Measure:1. Train staff on PSEA policy and procedures. 2. RSRP will work on raise awareness of targeted communities about PSEA and how to report misconduct.
