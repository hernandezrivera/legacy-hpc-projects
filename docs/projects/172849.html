Project Summary

WHO project aims to decrease avoidable under five mortality and morbidity related to Severe acute malnutrition through improved access and quality of inpatient care for the Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) with medical complications in 139 stabilization centers currently Operating in health facilities. The project includes curative, preventive and promotive measures in the same time. The activities will address the clinical life-saving interventions in Stabilization centers targeting the patients/children with SAM and the mothers/caretakers through IYCF counseling and young children nutrition awareness to lower relapse and prevent SAM cases in the same households. The project activities and costing factored in 36 SCs are supported by NGOs. The SCs Supported by NGOs will require only Medical kits (1st and 2nd line medicines) and technical support.. Main activities: Training of the health and nutrition staff of 103 SCs (all except the ones Supported NGOs) to include IPD SAM initial training (7 days) and refresher Training (3 days), provision of guidelines, Job Aids and IEC materials to all participants from the 103 SCs. WHO will procure and distribute to 139 SCs; medicines (SAM inpatient Kits – first and secondary level care), , medical equipment and other supplies based on the needs identified together with MOH. To improve the quality of care and cure rate in SCs, WHO nutrition and public health officers present in all targeted states will conduct jointly with SMOH regular supportive supervisions in all targeted 139 SCs conducting on the job training, coaching, and adherence to the national treatment protocols. Routine nutrition surveillance at HF level/SCs, essential for trends and gap identification and effective planning of activities will be enhanced through training, provision of recording and reporting tools for the SCs in 11 targeted states, and better integration into HFs reporting mechanism to promote sustainability. WHO nutrition team will provide technical and operational support to the MOH at federal and state levels to regularly analysis the data of SAM inpatient care services (using the agreed performance indicators) to identify the trends, and challenges disaggregated by sex and age. The information will be disseminated to all relevant stakeholders through nutrition cluster bulletins, and bring during nutrition sector meetings led by MOH and UNICEF. Effective coordination of WHO with all partners (Nutrition, Health, food security) will promote an improved referral system between the SCs, TSFP, OTP, and community. Linkages with communities will be strengthened through raising awareness and social mobilization conducted by trained community mobilizers/CHW and MSG in coordination with other UN. The modalities and subjects for these activities will be guided by the community members suggestions collected through exit interview and focus group discussions during supervisions. The project activities also includes small repairs of 15 SCs in order to ensure better environment for work and improved hygiene in the SCs.

1 - Needs

Sudan continues to have high prevalence of global acute malnutrition, The national prevalence rate of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) was recorded at 14.1 percent places Sudan at the very high level for public health significance according to the WHO criteria, with 7 out of the 18 states have acute malnutrition prevalence above 15% at emergency threshold. There has been substantial attention to the provision of treatment services in conflict-affected states over the years, however, most Sudan’s acutely malnourished children (52 per cent) live in nine non-conflict affected states. In reference to the HRP2020 addendum , the high malnutrition rates likely to be exacerbated by COVID-19 and the economic crisis. 522,000 children under five are severely malnourished, and some 2.2 million children are moderately malnourished (MAM). The economic challenges faced by Sudan since 2018 further destabilized the food security and nutrition status especially amongst the most vulnerable. The projections for 2021 HRP show that about 85,931 children suffering of SAM with medical complications will need in-patient care. Without specialized care, the risk of death for these children is extremely high. The target set for 2021 set by nutrition sector around 49,960 SAM with medical compilations. In spite of some progress in regard with access to, and the coverage of nutrition services over the last assessment for the TFU/SC situation in Sudan result (WHO & MOH 2019) came up with a many gaps in regards with access to SAM inpatient care services including capacity, quality of the services/staff qualification, availability of free of charge medicines, routine IYCF counselling for the mothers, and the quality of reporting and monitoring of SCs. As per the data received at the FMoH database, as of October 2020, 19282 SAM inpatient children (with medical complications) were treated (FMoH report) in all SCs. This shows that the geographical coverage of SAM treatment services remains low, especially for the inpatient treatment. The main causes are; unavailability of proper outreach services to early detect the cases, insufficient financial and human resources investment by the Government and Humanitarian Community for scaling up SAM services through integration into selected PHC health facilities and all hospitals; poor access due to insecurity and remoteness in some of the most affected areas; insufficient health staff trained and skilled, and high turnover for SC staff. •Technical support for the State and localities for the management and coordination of SAM inpatient care ensured by the WHO in Khartoum and targeted states. •Procurement and distribution of 3996 WHO SAM kits (first and secondary medicines ) for 139 SCs, and equipment for 139 TFUs as requested by MOH and partners The budgetary calculation excluded the costs of therapeutic milk and RUTF for 139 SCs provided by UNICEF. • Repairs of 15 SCs in 5 Darfurs, WN, BN, RN, KHR, 3 Kordofans and 3 Eastern states to improve the infection prevention, and optimization of the work environment •Establishment of 5 Centers of Excellence and Professional training for SAM inpatient care in 5 states initially. •Capacity building for the SCs including staff and procurement• Support the IYCF counseling for the mothers and care taker in all targeted SCs (139)• Joint supportive supervisions with MOH (every 3 months), aiming to improve the adherence to the treatment protocols. •Nutrition surveillance in all states with focus on Eastern, Darfurs, WN, BN, Kordofans and Khartoum states: training, reporting and recording tools, analysis of disaggregated data for dissemination to identify the trends and challenges. •Strengthen the linkages with communities through social mobilization in coordination with other UN agencies and NGOs , referral and treatment. • Contribute to the development of Sudan Multi-sectoral nutrition plans with related sectors (include Regional and HQs support)

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental protection of biological and chemical contamination is part of accountability to affected population. Working with patients who mostly suffer from communicable diseases creates real risks for staff, other patients, care=takers, environment and thus the communities. WHO will support and reinforce strict adherence to Universal Precautions (as per WHO/MOH guidelines) through provision of necessary materials such as safety boxes, self-degradable disinfectants, and personal protection equipment. In collaboration with WASH and Health partners support for availability and use of incinerators, pits for sharps, hand washing facilities in each SCs/HFs will be provided to ensure the protection of the staff, patients, care-takers, environment and communities. During each supervisory visit the environmental protection will be checked and the staff will be given orientation during the on job training. The training will include message on environmental protection from biological contamination as part of safe waste disposal and improved community level sanitation. Expired medicines will be destroyed (incineration) as per WHO guidelines and in the presence of health authorities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHO has a protection mainstreaming policy that all staff in the field have been trained in, at eld level WHO partners with organizations whose programming is wholly protection focused to ensure that beneficiary protection needs are properly addressed. Equally, there is a country child protection focal person who gives the child protection focal points remote support, this ensures WHO is equipped to capture and refer appropriately the protection issues to the best-placed protection partners. World Health Organization during the design phase, implementing SAM inpatient programs and activities, protection implies implementing projects in impartial and non-discriminatory ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the SAM children receiving treatment from malnutrition with medical complications. The principles of protection mainstreaming will be incorporated during the Monitoring and Evaluation. Assessment of the project incorporates the key elements of Protection Mainstreaming, which include Avoiding-causing harm and prioritize safety and dignity, Ensuring meaningful access, Practicing accountability and Promoting participation and empowerment of the stabilization center staff and beneficiaries. A confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system is in place and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents of rights violations. Feedback on the complaints should be regularly be provided to the beneficiaries.
