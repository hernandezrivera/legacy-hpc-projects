Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to continue answering to the most urgent humanitarian needs along the contact line while looking for medium to long term solutions to ensure an improved access to quality health services for the most vulnerable people affected by the protracted crisis in Eastern Ukraine (Luhansk and Donetsk Oblast). The project will particularly target vulnerable women and girls affected by on-going conflict by ensuring access to Sexual and Reproductive Health services and access to GBV prevention and response services. The project will also contribute to address the high level of mental health problems among internally displaced persons and host populations in conflict affected areas. In order to achieve this objective, MdM will target the most vulnerable communities and categories of people affected by the conflict (such as women and girls, elders, people exposed to conflict-related stressors): - The mobile unit approach in remote locations of Luhansk and Donetsk oblast close to the line of contact will ensure access to PHC, treatment and referral to specialized care. The mobile unit will promote a comprehensive approach by providing PHC, MHPSS and SRH services and will be integrated within the existing health facilities (feldshers’points). - The local health system will also be supported through targeted donations of supplies and equipment and health staff will be trained (with a specific focus on SRH including Family Planning and MHPSS) to face current lack of capacity building opportunities in conflict-affected Luhansk and Donetsk Oblast. The training plan will be developed in line with the ongoing Health System Reform and by creating synergies with public training institutes and/or hospitals. - Building up on MdM experience in the area MdM will build a new project aiming to strengthen local authorities’ and local NGOs capacities to provide a comprehensive package of services to survivors of Gender-Based Violence including the most vulnerable population, and by raising awareness on health and mental health consequences of GBV, promoting the development of a sustainable referral pathway. - MdM mental health and psychosocial intervention based on evidence-based methodology will be expanded and disseminated to support the needs of persons who deal with psychological problems such as: stress, fear, feelings of helplessness, as well as practical readjustment challenges related to conflicts within families, issues with communities and livelihood difficulties. MdM advocacy efforts will also continue to ensure the integration of MHPSS into PHC in the framework of the current Health Reform. In NGCA, in so-called Luhansk People Republic, where MdM has been registered since October 2017, MdM will continue to support PHC facilities close to the contact line, which have been particularly affected by the conflict. MdM will pursue with donations of life-saving medical equipment to Emergency departments of hospitals with focus on Children’s health and Sexual and Reproductive Health. Besides, MdM will seek to extend its activities in NGCA.

Needs

For over four years, hostilities in eastern Ukraine continue unabated. Shelling, small-arm fire and landmine contamination kill, maim civilians and pose deadly threat to millions of people every single day. Access to health services for population residing in government controlled areas (GCAs) remains challenging while the de facto authorities in Non-Government Controlled Areas (NGCAs) continue to hamper humanitarian access to those most in need of assistance. Freedom of movement needed by both patients and health care providers continue to be obstructed by insecurity. Impediments to procurement, delivery and maintenance result in lack of medicaments and equipment, ultimately decreasing quality of care. People with disabilities and the elderly are among the most vulnerable in the conflict affected area of eastern Ukraine. Many older people cannot easily move independently, while in many areas there are no available health care facilities within an accessible distance for health care maintenance and timely emergency care. MdM identifies several persisting elements contributing to qualify the proposed intervention as a priority: • The lack of health care providers in areas close to the contact line (5 – 20 km) is a major factor reducing the availability of health care in these areas. The conflict has increased distances and travel times to primary health and maternity facilities in particular in rural areas. Insecurity and disrupted transportation directly impede access. Besides, GCA facilities have been cut-off from their previous specialized referral centers located in NGCA;• Beyond the pre-existing low capacity of sexual and reproductive health services, access and affordability have worsened in the setting of physical constraints, preventing women and newborns from adequate access to appropriate services;• Inaccessibility and unaffordability of health care and medications lead to the worsening of the health conditions and increased acute health events, in particular for vulnerable patients with chronic diseases. Lack of treatment is life threatening for patients suffering from diabetes, cancer, and cardio-vascular conditions, including hypertension, which is extremely common among people older than 50 years-old;• In the current setting of conflict and displacement, aggression, violence, disrupted support social networks and incessant exposure to danger contribute to psychological stress and mental health disorders and deteriorate social resilience. Psychological stress and mental health problems have also been associated with poor lifestyle and more-risky behaviors (i.e. smoking, drinking alcohol and consuming drugs, less healthy diet, less exercise and risky relationships). Older people are often socially isolated and also need psychosocial support. Stigma associated with mental disorders often deters people from seeking help. Those who do seek treatment may not know where to turn and may seek help from family doctors who do not have the knowledge and skills to help or take medications without consulting with a professional;• The lack of reporting by de facto authorities in NGCA on key epidemiological and health system indicators significantly limits proper understanding of the real health situation overall. Restriction of movement for people and goods between GCA and NGCA reinforces isolation, limiting supply of crucial diagnostic consumables and prevents proper maintenance of laboratory equipment;• Women and girls face higher vulnerability due to crisis-induced risks such as risk of domestic violence, rape, intimidation, sexual harassment and survival sex, especially in households from disadvantaged economic groups. Gender- and sexual-based violence (including conflict-related) remain much underreported and are not adequately addressed through available outreach and health services.

Activities & outcomes

In line with HRP,MdM will continue answering the most urgent humanitarian needs along the contact line (LoC) to ensure an improved access to quality health services for the most vulnerable people affected by the crisis in Eastern Ukraine (Luhansk and Donetsk Oblast). MdM will target the most vulnerable communities and people affected by the conflict (such as women and girls, elders, people exposed to conflict-related stressors) through a mobile unit approach. The full medical mobile unit will operate in remote locations close to the LoC to ensure access to PHC, treatment, SRH and referral to specialized care where the health system is still severely disrupted. The referral system will be completed by a cash-for-referral approach. The amount of cash provided to beneficiaries covers the costs of transportation and/or cost of service and diagnostic tests (all costs are assessed by MdM in locations and referral facilities). The main areas of MH trainings will be Integration of Mental Health into PHC principles, including key psychosocial skills, namely interviewing, counseling and interpersonal skills, (basics of psychological first aid, self-help MH). The methodological tools used will be WHO evidenced based interventions, as Problem Management Plus, which has been already piloted and contextualized for the use in Ukraine in PHC by MdM, and mhGAP. A specific project on GBV response and prevention submitted by MdM to the GBV working group of the Protection cluster, builds on MdM best practices with Mobile Units and MHPSS counselling, and is complementary to activities of the current project. MdM involves beneficiaries and local partners (including local authorities) in decision making related to the design of its projects by collecting their feedback and adapting MdM’s strategy accordingly. This also enables MdM to define their role in project implementation and monitoring. Involvement of beneficiaries and local partners includes: • A stakeholders’ consultation process, which enables MdM to gather feedback on current activities and suggestions for improvement and further development from different stakeholders; • Capacity building components are determined through a consultation process with beneficiaries and local health authorities; • The assessment and selection of health facilities to be supported by mobile units are always done in cooperation with local health authorities (Department of Health and relevant PMSAC); • The weekly work plan of the mobile unit is designed with the head of the relevant PMSAC; • Monthly reports on activities are sent to Department of Health and relevant PMSAC and bi-monthly meetings are organized with Chief Doctors of relevant PMSAC, to discuss the achievements of the project and the challenges faced; • MdM teams are continuously collecting informal feedbacks from patients and beneficiaries of each activity. Starting in November 2017, a formal feedback mechanism have been put in place for the beneficiaries of MdM Mobile Units as well as a formal complaint mechanism introduced. The high level of acceptance that has been built since the beginning of the MdM program in Ukraine brings about a strong involvement from the various stakeholders during the overall project cycle. MdM’s presence and engagement with authorities, communities and patients allow MdM to be able to constantly adjust its support to ensure suitability and relevancy of MdM’s strategy and activities to address the most urgent needs and targeting the most vulnerable population. In this sens, MdM applies the Accountability to Affected Population Framework recommended by the Health Cluster.
