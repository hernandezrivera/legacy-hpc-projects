Project Summary

Planned Project
The impact of the project consists of increasing the integration in the labor market, along with improving their living conditions; reducing the social side effects of unemployment.
Whether in (Tell Abyad or Jarablus ), the project will focus on supporting 300 PWD beneficiaries ( mental and physical disabilities )to qualify them in the fields of work through vocational training and support them in starting their businesses and integrating them into society through open sessions with their host communities.
150 displaced persons with special needs who live in Azaz sub-district camps in Aleppo governorate, which host more than 1,50,000 displaced persons, will be targeted in the camps of Azaz District, north of Aleppo.
The Project will target 150 Syrian with special needs living in Tell Abiad, Syria.
The project is designed to build capacity and improve the personal skills of the targeted beneficiaries through a holistic approach of training including the project will provide a vocational course designated for people with special needs, the subject of the vocational training will be identified through the beneficiaries' identification and assessment.
MISK will support the beneficiaries also by providing transportation to the training location given their special conditions, and providing equipment to start their projects this will encourage the participants to attend the training sessions.
The targeted beneficiaries (300)will be divided into 10 groups of 30 people to achieve the maximum benefit of the training sessions.
the beneficiaries' selection will be based on vulnerability criteria, as well as the targeted people's ability and conditions to attend the training, in addition to their education and experience background. Interviews will be conducted with the targeted beneficiaries to assess their specific needs, their capacity, and skills, in addition to their desired vocational training to be received. MISK will identify the people with special needs from its existing database of Syrian families living in Tell Abiad.
MISK signed a memorandum of understanding with the local council to work in Tell Abiad & Azaz

4 - Needs

Syrians are trapped in a vicious cycle of poverty since they don’t have time/money to study and/or any vocational programs, but these courses are mandatory to help them overcome the low positions they are hired into. Dedicating time to study implies less work and less income. In the situation that most Syrians find themselves with low revenues barely covering their existence, having extra time to study is completely untenable. People with disabilities face many difficulties that prevent their ability to work, the most prominent of which are: poor self-confidence of people with disabilities and fear that society will not accept them; employers' lack of confidence in the skills and capabilities of people with disabilities and their ability to accomplish the tasks assigned to them; the poor financial situation of people with disabilities prevents them from being able to open their own businesses in the absence of small business support programs for them; many people with disabilities do not have the necessary skills and experience to start work; poor capacity-building and vocational training programs targeting people with disabilities in particular; the difficulty for people with disabilities to move to and from the workplace. The main training courses that people with disabilities wish to attend are foreign languages, computer skills, drawing, hairdressing, sewing, shoe moulding, protection and psychological support sessions, and speech training. However, people with disabilities face many difficulties to attend vocational training and capacity-building programs conducted by training institutions and centers.
