Project Summary

The project aims to integrate nutrition programs in primary health care facilities, applying the principles of Do No Harm and through outreach activities. The nutrition activities include Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) strategy, Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM), and screening for malnutrition through Community Health Workers (CHWs), referrals and treatment of severely and moderately malnourished cases (SAM and MAM cases). These interventions will be fulfilled through:
1- Supporting Health and Nutrition activities in 3 PHC centers in rural Aleppo (Bawabiyeh, Ghazawiyet Afrin, Jeineh).
2- Operating two mobile clinics in the proposed locations.
3- Supporting 40 CHWs for outreach activities in the rural Aleppo.

Needs

With the absence of the government health services, the increased health and nutrition needs in light of the existence of internal displacements (IDPs) and previous evacuation from multiple regions, the need to provide the proposed services can be only met through the humanitarian assistance. In addition, northern Syria witnesses an increased density of population forming an increased burden on the health and nutrition facilities. High costs of transportation, and difficulty for the patients to travel out of their communities is also one of key demands for proposing the project. In assessing these needs, Shafak depended on OCHA’s regular bulletins and reports, HRP and HNO 2019 of Syria, and the nutrition Cluster reports, as a secondary data source. Furthermore, Shafak confirmed the needs in the targeted areas through the field team and coordination with the local authorities and health directorates.According to Syrian HNO 2019-Over the course of 2018, ongoing conflict, shifts in areas of control, increased population movements and returnees have impacted the nutrition situation in Syria. SMART surveys, rapid nutrition assessments, and mass MUAC (mid-upper arm circumference) screening show that acute and chronic malnutrition of children 6-59 months and women of child bearing age remain a problem in localized areas across Syria. Of particular concern, is a significant increase of acute malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women, which has more than doubled since last year according to monthly reporting. According to Syrian HRP 219, Global Acute Malnutrition among boys and girls under the age of 5 years remains within acceptable international benchmarks in most of the assessed areas during 2018. However, there are some pockets where SMART surveys, rapid assessments, and nutrition surveillance information show an increase in chronic malnutrition rates.SHAFAK selected the targeted locations based on the gaps shared by the Nutrition Cluster, especially the list of PIN figures and severity scale which were based on in drafting the HNO 2019; according to the HNO estimation, 15529 individuals are in need of nutrition services. The severity scale classified the project’s targeted communities (Atareb, Daret Azzeh, Afrin and Zarbeh sub-districts) between 3 and 4 severity marks indicating the high needs in these communities. In addition, the selection of the locations was also based on the Health Cluster’s Health Resources Availability Mapping System (HeRAMS) report, which highlighted the unsupported PHC health centers and the need to integrate nutrition activities within the existed health facilities and through outreach activities in the proposed locations. Furthermore, the selection of the locations was in coordination with Aleppo Health Directorate (AHD), which also highlighted the priority of these locations, where they specifically indicated the need to support the PHCs in the following locations: Al-Jeineh, Bawabiyeh, Ghazawiyet Afrin in Atareb, Zarbeh and Afrin sub-districts.Shafak has strong connections with national actors and coordination institutions in the field and Turkey levels, and this will facilitate the implementation of the project’s activities and movement of the staff. In addition, Shafak has good access and presence in the proposed locations, having a field office in the middle of the geographical areas targeted under this project (see attached map). In addition, Shafak has been operating in these areas for the last five years, implementing different interventions in Health, Protection, and Education as well as other emergency response projects. Moreover, Shafak has large warehouses and good warehousing and management system in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, which ensures easy and regular supply chain of the basic supplies that will be required under this project.

Implementation of activities

In accordance with Shafak's cross-border strategy, we will be responsible for direct implementation in the target locations coordinating with and gaining approval from local authorities in Syria, building on their extensive network, and facilitating access. Shafak will be responsible for ensuring that quality and accountability standards are met, will provide required training to staff and ensure that contractual requirement are adhered to and met. Further, Shafak will be responsible for all program and financial reporting to the donor and will also continue to coordinate with relevant cluster and other coordination bodies in Turkey. This joint program will be conducted through three main phases as the following:Phase I:- Recruitment of needed staff (Health/Nutrition)- Procurement of needed furniture, tools for training and tools for screening.- Training sessions. Phase II:- IYCF activities.- Facility and community based screening activities.- CMAM services.- Outpatient services.- Treatment.-Follow up/-Referral. Phase III:- Reporting, data analysis, monitoring and evaluation

Protection Risk Analysis

The project overall objective aims at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individual where they need it the most. Throughout the project life cycle the principles of protection mainstreaming are incorporated. All activities are designed to avoiding causing harm and prioritize safety and dignity of targeted populations, insuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation through several, action such as:• The project is designed to meet the needs of the community especially women, boys and girls considering the context, tradition, and norms in the community• Location of facilities will be accessible and easy to reach for all people., also safe and away from any military location.• Community will be clearly and widely informed about the date, location and timing of the service provision, and their opinion will be highly considered. • Team staff will be trained to be skilled in delivering PFA, identifying CP&GBV cases and refer them to adequate services.• Insure that the location is suitable for women, girls, people with disabilities, children headed household, old people, and other affected categories in the community---outreach. • Distribution and providing services for specific categories mentioned above will be to the door. • Teams will be trained on PESA, Child safeguarding, and will sign the code of conducted related. • project team will set up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can receive the information they need, provide feedback on the activities. accountability officer will be responsible for information provision, collecting and responding to feedback and will report directly to HQ to ensure transparency • Feedback will also be continuously gathered in line with standardized center management protocol and monitoring and evaluation procedures, and doing so shall help improve protection mainstreaming. Shafak has its own Safety and Security Plan that includes, among other measures, safe in cash for rapid evacuation needs, mapping of an alternative road to avoid main road blockage. In case of security constraints, activities will be suspended, field staff hibernated, and to be restarted as soon as situation allows it.- For Shafak Organization, life proceeds over materials, and therefore no staff should endanger their own lives, or the lives of others, whilst attempting to protect Shafak Organization property, equipment, financial resources, documents or infrastructure.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks related to activities carried out by the nutrition sector are anticipated to be mainly to the health workers moving between camps, potential looting of nutrition suppliles, attacks on health workers and facilities as well as beneficiaries accessing the facilities, losing follow up of beneficiaries and creating tension when targeting IDPs for distributions in the host community. In order to mitigate these risks, and ensuring adherence to humanitarian principles, the sector adopted a “Do No Harm” strategy while providing nutrition services to vulnerable groups throughout Syria. These mitigation measures include the development and use of standardized approaches, services packages and tools when delivering services across Syria, community engagement and sensitization about targeting, and adapting a flexible delivery modality with adjustable distribution schedules. Nutrition partners will aim to set up centers in safe and accessible locations, that can be easily reached by beneficiaries, with the prospect to relocate nutrition centers in relatively safe areas, and/ or readiness of mobile teams. Additionally more community health workers will be trained to provide services at community level, should beneficiaries’ access be constrained at health facility level. Stock prepositioning in multiple, safe, and close locations to delivery sites, as well as good contextual analyses will be an ongoing exercise for the sector partners. The sector will review risks and mitigation measures regularly and make the necessary adjustments to programming modalities, and implementing methods depending on dynamics on the ground.
