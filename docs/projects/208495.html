Project Summary

The overarching objective of the 12-month proposed intervention is to contribute to improving the health status of conflict-affected population in Borno state. To achieve this goal and ensure the greatest and widest impact of its activities, PUI will continue to provide comprehensive and complementary lifesaving health services to both IDPs and host communities affected by the ongoing crises targeting 59,921. Protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project. The comprehensive feedback and complaint response mechanism set up in the frame of the ongoing project will be continued/re-established by the MEAL team, with information and how, when, and where to access it disseminated within the community and at all the facilities to ensure beneficiaries’ awareness on the existing mechanism.
This integrated approach will itself rely on a two-pillars response strategy based on 1) prevention and 2) response. Through health sensitization and awareness raising sessions, PUI will provide direct and indirect beneficiaries with necessary information on how to improve their health status, prevent diseases and relapse, prevent malnutrition, as well as with vital information on their rights and entitlements, and on available services and how and when to access them. This will help empower people on these issues.
On the response side, PUI will provide free of charge quality lifesaving health services to all beneficiaries irrespective of their gender, age, residential status (IDPs, Host Communities), religion, socio-economic status.
The proposed intervention will unfold along two key implementation axes: 1) a strong involvement of the community, through a community-based approach (iCCM) for mobilization, social norm changes and service delivery (provision of curative services in the community to children under the age of five years on common communicable diseases using the IMNCI guideline), while ensuring the local ownership and participation of the target beneficiary groups; 2) the integration with PUI project existing in Maiduguri supporting PHC services, to ensure wider impact and efficiency. Indeed, this intervention will itself be the continuation of an existing project supported by CDCS, building on the results already achieved to prepare and achieve the orderly and planned exit of PUI from this area.

1 - Needs

The recent Northeast (NE) Nigeria Nutrition & Food Security Surveillance (NFSS) (December 2022) report highlights critical gaps in the management of common childhood morbidity in Borno State. The state’s 30% global immunization rate is inferior to the current 50% national average and the 90% national target. A significant proportion of children remain unvaccinated. For instance, the medium coverage of measles vaccination is 72.4%, but only 68.4% of children have received the pentavalent vaccination against 5 killer diseases (diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, hepatitis B and hemphilus influenzae type b). In Maiduguri, between April 2022 and January 2023 included, PUI conducted 64,575 outpatient consultations at its supported health facility, with communicable diseases respectively 93% of these consultations. In Maiduguri, 58% of these consultations concerned children aged 0-14 years old, and almost 39% children under 5.Regarding Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services and data, numbers show a clear upward trend, with high absolute numbers (143 deliveries in May 2022, 154 in September, 166 in October, 145 in December, and 143 in January). In Maiduguri, in September and October, it has also been observed that many of the deliveries were conducted on women coming from outside the project’s catchment population area (more than 70%). PUI teams could also record an increasing number of complicated obstetric cases referred to the facility from the community – 45 in October, 48 in November 2022. PUI’s analysis showed that if the concerned women had been referred earlier to the PHCC, most of these complications would have been avoided. According to the latest UNFPA (United Nations Population Fund) study on global trends in maternal mortality rates 2000 to 2020, published in February 2023, Nigeria had in 2020 the highest estimated number of maternal deaths globally, accounting for over one quarter (28.5%) of all estimated global maternal deaths, with approximately 82,000 maternal deaths, and a maternal mortality ratio of 1047 women dying per 100,000 live births. In the country, according to another UNFPA-led study, Northeast Nigeria has the highest mortality ratio, with 1,549 women dying per 100,000 live births. This illustrates existing gaps in the provision of Sexual and Reproductive Health Services in this region where child marriage is very high. Indeed, 89% of women aged between 15 and 49 in Borno State were first married before age 15 (SCI, October 2021).The volatile security situation around Maiduguri exacerbates existing risks of sexual gender-based violence, while PHCCs are not yet fully equipped to provide the required services to survivors. They lack female healthcare gynaecologists and midwives, PEP (Post Exposure Prophylaxis) kits, STIs (Sexually Transmitted Infections/HIV preventive methods and skills in clinical management of rape.In Nigeria, it is estimated that 10 to 25% of the waste generated by medical structures are hazardous. Healthcare waste management remains unsatisfactory because of non-systematic practices in collection, storage, transportation, treatment and final disposal of healthcare waste. Healthcare solid waste is either indiscriminately dumped into public dumpsites, buried, or burnt at low temperature in open pits, with high risks of injury or infection for healthcare waste handlers, who often lack Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), and waste scavengers. This also has a negative impact on Nigeria’s natural resources, with soil, air, water contamination, impacting in turn people’s health.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Rehabilitation of and support to Maiduguri Ngarannam PHCC facility in terms of operations and capacity building. PUI will continue supporting one health facility in Maiduguri, Ngarannam Primary Health Care Centre (PHCC), , in the provision of essential health services. This support will consist of supplying the facility with necessary medical equipment and pharmaceuticals, providing needed human resources, providing capacity building to the facility personnel and conducting rehabilitation and maintenance work on the facility structure.Activity 2: Outpatient curative consultations and referral of cases requiring secondary health care. PUI-supported health facility of Ngarannam in Maiduguri will continue to provide primary healthcare consultations to all patients seeking care irrespective of their age, gender, religion, residential status (IDPs, host communities), or socio-cultural and economic backgrounds. Activity 3: Public health surveillance of epidemic prone communicable diseases. PUI will continue to reinforce its relationship and partnership with the SMoH by strengthening the health information system through the submission of weekly surveillance reports to the Disease Surveillance and Notification Officer (DSNO) at the Ministry of Health.Activity 4: Provision of maternity care including antenatal, postnatal care and family planning services, and referral of obstetric emergency cases. PUI will continue to implement in Maiduguri its reproductive health model intervention through the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP). At PUI-supported health facility of Ngarannam, women of childbearing age will benefit from comprehensive Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) services without discrimination. Trained and certified PUI and MoH midwives will conduct free-of charge Antenatal and Postnatal consultations for all pregnant and lactating women seeking these services.Activity 5: Provision of quality healthcare to survivors of Gender-based violence (GBV). PUI will continue providing clinical care to survivors of Sexual and Gender-based violence (SGBV). Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) will be conducted by trained and experienced staff following WHO and UNFPA guidelines.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

This Action will comprise part of a multi-sectoral intervention coordinated jointly via OCHA. PUI health program is implemented under the guidance of and collaboration with the State Ministry of Health (SMoH). Active referral system, exchange of resources, clinical expertise and coordination exists between PUI, WHO and UNFPA through pooling of resources and expertise to maintain program quality standards following the national guidelines. Subject to their availability, through its linkage with key health actors, the action will be supported with gift in kind (GIK) assistance from core pipelines including vaccines and supplies; HIV testing supplies; mosquito nets and malaria drugs; UNFPA reproductive health kits. Similar to previous years, PUI will continue to be an active member of the Humanitarian Response Plan, attending multi-sectoral coordination meetings with other actors. PUI will build the capacity of the MOH staff to provide services in the health facility beyond this project lifespan.

Throughout the course of the implementation of this action, PUI’s Complaints, Response and Feedback Mechanism (CRFM) will be employed to ensure meaningful participation of beneficiaries and create effective channels for feedback to be incorporated into program implementation. These will be done through weekly MEAL-led face-to-face sessions; recording and escalation of complaints and feedback through community committees; use of hotline phone number, complaints and feedback boxes as deemed fit by program beneficiaries and other community members. Child friendly methods will be relied on during face-to-face sessions with children to enable their meaningful participation.
