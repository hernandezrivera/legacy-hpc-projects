Project Summary

HI began providing services in Cox’s Bazar prior to the 2017 crisis, has been adapting ever since. Continuing to address complex, acute, multi-layered needs, HI proposes to foster a safe & protective environment with the provision of specialized & community-based services to Rohingya refugees & host community through a twin track modality.
Multi-sectorial (disciplinary) Mobile Units (MU) to address mobility or social barriers. Integrated outreach teams (PSS + physiotherapists, protection officers, sports & recreation officers) deliver home-based services focused on education for beneficiaries & their caregivers on safe, effective identification & safe referral internally & externally for therapeutic exercises & other interventions. Currently, HI has thirteen MU which are teams of trained staff providing direct specialized services (including protection, mental health & psychosocial support, physical/functional rehabilitation, basic health care/SRH) to vulnerable persons & persons with disabilities in a comprehensive approach. At HI service centers (static points), teams support beneficiaries with enough mobility to access the facility with rehabilitation, PSS, basic healthcare, safe space. The project is designed to meet the identified needs of vulnerable groups (particularly persons with disabilities, older persons, women/girls, and children).
HI will continue to provide individual life-saving protection assistance by safely identifying, assessment & referring individuals being most at-risk or survivors of violence, including women/girls with disabilities. Within its ongoing activities, HI has established strong referral pathways (using Inter-agency forms as per Sector recommendation) with specialized camp protection actors in each of its activity sites. HI developed awareness messages to address gaps identified on the following topics: Human Trafficking & Exploitation, PSEA, Inclusion, Children’s Rights & Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Violence(included IPV & GBV), Gender Equality & General Protection. HI also actively participate in the PERU teams, are fundamentally intended for deployment during disasters or emergencies in camp settings. PSEAH focal in HI are responsible for extensive training of all staff & volunteers using PSEA Network materials & recommendations to ensure adequate level of practice within the organization. Inclusive Feedback & Response Mechanism is in place where feedback is regularly elicited through suggestions boxes, a dedicated hotline for host & refugee communities, community discussions & 1:1 interview. A dedicated Focal Point to receive complaints is assigned in the Cox’s Bazar base to coordinate with camp level team leaders to ensure referral for needed services, ensure survivor advocacy as needed. HI is currently implementing four projects that address above mentioned services.
HI created & supports community groups (in form of peer support groups) composed of persons with disabilities, to enforce self-resilience & participation/representation to the community. HI has inbuild capacity to respond to disaster which has been demonstrated in recent fire & flood response. COVID19 protection measures will continue to be adopted & its awareness will be prioritized especially for people with limited access to information.
The project invests in capacity building of a variety of stakeholders at multiple levels to progress towards sustainable availability of quality and inclusive services in both camps and host communities.
HI works in partnership with the other actors present at camp level, to ensure the best response to the needs reported. HI coordinates with both NGOs and government bodies, depending on the areas of intervention. HI through his Compliance & Liaison department, is engaging with all relevant authorities (RRRC, DC, UNO, DSS, CiC, NGO AB) to ensure timely coordination & proper communication. HI is part of part of GO & NGO coordination groups at DC & UNO Levels.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In 2017, 721,641 Rohingya refugees fleeing violence and persecutions in Rakhine State, Myanmar, have sought protection in Cox’s Bazar district, triggering the largest and fastest refugee influx into Bangladesh. Over three years after the influx from Myanmar, around one million Rohingya refugees live in extremely precarious conditions in 34 camps spread between Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas in Cox’s Bazar district. As per PWG-REACH study, 2021, persons with disabilities and specific needs in both camps and host communities face the risks disproportionally compared to the rest of the population, given the fact that intersectionality of factors (gender, age, disability, ethnicity, religion, and other factors) lead to further exclusion, discrimination, hence increase vulnerabilities. Rohingya households reported high levels of insecurity related to limitations on movement, discrimination, harassment, and physical violence prevailing in camps. As per the JMSNA, 2021, 10% of the interviewees indicated that the safety and security situation in their area of residence deteriorated, with a 65% of the responders in need of more protection, in particular improved safety and security. In addition, anecdotal information on the deterioration of the perception of the safety and security situation by refugees has been reported by partners throughout the year, based on the activities of consultation conducted with the communities, before and after the lockdown period. (UNHCR 02/2021, 01-06/2021, 07/2020; IOM 01-03/2021, 04-06/2021; IRC 01/2021 03/2021 06/2021 09/2021; ACAPS/2021,). Persons with Specific Needs including single-headed households, elderly, and persons with disabilities, transgender population, and adolescent girls continue to require a community-based approach and individual case management support to respond to their needs. Additionally, social cohesion – already precarious – has deteriorated as tensions rose between the camp and host communities, owing to fears and misconceptions surrounding COVID-19 risks. Containment measures have hampered access to services; safety, mobility restrictions, limited transportation options, and limited access to information presented additional barriers for persons with disabilities and women and girls. The COVID 19 situation continued to exacerbate pre-existing protection risks, including neglect, violence, and exploitation and is reflected in the perception by refugees of a deteriorated security situation. Increased safety and security concerns, caused by the socio-economic impacts of lockdown measures and suspended protection programming, accelerated already rising protection risks, including GBV and child protection, while community protection structures weakened and inter- and intra-communal tensions exacerbated. About 33% of refugees reported experiencing insecurity incidents in 2020, compared to 15 percent of 2019 (The REVA 4 WFP 04/2021).

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

HI is implementing sustainable, inclusive and quality humanitarian response within the Cox’s Bazar district. HI ensures a holistic intervention through synergies with other HI projects, external actors and development actions. HI uses the standard referral pathway guidelines for the Rohingya response designed by the Protection Working Group to refer to external service providers, including humanitarian actors. Moreover, HI continues to participate as an active member of the Health, Protection, Education and Logistic Sectors; and Child Protection, MHPSS working group sub-sector and chairs ADWG to have regular interactions with all sectors. Being an active member, HI ensures effective coordination and synergies with other interventions and actors in the areas supporting to avoid any duplication of activities in the camps and host areas, as well as promoting inclusive humanitarian actions at the sectoral and ISCG levels. Additionally, HI coordinates with development actors in Bangladesh to focus on creating sustainable changes and mainstreaming actions ensuring the rights and inclusion of persons with disabilities. HI is continuously aiming to make its action sustainable and participates in building capacities of the community and local partners to foster community resilience.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The program incorporates protection principles throughout all the phases including the design & inception phases. Starting from the design phase HI has carried out protection needs assessment through community consultations analysis of data from ongoing projects & analysis of specific feedback received through accountability mechanisms. Consultation & dialogue with communities including women & girls with & without disabilities have been ongoing in the previous phases of the program & will continue throughout continued implementation to promote active participation of affected populations. Additionally, HI Project Staffs are in daily contact with HI beneficiaries through routine activities & more importantly at a home-based level. Those activities give the opportunity to collect feedback about services received or any updates regarding the camp situation. In this year proposed project will be implemented jointly with the Center for Disability in Development (CDD).

3.2 - Gender

HI is mainstreaming an age, gender & diversity approach throughout the planning, implementation & reporting processes for interventions in all sectors. Intersections of disability, age & gender discrimination have a multiplying effect on the barriers faced by children, especially girls, with disabilities & contribute to increased risk factors for vulnerability to poverty, social exclusion & violence. HI’s commitment to equality & equity of access for women/girls and men/boys in all areas of our work is set forth in our institutional policies (including “DGA Policy”) and through the adoption of a gender-sensitive approach throughout the project cycle. Practical actions to promote adherence to our institutional policies & values within the current program will include ensuring gender balance within our personnel, all personnel is trained on disability, gender & age inclusion & accountable to HI’s Code of Conduct. This includes staff, partners & stakeholders, consultants, & suppliers.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

HI’s Code of Conduct, Safeguarding Framework, Child Protection Policy, and Protection of Beneficiaries from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment Policy create enforceable accountability mechanisms that apply to all staff, partners, volunteers, and contracted service providers. Meaningful participation is one of the pillars of HI’s Disability, Gender and Age Policy. Meaningful participation of boys, girls, women and men of different age groups, including people with different abilities, in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of actions or policies affecting them is essential for ensuring a “do no harm” approach, relevance of the actions, and equity of outcomes. Engaging locally recruited volunteers who are capable of translating and contextualizing key messages in the implementation of the program facilitates meaningful participation of beneficiaries.

3.4 - Diversity

HI’s “Age, Gender, & Disability Policy”, whose guiding principles are participation, equality & non-discrimination, accessibility & protection, informs all aspects of project design, implementation, monitoring, & evaluation. The policy supports the development & implementation of transformative gender-sensitive projects, where age & disability are fully considered. HI has in-built human resources & technical tools dedicated to identifying the specific needs & capacities of people from different groups. The project aims to target mainly person with specific needs including persons with disabilities of all age, gender, ethnicity & religions through integrating protection mainstreaming at every stage of the project cycle. To ensure gender equality; the steps that HI takes include, gender-sensitive recruitment & gender-balanced teams. Children under 5 & adolescents will be specifically targeted within the Inclusive & Recreational sessions Activity.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

HI’s Code of Conduct, Safeguarding Framework, Child Protection Policy & Protection of Beneficiaries from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse & Harassment Policy create enforceable accountability mechanisms that apply to all staff, partners, volunteers & contracted service providers. Engaging locally recruited volunteers who are capable of translating & contextualizing key messages in the implementation of the program facilitates meaningful participation of beneficiaries. Prior to service delivery, HI will conduct individual assessments, protecting each beneficiaries’ rights to confidentiality, in consultation with beneficiaries & caregivers. Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanisms (CFRM) create two-way communication channels that enable teams to receive feedback from program participants & to respond in a timely way to their suggestions & concerns. It is designed specifically to gather & respond to feedback from program participants & other community stakeholders.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes HI continue to undertake concrete & innovative protection-sensitive preparedness interventions to mitigate risks of landslides & floods, particularly in areas where refugees will be unable to evacuate to safer areas. HI, trains refugee volunteers; strengthens essential infrastructure & prepositions relief items & equipment to respond as needed. HI place communities at the center of the response, with refugees’ own capacities to play a key role in preparing & responding to disaster, alongside critical social cohesion measures with host communities. HI is fully aware of the potential impact on the community environment, mechanism & relations any humanitarian aid provision can have specifically in the Rohingya crisis context with growing tensions between local & Rohingya communities, as well as specific contextual elements to analyze & take into account regarding implementation of activities in camps.
