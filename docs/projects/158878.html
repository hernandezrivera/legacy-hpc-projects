Project Summary

The project aims to support vulnerable groups of patients receiving organ transplants and mainly kidney transplants and children with Type I diabetes through the provision of life-saving medications, nutritional supplements, health and lifestyle education, and awareness as well as to conduct a scoping study to map urgent, unmet needs of these groups in Gaza Strip.
200 children with type I diabetes will receive insulin pens/vials, suitable needles, glucometers, and its consumables and laboratory investigations. more than 500 community members will receive health education sessions and materials.
More than 9000 beneficiaries will be reached through radio spots and released videos.
At least 15 patients will be able to receive life-saving surgeries (i.e. Kidney transplant) due to the availability of solutions, drugs and other supplies needed for the surgery. Additionally, more than 400 Kidney transplant patients will receive immunosuppressants and other life-saving medications that will prohibit getting back to dialysis.
A scoping study to collect and analyze the needs of vulnerable groups, disaggregation of these groups and then issuing a comprehensive report on needed interventions, service providers and gaps that need to be filled in.
Objective: The overall objective is to provide vital health services to vulnerable groups with the aim of improving their health and nutritional status as well as to help them make necessary lifestyle changes to cope with their conditions.
It is worth mentioning that the proposed intervention meets strategic objectives, and health and nutrition cluster objectives:
Strategic Objective #2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under International Humanitarian Law.
Health Cluster Objective #1: Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability, and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza and West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building.
In addition, the proposed intervention meets QRCS's general strategy: Saving lives and preserving dignity.
Specific objectives:
- To improve and maintain the well-being of boys and girls suffering from Type I Diabetes and to minimize rates of complications (e.g. Kidney failure, cardiovascular diseases, Ophthalmic diseases, ...etc.).
- To diagnose new cases suffering from Type I diabetes and render them to treatment.
- To improve the lifestyle, knowledge, attitudes, and practices of the community regarding Type I diabetes.
- To ensure a proper feeding pattern of infants and children affected by Type I diabetes.
- To increase awareness regarding different types of diseases for community members.
- To develop more comprehensive networking with other health providers aiming at securing a comprehensive management approach for Type I diabetes.
- To reduce risks faced by organ transplant patients by securing needed antiviral immunosuppressants and steroids of which MoH is suffering shortages of them.
- To enable more patients to receive life-saving surgeries through the provision of urgently needed instruments (e.g. Omni-tract), drugs and consumables needed for surgeries.
- To enable health providers to target their services towards the most vulnerable through a scoping study of urgent unmet needs of marginalized groups in this project.

1 - Needs

The project meets needs that have been identified for a number of NCDs including DM Type I, and Kidney transplant patients. Needs have been identified in the Gaza Humanitarian Needs Matrix. Diabetic Type I patients under the age of 18 suffer from drug shortage, inability to keep track of their disease due to lack of diagnostic instruments and self-monitoring tools, and low levels of awareness especially in terms of nutrition.Kidney transplantation surgeries have been identified as a need for kidney failure patients. Reports by the MoU continue to specify surgical instruments and consumables as an ongoing shortage. In addition, patients who receive kidney transplants need certain medications, such as immunosuppressants, for the rest of their lives.Some of the needs specified in the Humanitarian Needs Matrix lack disaggregated data as it is not available, therefore the project aims at issuing a report that fills the gap in terms of needs and services for these groups.According to the WHO situation report (1-31 Aug. 2019), 51% out of the essential medications were reported at less than one-month supply at the MoH Central Drug Store (CDS) in Gaza; out of which 90% were completely depleted and 239 items (28%) out of the essential medical disposables were reported at less than one-month supply at the MoH CDS in Gaza. According to the MoH Monthly report (Sept. 2019); and particularly for Kidney transplantation and Hemodialysis, 50% of drugs and 25% of consumables and disposables are at zero stock. Referring to the HNO report 2018, 40,000 children suffering from micronutrient deficiencies in Gaza.

2 - Activities

Provision of insulin pens/vials, suitable injection needles, glucometers, and its consumables and laboratory investigations to 200 children with type I diabetes, covering their needs for a period of 9 months. Execute health education sessions and provide PSS support to 500 patients, families and community members. Conducting radio spots, release videos and distribute brochures to reach more than 9000 people raising their awareness about DM 1, complications and many other related issues. Support and ensure executing at least 15 life-saving surgeries for kidney transplant patients through the provision of needed instruments, drugs and consumables. Additionally, more than 400 Kidney transplant patients will receive immunosuppressants and other life-saving medications for a period of 9 months, this will contribute to prohibiting getting back to dialysis. Conducting a scoping study to collect and analyze the needs of vulnerable groups, disaggregation of these groups and then issuing a comprehensive report on needed interventions, service providers and gaps that need to be filled in.
