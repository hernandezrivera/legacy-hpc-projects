Project Summary

Adequate supplies of good quality water are essential to maintaining health and reducing the risk of waterborne and food borne diseases. However, ensuring the safety of drinking water has been challenging in the country.
Due to the prolonged crisis, living conditions have deteriorated, especially in overcrowded settings, such as IDP camps and IDP shelters with limited available water and difficulty maintaining hygiene. In major cities, the water network has been available and functioning. However, the network has not been well-maintained for some time, and leakage from sewage often occurs, leading to contaminated drinking water. Also, efforts at sterilization of water networks with an electronic system have been interrupted due to frequent power failures. Thus, many people are forced to rely on unregulated local shallow wells that have not been controlled or monitored.
Those limitations of access to safe drinking water have affected the health of vulnerable populations. Throughout 2020, clusters of waterborne diseases have been often been caused by multiple viral, bacterial, and/or parasitic organisms, such as E.coli and HAV, which can contribute to potential waterborne disease outbreaks in fragile communities. Particularly, IDPs and returnees residing in camps and collective shelters are most susceptible and facing heightened risks of epidemic-prone diseases.
In addition to the already disrupted and fragile WASH system, the COVID-19 outbreak, which began in March 2020, has highlighted the essential needs on WASH in health facilities. WASH in health care facilities refers to the provision of water, sanitation, healthcare waste management, hygiene and environmental cleaning infrastructure, which plays an important role in infection prevention and control and improving quality of care. However, the country has faced challenges in ensuring proper WASH in healthcare facilities, especially primary healthcare settings, due to the prolonged humanitarian crisis. The essential physical infrastructure of primary healthcare centers is in a state of disrepair and requires extensive maintenance and rehabilitation to provide a minimum level of service delivery. Also, the shortage of trained staff is critical. An approximately 50% reduction of healthcare workers in the country has been observed compared with prior to the crisis.
As result of this lack of adequate WASH infection prevention and control measures in health facilities, frequent clusters of COVID-19 have been reported in healthcare facilities, with more than 700 cases of COVID-19 reported among healthcare workers from June 2020 through March 2021.
WHO is going to address those WASH issues which have adversely impacted health among vulnerable populations and ensure access to safe drinking water and appropriate WASH IPC measures in healthcare facilities. To ensure the safety of drinking water for vulnerable populations, WHO will continue to conduct surveillance of the quality of drinking water to regularly monitor chemical and biological contamination of different water sources, such as water networks, ground wells, reservoirs, water tanks, jerry cans, as well as promoting a culture to identify pathogens. This activity targets the areas where IDPs and returnees reside. Also, WHO will perform laboratory analysis of drinking water based on the surveillance data and sterilize the contaminated water sources. To enhance WASH in healthcare facilities, WHO will perform light rehabilitation of WASH facilities in primary healthcare centers in Hama and hospitals in NES. In addition, WHO will enhance medical waste management, which is key to preventing any nosocomial infections among healthcare workers and patients through capacity-building along with provision of healthcare waste management containers and vehicles.

4 - Needs

Poor quality drinking water and inappropriate WASH facilities in healthcare setting have been a concern in light of the expansion of COVID-19 outbreaks as well as potential outbreaks of water-borne diseases. There is a need for monitoring drinking water quality, especially for the most vulnerable communities in Aleppo, Al-Hasakeh, Deir-ez-Zor and Rural Damascus, where over 7 million people are in need of WASH assistance. This accounts for more than 50% of the resident population (HNO 2021). Particularly NES, the WASH intervention, is urgently needed. Only 13% of households have access to sufficient quantities of water in Deir-ez-Zor (HNO2020). People are forced to rely on raw water sources with poor quality, which has contributed to a high prevalence of water-related diseases in the community. Based on EWARS, diarrheal diseases are high-burden diseases that account for more than 50% of morbidity in NES in 2020, which is the third most significant cause of mortality among infants.In addition, inappropriate WASH in health facilities has been identified, especially in the light of the COVID-19 outbreak. According to MoH Syria, more than 700 cases of COVID-19, including 33 associated deaths, have been reported among healthcare workers from June 2020 through March 2021. This is due to the lack of appropriate WASH facilities in terms of infrastructure and equipment as well as the limited skilled workforce available in the country. According to HNO (2021), 35% of assessed public hospitals lack waste management services, and 6% of hospitals have no available safe water supply. In addition, there are only partially functional water supply services available in public hospitals in AlHasakeh, Rural Damascus and Homs governorates.

5 - Implementation of activities

- WHO will conduct surveillance of the quality of drinking water by regularly measuring PH, ATP, chlorine residual of water samples from various water sources (e.g. water networks, ground wells, reservoirs, water tanks, jerry cans) and culture to identify pathogens. These activities will cover Rural Damascus, Aleppo (Rural), Al-Hasakeh and Deir-ez-Zor, where the severity scope is over 3, by deploying WHO focal points to conduct testing in collaboration with Ministry of Local Administration and Environment and WASH partners.- Also, WHO will perform laboratory analysis of contaminated water samples based on surveillance data at public health laboratories affiliated with MoLAE. - WHO will also support MoLAE in provision of laboratory equipment and supplies and sterilizing the contaminated water sources by distribution of chlorine tablets to households and Directorates of Health in the targeted governorates along with awareness campaigns for communities - WHO will perform light rehabilitation of WASH facilities in four primary healthcare centers in Hama (Dahriyeh, Hama, Fayhaa, and Baath) and hospitals (Hasakeh National Hospital and Mayadine Hospital) in NES. - In addition, WHO will enhance medical waste management, which is key to preventing nosocomial infections through procurement of 100,000 healthcare waste containers to be equipped in primary healthcare centers and four health-care waste transport vehicles for safe transportation of waste to those off-site facilities, along with building capacity of 400 healthcare workers on proper medical waste management and IPC measures. - WHO will regularly visit the project sites to monitor the progress and implementation status.

WHO has established mechanisms to treat reports of SEA as a priority. Based on the gravity of proven cases of SEA involving WHO staff members, and in addition to any criminal verdict that may result from legal proceeding for cases that are referred to national authorities for criminal prosecution, or other measures taken against the staff member concerned, WHO will apply disciplinary measures pursuant to Section 11 of the Staff Rules, including as applicable summary dismissal. Disciplinary sanctions will also apply to staff members who are proven to have failed to report SEA or to have encouraged or condoned SEA to happen. Malicious reporting of SEA without evidence or reasonable suspicions with the intention of harming another person’s integrity or reputation amounts to misconduct and is subject to disciplinary action. This is distinct from reports of suspected wrongdoing made in good faith based on the judgment and information available at the time of the report, which may not be confirmed by an investigation.
