Project Summary

SC will continue to implement its polio immunisation program in NWS with the support of the Gates Foundation. The EWARN (Early Warning and Response Network) has already been strengthened in Idlib Governorate and continues to run on an ongoing basis. Save the Children and our partners continue to participate in providing information to the surveillance system.
After a prolonged interruption in the Expanded Program of Immunization (EPI) services, preparations for re-launching the EPI project in Northwest Syria have started at the governorate level. This included all actors intending to support the EPI project in northwest Syria. Each organization indicated which health facilities included EPI activities, to avoid any duplication or overlapping geographical distribution, and to ensure that all communities have access to this life-saving intervention. The Syria Immunization Group is the technical committee responsible for planning and supporting all vaccination activities in northwest Syria. WHO and UNICEF provide the needed technical assistance to the SIG. A macro-plan was already put in place for EPI in northern Syria, into which Save the Children contributed through its membership in the SIG. Subsequently, the project developed a micro-plan for the pilot locations supported by Save the Children, in consultation with SIG, Syria Relief and IHD. SC considered and took into account the underlying assumptions for demographics and targets from previous polio vaccination campaigns.
Save the Children, together with our partners Syria Relief and Violet, coordinates with SIG and IHD for vaccination distribution, storage and cold chain maintenance and replacement. In addition, vaccination teams are part of vaccination campaigns, such as polio. Save the Children and our partners also participate in all the monthly meetings conducted by SIG.
Save the Children and partners are coordinating directly with SIG and IHD to Recruit new vaccinators with the technical support of the SIG and IHD and Train vaccinators, which is conducted by IHD and SIG
1. Save the Children proposes to extend the program for approximately eight months (January-August 2020) to continue supporting the seven centers currently covered by this project.
2. Save the Children proposes to include a series of trainings to IHD medical staff in Idlib Governorate. These trainings will support the capacity building needs of IHD. Syria Relief aims to collaborate with UK doctors who will travel to Turkey to deliver the training at the Bab Al Salama border to medical staff based in Syria. Trainings will include, but are not limited to, the following: Scientific Sessions for Medical Providers, Monitoring & Evaluation for Medical Providers, Advanced Trauma Life Support for Medical Providers, Advanced Cardiovascular Life Support for Medical Providers, Pediatric Advanced Life Support for Medical Providers, Advanced Life Support in Obstetrics for Medical Providers, Neonatal Resuscitation Program, Neonatal Intensive Care Unit, Pediatric Intensive Care Unit, Adult Intensive Care Unit, Neonatal Care in Incubator Department, Infection Control, Damage Control Surgery, cold chain training.
1. Save the Children will contract a local organization to conduct third-party monitoring of project activities in order to improve the quality of the program.. The third-party monitoring will increase the verification quality of the data provided by the partners, further identify issues and challenges faced, collect feedback and measure the satisfaction of beneficiaries to increase the response time for corrective supportive actions.

4 - Needs

Access to healthcare remains poor across Syria, especially in hard to reach areas and areas with increased rates of perpetual displacement, due to a lack of functioning facilities; shortages of health staff, drugs and medical supplies; and persistent clashes between various armed groups. -Idleb: The health situation in Idleb is particularly dire as a result of more than 500,000 displacements to/in the area since December 2019, coupled with limited access to health services. Improvised explosive devices, targeted assassinations, kidnappings and poor living conditions already made the situation in Idleb insecure and dangerous. -Afrin: In Afrin, 136,000 individuals are estimated to reside in the district, including more than 40,000 in Afrin city. An additional 134,000 people displaced from Afrin remains in the Tall Refaat sub-district, Nabul and Zahraa towns, and surrounding settlements. WHO has been responding to health needs of these populations since their displacement earlier this year, although it is unclear how future hostilities will impact them. Displaced people are under increased risk of infectious diseases due to limited access to safe water and sanitation, overcrowding and other risk factors. Some of these individuals are patients suffering from trauma injuries requiring post-surgical care, disability, and rehabilitation services. A majority of displaced requires continuous care for the prevention and treatment of cardiovascular and renal diseases, diabetes, cancer, psychosocial and mental health, and as well as maternal and child health services.

5 - Implementation of activities

Family Planning Services: All centers provide consultations on family planning methods to 100% of the clients, and there has been an increase in the number of clients who accept FP methods compared to the indicators planned. Following the recent displacement towards the target areas, the catchment population thus, demand in services and total reach were increasedSC and its partner will continue to provide family planning and post-abortion care services through six FP/PAC clinics located in primary care and maternity care centers in Aleppo and Idlib governorates. The health facilities have been accessible for the communities, and displaced persons and will continue to facilitate the provision of FP services within the primary care environment. Community Health Workers (CHW) actively continue to conduct household visits, delivering key messages on; danger signs of pregnancy and new-born care mothers and new-borne, as well as information about the available FP/PAC services at supported facilities. In addition, they raise awareness regarding the importance of FP and PAC, and utilized IEC materials during the household visits. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Affected populations and SC staff and volunteers, are placed at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, including harassment. Mitigating measures: Training will be provided to SC staff and volunteers on SC’s Anti-Harassment Policy and Code of Conduct. SC will also continue to provide trainings on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through SC’s PSEA focal points. Risk: The wellbeing of health workers are often neglected in the heath of urgent response and overwhelming demand for services. Mitigating measures: SC and partners have the duty to take care of the physical and mental wellbeing of their personnel. As every personnel is entitled to benefit the same services and rights of other SC workers around the world, SC ensures that its partners are following the same standards. Overall Monitoring: SC upholds the importance of setting up various channels of communication and complaint to mitigate the risks or solve the issues in a rapid and most culturally sensitive way. As some of the activities are harboring high risks for both beneficiaries and humanitarian workers (including violence against health workers/facilities, risk of sexual exploitation or harassment), SC and partners’ staff will be trained on various topics on potential risks and how to mitigate them and provided with confidential complaint mechanisms to report in case of an event. SC and partners will be promoting different communication channels to provide their feedback and complaints such as complaint boxes, phone lines, email and one on one meetings.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Escalation of military activity at Turkey/Syria border SC program locations and contingency plans are constantly reviewed and the Hibernation, Relocation and Evacuation plan is shared with staff. Offices and guesthouses are located away from military bases and equipped with S&S infrastructure. Attacks by armed groups Security assessments are conducted on potential project sites. Proximity to military locations is monitored and a back-up vehicle is used in high-risk areas. Staff transit must be pre-approved and travel in daylight. Improvised Explosive Devices Shelling Whilst most of the shelling take place in southern rural Idlib and northern rural Hama, no area is immune. SC coordinates with Tetra Tech, Mines Advisory Group cleared new project locations and Staff to receive Mine Risk Education trainings. Road accidents SC drivers are tested; daily vehicle checks; vehicles equipped with tracking devices and satellite phones; mandatory seatbelts. Disruption to cash transfer operations In NES, SC is reviewing Hawala delivery to identify mitigation measures/additional suppliers. In NWS, NGOs faced challenges in money transfer due to GoT restrictions and temporary suspension of Bab Al Hawa. Resistance from local authorities to SC's programs SC has established relationships with relevant local authorities and stakeholders (Local Councils, Ministries) and will be involved in design and implementation Assumptions Qualified staff available for recruitment Cross border access and one-year extension of UNSC
