Project Summary

Overall, there are 6,596,342 persons in need of protection support across the country. This includes 3,036,593 IDPs, 969397 returnees, 1,098,272 refugees and 1,492,080 vulnerable Sudanese. New and protracted displacement, lack of basic services and the deteriorating economic situation, have all had profound consequences on conflict-affected persons’ immediate physical and mental well-being, living standards, and their long-term resilience.
Rule of law, access to justice and government social protection remains weak in conflict-affected areas of Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile including the two areas controlled by nonstate actors. The natural disasters, including disease outbreak and scarcity of resources have further impacted public service delivery and heightened the socio-economic vulnerability of displaced families.
General protection needs are most severe in the hotspot localities, where inter-communal conflict and factional fighting have been reported throughout 2021 and people remained deprive of access to government services in the two areas. Such chaotic situation is exacerbated by the proliferation of firearms, competition over land and water resources, unregulated migratory routes, and weak systems for land administration. As a result of reported incidents: hundreds of civilians were killed or wounded; HLP damaged, looted, destroyed or occupied; thousands of families displaced; and public facilities destroyed occupied. Furthermore, UNAMID withdrawal at the end of 2020 created a gap in the civilian protection framework in Darfur. This left humanitarian protection mechanisms and services, and durable solutions programming by protection and peacebuilding actors, as the mainstay across Sudan.
Vulnerable groups - such as women, children, elderly, persons with disabilities and chronic illnesses - face a range of challenges unique to their individual circumstances, compounded by their displacement where familial/community support networks not functioning or are overstretched. These and other groups of people suffer disproportionately from various protection risks, including violence, sexual and other forms of exploitation and abuse, and human-rights violations. For women and girls, risks of GBV remain significant, particularly when they are on the move, working in fields, collecting water or firewood. Boys and men remain especially at risk of physical violence, injury and death and arbitrary arrests, as they are often suspected of supporting armed opposition groups. Child marriage and child labor are on the rise. Access to birth registration continues to be a challenge in most locations, placing both IDPs and refugees at significant risk of not accessing basic services. Persons with disabilities continue to be marginalized and neglected, with few government or humanitarian services targeted to meet their unique needs. Access to health care, particularly for the elderly and those with chronic illnesses, who are at heightened risk due to COVID-19, remains limited.
In order to meet the sectoral and intersectoral needs of the targeted population, contribute to the achievement the HRP 2022 protection sector focuses its intervention around the following specific objectives:
• Provide protection services to vulnerable crisis-affected people, particularly those with special needs;
• Strengthen community-based protection mechanisms to prevent and mitigate protection risks, and to address protection needs where feasible;
• Contribute to a favorable protection environment through engagement of and support to authorities, humanitarian and peacebuilding partners.

1 - Needs

Inter-communal violence continues to remain one of the principal drivers of protection needs in Sudan. During 2021, inter-communal violence was observed in all five Darfur states, as well as South, North & West Kordofan, Blue Nile and Kassala states. IDPs, IDP and refugee returnees in large numbers were displaced in West, South and North Darfur, Kordofan, Blue and White Nile states multiple times, many were displaced, killed, injured, their houses, villages burnt, villages occupied, livestock and personal property looted. Women, children and people with disabilities suffered the most. The violence left communities with little communal and life-saving service facilities, which in itself can become a trigger for conflict. Protection Sector has identified 64 localities with over 200 hotspot locations. In some locations, IDP hosting and returnee receiving communities developed negative sentiments towards IDPs and returnees with whom they share their scarce resources. There are also multiple barriers faced by those seeking to access justice, including lack of courts and police provision in many areas outside urban centres, and prohibitive court fees in the formal justice sector. Access to social services remained limited, traditional and formal justice mechanisms weakened or remain un-operational. In 2022 the protection sector will target and provide protection services based on the severity of needs and vulnerability determined in the HNO/HRP and on multiple parameters including age, gender, disability of individuals. In line with established objectives, UNHCR and the protection sector will address the immediate protection needs of highly vulnerable people through identifying, assisting and referring them to service providers including for provision of legal assistance for access to justice, civil documentation, cash-based intervention, follow up with relevant authorities and communities for implementation of community support projects. Furthermore, UNHCR as the lead in protection sector will strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors and duty bearers at national, state and community levels to monitor, assess, analyze, and mitigate protection risks. To achieve maximum results, partners will increase capacity through building linkages and partnerships with all stakeholders, ensuring centrality of protection and integrating protection response into the multi-sector intervention at all levels. Through a collective effort and in line with the Protection of Civilians (POC) strategy, systematic data collection including through POC incident tracking tool, data analysis and evidence-based interventions and issuance of POC advocacy briefs will be scaled up. The protection environment will be strengthened through expansion of geographical coverage and coordination of protection actors’ activities, awareness raising and community support initiatives to foster social cohesion and community participation; coordination with relevant stakeholders in monitoring; evidence-based intervention; reporting and advocacy as well as capacity-building of authorities. This approach will help all stakeholders to avoid duplication of work and provide protection intervention in a coordinated manner. In hotspot localities, existing monitoring and reporting mechanisms for protection and response will be enhanced. Protection sector jointly with other UN agencies and protection actors will support the implementation of the GoS NPPOC through capacity-building, and technical support, at national and state levels. At the policy level, the sector will work with the MoLSD and Ministries of Social Welfare at the State level, to promote the integration of vulnerable IDPs in the social protection programs and remove legal/administrative barriers. To sustain durable solutions, the sector will support the implementation of durable solution strategy, peaceful coexistence initiatives that address root causes of protection problems and strengthen rule of law.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The Protection sector ensures that in all undertakings, primary consideration will be given to our accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their own protection measures. Different segments of affected people need to be meaningfully engaged in all decisions and actions that have a direct impact on their well-being. Protection Sector will play a crucial role in supporting humanitarian actors to mainstream protection throughout all sectors and to coordinate specialized protection services for affected populations. UNHCR will provide protection services and seek solutions for IDPs, IDP and refugee returnees, while working with partners at inter-agency level for provision of additional support, as follows: - IDP response will be characterized by enhanced coordination, protection monitoring, protection by presence including expansion of field units, data collection and analysis, support to community-based protection networks, and community reconciliation committees, building early warning systems and issuance of protection of civilians briefs, provision of tangible support to persons with specific needs through provision of legal assistance including for obtaining of civil documentation, provision of and referral to psycho-social support, or cash-based interventions, youth programming, quick impact community support projects in IDP hosting and returnee receiving areas, and will contribute to durable solutions profiling and planning.- Coordination leadership of the Protection Sector will be reinforced, through already deployed five dedicated subnational protection sector coordinators, deployment of such coordinators in Blue Nile, South Kordofan and White Nile states, bringing more partners and working with them and authorities to bolster more geographical coverage for protection monitoring and response, preparedness including early warning, as well as protection assessment, analysis and response capacities.- The Centrality of Protection will be championed within the UN/HCT, including through UNHCR serving as secretariat for the HCT Protection of Civilians Coordination Structure at the national level, whichoversees the implementation of the one UN POC strategy, UN support plan for the implementation of the National Plan for Protection of Civilians. UNHCR will work with the HCT, and protection coordination forums established under UNITAMS, on advocacy related to protection of civilians, peacebuilding and durable solutions, particularly during the transition to an integrated mission. UNHCR will also bolster its advocacy with GoS on ensuring that laws and policies conform with international standards and will support implementation of GoS’ National Plan on Protection of Civilians, at national and state-levels. These advocacy efforts will be buttressed by analysis of protection needs, threats and trends.- The UNHCR/UNDP co-led Durable Solutions Working Group will prioritize the collection of relevant data to inform the inter-agency and governmental drive toward durable solutions, complemented by monitoring of returns by UNHCR to ensure they adhere to minimum standards of voluntariness, safety and dignity.- UNHCR will strengthen the integration of PSEA actions, and maintain its active contributions to the RC/HC’s PSEA Network, and actively contribute to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) approaches through co-leadership of the newly established AAP/Community Engagement WG under the ISCG.
