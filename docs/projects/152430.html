Project Summary

The Project’s ultimate goal is to ensure that the displaced and host communities affected by the recent hostilities and subsequent displacement and trauma in Urban Juba, Lainya, Yei, Kajo Keji, Maridi, and Maiwut, including child survivors of GBV, 1) have access to, and receive comprehensive, gender-sensitive, survivor-centered, life-saving psychosocial, case management and referral services; 2) rebuild social cohesion and community-based support mechanisms; and 3) are prevented from further harm. To reach this ultimate goal, the project will seek the following outcome: Enhanced life-saving and survivor-centered prevention and response protection services of quality available to/and visible to vulnerable communities affected by the recent conflict, with a particular focus on GBV survivors.
In addition, the project will work with communities to (re) build community-based prevention mechanisms and ensure a safer environment for all, especially girls, boys, women, the elderly, and people with disability – who were particularly affected by the shocks of conflict, displacement and crisis. Thus, the project will ensure that communities are capable and prepared to address threats and vulnerabilities, while maximizing existing positive coping mechanisms, in accordance with the Protection Cluster’s second strategic objective to prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience.
One key aspect of IsraAID’s mandate is to accompany national/local actors in their efforts to design, implement and evaluate their own programs to ensure local ownership and sustainability. IsraAID identifies local actors to design and implement Protection/GBV programs. IsraAID’s role is to provide continuing technical support and on-going training and mentoring to ensure that service providers in each of its partner institutions have the skills and tools needed to deliver services of quality. In order to ensure that IsraAID’s programs have a long-term impact, the organization involves and trains local partners from the need assessment to the evaluation phase (including national CBOs and service providers). Throughout its programs, IsraAID places a particular emphasis on IASC Accountability for Affected Populations Principles (AAP). It continuously trains its own staff as well as national partners on those principles and how to incorporate them into activities.
In 2019 IsraAID will engage with new partners during 2019, including, Nile Initiative Development Organization (NIDO) in Maiwut/ Upper Nile State, Facilitating Action for Community Empowerment (FACE) in Maridi, and TITI Foundation in Juba. IsraAID will also continue to work with our existing partners, including Street Children Aid (SCA) in Kajo Keji, South Sudan Health Association (SSUHA) in Lainya and Voice for Change (VfC) in Yei.

Needs

Due to fighting which broke out on July 7th, 2016 in Juba, the number of IDPs in South Sudan has grown to 1.74 million, Juba County more than doubled (143,000 individuals). In the first quarter of 2018 lower intensity fighting continued to take place in the greater Equatorias, affecting and displacing populations in Yei, Lainya, Kajo-Keji and Mundri East, all of which have been subject to continuous violence and displacement since June 2015 and the 2016 crisis, further exacerbated vulnerability factors. Services (from any tangencies including government and other NGO’s) for host and displaced women and children remain extremely poor or non-existent. However in 2018 we have also seen the return of individuals and households displaced in 2016, which due to continued insecurity of rural areas and roads are settling in main towns- which led to urban displacement by IDPs, notably in Yei and Juba with large number of returnees from Uganda settling in Kajo-Keji and Lainya Counties. In Yei there was a displacement of approximately 3,300 individuals from Lainya county in early 2018. In June 2018 around 20,700 IDP’s fled to Yei town following fighting in Yei County, additionally A TITI Foundation assessment from September 2018 found that large number of returnees who fled to Uganda and Congo in 2017 and 2018. In Lainya approximately 3,100 people were displaced from Kupera to Lainya, followed by another displacement in July 2018 to Limbe, due to armed clashes in Mukaya Payam. The most affected groups are children, women, people with disabilities and the elderly. Vulnerability factors for women and girls are exacerbated by the presence of armed elements in civilian areas which severely limits the movement of people, access to farm lands, food and services. The community faces uncontrolled levels of violence, displacement, very limited access to basic services, substantial levels of emotional distress and fear. In Kajo Keji, SCA took part of a Rapid Needs Assessment in October 2018, and high rates of GBV including rape, domestic violence and many women and children showing the affects of trauma and in need of PSS. In July 2018 FACE conducted an Sexual Gender Base Violence Baseline Survey Report for Maridi Town and found that Sexual gender base violence (SGBV) situation in Maridi remains dire and critical due to the ongoing civil war and inadequate legal instruments to curb the prevalence. The SGBV situation in Maridi town is caused by multifaceted social, political, security, economic andcultural factors that have stifled progress in mitigating the practice. Further exasperated by the current tough economic condition in the country SGBV occurrences in Maridi have increases, as many women face both physical and psychological abuse within the family and community. Across the state, women also suffer the brunt of raising children without their husbands who might have died in the civilwar. In the last five years, cases of domestic violence in families have increased resulting from inabilityto provide basic needs such as food, education, health and shelter. Meanwhile, many young teenagegirls have been married off to lessen the family burden. This has exposed such girls to sexual andphysical abuse by their spouses In Mawuit there is a gap of GBV actors. NIDO conducted an assessment in August 2018 and found high levels GBV reported by women and elders, including sexual violence, physical violence and socio-economic violence.

Activities

The project intends to save lives and alleviate suffering through safe access to services with dignity, and fully reflect the objectives of the protection cluster to ensure the availability of, and safe and free access to quality protection response services. In addition, the project will work with communities to (re)build community-based prevention mechanisms and ensure a safer environment for all, especially women, children, the elderly, pregnant women, lactating mothers, and people with disability – who were particularly affected by the shocks of conflict and displacement. Thus, the project will ensure that resilient communities are capable and prepared to address threats and vulnerabilities, while maximizing existing positive coping mechanisms, in accordance with the Protection Cluster’s strategic objectives. Project activities are defined in consultation with national partners, local leaders, communities, and beneficiaries. Activities include: • Comprehensive case management for survivors- To provide case management (including referral pathway) services and psychosocial support (including Psychological First Aid and Post-Trauma Healing) that respect minimum standards of care to GBV survivors and their communities in cooperation with health, Rule of Law and other Protection actors; • PSS, PFA, individual counselling;• Safe referrals, including information dissemination on available services;• Women & Girls Friendly Spaces & Child Friendly Spaces programming and activities; • Creation of groups (women, men, adolescent boys and girls) to reduce violence and mitigate risk factors associated with SGBV, empower women, girl, men and boys to become agents of change, to provide basic life skills to adolescent girls and boys, and female single-headed households to ensure self-sufficiency, livelihood and skills training; • Community-based protection initiatives, through mobilization and training of community-based focal points identify conflict dynamics, generate early-response mechanisms (including GBV referral pathways in cooperation with health, Rule of Law and other protection actors) • Awareness raising campaigns;• Multi-sectoral training of police, health and legal actors to ensure survivors receive minimal standards of care;• Training of Social Workers and Service providers on GBV, ethical treatment of survivors, case management, PSS, counseling and more- IsraAID will expand its training offering in 2019 and will develop additional training modules to give on the ground service delivery actors more tools and comprehensive support to survivors IsraAID and partners have been active and have strong ties with communities, all above-mentioned activities are implemented in accordance with a thorough needs assessment in consultation with community leaders and representatives.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

All above-mentioned activities are implemented in accordance with a thorough needs assessment in consultation with community leaders and representatives. Throughout the project, IsraAID will continue to organize consultative meetings with community representatives on a regular basis to ensure inclusiveness, accountability, local ownership and sustainability. In addition, IsraAID will organize mid-term monitoring FGD to assess the perception of the programs by beneficiaries and analyze intermediate targets as well as final FGD to evaluate actual outcomes and outputs. Finally, at the end of the program, IsraAID organizes a discussion with both its staff and a sample of beneficiaries to analyze the evaluation and identify best practices, lessons learned, challenges and remaining gaps for knowledge management and reporting purposes.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

IsraAID makes sure to include community members, service providers and other beneficiaries throughout the project cycle, from needs assessment to evaluation, by organizing regular FGD and consultation meetings in the communities (on a bi-weekly basis) and briefings with service providers and partners. Those discussions and briefings provide a platform of dialogue for feedback mechanisms from the beneficiaries. They offer a unique opportunity to discuss challenges, gaps and explore solutions in a participatory manner. For sensitive requests and/or complaints, beneficiaries are also informed that they can directly contact (by phone, email or at the office) a specifically identified staff, not associated with the project. IsraAID has placed a particular emphasis on accountability to affected individuals with disability, ensuring their full participation in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation frameworks of the project. IsraAID has a series of practical and user-friendly tools and checklists that all staff are required to use both at management and at the field/community level to ensure sustainability, local ownership, do-no-harm and effective implementation of program. Tools are all available to all in hard and soft copies.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Focus group discussions (FDGs) are organized at the beginning of the project to further understand baseline, at mid-term to monitor the progress of the project, and at the end of the project to evaluate impact. FGD outcomes will be documented into IsraAID's FGD tools and shared with stakeholders. IsraAID has developed and tested a series of monitoring and evaluation tools that were endorsed by UNICEF and that enable us to assess the knowledge and perceptions of communities, as well as knowledge and practice of service providers and focal points. All IsraAID's programmatic staffs as well as partners have been comprehensively trained on the utilization and analysis of such tools. The tools are available in hard and soft copies to all stakeholders.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IsraAID has developed protection mainstreaming training modules (1-2 days) for both its own staff across sectors (health, protection and education) and for its national partners. Protection Mainstreaming is one of IsraAID’s core implementing principles.IsraAID ensures protection mainstreaming by:• Training staff and national partnersIncluding do-no-harm analysis in the planning phase• Conducting regular protection mapping and safety audits to identify and be able to decrease threats and vulnerabilities, as well as identify and maximize positive coping mechanisms (see latest in Mundri in January 2016 and Urban Juba August 2016) in order to mitigate negative impacts on safety and dignity of the beneficiaries• Developing, based on protection mapping, protection risk analysis and protection mainstreaming plans, with concrete mitigation measures and strategies• Conducting regular monitoring and evaluation exercises (FGD, consultative meetings and interviews with key informants) to assess the perceptions of the community towards the program and analyze the effectiveness of the mainstreaming activities on the protection environment• Collecting best practices, lessons learned, challenges and recommendations (in IsraAID’s knowledge management matrix) to inform future humanitarian interventionsAll tools are available to all staff and partners in hard and soft copies.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project fully embraces a gender sensitive approach. A gender analysis was included in the situational analysis, need assessment and risk assessment. All Community- and Professional-Based Focus Groups (as well as formal and informal discussions/interviews) before, throughout and after the completion of the project include a proportionate and representative number of men, women, boys and girls. Each category is fully represented in the decision-making process related to each component of the project. The activities also make sure to target a proportionate and representative number of men, women, boys and girls. Even in women groups’ activities, men are consulted, invited, and updated so that they can also benefit from the fruits of the activities. When some activities only target women, such as distribution of dignity kits, the staff makes sure to fully brief men on the nature of the distribution and involve them to the extent possible.
