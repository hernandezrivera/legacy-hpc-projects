Project Summary

Nutrition Sector data for HNO HRP 2021 for the two localities (Guli and Ad Diwaim) in White Nile being proposed for this intervention is as follows (on a scale of 1-5 with 5 being worst): For U5 GAM rate, Ad Diwaim ranks 5 and Guli 4; Both localities rank 5 for stunting rate, rank 4/5 for overall nutrition sector severity, are at IPC level 3 and above for food security needs. Majority of the population are practicing open defecation due to inadequate WASH facilities, being exacerbated by recurrent floods affecting White Nile (being on banks of river Nile) and people's deteriorating purchasing power due to collapsing economy and rising inflation rate. The impact of COVID 19 in 2020 has wreaked havoc on basic needs of vulnerable populations in White Nile.
AAH is present in Sudan since 2018, with operational bases in Khartoum, White Nile, Central Darfur and South Kordofan states. AAH is currently implementing two emergency interventions in White Nile State as part of the COVID-19 response (funded by GFFO) and flood response (funded by SIDA).
Overall objective of proposed intervention is Reduce severe undernutrition on children & PLWs of emergency-affected & highly vulnerable households through multi sectoral life-protecting interventions. Project targets already vulnerable (in terms of nutrition, health and WASH indicators)14 communities in 2 localities, comprising of around 190,209 people, recovering from shocks in 2020 (i.e. floods emergency and COVID 19 and its cascading impact on economy and livelihoods) while these communities were not fully recovered from prior shocks (for instance, 2019 floods emergency). Assistance will be in form of nutrition (support to essential primary health care and nutrition public services, as well as integrated community case management), WASH at health facility and community levels (improving access to basic water services, adequate (basic or limited) protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services and hygiene promotion services), and mental health care practices gender protection (IYCF, sensitisation on gender discriminatory harmful traditional practices such as FGM and early marriage). Community participation will be fostered via capacity building, mobilization and follow-up of community volunteers that will engage in sensitization activities (related to nutrition, health, hygiene, sanitation, gender). Some volunteers will also be supported to identify and manage cases of undernutrition and common childhood illnesses in the community. Select volunteers will be provided training to be able to facilitate participatory vulnerability capacity assessments (PVCA) at their community levels. Community volunteers will conduct screening and referral of malnourished children and PLW; they will identify, treat and refer children with common childhood illness, do rapid IYCF assessment at HH level and refer PLWs with IYCF problem, conduct home visit, defaulter tracing and community sensitization. AAH and their local partner in White Nile, CAFA, will co implement this project together, as per AAH localization strategy in Sudan. The proposed intervention will encompass emergency preparedness activities for sustainability of this intervention and as exit strategy - AAH and CAFA will establish Water Sanitation User Committees (WSUC), Disaster Risk Management (DRM) Committees at community levels, in addition to Mother-to-Mother support groups, and provide extensive trainings to community volunteers on several essential topics. All volunteers will be trained on needs assessment and AAH complaint response mechanism.
Target population is based on HNO 2021 data and White Nile MoH data, with vulnerability selection criteria focusing on very poor and vulnerable households. Key beneficiaries are 14 OTPs and 56 staff (4 per center); 150 community volunteers; Mass Screening: 32,336 (16,491 girls & 15,845 boys) ; 4300 HH receiving hygiene & dignity kits; soft activities to target all 14 village populations.

1 - Needs

In White Nile State, the food insecurity rate is 11.6 (Phase 3) and 6.2% (phase 4) which got aggravated after the 2020 floods and COVID 19 crisis and its cascading threats. This has a huge impact on nutrition and health status of populations. According to Sudan Nutrition sector HNO-HRP 2020 people in need projection based on S3 survey, El Douiem locality GAM rate is 17.4% which is above emergency threshold based on WHO classification (SAM rate 4.1%) while Guli Locality GAM rate is 12.4% (‘serious’ according to WHO standard); PLW GAM rate is 5.2% and 12% in El Douiem and Guli respectively. Preliminary results from U5 children and PLW MUAC screening in October 2020 conducted by AAH in Guli and Tendalty localities of White Nile indicates that 3% SAM, 8% MAM (U5) and 14% MAM PLW has been identified and referred to health facilities. Access to safe water is a chronic issue in White Nile, with many families having reduced water consumption at the household level or resort to secondary (lower quality) water sources such as seasonal rivers and rain catchment systems. In terms of access to improved sanitation service, the state coverage is 39% while at national level is 40,9%, with huge discrepancies between urban and rural areas (only 3,3% of coverage for rural areas of White Nile). Based on findings of HAC across 9 flood-affected localities in White Nile during assessment in 2020, flood water destroyed or damaged at least 7661 houses homes, displacing almost 6000 households, and impacted WASH and health facilities According to assessment reports of Plan International in several villages in 3 localities, including Guli and El Duiem, 85 % - 95% of the households assessed have no latrines as most of them collapsed due to heavy rains.. Household members reported diseases such as diarrhea, malaria and upper respiratory infection. Also, in several villages Plan International have assessed, health facilities (for most villages, miles away) have no capacity for nutrition treatment, lab facilities or EPI services. During post-floods joint assessment conducted by HAC, MOH and NGOs, including AAH, in 2019, key informants at Guli and El Douiem localities had indicated that latrines were completely destroyed resulting in contamination of drinking water sources and spread of diseases transmitters such as mosquitoes, houseflies and other insects leading to high cases of acute watery diarhoea, cholera, malaria, typhoid and inflammatory diseases. They reported people were living closely together with not enough sanitation facilities for them thereby forcing them to resort to defecating in open, near their shelter, neighbors‘ homes and water sources, which increased the risks of spreading of harmful diseases throughout the communities.AAH conducted baseline assessment in Guli and Tendalty localities this year during floods emergency, sampling 540 households (out of 3000 households targeted beneficiaries under a couple of emergency response projects in White Nile being implemented by AAH and funded by GFFO and Sida RRM), and 53% respondents said health was their biggest recurring challenge followed by water (30%). 35% respondents said they were unable to access a health facility. 63% respondents said that U5 children in their family had fallen sick during prior 2 weeks mostly from malaria (28%) and diarrhea (31%). 33% respondents said that PLWs in their family had fallen sick during last 2 weeks (from date of assessment). 29% respondents said their primary source of water is open, unprotected well and 21% said it was lake / pond. More than half of respondents said they do not treat water before drinking. 44% said they have to go to fetch water twice or more times in a given day. Only 38% respondents said they use soap and water for handwashing. Over 73% respondents said they practice open defecation. Nutrition, health and WASH needs are considered urgent as a result.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Community based screening, detection and referral of undernutrition cases: AAH will conduct household level screening for children 6-59 month and PLWs through Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) using standard community screening tools and refer to the nutrition centers for full anthropometric measurements and enrollment in the respective programs. Coverage of CNVs will be 1:250 HH who will be trained in key outreach activities, CMAM, ICCM and IYCF. CNVs will conduct rapid IYCF assessment for PLW and refer for counseling, defaulter tracing and home visits for children enrolled in the CMAM.1433 children (6-59 months and > 5 years) with SAM without medical complications will be admitted in the OTP, and receive systematic treatment; antibiotics, Vitamin A, deworming and referral to EPI for measles and other vaccines. Beneficiaries will receive weekly therapeutic food rations and follow up with appetite tests. Beneficiaries who develop poor appetite and/or medical complications are referred to the SC. Beneficiaries who fail to gain weight as expected to come to OTP in a daily basis for close follow up. All MAM and SAM children admitted in nutrition program are screened according to predefined vulnerability criteria (e.g. frequent relapse, defaulters, non-respondents’ case, poor mother child interaction etc.) and are referred for specific MHCP support in response to the identified needs. To mitigate the impact of under nutrition on child’s development, children receive age specific stimulation through mother-child play session reinforcing mother-child bonding and age appropriate development. Evidence-based actions will be carried out following the IASC MHPSS intervention pyramid aims to reduce distress, provide psychological treatment and promote general coping and wellbeing which is necessary to support the pregnant and lactating women and caregivers of malnourished childrenAAH has used PIN GAM and MAM rates to calculate the caseloads using incidence rate of 2.6% and coverage rate of 60% based on population of 14 villages in Guli and Ad Duwaim localities. Population figures were provided directly by MoH. ICCM intervention will focus on building capacity of health personnel and CHWs, provision of essential supplies, minor rehabilitation of health facility, support health information system and community mobilization. Integration with other health services (EPI, Maternal service..) will be essential focus. Children with common childhood illness (Malaria, ARI and Diarrhea)will be identified and treated by trained volunteers and health workers. To ensure quality of service, AAH will employ follow-up after training and performance review meeting using standard checklist tools and reporting system. AAH will provide equipment, training and technical support in 14 primary health care facilities. AAH will rehabilitate handpumps and communal latrines at health facility and community levels for improved access to basic water services and gender-sensitive sanitation services and hygiene promotion services; CLTS activities budgeted for community level for supporting their rehabilitation of 1744 latrines; AAH will establish WSUC at community level ; AAH to provide training and tool kits for operations and maintenance to health staff and WSUC; Distribution of hygiene kits to HH with U5 children and PLWs; AAH will support volunteers for community awareness, esp. among religious leaders and HH influencers/enablers (mothers in law, grandmothers, elderly) about harmful impact of early marriage and FGM, identification and referral of cases; Distribution of dignity kits for targeted households with women and girls of reproductive age AAH will support volunteers for formation of DRM committees to improve community preparedness capacity for floods, and to facilitate PVCA on monthly basis. AAH – CAFA will facilitate linkage of plans and assessments with emergency task force, HAC, relevant ministries and humanitarian actors in state.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Throughout the intervention, impact on environment / natural resources will be considered within the possibilities offered in the emergency context. Any lasting changes to landscape and / or infrastructure be accounted for and done in the least invasive manner possible. Throughout the intervention the activities of the environmental solid waste campaigns for instance the plastics/solid materials will be given special consideration towards environmental devastating or depletion of the natural resources like water, forestry, thus the plastics and solid waste will be disposed safely. During construction and rehabilitation of hand pumps and communal latrines, special consideration will be given to the natural resources for instance the woods will not be used, however the friendly approaches will be practiced. In the case of the water station rehabilitation to mitigate the impact of the environment, the diesel system will be experienced to replace solar system as new technology. Following construction and rehabilitation of the infrastructures either water systems or communal latrines will adopt environmental mitigation by avoiding natural resources depletion, meanwhile different community committees will be selected, trained and raised their awareness to conserve the water, forest and food. Also, communities becoming open defecation free (ODF) following CLTS approach will have sustainable positive impact on environment, community, household and personal hygiene and contribute to reduced morbidity and mortality especially of U5 children.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Gender dimension at all stages of the intervention will be considered through an active and inclusive participatory approach, working closely with CAFA and all stakeholders on the ground, ensuring appropriate responses throughout the ground for each target group: girls, women, boys, men and disabled groups. Activity data will be collected and disaggregated by sex and age where possible, and the results of their analysis will be used to adjust the program.AAH will also ensure gender balance in each deployed team, including maximizing women's and girls' access to the project and facilitating communication, awareness and exchange of information. AAH will also ensure that any arising doubt on protection or gender-based violence related incidents are identified and forwarded to the relevant partners for appropriate action. According to assessment findings of Plan International in Guli and El Duiem localities, girls are being subjected to early marriage (indirectly related to multi layered crisis this year) and boys and their families are refusing marriage if girls have not had FGM (even though it has been banned in Sudan this year). Also, girls and women are exposed highly to protection concerns during floods outbreak due to collapse of infrastructure, especially water and sanitation. AAH strongly adheres to the principles of Do No Harm and given the specific context will ensure close monitoring of project implementation and post-implementation in order to ensure supported rights holders are not subjected to negative externalities as a direct result of AAH or CAFA activities. Furthermore, Protection will be at all cases considered and AAH and CAFA will continue to actively work together with partners to ensure Protection remains a topic on the radar. AAH works in close collaboration with the UN Clusters (WASH, Health and NUT and the technical working group), which remind and include protection rules in the response so that all actors are in line with the same standards. Therefore, AAH and CAFA will follow the clusters’ recommendations together with their own standards against GBV. As with any emergency, there will be a Protection working group which includes the specific actors who are tasked with protection. AAH and CAFA will also ensure that any arising doubt on protection or gender-based violence related incidents are identified and forwarded to the relevant partners for appropriate action.Through proposed intervention, AAH will provide sensitise communities, including male community and religious leaders, on gender discriminating practices and provide dignity kits for girls and women of reproductive age. Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) is also mobilized by AAH MEAL through multiple channels (hotline numbers, complaint boxes) in order to maximise potential for having feedback from marginalized community members and enable them to have direct access to AAH, for reporting on any concerns, including protection issues.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

AAH is an active team member of the Emergency Cluster as well the emergency taskforce committee at federal and state level, with regular meetings with UNICEF, WFP and UNHCR for constant updates of the situation. Moreover through our active participation with local authorities, especially Ministry of Health, Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation and HAC, humanitarian actors and clusters / sectors at state and federal levels, AAH ensures that assistance is meant for households in hard to reach areas, areas going through protracted crisis and which have not received assistance from other actors in recent months. AAH is coordinating as well with different donors among which ECHO, UNICEF, WFP, FCDO, OCHA, GIZ, among others, in order to deepen our understanding about the humanitarian situation. AAH, as a technical partner with its nutrition expertise, is most welcomed and our integrated intervention approach is in line with national strategies to build resilience. In addition, AAH is in process of a partnership with Ministry of Health for building capacity for conducting nutrition surveys. AAH is a member of Nutrition Sector, WASH Sector, FSL Sector and from 2021 January, will be a member of GBV sub sector. AAH works in close collaboration with the UN Clusters (WASH, Health and NUT and the technical working group), which remind and include protection rules in the response so that all actors are in line with the same standards. Therefore, AAH and CAFA will follow the clusters’ recommendations together with their own standards against GBV. As with any emergency, there will be a Protection working group which includes the specific actors who are tasked with protection. AAH and CAFA will also ensure that any arising doubt on protection or gender-based violence related incidents are identified and forwarded to the relevant partners for appropriate action.
