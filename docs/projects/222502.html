Project Summary

Gender-Based Violence (GBV) remains a pervasive issue affecting individuals across all societies, but particularly those in marginalized and underserved communities. SAADO GBV intervention is aimed to end violence against women, promote gender equality and empower survivor through a multi-faceted approach. This will be achieved through the following GBV cluster objective; 1) Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. 2) Promoting nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, exacerbated by systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women and girls and PLW
The GBV intervention will targets the most Vulnerable Women, Girls, boys, Men and PLWD of the Returnees, IDPs and the host communities affected by shocks including Flood, conflict and influx of returnees and refugee in the hard-to-reach areas of Rubkona, Leer, Mayom, Makalal, RENK, Pigi, Fangak, Aweil North and Aweil East.
The beneficiaries for this project will be 16583; 7004 Women, 2598 men, 4709 Girls and 2273 boys respectively. With a budget of $600,000 over a period of 12 month from Jan to Dec 2025 , the project will focus on increasing access to, and quality GBV prevention, Risks mitigation and GBV response through the following key activities; awareness-raising on GBV and PSEA, integration of GBV actions into other cluster’s response, conducting multi-sectoral safety audits, Distribution of DKs, FES, Livelihood support, case management, PSS, Cash assistance, legal aid, safety and protection, WGFS, GBV referral pathways and GBV coordination and Data analysis through GBVIMS
For accountability to the affected population (AAP), SAADO will create a committee in each county that will compose of 50% women, 20% Men and 30% people living with disability (PLWD) to oversee the project implementation as from its initiation. The beneficiaries will be passing on their concerns and feedback to the committee after a series of consultation, then the committee will share feedback with the partner to enhance the quality of the project. The involvement of the community will be key in building trust and creating a sense of ownership thus increasing the sustainability of the project in the targeted locations. The GBV intervention will be implement in conflict sensitive manner to reduce the negative effect to the proposed beneficiaries. On inclusion of disability, barrier analysis and implementation of action plan will be key in this intervention. To enhance PSEA, training on PSEA will be provided to the direct staff and key community member who are directly involved in this project.
SAADO will mainstream protection in this project by contacting risks analysis and continuous monitoring access, Safety and dignity of all targeted beneficiaries. SAADO has prominent presence in this targeted location, implementing GBV project in Malakal, RENK, Rubkona, Mayom, Leer and Fangak. SAADO propose to expand its GBV programming to area that it operates including Aweil North, Aweil East and Pigi to enhance integration. All the mentioned area, we do have established offices and mean of transport thus reducing operation budget. Integration of GBV activities with other sector including Protection, WASH, FSL, Health, Edu and Nut will be key to enhance synergy in the targeted area. We will foster community ownership and engagement by involving local leaders, activists, and stakeholder in planning, implementation and monitoring of the project for sustainability. For sustainability of this project, we will seek partnership with Government and developmental organization. SAADO will regularly report GBV intervention in 5w and update FTS

1. Needs

Gender-based violence is one of the most critical threats to the protection and wellbeing of women and girls in hard-to-reach areas including Rubkona, Mayom, Leer, Malakal, RENK, FASHODA, Fangak, Pigi, Aweil East and Aweil North counties of South Sudan where SAADO has predominantly presence. Women and girls in proposed counties including the one fleeing conflict in Sudan continue to face high risks of GBV including rape, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), intimate partner violence and physical violence. Adolescent girls are even more vulnerable with child marriage increasing in these counties. The GBV is largely driven by harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality, severely affecting the safety and rights of women and girls. GBV was already rife prior to the conflict and is now nearing epidemic proportions. The full magnitude of the problem is unclear due to many cases being unreported. UNICEF studies reports indicate that some 65% of women and girls have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime, and some 51% have suffered intimate partner violence (IPV). Majority of girls and women experience sexual violence for the first time under the age of 18. The risk of child marriage remains high due to hard economic and harmful norms that perpetuate issues of GBV. Protection risks Monitoring system reported GBV as the main protection risk in 38 per cent of the 2,497 communities surveyed in 2024. The ISNA revealed that 36 per cent of women and girls avoid certain areas due to safety concerns including GBV, while 53 per cent of households’ face barriers in accessing vital services including response to GBV survivors. The report continues to state that, protection concerns for girls differs slightly with that of boys, with forceful marriage being the most prevalent concern reported by survey respondents at 15.5 per cent. Other concerns include threats of violence (10.2%) and exploitation (8.5%). Qualitative findings suggest that women and girls are at an increased risk of experiencing sexual and gender-based violence including rape. With severe food insecurity, according to IPC data projection of September and November 2025 state that 10000 people in Malakal are in Catastrophe food insecure stage, due to economic stress and less harvest. The other targeted counties are in Phase 4 classification which show higher level of hunger. Limited food which has been more exacerbated by higher number of returnees predisposed women and girls to walk further to forage for food, firewood and other necessities to survive, exposing them to a greater risk of rape and other forms of GBV. In many communities in the targeted counties, there is a lack of comprehensive education about GBV, leading to widespread misconceptions, denial, and underreporting of incidents. Survivors often face substantial barriers to accessing necessary support services, including counselling, legal assistance, and safe spaces to gaps in lifesaving GBV services. The absence of culturally sensitive and accessible support mechanisms contributes to prolonged suffering and impedes recovery. Furthermore, inadequate reporting and response mechanisms undermine efforts to provide timely and appropriate care, and the lack of strong policy frameworks means that legal protections and preventive measures are insufficiently enforced. Generally Displaced individuals, PLWD, returnees and those living in poverty are disproportionately affected, enduring severe physical, sexual and psychological abuse. Climate change and displacement play a considerable role in the increase of GBV. Recurrent flooding has left many households losing their homes and crops, causing financial hardship and internal displacement. The continued influx of returnees and refugees due to Sudan crisis have further exacerbated the humanitarian situation. This situation necessitates urgent GBV prevention, risk mitigation and expanded response services.

2. Project activities

SAADO proposal project will use several strategies to address the critical GBV situation in the targeted counties. The strategies will focus on prevention, risks mitigation and response. The GBV prevention will aim to challenge harmful norms through community program such as SASA together! Community cares program and engaging men and boys through accountable practices. The prevention strategy will also include community engagement initiatives such as awareness-raising on GBV and PSEA. GBV risk mitigation involves integrating GBV actions into other clusters’ responses, conducting multi-sectoral safety audits and dignity kits and fuel-efficient stoves. GBV response focuses on improving survivors’ access to services such as case management, psychosocial support, Cash assistance, legal aid, safety and protection, while enhancing availability of safe houses, Women and Girls friendly spaces, and GBV referral pathways. SAADO will continually attend GBV coordination meeting in the county, State and National level to learn and adopts innovation. Through the project we will enhance collection and data analysis by strengthening GBV information management systems. To ensure the project deliver the key needed services to the needy beneficiaries, SAADO will implement activities that are aligned with GBV cluster intervention; survivor response, Prevention and risk mitigation, to provide quality services to the vulnerable returnee’s, IDPs and host communities including PLWD survivors of Gender base violence. This will include • Conduct GBV awareness raising on GBV risks, available response services, PSEA and timely reporting to targeted Vulnerable community including person living with disability (PLWD) through campaigns (16 days of activism and international women days) door to doors, radio talk, and focus group discussion and printing IEC material with local language on BCC. • Prevention of GBV through SASA, Community cares Programming, engaging men and boys for accountable practice; confront harmful practices including child marriage, violence against women and girls including the genuine empowerment of girls and women• Supporting and establishing communities’ structures to mitigate risks, prevent and respond to GBV in the nine targeted counties• Provide cash and voucher assistance to the most vulnerable women and girls • Provision of Dignity kits to the vulnerable women and adolescent girls in the targeted counties• Fuel -efficient stoves (FES). • Provision of livelihood activities guided by the context including pastry, soap making, Mat making etc • Establishment and training of community structure members on GBV basic survivor centred approach including PSEA• Training of community leaders and staff on GBV basic survivor centred approach, referral pathway and community facilitation skills. • Conduct safety audits to identify GBV risks and implement actions to address them • Training of service providers on GBV integration in their sectoral response and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA)• Provide training on CMR to the health personnels in the hospital and PHCC to be improve knowledge and skill on how to manage rape cases.• Identify and provide support to 9 Women and girls friendly spaces (WGFS) and 9 mobile team/ Outreach team • Provide comprehensive GBV case management services and psychosocial support to survivors of GBV and at-risk women, boys, girls including persons living with disability (PLWD) of the returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host communities in the WGFS • Provide tailored materials (PSS) support to extremely vulnerable survivors in WGFS• Establish and support referral pathways in the targeted counties • Monthly reporting of the GV intervention on Cluster 5W • Resource Mobilization and Regularly updating financial tracking services (FTS)
