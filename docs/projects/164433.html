Project Summary

The overall objective of the Emergency Assistance for Syrian Households (EASH) project is to improve food security among vulnerable populations in Syria. This will be achieved addressing the emergency and protracted needs of vulnerable communities, utilizing a food voucher enabling vulnerable men, women, boys and girls access to approximately 70% of monthly food requirements.
Priority governorates identified by the F.A.S and where ADRA has operational presence and experience include Rural Damascus and Homs. Exact areas will be selected in close cooperation and coordination with the sector, as well as other humanitarian actors including WFP, SARC and FAO ensuring no response overlap. Areas will be prioritized by food security severity, presence of IDPs/returnees and newly accessible areas where assistance has been limited.
A total of 10,000 assessed food insecure households will be targeted to receive a monthly food assistance for eight months of the twelve-month program through a food voucher equivalent to the amount of $60, redeemable with local vendors for a specific range of culturally sensitive food items.

4 - Needs

In 2019 and early 2020, the protracted nature of the crisis in Syria continued to deteriorate food security conditions, with an estimated 9.8 million nation-wide affected and 7.3 million severely food insecure (39% of the population). Significant need exist in the targeted areas of Homs and Rural Damascus with many communities classified with a CARI score between 4 and 6. Food insecurity remains at critical levels with many individuals moderately to severely food insecure: 39% of Rural Damascus and 24% in Homs. (FAS. 2019 Extra indicators Factsheet).Food costs have soared and an estimated 70% of Syrians now spend 65% of their household income on food. It is also estimated that 57% of Syrian households resort to stress, emergency, or crisis level negative coping strategies such as a reduction in consumption by adults so children can eat, or a less diverse diet, placing additional pressure on women, young children, the elderly and people with special needs who have additional nutritional requirements for health. Food insecurity impacts severely and differently on different groups. IDPs have experienced multiple displacements, the loss of livelihoods and the subsequent lack of a stable income. Returnees struggle to re-establish an income and reintegrate into conflict-affected, impoverished communities. While the host communities must now share already strained resources with both returnees and IDPs. IDPs and returnees are also vulnerable due to a depletion in assets and loss of livelihood opportunities, with 46% reporting inadequate food consumption (OCHA, Draft HNO 2020). In all target areas, the community members most vulnerable to food insecurity remain female-headed households, children, people with a disability and the elderly.

5 - Implementation of activities

Potential beneficiaries will be identified through local partners and then verified to ensure that they meet the selection criteria of the project, in line with FAS recommendations which include household demographic indicators (female headed households, PLW, people with a disability, income, children under 5), access to income and accommodation status. Where possible, ADRA will identify female household members to receive vouchers as women are generally responsible for food preparation in the household. In this way, women will have a greater degree of control over food purchases.Each household will receive a monthly voucher, based on FAS guidelines estimated to cover a minimum of 1,500 Kcal per person per day of family food rations (approximately 70% of daily requirements) for a period of eight months which will allow the targeted households an opportunity to return to their previous livelihood or income generating activities and for communities to reinforce newly reestablished market linkages. Items will be identified for redemption under the voucher and will include items in the basic food parcel, as well as additional, nutritionally valuable commodities such as fresh fruit and vegetables, dairy, canned fish and other culturally acceptable items, designed to meet the nutritional needs of children, adolescents, adults, older adults, people with disabilities, as well as male and female. Nutritional and health awareness messages will be included in the voucher distribution and printed on the vouchers.Special consideration will also be given to encourage participation of females and people with disabilities through allowing a secondary family member to assist with the voucher collection and redemption, providing special services for transportation if necessary, gender and disability friendly distribution points and a gender balanced distribution team.Monitoring of the project will take place during distribution, as well as through post distribution monitoring.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

ADRA Syria will ensure that the FAV project works closely with all stakeholders, following a ‘do no harm’ approach however, due to the nature of conflict, it is possible that some risks may occur. There is a risk that the food/assistance will not be shared equally among family members, or that beneficiary households will be put under pressure to share the food redeemed through the vouchers or in-kind assistance provided with other families. There is also a risk of SEA from project staff or stakeholders of the project for beneficiaries to gain access to the assistance, or that beneficiaries will face violence or discrimination during the distribution or for being a project beneficiary. There is also the possibility that project staff will face undue pressure to add households to the beneficiary list that do not meet the required selection criteria. In all cases outlined above, ADRA will maintain strong community connections with multiple stakeholders, clearly outlining the aim, purpose and selection criteria of the project to all concerned. Tools will be developed to ensure the impartial selection of beneficiaries and awareness raising will take place among the stakeholders, including beneficiaries themselves about their rights and ways in which to report potential abuse or discrimination. ADRA staff will be trained in protection mitigation measures and humanitarian principals and extensive monitoring will take place to identify and mitigate any potential risks. Project monitoring will be designed to ensure that feedback is provided by children, adolescents, adults, older adults and people with disabilities, including male and female through key informant interviews, post distribution monitoring and the complaints mechanism, and that this feedback is taken into account when designing activities, making changes and assessing the success of the project.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Several risks and constraints could impact on the success of the project. Contextual risk: Although the security situation in target areas relatively stable, it is possible that conflict could reignite. ADRA will monitor the security situation of each area throughout the project lifecycle. ADRA will also provide basic services in many highly vulnerable areas which may place staff, beneficiaries or stakeholders at risk. ADRA will monitor the situation on the ground though UNDSS, local staff and local stakeholders to minimize the risk of harm to staff and beneficiaries. ADRA staff will be trained on security concerns to help prevent incidents. Unequal distribution of services: Due to the unpredictable and often high numbers of spontaneous self-organised returns to newly-accessible areas, it is possible that ADRA Syria will not have access to accurate records of the most vulnerable communities. ADRA will work closely with local communities and other stakeholders in order to obtain the most accurate information possible. Risk of fraud or misuse of project funds: ADRA Syria has board approved financial management, Anti-Fraud and procurement policies in place, that also include whistle-blowing, conflict of interest and anti-corruption policies. Project risks: The delay or difficulty in obtaining the necessary approvals needed to implement the project activities is a key project risk. ADRA will maintain regular contact with the relevant ministries and will encourage active involvement by all stakeholders in each stage of the project. ADRA will have a beneficiary feedback mechanism in place under this programme to ensure appropriate accountability and prevent harm to beneficiaries. ADRA already has an established presence in targeted locations with reliable contractors available to undertake activities.
