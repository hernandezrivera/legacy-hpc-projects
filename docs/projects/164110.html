Project Summary

The humanitarian situation across northwest Syria, particularly in Idleb and Aleppo governorates continue to deteriorate at an alarming rate due to ongoing violence, which has a devastating effect on the four million people in northwest Syria.
This plan aims to provide key and complementary services to cover the immediate needs for the most vulnerable newly displaced groups, supporting their resilience and preparedness.
This plan responds to the need of the most vulnerable groups including women, men, boys and girls all ages, both IDPs and host vulnerable communities as well as people with disabilities as the following breakdown:
IDPs [250,000] Host groups[20,000] Returnees[18,000]
- Girls 63,400 - Boys 55,300 - Females 77,900 - Males 91,400
People with disabilities 3,600.
It is to respond to the Sector basic objective of "Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support
Reaching the most severe communities the main outputs of this plan are:

- Camp operational and Regular maintenance activities.

(Vouchers to provide access to a range of goods/services, Multi-purpose - conditional and unconditional)

WATAN 2020 plan aims to provide suitable environmental conditions for camp residents and increase the capacity of IDP sites. By providing these services, WATAN responds to the shelter sector growing needs by reducing protection risks and working towards better stable early recovery systems.
This approach aims to promote peaceful coexistence and to reduce gender-based violence by taking into consideration the different needs of women, girls, men, and boys and how they are influenced by the shelter conditions.
Based on a technical assessment proving the availability of essential services (such as markets, public services, infrastructures…), also, taking into consideration HLP rights, WATAN will be operating in vulnerable communities following the Protection, vulnerability, and prioritizing the most in need Reference for Northwest Syria guidance and the AAP framework.

4 - Needs

Based on the primal HNO figures and the weekly and monthly updates from Shelter/NFI Cluster - XB, where updates are indicating the mass waves of displacement in Northwest Syria due to the control changes and the escalation starting from the beginning of 2020, WATAN conducted a survey to better understand the needs and status of IDP households. Implemented in 30 communities including 50 camps, the field survey included questions related to how IDPs cope with winter conditions, and imminent threats to vulnerable categories, such as women, children, and the elderly. Women accounted for 20% of the survey sample. Since the conduction of the baseline survey, WATAN has served more than 4,500 IDP households with NFIs, Tents and Winter Clothes from the beginning of 2020. Data are monthly updated as a monitoring tool to help to identify and cross-check the needs with Shelter/NFI Cluster online response overview, through a scoring system containing the following:1- Newly Displaced households who do not have secure access to camps and camp services and/or living in temporary or makeshift settlements.2- Households have more than 5 members.3- Female-headed / Elderly headed households.4- Households headed by a vulnerable person (with a disability - no secure source of income).5- Households containing pregnant or breastfeeding women - member/s with chronic disease - member/s with special needs. 6- Households hosting orphan, unaccompanied, or homeless relatives. Assessment findings:- 100% of respondents confirmed they do not have any methods to cope with the harsh winter while living in their tents, shelters or residential buildings (these are not equipped for the winter).- 2,000 households (11,895 individuals) are living in hand-made tents. Of the 11,895 individuals, 255 are people with disabilities, 243 are individuals with chronic diseases, 917 are pregnant women, 746 are orphaned minors, and 481 are female-led households.

5 - Implementation of activities

Community Selection: • As mentioned, the targeted areas will be communities with 4/5 severity scale. • Conduct needs assessment and identifies in-need locations in different IDP camps in both Aleppo and Idlib governorates. • Conduct pre and post-assessments using mobile data collection applications. • Coordinate response with different SNFI cluster members. • Obtain Housing, Land and Property agreements in accordance with the HOUSING, LAND AND PROPERTY RIGHTS IN SHELTER DUE DILIGENCE GUIDELINES cluster reference. 2- Community Engagement: Procedures and policies to involve targeted communities in action were built to build partnerships with IDPs women and men of all ages and backgrounds by promoting meaningful participation through structured dialogue. Separate discussions with women, girls, boys, and men, including adolescents, enable to gather accurate information on the specific protection risks that different groups face, identify underlying causes, understand refugees' capacities, and hear their proposed solutions. 3- Beneficiary and Criteria Selection: Eligibility and vulnerability indicators will be used in a specific scoring system. Eligibility Group: IDPs - Households who lost their shelter and do not have suitable living conditions. Beneficiaries from the Eligible group will be selected based on the ranking of the vulnerability index: 1- living in the IDP temporary settlement site. 2- Household no source of income or no other means of financial support. 3- Female and/or a single person head the household. 4- Household headed by an elderly, minor or person with a disability. 5- Household host orphaned, unaccompanied children, or homeless relatives due to the conflict. 6- Household member/s with a physical disability, mental disability/illness, special needs or limiting conditions.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Assumptions:1- BAH and BAS borders will continue to remain open.2- HLP will be taken into consideration before delivering field works or any assistance.3- Flexible in-site implementation/reallocation, when certain areas are inaccessible.4- Good collaboration with the IDPs and host communities.5- Access will be conducive to deliver assistance. - Risks:1- Borders closure can affect money transferring or any cross-border and local activities.2- Staff mobility and access restrictions to the affected population.3- The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy. - Risk Mitigation:1- Coordinate with Shelter/NFI Cluster to avoid any delay or lack of approvals.2- WATAN will utilize HLP methodologies to investigate the ownership of land and discuss with owners and available site management.3- Deconfliction referencing with UN OCHA.4- Daily inventory, control the treasury better.5- Unlikely Moderate Stable Local information shows an increase in conflict levels thus developing a contingency plan to work in an alternative location if the conflict continues.6- Coordination with local councils for distribution in their quality as partners.7- Signature of memorandums of understanding with local councils to gain confidence and to ensure the success of the project.8- Announcing project activities and criteria for selecting beneficiaries in distribution areas.9- Have a joint programmatic contingency planning with Shelter/NFI Cluster.10- Strategies to prevent and mitigate Gender-Based Violence have been included in all registration and verification of beneficiaries in the initial assessment, where the shelter rehabilitation works will aim at reducing dependence on harmful coping strategies (such as sexual exploitation) and ensuring no women and girls are exposed to the risk of GBV during or after the intervention, with special attention to sexual and physical violence, denial of resources and psychological abuse.
