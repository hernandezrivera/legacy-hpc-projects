Project Summary

Cholera has persistently continued seasonal outbreak in South Sudan between April to June and sometimes in January to February every year and so far enabled classification of hotspot with major four types presenting high level of cholera infection and cholera-related deaths. Whereas other areas prone to conflict continue to suffer from access constraint and reporting challenge “In-depth cholera epidemiological report for South Sudan April 2018”. The poor set up of South Sudan towns and inadequate water facilities to provide safe water to ordinary citizens has however turned-in Juba, the capital OF South Sudan to type 1 cholera hotspot with the highest infections and cholera-related death rate. Only 10% of the population have access to at least basic sanitation. The percentage is even lower in rural settings with approximately 6% of the population using improved facilities that are not shared with other household. 61% of the population resorted to open defecation “The last survey conducted by the Joint Monitoring Programme (JMP) in 2015” indicates.
The economic shocks; the devaluation of currency and inflation of prices of commodities in the local market has made all-times available truck water unaffordable by majority of the working poor in Juba. Thereto, the shock has also caused life hardship to extend that households prefer and prioritize consumable stuffs over good hygiene and sanitation practices in the house and it surrounding.
The five year political conflict in South Sudan has obviate development initiatives especially water infrastructure development in and at the Peri-urban of Juba Town and the population remain vulnerably expose to risk of contracting communicable disease during rainy season for instance, cholera and Acute water diarrhoea (AWD). The lack of knowledge and practices to properly prevent cholera infection at household level and community-level proves significant challenge in preparedness, response and prevention for control of seasonal outbreak of cholera in Juba.
Therefore, this action will reach 7000 individuals (50% males and 50% females) at Nesitu, Sherkat and Juba II to provide opportunity to capacitate women and girls on prevention of cholera, form and training community-based Cholera monitoring committee, empower school ongoing children on hygiene and sanitation practices, provide water and sanitation facilities to cemetery, establish and equip ORPs, provide variety WASH NFI and Household Disinfection Kits. These activities are aims at increasing early detection of cholera outbreak cases, enhance surveillance in the community and provide knowledge and skill for community to manage future emergency.

Needs

Juba county host estimate population of 579778 individuals and is identified and classified among 18 cholera hotspots as type 1 with 5 recurrence of cholera outbreak and accumulatively lasting for average of 26 weeks with attack rate of 43 out of 10000 “the In-depth cholera epidemiological report for South Sudan April 2018”. Its suburb of Nesitu, Sherkat and Juba II hold over 71000 individuals with no functional public health facility, very expensive private owns public toilets in Sherkat and Nesitu, High concentration of population along the road side in a mix set up of market, hotels, shops restaurants and homes that put the population at high risk of cholera infection and widespread. 67% of households do not have washing facilities and that 16% do not wash their hands with soap, citing the cost of soap as the main reason. 70% uses open defecation and the majority of the population most likely does not wash their hands with soap after excreta disposal, the KAP surveys conducted by Oxfam in 2016 and 2017 in Juba reported. The target locations have 16 highly populated primary schools with inadequate sanitation and hand washing facilities that expose children to high risk of contracting WASH-related diseases like AWD. The Jebel lemon Cemetery in Juba II receives minimum of 2 dead bodies for burial in a day and maximum of 5. The site do not have borehole, sanitation and hand washing facility and services for burial attenders and cemetery undertakers. However there is also lack of knowledge and absence of information for proper management of dead bodies either during burials or funerals that increases spread of cholera during the season with major outbreak especially the rainy season.

Activities

Project inception meetingsNurture South Sudan will conduct one week of meetings to introduce implement plan to target communities and schools. In the one week meetings, project staff will separately meet local authority, community and school administrations in the course of designated one week. Formation and training of community-based cholera monitoring committeeAt least 3 committees consisting of 60 persons (30 males & 30 Females) will be form in Nesitu, Sherkat and Juba II to enhance community-level surveillance and reporting of suspected cases of cholera to implementing agency for investigation at Oral Rehydration Point (ORP) and /or to provide onward referral to Cholera Treatment Center (CTC). The committee will play outreach role of disseminating information to the communities in regard to availability of services points within the community. Training of women and girls on cholera preventionNurture South Sudan intends to trains women and girls on cholera prevention focusing on safe food handling, safe disposal of excreta, hand hygiene, safe water, care-seeking behaviours, as well as disinfection of infected materials and surfaces (e.g., clothes, beddings, floor, etc.) using household disinfection kits (HDKs) to prevent infection and spread of cholera. Time and venue of training will be assessed for security and safety of women, girls and boys and, not to interfere with any other community business. Formation and empowerment of in-school hygiene clubEach of the 16 targeted primary schools will have hygiene club of 24 members plus one teacher. The clubs members will be train on hygiene and sanitation practice and monitoring and provided with materials for record of hygiene indicators, rubbish bin containers and water harvesting containers for their schools. This action will also initiate monthly inter-school hygiene and sanitation debate competition between schools’ to gauge pupil’s understanding of good hygiene and sanitation and to provide platform where pupils learn from each other on how to improve personal and in-school hygiene for the purpose of prevention cholera infection and outbreak. Drilling of borehole and construction of public latrine at Jebel Lemon Cemetery To prevent infection, this action will drill one solar-powered borehole and construct 2 latrines at opposite direction (one for females and one for males) each with 4 stance near the cemetery to be use by burial attenders and undertakers for cleaning up. The sanitation facility will be supplied with soap, tissues paper, drum for water for storing water, tripod stands and hand washing container and secure the facility with a lockable fence. The agency will provide information materials to undertakers for proper management of dead bodies and put up a large sign post at the entry of the cemetery with simply and cautious information for cholera prevention including availability of sex segregated latrines. Establishment of Oral Rehydration points (ORP)Nurture Sudan will establish 3 ORPs for Nesitu, Juba II and Sherkat. The final location of ORP will be determine after wide consultations with community duty bearer stakeholders, women group, youth group and schools, having into account, radius of distance for all households to access services at ORP, their safety and security for all at risk group. The ORPs will offer rehydration services to person experiencing AWD and also provide onward referral for those identified as having cholera vital sign to treatment at CTC. In each ORP, sex segregated tents will be establish, equip and 2 sex segregated latrines will be construct with 2 stance and washroom. The ORP will also be used as services (Oral rehydration salt) and information access point for community members who seek information on how to identify and take care of sick person and proper use of oral rehydration salt. Feedback and complaint desk established at each ORP to enable timely receive feedback and complaint from beneficiaries.
