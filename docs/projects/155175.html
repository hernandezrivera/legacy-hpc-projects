Needs

1. Need to strengthen access and quality of protection for individuals at heightened risk.Different types of violence are present in the camp due to multiple factors which include overcrowding, sense of impunity, change in social structures of the community in displacement context, and development of negative coping mechanisms to distress by community members. One of the most prevalent forms of violence is Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and most importantly domestic violence. Polygamy and early marriage practices are reportedly increasing, and the majority of families experience abuse and neglect in the home in a way or another.Patterns of trafficking and exploitation, including sexual and labour exploitation, also threaten individuals who are already the most vulnerable both in camps and in host communities.Therefore, there is a need to (1) provide basic psychosocial support to the refugee population to respond to distress, which will in turn contribute to mitigate negative coping mechanisms, and (2) provide immediate, life-saving support and protection to the individuals at heightened risk, including by specialized case management.Protection services - Physical access to protection facilities is challenging because of the nature of the field, which affects disproportionately women, elderly persons and persons with disabilities. Other barriers include lack of information of refugees on services, fear to report/fear of retaliation, growing distrust towards NGOs, and a general sense that "nothing can be done". Impunity is a major obstacles, with access to national justice system being very narrow, and lack of safe mediation mechanisms. 2. Need to engage refugees in protection.Engagement of refugees is critical to build their ownership and responsibility at different stages and to ensure sustainability of protection mechanisms. A main component is capacitating and empowering refugees in protection knowledge and skills, including psychosocial support, so that they can support individuals in need.Moreover, by understanding better existing positive community dynamics and drawing from them, community-led initiatives should be promoted. Engagement of all community members in protection, including men, boys and religious leaders, is needed to ensure a supportive and protective environment for all wherever the community is in the future, considering the many uncertainties surrounding them.In order to prevent incidents of violence, there is also a need to promote positive coping mechanisms, involving refugees in meaningful activities, such as livelihoods and vocational trainings. Fostering creation such as music, drama, sports, and learning, are all activities that need to be developed to give hope and a feeling of achievement to refugees and promote their positive engagement in the community.3. There is also a need for social system strengthening and peaceful coexistence initiatives in host community. Tensions are rising between the two communities, and host communities are also in acute need for protection, which should be provided in close partnership with the local authorities and according to the gaps identified with them. Along with this protection support for Host communities, should there be a focus on activities gathering the two communities when possible, encouraging peacebuilding approaches and mutual learning.4. Achievements to date.In 2018, Barefoot counsellor project (Community-Based Protection) has settled in Camp 4 extension. In November 2018, qualitative monitoring indicates a good acceptance of the project within the community, including religious members, and the volunteers. Feedback from the BFC include a member mentioning that "We will continue this work even when we come back to Myanmar. We do it because we feel helpful for the community, not because of the money or of Caritas."

Activities or outputs

"Barefoot Counselling" approach capacitates refugee community members to provide protection support and counselling, including GBV, to their community. 1. Training Rohingya refugees, men and women in Protection and GBV counselling to become Barefoot counsellors (BFC). Foundational training includes Protection and PSS & Counselling. Trainings topics are added based on the protection concerns identified by volunteers, with a focus on GBV, Trafficking and Exploitation. Regular refreshers are conducted so that capacity of Barefoot counsellors is regularly improved all along the project, and to ensure their motivation on the long-term.2. Deploying Barefoot counsellors to conduct household visits, with several objectives: - delivering basic psychosocial support and strengthening the natural community support network- sharing information- identifying the main protection trends and reporting them for protection monitoring- identifying and supporting individuals at risk. BFC being at the community-level, they play a vital role in accessing and identifying individuals at risk who are not usually reached by service providers, including women, men, persons with disabilities, the elderly. In a first instance, they refer to the GBV/MHPSS Officer for counselling and case management. In a second phase, they will become themselves counsellors for community members. Close coordination will be made with service providers for referrals as applicable.3. Capacitating Barefoot counsellors to conduct information and awareness workshops for the community, using creative material. The sessions will be led by BFC in an inclusive, participative and culturally-appropriate way, on the issues affecting them, in community centres.- No Strings International will work with CB to develop innovative material, based on puppet methodology, to address psychosocial issues of the community through the development of a puppet movie taking place in the Rohingya camp, in Rohingya language. - The sessions will also inform refugees on important issues in the camp, in close coordination with Site Management and the newly elected governance system, to promote their engagement in community life and choices affecting them. - Specific awareness sessions on cyclone and monsoon seasons preparedness, and practical measures to mitigate the effects of landslides and flooding will be co-facilitated by CB Shelter team (tying down shelters, re-enforcing structures, etc), taking into account the specificity of this camp which is on flat land. - Sessions on trafficking will be conducted using the material developed by IOM.- Specific sessions on GBV and IPV will be developed by Barefoot counsellors during a workshop on development of material supported by CRS India. Both women and men and boys, including religious leaders, will be engaged in defining and facilitating these sessions. - The community centre will be multi-sectoral and integrated to help reduce physical and psychological barriers to protection services: it will include a community hall and a counselling room.4. Host Communities. The project is currently conducting an assessment in Host community: Palongkali and Rajapalong Union, Ukhia Upazila, on the feasibility and modalities of Community-Based Protection programming. The assessment aims at defining the protection issues and resources in these host communities, the modality of working with local authorities according to their identified gaps and needs. It will also look at ways to promote social cohesion and peacebuilding approaches. CB does not have specialized internal expertise in these areas but will scope for technical support in order to develop this programming, according to opportunities identified in the assessment. These locations have been chosen because CB will intervene in Livelihoods, DRR, and Environment in these areas in 2019.
