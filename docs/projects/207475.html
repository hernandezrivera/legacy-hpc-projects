Project Summary

The project aims to assist refugees in acquiring transferable and portable skills relevant to the labour market needs in Myanmar and the camp context. This is with the objective of supporting refugees' sustainable return and integration in Myanmar when conditions permit, as well as enabling refugees to maintain hope for the future and lead dignified and productive lives in the camps, including through engagement in multi-sectoral camp-based activities, minor livelihood activities and community works, through the utilisation of acquired competencies. Accordingly, strategies will be geared towards enhancing skills development, economic and social empowerment, and self-reliance of the Rohingya refugees, pending durable solutions. UNHCR will also enhance the engagement of refugee women in productive activities inside the camps, with participants producing female hygiene materials for distribution in the camps. This will be achieved by strengthening the quality of final products, including through improving production processes and upgrading current production centres. The project will develop occupation-based skills with globally recognised competency standards (level 1) for 6,500 refugees aged 18+ years that include all gender groups and persons with disabilities (will be assessed during enrolment by using Washington Group Short Set Questions). An additional 250 refugees who complete occupation-specific skills training will receive industry readiness training to prepare them for immediate sector involvement. The project will implement 10 competency standards-based training for refugees inside the camps. The project will create income generation/self-employment opportunities for the skills-training graduates, covering 4,800 graduates who completed the training both during 2023 and in earlier years. The production of female hygiene materials and jute bags for distribution in the camps will also be facilitated through production activities, which will engage around 800 semi-skilled/skilled volunteers on a payment basis. In 2024, UNHCR will continue implementing pre-vocational education (accelerated adult literacy) for Rohingya adolescents and adults aged 18-24 in the Rohingya refugee camps. As part of the project, UNHCR will provide pre-vocational education to 11,568 youths. The course will cover basic literacy, numeracy, functional English, and life skills knowledge for the learners. To ensure the quality of the adolescent program, UNHCR will provide a basic pre-vocational module for training participants on literacy, numeracy, and soft skills for the adolescent program. In 2024, UNHCR will roll out an International English Language Testing System (IELTS) training. Administered by the British Council and co-owned and franchised by IDP Global, and assessed and validated by Cambridge, the IELTS is a globally recognized standardized test designed to assess the English language proficiency of non-native English speakers. The training will mainly target 150 refugee graduates who intend to apply for the Japan Initiatives for Education Pathways (JIEP) programme.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The baseline camp needs assessment conducted in 2022 highlights that 6.07% of Rohingya youth had participated in vocational training prior to their arrival in Bangladesh. Around 23.72% of refugee youths received non-standardised skills training inside the camps since arriving in Bangladesh, while 30.9% of Rohingya youth had received education while living in the camps. Moreover, the results of the assessment also highlighted that, among female refugees, the major skills training requested included industrial sewing machine operation (26.5%), nursing (8.6%), community health worker (6.4%), midwifery (4.3%), agricultural production (3.8%), banking and nutrition (3.8%), etc. In contrast, the interests of the male refugee population were related to the occupations of community health worker (17.4%), masonry (14.7%), machine mechanics (13.6%), solar electrician and system maintenance (10.6%), electrician-house wiring (10%), plumber (8.1%), etc. Challenges related to women's participation in productive engagement were reflected through three broad aspects: bias among groups of women due to low self-esteem, stereotype threats often caused by lack of prior information, and lack of motivation to participate in skills training and development associated with a low level of education. Skills development training solutions, whatever their extent would be, need to be offered to targeted women as a group considering their 'in-group' biases (i.e., neighbour influencing neighbour) and mobilizing female groups to enable sustainable solutions.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will target the following beneficiary categories: i) refugees with limited basic skills, including but not limited to literacy and numeracy skills, and ii) refugees who have some level of knowledge and skill capacity within certain occupations which enables them to work as low skilled or semi-skilled labourers. The project will also support advocacy on transferable skills development training opportunities for Rohingya refugees. With its inclusive character, the project focuses on the enrolment of refugees, particularly women, girls, and persons with disabilities, in training and learning opportunities based on their skill levels, needs and interests, and also links training graduates with volunteering opportunities in the camps. It also focuses on expanding women’s participation in non-traditional livelihoods through targeted entrepreneurship and skills development. In addition, the project aims to assist refugees in acquiring transferable and portable skills relevant to the context of Myanmar's Rakhine State, for the purpose of supporting sustainable return and integration when conditions permit while enabling refugees to maintain hope for the future and lead dignified and productive lives during their stay in Bangladesh, including through engagement in multi-sectoral camp-based activities, livelihood activities and community works, through the utilization of acquired competencies.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will focus on meeting the protection needs of refugees by ensuring their participation throughout the project cycle. The project will ensure equal participation of diverse groups, including persons with disabilities and vulnerable individuals, and adhere to the "Do No Harm" principle.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project will target refugees aged above 18 years old, given that there are paid volunteering opportunities available following the successful completion of the training. The project has an obligation not to include the child in productive engagement. Among the targeted participants, at least 50% will comprise women, while 1% will comprise persons with disabilities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will adopt a community-based approach which incorporates the principles of age, gender and diversity mainstreaming in its work with refugees in order to ensure accountability to affected populations; in other words, through consultation and participation throughout the programme cycle, refugee communities in each camp should be engaged meaningfully and substantively in all aspects of programmes that affect them and play a leading role in catalysing change and enhancing community resilience through the implementation of vocational skills training, which they can utilize when potentially repatriating or resettling to other countries.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project will ensure all that project participants and relevant staff are aware of their roles and responsibilities in the prevention and response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) at all level of implementation.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The Project will reduce the ecological footprint through economic empowerment, especially for women to cope with environmental and climatic shocks. The project will integrate the guiding principles of UNHCR’s Environmental Guidelines in alignment with the national legal framework (The Forest Act, 1927; Wildlife (Conservation and Security) Act, 2012) and Energy and Environment Working Group guidelines. All environmental and climate action activities will be implemented under the umbrella of UNHCR within or beyond the project scope in a comprehensive complementary approach. The focus will be to embed the principle of ecological restoration and reduce the risk of climatic hazards. The skills training will also integrate green skills concepts for cutting carbon emissions by running the centres with solar power and improving human skills in innovative green skilling.
