Project Summary

The World Relief Sudan (WRS) health and nutrition project planned to be implemented in five localities of central Darfur (Azum, Golo, Nertiti, Rokero & Umdukun), five localities of West Darfur (Geneina, Kerenik, Habila, Beida and Sirba), four localities of Khartoum ( Bahri, Karari, Khartoum and Jebel Awulia) and four localities of Blue Nile states (Demaine, Kurmuk, Bau, Rosaries). The project mainly focuses on improving vulnerable households’ access to sustainable health and nutrition services to reduce the morbidity and mortality attributable to poor access to health and nutrition services among, IDPs, returnees and conflict-affected people.
WRS started its operational in Sudan since 2004, responding to the sever food insecurity and humanitarian crisis. WRS is currently providing interventions in health and nutrition, FSL, peace building, education protection, and WASH activities. The health and nutrition project of WRS aims to address the immediate needs of 634316 people (310,815 men, 323501 women, 348874 children, 63432 elderly, and 15857 people with disabilities) returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host communities. WRS conducted the SMART and KAP surveys in March 2019 using the standard tools and procedures with the objective to determine health, nutrition and WASH related knowledge, attitude and practices and to know the prevailing needs of children, women and adults, specifically on health & nutrition in the Jebel Maraa Localities. Furthermore WR conducted detail needs assessment in Khartoum and Blue Nile states to determine needs and priorities of the states. Furthermore, in 2020 World Relief conducted integrated and multi indicator survey in West and Central Darfur states.
WRS’s Needs Assessment reveals poor access to health and nutrition services, low capacities of health and nutrition staff, disease outbreaks, high rates of malnutrition, and poor hygiene and sanitation situations. To address these challenges, WRS will provide life-saving interventions in the Health and nutrition sectors, which will be integrated with FSL, protection and WASH sectors that is ongoing in the in the proposed states using resources obtained from different donors. To increase community access to primary health care services, WRS plans to rehabilitate, equip and provide medical drugs to 38 health clinics. Services include providing outpatient services, medical consultations and treatments, expanded program of immunization (EPI) with routine immunization against all national target diseases, cold chain support, and antenatal and post-natal care with SMoH to provide overall operation and management of the health facilities proposed to be supported by this grant. In Addition, Mobile clinic services will be conducted, reaching out to populations who are unable to access the static facilities, thus ensuring that individuals afraid or unable to move to the static facilities are not denied assistance or services.
Regarding nutrition sector, the primary focus is reducing and maintaining mortality level below the emergency threshold by implementing CMAM & IYCF activities in order to improving the nutritional status of vulnerable members of the communities especially children under five years of age and PLW. The project will be implemented by, identifying, screening and treating malnourished boys and girls under-five, pregnant & lactating women, and providing preventive nutrition services. The project will implement its nutrition program by supporting 63 (35 WD, 16 CD, 7 Khartoum, 5 Blue Nile) nutrition centers in the four states.
All interventions will be done in partnership with government line ministries, community-based committees, local authorities, community leaders and beneficiaries themselves. As described further below, interventions are designed to meet immediate emergency needs of the beneficiaries mainly women, girls and children while employing approaches that increase community ownership and thus sustainability.

1 - Needs

WRS conducted a KAP survey funded by OFDA in March 2019. The survey gathered data related to health, nutrition, and WASH and covered WRS’s current operational areas including North and Central Jabel Marra areas. WRS also conducted a Nutrition SMART survey funded by USAID/OFDA in May of 2020. In addition, WR conducted Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) in West and Central Darfur States in April 2020. WRS conducted comprehensive needs assessment in Blue Nile State in November 2019. The assessment report included the voices of the communities collected from focus group discussions (FGDs), and interviews and discussions were conducted with line ministry offices and relevant UN agencies. WRS also analyzed data from its existing health and nutrition sites and consulted secondary sources. The deteriorating macroeconomic situation in Sudan is worsening household food and nutrition insecurity for all Sudanese, particularly for the most vulnerable families and children. Data from WRS’s existing nutrition sites in Central Darfur shows that admission to the outpatient therapeutic feeding program (OTP) increased by 10% from 2017 to 2018, while cases admitted to the therapeutic supplementary feeding program (TSFP) increased by 10.9%. This indicates not only the high needs in the area but also demonstrates the increased reach in WRS’s program, which his critical for ensuring cases are identified. WRS’s Nutrition SMART shows high rates of malnutrition, with a GAM rate of 20.2% which includes a SAM rate of 4.6% and a MAM rate of 15.6%, which are above the WHO emergency thresholds. The KAP survey revealed a very poor practice on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF). Only 13.6% of the surveyed mothers practice exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) for six months, and only 29% of the lactating mothers reported breastfeeding their infants for one year. Furthermore, the overall minimum diet diversification rate was 27.8%, and the minimum meal frequency for children in age group of 9-11 months was reported to be 2.4 complementary meals per day compared to the standard minimum of 3 meals per day. There is a need also to strengthen skills of service providers in order for early identification of medical complications associated with malnutrition and other relevant field. Closely linked to high levels of malnutrition are poor health indicators. Factors, including limited access to and poor performance of health facilities, access to potable water sources, low community awareness, and low coverage of sanitation facilities also contribute. Communicable diseases are a major cause for morbidity and mortality in Sudan, and the country remains prone to measles, meningitis, acute watery diarrhea (AWD) and most recently outbreaks of chikungunya and dengue fever. According to WRS’s KAP survey, diarrhea was the most common illness in the areas surveyed with 37% of the children reportedly affected. An additional 10.1% of children were reported to have experienced acute respiratory infections. These epidemiological data points reflect the data from other health facilities in West and Central Darfur, whereby the top three reasons for consultations are ARI, malaria, and diarrhea diseases respectively. Furthermore, WRS’s Nutrition SMART survey reported an overall vaccination coverage was 57.4%, however only 32.9% were confirmed by vaccination cards, and the remaining 24.5% on mothers’ recall. At the time of this proposal, there is an ongoing measles outbreak in Central Darfur with 71 cases already reported in Umdukhun locality and Dengue fever and Chikungunya in West Darfur. Data from WRS’s clinical records show that reproductive healthcare is also of major concern in both states. In both States, only about 26% of women have access to skilled care during childbirth at HF and only 55% attend at least one ANC visit during pregnancy period (WHO recommends 4 standards visits).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Civilians have suffered from years of conflict, exposing them to protection threats including gender-based violence, targeted attacks, and violations of basic human rights. Freedom of movement, especially in conflict-affected areas such as Darfur, remains restricted. This greatly impacts communities’ ability to engage in livelihoods, undermining opportunities to support self-reliance and preventing durable solutions to displacement. It also restricts people’s access to basic services. More importantly, the causes of the conflict particularly in Darfur remain unresolved. Long-standing grievances between communities continue to give rise to clashes which are aggravated by the involvement of heavily armed tribal militias and the proliferation of small arms and light weapons. Political instability in 2019 had a particular impact on children and adolescents, who were affected by the closure of schools. Sexual violence particularly affects women and girls in displaced persons’ camps and in rural communities, where they are often preyed upon by armed men, especially when they engage in livelihood activities like collecting water or firewood or working in fields. Protection will be mainstreamed across this project, to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse, or exploitation towards the targeted population. Minimizing the distances that people have to travel to receive services minimizes the risk of beneficiaries being targeted by armed groups. Therefore, locations of health facilities, nutrition feeding centres, and agricultural demonstration plots are managed as safe spaces that are centrally located and are accessible to large numbers of the population. The locations also minimize the distance that distributed items need to be transported to the home and mitigate the risk of theft or harassment during transportation. All project staff will undergo training on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and be sensitized on the risks faced by many of the women and children accessing services. The various partners and stakeholders mentioned throughout the proposal, as well as the beneficiaries themselves, will be asked to participate throughout all stages of the process from beneficiary selection to building the work plan, to scheduling and designing trainings, to monitoring and evaluation. Additionally, WRS will create a number of reporting mechanisms including a hotline, comment boxes, trained community volunteers, and mechanisms at project sites through which community members can report incidents. Please refer to the attached document, Accountability to Affected Populations, for more information.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

WRS believes that the organization is accountable to the donor, line ministries, HAC, local authorities where the organization operates & beneficiary communities. WRS also maintains that beneficiaries have the right to know and be involved in projects designed to provide service to them. As a result, WRS promotes community participation in all stages of the project life-cycle by working with the communities from the initial identification of needs to the program phase-out. WRS's M&E system involves all stakeholders for timely information sharing and direct participation in implementation and M&E at all levels. In addition to ensuring openness and transparency, this will also ensure best use of the resources according to the scope of the project within the budget and priority areas of the beneficiary communities. To this end, WRS has established community level committees in its operational areas and they are responsible to oversee the implementation and monitoring of project activities in their respective areas. To further ensure the communities' engagement, WR strengthens community level management staff. The community level management is led by Area Coordinators (ACs) based in deep field who work closely with community leaders. Regularly the ACs review project activities and progress of the project with community committees and share pertinent information. The outcome of community review meetings will be reported to the Nutrition coordinators, Health and Nutrition Manager, Area Manager and the Program director. The information obtained from ACs will be discussed during monthly review meetings. Besides, the Health and Nutrition Manager, Nutrition Coordinator and Officers, Field Operations Officer and M&E Coordinator regularly visit the beneficiary communities to ensure the accountability measures are functioning properly. WRS uses suggestion boxes to be placed at each feeding center to get the feedback from beneficiary communities on health facilities services. The beneficiary community will be informed to put their feedback anytime in the suggestion box and WRS's Nutrition and health Coordinator will collect information from the suggestion box on quarterly basis and discuss with Nutrition manager, Area Manager and Program director on improvements of some of the beneficiary complaints and also keep up the good side of our services. Also the findings from the beneficiaries comment will be shared with SMoH for taking collective action. The Nutrition and health coordinators compile project information and share pertinent information at the state level with close supervision from the Health and Nutrition Manager and get feedback from partners to further strengthen the project implementation and information dissemination. Being cognizant of the ‘do-no-harm’ principles, the project is designed to address nutrition needs of the communities while mitigating unintended social, economic, and environmental consequences.
