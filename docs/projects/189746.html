Project Summary

Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) seeks to provide protection assistance to most vulnerable girls, boys, youths, women, and men in Jur River and Wau Counties of Western Bahr El Ghazal State, and Mundri West County of Western Equatoria State reaching out to 18,700 participants (3,000 girls, 3,000 boys, 6,600 women, 6,100 men). Currently, SEM has a well-established static presence in Jur river, Wau, and Mundri West counties.
SEM will collaborate with existing community structures like protection committees, community-based protection groups, community leaders, faith leaders, other agencies to create synergy during the implementation of the proposed interventions. SEM proposes the following activities under (SO 2.4) Ensure the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and (SO 2.5) Enhance protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response:
1) We shall promote psychosocial wellbeing by providing group and individualized psychosocial support (PSS) and recreational services reaching out to 500 girls, 500 boys, 1000 women, and 500 men according to their individual needs. The activities will include recreational activities targeting adolescent girls, boys, and women to connect socially with each other and create a platform where peer-to-peer support will be strengthened. This activity will have a component of skills provisions like bread making, basketry, and needlework based on the preferences of participants to create an opportunity for income creation to care for their families and build their resilience to economic hardships.
2)We shall train 600 women and 600 men in conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation, and peacebuilding for the participants to understand their roles in the process where broken relationships are healed and mended. The community will be supported to map out a list of structures, institutions, and processes that they shall build or rebuild for ensuring sustainable peace in the community. They will promote localized reconciliation and conflict management mechanisms to deal with community disputes and other conflicts arising in their areas.
3)We shall conduct protection awareness-raising through interactive mediums like community public rallies, sports, cultural dances peer to peer sessions to reach out to the community on protection issues. We shall reach 15000 people (2500 boys, 2500 girls, 5000 men, and 5000 women).
4)We shall carry out regular protection assessments to identify high-risk areas & dynamics exposing civilians to violence and conflicts. We shall collaborate with other partners in the field while carrying out this activity and share the assessment reports with county and national level cluster and sub-cluster coordinators. The produced assessment and monitoring reports will be used for advocacy in protection with stakeholders and inform protection response.
The above activities will promote positive coping mechanisms, identification of local resources that the communities can use to enhance their protection, and peaceful coexistence among various sections of the population. Additionally, this project will increase the resilience of the most vulnerable population by promoting psychosocial wellbeing through PSS sessions that will focus on handling stress, coping with loss and grief.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2022), years of conflict, outbreaks of sub-national violence, growing uncertainty about the political process to achieve peace, and recurrent flooding due to climate change are severely impacting the conditions of life of people in South Sudan. In response to this, this project seeks to addressing the needs of the people in Jur River, Wau and Mundri West Counties by drawing attention to their fundamental needs and building local capacities to prevent conflicts, mitigate the impacts of conflicts and promote a greater degree of social transformation. Related to the targeted project sites, the HNO indicated that armed violence flared up in Western Bahr el Ghazal (WBG) and continued throughout 2021 with heavy clashes and incidents also in Western Equatoria. This project has an activity that will build the capacity of men and women in the community to tackle conflict and promote peace on themes like conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building. The training will boost confidence in conflict resolution and create a sense of community ownership of the peacebuilding process leading to sustainability. The trained beneficiaries will be able to make valuable contribution in addressing the root causes and drivers of such conflicts while involving socially marginalized groups such as people with disabilities, widows, and IDPs to make an active contribution to the process of reconciliation.The 2022 HNO further explains how repeated shocks have weakened community-based structures and services, and limitations to access services will continue to push families to adopt negative coping mechanisms that expose the vulnerable, particularly children and women, to violence, exploitation, abuse, recruitment, trafficking, child labor, and forced marriage. In response to this, our project has a component of PSS targeting men, boys, girls and women. Such shocks experienced by vulnerable persons cannot be overlooked, and require to be addressed by psychosocial support to increase their resilience and create an integrated development approach to promote their psychosocial wellbeing.

2 - Project activities

Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) will achieve two Strategic Objectives (SO) outlined by the general protection cluster through the implementation of this project. (SO 2.4) Ensure the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and (SO 2.5) Enhance protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response. The following activities will be undertaken to accomplish the above Strategic Objectives:1.Provision of a group and individualized psychosocial support (PSS) and recreational services: we shall target 500 girls, 500 boys, 1000 women, and 500 men according to their individual needs. We shall include recreational activities targeting adolescent girls, boys, and women to connect socially with each other and create a platform where peer-to-peer support will be strengthened. Provision of basic livelihood skills like bread making, basketry, and needlework will avail an opportunity where participants will create income to care for their families and build their resilience to economic hardships.2.Training 600 women and 600 men in conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation, and peacebuilding: where the community will understand their roles in the process where broken relationships are healed and mended. The community will be supported to map out a list of structures, institutions, and processes that they shall build or rebuild for ensuring sustainable peace in the community. They will promote localized reconciliation and conflict management mechanisms to deal with community disputes and other conflicts arising in their areas.3.Conducting protection awareness-raising: we shall use mediums like community public rallies, sports, cultural dances peer to peer sessions to reach out to the community on protection issues. We shall target will 15000 people (2500 boys, 2500 girls, 5000 men, and 5000 women). We shall target various groups via different modes, for example, young people would benefit more through peer-to-peer educational programs. The sessions will help the community to widen the social responsibility of enhancing protection, where the communities will understand they all have a role to play in laying a stable ground for their protection.4.Conducting regular protection assessments: the assessments will be carried out to identify high-risk areas & dynamics exposing civilians to violence and conflicts. We shall include other partners in the field while carrying out this activity. We shall share the assessment reports with county and national level cluster and sub-cluster coordinators.

3 - Project indicators

# of women, men, girls and boys provided with individual, group, specialized PSS and other related general protection services (Psychosocial support and access to documentation): for this indicator, we shall measure the increase in number of survivors who receive individualized and group psychosocial support, recreational services, and basic livelihood skills from this project. # of people of concern trained on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building: we shall measure this indicator by counting the increase in number of men and women who have developed skills, knowledge, and attitude change in conflict resolution, mediation, community peace dialogues, and reconciliation activities. # of women, men, girls and boys reached through general protection awareness raising activities: we shall measure the increase of the total number of boys, girls, men, and women who receive key messages that will focus on awareness of gender-based violence, prevention/ harm reduction, within the targeted project locations.# of protection monitoring reports published and shared: we shall measure change of this indicator through the total number of protection monitoring reports produced and disseminated to county and national level clusters/ sub clusters and to partners.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback that we collect from our beneficiaries will be crucial to enable us to adjust our programming. One type of feedback can be a complaint. For complaints we shall use them to enable us to adapt this project to the changing context that was triggered by the feedback received from our beneficiaries. This means that we shall review actions and undertake an investigation on issues that need to be put right, amended, or changed to improve the situation and resolve the issue.Such feedback will provide us with an early opportunity to handle complaints at an early stage to manage risk before it gets out of hand.Therefore, we shall take complaints from beneficiaries for learning and project adjustment to best fit their emerging needs.Effective response builds on the integrity of this project as it demonstrates our willingness in listening to our beneficiaries and create confidence among them as well as demonstrate the inclusive of the feedback process that takes into consideration the views of all the beneficiaries including persons with disabilities, women, men and less literate persons.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As an institution already implementing disability inclusive projects, we shall employ diverse and inclusive mediums to ensure that persons with disabilities, the less literate and other vulnerable people are able to give feedback.We shall ensure that locations selected for project activities are safe, accessible and friendly to boys, girls, men and women. By eliminating physical barriers, persons with physical disabilities will be able to access the project activities and give feedback. For such activities we shall give a provision where persons with disabilities will attend the company of their personal assistants to facilitate translation to persons who are deaf, or to assist persons with physical disabilities to move from home to FGD venue. We will make our feedback mechanism to be friendly to the less literate through availing mediums like our designated feedback telephone number where they can call if they are not able to write to our suggestion boxes.Our field staff who are conversant in using South Sudan sign language skills to communicate to the deaf will also be used to communicate and collect feedback from persons who are deaf.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

As we engage our beneficiaries, we shall reiterate our zero tolerance principle to PSEA and using verbal means and through IEC materials that we shall distribute to them. Presenting the information in a language that beneficiaries understand enables them to be empowered and become active participants in our planned response. Our beneficiaries will be informed of our organization values on upholding and promoting the highest standards of ethical and professional conduct. They will be notified of their ability to access humanitarian assistance freely and without any sexual exploitation or abuse.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

As we launch this project, we shall use the startup meetings with community members and their representatives to obtain feedback related to this project.We shall use appropriate languages that are used in the target locations, and ensure that our communication is sensitive to the culture on the areas that we shall implement this project including using IEC materials that contain pictorials for reference to enhance understanding. We shall collect beneficiary complaints and feedback will enable us to adapt this project to the changing context that was triggered by the feedback received from our beneficiaries.Through suggestion boxes that we place in community centers, we shall collect feedback from our beneficiaries regarding their complaints or satisfaction with our services. We shall also involve beneficiaries in the process to update, revise or redesign project plans on the basis of the findings from consultations, and feedback that they gave to enable an inclusive process that takes into consideration the views of all the beneficiaries including persons with disabilities, women, men and less literate persons.We shall solicit feedback from beneficiaries by carrying post distribution monitoring, end of training evaluation to collect feedback from them regarding their satisfaction with our services.
