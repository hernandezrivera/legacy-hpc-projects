Project Summary

The project is in line with HRP 2023 objective 1 and also with cluster/sector specific objectives 1,2& 3, Besides, it aligns with the cluster strategy to ‘Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5’ have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral essential services to meet their needs.
This project will improve access to safe, quality and inclusive protective education services to 4002 emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host communities’ children in Fangak, Pigi, and Longochuk and Nasir Counties by 2024. The integrated education project by ADA in hard-to-reach areas aims to ensure that children (boys and girls) have access to quality and inclusive education and those already in schools are retained in Nasir and Longochuk Counties (Upper Nile State), Fangak and Pigi Counties (Jonglei State). The following interventions are envisaged: establishing safe learning and recreational facilities in 12 locations – 3 in each County; teacher training to enhance the teaching/learning function supported in schools; providing monthly stipends to 80 volunteer teachers - 20 teachers per County; campaign for education, encouraging children, especially girls and people with disability to return to school; and provision of school feeding programs to enhance attendance and retention of children at schools. Response priorities will include gender-segregated WASH facilities with much concentration in COVID-19 prevention measures, inclusion of GBV counselling rooms in the schools, provision of learning, teaching and WASH materials, capacity building of 40 School Management Committees (SMCs) and Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs), life-skills and recreational opportunities to support the recovery of children and to promote their overall well-being. Equal access to inclusive education will be prioritized regardless of gender, disability or ethnic background. Through introduction of school gender-sensitive WASH programme, this project will have established strong WASH facilities in schools.The project will seek to address one of the top causes of school dropouts (School feeding) that will help increase and maintain school enrolment and attendance, and reduce risks of death, disease and cognitive underdevelopment. School feeding will also improve protection of- in-school and out of school children by keeping them away from potential recruitment into armed groups, child labor, early marriage, exploitation, sexual violence and abduction while on their way to schools and back home. This will help to reduce morbidity and absenteeism, support retention in schools and children to help sensitize their communities. Crisis-affected children will be engaged in basic education and recreational activities in order to create an enabling environment for their critical development and positive engagement with the community. Youths will catch up on primary education through accelerated learning programmes (ALP) to improve their literacy and numeracy, technical and vocational training, and life skills. The project will seek to include physically and mentally challenged children; girls and boys vulnerable to recruitment into armed groups, and child labour; youths; adolescent heads of households; young mothers; and children from different ethnic groups. Teachers, SMCs and PTAs will be trained on life skills and referral pathways to enhance the protective aspect of education in emergency (EiE) intervention. ADA has a policy on, and extensively uses feedback mechanisms in project implementation. This provides for a complete loop of communication between the field, programs and management. In essence, ADA employs four key steps in providing beneficiary feedbacks: design, implement, collect data and analyses; provide feedback through an agreed feedback loop.

1. Needs

Fangak and Nasir Counties remain continues to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions largely due to Sudan conflicts, flooding, severe acute food insecurity and further constrained access to lifesaving services. In the county, access and quality of education are poor and critical protection concerns for the most vulnerable children, women, and PWD remain an impediment to their survival. According to the report the needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding and in many parts of South Sudan. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing Schools infrastructure in displacement sites.In September 14 , 30,000 Sudanese returnees have been recorded crossing the border into Maiwut, Nasir, and Ulang Counties in Upper Nile State. Returns are driven by cuts in humanitarian aid in camps, and due to the escalated conflict in the area. Children are faced by a multitude of challenges with 2.7 million children, adolescent and caregivers at risk of violence, unprecedented flooding, neglect, harmful practices and hyperinflation, child marriage, GBV, effects of COVID-19, exploitation, abuse, weak presence of education partners, family separation, risks of recruitment into armed groups, loss of life, (UASC) who continue to remain unregistered. In addition, Vulnerable children and youth have been instrumentalized by armed groups, mainly in Upper Nile and Jonglei State. Lawlessness and harmful practices undermine child protection in all states. There are few available child protection services with limited scope to support affected children; Safe houses for children and women are not present outside Juba, resulting in girls escaping child marriage frequently lack any safe space to request for assistance. Clinical services for child survivors of rape and sexual violence, or those maimed through gang violence, inter-communal violence or conflict are provided by humanitarian agencies. Mental health and psychosocial support services are being provided in a haphazard manner entirely dependent upon the availability of short-term humanitarian finance. (HNO 2023/2024.). Sexual abuse and often limit their mobility to services like water points, and farmlands as a coping strategy. (2022 FSNMS+). The weak link of the problem is on the effects it has on Boys and girls who are severely affected by conflict and distress and lack of access to education, nutrition and healthcare due to attacks on schools and hospitals. A child protection policy in South Sudan is primarily to protect children and young people. It also protects all of those involved in youth work such as staff and volunteers while also taking into consideration the rights of parents/guardians and obligations of boards of management. The convergence of multiple shocks on already vulnerable communities’ calls for a scaled-up response and education systems to provide children to education services and fair justice systems – starting at birth. These will provide care to the most vulnerable, including children uprooted by conflict, poverty and disaster; victims of child labour or trafficking; and those who live with disabilities or in alternative care.
