Project Summary

The ongoing economic situation, along with the recent lifting of fuel and wheat subsidies, continue to contribute to the devaluation of the local currency and high rates of inflation resulting in an increase in food prices and costs of agricultural production. Although crops benefited from favorable precipitation and harvest in 2018, the high cost of production a result of high agricultural input prices as well as shortages of fuel and cash have affected farming production. Subsequently, during the past year, the national average price of crops and livestock on the market has increased significantly compared to historic averages. Rising prices, compounded by limited access to livelihoods opportunities, have been identified as the main drivers of food insecurity and poor food consumption, which have led to a severe decline in the purchasing power of the most vulnerable communities including returnees, refugees, IDPs, as well as vulnerable host communities, vulnerable residents in West Kordofan, White Nile States in Sudan as well as Abyei PCA are critically affected and in need of urgent support as all available livelihood resources are under pressure from the overwhelming increase number of demand for livelihood services which poses a threat of conflict out break among different diverse communities in the targeted states.

Needs

Around the turn of the millennium, Sudan’s Southern states of WK, WN & Abyei PCA went through a period of violence and turmoil, which was partly caused by fragile ecosystems, deeply entrenched poverty and under investment. After the signing of the Sudan Peace Agreement (SPA) in Sep 2012, the situation has been calm, but the three states still have high rates of poverty and malnutrition. Livelihoods in the three states are predominantly rural, centered around animal husbandry and agriculture. Average annual rainfall varies from less than 100 mm in parts of WK, WN & Abyei to over 700 mm in the southernmost parts. Being so scarce, water is a primary factor that constrains rural livelihoods in the three states. In order to structurally improve the productivity of animal husbandry and agriculture several NGO,INGOs and UN agencies have responded in providing water facilities for livelihood improvements. In Sudan, 159 out of the 171 assessed localities by the IPC in October 2018 are classified as food insecure at crisis and at emergency levels, these localities include Abuzabad, Muglad & Keilak in WK, Jabalian, Elsalam & Kosti in WN and Abyei PCA Area, and these localities will be prioritized by GAH for intervention and targeted for assistance according to their humanitarian needs. Specific attention will be given to households with persons with specific needs, including older persons, female-headed, single women, children under five, pregnant and lactating women and people with disabilities. GAH FSL sector strategy will be tailored to integrate and compliment emergency response, recovery and resilience, building activities to save lives, protect and restore livelihoods and initiate longer-term solutions for all vulnerable communities and people living in protracted displacement. These interventions will facilitate achieving longer-term food stabilization, early recovery and create linkages to the Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and development programs within the targeted States targeted by Global Aid Hand Organization.

Activities

GAH currently is implementing FSL Project in Alagaya in WN State funded by UNDP and aims to source direct funds to cover other urgent areas with aims to enhance the self-reliance and resilience of the most vulnerable communities to cope with current and future shocks and stresses. GAH will implement below activities;- Training of pastoralists on improved methods for animal husbandry - Training of para-vets and Community Animal Health Workers- Improving of animal vaccination systems, provision of parasites sprays where livestock can be treated against ticks.- Through cash modality, GAH will provide vouchers to the trained food processing trainees in order to access start up materials for their businesses from identified and authorized vendors in targeted localities. - Improvements to livestock corridors- Training of farmers on improved agricultural practices -Training of agricultural expansionists- Construction of stores for agricultural produce.-Training on the importance of good nutrition- Training on (and provision of basic inputs for) kitchen gardens using wastewater- support to small-scale irrigated vegetable cultivation-provision of climate-smart crop seeds for production-Training on food processing, production of fuel-efficient stoves, etc. and support to enterprise development, specifically but not exclusively targeting vulnerable households.
