Needs

Gender-based violence, particularly verbal harassment, domestic violence (including family violence against women and girls), child marriages and the fear of sexual violence and harassment, continue to be part of the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe. Assessments confirmed the need for women and girls spaces and other GBV specialized services in 59 per cent of communities. The lack of awareness of the need for GBV health services in some communities remains high, indicating a need to strengthen GBV awareness, outreach and prevention activities. Sudden displacements and shifting lines of control continue to put women and girls at risk. The fear of sexual violence, often associated with abduction, is a concern raised by many women and girls, contributing to psychosocial stress and further limitation of their movements. The restriction on freedom of movement of women and girls also inhibits their access to services, humanitarian aid and ultimately their rights. In some parts of the country, women and girls report fearing so called honor killings as a result of sexual violence. Families arrange marriages for girls, believing it will protect them and ease the financial burden on the family and some girls are reportedly being married at the age of 12 and 13. The socio-economic situation, lack of livelihood opportunities, and increased poverty is ultimately leading more women to resort to harmful coping strategies such as survival sex. As in 2017, concerns regarding access to humanitarian assistance were raised. Distribution sites are often perceived by women and girls as unsafe places. (HNO(2018Women and teenagers girls reported very positive experiences at safe spaces for women and girls in Aleppo governorate. They emphasized that the safe spaces support groups of women and girls that would otherwise not receive any help. In the majority of assessed communities in Aleppo governorate, psychosocial support as well as medical treatment for survivors of sexual violence and/or domestic violence were reported as not existing and being needed, according to quantitative data sources. Women and girls safe spaces were also reported as not present and being needed in the majority of communities GBV HNOWomen indicated that the most vulnerable were group of "widows and divorcees" who do not have a breadwinner and do not have a profession to help them work. Followed by those with "special needs" who experienced physical violence and emotional abuse.This underscores the importance of safe spaces for women and girls who engage in professional activities that help women learn a profession and can support themselves according to the divisions of age groups.

Implementation of activities

Early in the project implementation phase, the facility-based staff are planned to be provided with capacity building sessions including and not limited to psychological first aid PFA, psychosocial support PSS and GBV, were the skills achieved through those training sessions will be potentially utilized by the staff while providing similar quality services beyond the duration of the proposed project. In addition, RF plans to equip a facility with the required items for conducting both case management, psychosocial support and referral. RF and will conduct a mid-term and final review. Standard monitoring and evaluation techniques are applied throughout the project phase to ensure progress and achievement of the set objectives, outputs and targets and for transparency. Project activity will including: Processing the center (safe space) through the purchase of tools and equipment necessary for the work of the center, and provide GBV (protective and responsive) specialized services, including case management, psychosocial support and referral, and to raise the capabilities of the team and achieve the purpose of activities, RF will train the staff on PSS, PFA, Case management, and skills of dealing with traumatized persons. Provide vocational trainings as (Hairstyle course, literacy, IT skills, English language, Wool works), also conducting structured group sessions that address elements of self-protection, safety, prevention, General activities to support MHPSS, self-care, Reproductive Health and Early Marriage, domestic violence, Psychological first aid (PFA). Protection Risk Analysis

First risk Social unacceptable: by local community of GBV messaging. Impact: HighHow to mitigate: - Community engagement throughout all project cycle. - It includes meetings with all related stakeholders and community leaders. - Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Second risk: Lack of quality services also lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in service. Impact: Mediu How to mitigate: - Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centered services.- Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral.- Traditional legal systems put women more at risk, message to be mainstreamed.- Include supervisory structures in response services. Third risk is Little participation: due to customs and traditions where they feel embarrassed and risk if they entering into the protection center. How to mitigate: Impact: High - Choose a suitable location for the safe space. - Establish protection center within the health center in one center so that everyone can visit it.
