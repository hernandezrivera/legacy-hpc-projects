Project Summary

Through the proposed project, UNFPA will invest in empowering youth, especially young girls aiming to enhance their resilience, by building their personal and social assets that can promote their positive development and enable them to reach their full potential. The Project will also contribute to the wellbeing of the young people’s families and communities, enabling them to have access to livelihood opportunities and social participation by building the capacity of youth to take the lead and the responsibility to identify and prioritize their needs as well as the community needs. Specifically, the Project will focus on supporting youth female headed households on business startups and provision of seed funding to support them in leading entrepreneurship projects.
Two youth friendly spaces will be established in Rural Damascus and Hama. A set of activities will be provided at the center aiming to promote young people’s wellbeing, leadership, participation and economic empowerment. The project will be implemented in Rural Damascus and Hama, the communities selected based on the 2024 severity scale and on the fact that they are priority return destinations and accessible to UNFPA IPs.
The project will follow a gender transformative and gender equality approach, as per existing IASC guidelines. More so, the interventions in this proposal are in line with the “Compact for Young People in Humanitarian Action” launched at the World Humanitarian Summit (2016). UNFPA promotes efforts to put young people at the center of humanitarian action and does so through key actions as follows:
1) On services: Promoting and increasing age- and gender-responsive and inclusive programmes that contribute to the protection, health and development of young women, young men, girls and boys within humanitarian settings
2) On participation: supporting systematic inclusion, engagement and partnership with youth, in all phases of humanitarian action, through sharing information and involvement in decision-making processes.
3) On capacities: Recognizing and strengthening young people’s capacities and capabilities to be effective humanitarian actors by their active participation in the community and livelihood opportunities.
4) On resources: increasing resources intended to address the needs and priorities of adolescents and youth affected by humanitarian crises and identify ways to more accurately track and report on the resources allocated to young people in humanitarian contexts.
UNFPA is committed to invest in youth and emphasizes the rights of adolescents and the need to increase their potential to contribute to positive social transformation. UNFPA is mandated to address the needs of young people that are considered life saving for their future well-being, and work on projects that will contribute to empower young people and prevent them from participating in harmful practices that affect their decisions, health and future.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The multifaceted crisis including the persistent economic crisis and hostilities in Syria creates continuous cycles of vulnerability, extremely impacting those most in need, including individuals with disabilities. High inflation, currency depreciation, and increases in commodity prices resulted in an increase in poverty and reliance on humanitarian assistance. Since February 2023, the Syrian pound (SYP) has lost about half of its value against the US dollar (US$), averaging around SYP 14,200/US$ in the parallel foreign exchange market in November 2023.The society is under immense stressful conditions, households unable to meet needs, depreciation undermines household purchasing power, lack of jobs, services, income from employment is insufficient, households facing significant income-expenditure gaps, there is widespread reliance on unsustainable coping mechanisms, lack of electricity access exacerbates pressures on households, Around 50 percent of Syrians have no jobs, which affects their overall wellbeing and social engagement. In the absence of decent job and livelihood opportunities, more and more people living in Syria are resorting to harmful coping mechanisms including gender based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse and early and forced marriage. Young girls who marry early get pregnant and have rapid repeat pregnancies. The crisis has led to a significant change in the role of women, including female youth, in society and many of them became the only bread winners in their families. Limited participation in community life and decision processes from females and young people is observed across the country. Between 50 and 55 per cent of the respondents declared that the one taking decisions on behalf of the community are “senior males” . A decade of the crisis has placed a mental strain on Syrians with increasing reports of stress and mental health concerns, particularly among youth. In a conflict and protracted crisis setting like Syria, youth can play a special role in building social cohesion in their communities. One of the key challenges is how to build a positive outlook for Syrian youth and hope for their future and unleash their creative potential.
