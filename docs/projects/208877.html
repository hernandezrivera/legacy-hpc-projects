Project Summary

FAO under HRP 2024, is planning to provide emergency agriculture and livestock-based livelihoods interventions to support vulnerable farmers, agro- pastoral and pastoral people from IDPs, host communities and venerable non-displaced population in Sudan. The vulnerability of the target populations stems from the protracted crisis, the ongoing economic hardships at the macro and micro-levels, negative impact of long dry spells and floods and recently the ongoing conflict.
This project covers all states in Sudan targeting 9,023.696 people among the most vulnerable communities including 3,429,004 IDPs, 2,255,924 host communities, and 3,338,768 non -displaced people. The planned interventions will benefit 4,602085 females, 4,421,611 males and 3,609,478 children of which 1,804,769 girls (less than 18 year of age), and 1,804,769 boys. (less than 18 year of age). The project will also support 902,370 elderly (above 60) and 451,185 male and female people with disabilities. The proposed interventions are based on the basic and specific needs of the beneficiaries considering the factors of age and gender. FAO will coordinate with sector partners, government, and UN agencies to get actual information on numbers and locations of the people with disabilities to support them with suitable livelihoods interventions.
The project interventions under FSL SO1 (Improve the food security status of assessed severely food insecure people through emergency live-saving agriculture, livestock, emergency livelihood and social protection support through cash and in-kind transfers for 8,250,000 person (1,650,000 households). And 773,696 persons (154,739 households) under FSL SO2 (Strengthen affected food insecure households through live-sustaining and restoring food, agriculture, and livelihoods) to ensure that people have regular access to enough high-quality food from their own production to meet their family needs and sell the surplus in the local markets to generate income and avail diversified foods for others at their locations and at a reasonable price. During that process they will receive extension support to intensify their production and build their resilience to overcome natural or manmade risks and hazards. They will be encouraged to protect their natural resources to sustain their livelihoods base.
The FAO will collect information, conduct assessments, studies to better inform the decisions on the implementation of this project. Crop and Food Supply Assessment Mission (CFSAM) will be conducted during 2024/ 2025 to enhance the country planning and ensure that all Sudanese are food secure otherwise suitable interventions will be developed.
FAO will support coordination with FSL sector partners and harmonises the response to food chain threats including animal and plant pests specially locust and birds; in addition, communities will be involved in protecting and maintaining their productive assets through awareness raising. Accountability to affected populations and environmental concerns will be mainstreamed into the proposed activities, also risk to GBV will be taken into consideration and application of the PSEA measures will be applied in collaboration with sister UN agencies.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The FAO project contributes to the protection of people’s livelihoods and food security by mitigating and addressing food chain threats. Food security, livelihoods, and national and international markets are all impacted by threats to the food chain. Food security and food safety may be significantly impacted by risks, which include illnesses and pests of animals and plants as well as dangers to food safety. FAO depends on Emergency Prevention System (EMPRES) to capture those threats on time by obtaining many assessments. FAO strengthened the process of helping transform its agri-food systems to achieve better production, better nutrition, a better environment and a better life, by supporting farmers and rural communities with seeds and livestock to strengthen their agricultural livelihoods and produce healthy, nutritious food and hence protect people from hunger.The Organization’s overarching goal is to improve the food security and nutrition of communities affected by conflicts, natural and manmade shocks and disasters, while enhancing families’ self-reliance and resilience to threats and crises in line with national priorities and FAO’s corporate Strategic objectives. The project will consider identifying who at risk and how to support them. Considering vulnerabilities that underlie these risks and gender. The project will provide suitable interventions to improve capacities of women, men, youth and people with disabilities to produce and meet their needs. The project will give equal opportunities to all categories of the population including women, men, girls, boys, elders and people with disabilities to access the provided livelihood support, addressing the rights of all population categories to decent work to restore their food security and livelihood situation. The targeted communities have been involved in dialogue through meetings, interviews and group discussions throughout steps of this project pre-design and they will be directly involved during the implementation including beneficiaries’ selection, registration and implementation of the activities, monitoring the implementation at the field and beneficiaries’ levels. FAO provides the necessary guidance for promoting the four pillars of decent work for the beneficiaries in the targeted rural areas. Under the present circumstances, where the fighting is still going on and many cases GBV were not reported because of absence of law enforcement bodies and weak access to hospitals besides the social stigma; FAO will mainstream awareness on GBV and PESA to protect women, girls and boy’s sexual abuse and violence, during all. The sector Partners will provide the training and input distribution in suitable safe locations at their resident villages. Consult them and their leaders on the appropriate time for the training. Awareness on GBV and use of fuel-efficient stoves to avoid distant firewood collection in far unsafe places.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Community feedback is essential to build trust for successful projects. FAO is using different methods and tools for community feedback during the various stages of the projects ‘cycle. Compliant box and telephone numbers are shared during the distribution of support at village/ community levels to provide information and solve problems at early stages and keep record for further follow-up when needed. The community feedback is collected regularly by the field staff from beneficiaries, implementing partners, government at different levels and stakeholders. Their views and recommendations are seriously taken to adapt, improve or change. The information is collected in a simple excel sheets or it can take the form of questionnaires or assessments based on the need and situation. The information usually shared with project supervisors and M&E unit for analysis and to assess their impact on projects’ outcomes for learning and considering adjusting activities for future projects. Feedback is shared and discussed at the various levels and relevant information is shared with stake holders and communities during awareness or training programs. This process is regularly undertaken and accordingly type, time and strategy of the interventions are selected. The seed package is frequently revised and adjusted based on FAO feedback mechanism. The livestock package depends on the needs and selected in consultation with the beneficiaries as well as stake holders and coordination with livestock authorities and even the neighbouring countries to avoid trans-boundary diseases. For new interventions or technologies that potentially suitable to FAO projects; investigation on its benefits, operationally and adaptability to the targeted groups and locations. This information is shared with the local teams and beneficiaries and the stake holders. Introduce small number, train sailable interested groups and test. Regular feed back is collected evaluated to decide to forward, adjust or decline.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The accountability to affected population is integrated into all aspects of the way that FAO works. Accountability towards affected people starts from selection of interventions, varieties and quantities of livestock and agricultural inputs. The inputs selection defined in a participatory manner during the consultation phase. FAO encourages the local communities not only to participate in the implementation of the activities but also to ensure ownership of the project. As a result, FAO assists beneficiaries to form a local committee that represent all people in the community, train them on their roles and responsibilities. This training facilitates the fair selection of beneficiaries and distribution of livelihoods support to its members. Either Beneficiaries” complaints about types, varieties and quantities of inputs or any other question received directly by FAO staff, or they can call the Service Provider nominated person to respond or in case the reply is not convincing, he/she can raise the issue to the higher managerial level in FAO Sudan. Comments and recommendations usually collected during post distribution missions.FAO shows continues interest to hear the voices of the beneficiaries about its standard packages of inputs, which have been subject to change every year taking into account the views of the targeted people. FAO urged all its implementing partners to open hot lines with the beneficiaries to allow them to express their opinions about the intervention and give them the chance to bring out any grievances they may have about the interventions. The project will promote understanding between the different livelihood groups (pastoralists and farmers) through group meetings, discussions and other community platforms to reduce conflict and encourage mutual support. Special focus will on be enhancing the knowledge and skills of men, women, adolescent girls, boys and other at risk-groups on natural resources management and leadership to ensure sustainable livelihood interventions; this will help in avoiding competition over natural resources. Conflict drives sexual violence in multiple ways. Rape and other sexual abuses may be directly perpetrated by armed forces, while heighted economic and social instability provide the conditions for increased sexual abuse. At the same time, the breakdown of justice, healthcare and other systems makes support even harder for the survivors. Activities on the ground confirm the severity of the situation and therefore more efforts will be exerted to protect the sensitive beneficiaries through more coordination with UNFPA, UNHCR and other UN sister agencies for advice or action.
