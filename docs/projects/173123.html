Project Summary

The objective of the proposed intervention is to respond to the acute vulnerability of conflict-affected populations, inclusive of refugees and migrants living in urban settings. The action aims to achieve this through protection assistance whilst building community resilience and self-reliance by empowering Libyan civil society organizations.
As a result of the protracted conflict in Libya and the constant population movement that is a characteristic of it, many Libyan residents – including host community members, returnees, and displaced persons – and non-Libyans face heightened vulnerability in the absence of access to effective protection assistance. The conflict and its effects have substantially affected the population’s well-being and physical, psychological, and emotional health. At the same time, public service provision has faltered as a result of the conflict. In response to these challenges, ACTED will provide protection assistance by conducting protection monitoring in the East and South of Libya, and by maintaining and strengthening its local referral pathways. In addition, ACTED will support the resilience and self-reliance of vulnerable communities by supporting Libyan civil society organizations technically and financially to carry out community-based protection initiatives.

1 - Needs

Libya has witnessed several waves of conflict, with episodic escalation across different regions. As a result of the protracted conflict in Libya and the constant population movement that is a characteristic of it, many populations in Libya – including host community members, returnees, displaced persons, migrants, and refugees – face heightened vulnerability. The protracted conflict has affected an estimated 1.8 million people on a national scale. Out of this 1.8 million, OCHA’s 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview identified 893,000 people in need of humanitarian assistance. According to the results of the 2020 REACH MSNA and from ACTED’s ongoing protection monitoring activities in Libya, vulnerable populations are forced to resort to negative coping mechanisms, such as reducing the number of meals per day, reducing expenses on essential health services or education, or even begging or exploitative work. Women and female-headed households in Libya are also at particular risk of food insecurity and malnutrition, and therefore more liable to resort to emergency level coping mechanisms. Many Libyans and non-Libyans face heightened vulnerability in the absence of access to effective protection assistance and services, as public service provision has faltered. The critical deficiencies in access to essential goods and services at the household and community level across multiple sectors are exacerbating the vulnerability of conflict-affected populations and increasing protection risks. This is partly owing to the undermined system of basic service delivery. The security situation in Libya remains fragile, with ongoing protection risks and barriers to meeting basic needs as well as awareness on these issues at the community level is hindering the process of return, reintegration, and recovery. Available information and assessments suggest that refugees and asylum-seekers and IDPs are the least resilient to the impact of the current crisis in Libya. They are more likely to have been displaced several times, to live in more sub-standard accommodation, and to have been exposed to threats, intimidation, harassment, physical assault, or exploitation. During their stay in Libya, a significant number of asylum-seekers, refugees, and migrants are detained or held in captivity for various periods of time, including following interception or rescue at sea. Many, including children, are at risk of being subjected to widespread and systematic human rights violations and abuses at the hands of smugglers traffickers, armed groups, militias, criminal gangs and others acting with impunity. Women and girls, but also men and boys, are subjected to rape, forced prostitution, and other forms of sexual violence. An estimated 425,714 internally displaced persons (IDPs) are recorded to be in need of humanitarian assistance as the protracted nature of the conflict in Libya has resulted in severe damage to housing and infrastructure across the country; impacting the living conditions of IDPs and returnees. Many IDPs face significant difficulties in securing affordable and adequate housing with some being at risk of eviction. Poor living conditions, negative coping mechanisms, limited access to services, sexual violence are among the protection risks reported to face IDPs. Security, lack of livelihoods, limited public services, lack of social cohesion and risk of exposure to explosive remnants of war (ERW) are some of the challenges hindering return. Protection is at the core of the operational response to refugees, asylum-seekers, IDPs, returnees, and host communities in Libya. Through protection monitoring in urban areas, ACTED aims to facilitate the identification and referral of vulnerable individuals of concerns to relevant service to reduce the acute vulnerability. By applying a community-based approach, ACTED’s objective is to strengthen community structures and communication channels and empower refugees, asylum-seekers, IDPs become self-reliant

2 - Activities

ACTED’s protection monitors will conduct household visits or phone interviews throughout the year to identify and monitor the protection needs of the most vulnerable households in targeted areas. The visits provide detailed household-level information for case identification and follow up and will be related to a range of potential protection risks. ACTED tailors its protection response to the specific needs of each household. ACTED aims to refer beneficiaries internally to its Cash for Protection Fund, which provides beneficiaries with a one-off cash grant to offer an immediate response to address the needs of beneficiaries. In addition, ACTED will continue operating its external referral mechanisms, an extensive network with local service providers for health, psychosocial support, disability, legal assistance, survivors of gender-based violence, family reunification, and NFI and cash assistance. Moreover, ACTED refers beneficiaries to other INGOs where relevant. ACTED will conduct general awareness raising sessions around protection risks encountered by vulnerable and marginalized populations such as on mental well-being and psychosocial assistance, ACTED’s protection helpline, positive/negative coping mechanisms, and strengthening existing community owned protection models.ACTED will strengthen the technical capacities of protection CSOs in the East and South of Libya. ACTED will organise training workshops, providing capacity building on community-based protection to ensure a high standard of service delivery, combined with subsequent trainings targeting organisational capacities as project design and proposal writing.ACTED will provide sub-grants to CSOs, who will implement community-based protection activities aimed at addressing the needs of vulnerable populations in the South and East of Libya. Throughout the project cycle, ACTED will provide technical, financial, logistics, and M&E guidance to local partners to carry out the envisaged activities.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.2: ACTED will conduct awareness raising sessions on a monthly basis with members of the targeted communities. The content of the awareness raising sessions will include ACTED’s beneficiary selection criteria and process, introduction to the Complaints and Response Mechanism, and ACTED’s commitment to humanitarian principles. ACTED will also engage with conflict sensitivity experts such as PCI to ensure ACTED is well-informed of power dynamics in current and new areas of operation.4.8: ACTED will highlight key protection trends and risks that affect newly displaced individuals or individuals caught up in protracted displacement in the East and South of Libya, to donors and UN/humanitarian stakeholders, to ensure their needs are considered in international humanitarian dialogue and decision making.4.9: ACTED’s Protection team will ensure beneficiary lists are collected from diverse sources, including municipalities, CSOs, LibAid, and MoSA. ACTED’s AME unit will conduct pre-tests through phone interviews and households visits when possible to verify beneficiaries’ vulnerability. 4.18: ACTED’s Protection team works closely with the Cash team to support the replacement of documentation where possible, and to deliver other types of assistance such as NFI kits where relevant. The Protection team cannot provide assistance without documentation such as ID, family registration number, national number or passport. However, assistance can be provided through a relative or next of kin by undergoing a POA process, based on the documentation status of the relative or next of kin. In case of migrant and refugee communities, only in-kind assistance can be provided in urban areas, in coordination with CSOs and local authorities.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

ACTED has a dedicated Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM), consisting of two helplines and an email. The helplines are operated from Benghazi, covering the Eastern region of Libya, and from Tripoli, covering the West and the South of Libya. This allows both direct beneficiaries and community members in all of ACTED’s intervention areas to reach out to ACTED directly via phone or email in order to express their concerns, grievances, or inquire and give feedback on ACTED’s programs. ACTED will ensure that beneficiaries are sensitized to the existence, the purpose, and the processes of the CRM and will adapt interventions accordingly to both specific information and emerging trends identified through the CRM.
