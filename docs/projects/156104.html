Project Summary

In 2019, UNRWA will continue to provide essential WASH services to Palestine refugees, with specific attention to areas and camps affected by the conflict. The crisis in Syria has resulted in extensive damage to water supply systems and sewer infrastructure, resulting in restricted access to potable water and affecting domestic and personal hygiene, which in turn has increased the risk of disease outbreaks.
UNRWA will continue its effort to repair, rehabilitate and maintain water supply systems and to ensure that Palestine refugees, particularly in newly accessible camps, have access to potable water. The Agency will also repair, rehabilitate and maintain infrastructure systems including sewerage and surface drainage systems in addition to collecting and managing solid waste rubble removal, and controlling rodents and insects in accessible camps.
Furthermore, hygiene kits will be distributed to Palestine refugees affected by emergencies as well as Palestine refugees spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas and those in hard to reach areas and areas facing movement restrictions, to help safeguard their personal and domestic hygiene.
In the course of 2018, UNRWA was able to extend services to newly accessible areas, requiring extensive work on WASH infrastructure. This trend is expected to increase in 2019, with potential resumption of access to key areas like Yarmouk and Dera'a camps.

Needs

Eight years of conflict in Syria continue to have dramatic consequences for the people living in the country, including Palestine refugees. Violence, economic disruption and a contracting job market as well as a decrease in provision of public services and social security programmes have created and compounded vulnerabilities within the population. Intense and widespread hostilities have resulted in deaths and injuries, continued internal displacement, large-scale migration to other countries, lost livelihoods, mounting humanitarian needs and access restrictions hindering the emergency response.In 2018, UNRWA estimates that 438,000 Palestine refugees remain in the country out of 560,000 individuals registered with the Agency in Syria (the number of Palestine refugees in Syria is expected to increase to 445,000 in 2019 due to possible returns from outside Syria; mainly from Jordan and Lebanon). Displacement, loss of assets and livelihood and high levels of inflation have contributed to the increased vulnerability of civilians, including the Palestine refugee population. Many families have lost their main breadwinner, whether through death, incapacitation, detention or departure to another country in order to seek protection and asylum. Almost 60 per cent of Palestine refugees have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict and, according to UNRWA estimates, more than 180,000 have had their homes destroyed or severely damaged due to hostilities. Yarmouk, Dera’a, and Ein El-Tal (Aleppo) camps, previously home to more than 30 per cent of the Palestine refugee population in Syria, have been almost completely destroyed. A vulnerability assessment conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 90 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in absolute poverty (less than USD 2 person/day) prior to receiving UNRWA cash assistance. The conflict in Syria has also had a damaging impact on water and sewage infrastructure inside refugee camps. Recent assessment conducted by UNRWA in October and November 2018 revealed widespread and massive destruction of water and sewage networks in Yarmouk and Dera’a camps; the same is also observed in Ein El-Tal camp in Aleppo. Other camps affected by conflict such as Sbeineh camp and Khan Eshieh camp have witnessed a lesser level of destruction and damage to their water and sanitation infrastructure, but are still in need of rehabilitation and regular maintenance. Palestine refugees rely on UNRWA to support the rehabilitation, maintenance, and repair of water and sewer networks inside the camps. It is worth mentioning that up to 40% of Palestine refugee population in Syria are currently residing in nine camps in Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Hama, Homs, and Latakia. This percentage is expected to increase as refugees start returning to the three remaining camps: Dera’a, Ein El-Tal and Yarmouk. Meanwhile, solid waste management remains a priority for UNRWA inside all camps, which rely on the Agency for this purpose. Any failure or suspension to the provision of basic WASH services inside camps could have disastrous implications on the health and wellbeing of Palestine refugees, exposing them to the risk of epidemics and diseases. UNRWA collects solid waste from streets and alleys inside Palestine refugee camps and transports them to specific dumpster locations where they are picked up by the neighbouring municipalities. There is a need to hire sanitation workers and to cover the running costs of collection trucks in all of the camps. Palestine refugees in newly accessible areas, areas severely affected by conflict, and areas with movement restrictions (such as Yalda) will also be in dire need of hygiene kits in order to maintain healthy hygiene and sanitation conditions and mitigate the risk of diseases and infections. It is estimated that up to 20,000 Palestine refugees severely affected by conflict in Syria will need hygiene kits in 2019.

Implementation of activities

In order to address the WASH needs of most vulnerable Palestine refugees, UNRWA plans to:1. Support the access of Palestine refugees to WASH services (e.g. emergency repair of sewage networks; collection and management of solid waste; and provision of safe drinking water) in areas of high concentration of Palestine Refugees (e.g. camps and gatherings);2. Resumption of WASH services, including emergency repair (reconstruction/rehabilitation) of water and sewer networks, in newly accessibly camps and camps severely affected by conflict (Yarmouk, Dera’a, and Ein El-Tal camps)3. Provide hygiene kits to all Palestine refugees affected by sudden emergencies and living in hard to reach areas and areas with restricted access (such as Yalda in Rural Damascus), in addition to those returning to newly accessible areas;

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Improved access with localized conflict in certain areas of the country: Overall, humanitarian access is expected to improve with fewer Palestine refugees in hard-to-reach areas (13,500 as of end October 2018). At the same time, hostilities may continue in certain areas of the country. Should safe access resume for civilians in areas like Yarmouk and Dera’a camps, the Agency will focus on resuming its basic services in these areas. Most Palestine Refugees in Syria will remain displaced: It is anticipated that the levels of displacement will remain high among Palestine refugees, as the housing stock in Yarmouk unofficial camp suffered significant destruction during the conflict, most recently in May 2018. The majority of the 160,000 former residents of Yarmouk are therefore expected to remain in displacement, often paying high rents, which further increases their vulnerability. A similar situation is expected in Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps which have sustained major destruction; these three camps used to provide housing for more than 30 per cent of Palestine refugees in Syria. Increase in spontaneous returns as more areas become accessible: As new areas become accessible to civilians, as observed for Sbeineh and Khan Eshieh camps in 2018, additional small scale spontaneous returns are anticipated, provided that safe access is granted for civilians and basic public services (water, electricity) are restored by the authorities. UNRWA will therefore seek to resume its education services in defined locations where feasible, to ensure that Palestine refugee school children are served. While a small number of spontaneous returns of PRS (Palestine refugees from Syria) from outside Syria were observed in 2017 and 2018 (about 2,000) and are expected to continue, most returns are expected to involve Palestine refugees displaced inside Syria. UNRWA continue to raise sufficient funds to sustain its operations in Syria: UNRWA will continue to mobilize resources and to advocate for the needs of Palestine refugees among donors, partners, and other relevant stakeholders. UNRWA will need an estimated US$ 220 million to respond to the emergency needs (health, education, protection, humanitarian assistance, WASH) of 445,000 Palestine refugees in Syria in 2019. UNRWA will mitigate the risk of limited funding by prioritizing key projects and interventions, and focusing on the needs of the most vulnerable.
