Project Summary

This project proposes provision of timely and quality lifesaving management of acute malnutrition through a full CMAM package of services for the most vulnerable children Under 5, pregnant and lactating women.
The project will to reduce morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable population with who have fall into alarming status (IPC 4) in the 3 Counties. CMD will increase equitable access and utilization of comprehensive treatment and preventive nutrition specific services which are essential for addressing the key drivers of acute malnutrition among children and women.In line with CMAM Protocol, through detection and treatment of children under 5 reaching at least 10,656 children with SAM, 40,125 with moderate acute malnutrition(MAM) and 21,521 malnourished PLW.
CMD will establish 6 nutrition sites to including at 4 outreach/outreach static sites for remote and hard to reach areas at island locations through outpatient Therapeutic Program(OTP) for SAM children as well as Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program(TSFP) for MAM children and PLW. SAM Children with medical complication will be referred to existing stabilisation centres (SCs). At the community level, Community Nutrition Volunteers will conduct active case finding to strengthen early detection and timely referral of malnourished children and PLW,Both through routine community screening and family MUAC Approach.All children(6-59M) with SAM without medical complications who are enrolled in OTP program will be tested for malaria as per the national CMAM guidelines and those tested positive will be treated.The nutrition staff at the outreach will be trained on national CMAM guidelines.

Prevention of malnutrition in children under 5 and PLW through promotion of MIYCN,Health,WASH and appropriate feeding and care practices through sensitisation sessions at the nutrition sites and as well community sensitisation. All mothers/caregivers will be counselled.The staff at the outreach sites as well as CNV's will be trained on the national MIYCN guidelines.
Mother to Mother Support Groups(MtMSGs) will be formed and trained.To meet immediate food security needs,MTMSGs will also conduct sensitive cooking demonstrations as well as kitchen gardening activities.Led by male staff, men Group Discussions will be formed and initiate a dialogue around crucial nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive topics and gender issues(esp.MIYCN,GBV), enable women's access to services, and safety promote gender equality through encouraging mutual support between gender groups.Safety audits will be conducted at all nutrition sites and outreach sites at least once during the project period using the tool endorsed by the nutrition cluster.A standard Feedback and Complaints Mechanism(FCM) will be conducted across the sites to understand the concerns of the beneficiaries and collect information on how continue improving the program activities.The community activities will be carried out using other funding source.
CMD will work closely with Nutrition cluster,MoH,UNICEF and WFP to secure timely provision of Nutrition supplies which are necessary for nutrition services. CMD will also work with other partners/actors on ground implementing other interventions such as WASH,Health,NFI's and Food Security and Livelihood to address the emergency nutrition needs of the targeted population.

1 - Needs

Between July 2022 and June 2023, an estimated 1.4 million children under 5 years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition based on the analysis and estimations from the recent SMART Survey and FSNMS reports. The recent FSNMS R-28 report shows Ayod, Bor and Akobo West GAM rates of Ayod(22.2%),Bor South(22.3%), Akobo(25.5% )and Prevalences of SAM is 4.5%,MAM 17.7% (Ayod ), SAM prevalence is 3.3% ,MAM 19.0(Bor South), and SAM prevalence is 7.4%,MAM 18.1% (Akobo) the 3 counties falls into IPC AMN Phase 4 + (critical). The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases. 62.5% of children reported to have been ill due to diarrhea, fever, cough, pneumonia, malaria among other diseases. 30.5% of HHs are reported to have poor food consumption score and household dietary diversity score and 23.8% of HHs experienced severe hunger. The factors attributed to the high GAM rate are poor household food security, prevalent childhood illness coupled with inability to get early treatment. The latest food security analysis estimates that the 2022 lean season, between April and July, shows constant high level of acute food insecurity, with the situation hardly changing in 2023 compared to 2022, the situation will be worse ever in terms of severity, with 194,396 people(Ayod) in catastrophe(IPC Phase 4) ,340,261 people(Bor south-IPC Phase 4),226,978 people(IPC Phase 5). Early seasonal rains resulted in the canal river to overflow, flooding vast areas of land and settlements along the canal. According to Multi Sector Assessment Report on flood affected communities in Bor South and Ayod county, a total of 78,000HH in Ayod and 51,000 HH in Bor South and 102,000 HH in Akobo have been affected covering 430 villages from 102 bomas/payams. The elderly, children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women are most affected and settled along the canal. With the current situation drought/wet season has become an issue most of the community are experiencing hunger since they skip the harvesting season due to heavy rain and /flood which has caused lots of damages, compounding the pre-existing humanitarian needs, including food insecurity, disease outbreak leading to malnutrition among vulnerable children under 5 and PLW. In line with cluster objective #1, CMD will increase equitable access and utilization of comprehensive treatment and preventive nutrition specific services, which are essential for addressing key drivers of acute malnutrition affecting children and women in the aforesaid project locations through promotion of appropriate Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition Feeding (MIYCN) practices. Provision of Vitamin A and Deworming tablets to children 6-59 months SO 2: Under CO#2, CMD seeks to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of (AM); of 10,656 SAM children 6-59 months, 40,125 MAM Children 6-59 months and 27,513 Pregnant and lactating women will be enrolled in 8 Nutrition sites which will be established.

2 - Project activities

CMD intends to support the existing 19 sites in Ayod and establish 8 nutrition sites and 4 outreach sites to meet the needs of the vulnerable populations in Bor South and Akobo West counties. The Project will target 6 bomas in Ayod) Normanyang, Buot, Mhaar, Kuachdeng, Paguong and Gaar, 1 payams in Akobo West (Barmach payams)and Bor South (Makuolcuei -Baldit Payam).The project will support 3 existing SC’s ,6 OTP’s and SFP in Ayod, 1 OTP/TSFP in Akobo West and 1 OTP/TSFP in Bor South in order to provide services to the affected population within and outside the villages of Ayod, Akobo West and Bor South. The project focus in reduction of morbidity and mortality, and suffering from flood trauma, drought and protection threats and incidents of most vulnerable in Ayod County (3,428 SAM,6,743 MAM and 8,850 PLW) Bor south county (2,705 SAM,15,577MAM and 10,512 PLW’s), Akobo (SAM 4,523 MAM 11,063, PLW8,142 in Akobo). The program will ensure all caregivers in the Stabilisation centers and OTP have received seeds and tools admission of SAM cases and will be encouraged to practice kitchen gardening. The trained CNV’s will provide services through moving to the villages, screening, identify, passing messages on MIYCN to mothers, family MUAC Screening Approach, follow up of the recoveries, defaulter tracing and refer SAM cases to the OTP sites. Mother to Mother Support Groups trained will be able to provide messages on the important of IYCF to the caregivers/mothers at the facility level and community level. CMD Nutrition and health staffs trained will provide quality services to the SAM cases within the OTP’s and Stabilisation centers. During admission nutrition is integrated into health and so screening of malaria will be done at the triage, MUAC tape distribution to caregivers and ILLT Distribution to SAM/MAM admitted children, referral of SAM cases who are severely malnourished is done within Health and Nutrition compound where CMD provides all these services initiate the same in the new established area. CMD will leverage on its ongoing responses on thematic areas of WASH,FSL, Health and Education. CMD currently runs WASH, Health interventions in Ayod, mainly through the provision of WASH services at nutrition OTPs and Stablization centres run by CMD. CMD will link/integrate the program with Health partners on ground.Promote appropriate MIYCN Key messages and good hygiene practices with the use of appropriate pictorial IEC materials.Establish of child friendly spaces and breastfeeding corners in all OTP/TSFP sites; provide individual counselling on MIYCN for pregnant mothers and caregivers of children 0-<23 months at the nutrition sites.Provide Vitamin A and deworming to children aged 6-59 months during the National Immunisation Day(NIDs).Sensitize the community/households on correct use of aquatics by CNV's; provision zinc and ORS to children with diarrhoea at health and nutrition sites; Integrate child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM;Conduct refresher and on job trainings for health and nutrition staffs,CNV's using CMAM and MIYCN Guidelines.Undertake joint Programmatic supportive supervision to nutrition sites; Mainstream Child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites; Establish a community complain and feedback mechanisms in all nutrition sites by installation of suggestion boxes.Provide timely updated nutrition data to state and national nutrition cluster and participate actively in coordination meetings.

Specification, protection and GBV activities will involve all affected populations in the response and their views will be taken into consideration. Specifically, child protection and GBV activities will be mainstreamed by integrating them into Nutrition activities, in that within this context,CMD recognises the need to continually assess the safety of project sites(nutrition sites) where services is provided through gender-sensitive safety audits and safety perception mapping.CMD will adapt sites or implementation methodology to reduce risks, address gendered barriers to access and control, and engage community members consistently across all aspect of the project to promote gender-transformative attitudes and practices.The project will be supported by the CMD gender and protection policy, monitoring tools for feedback response mechanism will be provided to all nutrition site and staff trained on how to handle the tool e.g number of specific nutrition staff will be trained in GBV mainstreaming and number of male and female staff in all sectors.All CMD Project activities from design, assessments, implementation and monitoring of activities aim to mainstream gender sensitivity.For instance, during project design the unique vulnerabilities for men, women, boys and girls are identified and analysed in terms of how the project can appropriately and adequately address each set of needs.For implementation, gender breakdown of staff hired and volunteers mobilised by CMD is also considered as an important component of gender mainstreaming. CMD will ensure PSEA is mainstreamed in project activities.The PSEA will be a key topic in training and awareness sessions for local leaders and volunteers.The local communities and complaint-desks personals will also be empowered with the knowledge to cascade to leaders and volunteers and also address arising PSEA issues alongside CMD.
