Project Summary

AL Shouf Cedar Society was established in 1994 with the main mission of the conservation of the natural and cultural heritage, managing Al Shouf Nature Reserve, which in 2005 expanded its scope to become a Biosphere Reserve, a member of UNESCO’s Man and the Biosphere Programme (MAB): “an Intergovernmental Scientific Program that aims to establish a scientific basis for the improvement of relationships between people and their environments. MAB combines the natural and social sciences, economics and education to improve human livelihoods and the equitable sharing of benefits, and to safeguard natural and managed ecosystems, thus promoting innovative approaches to economic development that are socially and culturally appropriate, and environmentally sustainable.”
ACS have been providing a lot of in-kind support to agricultural coops and farmers (ie. distributing seedlings to farmers, establishing green-houses, providing kits to coops)
In 2020-2021 ACS became more involved in cash assistance programs such as cash for work with UNDP and Cash for training with WFP

While Al Shouf Cedar Society (ACS) is progressively building up interventions towards sustainable agricultural practices and a proper use of the surrounding landscape that revives culturally as well as economically, ACS is providing cash assistance to farmers as well as other individuals who currently rely on such programs to provide for themselves as well for their families. ACS have been receiving a lot of calls from individuals from the local community asking to participate in cash for work and cash for training programs while the number of allocated beneficiaries and the period of the projects cannot cover all of the people who are reaching to the institution for help. Providing unconditional cash assistance will provide immediate relief to farmers and agro food processors which will strengthen their resilience and assist them on holding onto their land and assets thus maintaining agricultural production and improving food security and nutrition. Cash transfers is an extension to the interventions that ACS have been doing to respond to the vulnerabilities triggered by the socio economic crisis that Lebanon is witnessing while helping the local community bridge the food gap often faced before a harvest and in times when no other assistance is provided.
The project proposed titled “Cash Assistance to famers and food processors” aims at supporting vulnerable farmers, beekeepers, livestock producers and agro food processors while fostering the small scale initiatives that ensure food security of the local community. Providing cash assistance to famers in the SBR and nearby villages will help sustain the farming activities by decreasing the financial burdens on those individuals and their families. The implementation will be in collaboration with local stakeholders including cooperatives, NGO’s, national structures, municipalities, agricultural technical schools and the private sector. The purpose is to assist vulnerable people while maintaining high performing food systems that support the core functions of food production, transformation and consumption efficiently and predictably. Those systems provide adequate incentives and returns to food producers, processors, and distributors. They deliver sufficient, safe and nutritious food to well-informed consumers, with minimum delay and spoilage. Such systems also support inclusive structural transformation of economies, boosting productivity and incomes that cut poverty and hunger.
In the period extending from August 2020 till July 2021, the focus will be on conducting the outreach to at least 14 villages in Shouf and West Bekaa in order to allocate 1000 vulnerable families of small scale farmers, food processors and beekeepers. The E-cards will then be issued to those 1000 families who will then be assisted throughout this period in order to address to their basic needs.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

The socioeconomic outlook in Chouf and West Bekaa district is highly comparable to the outlook in other struggling areas in the country. According to The Investment Development Authority of Lebanon IDAL (2017) 32% of Shouf population is deprived and employment opportunities are limited. Therefore, there is a pressing need in supporting the increasing vulnerable individuals with the emphasis on those working in agriculture and agro food processing small scale industries. Despite the challenges, the district is endowed with an untapped pool of natural and human resources which are struggling to sustain their farming and food processing activities. If those human resources are offered adequate cash assistance, they can further afford to keep their agriculture activities ongoing which can promote the sustainable development process of the local community and enhance food security. Around 600 Lebanese and Syrian individuals working in livestock production, agri tourism, agricultural lands, beekeeping and agro food processing will be provided with cash assistance through a money transfer agent (third party).

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The organization has a code of ethics that ensures the safe and considerate working environment of all employees and program based workers or beneficiaries. In addition to assigning a protection officer ( project based) to ensure the upholding and promotion of ethical and professional conduct in relation to UN and ACS values including safeguarding against the violation and abuse of beneficiaries, including sexual exploitation and abuse.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

After conducting some of the activities, a feedback links are sent to the beneficiaries to be filled and sent to ACS. In addition to having a live chat and an offline Q&A platforms. The feedback of the beneficiaries is usually taken into consideration by adjusting the activities when possible to account for their needs.
