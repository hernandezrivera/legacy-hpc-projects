Project Summary

Medair’s proposed intervention will provide life-saving and sustainable shelter interventions at household-level to target beneficiaries in areas that are hard to reach, newly accessible, or with recent or sudden population movement. Medair will meet HHs in urgent need for basic shelter-related assistance for vulnerable, conflict affected households. The intervention builds on the experience, expertise, and relationships from Medair’s previous project cycles to reach those in-need, with protection mainstreaming and sustainability at the core of its programme. Medair intends to reach up to 500 with this shelter intervention through:

1. Rehabilitating HH-level shelter units of damaged houses via tailored rehabilitation to improve physical safety and security, privacy, and provide more digniﬁed living conditions to a total of up to 100 household. This will target the most vulnerable households according to Medair’s vulnerability criteria. Exact locations have not yet been identified
2. Special adaptation will be made for People Living With Mobility Impairment (PLWMI). Based on the result of house to house assessments that Medair has conducted and through previous shelter rehab projects implemented around Syria, it is expected that 12% of HHs are in need of disability adjustments, which will be provided alongside the rehabilitation of the damaged houses. Medair will follow accessibility and universal design principles to complete adaptations for up to 12 households with members who live with mobility impairment, with disabilities, or older people wherever this is possible within the constraints of the building structure.
Assistance might also be given to extremely vulnerable HHs returnees and host communities who have no capacity to meet growing needs. Medair will remain flexible in terms of location of the intervention and hard-to-reach areas will be prioritized. Medair teams are able to conduct independent house-to-house assessments jointly with SARC, using Medair’s own vulnerability and needs assessment criteria to identify and prioritize HHs. As Medair uses a multi-sectoral approach, needs assessments cover Shelter needs, as well as WASH and health needs and interventions are customized for each community and/or HH. Medair will conduct monthly FGDs wherever possible prior to the interventions to obtain beneﬁciary feedback and continue to recenter beneficiary needs.

3 - Needs

It is estimated that 5.5 million people in Syria lives in substandard shelter (34% of the overall population). with the economic deterioration, there are increasing shelter adequacy issues such as limited electrity, lighting, poor insulation, and lack of privacy. (draft HNO 2023) Shelter needs are concentrated in NES and NWS, with 44% of returnees living in substandard shelters as well as 27% of (often overburdened) host communities, and more returnees are expected to arrive. (draft HNO 2023) In the fourth quarter of 2021 Medair conducted house to house assessments in the Al-Hamdaniya neighborhood of Deir-ez-Zor city, an area left decimated during the crisis. Results found many people to be living in damaged buildings due to poverty and desperation (e.g. high rent in their previous living situations). Many were forced to return to their homes, which are now damaged and lack essential protection needs such as doors and windows. Intervention by iNGOs in this specific area was limited when compared to the size of the damage as a total of 623 houses were rehabilitated. Deir-ez-Zor and rural Aleppo have been chronically underserved with limited humanitarian presence. Communities such as these are Medair’s target population.

4 - Implementation of activities

Medair will carry out up to 100 shelter rehabs. Technical assessments deﬁne basic required rehabilitation for each site. Rehabilitation focuses on basic house repairs, protection, security, and WASH needs. Each apartment will be rehabilitated according to Sphere standards. Additional work will be completed to accommodate special needs of elderly or PLWMI. Medair will target within Deir-ez-Zor city and Aleppo. This will be done using a vulnerability assessment criteria framework. The project will incorporate HLP components as per sector guidelines. Based on technical assessments, rehabilitation may include walls, doors, windows, roofs, electrical wiring and chimneys for winter fireplaces. For each apartment, Medair customizes interventions per beneficiary needs, tailoring up to 12% of 100 targeted households for members with PLWMI and the elderly.
