Project Summary

Gender Based Violence (GBV) remains a serious concern particularly for women and girls in Somalia which in most cases is escalated by state of insecurity. Due to the prolonged protracted conflict, protective environment is weakened for the Internally (IDPs) and civilians. (UNOCHA Report on IDP Assessment 2016). Forced eviction coupled with unrelenting pervasive GBV trends subjects the most vulnerable in the community especially women and children and persons living with disability to further violation of
rights. During these displacements, the civilians are fearful of the explosives on the conventional routes by using other routes that are not planned hence raising more safety concerns for them. Key risks for children are family separation and child recruitment to armed groups, arbitrary arrest, and elevated exposure to GBV including early marriage, trafficking, psychosocial distress, a lack of access to education, and hazardous child labour (HNO 2017) From January August 2017 a total of 3,443 grave violations, affecting
2,965 children (463 girls, 2,502 boys) was reported out of which 1,202 children (1,169 boys and 33 girls) were recruited by armed forces and groups, an average of 150 children per month (HNO 2017). Most of the cases reported in the year 2016 involved rape,physical assault and sexual assault and rape alone with the GBV survivors cases accounting for 76% in all GBVIMS (HNO 2017). Gender based violence affecting women and girls remains underreported but widespread, with IDPs remaining extra vulnerable (96
per cent of GBV survivors who accessed services in 2016 were female; 76% IDPs). Data indicates that GBV occurs while in transit to IDP settlements, while exposure is especially high in IDP sites,eg during water collection after dark or in shelters that offer little physical protection. IDPs from minority clans are reported to be more frequently affected allegedly due to outsiders intruding into IDP sites.
The proposed project will be geared towards addressing gender inequalities in protection through identification, reporting and response of scenarios of sexual exploitation and abuse against women and children. The proposed project will be achieved the following: To improve access to timely basic services to survivors of sexual and gender based violence with special focus to health and psycho social support to women and children and elderly persons , to strengthen community based structures to mitigate and respond to women and children safety issues, with focus to sexual and gender based violence and to strengthen resilience and coping mechanism for women and children against sexual and gender based violence.

Project Activities

-Conduct trainings on basic psychosocial support first aid to women and men on a ratio of 2:1, with special focus to sexual and gender based violence and post rape care management -Provide basic training on post rape care management skills to health workers in the health facilities and including clinical management of rape cases targeting the vulnerable members of the family. -Provide training to the health record workers/ registrar on rape case data management and entry to the GBVIMS ( gender ratio of 2:1 female to male) -Conduct a gender sensitive community driven mapping exercise to identify critical referral points to aid in development of reporting and referral pathway in the IDP settlement areas -Hold stakeholders forums including the women safety and child protection team, the community psycho social support team, the health officers, local authorities, GBV and protection clusters IDPs representative and other referral points representative to disseminate and validate the reporting and referral pathway. -Hold women and children safety audit in the two locations in order to identify women and children safety issues and protection concerns, trends and hotspot points for sexual and gender based violence and other human rights violations against women and children within the IDP settlements -Training leaders on safety and children protection to community members including IDP women representative, representative of psycho social support team and health facility workers -Conduct mass media campaigns through local radio stations on the need to protect women and children against sexual and gender based violence- Provide psychosocial support, including counselling for victims of sexual violations including rape-Clinically manage post rape victims in 6 health facilities

Mitigation Measures

For IDP in Mogadisho the main risk is displacement. for this the organisation has maintained a database for all beneficiaries with telephone contacts and that of relatives. For those in rural Mudug the risk is spontenious clan clashes. While it is difficult to deal with this, we have ensured that services are provided for the different clans in their areas equitably. Interclan clashes therefore have minimal effect our programs. in all location the organisation will seek to provide safety nets by implementing BSFP . Victims of GBV will also be provided with an organisational GBV focal point who will link survivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensure IDP have access to basic services in an equitable and dignified manner with equality also observed in job allocation in projects serving IDP population. Among IDP the risk of exclusion based on tribe is real. Most IDPs in Kismayo are from a certain community who found it hard to access services in the recent past based on their tribeand skin color. The organisations' policy does not allow exclusion and actually reaches out to all community members to receive services. Recruitment of staff is not based on tribe but professional competence. This has produced staff from a hybrid of cultures who have learned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved to the target communities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs and women so that equity is achieved. All gender will be awarded opportunity to benefit in equitably. Women and youth who are mostly left out in recruitment will be consideredin all projects and positions will be awarded equitably. Needs assessment will include all community groups so that an all conclusive, agreeable and fair assessment is reached. both men and women leaders with an additional youth representative will be part of community health board as per organisational policy. This will ensure decisions made are all inclusive. SAF UK policies have sought to put men at the helm of needs affecting their wives, daughters and female relatives. The organisation's APP framework seeks to ensure men participate through affirmative action for men ensuring that men are not excluded from interventions targeting women beneficiaries.Children who are often left out will not only be seen as beneficiaries but their views on service delivery ad need will occasionally be sought by a professional child specialist so that the whole community participates in program design and implementation. Community diagnosis, community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will seek to include all community groups to ensure all are reached. Dialogues, evaluations and conversations will include women, children and youth with their own forum so as to ensure their views are considered and used to programing. 2020 programs will seek toimprove access to women and girls , who are key beneficiaries of proposed interventions by scaling up services to be within a few kilometers walk. Far and hard to reach areas in rural settings will be served by mobile teams so as to reduce risks and hazards faced by these groups enroute to accessing services.

Please provide further explanation

In all proposed areas the environment is sufficiently safe to enable implementation of resilience activities. All our projects have a good working relationship with the government and other relevant stakeholders. Some of our facilities were handed to the organisation by the government and work under strict supervision of the same. Others run by the organisation are community owned but supervised by MoH, possessing long term agreements witnessed and endorsed by government. In all proposed districts, project involve government entities in all activities and receive several monitoring and supportive supervision visits. The organisation is also not only registered bygovernment to operate in the locations but also have Memorandum of understanding with clear terms of engagement including relationship with government, commitment to serve and deliver, allegiance to total transparency and accountability, and commitment to deliver positive outputs and outcomes. The organisation also has strong working relationship with communities we serve working closely with all age groups who are involved right from needs assessment. In all areas, community health boards have been formed not only to represent the community in matters health but also provide leadership and guidance. Sustainability continues as our focus area in 2020 and the same is being entrachanced in our policies. As part of our policy and APP framework,beneficiaries are informed of their entitlement to quality service delivery and access to utilities as per project requirement. This aspect is currently operational with a strong, non- bias and functional feedback mechanism inplace.
