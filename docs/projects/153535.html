Project Summary

The goal of this project is to protect the rights and uphold the dignity of the most vulnerable in Eastern, Central Equatoria and Bahr El Ghazal, South Sudan. The main objective is that risk of GBV is reduced and quality of response is strengthened for 25,000 rights holders. The key outcomes include IDPs, Host communities mostly women and girls have improved access to existing services for GBV survivors through a secure and confidential referral and reporting system, increased community participation in GBV prevention and response activities. The intervention aims to achieve these outcomes through raising awareness amongst rights holders on GBV concepts, addressing stigma that GBV survivors are subjected to, capacity building of service providers on effective response to GBV survivors a as well as empowering survivors through psychosocial support and where necessary, provision of material and livelihoods support. Through this intervention, NCA will identify and address barriers to reporting with survivors and how they feel service provision can be enhanced. NCA will use an interagency approach in the implementation of the project to ensure that comprehensive services are accessible to survivors.

Needs

High incidents of Gender Based Violence including rape, sexual assault, domestic violence, forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, abduction, discriminatory practices within the legal system and harmful traditional practices are major problems in South Sudan (South Sudan GBV Cluster Strategy 2017). According to data collected by the Gender-based Violence – Information Management System (GBV-IMS) in South Sudan during 2016, approximately 98% of reported GBV incidents affected women and girls. In addition to gender, age and disability can increase risks to GBV, with adolescent girls, unaccompanied children, elderly women and persons with disabilities among the populations most at risk. The escalation of sexual violence is largely caused by ethnic conflicts. At least 4 out of every 10 women are reported to have experienced a form of Gender Based Violence (UNFPA). High illiteracy rates among women and negative cultural further perpetuate sexual violence. The main problems and needs of GBV in South Sudan include; Congestion in PoC and collective centres compromises safety and privacy of women and girls, resulting in adolescent sex and pregnancy, unsafe abortion, survival sex and psychological effects, Women walk long distances to seek both reproductive Health services and GBV services, incidents of rape, psychological and emotional abuse, and forced marriages are on the rise and under-reported, need to empower the survivors with self-awareness to mitigate violence in their lives and psychosocial support to ensure full recovery and resilience for survivors of violence. Further, Food insecurity and inadequate services increase the likelihood of women engaging in negative coping strategies such as transactional sex or sex for food as they struggle to meet basic needs. According to NCA GBV reports, poor coordination of GBV interventions among agencies often lead to duplication of activities affecting quality of GBV response.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

NCA has a Complaints Response mechanism at the country level which highlights communication modalities with communities. The CRM has contact details of focal points at project, country and HQ level where complaints are handled. There is a working email and telephone number for sensitive complaints. In areas where communication by telephone and email is not possible, NCA CRM focal points will consult with communities on best alternative channels for communication. Before projects are started or implemented, communities will be provided with information about the project, details about its scope, funding, objectives, beneficiaries including how the targeting of the beneficiaries of the project was derived. Implementation methodologies including entitlements and rights to complain will be discussed with the communities.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The messages are context specific and tailored to different age groups. For instance, women are consulted separately from men. Specific needs are identified according to expectations of women and girls, boys, while taking into account men’s needs based on their specific needs. Before the start of the project, separate discussions with men, women children, elderly and other interest groups are undertaken. The unique needs and expectation of the group will be recorded. Proposed assistance is planned and tailored to address the specific needs of the group. Disaggregated data based on age and gender is collected from the needs assessment and context specific responses. During monitoring, data collected is disaggregated by Age, Gender and vulnerability. NCA monitoring tools are adapted to ensure that disaggregated data is collected and analysed.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Staff will be made aware about their expected conduct while working at the community level. All NCA and partners staff will be trained in different polices on code of conduct safeguarding sexual exploitation and abuse. Any breaches to the code of conduct will be addressed in the manner prescribed in the HR and employment policies. Community members and their leaders will be informed about their right to complain if any member of the staff or of partner staffs does not uphold their values, breach any policies according to the expectations of the community , They will be provided with avenues for reporting any breaches and complaints. NCA recognizes our responsibility to create a safe and sexual exploitation and abuse-free environment and in doing so, will treat anonymous complaints seriously because of the potential for future abuse and harm. Therefore, every possible effort will be made to investigate the sexual exploitation allegations and satisfactorily address any evidence of abuse, misconduct or malpractice. All NCA employees are requested to report allegations, concerns and suspicion of breaches related to sexual exploitation and abuse. Deliberate non-disclosure will lead to disciplinary action. Mechanisms have been established for whistle blowing as well as for Complaints response mechanism which will help us to monitor and act on any breaches.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

In our assessments, we have consulted a broad range of stakeholders. These have included not only community leaders but also women, men, girls, boys, IDPs, elderly, Disabled, returnees and host communities. When we carry out community consultations in focused group discussions, we also carry out separate discussions with Women, men, girls, elderly, children, displaced and host communities and many other diverse interest groups. During assessments, the assessment teams deliberately hold separate meetings with different community groups to listen to their views about issues affecting them. The nature of consultations included one on one meeting with key informants and opinion leaders, Health workers, school teachers, traders, chiefs. At the household level, these consultations were undertaken with women, widows, returnees, host communities and IDPs.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Since NCA works with and through partners, right holders will either file a complaint with partner or directly to NCA. Partners shall be required to inform NCA country or field offices immediately a complaint is received from a rights holder. All complaints shall be received by CRM focal point in NCA Country Office in Juba, NCA field offices in Torit and Kuajok. Non-Sensitive complaints shall be filed and handled in Juba, Torit and Kuajok field offices while sensitive complaints shall be registered, filed and forwarded to the NCA Head Office in Juba. Sensitive complaints shall be handled by Country Director and/or an Investigation Team constituted by the CRM Committee. Suggestion boxes and registers shall be placed at strategic points within NCA Head office in Juba and field offices for use by rights holders and stakeholders in lodging complaints. E-mail addresses and telephone numbers shall be included in partners’ contracts and shall be clearly visible in public places within partner office premises for easy access by rights holders and stakeholders. Other methods identified or outlined by communities can also be established in cases where email and telephone communication systems are not available. Once complaints are received, they will be registered and handled in a timely and appropriate manner that protects dignity and safety of the complainant. NCA shall acknowledge receipt of the complaint within 24 hours. CRM focal point shall convene a CRM Committee meeting in each office to review the complaint and take appropriate action including recommendation to NCA Country office in Juba. In dealing with sensitive complaints such as allegations of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and fraud and corruption, NCA CRM Committee shall refer to relevant sections of our Code of Conduct and institute an investigation in the matter. The process for fast-tracking of investigations into allegations of sexual exploitation and physical abuse shall be triggered immediately.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

NCA has an elaborate policy of community engagement at every stage of programme cycle management, right from needs assessment, design, implementation to monitoring and evaluation stages. Needs assessment and monitoring data collection methods for our programmes include key informant interview, focused group discussions and household interviews. NCA ensureS that in all these processes for social enquiry, all members of the community are consulted to provide input and feedback on how the project components has affected them positively or negatively. Our monitoring processes are undertaken regularly ON monthly and quarterly basis. Complaints response and feedback mechanism is part and parcel of NCA monitoring frame work. NCA uses a variety of tools to ensure effective and structured community engagement at all levels of information collection and analysis. Normal community meetings are undertaken to engage community members during programme implementation. This helps to improve NCA engagement with community. Data collected during monitoring and evaluation is also shared with community as part of feedback and lessons learnings.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This intervention considers the protection status of women, girls, IDPs, host communities and other interest groups. The intervention aims to reduce or mitigate protection risks of beneficiaries and affected populations especially IDPs. When NCA carried out needs assessments, people’s right to protection as enshrined in international humanitarian and national laws was considered. This analysis provided the basis strengthening protection mechanisms for most vulnerable populations especially women. Assessing and analysing protection risk is done by assessing the risk based on community perceptions and likelihood of occurrence of the risk. The risk to gender-based violence during crisis and emergencies is considered and adjustments made to mitigate or prevent the risk.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This intervention considered bureaucratic and physical barriers to access to GBV services. For instance, the provision of save haven for most vulnerable women and girls could not be obtained due to bureaucratic and cultural impediments. The access to livelihood and material support has been cited as a problem by extremely vulnerable women and girls who are subjugated by stigmatisation. We have looked into the issues of IDPs and hosts communities and their access to medical services. The issue of women access to facilities and risks associated with intimate partner violence and negative cultural practices has been considered and will be addressed by increasing access to GBV response services in areas that are considered safe.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities aim to address neglect by identifying women, girls especially among IDPs and host communities who are extremely vulnerable and deserve protection. Where protection needs identified meet the thresholds of assistance, and where assistance ought to have been provided but not provided to an extremely vulnerable group, this project will provide the needed assistance for the vulnerable group. And where assistance is targeted at only one community eg IDPs and where not assisting hosts who equally maybe vulnerable would exacerbate protection risks, NCA will provide assistance to both communities to address inequities between between hosts and IDPs.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

When we carry out assessments we analyze information to determine the level of needs and assistance provided. Often we consider information such as human rights abuses, violence against women, girls and derivation of basic needs. Where breaches are noted, NCA reports the breaches at the GBV sub cluster level and where assistance is within NCAs ability to intervene, we address the problems after consulting the respective clusters

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

When NCA develops programme implementation plans we consider the time it takes to complete the most critical aspects of the project assuming there will be reasonable access to locations and vulnerable populations without hindrance. Sometimes, our visits need travel authorizations to allow us to visit those locations. We have developed benchmarks for turnaround times for obtaining approvals from various Government authorities. Any time an approval is not granted, this is recorded as an incident within our security management system. When our teams travel in the field and are refused to pass roadblocks to access our intended project areas, we also record this as an incident on the Simpson website. These tools help us to monitor access for beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

We carry out Gender audits as part of our ongoing programme planning and implementation. The gender audits help to understand what risks our programmes are likely to affect vulnerable women, children, elderly, disabled. For instance, we interview women and girls to find out if reporting GBV cases makes them more vulnerable. We find out if location of response support centers makes them more vulnerable. When risks are identified, the project team working closely with GBV survivor identify how these issues of risk and safety can be mitigated by adapting the programme so that the implementation of the project or any part of the project does not contribute to more harm to the beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

We carry out participatory community consultations to find out what needs are critical and a priority to extremely vulnerable segments of population to find out what dignity means to vulnerable groups especially women and girls. This is carried out by finding out what dignity means to different members of community and how this can be strengthened, monitored and measured. Once the important aspects of dignity benchmarks are established we include them in our monitoring systems and measure them regularly to establish progress towards meeting the objectives.
