Project Summary

The project seeks to increase access to life saving health care services by providing an integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys through static and mobile health care services.
The project intervention will focus on expanding essential primary health care emergency clinical services including HIV/AIDS/TB care,SGBV and reproductive health services;implement integrated response for severe acute malnutrition with medical complication; integrated response for epidemic prone communicable including diarrhea, vector, viral Hemorrhagic diseases with WASH and Emergency health protection for people with disabilities ; scale up integrated response to SAM with medical complications, HIV/AIDS/TB; eMTCT; basic emergency Obstetric and strengthen referrals for SGBV and CeMONC accordingly , vaccination against measles and Polio for Under five ,mass vaccination and management for epidemic prone diseases ( Meningitis , Measles and OCV) , vaccination against tetanus for the pregnant if not received, routine EPI and mass vaccination.
The project aims at ensuring:
• Under 5 for malnutrition are screening with MUAC,
• Pregnant women attend at least four times have ANC visit with the first one before 4 months (16 weeks) pregnancy.
• Pregnant woman receive at least two doses of SP
• Pregnant woman receiving 2 or more tetanus vaccinations
• ITN are distributed to pregnant woman attending ANC
• Pregnant mother who attended ANC are Screened for HIV
• Pregnant mother who attended ANC are screened for for STDs
• HIV+ pregnant mother are initiated on eMTCT programme
• Exposed babies are initiated on NVP and Septrin and child
• Deliveries at facility are assisted by skilled staff
• Management of SAM with medical complications in health center
• Provision of in patients services (medical, pediatrics, obstetrics and gynecology wards), Emergency and elective surgery, Laboratory services , Blood bank service and X-Ray service.
• Provision of outpatient services for common disease.
• Management of children classified with severe or very severe diseases
• Management of maternal and newborn health care (Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care) trough provision of care and referrals;
Expend management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment of STI, wound care , emergency contraceptive pills, Hepatitis B and mental health care ); Management of HIV/AIDS/TB
• Weekly Surveillance IDSR & EWARN data, activity based reporting
• Injuries and mental health including Disabilities and injuries rehabilitation,
• Outpatient psychiatric care and Acute psychiatric inpatient unit

Needs

The conflict in the Eastern part of Upper Nile State (Nasir and Ulang Counties) has contributed to displacement and increased the vulnerability of the populations. During the last fighting erupted in February 2018 in Nasir between Government and opposition forces associated with interclan conflicts created more displacement of people. Ulang is known to be hosting a large number of IDPs currently at an estimate number of 30,000 due to its relative peace and proximity to Ethiopia; the basic health care system was extremely weakened due to shortages of funds to inadequate number of qualified health workers and non-functional health facilities. All functional health facilities are supported by NGOs: 16 by UNKEA, 1 by Relief International. No secondary health care services in Ulang and Nasir are functioning effectively. The only hospital in Nasir county was destroyed during the war and currently located in government location and currently inaccessible due to insecurities and difficulties of movement due to lack of means of transport; therefore there is need to scale up services to include Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (CeMONC) including eMTCT, management of minor and surgery ,Management of SAM with medical complications, and HIV/AIDS care. Bordering Ethiopia Nasir and Ulang Counties have an increased risk of Sexually Transmitted Infections (STI), SGBV, including HIV/AIDS, with no health facility currently providing testing nor treatment services(UNKEA 2018) HIV/AIDS care are not implemented effectively due to lack funds. The security situation in the country remains volatile and unpredictable with reported incidentsof inter communal fighting mostly cattle raiding and revenge killings in various locations , currently the security situation remains tense along the border between Unity state and Gogrial East counties. In addition the outbreak of conflict between armed groups in South Sudan’s Deim Zubier town on the first week of April displaced thousands of civilians to multiple locations in the western parts of the country contributed to poor implementation of essential primary health care emergency package. This call for the scaling up the health care services in order to implement effectively the essential primary health care emergency package.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Project shall be guided by UNKEA's internal policies in regared to sexual exploitation and abuse. UNKEA has adopted the Core Humanitarian Standard and incorporated provisions that protect staff, beneficiaries, members from the benefiary community, and humanitarian workers from abuse at all times. All project staff, contractors, and persons engaged directly in the project implementation shall sign up to the code of conduct that restrict behaviors to avoid harm. UNKEA has reporting protocal that is communicated to stakeholders at all interfaces to encourage communication of related instances of sexual exploitation and abuse. At field level, project officer is a recipient for cases where instances do not directly target staff but entities the project engages to contribute to project objectives. Cases involving have provisions where Payam oversight committees communicate directly with national office through senior management staff that travel to field on quarterly basis to interact with the committees. The project will ensure management of rape or any kind of domestic violence and refering the cases of SGBV according to the needs.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The project has two layers of complain mechanisms:First layer is the project steering committee that directly handles the complaints desk in the regular undertaking of project activities. They are comprised of beneficiary representatives constitution of reprentatives of IDPs, Returnees, host communities, women leaders, religious leaders, and local authority. The second tier is the Payam level oversight committee that does not handle any activities but observe and recieve complains on all actors including on project staff, steering committee members, and all actors in project execution. The oversight committee reports directly to Juba based senior manager who enagages with the field on quarterly basis. In addition, the project has in its locations; suggestion boxes that beneficiries use to communicate concerns to project management.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through the project design, communities are engaged both at Project Steering Committee levels and at Payam oversight committee. In addition, monitoring and evaluation tools have included provisions to directly solicit inputs of the county health department, commissioners, local authorities, who are not comprised in any of the two structures. In addition, beneficiary selection, local procurement, local staff recruitment are done with direct input and supervision of local authorities. Authenticity of documentation for each of the above categories of activities are only considered complete with endorsement of local authorities - as a mandatory internal requirement.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In the targeted project locations, possible tensions of access to limited resources between IDPs and host communities have been factored in the project engagements with key stakeholders whom UNKEA is already currently engaging through other projects in the county. Past experience shows that when prior engaged, local authorities are willing to protect IDP populations to access resources. The evidence of similarities in ethnic backgrounds between IDPs and host communities have also played a vital factor in easing tensions that could otherwise emerge from competition for limited resources.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Access barriers include distance to health facilities, shortage of supplies and basic health care packages, poor community attitude to seeking vital health care. These barriers have been taken in to account, including plan for monitoring functionality of health facilities, ensuring that essential medical care packages are available and accessible, among others. Initial interfaces between project staff and local authorities and their subsequent engagement in delivery structures shall provide stronger advocacy appeal to consider marginalised communities to promote their access and participation in the project.
