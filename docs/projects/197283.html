Project Summary

The project aims to address the mentioned issue through a community-based approach and strong partnership with local governments at the municipal level and other relevant humanitarian partners. IMC will engage with communities in the target municipalities through members of community-based organizations (CBOs), traditional and religious leaders, etc… IMC will also put in place community structures that will support different interventions for child protection and GBV. It should be noted that these structures will be made up of both nationals and refugees. In addition, IMC will work in close collaboration with the municipality, social centers (MINAS), schools as well as with the Ministry in charge of the Promotion of Women and the Family (MINPROFF).
Key interventions include trainings of project staff, community volunteers/animators, relevant humanitarian partners, community and religious leaders, women's advocates, leaders of community organizations and members of established community mechanisms on the issues related to GBV, child protection, promotion of girls' rights, inclusive education, ... Awareness-raising activities will also be carried out at the community level on various subjects, including reporting of Protection cases, the rights of children and adolescents to be protected from violence, the harmful effects of early marriage and other cultural practices and behaviors affecting children and adolescents, the importance of education inclusive. Additionally, IMC UK will provide material support to vulnerable children and those at heightened risk of violence, abuse and exploitation and to foster families of unaccompanied and separated children.
IMC will adopt the's innovative approach will be the "Girl Shine" model for the development of life skills for adolescent girls at the community level to improve their protection against all forms of violence and promote their rights.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In recent years, Cameroon has been affected by crises in several neighboring countries, which have led to insecurity and left Cameroonian communities in state of vulnerability, especially women, and children living in border areas. The Eastern region hosts more than 154,067 Central African refugees1, including 39,439 girls under age of 18, with 86.02% of them living in the sub-division of Garoua-Boulai, Kette, Batouri and Kentzou,. This influx of refugees places significant pressure on already limited resources and basic social services in host areas, exacerbating pre-existing vulnerabilities, particularly affecting women and children. The gradual decline in humanitarian aid and the insufficient level of funding for development projects further negatively impact vulnerability of refugees. Refugees and host communities therefore find themselves with substantial humanitarian needs through access to basic services such as nutrition, health, education, protection which require a combined response effort. In this climate of insecurity and poor access to services, women and children represent the part of population which are most exposed to violence. Violence against women and girls is common and poses a threat to their health and well-being as well as to social cohesion of communities. According to the Demographic and Health Survey 2018, 07.7% of girls against 02.9% of boys aged 15-19 are sexual violence survivors. From January 2021 to September 2022 in the sub-division of Garoua-Boulai, Kette, Batouri and Kentzou, 225 GBV cases were registered, 24% were against children aged under 18, 16% were rapes and 10% forced marriage (IMC activities reports). Regarding child marriage, MICS 2014 report reveal that 11% and 36% of girls is married before 15yo and 18yo respectively. The same survey shows that 47% of children aged 5-17 were involved in child labor, with girls (44%) slightly less than boys (50%). This phenomenon is more pronounced in rural areas (61%) than urban areas (30%). From IMC 2021 activity reports, 126 cases of children at risk of protection (65% girls) were identified in the sub-division of Garoua-Boulai, Kette, Batouri and Kentzou. Of these protection cases, 43% were involved in worst forms of labour with more than half being girls.
