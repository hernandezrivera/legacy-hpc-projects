Project Summary

The Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) project in South Sudan seeks to enhance the living conditions, protection, and resilience of displaced populations, refugees, voluntary returnees, and host communities in the states of Upper Nile, Unity, and Central Equatoria. It targets individuals in displacement sites and return areas in Renk, Maban, Malakal, Ulang, Panyikang (Upper Nile); Robkona, Leer, Mayendit, Panyijar (Unity); and Juba, (Central Equatoria). The project aligns with broader CCCM objectives in South Sudan, addressing the needs of vulnerable populations amidst conflict, intercommunal violence, and natural disasters.
Context and Target Areas
South Sudan is enduring a prolonged crisis marked by ongoing conflict, intercommunal violence, and frequent natural disasters, all of which have led to large-scale displacement. In recent months, the country has experienced an influx of refugees from Sudan and a return of South Sudanese nationals from neighboring countries such as Uganda, Kenya, and Ethiopia. These movements have placed further strain on already overburdened host communities, which are struggling to meet basic needs. The CCCM intervention specifically targets key areas of displacement and return across the three states, which are vital for both current and future humanitarian needs:
Upper Nile: Renk, Maban, Malakal, Ulang, Panyikang
Unity: Robkona, Leer, Mayendit, Panyijar.
Central Equatoria: Juba.
These areas have significant populations of internally displaced persons (IDPs), refugees, and returnees, alongside host communities. The target regions face acute challenges related to overcrowding, insufficient infrastructure, lack of services, and limited access to resources, which the CCCM project aims to address.
The CCCM intervention is designed to address both the immediate and long-term needs of displaced populations, returnees, refugees, and host communities in South Sudan. Through strengthening coordination, improving site management, promoting community engagement, and addressing protection risks, the project aims to foster resilience and contribute to the achievement of durable solutions. The capacity-building components will help ensure that local stakeholders are better equipped to manage displacement sites and integrate displaced populations into host communities.
Through this comprehensive approach, the project seeks to reduce dependency on humanitarian aid, improve living conditions, and promote social cohesion among displaced and host populations. The CCCM intervention will support the overall humanitarian and development objectives in South Sudan, ultimately contributing to greater stability, enhanced protection, and sustainable recovery for affected communities.
The project is aligned with South Sudan’s broader CCCM objectives and will play a crucial role in ensuring that all displaced populations, returnees, refugees, and host communities are supported with dignity and respect, helping them move towards a more stable and self-reliant future.

1. Needs

The CCCM project identifies a range of critical needs among displaced populations, refugees, voluntary returnees, and host communities in Upper Nile, Unity, and Central Equatoria States. These needs, categorized through extensive analysis and consultations, highlight the necessity of immediate and sustainable solutions. 1. Camp Coordination and Management NeedsLack of effective site management: Many displacement sites lack robust coordination mechanisms to ensure efficient service delivery, leading to unmet needs and disorganized management.Service delivery gaps: Essential services such as shelter, WASH, and food distribution remain inadequate or poorly managed.Limited access to information: Displaced individuals often lack clear, reliable information on available services, assistance programs, and durable solutions. 2. Protection and Safety Needs.Heightened protection risks: Vulnerable groups, including women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities, are at increased risk of exploitation, GBV, and neglect.Safety challenges: Inadequate lighting, insufficient security, and poorly planned infrastructure in camps heighten risks of violence and insecurity.Social tensions: Increased pressure on host communities from refugee and returnee influxes strains resources and fosters tensions. 3. Integration and Livelihood Needs.Barriers to integration: Refugees and voluntary returnees face challenges integrating into host communities due to limited resources, documentation barriers, and inadequate infrastructure.Lack of livelihood opportunities: Dependence on humanitarian aid is exacerbated by limited access to income-generating activities, further entrenching poverty. 4. Access to Basic Services.Shelter deficiencies: Overcrowded, inadequate shelters expose individuals to weather extremes and health risks.WASH challenges: Limited access to clean water and proper sanitation increases vulnerability to waterborne diseases.Healthcare and nutrition gaps: Insufficient healthcare services and food insecurity have contributed to malnutrition and preventable diseases.Education access: Displaced children face barriers to education due to inadequate schools, overcrowding, and resource shortages. 5. Community Engagement and Capacity Building Needs.Limited participation: Displaced populations are often excluded from decision-making, reducing the relevance and effectiveness of interventions.Capacity gaps: Local stakeholders lack adequate training and resources to manage displacement sites and address evolving challenges effectively. 6. Social Cohesion and Resource Sharing Needs.Strained community relations: Resource competition has led to tensions between displaced populations and host communities.Conflict resolution mechanisms: The lack of effective dispute resolution frameworks undermines social cohesion and stability. 7. Durable Solutions Needs.Barriers to return or relocation: Insecurity, destroyed infrastructure, and livelihood challenges hinder voluntary return or integration.Need for sustainability: Long-term support is required to build self-reliance and reduce dependence on aid. Conclusion.A comprehensive approach addressing these assessed needs is essential to improving the lives of displaced populations, refugees, returnees, and host communities. The CCCM project integrates immediate service delivery with strategies for resilience, social cohesion, and sustainable development to support individuals with dignity and promote long-term recovery.

2. Project activities

The CCCM project addresses critical needs of displaced populations, refugees, voluntary returnees, and host communities across Upper Nile, Unity, and Central Equatoria States. The approach prioritizes improved coordination, service delivery, protection, and resilience while ensuring inclusivity and sustainability. 1. Strengthening Camp Coordination and ManagementSite management mechanisms: Deploy teams to oversee service delivery, identify gaps, and coordinate responses with humanitarian actors, authorities, and community representatives.Site infrastructure development: Improve shelters, pathways, lighting, and security perimeters to ensure safe and functional environments.Information management: Create systems to monitor needs, service coverage, and population movements effectively. 2. Enhancing Protection and Safety.Protection assessments: Conduct regular evaluations to identify risks, focusing on vulnerable groups such as women, children, elderly persons, and individuals with disabilities.GBV prevention and response: Develop referral pathways, provide psychosocial support, and raise awareness to mitigate gender-based violence.Community safety: Install lighting, enhance camp security, and encourage community-led safety patrols to reduce violence risks. 3. Supporting Integration and Livelihoods.Facilitating integration: Mediate between returnees, refugees, and host communities to reduce tensions and promote resource-sharing initiatives.Livelihood support: Offer vocational training, small business grants, and skills programs to foster economic independence and reduce aid reliance.Access to documentation: Assist individuals in obtaining identification to improve access to services and legal rights. 4. Improving Access to Basic Services.Shelter upgrades: Rehabilitate shelters to meet safety and dignity standards.WASH improvements: Enhance access to clean water, sanitation facilities, and hygiene promotion to minimize health risks.Healthcare and nutrition: Establish health outposts, strengthen mobile clinics, and target malnutrition among children, pregnant women, and lactating mothers.Education access: Build learning spaces, train teachers, and supply materials to support education continuity. 5. Community Engagement and Capacity Building.Participatory decision-making: Create community committees to ensure populations are included in planning and implementation processes.Capacity building: Train camp management teams, authorities, and leaders to enhance local capacity for effective management.Awareness campaigns: Inform communities about their rights, services, and durable solutions to empower better decision-making. 6. Promoting Social Cohesion and Resource Sharing.Conflict resolution: Establish dialogue platforms to mediate disputes and foster peaceful coexistence.Community projects: Support joint initiatives, such as infrastructure repairs and community farming, to build collaborative relationships.Cultural integration: Organize events to build trust and understanding between diverse groups. 7. Facilitating Durable Solutions.Support for voluntary returns: Provide logistical and reintegration support for individuals returning to their areas of origin.Local integration: Assist refugees and returnees with housing, livelihoods, and services to integrate into host communities.Pathways to self-reliance: Promote job creation, agricultural support, and vocational training for sustainable livelihoods. 8. Monitoring and Evaluation.Needs assessments: Regularly assess needs to adapt and refine response strategies.Service monitoring: Ensure services meet standards and address community priorities through continuous evaluation and feedback.Impact evaluations: Measure effectiveness to inform future programming and enhance outcomes.
