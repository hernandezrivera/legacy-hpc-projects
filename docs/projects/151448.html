Project Summary

Through this project FAO aims to investigate and map potential groundwater aquifers in four regions of South/Central Somalia, and develop 2 boreholes out of the identified aquifers for improved safe water supply to the communities. Over 25,000 persons of all genders are expected to benefit from this project through sustainable access to safe water.

Needs

The Somali water sector has been adversely affected by consecutive seasons of poor rainfall leading to severe drought and acute water shortage in the recent past. A large population across the country do not have sustainable access to safe drinking water. At the national level access to an improved water source is below 40%, while in some regions especially in the southern parts of the country such as in Bay and Bakool regions the levels are below 25%. The problem is compounded by the current high levels of food insecurity. As assessment findings from Gu 2018 indicate, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis, IPC phase 3, or worse up to the end of the year (Source: FSNAU). In perennial drought-affected areas, there is need for long term solutions to safe water supply with environmental and functional sustainability. In times of drought, women and children responsible for fetching water for domestic use are forced to walk long distances in the search for water. This burdens women, taking time away from income generating and productive activities. Children on the other hand find themselves unable to attend school in order to support their families. Women and girls are especially vulnerable to sexual violence as they must walk long hours well into the night to fetch water. The establishment of sustainable boreholes, bringing water closer to communities without access, requires good hydrogeology information. Somalia lacks a national hydrogeology map, and information on hydrogeology to facilitate drilling and development of boreholes is limited due to site-specific surveys carried out for different purposes. As a result, resources have been wasted in the drilling of dry boreholes. The proposed project aims to address these issues through professional assessments and the establishment of boreholes in selected areas of Galgadud, Hiraan, Bay and Bakool Regions in Somalia.

Activities

1. Remote sensing analysis of multispectral satellite images: Satellite images will be procured and analyzed to identify features associated with groundwater occurrence. Using the outcome of the remote sensing analysis, preliminary delineation of potential groundwater aquifers will be done.2. Field hydrogeological/geophysical surveys and mapping of water sources: Accessible areas within the four regions will be visited and data collected on strategic water sources. Hydrogeological/geophysical data will also collected in at least 8 locations (2 per region), identified from the remote sensing analysis to have good potential for groundwater. Data from the field surveys will be used to further refine the remote sensing results/maps.3. Drilling of boreholes in selected aquifers: Potential groundwater aquifers identified through remote sensing and confirmed through field surveys will be presented to MoEWR, WASH Cluster, and other stakeholders for consultative selection of two drilling sites based on access and humanitarian needs. The selected sites will be drilled through a contract following the FAO procurement procedures.4. Installation of water pumps: Each of the two drilled boreholes will be installed with an appropriate pump and an outlet point where water can be fetched from or further distributed. The targets and indicators of the project are:1. At least one hydrogeology map at scale of 1:500,000; with accompanying GIS database.2. Updated water sources database and 8 geophysics logs with recommendations on drilling depths, water quality, etc. 3. Two boreholes drilled. 4. Two pumps installed in the drilled boreholes to facilitate safe water access to households. Trainings will also be done on the use of the hydrogeological information and products for groundwater development to the Somalia Government Ministry staff and partners.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project will be implemented in collaboration with the MoEWR. Water supply to the urban and rural communities across Somalia is a key priority for the ministry, in line with the medium and long term strategy. During this project technical staff from this ministry will be involved in all stages of implementation as a way of capacity building for future sustainability. Specific trainings will be done to the ministry staff on hydrogeology data collection, analysis and interpretation for identification of ground water aquifers. Prior to drilling the two boreholes, detailed hydrogeology and geophysics investigations will be done to identify good yielding aquifers. Further, after drilling test pumping will be professionally carried out to determine the capacity of the aquifer. The pumping test results will be used to design the pump, and define the limits on the amount of water extracted from the borehole at a given time to avoid over pumping. This will ensure sustainability of the borehole. At least eight identified sites will be presented to the MoEWR and other stakeholders for consultation and prioritization based on the most vulnerable population. The selection process will be open and transparent. A procedure to be followed will be developed at the initiation stage of the project and agreed upon by the MoEWR and other stakeholders. One key factor to be considered in the selection process is distance to ensure that established boreholes, or the water collection points, are as near as possible to the community – preferably within 500m. A management committee for the established water source will be created, comprising of both men and women. The committee will be trained on the management of the water points before a hand over is done. The committee will be empowered to make key decisions regarding the borehole, including setting prices for water as well as disciplinary actions for non-compliant members. Technicians will as well be appointed from within the community, and trained on the operation and basic maintenance of the borehole. FAO SWALIM and implementing partner/s will continue to offer technical support to the management and technical team of the water source, and organize refresher trainings as a means of capacity building for long term sustainability

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Displacement especially due to natural disasters such as floods could render access to water sources/boreholes impossible. In the development of the two boreholes standard guidelines will be followed to ensure they can withstand floods. Proper maintenance of the facility and awareness of the measures to take during emergency will form part of the community training before handover of the project. Selection of the borehole development site will also take into account areas least likely to be affected by floods. There is however a risk of persons displaced from other areas setting up camps near the established boreholes, where there is assured supply of water. This is likely to cause a lot of pressure on the borehole, with a risk of over pumping. Coordination between the government and other humanitarian partners would avert this challenge, should the problem occur.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women and children are the main beneficiaries of a water supply project, as they are more involved in collection of water for domestic use. In this project there is equal representation in terms of gender and age. However, there is a risk of excluding women in the formation of management committees for the boreholes. A minimum 1/3 gender rule will be applied in selecting members of the borehole committee, to ensure good representation. The selection of participants for any training undertaken during the project will also be taken into consideration to achieve adequate gender representation.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Assessments and the establishment of groundwater sources such as boreholes requires many field surveys for data collection. There are sections of Somalia where insecurity hinders access for field data collection. Unfortunately, the affected areas are most in need of the water sources, as they have long been excluded from humanitarian and development interventions due to insecurity. Borehole drilling in such areas is difficult, as few contractors are willing to risk their equipment due to local instability. In some locations, however, there are community members with connections to local contractors. But many local contractors with drilling rigs lack the technical know-how to undertake sustainable, damage-less drilling. For the project to hire professional drillers may therefore generate tension, which can lead to conflict. This would negatively impact the project, ultimately impeding community access to the project’s services. To address this challenge Somalia’s Ministry of Energy and Water Resources will register qualified drillers. Once FAO’s selection is done, the ministry will be informed of the selection, as per FAO’s impartial procurement system. For the proposed project, physical presence on the ground will be minimized by initiating the analysis with remote sensing data. Through analysis of satellite images by experts, areas with potential for groundwater will be delineated. Only areas identified to be potential will be visited for field data collection. Local experts will be trained to carry out the field data collection, with FAO providing supervision and technical backstopping. This will further minimize the risk on FAO, as the local staff are more acceptable within the communities, and can also easily maneuver their way in case of conflicts. Through this approach the objectives of the project will be achieved as planned with minimal risk to beneficiaries and vulnerable communities.
