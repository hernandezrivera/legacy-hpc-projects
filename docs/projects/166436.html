Project Summary

In addition to the health impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic in Bangladesh, there are long-term effects on socioeconomic factors as a result of the health crisis. While needed to save lives, restrictions on movement and trade have crushing effects on local and national economies. Incomes of vulnerable people are disrupted; businesses shrink or close; supply chains are impeded, and hundreds of millions of people are losing their jobs in the informal and formal sector, resulting in loss of access to the cash or credit needed to buy life essentials. Highly vulnerable individuals who already live in poverty and rely on daily or wage labor are struggling to buy food, maintain access to safe housing, purchase health and hygiene supplies, or meet other basic needs as they lose crucial income. The project will focus on the health response through utilizing local capacity in the production of hygiene materials such as cloth-masks, hand sanitizer and PPE. In line with the Food Security Sector (FSS) Strategic Response, IOM will ensure host communities, front line health responders, local government authorities and government law enforcement are equipped with preventive hygiene materials to mitigate the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Needs

The COVID-19 crisis is expected to have massive health and socioeconomic implications on the District of Cox’s Bazar —one of the country’s most vulnerable districts with more than 700,000 people estimated to lose their livelihoods. Through livelihood opportunities for the local production of hygiene materials, IOM aims at both reducing the transmission of COVID-19 and utilizing local capacity to reduce socioeconomic impacts. In environments where social distancing is difficult to maintain, the use of simple, low-cost materials can be an additional public health measure that helps to break the chain of transmission through slowing the spread of the virus and alleviating pressure on the supply of medical-grade masks required by health care facilities. The current global stockpile of PPE is insufficient, particularly for medical masks and respirators; the supply of gowns and goggles is soon expected to be insufficient also. Surging global demand − driven not only by the number of COVID-19 cases but also by misinformation, panic buying and stockpiling − will result in further shortages of PPE globally. The capacity to expand PPE production is limited, and the current demand for respirators and masks cannot be met, especially if the widespread, inappropriate use of PPE continues.

Activities or outputs
Key project/performance indicators

Following IOM Global Appeal Pillars, the project will focus on Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) targeting not only both host and Rohingya populations but also frontline responders of Heath, WASH, camp management and government law enforcing agencies to protect their lives while serving people in need. Particularly, IOM will utilize local capacity in the production of hygiene materials such as cloth-masks, hand sanitizer, face shield etc. to complement government efforts during the health emergency measures and to maximize the impacts for the response. The proposed hygiene materials will be produced simultaneously throughout the project period (9 month) considering government directives on stockpile of such materials to be ready with any potential health related outbreak. Given allocated budget aims to cover 220,000 beneficiaries with hygiene materials and production process with will be implemented with IOM’s partners who already have been engaged in this activity so that start-up phase and cost could be minimized and contributed to cover wider populations. Furthermore, the project will ensure inclusion of vulnerable households, different needs of gender and age by distributing hygiene materials particularly to EVIs, women headed households, PwDs, girls and boys both in host and Rohingya communities, and engage more than 60% of women to the production activity to generate incomes to meet basic needs. Considering the restriction in the movement and importance of women’s role in household level during COVID 19, flexible working modality will be applied such as home-based production, salary celling and rotation so that women can participate in the designing of the activity. IOM Livelihood has been maintained its commitment to engaging with and being accountable to affected communities at all phase of the programme cycle in close coordination with Site Management and Communication with Communities (CwC) through established feedback mechanism in the field. The project will conduct Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) as a systematic method of collecting feedback to ensure any comments or complaints are heard and addressed by the project. In consultation with health sector, Civil Surgeon and following Food Security Sector guideline, the disposal of cloth mask will be reflected and instructed to the beneficiaries.
