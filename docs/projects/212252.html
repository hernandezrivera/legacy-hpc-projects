Project Summary

Needs:
The 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) Summary states that 7.2M people are in need of Education assistance. Also, 161 subdistricts are categorized as severity score 4 or 5 in terms of education needs. The past 13 years of conflict have led to the fracturing and under-resourcing of the education system, consequently limiting the ability of children to access education. The February 6th earthquake in the northern governorates, COVID-19 and Cholera outbreak have further exacerbated education constraints. This project is aimed at increasing access to quality education for the most vulnerable children affected by conflict in target locations and build the capacity of the education personnel.
Intervention, implementation of activities:
ZOA will coordinate closely with MOE to select schools. The activities are in line with Education Sector Objective 1: ‘Increased safe and equitable availability and access to education for crisis-affected children and youth’. Proposed activities are 1) rehabilitation of schools/classrooms to increase access to protected, safe and friendly learning spaces and to ease overcrowding and encourage smooth return to schools; 2) improvement and rehabilitation of gender-sensitive WASH facilities, 3) provision of education supplies for both students and teachers; 4) provision of school furniture, school bags and teaching/learning materials; 5) teacher training and capacity building; and 6) PSS activities. ZOA’s MEAL is the responsibility of the country-based Manager of Program Quality. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework indicators and activities with sex, age & disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability & for demonstrating impact. The Project Manager & Education Officer will make frequent site visits to monitor CB/PSS activities, initiatives & contract implementation. Monitoring data will be used to identify opportunities to adjust the implementation strategy of the project thus contributing to improved results. Across the intervention, ZOA is targeting around 5% of beneficiaries with disabilities.
Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures
Project related risks & mitigations:
1. Changes in exchange rate & increasing inflation: ZOA closely monitors market prices, and contractors are committed to their offers for a specific period to avoid big changes in pricing due to exchange rate changes or inflation.
2. Lack of fuel availability: ZOA has contracts with several transportation service providers, allowing us to conduct the necessary trips without major constraints.
3. Lack of skilled labor: ZOA already has contracts with experienced local skilled laborers.
4. Delayed approvals: ZOA has an MOU with MOWR to facilitate faster approvals.
5. Security risks affecting implementation in the field: ZOA continually assesses the situation in the field and coordinates wit INSO, UNDSS, local authorities and community leaders.
6. Fraud and corruption: ZOA has clear policies preventing and mitigating risks of fraud and corruption including segregation of duties, and regularly trains its staff on these topics. All financial transactions are documented, filed and monitored and regular spot-checks and audits are conducted. Communities and stakeholders are well informed about their entitlements using multiple channels and good reporting channels are in place.

Please note that the activities mentioned are covering two years (2024-2025), but the targets and budgets included in this intervention only cover the year 2024. Additionally, in 2025 ZOA aims to support an estimated 1500 persons with a budget of around 1,5M USD.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis:The project will adopt an inclusive and non-discriminatory approach so that all groups can safely and equitably access assistance. Target populations are at the center of decision-making processes throughout the project, for example consulting on the eligibility for assistance, which are based on context specific needs, preferences and vulnerabilities. This project considers the safety and dignity of beneficiaries to avoid creating or exacerbating protection risks for individuals, households or communities. (i.e. safety of beneficiaries, social tensions, intra-household dynamics). Project related protection risks include potential abuse, exploitation or other unacceptable behaviors by contractors, suppliers or staff within the community during the implementation of the works or during distributions. Therefore, all contractors, suppliers and ZOA staff/ volunteers will sign ZOA’s Code of Conduct, including a child protection policy. Also, contractors are required to provide a safe and secure work environment for their workers. Multi-channel feedback mechanisms are in place and ZOA staff knows how to deal with different types of feedback, including referrals for psychological and protection services and support. All staff including volunteers are trained on protection, PSEAH, ZOA’s CoC and child protection. PSEA strategy:All ZOA staff, volunteers and contractors sign a Code of Conduct, including a child protection policy, informing them of their obligations as well as consequences of involvement in sexual exploitation and abuse. Staff is trained to make safe referrals to relevant services for people identified to be in need of specialized protection services in coordination with the Protection Sector. The project will put in place several accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries, staff and partners, that include a variety of confidential feedback channels, eg. hotlines, social media, complaints box. Beneficiaries and other stakeholders will be informed about these feedback and reporting channels from the start and throughout the intervention and are also encouraged to share their preferred way of providing feedback or reporting issues and complaints. All the feedback and complaints from beneficiaries will be systematically collected, assessed and responded to in a timely and confidential manner.
