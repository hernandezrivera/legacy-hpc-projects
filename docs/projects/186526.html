Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Nigeria’s north-eastern states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (the so-called BAY
states) is expected to persist unabated in 2021: the continuing conflict will still severely affect millions of
people in 2021, subjecting them to displacement (new or continued), impoverishment and threat of violence. Some 1.92 million people are displaced internally, and 257,000 have sought refuge in neighbouring Cameroon, Chad, and Niger. The majority (54%) of the internally displaced people (IDPs) have found refuge in host communities. Borno State has 81% of the IDPs, of whom slightly more than half (54%) stay in IDP camps. In 2020, some 81,000 newly displaced people arrived in camps and the host communities across the BAY states. The armed conflict has no clear end in sight (HRP 2021).
According to the Health Sector Bulletin (May 2021) by WHO for northeast Nigeria, there are 5.8 million
people in need of healthcare out of which 5.3 million people were targeted. Only 1.0 million people were reached in 2021 with around 1.9 million IDPs in the 3 BAY states in need of healthcare.
Furthermore, The COVID-19 pandemic both deepens humanitarian needs and complicates the response. The Nigerian economy has suffered from the fall in global oil prices and from restriction measures to curtail the spread of the virus, particularly intermittent border closures and the need to dedicate resources to respond to the pandemic (HRP 2021). There is also limited resource to expand the existing health facilities or improve services while the number of displaced people and number of IDPs relocating to the communities continue to be on the rise.

1 - Needs

Improving access to and the quality of health of the IDPs and people in host communities is important as it contributes to improved economic situation of the family, communities, and the affected states. According to the HRP 2021, the number of live births in the next 12 months will be 326,000 and the number of stillbirths in the next 12 months 7,500 among the 244,000 number of currently pregnant women in the conflict zone. Out of the population of those affected by conflict, the number of women of reproductive age is 2 million number of adolescents (10-19) is 2 million, the number of sexually active men is 1.7 million and the number of adults living with a sexually transmitted infection is 297,000 while the number of sexually active men who use condoms is 348,000 compared to women of reproductive age who use modern contraceptives 306,000 (HRP 2021). This figure shows the huge gaps that are existing in the health sector despite the efforts put in by health partners. Most of the areas in these conflict zones were inaccessible in the last one year but with the improved security situation and especially with the closing of IDPs camps in the city following the relocation of the IDPs to their host communities, there is an urgent need to improve the health conditions of the population in area of SRH and for women, girls, pregnant and lactating women and men as well as those who are infected with sexually transmitted infections in the hard-to-reach areas. GHIV Africa’s mandate of delivering healthcare at the doorstep of every community has SRH as one of its components and has been using community-based and facility-based approaches in programming for SRH interventions using the MISP aproach. GHIV Africa intends to improve and scale up existing health services in the hard-to-reach LGAs of Kaga, Banki, Sani and Bayo through community engagements and sensitizations and service delivery at the health facilities for the targeted population.

2 - Activities

GHIV Africa shall be carrying out the following activities:1. Procurement of essential maternal health medicines and equipment. 2. Refresher training of Midwives and Community Extention workers on the provision of quality family planning, antenatal care, safe delivery and emergency care to mothers and newborns. 3. Facilitating deployment of Midwives and Community extension workers to remote/hard-to-reach communities. 4. Strengthening referral networks for maternal and newborn emergencies within communities. 5. Provision of adolescent sexual reproductive health counseling and education. 6. Mobilization and sensitization of communities for the uptake of SRH services. 7. Recruitment of 30 community health volunteers (CHV) and training them on Antenatal, Post-natal referrals, and sensitization on danger signs in Pregnancy and reporting. 8. Working and interacting with community members through focus group discussions on preventive health care. 9. Interacting with clients who had accessed reproductive services through client exit interviews. 10. Physical observation of stores where commodities are kept for sustainability. 11. Strengthening referral networks for mental health concerns and emergencies within communities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GHIV Africa will ensure that safety, dignity, accountability and do no harm procedures are implemented and respected during the entire period of implementation. GHIV Africa will constantly monitor the security situation of the area of intervention in order for the mobile teams to choose secured and authorized roads to access the vulnerable IDPs in need. The mobile teams will only organise their activities in places considered as safe, in areas agreed on with the local authorities to minimize the risks taken by beneficiaries of settling in areas of continuing conflict or areas that have not been cleared of landmines and unexploded ordnance.To support the recognition of protection concerns, GHIV Africa field staffs will receive an initial training on child protection and GBV mainstreaming before the implementation of the activities. There will also be an emphasis to ensure an equal number of project staff will be female to ease the communication and interaction with female beneficiaries. Mobile teams will allow a flexible access to various locations where IDPs will be established, even temporarily, protection services will thus be available during day time, ensure safety for children, and the teams are free to come back to any location if any urgent needs arise. In order to avoid the creation of tensions or added risk for the beneficiaries who will receive items through the distribution of NFIs and food distribution for in the project activities, GHIV Africa will ensure strong information sharing on selection criteria, the value of items and usage and distribution processes, and that beneficiaries’ specific needs and opinions are taken into account. GHIV Africa’s MEAL department will establish a feedback mechanism that beneficiaries can access.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GHIV Africa has established a Complaint and response mechanism (CRM) – a formalized process providing a safe confidential, accessible, transparent and effective channel for beneficiaries and other relevant stakeholders to raise concerns, register complaints, call for investigation and make suggestions. GHIV Africa will systematically investigate and follow up on complaints in a timely manner. The purpose of the CRM and relevant contact information (phone, email, physical address etc.) is communicated to beneficiaries through complaints boxes wherever relevant, project activities as well as on distributed materials and / or at distribution sites. Staff will explain the purpose to beneficiaries. Received complaints are categorized according to level of sensitivity and severity and responded to in accordance to GHIV Africa’s Standard Operating Procedures for Programme-related Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism. GHIV Africa will inform beneficiaries and the broader community on the selection criteria as well as on the distribution process in order to avoid the creation of tension. GHIV Africa’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) mechanisms provide a two-way communication channel through which beneficiaries and affected populations can give feedback and complaints, which will enable GHIV Africa to adjust its intervention accordingly and address issues directly in a time-effective manner. GHIV Africa will also ensure that its staff will comply with its code of conduct and serious measures will be taken if staff violates it during the course of the project. Lastly, GHIV Africa's firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance.
