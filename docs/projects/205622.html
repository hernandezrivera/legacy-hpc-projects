Project Summary

The aim of the project is to facilitate the targeted community composed of: returnees, vulnerable host community and IDPs to have access to agricultural inputs (tools and seeds) specifically short season and drought tolerant seeds The farmers groups will be organized into Saving groups after they have received improved agricultural inputs. This support will enable to harvest at least twice depending on the agricultural calendar in the project region.
In the actual context, the population of South Sudan particularly the vulnerable community groups are not getting sufficient food to cover the nutritional body need.
The project activities will focus on an integrated approach of food nutrition security and saving groups for the communal economic stability.
The crop seeds delivered will be multiplied in the community. For the environment protection, solar irrigation pumps will be promoted and trees nurseries, these under organized farmer groups.
Malteser International will conduct vulnerability mapping with the involvement of Payams representatives and local community leaders (Boma Chiefs and villages Chiefs).
The project will target 2.200 beneficiaries’ households: 2.100 HH with agricultural support and 100 households in the disaster risk reduction and anticipatory approach). The beneficiary’s selection will be based on criteria such as: returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host community (widows; disable persons…) at household level. Good Agricultural practices will be provided to the households.
The project will, finally help local farmers to protect their crops from further damage; Promote SMART agriculture practices that does not make climate issues worse by using crops that require less water, less space and are more nutritious. It will also contribute in Holistic approaches to farming and health which take into consideration the interlinkages of climate, human health, and animal health.

1. Needs

Levels of acute food insecurity will remain high during the main harvesting period from October 2023 through January 2024, with the persistence of Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes in many areas. In the east where forecasts anticipate a deepening of seasonal rainfall deficits and where crop production is already typically limited, households are likely to harvest at most a few months of stocks. Conditions will be exacerbated further by persistently high staple food prices, high needs linked to large-scale arrivals of South Sudanese returnees and refugees in bordering counties, and associated disruptions in trade flows from Sudan and the region. Additionally, the seasonal reduction in assistance despite rising needs in the northern areas and the anticipated large funding shortfalls that are likely to interfere with a necessary scale-up in assistance in the north is expected to contribute to persistent high needs in the harvest period. Overall, Emergency outcomes are expected in 18 counties mainly in Upper Nile, Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and Warrap, while Crisis outcomes will remain widespread, with 54 counties assessed to likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). Meanwhile, Fashoda, Panyikang, and Renk of Upper Nile; Fangak and Canal/Pigi of Jonglei; and Aweil North and Aweil East of Northern el Ghazal will remain counties of greatest concern due to the protracted negative impacts of conflict, the expectation for below to significantly below average harvest, and the large-scale influx of South Sudanese returnees.Despite the counties in phase 4 – 5, a particular intention and urgent action should be considered for populations in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) to protect their livelihoods and reduce household-level food consumption gaps. The inequality of rainfall, the prolonged drought and floodings are currently observed in Wau – Yei – Juba (peri-urban areas); these different phenomena are due to climatic changes and are negatively affecting the agricultural calendar specifically in the pre- and post-harvesting stages which seriously reduce crop yields and seed production. Also the grazing spaces are reduced due to the prolonged drought and the persisting flooding. Malteser International will be monitoring the situation on the ground and consult the actors in the domain of Food security and Livelihood specifically FAO. A closer follow-up of the rainfall distribution and prevision will be done to ensure the planting seasons are observed.

2. Project activities

-Distribution of agricultural inputs: farming tools and improved seeds to 2200 households in Wau – Yei – Juba (peri-urban areas).-Establishment of farmer groups comprising of at least 20 households.-Establishment of 2 trees nurseries in 2 the bomas targeted.-Distribution of 10 portable solar irrigation pump to the targeted groups near a water source.-Trainings in Good Agricultural practices at the beginning of the rain season.-Trainings in nutrition covering food diversification, meal nutritional value and culinary / cooking demonstrations at 20 demo centers. -Postharvest and Marketing skills trainings to 2200 hh small scale farmers.-Introduction and training of progressive groups in Village savings and Loan associations (VSLA) to 20 groups
