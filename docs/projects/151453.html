Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to reduction of infant morbidity and mortality through improved access to screening, treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition; as well as availing preventive micronutrient supplementation among some of the most vulnerable communities and regions in South Central Somalia.

Needs

Malnutrition remains a significant problem in Somalia, especially amongst children under 5 years of age and women of reproductive age, contributing to both infant and maternal mortality. Despite a fairly good rainy season in 2018, recovery within some communities remains fragile. Gu 2018 assessments by FSNAU indicate at National level, median prevalence of acute malnutrition improved from Critical in June-July 2017-( GAM of 17.4% and SAM of 3.2 %) to Serious (14.0 % GAM and 3.2 % SAM) in June-July 2018 (Gu 2018). Certain population groups such as Baidoa, Mogadishu and Galkacyo IDPs are of concern due to chronically high rates of acute malnutrition. Widespread acute malnutrition continues to persist across Somalia as 36% of the livelihoods surveyed during the post Gu assessments show prevalence of Critical levels of GAM (≥ 15%). High morbidity from communicable disease, inconsistent livelihood income (reflected in poor diet diversity) and poor infant young child feeding behaviour are all contributing factors. IYCF practices for children aged 6-23 months remain sub- optimal across the Country, with less than 50% of caretakers currently implementing the recommended IYCF practices. SMART surveys conducted by IMC in Jowhar and Abudwak districts in July 2018 found low coverage of deworming, vitamin A, iron multiple micro-nutrient supplementation in both districts with moderate coverage of measles and polio. More than three quarters of the children do not meeting the minimum acceptable diet i.e. 82.5% and 96.3% in Jowhar and Abudwaq districts respectively. Poor water and sanitation facilities and poor storage of complementary foods exacerbates the situation due to resultant diarrhoeal disease. A 2018 barrier analysis by IMC also confirmed poor health seeking behaviour among caregivers of children aged 0-24 months.IMC programme data shows high prevalence of anaemia among PLW which confirms nutrient insufficiency in diet, even in the absence of drought conditions.

Activities

1. Conduct Individual and Group IYCF-E counselling among PLW and caregivers of children aged 2 years and below2. Scale up community based malnutrition detection and identification of acute malnutrition through FHWs and Mother MUAC.3. Provide lifesaving therapeutic treatment for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) 4. Provide Deworming, Multiple micronutrient and Vitamin A supplementation as part of Outpatient management of acute malnutrition.5. Establish community based Mother-to-Mother support groups for PLW.6. Intensify breastfeeding counseling and re-lactation in stabilization centers for acutely malnourished children under 6 months of Age. 7. Conduct community mobilization activities for screening and referral of malnourished children. 8. Capacity strengthening of health workers (such as midwives), CHWs and FHWs to implement CMAM through classroom and on-job mentoring and refresher trainings.9. Conduct blanket supplementary feeding to prevent deterioration into acute malnutrition or relapse in children discharged from OTP.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

1.Risk: Distance to health facilities Likelihood: High - Impact: HighMitigation measures:- Encourage uptake of mother/caregiver-led MUAC to improve screening and prompt early/timely contact with lifesaving services.- Use of Mobile Health and Nutrition teams providing mobile OTP, deworming and Vitamin A supplementation to eliminate distance as a barrier to access. 2. Risk: Socio-cultural beliefs and practices:Likelihood: Medium/High - Impact: Medium/HighMitigation measures:- Culturally appropriate IEC materials that encourage uptake of positive behaviour.- Ensure caretakers of children aged 0-23 months’ infants and young children and PLW are primary targets of WASH interventions i.e. distribution of specific hygiene kits, water treatment and promotion of nutrition and hygiene messages. 3. Risk: Low literacy Likelihood: High - Impact: MediumMitigation measure:- Provide health and nutrition information through various forms: audio, visual/pictoral and through mass media e.g. radio messages/jingles.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

1. Distance to nutrition servicesLikelihood: High - Impact: HighMitigation measures:- Location of OTP and SC sites within access. - Use of mobile health teams- Ensure safe opening times so that mothers/caregivers attending nutrition programmes do not have to return home in the dark. 2. Maternal ill healthLikelihood: High - Impact: HighMitigation measures:- Incorporate maternal nutrition programming through integrated nutrition programming. - Form community based mother-to-mother support groups (with mothers of different age groups), as a forum for peer led health promotion and nutrition support. 3. Risk: Low uptake of nutrition services by caregiversLikelihood: Medium/High - Impact: HighMitigating measures: - Utilise mobile OTP clinics to eliminate distance as a barrier to access to healthcare. - Provide health information in various community locations (such as markets, water points) and through various forms: audio, visual/pictoral and through mass media eg radio. - Recruit staff representative of the community served, including persons with disability, IDPs and other marginalised groupings. - Form representative community health committees/health centre committees for community oversight over nutrition facilities.- Link mothers/caregivers to local livelihood or cash programmes.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

1. Risk: Community tensionLikelihood: High - Impact: HighMitigation measures: - Consult both displaced and host communities and different clans about nutrition needs and assess whether interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between communities. - Avail mobile health services to ensure to services for conflict displaced populations.- Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on nutrition project.
