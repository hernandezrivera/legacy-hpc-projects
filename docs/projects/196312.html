Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to provide displaced and affected vulnerable host communities with essential Non-Food item and shelter materials to ensure their protection and life with dignity through distribution of basic household and shelter materials as well as build the resilience and preparedness of communities to crisis and shocks. It will be implemented in Kapoita East, Tonj North, and Tonj East where NCA e already on the ground supporting the affected communities. This main objective is consistent with Strategic objective 3 which aims to ensure participation of displaced persons, to enhance the community’s ability to respond to new shocks, build resilience and support existing skills.
The project will target 29,200 (Girls 7615, Women 6158, Boys 8880, and Men 6547) of displaced conflict and flood affected people through provision of shelter and non-food items in a situation of emergency and transition in an ability-challenged accessible, gender-responsive manner.
NCA will also support with distribution of in-kind Shelter and NFI materials and also carry out training to communities and local partners to impart skills on improving accountability, improved feedback systems and also preparedness and response initiatives, we will train community members and committees on assessments and post distribution monitoring and reporting. NCA also consider Cash and vouchers assistance where feasible to provide right holders a more flexible access to much needed NFI and shelter materials according to their priority and need whenever they needed. In-kind distribution only carry out where the market is not functional and couldn’t support the load of the urgent requirement.
The project is 12 months and will be delivered through local partners working closely with local government departments, UN and other international organizations. NCA will ensure coordination and that quality of the response and to ensure that it delivered within agreed timelines.

1 - Needs

According to HPC; In 2022, the precarious situation is expected to be further exacerbated by the country’s increasing humanitarian caseload, the expected increase in the duration of needs in the most affected areas and the projected further reduction in humanitarian assistance across the country. A continuation and increase in sub-national violence reported in many states across the country, including Warrap (particularly Greater Tonj), will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2022. IRNA need assessment report shows that between January and Feburary 2022, large numbers of people (about 5000 people) displaced due to conflict and started to return back to their place called JIE Payam in Kapoita East, which also consequently places NFI and shelter services under severe strain. The cessation of hostilities and prevailing calm that has enabled many families that had been IDPS in Kapoeta North town to return. The small number of operational water points coupled with low capacity for maintaining the existing facilities means that there is overcrowding which further exacerbates the challenges of maintaining social distancing for COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures. Additionally, access to water for basic needs is severely constrained.In Tonj East and Tonj North, the need is for rehabilitation and improvement of shelters for transitional as well as vulnerable host communities for now, However, the need anticipated to be higher due to the frequent flooding of the area during the rainy season which expected to start in two to three months’ time. Currently, conflict and flood affected communities of the counties retuned back to the counties following the peace dialog and agreement between two fighting groups early 2022. However, the situation is still dire based on the NCA assessment that NCA has conducted at the beginning of the Year 2022. According to the assessment, the household economy has been affected due to the lootings during the conflict and have been spend all they have to survive throughout the last rainy season. Now the paliang market (the new location of the market after the total destruction of Romic Market) is functioning with a supply from the Tonj South and Wau Markets following the peace dialog agreement which lead them to access the road to Wau and Tonj South supply markets.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

NCA has conducted market feasibility assessment in Pibor, and Tonj East between n October 2021 and January 2022, respectively.At the design stage, we considered issues of access particularly in wet season where costs of transportation of humanitarian materials becomes difficult due to bad roads. We considered the challenges related to costs for transport as well as costs and risks associated with in kind donations and also its effects and impact on local businesses. We considered the existing markets and their ability to supply some of the goods that we transport from big town e.g., Juba and Wau. We considered the importance of promoting local bussineses, the importance of not disrupting the function of markets by introducing external goods which are already available from the markets, We considered the need for sustainable access to WASH services as well as the need to build resilience of communities and markets as well as issues and factors for safety, market supplies and costs. We considered that markets get disrupted by road access constraint but also considered that local businesses are more resilient and more adaptive to shocks. We considered the impact of the economic crisis and how humanitarian responses can also play a role in supporting markets for delivering goods.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

NCA have been implemented CVA in Gumuruk to distribute Hygiene NFI using Redrose platform last year. The cash was distributed through E-voucher (Plastic card with electronic chips inside) to the targeted vulnerable households that could able them to access the items from the selected local vendors’ shops. the freedom of the beneficiaries to decide on their need what and when to get it. In addition, NCA have witnessed the impact of the intervention as a stimulant of the local economy and functionality of the market due to the new money ejected.The potential problem of price inflation has controlled as NCA will sign an agreement with the selected vendors including the maximum price of each items under the normal circumstances as it is revealed with the market price survey prior to the intervention starts. Again, on our discussions with Women and Girls has shown that there are no barriers to receiving hygiene materials from shops and that cash vouchers are generally safe. The distances and routes for right holders’ access to markets has undergone and In order to ensure a more resilient way to access sanitation materials as well as access to soap and other sanitary products, we have determined that a cash and voucher system is most ideal in this areas. Traders are happy to assure of a steady resupply of goods based on market demand and have a price range that does not fluctuate beyond a certain threshold. In Tonj East, we have similarly carried out an assessment and analysis of needs, including function of markets survey as well as a protection survey with right holders and we have determined that it is possible to provide soap, sanitary materials, hand washing facilities from local traders. The existence of money transfer services and accessibility of the area for monitoring shows that we would provide better services and ensure sustainability of interventions by using cash and vouchers. Overall, we have determined that using cash and vouchers will give right holders better value and choices and that we will be able to deliver our responses more quickly, with better value and more appropriately compared to in-kind assistance.NCA presented the successful CVA project to the national CWG in January 2022. NCA believe the same modality can be scaled up in Tonj East and Tonj North where there is reduced functional market in addition to Gumuruk market that we have operated CVA successfully in 2021.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NCA has a very deliberate policy of community engagement at every stage of our programme cycle and this includes at various stages. For instance, at the inception stage, we will hold community sensitization sessions with community members through a variety of approaches including focussed group discussions with key informants, leaders, religious leaders, representatives of minorities and people living with disabilities, women, youth and other gender groups. This approach will help us to understand the issues and needs and gaps within the community and among the various gender groups. We will use this information to carry out a participatory and focused assessment of needs and to map vulnerability which will help us to design our programme. The information from this process will be fed back to the community and their views incorporated. We also involve and consult community members. When we need volunteers or local staff to work on projects at the community, we as the community members to identify and provider names for volunteers to work on programmes. At the monitoring and evaluation stage, we try to ensure that in all these stages of the programme that all members of the community are consulted to provide input and feedback on how the project components has affected them positively or negatively. Our accountability to affected populations strategy is based on NCAs Complaints response management strategy. NCA system at the community level involves programme staffs and community members having dialogue at the initial stages to identify how community would like to provide feedback or receive it. Community members discuss a range of options that may include email & telephone, complaints register, suggestion box, face to face or community meetings or a mix of these options. We then set up meeting with community members to discuss ways of setting up the system at the community level once the most preferred system is outlined and discussing on how it will operate. In many of the community settings, electronic systems are not possible, and communities often choose face to face meetings and complaints boxes and complaints registers. The practicalities of the system in relation to local set up is discussed. The key issues discussed include what is a valid complaint and how to handle sensitive complaints, what are sensitive and non-sensitive complaints and how they are handled. The complaints and feedback mechanism is incorporated into the NCA monitoring framework. Issues Once system has been discussed and developed, NCA, Partners and the community agree on a monitoring framework for AAP and then integrated to the NCA CRM. All complaints (sensitive and non-sensitive are registered and forwarded to the NCA focal team on CRM. This teams ensure that each and every complaint is action on, addressed and feedback provided based on agreed feedback mechanisms. To receive complaints, the community will appoint a focal person to register the complaint. We use these engagements to also provide feedback and receive back from community and we use this information to address concerns but also to ensure that the community is at the centre of our response actions. Normal community meetings are also undertaken to engage community members during normal course of programme implementation. This helps to improve our engagement with community. Data collected during monitoring and evaluation sessions is also shared with community members, leaders and stakeholders as part of feedback and to also see how their input was reflected. Information we collect from our monitoring evaluation is analysed to inform us on whether we are on track and if needed to carry out adjustments and for learning. We also use evaluation feedback for learning

Mechanisms have been established for whistle blowing as well as for Complaints response mechanism which will help us to monitor and act on any breaches. 8.11. Mitigation Measures.

In our GBV programming, NCA has established and mainstreamed referral pathways with its partners and sub cluster members and enacted mechanisms to creates awareness to beneficiaries about the available referral services. Through safe, confidential, and accessible referral systems, NCA has been able to refer survivors of GBV and other vulnerable population groups to service providers for life-saving services like clinical management of rape and safe maternal care services. NCA has trained its staff on data safety and security as well as confidentiality. All survivor’s information that is in hard copy is kept in files and locked up in a cabinet. Survivor’s personal information is entered using codes with no disclosure of names. Additionally, all staff including implementing partners’ case workers and community mobilizers are trained on case management and GBV case management operating procedures, including the GBV guidelines for case management adopted from IASC and UNFPA GBV guidelines for case management tool kits. 8.5. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. NCA has a very deliberate policy of community engagement at every stage of our programme cycle ad this includes at the inception stage, we plan to hold a community sensitization session with community members through a focussed group discussion as well as key informant interviews with key leaders, key informants, women’s groups, and the various gender groups including people with disabilities, elderly and minority groups. This is in order to help us understand the issues and needs and gaps within the community and among the various gender groups. We will use this information to carry out a participatory assessment of needs and to map vulnerability which will help us to design our programme, the information from this process will be fed back to the community and their views incorporated. We will also involve and consult all community members on their specific and individual needs, including minority groups to ensure that no one is left behind. We have a policy for programming which is called TOFI (Together for Inclusion) We use this policy to ensure that all groups are heard and involved before, during and after the implementation of our programmes. We carry out due diligence on issues of inclusion to ensure that the voices of minorities are heard, and their specific needs mapped, and initiatives put in place to ensure that they are attended to. When we assist displaced and vulnerable populations with shelter support, we aim to create awareness amongst key stakeholders and community members about the connection between land rights and shelter and we aim to mainstream basic land rights and ensure verification of land rights of all right holders of shelter support to ensure that their rights to the land they occupy are approved by the relevant organs (community or Government) to mitigate risks of evictions. NCA and partners work closely within the Shelter and NFI cluster, Government, and local authorities in order to share and access of information regarding land rights’ issues of hosts and other right holders as part of due diligence processes in HLP programming. In NCA programming, we have integrated and adapted the HLP guidelines for shelter and NFI cluster on mainstreaming due diligence procedures before implementation of Shelter programmes top mitigate the risk of evictions or demolitions of households and shelters of right holders as well as to ensure that that shelter programmes do not result or contribute or exacerbate tensions on land and or on properties between different groups.
