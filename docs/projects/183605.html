Project Summary

Arab Ar-Rashayida is a village located in the eastern Bethlehem governorate and away from the center of Bethlehem city about 20 km.
The population is approximately 2,500 capita (1140 under 18 years old, 53 over 65 years old, 1271men and 1223 women) distributed on housing within the community as well as in the surrounding areas as livestock breeders residing in the eastern slopes where they live in permanent homes (not tents), this will ensure sustainability and the maximum benefit of the water network services. They own over 25,000 sheep and about 600 camels as well as several chicken farms (the largest number of sheep in the governorate).
The project will aim to provide Ar-Rashayida village the access to safe and affordable drinking water and to end the suffering of the Bedouins by ensuring proper water network services, through the renewal of the available water network (As water is available and available, but the townspeople lack access to it due to the damage of the current network, which was established in 1980. to serve the Israeli army camps) and linking it with a new water source which is provided by the Palestinian Water Authority(PWA) in a way that ensures the improvement of social, economic and health conditions in the targeted community.
This will be done through the rehabilitation of the existing water network by installing 5800 meters of pipelines of different diameters, and through the extension of an additional 1200 meters of the network pipelines in order to serve the whole targeted community.
The Bedouins of Arab Ar-Rashayida are the most vulnerable and marginalized communities in the governorate according to the latest statistics by PCSB, which are represented by harsh and unstable conditions, which put more burdens on the community in general and on women specifically. Women play vital roles in dealing with WASH issues in the households traditionally, they are usually responsible for providing the drinking water to their houses from different water sources which are sometimes far away and dangerous due to the risks of settler attacks or wild animals.
Recently, increased numbers of Kidney disease cases were documented, this is mainly due to the contaminated water caused by the damages in the existing distribution network, this raised the need for renewing the existing water network in addition to the high percentage of water loss and the high rate of rust to the highest level.
The Council is also forced to close the main water valves to reduce the amount of lost water that reaches more than 50%. Because the water supply service is not available through the water networks, the village council is unable to collect water bills from the local community and this puts a financial burden on him

1 - Needs

AAA has conducted a comprehensive needs assessment for the community with participating representatives of the village council, youth, and the women's center, the result revealed that Ar- Rashaiyda suffers from a significant shortage of infrastructure and basic services and it needs rehabilitation and installation works. AAA found that the core need of the targeted community is the provision of safe, affordable, accessible, and good quality drinking water implemented to serve the community in a sufficient and sustainable way. Ar-Rashaiyda village is provided with water by the West Bank Water Department, through the public water network established in 1980. About 67.6 percent of the housing units are connected to the water network, 31.5 percent are dependent on the purchase of water tanks, and 0.9 percent are dependent on rainwater harvesting cisterns. The quantity of water supplied to Ar-Rashaiyda in 2008 was about 0.0297 million cubic meters/year therefore, the estimated rate of water supply per capita is about 80 liters/day. Here it should be noted that many of Ar-Rashaiyda's citizens do not, in fact, consume this amount of water due to water losses, which are about 50 percent. The losses happen at the main source, major transport lines, distribution network, and at the household level (Ar-Rashaiyda village council), thus the rate of water consumption per capita in Ar-Rashaiyda is 45 liters per day. This is a low rate compared with the minimum quantity proposed by the World Health Organization, which is 120 liters per capita per day. The Ar-rashaiyda women suffer from the lack of water quantity and water quality for their household. This effect directly their cleanliness, health, and the health of their babies and children as the lack of quantity and pad quality of water increase the diseases and infections, and women should take care of that and they should spend more time, more effort, and money to bring more water with acceptable quality to manage their household. Do not forget also that women are responsible for livestock managing at the farm (feeding, milking, and cleaning) and this needs a good quality and enough quantity of water, and women have to manage to bring water to implement her jobs at farm. All of these obligations and responsibilities affect the role of women and the adolescent girl and her social life in Rashaida, especially female students, as they may leave school because of obligations and duties and there are many cases of school dropouts that have been recorded over the last years. As a tool to indicate the effect of the responsibilities, the number of first-grade students is 110, and the number of twelfth-grade students is only 15, both females and males.Providing water in an acceptable manner in an appropriate quantity reduces the risks that women face when they check the water at night or when they extend temporary connections to provide water to the house and raise the welfare of society and women in particular, as they will get rid of the burdens of transporting water, and the health level will improve due to High level of hygiene and sterilization. Water and sanitation are a pressing need for the community, hence AAA proposes to renew the water network and expand it to cover more areas and reach more people, in particular, that the PWA showed interest to connect the community with an additional good source of water if the water network is rehabilitated.

Monitoring and evaluation: • A baseline survey will be conducted at the initial phase of the project, which will be updated until the final evaluation. Information will be collected through semi-structured interviews, household visits, and field observations. Regular monitoring will allow AAA to document the impact of the activities, document challenges and constraints on a monthly basis, and suggest the right solutions. Attention will be paid to the quality of implementation and will ensure close liaison with PWA as well as Arab Ar-Rashayida council, community leaders, and local protection committees. AAA will consult prior to the implementation with the protection cluster and NRC to ensure legal advice, second legal opinion will be consulted to ensure minimum risks on the implementation is likely to happen. Accountability will be ensured through ongoing follow up by AAA with field teams and beneficiaries. The expected indicators and targets of the project are: - A total of 2000 direct (912 under 18 years old, 37 over 65 years old, 513 men and 491 women) beneficiaries will benefit from enhanced access to drinking clear water. ِIncreasing of 20% of the population (men and women) who have access to water. - Expenditure on drinking water will be decreased by 70%. - Reduced time and effort required by women and men by 50%. - Reduced the lost water in the pipeline to 50%. - Decrease the community municipality water bill deficit by 40%. - Gender aspects in the project: The identified indicators will be disaggregated by gender to ensure inclusion of women, and other groups as well as involve men, girls, and girls at schools, youth, PwDs and elderly. Women and girls have considerable responsibilities for water bringing and use at home, as well as ensure adequate hygiene is maintained for children. The intervention will ensure this role is respected and enhanced by reducing the pressure on women in the physical labor of bringing water on a daily basis. The implemented activities will take into consideration the needs of diverse groups of women and girls such as the hygienic aspects that comes more essential with better availability of drinking water that is close to the community, and that bringing water will be of much less burden on women who usually do this responsibility of work. Reports gathered on women's roles will be capitalized and used in AAA women empowerment as well as used to support local AAA initiatives on policy change towards marginalized areas. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

This project will help in mitigating the violations affecting human rights by providing people with their right to get clean and affordable drinking water. Overall AAA aims to take a high level of humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. Indeed, this project will implement 7000 meters in the densest sites affected by the water crisis, through the committee set up to design the project after ensuring that the proposed pipelines will meet the urgent need to stop the deterioration water crisis in the region. Moreover, 85% of the implementation place is somewhat far from direct contact with the occupation forces because it will be between the neighborhoods, and therefore there is no risk or damage to the population or project during the implementation period. In determining the project's activities and specifications, future development was taken into consideration so as to ensure the continuity of the supply of healthy water in the appropriate quantity and quality in case of extension. AAA will conduct a risk analysis for proposed activity in the targeted location to identify potential risks and propose solves like a community legal status: to assess if there are military orders or other legal concerns and to ensure that the proposed intervention will not trigger the community legal case in a harmful way. By including different stakeholders in the community and other actors from outside the community, including population and owners, AAA believes they have taken concrete steps in considering Do No Harm policy. AAA will keep an eye on any new issues in the area to avoid any negative impact as a result of the project activities. AAA will hold a monthly meeting with the stakeholders ensuring a guaranteed complaint mechanism in order to raise the voice of the most vulnerable groups of the local community especially women, children, and people with disabilities. Moreover, a suggestion box will be placed in the village council to receive complaints, suggestions, and any notes regarding the implementation and selection of beneficiaries
