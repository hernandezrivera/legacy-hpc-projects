1. - Projects activities

According to the Somalia Drought Impact Needs Assessment (DINA) drought has increased conflict over natural resources and pastureland in Somalia, and armed conflict and instability have weakened already vulnerable agricultural and pastoral livelihoods, increasing displacement. Drought conditions in northern and central Somalia in early 2021 resulted in some livestock losses and increased household spending on animal feed and water. Additionally, dry conditions and an early end to the Gu rainfall season resulted in crop losses and decreased agricultural labour income. Due to the existing and forecast shortages of pasture and water, as well as the low number of livestock conceptions during the 2021-year, livestock health, births, and milk production is expected to stay below normal. The impact of two consecutive years of below-average rainfall on agricultural and livestock output in late 2020 and early 2021 have resulted in a significant increase in Somalia's food insecure population, particularly in rural areas. Drought conditions are projected for 2022, owing to long-term projections indicating a third straight season of below-average rainfall in late 2021, resulting in a three-season droughtIn pastoral areas, including Sol which this action is targeting, large-scale livestock migration was reported from rain-deficit areas to areas that received relatively better rainfall. In many pastoral livelihood zones, poor households with will face moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021 due to below average or poor milk availability, a limited number of saleable animals. Consequently, we will target 820 households, translating to 4920 people over 7 months, and will provide life saving emergency support in the form of unconditional cash transfers in Sool Region, Laas Canood. ActionAid will target persons with disabilities, women and child headed households, youth, people in need i rural settings, elderly, school aged children and chronically ill to enhance protection. The UCT modality will use mobile money which will also serve as a social protection measure by protecting beneficiaries from shocks, reduce the use of negative coping strategies such as selling of productive asset with impact on longer-term livelihood sustainability. As recommended by the National Cash Working Group (NCWG) the action will give a Multi-purpose Transfer that is specific to selected people in Laas Canood, which is pegged at USD70 per household. Cash-based assistance gives beneficiaries the ability to shop in local markets and buy food that is culturally familiar

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The assessments and needs assessments from ActionAid, Humanitarian Needs Overview and WFP indicate that in some of the regions targeted by this action, cash will be sufficiently absorbed in the markets, and that the requisite nutritional commodities needed in the household are available, and local markets have sufficient food and people cannot afford it The rapid needs assessment undertaken by ActionAid during project development and design also observed that the markets are adequately stocked with various food items like vegetables, flour, rice, milk, oil and pulses. The cash transfer is denominated in US dollar to hedge against the risk of inflation. The assessments also show that food needs are so severe people will spend most new income on food, especially in conflict areas which creates internal displacement, reduces food access and disrupts livelihoods. In response, the project will provide cash transfers, among other modalities, to internally displaced persons and host communities. Cash-based assistance gives beneficiaries the ability to shop in local markets and buy food that is culturally familiar.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

A needs assessment was undertaken by ActionAid, which included market assessment to determine the functionality of food market systems (for maize, rice, pulses, and cooking oil) in the targeted regions for this project. The project also used secondary data from assessments done by the FSNAU in the country. The market assessment identified whether local markets have the ability to effectively respond to increased consumer demand by verifying adequate food supply sources/levels and that the likelihood that food prices will remain stable in the short and long term. Specific objectives of the assessment included the following: • Market structure - Identifying the key actors and institutions as well as assessing the supply chain for cereals (maize grain, maize meal, and rice), pulses (sugar beans) and vegetable oil • Availability of food items• Analysis of current and projected availability of cereals, pulses, and cooking oil in local markets across the targeted communities• Established how well the source and supply markets are linked Market patterns • Analysis of volumes stored and traded, price levels and trends, price setting behavior, competition, and seasonality • Capacity to meet consumer demand • Analysis of the market’s potential to respond to current and transfer-induced increases in consumer demand, e.g., through storage facilities, stocking levels, stock replenishment lead-time, etc. • Use of markets

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

ActionAid has consulted with the Lasanood district councils and regional authority on the priority needs of the communities to design the project interventions. The diverse social groups in the village have been consulted about the top priority needs. The collective responses from the consultation meetings have informed the activities in the proposed project. Once the project is approved, ActionAid will share the details with the district councils, regional and national authorities for endorsement and technical inputs. These authorities will receive periodic project updates and will witness the progress in the field. ActionAid will liaise with other humanitarian actors in the area for sharing plans and common approaches. At the site level, ActionAid will share the project details such as number of beneficiaries, duration, and activities with large gatherings at each project site in which all social groups are represented. ActionAid will organize meetings with key community representatives (women groups, elders, community committees) of the targeted communities and explain the planned response project activities (Project design, implementation, targeting criteria, registration, duration etc.) and collect their views and concerns. In addition, the project will also carry out focus group discussions (FGDs) with different groups of people in the targeted communities to ensure that the unique needs and voices of the key segments of the community, especially the most vulnerable, are heard and considered. The FGDs will specifically focus on the existing and potential protection risks associated with the project. Community committee and women groups will be responsible for the beneficiary selection/verification on pre-agreed criteria. Once the community committees submit the confirmed lists, ActionAid team will read the names in public gatherings for validation. The village committees and women groups will liaise and assist project team in registration, conflict resolution and card top ups. In addition, the community institutions will be responsible for safe custody of the security of the project team, equipment, and visitors.
