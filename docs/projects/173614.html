Project Summary

This project is written to address the water, sanitation and hygiene needs of people (men, women, girls and boys) affected by the socio-political crisis in the Northwest and Southwest regions. It will contribute to improving access to safe drinking water for vulnerable populations in the peri urban areas, particularly women and girls who collect water within households and are the main users and managers; At the same time, this project will improve women's, girls', men's and boys' access to basic sanitation facilities, thereby reducing the rate of open defecation and consequently waterborne diseases, particularly among children under 5 years of age. This project will also seek to improve good hygiene practices in the affected communities especially areas with the high risk of contracting cholera..

1 - Besoins / Needs

Armed conflict between non-state armed groups (NSAGs) and the military in North West (NW) and South West (SW) Cameroon has resulted in a severe humanitarian crisis. The so-called Anglophone Crisis has been ongoing since October 2017, when NSAGs began fighting for separation of the predominantly English-speaking NWSW regions from the rest of Francophone Cameroon. The crisis has affected three million people (a significant increase compared to 1.3 million reported in need in early 2019) , with less than 50% targeted for assistance. This includes 679,000 IDPs who have been displaced due to the conflict , estimated to include 60% women and girls. The security situation continues to deteriorate in both regions, especially in the NW where attacks have intensified, and targeted lockdowns have recently been extended from one day per week to three days. Heavy armed confrontations between the military and NSAGs, military raids, indiscriminate killings and burning of villages lead to further displacements and put civilians at grave risk.The degradation of security situation and at times natural disasters like floods and landslides continue to limit the access to safe drinking water and basic sanitation for population affected by the crisis. An assessment conducted in the zone reveal that in 71% of assessed village, population need more than 30 minutes for a round trip to fetch water. 53.4% of assessed village collect drinking water from unimproved water sources. The most affected divisions in the North West are Menchum (85.6%), Ngo Ketunjia (68.8%), Donga Mantung (68.3%) and Momo (56.1%); Manyu (68.1%), Lebialem (68.1%) and Ndian (56.9%) are the most affected divisions in the south West region. Main challenges related to Water access include insufficient of water points reported in the 46% of villages assessed. Also, 41% and 38% of the assessed villages reported a lack of water containers and distance to water points, respectively, as other challenges they face. Concerning sanitation, the Menchum division in the North West is the most affected with almost 27.9% of population practicing open defecation (OD). As far as access to basic sanitation is concerned, pits without slabs, reported by 63.2% of the assessed villages, are the main sanitation facilities used by affected population, making it difficult to maintain them. The use of plastic bag is also reported and represent an inadequate copying mechanism to open defecation. This situation is similar in communities hosting NWSW displaced population in the neighborhood regions (Littoral and West). With regard to Hand washing facilities, 24% of the assessed villages reported that almost no one has access to HWF. The situation is not good considering the presence of COVID-19 pandemic and frequency of cholera outbreaks in the South West region. Menchum (61.2%) and Momo (43.9%) are the most affected divisions in the North-West and South-West regions. Given the prevailing poor hygiene practices and lack of access to basic WASH services in targeted areas, there is an elevated risk of spread of COVID-19 and water-borne diseases. An outbreak of cholera was recently reported in September 2020 in Buea Health District, in Fako (SW). Affected communities, especially IDPs, have increased risk of infection due to inadequate access to soap and water for proper handwashing or alternatives like hand sanitizer and personal protective equipment (PPE) such as masks. There is an urgent need to increase awareness about self-protection against COVID-19 including sensitization to tackle misinformation about the virus, such as that COVID-19 is a myth. Plan’s Rapid Gender Analysis in NW found that girls specifically requested access to dignity kits including MHM materials and information. In the north-west and south-west regions, internally displaced populations living in the bush are far from basic drinking water and sanitation infrastructure.
