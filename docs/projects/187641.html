Project Summary

The project is designed to reduce shelter vulnerabilities of host communities, returnees, and IDPs for both insides of camps by restoring basic infrastructure to mitigate the risks of floods, replacing old tents and delivering alternative transitional shelters, and then outside of camps by rehabilitation of damaged houses. On the other hand, the project aims to fulfil winter needs of targeted population through fuel support, as well as target most in need HHs with NFI kits according to cluster criteria of targeting. The main achievement for the proposed interventions is to enhance the situation for the current shelter in order to improve the living conditions for those IDP’s who are living in the informal settlements, self-settled sites, and managed camps in order to avoid the negative effect of the weather actuations along the different seasons which might lead to food and losing their shelters, in addition, to enhance the targeted population’s resilience against weather conditions by conducting the following activities:
a. Provision of core NFIs.
b. Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs.
c. Installation of new/ replacement of existing tents.
d. Installation and/ or construction of temporary transitional shelters.
e. Implementation of HH level improvements (tent graveling)
f. Implementation of site level improvements/ infrastructure
g. Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished housing

4 - Needs

Based on HNO/21 findings, SNFI needs in Syria are widespread and acute. A significant portion of the population continues to experience protracted displacement. In addition, returns have begun to some areas, and new IDPs swell already overburdened communities, especially in North-east and North-west Syria.Shelter needs are concentrated in Aleppo, Idleb, Ar-Raqqa, Rural Damascus Governorates, which are overburdened as they host more IDPs than resident populations with their limited shelter and hosting capacity, in addition to the displaced, nearly 25% of HC and 50% of returnees are living in damaged and inadequate shelters where repair/rehabilitation is needed alongside a multisectoral response due to lack of services, and camps in the north-east and north-west where at least 1.95 M IDPs are residing in last-resort informal and spontaneous settlements which suffer from lack crucial infrastructure and regularly experience severe flooding while all emergency or improvised shelter require regular replacement.Access to NFIs is seen as lifesaving, especially during winter when vulnerable families are unable to access basic NFI items to keep them warm, such as heating fuel, blankets and winter clothes. The overall NFI PiN has increased by 37% during 2021. Of this, 2.70 million people 57% are residing in areas of severity three or higher. According to the latest assessment, 90% of families report diminished access to NFI’s with unaffordability being the main constraint.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ATAA PSEA policy was updated in December 2021 and shared with donors, and set a dedicated PSEA committee including (PSEA focal point, HR focal point, MEAL focal point, Grants and partnership focal point).ATAA adopted a core concept in all intervention to make PSEA and CSG as mandatory in all projects and activities, in this regard, all ATAA staff, volunteers and contractors will got orientation sessions about PSEA and CSG policies and sign both PSEA and CSG code of conducts.All project locations will include banners and complaint boxes with delivering messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels in addition to stand alone system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case starting from receiving the report, to investigation process, and provide aid and support to the survivor till closure of the case after achieving the objectives of investigation.ATAA is PSEA network member participate in all regular network meetings, in addition to submit monthly reports to the network, in addition to conduct training for all staff about PSEA and target humanitarian workers in the intervention location to support the role of PSEA network.Gender balance will be a core point in the project within all teams (programs, media, MEAL) and locations will be accessed by women and girls safely.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and Mitigations: 1- Overlap, lose some locations due to other NGOs intervention, loss of access. - will perform multilevel coordination (Cluster, Field) -coordinated with all the stakeholder during the design phase 2- Risks related to HLP and propriety conflicts - Ensure validation of the documents during the project - communicate clearly with all the stakeholders - Involved the camps management committees, LCs, KIs in the process in case of conflict 3- Due of the conditions on the ground that may force some beneficiaries to displace. - ATAA will monitor all updates on the ground if any of the beneficiaries displaced, we will choose alternative beneficiaries who meet the criteria in the same location. 4: Increased # of COVID cases - Train ATAA teams about best practices including social distance, sterilization and wearing face masks. - Providing teams with PPE kits - Printing awareness posters on food baskets about COVID-19 and best healthy practices to avoid it. - ATAA distribution teams will organize beneficiaries and make social distance between them during distribution. 5- SEA of affected community members by anyone associated with the provision of aid. This risk will be mitigated by strict measures and the awareness of the field staff on this topic and by signing codes of conduct of all staff relating to PSEA, and reporting via established reporting mechanisms of any concerns or suspicions regarding SEA, and investigation if necessary (hotline).
