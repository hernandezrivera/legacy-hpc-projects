Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to the revitalization of conflict-affected communities through integrated psychosocial support for IDPs, returnees and other community members and social cohesion activities aimed at increasing integration and further reconciliation, with particular attention to the needs of most vulnerable groups– women, youth, IDPs, national minorities, ethnic groups, elderly and persons with disabilities.

Needs

The ongoing conflict in eastern Ukraine continues to generate significant displacement of Ukrainians throughout the country. According to the Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine as of October 10, 2018, 1,521,542 internally displaced persons (IDPs) from Donbas and Crimea were registered. The majority of these IDPs are registered in Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts. The high number of IDPs in these two oblasts places a significant strain on various public services and increases competition over already limited employment opportunities, leading to increased tensions between IDPs and members of the host community. According the latest National Monitoring System Report on the Situation of Internally Displaced Persons, June 2018, the share of the women in surveyed IDP household was 57% that is slightly higher than proportion of women in an average Ukrainian household (54%). Among these 57% women, 19% were women aged over 60 years.The larger share of women among IDPs was observed in all age groups 18 years and older;According to the HelpAge findings, presented in September 2018, 48,9% of older people report they are feeling isolated. Out of 84,3% respondents who mentioned that they have experiences changes in sleeping patterns, 76,8% were women aged over 60. Among the 51.1% of elderly that have reported having crying spells, 85% are women. Seventy-one (71%) of older residents along the contact line have persistent memories associated with the conflict;Conflict-affected populations, and especially IDPs, having been uprooted from their places of origin, struggle to rebuild their lives in new communities. Fear for family and friends remaining in the non-government controlled areas (NGCA) as well as uncertainty over their future has an immense impact on the wellbeing of conflict affected individuals. Addressing immediate psychosocial (PSS) needs of GBV survivors, conflict-affected children and their parents, and other members of vulnerable population therefore must be a priority to improve wellbeing and reduce long term psychological impacts from the conflict. The events that force people to escape hometowns are so far beyond the normal life conditions and therefore undoubtedly stressful for anyone (whether the displacement was caused by just anxiety or by real life threat during the hostilities). It should be also taken into account that as a rule, the IDPs undergo stressful conditions at least three times: prior to displacement, during and after it. The aim of the PSS is to reduce the negative impact and to help finding the balance and feeling of security after resettlement.According to IMC MHPSS Assessment conducted in 2018, there is a gap in MHPSS support for working-age people. Nevertheless, psychological issues have been reported among all age groups, including children, the elderly and adults;According to the National Monitoring System Report on the Situation of Internally Displaced Persons, June 2018, only 41% of IDP respondents residing in Donetsk and Luhansk regions self-assessed their level of integration as fully integrated into the local community.The conflict continues to disrupt community relations and social bonds due to opposing political views of the ongoing situation in addition to the strain on local resources and infrastructure as noted above. Certain lack of services to support psychological wellbeing and increase individual’s ability to cope and recover from the traumatic events is observed. The World Bank Report points out that 16% of IDPs identify the lack of psychological support as their main concern. Along with addressing the economic and financial difficulties both locals and IDPs are faced with, tackling psychosocial problems while simultaneously sustaining social activities is crucial. IDPs and the local population in conflict-affected areas are facing profound divisions that the conflict has created within their communities and which often generate further distrust and discrimination.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome: Conflict affected community members have increased access to PSS services available in their community to mitigate negative effects produced by the conflict Output 1: Psycho social support and social cohesion resources and needs of IDPs, returnees and vulnerable groups are assessed - Activities for output 1: Identify local civil based organizations (CBOs) or community initiative groups (CIGs) through a competitive process, where applicants are requested to submit analysis of local PSS and social cohesion needs, along with proposals for related initiatives to be implemented; Identify existing psychosocial support resource persons (e.g. community leaders, local social service workers, psychologists etc.) through a mapping exercise in targeted locations; Coordinate planned and existing intervention in the area with the Protection Cluster and Mental Health and Psychosocial Sub Cluster partners, as well as within the United Nations Country Team, Humanitarian Country Team and United Nations Eastern Team. Output 2: Psychosocial support and community level referral system is established and operational in targeted communities - Activities for output 2: Establish PSS centres in the communities where they are not available in coordination with municipal authorities. Newly created PSS centres will be set in the premises of the social infrastructure objects and will be a space for the provision of individual and group counselling sessions; Conduct peer to peer psychosocial support training to increase their awareness of the effects of psychological trauma, symptoms, measures individuals can take to reduce the impact, support networks for members of selected CBOs and CIGs in targeted communities and create PSS teams ; Conduct advanced PSS training and refresher trainings for identified local psycho social specialists and implement focused support activities and referrals in cooperation with international experts. To improve skills and abilities of the PSS specialists to support the conflict-affected population to address the psychological impact of the ongoing conflict the following topics will be covered during the training: 1) tools for screening and detection of stress and post traumatic symptoms; 2) coping strategies, techniques and actions to build resilience; 3) best practices of PSS interventions for the IDPs and conflict-affected populations; 4) case management and supervisory support; Identify and refer vulnerable cases to the existing services. Moreover, an online coordination platform to share opinions, materials and techniques among the PSS specialist will be created within the project. During the project implementation, IOM directly and through the selected implemented partner will provide mentoring and supervision support to the PSS specialist. Regular filed monitoring visits to supervise the work of PSS specialists and individual and group supervisions by Skype and phone will be conducted. The regular supervision support will provide the possibility to receive practical and personal in–time support and expert consultations during PSS specialist daily work with vulnerable persons. Output 3: Conflict affected communities are aware about the importance of psychosocial support (PSS) after traumatic experiences through implementation of community development initiatives - Activities for output 3: Open a call among the targeted communities to apply with community-led development initiatives to share the information about PSS related issues; Select community level development initiatives through the competitive selection process; Monitor the implementation of community led-initiatives. This component of the project will focus on supporting community level events targeting the sharing information about the importance of psychosocial support (PSS) after traumatic experiences such as conflict and/or displacement, as well as access to information about how such services can be accessed within the particular community.

2) # of local counterparts involved in target areas (Target: 50 counterparts); 3) # of CBOs identified for PSS trainings, activities and social cohesion initiatives in target areas (Target: 20 CBOs); 4) # of PSS resource persons identified for focused support and referrals in target areas during the project start up phase (Target: 40 resource persons); 5) # of secondary and tertiary care centres mapping exercises conducted in targeted areas during the project start up phase (Target: 20 exercises); 6) # of secondary and tertiary care centres identified in target areas during the project start up phase (Target: 50 centers); Output 2: Psychosocial support and community level referral system is established in targeted communities and operational: 1) # of PSS centers established in coordination with local counterparts (Target: 20 centers); 2) # of peer psychosocial support trainings organized in target areas (Target: 20 trainings); 3) # of selected CBO members receiving peer psychosocial support training (Target: CBO 200 members); 4) # of advanced PSS trainings for identified local psychologists organized in target areas (Target: 5 trainings); 5) # of selected psychologists receiving advanced PSS training (Target: 40 resource persons); 6) # of individuals benefitting from group PSS in target areas as administered by the CBO members (Target: 20,000 individuals, of whom 12,000 women and 8,000 men); 7) # of individuals benefitting from individual counselling sessions as administered by trained psychologists (Target: 1,000 individuals, of whom 600 women and 400 men); 8)# of individuals referred to secondary and tertiary care centres (Target: 588 individuals, of whom 353 women and 235 men); Output 3: Conflict affected communities are aware about the importance of psychosocial support (PSS) after traumatic experiences through implementation of community development initiatives of community development initiatives: 1) # of grants assigned to conduct activities listed in the selected community-led development initiatives (Target: 20 grants); 2) # of beneficiaries of the activities aimed to spread information about the importance of PSS (Target: beneficiaries 20,000, of whom 12,000 women and 8,000 men). IOM actively seeks for the views of affected population to improve the project implementation and to identify the existing gaps that should immediately addressed. For the urgent response to the complains and suggestion on how to improve project implementation, feedback mechanism thought the Internet and telephone will be established during the project implementation. IOM will regularly provide information to beneficiaries how the provided inputs were used. Beneficiaries of the current project will be provided with the phone numbers and links to leave their comments on project activities. IOM staff that works with feedback mechanism will be trained how complaints and response mechanism works. IOM will conduct regular monitoring and evaluation activities in all project locations to assess progress against set project indicators, to collect sex and age disaggregated data and to allow for necessary adjustments that might arise as lessons learnt. Phone surveys, individual interviews and monitoring on-sight visits will be carried out by IOM to collect both quantitative and qualitative data to assess the level of satisfaction and how beneficiaries are impacted by project activities. Drafting of regular and ad hoc progress reports, infographics, briefs and other documents for the donors and the broader humanitarian community, as well as a final evaluation report meant to assess performance and inform future similar programming will also be carried out.
