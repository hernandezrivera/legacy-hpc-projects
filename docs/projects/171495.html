Project Summary

The targeted areas are herding and Bedouin communities in the Jordan Valley and East Jerusalem. The project target 31 locations, 13 Bedouin and herding communities, located in Area C in Tubas, 1 in Jericho and 17 in Jerusalem. The locations are( Jerusalem: Az Zaayyem, Az Za'ayyem Bedouins, Beit Iksa, Beit Iksa Bedouins, Beit Iksa Bedouins1, Kasarat, Khan al Ahmar - Ab al Helw, Khan al Ahmar - Abu Fellah, Khan al Ahmar - Kurshan, Khan Al Ahmar - Makab as Samen, Khan Al Ahmar - Wadi As Sider, Khan al Ahmar Mihtawish, Nkheila1, Nkheila2, Wadi al Awaj, Wadi Sneysel, Zaatreh Az Zaim Bedouins, Tubas: Al Deir, Al Farisiya-al Zubi, Al Farisiya-Ihmayyer, Al Farisiya-Khallet Khader, Al Farisiya-Naba al Ghazal, Al Hadidiya,
Hammamat al Maleh, Hammamat al Maleh-al Meiteh, Ibziq , Khirbet ar Ras al Ahmar, Khirbet Samra, Khirbet Tell el Himma, Khirbet Yarza, Jericho: Khan al Ahmar - Wadi Abu Sid)
which all suffer from the Israeli occupation annexation policy and the settlements nearby. There are numerous violations of the rights of the local communities in these areas including as in the case of demolitions and based on international humanitarian law, including by reference to the following stipulations: Article 49 against individual or mass forcible transfer and Article 53 of the Fourth Geneva Convention against the destruction of property. Those stipulations prohibit the destruction of civilian property unless necessary by military operations. Destruction of objects essential for the survival of the civilian population is prohibited under any circumstances. Extensive destruction not justified by military necessity and done deliberately is a war crime under Article 147 of the Geneva Convention. As a state party to the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, the GoI is furthermore required to ensure adequate housing without discrimination (Art. 11.1). Further violations include movement and access restrictions Article 25.
As such, the targeted communities suffer from threats of orders of land confiscation, eviction, demolitions, settlers’ attacks against herders and scarcity of water where most is diverted for settlement use forcing farmers to purchase water at high costs threatening the sustainability of their livelihood that is based mostly on agriculture and animal herding.
The conflict situation in the Jordan valley and east Jerusalem areas is characterized not only by spatial domination but can also be measured in socio-economic terms. Due to restrictions like land expropriation, planning restriction, lack of access to the existing infrastructure and unequal access to natural resources, the communities in Area C suffer from unemployment, general marginalization, (forced) migration and, in the vicinity of Israeli settlements and security fence, the risk and occurrence of land confiscation by settlers against people and property.
The target communities are located in Jordan Valley in Tubas and Jericho governorate and Jerusalem governorate. These communities are facing Israeli and settlers’ violations against them and their natural resources (land and water). Geographically, these communities are located on the eastern basin of water (aquifer) in the West Bank, one of the wealthiest water aquifers in OPt, but unfortunately, they can’t use one drop of its freshwater without permission of Israeli authorities. Besides, the Palestinians in the target areas live in very primitive residential shelters such as tents, plastic sheets or metal sheets that are not suitable for human living especially in the summertime and the cold and massive storm raining in the wintertime. Even of all of these dire conditions, the Israeli authorities did not permit them to live in such humane conditions. Also, ICA demolished their shelters many times.
COVID 19 pandemic increase the vulnerability of target areas, especially the need for healthy shelter provide enough ventilation to all target group, especially women, children and elderly.

1 - Needs

In August 2020, communities needs for shelter cluster were updated. In the target areas, there are 572 Bedouin and herding families; among them, there is a crucial needs for shelter rehabilitation to 385 families. The shelter situation in these areas considered terrible. Among the 25 targeted communities, eleven of them live in tents, and seven live in zinc, four in caravans, two in zinc and tents, and one community live in zinc with stones. In the targeted area, there are 25 Bedouin and herding communities; the main source of income is herding the animal. The total population is 2802. In JV, Israeli government establish six outposts that are illegal under International Humanitarian Law IHL. All of the target areas are classified as area C that is mean under full Israeli control. Also in this area, six Israeli military bases confiscated more than 15 thousand dunums of Palestinian lands. According to all of that, the Israeli authorities prohibited Palestinian from building or doing any construction in this area because they claim it state lands or military areas. In 2019, according to UAWC's documentation report of Israeli violations including the settler violence, 1. The Israeli settlers' violence: The settlers in the JV region carried out 180 attacks, ranging from expelling, assaulting herders, and seizing land.2. Israeli army: The army carried out 70 attacks against citizens in the Jordan Valley, which varied between the detention and expulsion of herders, the prevention of citizens from cultivating their lands, the construction of military roads, and the implementation of military exercises with live ammunition and artillery.3. Israeli Civil Administration (total notifications of demolition, stop of work, and evictions 585 orders). From the fore mention numbers, the majority of these incidents happened in Al Maleh Bedouin localities.The results of the need assessment showed that the herders and Bedouins in target areas need residential shelter rehabilitation, including internal thermal insulation and preparing concrete floors and legal awareness. The total residential shelters in the target areas are 572 shelters, 385 shelters the most needed rehabilitation.All activities in this project aim to support immediate response and time sensitive, high impact interventions to prevent and stop protection violations or abuse and or / alleviate needs/ effects of protection violations as much as possible. This Project contributes to the protection of vulnerable Palestinians families in the Jordan valley and east Jerusalem from forcible transfer (FT) through a protection-centered approach to humanitarian responses incorporating emergency support (cash/in-kind); provision of material assistance and essential services; legal aid; advocacy; and community empowerment. 31 Bedouin and herding communities will be specifically targeted for medium-term resilience-building support based on their vulnerability to FT, initially assessed using the OCHA VPP, supplemented with in-depth protection assessments taking into consideration the specific needs of women, men, children, elderly, and people with special needs. Legal aid activities will be implemented across target areas, based on needs and referrals. Within the framework of the HCT, the Project supports the implementation of the Area C Policy Framework and coordinates with other actors meeting humanitarian needs of communities at risk of FT. The Project provides a mechanism to respond to OCHA triggers and ICCG coordination of response to demolitions. Shelter, WASH, Education, Health, FSS, legal responses, settler violence interventions and advocacy will be coordinated through the relevant Clusters/WGs. The Project is built on an integrated protection approach envisaged to contribute to all HRP objectives:• STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 1: The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL), while duty- bearers are increasingly held to account.• STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.• STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 3: The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with, and overcome, a protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.The Project further contributes to the three Shelter Cluster objectives I. Support to the displaced; II. Support to vulnerable households; III. Increased response capacity).The target beneficiaries are 385 families and benefit 1887 person. The beneficiaries distributed among gender as Female: 903, Male: 984, children under 18: 972, adult (18 -59): 944, elderly (above 59): 71.

2 - Activities

The standard of security and protection has been taken into consideration, which is considered through beneficiaries applications and contracting and implementation methodology. Therefore, “do no harm” is inflicted on the target groups or in the sites that have been targeted so that the beneficiaries are not affected by the abuse of their dignity through the method of communication or the method of implementation so that it is ensured that all beneficiaries receive the service they need with respect without affecting them psychologically or socially. The project guarantees equality and integrity among all the beneficiaries, so that the project is announced at the beginning in the locations, through official government bodies and agricultural committees, and accordingly forms are filled out for all beneficiaries and through these forms are analyzed, and standards are developed that are compatible with the needs and in the families that support them. Women and families that among their member's people with special needs or who have been subjected to repeated attacks by the occupation and settlers receive a higher score in the evaluation. On the other hand, the project staff is trained through communication with international institutions and United Nations organizations for training on the PSEA, which is a special program for the beneficiaries, especially women, to not be exposed to sexual harassment and children.The project focuses on rehabilitating 210 shelters in the targeted areas by using thermal insulation from the inside to protect families from the summer heat, winter cold and harsh climatic conditions, as well as providing adequate ventilation for families so that all their members can live inadequate sanitary conditions that meet the health standards of housing according to the standards of the World Health Organization, especially in light of the exceptional circumstances the country is going through in terms of the spread of the COVID 19.The second activity is to rehabilitate the floors, where 175 shelters will be rehabilitated by providing a concrete floor that provides the family with the ability to continuously clean and the ability to provide conditions free from the organisms that cause deadly bites such as snakes and scorpions that spread in the target areas as a result of high temperatures, especially in the summer season as well. Protecting them from floods in the winter that could affect the dwellings and their floors.On the other hand, 50 different training days will be held to provide legal awareness regarding beneficiary families, farmers and livestock breeders in those areas as a result of their continuous exposure to the attacks of the occupation and settlers, as this activity is a significant and essential component of the project so that these beneficiaries and farmers are linked with legal and human rights institutions. Operating in this field will be through a legal referral system.The target beneficiaries are 385 families and benefit 1887 person. The beneficiaries distributed among gender as Female: 903, Male: 984, children under 18: 972, adult (18 -59): 944, elderly (above 59): 71.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

UAWC adopts different mechanisms of stakeholders’ feedback. They are verbal feedback to the project team in the field or over the phone, filling out the Complaint Cards, and finally through the UAWC field monitoring visits by UAWC MEAL Officer. The feedback is treated seriously and confidentially, and then taking the proper action, either preventive or corrective actions. The feedback may be received by direct beneficiaries, official institutions such as local municipalities, or from the out-sourced service providers such as contractors, or other stakeholders. By UAWC experience, the complaining parties contact the coordinator of the field team directly, either by face to face while being in the field or over the phone. In this case, the coordinator takes the right action and may inform/consult with the UAWC higher decision-making level if necessary. Even the stakeholders are informed about the possibility to share their feedback via Complain Card - CC or over the official phone of the institution; complaining parties use the CC in a few cases. On a regular monthly basis, and with attendance of the head of the UAWC Operational & Development Head of Department, MEAL Officer and Program & Project Section Head, UAWC Project Coordinators meet and discuss the project progress and issues including mainly the received complains. The main purpose of this monthly meeting is to share experience on how to react with all project issues and discussing the lessons learned and best practices. On the other hands, and to ensure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with the project deliverable and team (internal or external team), the MEAL officer conducts desk review of the project reports and field visits to meet with a random sample of direct beneficiaries as well as with official institution representatives. Before each field visit, the MEAL Officer customized the M&E form, considering the type of interventions, provided service/product and direct beneficiaries.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Based on Human Right Approach (international human rights law and international humanitarian law), UAWC ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved. In this project, strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities are taken, particularly concerning designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, UAWC analyzes protection risks and possible consequences that may arise and finally are reported to the right concerned agencies.In the field, UAWC conducts regular visits to the communities and beneficiary HHs to ensure that protection concerns are considered throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for vulnerable groups including mainly women, children. UAWC is highly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For that, all staff are made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in UAWC’S Code of Conduct and related to other policies including Child Protection; and others. On the other hand and to avoid negative impacts and maximize the positive impact of the project interventions, UAWC adopts different principles to avoid any attempts of any individuals/groups/political parties to control and use aid resources to support their side. These principles are mainly the transparency, professionalism and considering some cross-cutting issues, mainly gender equality and giving priority for the most vulnerable groups, including small farmers, women-headed families, people with disability. To ensure Do-No-Harm, UAWC involves and consult with different stakeholders in the project design, beneficiary selection criteria, project implementation mechanisms. It should be noted that the UAWC Team is aware of Palestinian context, and interaction between the different intervention and this context, so that avoid negative impacts and maximize positive impacts on the conflict.the UAWC and M/E officer will take on considerations the beneficiaries rights to participate in proper way in all project implementations processes starting from designing the activities till the execution of interventions and evaluation of project outcomes. the project will ensure the equal rights of both Female and Male beneficiaries to participate in project activities and to ensure proper way to deal with them with respect to their dignities' and rights.also, UAWC ensure in all activities that no one women or men to be harmed or abused by any one, and use the PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) standards in all project documents such as agreements with contractors, village councils and inside and between UAWC staff.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Since its establishment, Union of Agricultural Work Committees - UAWC has reliable local agricultural committees in all mostly Palestinian locations, through them, the information on assistance are disseminated. To ensure all targeted beneficiaries, specifically the most vulnerable group such as small farmers, women-headed families, and people with disabilities, are fully informed of the assistance available under the project, UAWC field team ensures that information is provided through accessible channels, formats and languages. In general, UAWC adopts a different way of dissemination about the (Direct beneficiary groups, list of the beneficiary of high priority, and beneficiary selection criteria, etc...). These ways are mainly through UAWC local agricultural committees and their representatives in all Palestinian locations, using announcement board in the local official entities such as the local municipality, the cooperative and local CBO. For the mentioned interventions such, beneficiaries are requested to fill-out applications form that is studied with high transparency by a committee formed by the UAWC team, a representative from the local official entity, local agricultural committee focal point and PA official bodies.To ensure equal and impartial access to aid, UAWC M&E Officer carry out a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process, and all applicants are notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process.
