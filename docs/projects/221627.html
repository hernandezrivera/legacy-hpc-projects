Project Summary

The project represents an initiative aimed at supporting displaced and host populations in the context of rapid and humanitarian WASH provision that saves lifes amidst the conflicts and security crises in Cabo Delgado. The intervention areas (Ibo, Mecufi and Metuge districts) have been among the destinations for internally displaced persons due to violence occurring in districts of Macomia, Mocimboa da praia, as well as some remote communities in Metuge and Mecufi.
The project aims to provide emergency humanitarian assistance, ensuring that affected families have rapid and safe access to potable water, basic sanitation conditions, and good hygiene practices. These actions for displaced and hosts populations will enable the prevention of waterborne diseases and ensure basic health and dignity for these populations, fostering community resilience and strengthening social cohesion in a context of vulnerability.
The project will target displaced communities currently residing in resettlement areas or transit camps in Metuge and Mecufi, as well as host families, specifically in Ibo district, and hosts communities in all intervention districts. And will be prioritized women, youth and persons with disabilities and elderly.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The intervention districts in Cabo Delgado have been receiving a large number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) due to the security crisis in the region. This population increase places pressure on local resources, severely affecting access to potable water, sanitation, and hygiene. In specific cases such as Mecufi and Ibo, where there is a higher occurrence of brackish water and low-yield boreholes, the situation is even more critical. Currently, there are approximately 47,527 displaced persons and 24,706 host community members in need in the three intervention areas. Through this project, Istituto Oikos aims to improve the health and well-being of communities by promoting life-saving interventions and ensuring the survival of families in the water, sanitation and hygiene sectors, reaching about 28% of displaced persons in the three interventions areas.The project integrates data collection to identify beneficiary families for sanitation and hygiene activities, ensuring community engagement and coordination with health authorities to monitor the epidemiological situation, especially during the rainy season. Furthermore, there will be cooperation with provincial and district planning and infrastructure services to strengthen potential synergies in interventions.In the resettlement centers, emergency community latrines will be constructed to enable the transition from using these communal facilities to building individual family latrines through the community led total sanitation adapted to emergencies, the project will aim to eliminate open defecation. The most vulnerable families identified will receive survival kits, and to address the specific needs of women and adolescents, the project includes a menstrual health activity, promoting educational sessions for the identified families. All efforts presented here also aim to strengthen resilience and promote social cohesion between displaced populations and host communities, ensuring a safer and healthier environment for all

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project will construct or rehabilitate water sources quickly and strategically located to expand access to potable water, also considering the safety of the location, quantity, potability, and accessibility for persons with disabilities and women. To ensure the functionality and prompt maintenance of the water sources, the project, in coordination with SPI (Provincial Infrastructure Services) and SDPI (District Planning and Infrastructure Services), will conduct accelerated training for water committees responsible for managing and performing basic maintenance on the sources, with a focus on immediate solutions for simple repairs. The selection of committee members will be carried out in collaboration with SDPI, local and resettlement leaders, prioritizing the inclusion of women, displaced persons, and individuals with disabilities to make decision-making within the committees more inclusive.The project will promote awareness campaigns on good hygiene practices, with a special focus on menstrual hygiene involving women from families benefiting from survival kits as part of humanitarian assistance packages. It will ensure that women and girls have immediate access to essential items and conduct quick and simplified educational sessions on menstrual health in resettlement centers and communities. These sessions will integrate key messages on menstrual hygiene and reproductive health, aiming to empower women and girls and reduce the stigma associated with the topic.The project will build or rehabilitate temporary or emergency latrines in resettlement centers or densely populated areas, including handwashing stations, prioritizing safety, privacy, and accessibility for women, children, and persons with disabilities. It will combine the PHAST and CLTS methodologies focused on short-term actions to eliminate open defecation, including the use of shared latrines (emergency latrines) until family solutions (family latrines) can be implemented. The project will also identify key public health risks and the main actions contributing to those risks and work with affected families. Through these activities, the project will ensure rapid access to safe emergency sanitation and hygiene practices to reduce the incidence of diseases and/or eliminate open defecation, while also promoting the adoption of good hygiene practices.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project adopts a comprehensive approach to identifying and preventing potential risks, prioritizing the safety and well-being of communities in Cabo Delgado. By working closely with local institutions, the WASH cluster, and partners, the project will utilize existing feedback mechanisms, such as the green line, and actively engage communities through consultations, focus groups, and participatory data collection to identify and mitigate risks.These participatory processes not only identify limitations but also empower communities to contribute to risk identification and mitigation strategies, promoting a "Do No Harm" approach. The participation will ensure that interventions are culturally appropriate and meet the needs of different groups. Additionally, Istituto Oikos will use the checklist for integrating protection into the WASH sector to ensure that all planned interventions comply with required standards. Water committees will be formed by community members, including displaced persons and host communities, to monitor and manage water sources and latrines, detect emerging risks, and quickly address issues

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The creation and training of committees that include women are essential to ensure the inclusion and sustainability of water sources. By involving these groups, the project ensures that specific needs and concerns are heard and addressed. Additionally, elderly individuals and women who participate in activities promoting good hygiene practices become agents of change, spreading knowledge within their families and communities. The project aims to build inclusive latrines and water sources, which will facilitate equitable access to infrastructure. On the one hand, this ensures accessibility, and on the other hand, by promoting menstrual hygiene education and distributing kits, women will be empowered, gaining greater autonomy and dignity.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

During the inception phase of the project, a detailed survey will be conducted to identify persons with disabilities and their specific needs, as previously carried out in the Ibo district. This activity will be undertaken in collaboration with community leaders who are familiar with the local population. Following this, Istituto Oikos will carry out an accessibility needs assessment, which includes understanding the barriers they face, such as reduced mobility and other challenges.The infrastructure will be designed to ensure accessibility for everyone. Water boreholes and latrines will include ramps, handrails, and other features based on the identified needs. For more complex cases, Istituto Oikos will consult the cluster and the project leader to collaboratively develop a viable solution.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project anticipates potential environmental impacts such as soil degradation, as activities like excavation and construction may cause erosion and soil compaction. Overuse of water resources could affect aquifers and reduce their productivity, and water contamination might occur due to the proximity of latrines to water points.To mitigate these potential impacts, the infrastructure will be designed with sustainability criteria, including efficient use of materials and consideration of the surrounding environment. Latrines and water points will be constructed in compliance with minimum distancing standards. Whenever possible, local materials and low-impact construction methods will be prioritized. Local committees will be trained to monitor water consumption and the health of water sources.
