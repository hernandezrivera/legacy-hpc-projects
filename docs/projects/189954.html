Project Summary

This project targets a total of 12,000 vulnerable IDPs HHs who require basic domestic items and shelter assistance. CARE proposes to distribute comprehensive family kits to a total of 6,000 HHs, conduct shelter training targeting 1,500 shelter volunteers and construction support to 1,500 household to construct (type A) emergency shelters. Additionally, CARE will distribute survival kits to 6,000 HHs in hard-to-reach areas. NFI/Shelter activities will be closely linked with PSEA trainings and awareness-raising in line with CARE global policy.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

CARE proposes to target 12,000 vulnerable IDPs HHs (60,000 people) who require basic domestic items and shelter assistance, using CAREs vulnerability criteria which targets:• Female-headed HHs• Child-headed HHs• HHs with the chronically ill, PWDs or elderly persons• HHs with more than 5 members• Newly arrival IDP households• IDPs who have been excluded from previous assistanceCARE proposes to distribute comprehensive family kits to a total of 6,000 HHs in districts 60% of which will be prioritized for assistance targeting female-headed. This includes 2000 in Mueda, 2000 in Montepuez and 2000 in Metuge, reaching an estimated total of 30,000 people. Additionally, CARE will provide shelter training in each district and support with the construction of 1,500 shelters for the most vulnerable families. This includes 250 Type A and 250 Type B shelters in each district, in line with shelter cluster guidelines, which will be supported by trained community construction volunteers. CARE will target an additional 6,000 HHs (30,000 people) in the hard-to-reach areas of Mueda, Quissanga and Macomia with survival kits. CARE will also conduct PSEA training in each district to strengthen the capacity of staff and key community stakeholders (community leaders, site focal points, CBO members) to prevent and respond to protection risks.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Activities: Conduct a rapid needs assessment; Conduct a safety and security risk assessment; Facilitate PSEA training to staff and key stakeholders at the community level; Identify and register beneficiaries according to vulnerability criteria; Distribute 6,000 family kits; Distribute 6,000 survival kits; Conduct PDM to ensure accountability and satisfaction with assistance provided; Select and train community construction volunteers on shelter construction; With support of community construction volunteers, support the construction of 1,500 Type A & Type B shelters for the most vulnerable HHs; Conduct endline evaluation. Locations: Selected sites in Metuge, Mueda, Montepuez, Quissanga and Macomia districts

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project will adhere to the Do No Harm principle. This begins at the beginning of the project cycle, with project design being informed by various analyses conducted by CARE including a recent Conflict-Sensitive Gender Analysis and the needs assessment to be conducted at the beginning of the project to ensure a relevant and appropriate response and identify potential risks. CARE will regularly collect data through our Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAMs), as well as complement them with secondary data, to best examine how our intervention may interact with the conflict and social context. CAREs 4 FAM entry points include Linha Verde, a toll-free hotline that serves as a feedback mechanism for humanitarian assistance recipients, Complaint Boxes, Beneficiary Registration/Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and directly with project focal point during activities. The results will inform any adaptation of activities to prevent negative impacts. Finally, CARE has robust safeguarding policy in place, with accountability holders throughout the organisations, ensuring safety and dignity are prioritized. In the proposed project, CARE plans to conduct a training on PSEA to all staff and key community stakeholders so they can identify, respond to and prevent protection risks.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

To ensure accountability to affected people and communities, CARE will utilize its established Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAM). CARE will encourage the use of its established FAMs through verbal messaging and IEC material during project activities such as distributions and shelter trainings. The FAMs allows all groups including men, women, boys and girls of different ages, illiteracy abilities and other vulnerabilities to provide feedback at different points during implementation. The 4 FAMs entry points include Linha Verde, a toll-free hotline that serves as a feedback mechanism for humanitarian assistance recipients, Complaint Boxes, Beneficiary Registration/Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and directly with project staff at distribution exit points. CARE will be using all 4 channels noted above but will consider any communicated preference by the target population. The availability of these CFMs ensures at least one accessible option for all. For those who cannot access the hotline or are illiterate, PDM and direct communication with CARE staff provide an appropriate option. CARE has experience operating in the target districts and is currently (November 2021) undertaking need assessments in Mueda, which includes participants of different ages, sex and ability – with a needs assessment completed in August 2021 in Montepuez and Metuge district which continues to inform CARE’s planned interventions into 2021 and early 2022. Their needs and preferences will inform project design and implementation.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

During the needs assessment planned at the beginning of the project, CARE will be able to identify and assess the needs of People with Disabilities (PwDs). This will allow CARE to consult PwDs to determine how assistance can be delivered in an appropriate and accessible manner. During distributions, CARE will accommodate PwDs to remove barriers to assistance. This includes a bypass system for the queue, registering an “alternative collector” to pick-up items and adapting the distribution site where possible to ensure they’re safe (e.g. removing tripping hazards, etc). For the Shelter Type A & B assistance, CARE will use Community Construction Volunteers to support PWDs in the reconstruction of their shelters. By conducting PSEA training, CARE will highlight the specific protection risks PwDs face and how they can be responded to and prevented. Finally, CARE having Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAMs), information dissemination and awareness raising will come in a variety of formats, to account for different disabilities (radio, use of images, speakerphones, etc.).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Overall, the project will have a minimal impact on the environment. As this project focuses on material support through survivial kits, aid packaging waste will not have a negative impact as nearly all materials/packaging will be re-used by beneficiaries in some way. For example, at the CARE warehouse, pallets will be reused (all pallets made of wood which upon end of lifespan can be utilized by community members); items such as sacs distributed alongside family kits are used by community members to sell charcoal and other goods. For aid packaging, plastics straps can be used ot secure tarpaulin in shelter and plastic bags have been used to transport and plant seedlings and wrap food in, such as cereals and beans. Bales can be used to demarcate spaces in agricultural plots and home gardens (as a type of fencing) so that chickens and other animals do not invade the area. This will prevent beneficiaries from using mosquito nets for this purpose, which has been seen in the past. Resourcefulness of the recipients of aid, and CAREs own reuse of material/packaging in its own warehouse, ensures resource efficiency. During project activities, CARE will monitor the use of material and packaging, to ensure no environmental damage occurs.
