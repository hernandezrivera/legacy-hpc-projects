Project Summary

The specific objective of the project is to improve access to specific services for the most vulnerable people in Ninewa, Kirkuk, Anbar, Diyala and Baghdad Governorates.
Through this intervention, Handicap International (HI) plans to provide direct psychosocial support (PSS) services, ensure effective referrals of people in need of specialized services (including protection services) and work with communities and service providers to provide information, education and awareness sessions on the PSS and the basic principles of inclusions. The total number of beneficiaries targeted for this project are 10,150.
In direct relation with the priority needs identified, the project will provide both an immediate and long term response. This will be delivered through both direct service provision and capacity building with a view of addressing the short term and long term needs for Iraq. The project will target IDPs, returnees and host communities identified as being vulnerable within this context including persons with disabilities, children, and youth. The psychosocial support will target beneficiaries showing signs of psychological distress such as non-severe symptoms of anxiety and depression (stress, sadness...) and /or psychosomatic symptoms, and or social withdrawal and/or avoidance. The psychosocial support follow-up will aim at stimulating the process of resilience of beneficiaries in psychological distress, to reinforce their coping strategies and to accompany them to reach a better self-esteem. The implementation of PSS group sessions will allow beneficiaries to express their experiences and emotions in a protected environment.
Furthermore the intervention will follow a comprehensive approach as it will benefit from other HI interventions to ensure multiple yet integrated activities (including risk education, MHPSS, physical rehabilitation, and inclusive access to service) in order to maximize the intervention for beneficiaries. Finally, inclusion mainstreaming will be applied throughout the intervention with a specific focus on Gender, Age and Disability, and HI will advocate for inclusive and accessible humanitarian services at all Clusters, as well providing technical training to humanitarian actors seeking to mainstream inclusion in their projects.

Needs

71% of persons with specific needs identified by Handicap International (HI) throughout its past and current projects show concerning signs of psychological distress and psychosocial difficulties, with considerable impact on daily lives. They report experiencing or exhibiting symptoms such as hopelessness, isolation, persistent existential concerns of safety, loss of social role in the new environment; and depressive signs such as anxiety, disturbed sleep, loss of appetite, and loss of motivation for recovery from injury. In general there is a lack of trained non-specialized mental health care providers to provide community-based integrated care as part of the current humanitarian response. Mental health is not adequately considered and routine reporting at health facilities and primary healthcare centre (PHC) facilities does not include classification of mental illness. There is a lack of coordination on mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) assessments and availability/sharing of MHPSS information that helps to improve programming and support to affected populations. In a recent seminar discussing mental health issues in post-war Iraq, an MSF panelist stated that, “Many Iraqis have experienced years of violence. They have lost their loved ones, livelihoods, homes and dignity... People are resilient to a certain point, but exposure to continuous trauma eventually takes its toll” (Rudaw, April 2018). In living with the trauma of such life events, it can result in anxiety, depression and low self-esteem (IOM MHPSS Report June 2018). The country is now in between emergency and recovery phase with very few actors to address gaps in MHPSS acknowledged by WHO and the Health Cluster). According to IOM, as of the 31st October 2018, there were 1,866,594 IDPs in Iraq and 4,113,624 returnees. Many are often facing widespread devastation and lack of services, individuals and communities continue to be urgently in need of psychosocial support services and mental health case management. Moreover, in retaken communities, where the security context has improved, there is a need for supportive community structures that can provide community based activities to facilitate social cohesion and help rebuild support social connections. Psychological distress not only puts conflict affected people at risk of major depression, post traumatic stress disorder, and mental disorders that can pose challenges to recovery, it also puts communities at risk of enduring fractures and being unable to rebuild essential social fabrics and social safety nets. Lessons learned through HI’s recent and ongoing PSS interventions demonstrate the appropriateness and effectiveness of delivering direct Psychosocial Support group sessions in reducing the risk of deterioration of people with psychological distress and in supporting their recovery. Since the delivery of PSS services support individuals, facilitate social connections, and promote positive coping mechanisms, HI’s teams have observed improvements at the level of individuals, households, and communities among beneficiaries. HI recognises that exposure to conflict, displacement and the associated stressors affect men and women differently. In displacement settings women often suffer psychological distress due to a perceived neglect of their family duties, isolation, abandonment by their spouse, and exposure to gender-based violence (GBV); a situation often amplified by cultural constraints on their movement outside the household, particularly for women with disabilities. Girls too suffer from limited access to services as they assume early responsibilities within the household. In returnees areas, persons with disabilities face a lack of specialised services compounded by a lack of information, travel distances and related costs.

Activities

In response to the assessed needs, Handicap International proposes the following activities to ensure increased access to protection services for vulnerable conflict affected people:- Direct provision of psychosocial support (PSS) services: building on a history of psychosocial support programming, leveraging on lessons learned from passed programs, Handicap International will provide PSS services for members of the community with a specific attention for people with specific needs. HI will intervene either in camps settings in HI fixed points and by door to door modality or in identified and existing structures such as youth centers,- Effective referrals of people in need of specialized services (including protection services). Referrals can be internal following prioritisation criteria (for physical and functional rehabilitation and PSS) and external (for other protection cases), using the Inter Agency Referral Form (IARF) and following IARF SOPs. - Information, education and awareness sessions to be provided to communities and services providers in order to promote an inclusive response for the affected communities at both displacement and return situation in Iraq through awareness raising, training and coaching of actors. This activity forms part of the sustainability strategy as it involves the transfer of knowledge to the communities and service providers.- Information, education and awareness sessions to be provided to communities and services providers to reduce stigma and encourage uptake of support services and holistic service provision. - Finally, PSS training will be provided to psychologist and/ or social workers in order to improve their capacities to support patients in need of psychosocial support. Technical expertise will be mobilized to ensure the quality of support provided and to ensure that training curriculums are adapted to the local context and level of knowledge in health facilities. An MHPSS expert will ensure regular technical monitoring and mentoring so as to guarantee the respect of HI and international technical standards. This also forms part of the sustainability strategy as it is an activity focused on capacity building so that the skills and transfer of knowledge can be maintained after HI leaves. Handicap International (HI), globally and in Iraq, is committed to complying, to the extent possible, with core commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations as agreed upon by the IASC. All HI interventions require beneficiary consent and participation, and operations staff are required to solicit feedback and complaints. HI’s senior management regularly reviews staff and beneficiary feedback and incorporates it at every stage of project design and operational decision making. Please note that the proposed project does not reflect the full extent of activities and budget required to meet all of the assessed need, but reflects an approach to meet prioritized needs of the most vulnerable people.

Protection Risk Analysis

- Risk of increased harm or distress from interventions such as mental health and psychosocial support involving highly sensitive issues without appropriate referral / follow up services and procedures in place. - The risk of non-meaningful or non-adapted services preventing access for individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable, such as children, women, persons with disabilities, ethnic minorities and populations with perceived affiliations. - Due to the nature of this work, the activities will involve working with those who are at a heightened risk of human rights violations- The risk that HI staff and beneficiaries may be vulnerable to harassment, sexual assault and abuse- HI staff and beneficiaries not understanding HI’s approach to protection against sexual exploitation and abuse or understand how to raise concerns to allegations related to a safeguarding issue. - Working with children who may have already have experienced previous traumatic incidences - Risk of violence or harm due to a change in the context including unrest or political tension- Risks posed by existing Explosive remnants of war, Improvised explosive devises and mines to both HI staff and populations drawn towards locations of service provision

Mitigation Measures

Protection policies in HIThe principles of equality, non-discrimination and protection are outlined in HI’s Gender Policy, HI’s Child Protection Policy and HI’s Protection of Beneficiaries from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy, which every HI employee reads and signs at the beginning of their assignment. In Iraq, HI designs a feedback mechanism for receiving complaints. HI also conducts awareness workshops and training sessions for all staff on PSEA in order to prevent incidents and to raise awareness on the complaint mechanisms. Finally, HI staffs received protection training on GBV, protection principles, humanitarian principles. Do No HarmHI’s activities are designed and implemented so as to not cause harm and to be consistent with core humanitarian principles. A do-no-harm approach seeks to mitigate any negative impact of external assistance, and ensure that the intervention does not reinforce or exacerbate the conflict and associated tensions. This is bound up with conflict sensitive programming, which entails close analysis and understanding of intergroup relationships within an operational context, as well as any potential interaction between the intervention and these dynamics, in order to avoid any negative impacts. Inclusion of Persons with DisabilityIn Iraq, persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected in situations of risk and humanitarian emergencies, and face multiple barriers in accessing protection and humanitarian assistance, including relief and recovery support. They are also particularly exposed to targeted violence, exploitation and abuse, including sexual and gender-based violence. HI has been working with LNGOs, INGOs and the government in order to address these barriers faced by different groups and also build the capacity of actors to promote an inclusive response for the affected communities at both displacement and return situations through awareness raising, training and coaching. Using the findings identified by HI in surveys, HI will aim at tackling existing barriers to increase level of access to those services. By doing so, HI expects to ensure meaningful access to all planned activities. Gender and Age MainstreamingHI is committed to equal access for people of all genders and a gender-tailored, conflict sensitive response that caters to the different needs and vulnerabilities of all people. The project design seeks to ensure access to services for women, men, boys, and girls of all ages through integrating gender mainstreaming at every stage of the project cycle. Among the steps that HI takes include, but are not limited to, the following:• Gender Sensitive Recruitment and Gender Balanced Teams: HI’s Human Resources seeks to recruit, to the extent possible, gender-balanced teams for Risk Education, Psychosocial Support, and Physical Rehabilitation support. Moreover, all HI staff are required to sign and comply with the PSEA policy. • Gender and Age Disaggregated Data Collection and Decision-Making: HI’s operational and monitoring teams collect data disaggregated by age and gender at every stage of the project, to ensure that the different needs of men, women, boys, and girls inform all decision-making. • Gender and age tailored programming: HI undertakes all measures, to the extent possible, to reach all beneficiaries through its project design and implementation process, based on its contextual experience. ERW/IED/Mine contaminationConsidering the level of contamination in HI areas of intervention, HI, as part of its comprehensive approach, combine to the extent possible its health programing with its protection programing related to Arms Risk Reduction and aiming at reducing the impact of the Conventional Weapon and Improvised Explosive Devices for conflict-affected populations by promoting safety messaging through Risk Education and land release. HI provides regular Mine Risk Education sessions to all HI staff and other service providers at request.

How is your project accountable to affected population

HI's monitoring evaluation accountability and learning (MEAL) practices are framed by HI's global quality policy and tools and HI Iraq MEAL strategy. HI Iraq understands accountability as giving HI beneficiaries, HI staff and partners the opportunity to understand and/or influence the key decisions which are made during HI actions. HI Iraq accountability commitments are the following;• Transparency - Information sharing: by making information available about HI activities• Participation - Listening to the people HI aim to assist, incorporating their views and analysis in program decisions is a key in accountability. It allows beneficiaries to share their feedback on HI activities. Attention is put on encouraging beneficiaries feedback during the implementation of the activities, at the discharge phase and at monitoring stage, through field based feedback mechanisms and discharge satisfaction surveys.• Monitoring and evaluation - HI undertakes regular post intervention monitoring of beneficiaries currently receiving its assistance in order to gather feedback on the quantity and quality of services received, as well as on what beneficiaries found to be lacking or not useful. This feedback is reviewed and considered by HI's operations team and technical advisors, in order to inform project decision-making, as well as HI's long-term strategy.• Requests-Complaints management - having a requests and complaints mechanism for the beneficiaries to report directly any issue to HI. In addition to coordination with the IDP call centre. All complaints, responses and follow up are documented. HI remains committed to take into account beneficiary perspectives at all stages of the project cycle, from needs assessments, to collaboratively determining support plans, and to decision-making over the course of the project. HI conducts regular needs assessments utilising inclusive methodologies to complete Surveys, FGD and analysis of the existing services, meeting with DoH, DoE, community leaders, and interviews with potential beneficiaries, in order to understand needs self-identified by the communities of concern. Findings from these surveys and consultations inform the design of proposals. This ensures that the different needs of men, women and children as well as age are taken into accounts when implementing interventions: for all PSS interventions, HI has emphasized the involvement of the beneficiary in determining the support plan, as well as in setting goals and timelines necessary to reach them. HI's teams are trained to ensure that the goals set are always beneficiary-oriented and tailored to both their circumstances and expectations. In a survey carried out in August 2018, (93%) of surveyed beneficiaries reported the PSS sessions helped them to deal/cope better with the situation because sessions encouraged and supported them by improving psychological situation to continue their life.
