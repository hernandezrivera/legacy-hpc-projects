1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

PSEA is a cross-cutting issue and joint responsibility of all humanitarian partners. The scale-up of operations to respond to the humanitarian crisis in Cabo Delgado and to a lesser extend in Nampula and Niassa brought with it an increase in humanitarian presence, which warrants heightened attention to PSEA. The 2021 Joint SEA Risk Assessment identified gaps in capacities of humanitarian actors to fully understand the root causes and principles to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse, and even more so to investigate complaints and assist victims of SEA in line with existing international guidance and standards. This is not least due to a lack of dedicated and trained staff and funding, which would allow for PSEA mainstreaming across all operation. At the same time, the risk assessment revealed low awareness of communities regarding rights to humanitarian assistance, expected conduct of humanitarian staff and significant barriers to reporting at community level. Proposed project will target male and female humanitarian staff as well as affected communities in site-setting as well as host communities. The project will also support effective coordination of the Cabo Delgado PSEA Network and technical support to Nampula and Niassa operations, where PSEA capacities are only being built.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Coordination and technical support to PSEA Network in Cabo Delgado and humanitarian operation in Nampula as well as induvial agencies in implementing minimum standards and Joint Risk Assessment recommendations; 2. Investigation trainings for (I)NGOs based on global standards and curricula in Cabo Delgado and Nampula; 3. Strengthening PSEA integration and operation of community-based feedback and complaints mechanisms at resettlement sites and host communities through trainings, community consultations and provision of IEC materials in all districts covered by HRP 4. Development of innovative, inclusive and participatory methods and tools for PSEA awareness raising in communities, using drama and arts, in all districts covered by HRP5. Improving capacities for victim assistance and referral, provision of legal aid and other support to survivors in line with IASC Victim Assistance Protocol, in all districts covered by HRP

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers is a violation of international principles and a grave protection concern. The targeted PSEA actions foreseen in this project will actively contribute to ensuring the protection of people affected by the crisis in northern Mozambique, in particularly woman and girls, suffering or at risk of SEA. Raising humanitarian staff capacities and mainstreaming PSEA throughout the whole operation is a measure to ensure that no further harm is caused by the humanitarian presence. Improved investigation and victim assistance capacities of humanitarian organizations will enhance their safety and dignity. Ensuring that PSEA is systematically mainstreamed into community-based feedback and complaints mechanisms and reporting barriers for children and persons with disabilities are lowered will improve meaningful access of victims and communities to information about their rights, but also redress and protection. Communities will be encouraged to actively participate in the development and implementation of awareness raising and define the reporting and complaints mechanisms which best suit them. The PSEA Network cooperates closely with the Protection Cluster on community-based actions and protection monitoring as well as with the GBV and Child Protection AoRs on mainstreaming PSEA into GBV referral pathways and trainings for service providers.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

The project is aimed at strengthening PSEA across the entire HRP. Its coordination will lie with the PSEA Coordinator, and actions will be implemented by members of the Cabo Delgado PSEA Network and humanitarian actors in Nampula. Mozambique has in place SOPs on Recording and Processing SEA Complaints. In 2021 efforts have started to integrate PSEA into GBV tools and train GBV actors and PSEA focal points on SEA victim case management, which is aimed to improve available services for survivors. These efforts will continue in 2022 and be expanded to Nampula province.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Persons with disabilities, especially women suffering from mental disabilities, have been found to be at high risk to suffer SEA in Mozambique. The project will specifically address gaps in access to reporting and services for persons with disabilities, who wish to disclose or have suffered from SEA. Members of the PSEA Network who specialize in disability inclusion will be involved in the development of tools and methods for community engagement as well as in the integration of PSEA into CFMs/CBCMs to ensure that those are inclusive and targeted. They will also be consulted where the provision of specific services to survivors is needed.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

No specific environmental considerations are foreseen for the project. Wherever possible, PSEA activities will be integrated into ongoing actions and programmes and not cause any additional impact.
