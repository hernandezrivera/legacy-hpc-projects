Project Summary

The project is designed to contribute to achieve the HRP SO2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services & improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL. It will aos contribute to the SO 3; The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome, a protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”
The project is designed to provide shelter rehabilitation to 300 vulnerable HHs, 1710 in total “670 men, 443 women, 200 boys and 397 girls” in 9 vulnerable Bedouin communities in Gaza strip that are lacking shelter basic needs of hygiene & privacy, prone to floods & at risk of manmade or natural disasters. The project will provide rehabilitation to substandard shelters to increase HHs resilience and create adequate living space with integrated hygienic WASH facilities with focus on HHs at higher risk of being affected from COVID-19 crisis & harsh weather conditions to increase their protection measures towards extreme weather conditions in winter.
Tthe project will also include awareness sessions to the 300 targeted HHs for safety and social distancing measures including distribution of PPEs. Tthe awareness activity will use the door-to-door visit, visual tools and brochures. This will be implemented in close coordination with medical staff to ensure safety measures as per latest updates of the related formal bodies.
The HHs shall cover the selection criteria of: (1) located in one of the targeted locations; (2) Are prone to risk of harsh weather; (3) have poor WASH and Hygiene infrastructure; (4) family size above 5 people, (5) priority to female headed HHs, (6) with low/ no source of income.
The project is intended to conclude an agreement with local supplier, with the possibility to go for the self-help approach in case stable health conditions (conditional cash transfer), to conduct the assigned rehabilitation works required in worksites for the 300 substandard housing/shelters. Tthe local supplier shall be able to provide maximum safety conditions in the work sites under Covid19 settings as well as show well developed protocol for safety measures in worksites. the supplier shall work under full technical support of the PARC Team. Constant and full consultations with targeted HHs will continue to make sure that gender and age and disabilities preferences and needs are covered as possible. The rehabilitation works will include repairing activity for ( Replace & fix the ceiling, Install & repair wash facilities, replacing destroyed or worn out pieces, install/ replace & fix broken windows & doors to assure the protection & privacy, Install & repair Electricity for damaged or worn put networks, with ability to install alternative lightening system, Install & repair concrete & block works to assure the proper protection of HHs. it will also include tilling and/or concrete basin to facilitate cleaning process.
PARC will use the local resources meaning that all materials are available in the local markets that inputs are environmentally friendly with quality and durability that don’t cause odours/emissions).
Important to mention that PARC will be applying the special Covid19 Standards of Operations (SoPs) for safety measures in construction worksites. This SoPs were adapted based on the guidelines and shared experience with Premiere Urgence international worldwide.

1 - Needs

Based on the Humanitarian Needs Overview HNO under the shelter and NFIs for 2021, there are still 21,500 substandard units, which need rehabilitation of essential components to provide minimum requirements of waterproofing, hygienic living space, privacy, and protection from weather extremes.PARC has designed this action on a specific needs assessment that was organized to cover 9 vulnerable Bedouin communities in the Gaza strip, in (Om Alnassir, Almalaha, Almoghraqa, Alfokharii, JohrAldeik, Wadi ALsalqa, Bedouin community in the east of Rafah, Alsawarha, the Bedouin community in Alnemsawi).The needs assessment has covered 400 HHs in the said locations. 100% of the surveyed HHs were identified in need to urgent rehabilitation interventions and winterization/summarization including protection and wash. Iit was found also that some of HHs are of the most vulnerable HHs in their communities. the survey included a comprehensive individual needs assessment to each visited HHs for the houses needs including wash facilities, protection, privacy and needs of PWDs. The data was collected using a well-organized comprehensive tool. The collected data was then analyzed and provide comprehensive data on the context, priority needs ( food, shelter, wash, education, privacy and protection). The tool also applied the gender and age analysis to identify their need and integrate them into the design as much as possible. The results of the need assessments showed that 15%of surveyed HHs are headed by women, %22.2 of HHs stated that they have at least a family member is person with Disability or have special needs and the house they live in doesn’t provide them with proper accessibility to facilities and services. 95 % of HHs are relying on assistance (UNRWA, relatives, assistance of MoSD, or others). As well, 44 % of the visited HHs lack structural integrity and proper WASH facilities and not connected to public sewage network, 38% of the houses are not connected with public water networks, 86.2% of HHs facing problems during winter season (rain leakage and floods), while 68 % of the HHs are dealing with floods manually (Emptying water by bucket), while 8.9 % have to flee their homes. Accordingly, 43% of visited HHs are suffering from dermatology and intestinal diseases.In relation to protection, gender and age aspect, the assessment has found that: 64% of the visited HHs stated that their houses lack to protection and are without doors, 8.4% with no windows and 36% without windows; covering them with plastic sheets. Some of 67% of HHs houses have no separated rooms or any kind of privacy for girls, women, boys and men, while 42.8 % have no doors and locks for the Bathrooms to assure privacy while taking shower or using bathrooms.In addition, in April 2020, PARC has led a community-based disaster risk management/CBDRM process in the Bedouin communities to identify the risks, challenges, needs and priorities in the targeted locations. The preliminary finding reveals that HHs there are lack to adequate and decent houses that also lack to humanitarian standards. Based on the above results & from our experience in dealing with vulnerable communities, the impact of poor living condition has resulted a negative impact/coping strategy on the HHs members at the health, educational, and humanitarian levels. PARC, therefore, would seize this important opportunity available to assist this vulnerable group in these communities and provide emergency shelter rehabilitation according to the minimum house standards. The needs assessment report is available on request.

2 - Activities

The project aims to achieve the overall objective of “Contribute to increasing resilience and protection against risk for vulnerable HHs in the most affected Bedouin communities in Gaza strip through shelter rehabilitation interventions”.Project outcome aims at “enhanced coping mechanism and protection against risks for 300 vulnerable HHs in most affected Bedouin communities in the Gaza strip through shelter rehabilitation interventions”The project is designed to target 300 most vulnerable Bedouin HHs across the Gaza strip with emergency shelter rehabilitation interventions. The project will be achieved by implementing the following activities:1. Formulating a project local committee PLC: PLC will be formed in each locality that will include representatives from Community member/ leaders, Municipalities, CBOs, MoA, governorate representative and other relevant stakeholders. 2. Organizing for the coordination and work approval with the local authorities: this will include sharing templates and forms for the needs assessment that will be conducted with the targeted beneficiaries in the field. 3. Organizing for inauguration/info sessions: field info sessions in the 9 target locations to inform the community of the project information, selection criteria and application, verification process, and complaint mechanism.4. Verification visits: Final field verification visits of the nominated BENFs will be conducted along with the updated Bells of quantities for each housing unit will be issued in order to start the coordination process with the other stakeholders. 5. Coordination with Shelter cluster and MoWPH: sharing the final list of candidates with the shelter cluster coordinator and MoWPH in order to cross-check to avoid overlapping and/or duplication and to maximise community benefits. 6. Initiate the baseline survey: Baseline line survey will be conducted to cover the 300 final selected HHs. the Baseline will be used as benchmark to measure the project progress and final success. 7. Signing the contracts/agreements with the targeted beneficiaries: this process requires that Beneficiaries to show the legal support to issue and assure the ownership, the legal consultant will assist the targeted beneficiaries in having ownership documents to save their rights in their houses.8. Preparing tenders and awarding: Announcing and awarding the rehabilitation process to local contractor to lead the rehabilitation works with the targeted locations. The contractors shall be familiar to the targeted location, provide commitment to invest local workers whenever possible, assure the application of Covid19 safety measure and Special SoPs in construction worksites that covers the targeted HHs and workers. 9. Emergency rehabilitation works: Starting the rehabilitation process for the targeted beneficiaries, taking into consideration the Covid19 safety measures. The rehabilitation works will include repairing activity for ( Replace & fix the ceiling, Install & repair wash facilities, replacing destroyed or worn out pieces, install/ replace & fix broken windows & doors to assure the protection & privacy, Install & repair Electricity for damaged or worn put networks, with ability to install alternative lightening system, Install & repair concrete & block works to assure the proper protection of HHs. it will also include tilling and/or concrete basin to facilitate cleaning process.10. MEAL activities: the project will have a MEAL plan and MEAL frame with clear and well-set progress indicators. This will include well-established complaint mechanism, satisfaction and impact survey…etc. 11. End-line report: Conduct an End line survey/report to compare the situation and measure impact of the project that will provide main findings, challenges and lessons learnt to feed the final report and next project cycle. 12. Close out and final reporting. 13. PARC will make sure that community engagement is achieved though taking into consideration the specific needs of different HHs gender and age groups ate the design and implementation phase. PARC will also have continuous group and individual consultations with the community members and target groups to make sure that cultural and personal norms are respected in terms of behaviours and quality of performance. given the specificity of this Bedouin communities, PARC will make sure in case of contract modality that the supplier is investing local skilled workers from the target area. this will be in close coordination with CPCs and CBOs available in the target area.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

PARC has a mechanism regarding accountability and receiving feedback from beneficiaries; Handling complains and ensuring accountability measures are implemented since the design phase of the project until the handing over of all activities. Feedback is ensured via HAP mechanism that is adopted by PARC which ensures to provide a secure space for targeted population to express their satisfaction and dissatisfaction. In 2015, PARC developed its guidance to HAP policy systems in support and collaboration with Christian Aid Britain, which includes participation, information sharing, and complaint and response policies. This mechanism includes a well-designed complaint form that will be tracked through PARC’s MEAL officer and documented through the MEAL documentation system. All project activities will be followed by the MEAL officer who collects data to develop the lessons learned on two levels; during the implementation and after achieving the project, which PARC builds on for future similar designed interventions. A HAP complaint mechanism will be elaborated at targeted communities and audience will be encouraged to claim their rights. The mechanism allows beneficiaries and community members to provide their feedback and report to PARC in a formal and confidential manner. This is promoted and implemented in coordination with local committees (LCs). A project LC is formulated (women and men) representing community stakeholders and target groups (village councils (VC), CBOs, activists, farmers’ committees, women clubs, etc) in which they are given the opportunity to participate effectively in overseeing project implementation. Hence, a complaint and feedback box will be introduced to VCs, placed in the targeted communities in addition to a generic e-mail, and is easily accessible to community members (males and females). PARC team will ensure that health and safety measures are taken into consideration throughout the project life cycle.

PARC has developed its Standards of Operations (SoPs) for the for the safe implementation of activities with commitment to precaution measures and enhanced mitigation approach of risks. PARC staff received special training on how to apply these standards while in office and in field work. Special HR staff member was designated to monitor the process and make sure that the staff apply the social distancing and measures are well applied. PARC team will adhere to the developed SoPs for the implementation of the different activities, field visits, M&E, complaint and feedback mechanism to ensure all safety and security measures are taken against COVID-19.PARC has used the from-door-door modality in order to keep minimum people movement to get the assistance. PARC also share clear instructions with its staff as well as its beneficiaries about how vulnerable people and people at risk due to be elderly or with chronic diseases and/or have immunity issues to keep safe in their homes and that PARC will reach them to provide the required services. For this specific intervention, PARC will use the contractor modality to minimize people movement. PARC already developed standards of operations for the work in construction worksite. These measures will be applied in the construction worksite. Also, the contractor will be asked to provide certain safety measure for the field workers and how to work inside houses. PARC has a well-developed policies for community engagement and accountability principles and code of conducts. “Do No Harm" as seen to be part of ensuring accountability towards the local communities used and monitored during any intervention implemented by PARC including participation, information sharing, enabling complaints and "do no harm”, environment protection and gender mainstreaming. Continued consultations will be conducted with the project target groups/stakeholders and HHs to analyse potential risks that the project team will take into consideration while progressing/implementing the project. PARC believes in equal opportunities for all beneficiaries; ensuring equal access of information, neutrality and assistance through the project cycle starting with the inclusion of all relevant stakeholder’s in the need assessment. Coordination with the local authorities, CBOs, and community leaders in the project planning and selection process to ensure optimal community participation, outreach, &transparency in service provision. 9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PARC work has been central to remote and marginalized areas, which enabled the organization to gain full access to the targeted area of the project. In the last five years PARC has been implementing leading project/interventions in the marginalized Bedouin communities (in crisis and post crisis time). PARC is well known in the target area and has an established network of key stakeholders; governmental bodies, project local committees and the agricultural committees, which facilitates effective and efficient access to the targeted location. Work in the targeted locations will be aligned with operational SOPs for the implementation of the different activities, field visits, M&E, complaint and feedback mechanism, which PARC staff, partners, stakeholders, and Community Protection Committees (CPCs)will abide by. Safe construction work in work sites will be adapted to maintain safety and security of staff and stakeholders under emergency sittings namely outbreak of COVID-19. The targeted area is accessible with limited restriction on movement into or out of the area, but beneficiaries in the targeted communities live under a classic rural bad cycle typified by a lack of access to natural resources including agricultural land; which are often difficult accessible due to poorly maintained roads. The materials and labors required to implement the activities are available in the local market.In case of lockdown due to COVID-19 pandemic, PARC will shift the project to self-help modality to enable people from accessing services, shelter rehabilitation by investing the local resources available in the closed community by hiring relative skilled workers. PARC also will also assist HHs to be able to access to service suppliers of construction inputs and tools and will be providing full consultations to make sure that HHs are satisfied of the service quality and delivery. PARC will maintain communication and coordination with the government bodies in relation to movement to get required movement permits to its staff and the contractor. PARC will also make sure that its staff and contracted contract will apply site safety measures under COVID-19 context.PARC ensures that aid and impartial access reaches the most vulnerable since the design phase when conducting a need assessment at communities. Gender mainstreaming and protection will be integrated within the project activities. In need assessment PARC ensures to engage local community members, VCs and other relevant stakeholders to have a comprehensive understanding of the targeted community’s needs. The findings of the need assessment define the proposed interventions. This is then followed at the implementation phase of the proposed project, in which PARC starts its intervention with formulating a project Local Committee (LC) that represents the targeted population and include key actors from the area and representatives of Community Based Organizations The project LC and PARC team plays a role in ensuring the access to information to all community members through a clear project announcement in the mosques, village councils, and PARC’s social media platforms. PARC has a well-defined selection criterion that responds to the needs of the targeted population and ensures accessibility and sensitivity to the most vulnerable. This will be hand on hand with the project local committee. An open call of applications will also ensure the meaningful access, empowerment and transparent process in selecting final beneficiaries which will also be in participation with Project Local Committee. This participatory approach emphasizes ownership, transparency, good governance at beneficiary and community level.
