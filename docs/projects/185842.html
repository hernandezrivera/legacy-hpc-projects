1 - Identified Needs

"Cox’s Bazar is a disaster-prone area with small- and large-scale weather events like cyclone, flooding, landslide, heavy rain, heavy wind etc. causing massive damage to shelter interventions which require constant repairing and more durable shelters. The camps are densely populated which has low scopes for maintaining plot standards and site plan. The COVID-19 pandemic and corresponding restrictions imposed disrupted shelter and settlement support to a great extent and shelter condition became most vulnerable which increases the risk as well. As the shelter assistance is only provided with the non-durable materials and being subjected to declining funds and frequent natural and human induced disasters, the shelter assistance must be topped up every year. The joint MSNA published in August 2021 identified that 74% of households have shelter & non-food items sectoral living standard gaps. The same report pointed out shelter materials/upgrade as one of the top three priority needs includes households with NFI also in the list. Within the process of shelter assistance, 88% of the households preferred in-kind assistance, 38% of HHs requested cash assistance and 17% said that they require labor support. More focused should be put on persons with disabilities, women, elderly aged people as they find it difficult to access to different areas of the camps and they feel unsafe within their own communities. The ‘Shelter Standard Assessment’ conducted by Shelter/NFI sector supported by NPM and Reach (as of September 2021) indicates that there are still gaps to achieve minimum performance standards in some shelters in terms of bracing, TDK, kitchen area, gutter, non-flammable materials for protecting kitchen area, wall improvement, roof improvement etc. On the other hand, information from SNFI also referred that a huge gap was found in household level drainage, retention wall and pathway after the recent flood incident. Community level awareness on DRR and shelter construction and upgradation was still found to be low compared to the desired level. Women, elderly aged peoples and person with disabilities participation is still low in the shelter assistance activities which make them more vulnerable to external threats. Flooding, fire, landslides, heavy rain, strong winds, theft, lack of non-durable materials etc. are still identified as major gaps in this response. In 2020, the RDV supported shelters were 18% which was increased to 24%. This increment was experienced due to lack of regular shelter assistance in the COVID-19 lockdown. In the face of declining funds and risk of COVID-19 pandemic lockdowns, this percentage is likely to increase in the upcoming years. Moreover, as Rohingyas are being relocated to Bhasanchar, the left-out shelters of these families require emergency shelter assistance for other families to be relocated back there. Therefore, frequent support needed in these shelters compared to last few years. On the other hand, shelters in host community also faces damage every year due to heavy rainfall, flooding and heavy wind. The JMSNA also found that 71% of the household’s face shelter issues in the host community. The report also finds that 54% of the respondents lived in kutcha/Jupri shelters which make them vulnerable to natural disasters. As there is low opportunities of income generating activities in the declining economy, the households need NFI for meeting their basic needs. The local community is still largely male dominated which makes addressing gender & Protection related issues very important. Moreover, There is a huge gap of community participatory approach all through the program cycle."
