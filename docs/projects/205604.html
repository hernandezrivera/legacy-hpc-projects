Project Summary

This project will save lives by addressing the acute and severe food insecurity of 24,000 most vulnerable and food insecure returnees, IDPs and Host communities in Twic East, Fangak, Akobo and Uror counties of Jonglei state, Lainya and Terekeka Counties of Central Equatoria State with enhancement of agricultural production through distribution of crop and vegetable seed kits, agricultural tool kits, fishing kits and provide agronomic training to 15,000 HHs. Overall, SLDA aims to reach out to severe and acute food insecure people in IPC phase 4 and 5. The purpose of the project is to enhance emergency food production, diversify food consumption and strengthen the resilience and coping mechanism of targeted communities.
The project will implement activities that will prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies of target beneficiaries in IPC 4 and 5. The project provides food support for the protection of lives and human capital of IDP, returnees and host communities. The project will specifically focus on two main areas of support: (i) assisting farmers through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support; and (ii) reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. This will allow severe and acute food insecure people in IPC phase 4 and 5 to meet their immediate food needs whilst allowing them to rebuild their assets. In addition, by improving people's food security, acute malnutrition will be reduced.
Mitigation measures to reduce and prevent the occurrence of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) will be incorporated in all interactions with beneficiaries, including training and general awareness of beneficiaries. The project will improve accountability to affected populations (AAP) in a more holistic way by integrating four core people-related issues: age, disability, gender and protection. Similarly, protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle to ensure beneficiary safety and dignity, avoiding doing harm as result of the intervention, providing meaningful access to all sets of beneficiaries and ensuring all groups are actively involved and participate in the project in an inclusive manner.

1. Needs

South Sudan’s vulnerability to climate change and natural disasters compounds the country’s humanitarian crisis, jeopardizing recovery and undermining development efforts. Since its independence in 2011, the country has suffered from severe droughts (2011, 2015) and floods (2014, 2017, 2019, 2020, 2021, and 2022), resulting in high numbers of fatalities, displacements and loss of livestock which have severely impacted people’s livelihoods.IPC analysis for the period between September 2023 to July 2024 indicates that in Jonglei State during the lean season (April 2024 and July 2024), an estimated 696,000 people (68% of the state’s population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 11,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, 300,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 385,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. All of the 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity are in Pibor County of Pibor Administrative Area. All the counties in the state will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, except for Bor South, Pochalla, and Twic East counties that will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. In Central Equatoria state during the same lean season period (April to July 2024), food security will deteriorate, with an estimated 686,000 people (52% of the state’s population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 190,000 people will likely face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 496,000 people will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. During this period all counties in the State including Lainya and Terekeka will maintain their classifications of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity.The deterioration in the food security situation in these two states during the lean season period is due to the exhaustion of own-produced food stocks, high market dependency amid a rise in staple food prices, limited income-earning opportunities, limited availability of wild foods, likelihood of increased insecurity that will lead to reduced access to market and livelihoods activities, and election-related insecurity fears that are likely to interfere with trade flows and market functionality across these states and the country in general.The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) predicts that El Niño will cause above average levels of rainfall, resulting in flooding and flash flooding. Displacement is expected (especially for those living in flood-prone areas) as well as crop and livestock destruction and damage to infrastructure, increasing the health, nutrition and mental health needs of individuals affected by the events. 450,000 people are at risk of being exposed to floods, especially in Unity and Jonglei State. In addition, across the country, the price of food commodities has increased drastically. The price of beans for example, increased 122 per cent as compared to August 2022, and 11 per cent in the last month. With this increase in pricing, we expect an increase in malnutrition rates given the unaffordability of food products for individuals. A 25 per cent increase in admissions for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) treatment as compared to August 2022 indicates a decline in South Sudan's nutritional status (UNICEF South Sudan Humanitarian Situation No.8 –August 2023) This project will target 24,000 most vulnerable conflict and food insecure returnees, IDPs and Host communities in Twic East, Fangak, Akobo and Uror counties of Jonglei state, Lainya and Terekeka Counties of Central Equatoria State through an inclusive assistance, and will ensure that protection and gender are mainstreamed.

2. Project activities

• Distribution of emergency livelihood inputs (vegetable, crop seeds and fishing kits) to 24,000 beneficiaries. Registration and targeting will be based on vulnerability criteria such that the most vulnerable members get prioritized.• Livestock treatment and vaccination and disease surveillance targeting 120,000 livestock (cows, goats and sheep): The project will facilitate training of 60 Community Animal Health Workers and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonoses e.g. RVF. • Training and capacity building of 15,000 farmers and fisher folks: Beneficiaries will receive training and extension services on a range of topics including but not limited to: training on fishing best practices; food utilization and appropriate feeding practices; crop and vegetable production; adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots); business skills development, entrepreneurship and financial literacy.
