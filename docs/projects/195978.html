Project Summary

Under this project FIVDB intends to target 5 FDMN camps i.e. 1 E, 4, 14, 21, 26, 27 and Palangkhali, Haldiapalang, Nhila, Teknaf Pouroshava, Baharchara and Whykong under Ukhiya and Teknaf region respectively. FIVDB is successfully implementing FSS and livelihood projects in camp 21, 14, Teknaf Pourashava and Baharchara and learnings from the projects will be incorporated under the proposed project. The project participants will be identified from most vulnerable HHs i.e. female headed HHs, HHs with person with disabilities, HHs with large number, orphan hosted HHs, HHs with elderly person, LGBTI etc. maintaining SAAD criteria from age 18 to 65 through checking NID’s using sectors’ duplication exercise.Protection mainstreaming, gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion through WGQ questionnaire, meaningful participation, community engagement, AAP are always prioritized by FIVDB following sector approved guideline. FIVDB will enable person with disability friendly project activities targeting 4% person with disabilities. The project will try to identify vulnerable LGBTI person and enable separate modality if the participants face any challenges to participate in the activities due to social stigma, taboo and prejudice. 260 (140 FDMN,120 HC) participants will be identified and receive 5 days training on condiment production, 1750 (840 FDMN, 910HC) will receive 3 days training on climate smart gardening, DRR, environment friendly pesticide production, bio degradable waste compost, communication skill etc. 420 (210 each from FDMN and HC respectively) previous beneficiaries from former projects in Teknaf paurashava and Baharchara will receive technical support. 240 participants from HC will receive an investment grant via cash transfer through mobile to start small business such as tea stall, grocery shop etc. The project will target 100% women in agricultural training to enable the scope for women to engage in agricultural production with non-cash input support (seeds, manure etc.) for FDMN and startup capital cash for HC to build their garden. 30% male will be targeted under condiment production to. discourage the idea that only women participate in such activities with non-cash input support for FDMNs and startup capital for HC.12 participants (50% women) will be capacitated in building nurseries with startup capital in HC including.520 participants (280 from FDMN and 240 from HC) will be included under cash for work activities based on community demand and followed by coordination with local government targeting at least 30% women as women has less scope in labor force participation.1600 participants (700 from FDMN, 900 from HC) will receive 5 plants each to plant them in their community as an ecofriendly approach and natural resource management. 8300 (4300 from FDMN community, 4000 from HC) will receive awareness raising sessions on disaster risk reduction, GBV, peaceful co-existence etc. The project will arrange 12 inter community dialogue to enhance social cohesion and promote peaceful coexistence in project intervention areas. The project will follow result based management and result based monitoring approach ensuring community engagement from design to implementation. The impact of the project will be evaluated following OECD- DAC criteria. All the staffs will be provided protection gears and FIVDB’s own security protocol will be active throughout the project. Any context specific risk, natural hazards will be assessed and dealt with as per the protocol. FIVDB’s PSEA policy will be active throughout the project to address and respond to any PSEA concern. Complaint and feedback mechanism will be established to address and respond to any compliant and feedback received from the community. FIVDB will actively coordinate and participate in EETWG sector. Over all the project will target to improve self-reliance and promote peaceful coexistence among the targeted project participants.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

At present more than 0.9 million FDMNs are living in Bangladesh in 33 camps in Teknaf and Ukhiya region of Cox’s Bazar. Food, access to livelihood, access to income generating activities, access to NFI, Wash facilities, Education etc. have always being the top priority needs in FDMN camps. Followed by the influx, adjacent host communities have been affected as well and prior to COVID 19 the situation has worsened. The instability in economy has also played a major role in boosting the vulnerabilities in the host community. The identified priority needs among host community are access to food, access to livelihood, access to IGA etc. (MSNA, REVA). Most vulnerable HHs are identified as Female headed HHs, HHs with person with disabilities, HHs with elderly persons, orphan hosted HHs, HHs with large numbers etc. in both FDMN and Host communities. Since 2017 several organizations have extended their support to affected population in terms of food security, enabled livelihood scopes, shelter, NFI support, education. WASH etc. But for such as large number of population all the assistances combined are never adequate and vulnerabilities continue among the population. According to JMSNA, 72% HHs in refugee camps is living with food security and livelihood gaps. 59% HHs reported food and 30% reported access to self-reliance activities as priority needs. 10% HHs has poor, 43% HHs has borderline and 48% has acceptable FCS score as per the report. HHs with person with disabilities have lower FCS score comparatively.89% HHs reported to have adopted negative coping strategies. FCS score has deteriorated in HC compared to 2019 in 2021 as per the JMSNA report. 80% HHs are found to be moderately and 95% HHs are found to highly vulnerable in FDMN community. In HC, 52% HHs have found to be highly vulnerable due to economic downturns, lacking adequate and expanded assistance, adequate livelihoods recovery initiatives, and skyrocketing price spikes of essential commodities, especially food. (REVA, 2022). As per the data of 2021, 79% HHs in FDMN community and 72% HHs in HC is adopting consumption based coping mechanism. Sustainable food security, access to income generating activities are now priority needs among refugee and host community. HHs with persons with disabilities have lowest FCS score and 95 % HHs with person with disabilities reported to have adopted livelihood coping mechanism due to short of money. (JMSNA 2021). 60% of FDMN community and 50% of Host communities are reported to have poor resilience. According to Labor Market and skill assessment report DCA 2020 major constraints in FDMN and Host communities in Teknaf are limited job/employment opportunities, limited access to product or service market, low business skill among HC, weak linkage between vocational education with labor market in HC, gender discrimination etc. The situations have reported to have worsened prior to COVID 19. Ukhiya is highly disaster prone area where locals and FDMNs are more vulnerable to natural calamities and disaster risks. Disaster preparedness is reported to be low among the population. As per various FGD reports conducted by FIVDB lots of HHs suffer from livelihood damages, shelter damages, property damages because of flood and other natural calamities. The issues need to be addressed and sustainable livelihood options, access to IGA, nutritious food, disaster preparedness should be enabled and improved among the affected population in FDMN camps as well as in Host communities. Also environment pollution is another major issue in refugee camps. Due to lack of awareness people throw wastes here and there resulting various diseases, environment pollution, inconvenience for the population and other hazards threatening peaceful coexistence within or between the communities

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

All the refugee population living in Cox’s Bazar depend on humanitarian assistance and services to meet their basic needs and since 2017, already vulnerable Host community people in this area are also in stress due to the huge influx and current global economic fallout. The proposed project will ensure access to diversified foods for both the targeted host and Rohingya households. An integrated and coordinated approach – capacity building, input support, risk mitigation, linkage with the already available government and non-government services, encountering early marriage by engaging adolescent and youth girls, and protection mainstreaming, has been proposed under this project based on the previous learning of the organization since 2017 from its own assessment and from the sectors, camp administration, local government and community consultation. For every project, FIVDB always prioritizes coordination with and reporting to the sector, camp level coordination, coordination with the CiC, local government and administrations. The interventions proposed under this project are fully aligned with the sector priorities and Bangladesh Government priorities and strategies. FIVDB believes that the impact and sustainability will be remarkable as the approach and modalities that FIVDB is proposed are already tested in development projects and in humanitarian settings with changes in Cox’s Bazar. Over the last five years, FIVDB implemented blended approaches based on need and that were useful to bring expected results. Lastly, from the beginning of FIVDB’s Humanitarian Response program, multi-dimensional community engagement approaches - community consultation, door-to-door visit, help desk, interactive popular theater were undertaken for trust and relationship building with the communities which played the most vital role in the successful implementation of every project and it will also be followed for this proposed project.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

During beneficiary selection priority will be given to most vulnerable HHs such as persons with disability, women headed HHs, HHs with elderly person, HHs with large house, LGBTI group maintaining SAAD criteria. Proper consent will be taken before data collection and confidentiality will be maintained as per implementing partner’s policies. Protection mainstreaming, gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion, meaningful participation, community engagement, accountability to most affected population are always prioritized by FIVDB. Community engagement in every phase of implementation and monitoring of the project and it will carry out by arranging formal and informal community consultation session, door-to-door visit, introducing feedback mechanism and regular observation and satisfaction survey etc. All the staffs will be provided protection gears and FIVDB’s own security protocol will be active throughout the project to deal any context specific risk and natural hazards.

3.2 - Gender

Addressing the gaps in labor force participation FIVDB will aim to engage 100 % women in agricultural activities and 30% women in cash for work activities. Additionally, male participants will be encouraged to participate in pickle making trainings. All the self-reliance activities will be enabled for the participants based on needs and vulnerabilities considering coordination, participation, transformative change. Most vulnerable HHs will identified and empowered under the program irrespective of sex, gender, ethnicity, religion etc. ensuring disability inclusion. The program will try to identify person of concern from LGBTI community and ensure inclusion of person of concern from the community and will initiate to respond to their needs separately if they face challenges to participate in the programs due to social barriers and taboo. The program will initiate to aware community people on GBV, violence against women and child and related PSEA issues.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The activities will be enabled for age group 18- 60 under the project. FIVDB discourages child labor and any violence against children. Inclusion of child in any cash for work activities, distribution program is strictly prohibited. Additionally, FIVDB will introduce nursing and breast feeding corners for lactating mothers in every distribution point and a nursing corner will be placed in project office for female participants with children. FIVDB’s child safeguarding policies will be active throughout the project to deal with any safeguarding concerns. HHs with pregnant and lactating women will be prioritized during selection of persons of concern. The project will essentially include awareness and sensitization session on child labor, child trafficking, violence against child, chill marriage. FIVDB will additionally include drop out adolescents and youth in the self-reliance activities.

3.4 - Diversity

The program will be designed to address and respond to the needs of more vulnerable communities irrespective of gender, sex, religion, ethnicity, culture etc. All the programs will be designed in person with disability friendly way and target to ensure disability inclusion in the program The program will target to include 4% HHs person with disabilities in overall programs. The person will also initiate to identify vulnerable LGBTI persons of concern and will initiate to respond separately to the needs of LGBTI person if they face any barrier in the program due to social stigmatization and barriers. The program will respective toward local culture and practice. All the modules and materials will be designed considering local and cultural context and practiced.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Community engagement in every phase of implementation and monitoring of the project. Information sharing, regular communication with donors and other stakeholders, maintaining transparency will be regarded as top most liability by the organization. FIVDB’s own safeguarding policy & PSEA policy (assessed by UNICEF) will be translated into very local language and sign language and disseminated among the project participants and every staff will be oriented as per the policy. Any PSEA incident reported will be dealt with by authorized investigation committees with trained members as per policy. FIVDB will prepare a risk assessment and mitigation plan at the beginning and will address and take mitigation measures for any risk that arise during project implementation. Hotline number & complaint box will be disseminated and established respectively in project areas to address and deal the complaints and feedback by the community people.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. FIVDB will organize courtyard/ awareness sessions on DRR, disaster preparedness, landslide warnings, DRR training for participants, climate smart homestead gardening, skill development on environment friendly pesticide production, bio degradable waste compost, under the project. FIVDB will arrange 4 inter community dialogues in project intervention areas to enhance social cohesion.Also, for ensuring environment friendly vegetable gardening, FIVDB encourage and monitor beneficiaries to not to use any materials for making and maintaining vegetable gardens such as chemical fertilizer, chemical pesticide and plastic rope etc. Over the last five years, FIVDB was able to reduce significantly the use of such materials which are not environment friendly. As a part of ecofriendly approach and natural resource management project participants will receive 5 plants each to plant them in their community.
