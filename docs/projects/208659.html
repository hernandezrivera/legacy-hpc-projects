Project Summary

Wellspring Humanitarian Foundation (WHF) seeks to implement the project titled Addressing SGBV Mistreatment and Referrals Difficulties in Northeast Nigeria (ASMRDN) in Yobe State which will cut across 5 LGAs. The goal of this project proposal is to improve referral channels while addressing and combating gender-based violence (GBV). Through a multifaceted strategy, the project seeks to end violence against women, advance gender equality, and empower survivors. Our goal is to establish a society that is safe and inclusive for everyone by putting advocacy campaigns, community-based interventions, and capacity-building programs into action. Target Beneficiaries include Survivors of GBV, Women, girls, and marginalized individuals who have experienced GBV and require immediate support and long-term assistance. Community members where both urban and rural communities affected by GBV, where awareness, prevention, and support services are necessary. And MDAs (Ministries, Departments and Agencies) which are government agencies, law enforcement, healthcare providers, educational institutions, and other stakeholders involved in addressing GBV.
Project Objectives:
 Campaigns and awareness sessions: Run awareness initiatives that question social norms, encourage respectful interactions, and inform communities about the origins and effects of GBV.
 Thrive to prevent GBV: Implement prevention initiatives targeting individuals, communities, and institutions to address the root causes of GBV and promote gender equality.
 Upkeep survivors report: Provide full support services to survivors of GBV, including medical, legal, emotional, and economic help.
 Strengthen communities and institutional actions against SGBV: Advocate for the strengthening/development and implementation of laws and regulations that protect and support survivors of GBV, while holding offenders accountable.
 Empower survivors, and community women and girls: Improve the socioeconomic empowerment of survivors (girls and women) by providing possibilities for entrepreneurship, resource access, and skill development.

1 - Needs

An assessment and analysis conducted by the Nigerian for Women Project (P161364), 2018 to date supported by World Bank Group revealed that Gender-based violence, or GBV, is one of the most oppressive forms of gender inequality, posing a fundamental barrier to the equal participation of women and men in social, economic, and political spheres. GBV includes intimate partner violence, non-partner sexual assault, female genital mutilation, sexual exploitation and abuse, child abuse, female infanticide, and child marriage. Such violence impedes gender equality and the achievement of a range of development outcomes. In Nigeria, like in other jurisdictions, GBV remains a challenge that significantly constrains women’s autonomy and opportunities. Conflict in Nigeria’s North East geopolitical zone has further contributed to a steep rise in violence targeted against women and children by Boko Haram. And in Yobe State is rural, and its inhabitants are primarily Muslim, monoethnic, and monolingual, with the majority of the population Kanuri, Hausa or Fulani; and the Boko Haram insurgency contributed towards perpetration of GBV in the State. This report uses the social ecological model as the basis to analyze prevalent GBV risks in Nigeria. It first describes the legislative and policy level frameworks associated with violence against women and girls as well as their implementation at the national and subnational levels. It then provides a detailed analysis of specific social norms that have created acceptance for or at least a lack of condemnation of GBV at the community level. Finally, based on field research, it delves deeper into some of the interpersonal and individual drivers of various forms of GBV in the selected communities.

 WHF will Conduct advocacy and Stakeholders mapping activity Identification and Engagement of Health Promoters Training for Health Promoters and relevant stakeholders Train healthcare professionals, police officers, and judicial personnel to sensitively respond to and support survivors of GBV. Conduct community-based awareness campaigns through workshops, seminars, and information sessions to promote understanding, empathy, and respect. Establish safe spaces and helplines to provide confidential support, counseling, and referrals for survivors of GBV. Strengthen/Develop and implement prevention programs in schools and workplaces to foster gender equality and challenge harmful social norms. Provision of visual aids against GBV/SGBV Facilitate legal aid clinics and empower survivors to access justice through legal awareness programs and pro-bono legal assistance. Provide economic empowerment opportunities aand skills training, access to resources, and entrepreneurship opportunities for survivors. Advocate for policy reforms and legislation to strengthen the legal framework for addressing GBV and ensuring survivor support. Establish partnerships with local organizations, civil society groups, and government agencies to create a coordinated response to GBV. Develop a robust monitoring and evaluation framework to track project progress, assess outcomes, and measure the impact of interventions. Conduct regular surveys, focus group discussions, and case studies to gather qualitative and quantitative data on changes in attitudes, behaviors, and access to services. Review project activities periodically, adjusting strategies as needed to ensure maximum effectiveness.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

WHF policies recognize that SGBV is a violation of human rights and seeks to mainstream work to reduce SGBV and protection into all programs. WHF has an internal gender policy, a SGBV policy and has minimum standards for gender in emergencies as a tool to measure consistency in promoting gender equality through interventions. The Organization trains its programme personnel in gender mainstreaming and has GBV and PSEA focal points that work closely together to mainstream gender into programmes and projects. All activities will incorporate considerations for gender and different needs of women, men, boys, and girls as well as people with disabilities.
