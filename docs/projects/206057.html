Project Summary

South Sudan's struggle with access to basic water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services stems from a chronic lack of investment in WASH infrastructure, recurring conflicts, and devastating floods that have left facilities in ruins. The WASH cluster estimates that out of the country's 12.44 million inhabitants, 8.5 million will require humanitarian assistance in 2024, with 5.5 million specifically targeted by the WASH cluster. Leer, Mayendit, Koch, and Rubkona in Unity state and Maiwut in Upper Nile state are among the worst-hit counties, facing a multitude of hazards that threaten to cripple their livelihoods. According to HNO 2023 (Inter-sectoral analysis), the limited access to WASH infrastructure coverage alone, such as water points and latrines, poses multisectoral impacts. The dire sanitation and water access conditions in the counties are expected to deteriorate further with the influx of returnee populations from the Sudan Crisis (OCHA Sudan Crisis-SITREP No. 16)
A recent Coalition for Humanity survey revealed that a staggering 73% of households in Leer and Mayendit already lack adequate sanitation facilities and resort to open defecation, a practice attributed to various factors. For instance, reconstruction of latrines is a constant struggle due to soil erosion and flooding, depleting household resources and energy. The market for affordable and durable sanitation facilities is underdeveloped, making it challenging for households to access suitable options. It is also evident that the absence of stable and sustainable livelihoods hinders households' ability to invest in latrine construction and maintenance which affects about 41% of households. Conflicts and acts of criminality have also led to the destruction of sanitation facilities, further exacerbating the sanitation crisis (Coalition for Humanity WASH KAP Survey June 2023)
Furthermore, 24% of households continue to source their drinking water directly from rivers and streams, exposing them to contamination and health risks. This highlights the urgent need for improved water infrastructure and access to clean water sources.
The proposed project will introduce several initiatives to enhance and save the lives of affected populations in the project area. These initiatives include the construction of flood-resistant latrines, the conversion of boreholes into solarized water yards in the community linked to nearby nutrition centers, the provision of life skills training to households, the distribution of WASH minimum kits for SAM and MAM cases, and the implementation of hygiene promotion campaigns across the project locations.
The project aligns seamlessly with the WASH cluster's strategic objectives and the Humanitarian Response Plan for 2024, which aim to
1. Contribute to reducing prevalence of malnutrition through integration with nutrition at community level and integration with nutrition at facility level
2. Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality through integration with health at both community and facility level (technical support).
3. Provide access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster-through delivery of services through static partners and mobile emergency response mechanism (EPR).
4. Disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WASH facility- through mainstreaming protection with a focus on GBV mitigation (safety audits) and disability inclusion.
5. Provide durable access to services in long-term IDP sites and communities chronically affected by disaster

1. Needs

The dire sanitation and water access conditions in the counties are deteriorating further due to influx of returnee populations from the Sudan Crisis (OCHA Sudan Crisis-SITREP No. 16). A staggering 73% of households in Leer and Mayendit already lack adequate sanitation facilities and resort to open defecation, a practice attributed to various factors. For instance, reconstruction of latrines is a constant struggle due to soil erosion and flooding, depleting household resources and energy. The market for affordable and durable sanitation facilities is underdeveloped, making it challenging for households to access suitable options. It is also evident that the absence of stable and sustainable livelihoods hinders households' ability to invest in latrine construction and maintenance which affects about 41% of households. Conflicts and acts of criminality have also led to the destruction of sanitation facilities, further exacerbating the sanitation crisis. Households (24%) continue to source their drinking water directly from rivers and streams, exposing them to contamination and health risks which highlights the urgent need for improved water infrastructure and access to clean water sources (Coalition for Humanity WASH KAP survey report June 2023). Malnutrition is a major concern in the counties with at least 26% of households having children with a slow growth rate which can be attributed to poor WASH conditions. An estimated 62.50% of the deaths among children under the age of 5 in Leer County is attributed to diarrheal diseases and lack of food (Coalition for Humanity Health Survey report, A gap Assessment, Leer County, February 2023). This project will seek to address malnutrition through increasing access to water at nutrition facility and its catchment areas. Water yards will be constructed, in the community to increase supply water in addition to distribution of WASH kits to SAM/MAM at nutrition facilities. Community members will be trained on life skills to increase their level of income and improve the general sanitation and hygiene conditions.

2. Project activities

Coalition for Humanity proposes the following activities. 1. Construction of flood resilient latrines-CH will construct permanent flood resilient latrines in high ground places where IDPs are settled as part of the initiative to providing durable solutions to recurrent floods in the project locations. The sanitation facilities will be designed to allow future desludging, so as to prevent year-in-year out reconstruction of sanitation facilities which is not cost effective. The sanitation facilities will be designed with additional features to ensure they are easy to clean, they are free from bad smell, free from flies and can be easily used by children. One door, in each block that will be constructed will be set aside, and fixed with commodes to support pregnant women, people living with disabilities, and the elderly. 2. Upgrading of boreholes into flood resilient solarized water yards-Coalition for Humanity will upgrade boreholes and network them to a minimum of 3 water kiosks in the community where they will serve schools, nutrition facilities and/or health facilities within the community periphery. These water kiosks will be raised as well, above the highest assessed flood water levels. They will be designed and constructed to be easily used by people living with disabilities, pregnant and lactating mothers as well as the elderly people. 3. Training of local artisans on construction of flood resilient latrines for households as part of market-based programming. Coalition for Humanity will integrate this activity with conditional cash distribution for WASH items where beneficiary households will be given vouchers to linked to purchase of locally produced latrine components which will have been produced by trained artisans, and they will also be able to purchase locally stocked hygiene items such as soap and MHM kits from local shops. Coalition for Humanity will train women how to make menstrual hygiene pads from locally available materials to reduce scarcity and ease the burden and pressure on the limited MHM kits from the pipeline. 4. Distribution of WASH minimum kits for SAM and MUM populations-Coalition for Humanity will integrate this response in nutrition facilities to SAM and MUM caregivers. 5. Hygiene promotion -Coalition for Humanity will sensitize the community on the benefits of good hygiene practices at household level.
