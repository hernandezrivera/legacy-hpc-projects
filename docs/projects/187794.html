Project Summary

Through this project, Dorcas and its partners will be providing food vouchers, food parcels and healthy meals to vulnerable population in Aleppo, Homs, rural Damascus and Idleb.
The food vouchers will be provided to 400 HH (400 in 2021 and 400 in 2022) from Aleppo and these families will get vouchers during 3 months, in addition Dorcas through its partner MSJM will provide 1,000 household with food parcels (1,000 each year).
Moreover, Dorcas will continue to provide daily healthy meals to more than 1,400 students attending private schools in Aleppo and activities at the 2 community centers run by the partner in close collaboration with Dorcas team.

4 - Needs

More than half of Syrian households spend 75% of their income on food, and 28% are adopting crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies. Year-on-year inflation hovering between 200 to 300% is heavily impacting purchasing power, as the country imports most of its staple needs, as well as essential agricultural inputs. An estimated 14.2 million people are in need of some form of food & agriculture assistance, representing 68.6% of the total population. At least 12.4 million Syrians are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million are considered severely food insecure. There is a sharp increase from last year, with an increase of 4.5 million people facing acute food insecurity in 2021 compared to 2020. The estimated sector PiN for 2021 – of food insecure plus those at risk of food insecurity – has increased from 9.8 million to 14.2 million. The global situation and unilateral coercive measures have also resulted resulted in a substantial decrease in remittances.Dorcas will provide food assistance through food vouchers or parcels since the MSNA highlights 12% out of 36% of respondents mentioning depending on humanitarian assistance to cover their basic needs in food, while 6% mentioned borrowing from better off households. The most urgently required food security interventions in the community were as follows; in-kind distribution of food baskets (25%) and cash/voucher assistance - unconditional (23%) and cooking fuel (16%).

5 - Implementation of activities

Dorcas will also provide food assistance through food vouchers since the MSNA highlights that 12% out of 36% of the respondents mentioning depending on humanitarian assistance to cover their basic needs in food, while 6% out of the 36% mentioned borrowing from better off households. Therefore, needs for food assistance remains high.In Aleppo city where the local markets are functional 400 families will be provided with vouchers to purchase food items, the preference for food vouchers is noticed due to difficulty in providing cash assistance because of the current limitations in the country mainly in cash withdrawal but also needs for approvals needed from authorities. This modality is chosen in response of the wish from the beneficiaries to choose the food items according to their preference and needs. In Aleppo city the food vouchers will be distributed through 6 different centers which are run by the local partners of Dorcas (3 times). These FBOs select the most vulnerable people from the community (according to pre set selection criteria) and are able stay in contact and follow up with the beneficiaries on their needs. In Idleb, Dorcas will support food insecure families by access to food. In 2022, the intervention will cover 1,000 food baskets for families that are mainly female headed and has no bread winner or source of income. In Homs and Rural Damascus, Dorcas will also provide food parcels (1,000 HH in each location).

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

This project will continue the work that Dorcas has been doing through its community centers. Dorcas’ community centers/partners are well-known & trusted; individuals and families understand that Dorcas has the ability to provide a multitude of protection services, as well as many humanitarian services like hygiene items and food parcels. Working in conversation with the community, Dorcas develops many proposals and activities based on information received from its feedback systems and the needs of the community expressed by those coming into the center. Dorcas’ two-way beneficiary feedback system was established in the community centers to ensure that beneficiary populations have a voice in the planning, activity implementation, and evaluation of the project. Feedback and suggestions are listened to as part of the protection risk monitoring and subsequently included in the implementation. This also includes a process to confidentially handle allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in an effort to ensure that beneficiaries are protected against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Dorcas ensures beneficiaries have multiple ways to provide feedback, including a hotline, text messages, emails, & box established within the centers. Dorcas’ long term presence within the community also ensures that the most vulnerable, such as the elderly, children, PWD, and widows are reached through its services. Dorcas also has been supporting its partners to implement PSEA strategy.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Beneficiaries and staff are at risk of covid-19 when implementing the activities. Dorcas/partners taking measures to prevent risks through provision of PPE materials to staff and beneficiaries but also awareness campaign, posters, pamphlets...Inflation is also a main risk. Dorcas will be reviewing the amount of the voucher after getting the donor'sapproval, the amount can change in order to be in line with the recommendations of the working group, this also happened in one of Dorcas project in 2020 when the amount was reviewed and adjusted. Assumptions: Enabled access to targeted areas is maintained with acceptable security conditions during the intervention. The political and economic situation of the country remains at a level allowing effective humanitarian action. The Covid-19 pandemic remains contained where the implementation will take place. Risks: Restrictions imposed by the Government of Syria on humanitarian interventions, including humanitarian access. Civil unrest in project locations prevents full geographic coverage as anticipated. Confirmed increasing number of cases and/or potential outbreak of Covid-19 in project locations. Significant in-flux of conflict affected populations into project locations leads to over stretch of project delivery. Mitigation: Dorcas/the partners will closely monitor any safety and security concerns in targeted areas and act upon its Risk Mitigation Plan. Dorcas/partners will ensure strong participation in field coo
