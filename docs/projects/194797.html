Project Summary

DRC aims to work alongside affected communities and local authorities to address protection issues and meet basic needs in food security and livelihoods, shelter and non-food items, and humanitarian mine action in Sudan. DRC will target Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), host communities and returnees in Central and South Darfur, Khartoum, and South Kordofan. In addition to direct and immediate humanitarian assistance, DRC remains committed to supporting communities in achieving durable solutions following years of conflict and displacement in the country and the region. Recognizing that durable solutions are not something that can be achieved in isolation, DRC’s strategy in Sudan is to build partnerships around durable solutions projects, working closely to offer holistic opportunities on durable returns, by assisting displaced and returnee communities.
Throughout its interventions, DRC works closely with national humanitarian actors and relevant Government of Sudan ministries to ensure that the activities planned are in line with government broader standards and plans as well as community needs, and that local duty bearers have the capacity to build upon the integrated program that DRC will implement and sustain its positive impacts.
**DRC has updated this HRP project to reflect the changes in context since conflict began in Sudan on 15 April 2023. DRC is launching multi-sector emergency response activities to support the sharp increase in IDPs in White Nile and Aj Jazirah states, which are receiving IDPs from Khartoum state (HRP/DTM May 2023). We are also planning expand operations in West Darfur state, where needs are high, and our assessments are ongoing to determine what is feasible in current context there.**

1 - Needs

The contextual analysis underpinning the proposed DRC (Danish Refugee Council) 2023 Integrated Response (multi-sector) programme for Sudan draws primarily on external sources such as: the Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan 2022 and Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), sector/cluster reports, which DRC cross-references with its internal sources including: DRC Sudan Strategic Program Document, regular situation reports, rapid assessments, etc. Sudan is classified among the least developed countries. According to OCHA’s most recent HNO, an estimated 15.8 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance in Sudan in 2023, which is a significant increase from 2022. There are approximately 3.7 million displaced people and 1.1 million refugees in Sudan. Humanitarian needs in Sudan are by no means limited to conflict-affected areas. Sudan faces competing humanitarian crises and formidable development challenges, leaving millions highly vulnerable and, at a broader level, threatening the country’s stability. 11 million people are expected to require emergency assistance and 11.7 million will need food and livelihood support in 2023. The Sudanese population’s ability to cope with climate and other shocks is severely undermined by the protracted economic crisis, which has caused hyperinflation and reduced purchasing power nationwide. Protests continue to occur across the country as people are pushed into further financial distress.Significant protection needs are observed in Central and South Darfur, Gedaref and South Kordofan, as well as in Khartoum. In Central and South Darfur and in South Kordofan, the scarcity of protection actors and functional national services providers is critical. In Khartoum, a host state for large numbers of refugees, IDPs and migrants, the resources available to address their complex needs fall far short of what is needed to mitigate the protection risks and vulnerabilities that result from a chronic lack of access to services, and difficulties in receiving adequate assistance and integration. Extensive known and suspected minefields and unexploded ordnance restricts access for communities in the affected states to land and basic services including health care, education, safe water sources and livelihoods, and has been a driver of forced displacement and intra- and inter-communal tensions for decades. OCHA reports in the 2023 HNO that there are more than 2M people in 416 hazardous areas in 38 localities in Sudan, including South Kordofan and Darfur. The Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) estimates that as of July 2022, there have been 2,495 casualties from mines and ERW, including 636 people killed and 1,859 injured in Sudan. However the true extent of accidents caused by mines and explosive ordnance is expected to be much higher due to under-reporting, particularly in remote and non-government-controlled areas. Sudan’s mine action program was nationalized in 2013, however, the political transition that followed the 2019 revolution, and the national priorities to establish peace and enable economic recovery signal the time for enhanced and coordinated mine action efforts. Equipping I/NGOs with response capacity in order to respond high FSL, S/NFI, HMA and protection needs remains critical for 2023. **DRC has updated this plan to reflect a sharp increase in emergency response activities following conflict that began in Khartoum and other areas of Sudan on 15 April 2023. This plan reflects needs described in the revised HRP which was released on 17 May 2023.**

2 - Activities/Outputs

In 2023, DRC Sudan will focus on four sectors (based on OCHA’s coordination clusters in Sudan) these are: Protection, Food Security & Livelihoods (FSL), Shelter & non-food items (SNFI), Humanitarian Mine Action (HMA). With regards to the protection program, DRC focusses on 3 main program pillars: community-based protection/PSS, case management, and protection monitoring, and includes activities which identify, mitigate and respond to protection needs and incidents. The GBV response will focus on establishing and/or rehabilitate women centers to complement wider protection programming with community outreach for awareness and sensitization and to provide individual case management services. DRC will select the locations of the space based on need and priorities of communities. In addition, DRC DRCs structures CBPN at the local community level, such as village or neighborhood, as opposed to at a larger locality level, thus increasing the buy-in and participation of local communities and taking into account local dynamics in a conflict sensitive manner. Dignity kit distribution is not limited to distribution but is coupled with sharing information on GBV, available GBV services, GBV helplines numbers, and messages on PSEA. Dignity kits distribution is entry point to raising awareness and reaching women and girls. In order to respond to food security and livelihood needs, food and in-kind distribution will be provided as well as agricultural support depending on the needs of each area. In Central Darfur, S/NFI kits will be distributed to respond to recent population displacement. HMA action will take place in South Kordofan, which according to OCHA has one of the highest concentrations of landmine contamination, and potentially in Central Darfur where old and new battlefields are contaminated by UXOs and landmines. HMA outputs will include land release through cancellation (90,000 m2+), 7,900 explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) beneficiaries demonstrate increased knowledge and knowledge retention, 1,080 EORE sessions and 21,600 direct EORE beneficiaries. DRC will also build capacity with national HMA actors. HMA activities will utilize the capacities of non-technical survey and multi-task teams to implement activities such as manual mine clearance, battle area clearance, and EORE to supported thousands of individuals in contaminated areas. Specific priorities for EORE will be determined based on a KAP survey done with NMAC; methodologies may include direct community-based risk education; house-to-house; school-based; community liaison; etc. Collectively the integrated intervention proposed will make a significant contribution to reducing humanitarian needs in line with HRP objectives. **This plan reflects an increase in certain activities based on the change in context and needs since 15 April 2023. DRC plans to deliver more emergency response activities than previously planned, including supporting in shelter and NFI, multi-purpose cash assistance, and additional protection in emergencies activities. This will include in Humanitarian Mine Action Risk Education.**

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing projects, DRC develops activities and selection criteria based on a context specific risk analysis and continued engagement with local communities. All activities are designed in consideration of the risks and vulnerabilities of local communities and target beneficiaries, and include components aimed at mitigating risk factors and strengthening protective factors in the community. Throughout implementation, DRC will conduct ongoing protection monitoring in order to identify new risks and vulnerabilities and assess the impact of our programming on the protection environment. While DRC has these structures in place, it will continue to provide training and capacity building opportunities for all staff around protection mainstreaming to ensure that there is not only a culture of protection mainstreaming but also the capacity to mainstream protection effectively. Specific activities and considerations include:- Targeting based on vulnerability and giving priority to vulnerable households such as women headed, elderly, etc. - Ensuring that modalities of support are the safest and most appropriate and accessible to caseloads. - Ensuring that protection maintains a protection desk at distributions to receive referrals. - Ensuring that MEAL has a functioning FCRM presence during all activities and especially at distributions. - Strong community engagement and CwW ahead of assistance provision so selection/targeting criteria is clearly understood prior to assistance provisions. - PDMs 3-4 weeks after assistance to confirm no unintended consequences materialized as a result of assistance and that people felt the way assistance was provided was appropriate to needs, safe, and effective.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to affected populations is a core component of DRC’s humanitarian response. The participation of the affected population in project activities has been ensured through needs assessments, household surveys, beneficiary registration events, distribution events, focus group discussions and key informant interview processes. In the different community engagement sessions, men, women, boys, and girls participate in the prioritization of needs and identification of the most vulnerable persons for support. As part of do-no-harm, DRC staff continuously receive training sessions on PSEA in humanitarian response. The aim of the training is to ensure that humanitarian support is delivered in a safe, accountable, confidential, appropriate, and inclusive environment. To further reinforce inclusive beneficiary participation in project activities, DRC’s comprehensive complaints, and feedback mechanism (FCRM) provides pathways through which community participants can channel their complaints and suggestions to program management. Such platforms include; a toll-free hotline, WhatsApp hotline, SMS helpline, static information desks, mobile protection desks, post distributions monitoring (PDM) surveys and assessments, focus group discussions, key informant interviews, and ad hoc walk-in meetings. All feedback received from the affected populations is treated with confidentiality, logged into the DRC feedback register, and categorized according to the level of sensitivity. Feedback on very sensitive matters is immediately referred to the protection sector and/or senior management for investigation and action within 72 hours, whilst non-sensitive feedback is referred to the programmatic sectors for investigation and response provided within two weeks’ utmost. In either case, the complainant is traced, and an appropriate response or feedback is provided on the subject matter raised. Where a complainant prefers anonymity, the feedback is provided to the general community where the issue was raised. A summarised FCRM report provides highlights of total feedback received by location and sector, and includes suggested recommendations for management attention. The established FCRM channels allow DRC the project management team to maintain dialogue with affected populations and address/modify project activities in a timely manner, facilitating accountability to vulnerable populations and ensuring that their needs, interests, concerns, and rights are at the center of management decision-making.
