Project Summary

In order to leave no one behind, humanitarian actions need to take widespread discrimination on the grounds of disability, gender and age into account, and proactively engage in removing barriers to equal opportunities. Reduce vulnerability and improve access of persons with disabilities ( PWD), mainly women with disabilities and older persons to humanitarian assistance. Despite the ratification of the UNCRPD in 2009 by the Syria Government and the adoption of IASC 2019 guidelines, several barriers continue to negatively affect PWD capacity to enjoy their rights. Evidence based data have been reported by the GAD protection analysis, conducted in 2020 by Syria Resilience Consortium (SRC). The organization’s action focuses on providing guidance on how to include PWD mainly women within humanitarian assistance and to overcome risks of increasing vulnerability of PWD.
The INGO is conducting regular analysis of barriers faced by PWD The analysis finding raised: i)lack of understanding of the identification process of PWD, ii) lack of clarity among key services providers and humanitarian actors regarding the PWD human rights; iii) lack of involvement of PWD in the design, provision and evaluation of services provision and in the delivery of the humanitarian assistance; iiv) lack of human, operational and financial resources to provide inclusive and accessible services and assistance; iv) negative beliefs, attitudes and practices limiting PWD participation in social life at the community and family level.
The organization acknowledges, among other factors generating inequalities and that need to be considered, that:
•Disability, gender and age as socially constructed norms can be observed in any human group, within which produce different outcomes between people;
•Disability, gender and age are key factors to understanding different people’s capacities, needs, and exposure to risks in any context or crisis.
The impact of inclusion is not only through the direct targets, we rely on increasing the understanding of disability, gender and age vulnerability risks, within communities and humanitarian actors, which will lead to include PWD, older persons and women with disabilities in social, cultural and socio economic activities. Moreover, all trained persons with disabilities will target wider communities, to improve inclusion principles, which will decrease vulnerability risks.
As for the calculation of targets, for example each PWD who is trained as a ToT on DGA inclusion, will conduct at least 5 awareness sessions within his\her communities (5 sessions* 10 persons).
Another reflect is the increase of % of PWD through actors projects. For example for partners who will be trained, we are aiming to increase the % of PWD among their projects up to 15% at least as some of our partners they only target between 4%-7% only.
As for monitoring and evaluation mechanism, the project is regularly monitored through several approaches, such as documentation of success stories, training plan, beneficiaries data, case management, pictures of training sessions, pre post-tests reports, partners action plan and follow up achievements, Knowledge and Attitude and Practice (KAP) surveys and beneficiaries feedback.

4 - Needs

According to the HNAP Disability Report1 conducted in all governorates in Syria, the average prevalence of persons with disabilities is of 27 percent among IDPs, returnees and residents. This study also found that around 52 percent of households have at least one person with disability, out of which 46 percent have two or more members with disabilities. Out of the households reporting to have a person with disability, 80 percent have insufficient income to meet their needs. In addition, other recent surveys show a comparative increase of women in the population, especially in the age group 20-35 years old, due to the conflict’s impact on mortality and migration. Also according to the HNAP, disability prevalence among women is 25 percent and 28 percent among men. Children and older persons (above 60 years old) were found to represent respectively 42 and 4 percent of the population in need of assistance in Syria. Out of those, according to the HNAP findings, 15 percent of children have a disability, and among older persons the average is of 92 percent. Moreover, according to HNO 2021 report (Data from December 2020) demonstrates that households with more than one member with a disability are nine per cent less likely to be able to meet their needs. A study conducted by the Syrian Resilience Consortium (SRC) found that persons with disabilities, especially women and girls with disabilities face additional difficulties to participate equally in their communities and have less decision making power at different levels –within and outside the household.

5 - Implementation of activities

Implementation activities Enhance inclusion of Persons with Disabilities (PwD) in society and humanitarian assistance through mutually supportive programming to increase their participation. Through the implementation of a twin-track approach, the logic of intervention will combine a package of activities aimed at empowering PWD from one hand; and building a more inclusive and accessible living environment at the community level, on another hand. Considering the technicality of the activities, the INGO will be implementing directly.Tailored support to PWD, local communities, humanitarian actors and services providers to promote a more inclusive living environment and improve inclusion and accessibility in the humanitarian action. In line with HNO/HRP 2021, PWD are considered among the most vulnerable groups disproportionally affected by the crisis. Their limited access to essential services, basic needs and livelihood opportunities has been reported by the organization studies. This is having a dramatic impact on PwD wellbeing with a further degradation of their already fragile living conditions. Thanks to a twin track approach, the project will be able to address key existing barriers and to promote increased access to comprehensive protection support. The package of activities will include: awareness sessions; tailored training plans aimed at empowering PwD knowledge on their rights as well as on existing tools to implement advocacy and awareness; trainings of humanitarian actors, services providers and other key stakeholders to improve PwD access to essential services, basic needs and livelihood opportunities; implementation of minor rehabilitations to remove existing infrastructural barriers in targeted facilities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

•The INGO applies its PSEA policy for the staff, partner staff and suppliers. Accountability measures are ensured through investigation and follow up of cases. The INGO also raises awareness on the INGO PSEA policy and its complaints and feedback mechanisms to follow up. As part of the inclusion mainstreaming strategy, training to the INGO staff and its partners to highlight specific vulnerabilities faced by persons with disabilities (GBV, sexual exploitation, etc.) is implemented. The INGO has a protection task force which ensures protection mainstreaming is optimized. Additionally, the referral task force also works towards improving protection referrals and its processes.•As part of its DGA mainstreaming strategy, INGO will also provide specific trainings on inclusive and gender balance staff recruitment, DGA inclusive practices, PSEAH and Child Protection policies, accessibility of structures using disability, gender and age sensitive standards and community awareness, and information sessions to ensure knowledge about rights and service information: staff is regularly trained on Humanitarian principles and how to interact, communicate and engage with people with various disabilities and needs. •Safe closure of facilities and protecting data: Field workers are trained on safe withdrawal and evacuation measures including ways to protect/destroy data. •Logistic and fund transfer issues will be mitigated by assessing transfer partners and by enhancing coordination with others INGOs. •Working through implementing partners: before becoming an implementing partner, each actor goes through a thorough assessment and vetting process. reporting is required, monitoring visits, feedback and response mechanism are in place.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Loss of access to intervention areas can be mitigate as the INGO developed an access strategy that is regularly reviewed and adapted to the context. The INGO also continuously monitors the security situation in its intervention areas and adapts its access strategy to the regular conflict analysis and how it affects its operations.• Lack of local community acceptance is mitigated through positive relations with the community. The INGO and its partners are well-perceived on the ground and always make sure to keep close coordination with the local authorities and maintain positive relations with the community. The INGO will also focus on increasing the community’s participation in its programs; setting in place community satisfaction surveys to measure the satisfaction of the community with its services. • Loss of funding is mitigated by the organization’s close communication with various donors, and direct negotiation for continuous funding. • The eventual spread-out of COVD-19 pandemic and its implications on the operational capacities, as per prior experience in 2020. To mitigate this risk, the organization and partners will ensure the following: i) continue exploring alternatives remote modalities of intervention ii) ensuring PPE and other hygiene items are available in targeted locations; iii) mainstreaming COVID-19 messaging in the overall package of activities; iv) monitoring the epidemic situation through the health cluster; v) ensure inclusion of DGA principles through response to epidemic.• The available budget reduction due to exchange rate loss is managed by the organization continuous use ofUSD value for money transfer and payment as well as, close monitoring the local market flow
