Project Summary

The project aims to revitalize the agricultural sector for mid-size framers and livestock breeders through supporting integrated cycles between agriculture and livestock sectors. These complementary interventions will increase agricultural production, protection of productive assets, and be restoring or creating income generation activities to prevent negative or irreversible coping mechanisms. Moreover, these interventions will reflect positively in revitalization of socio- economic opportunities for several sectors (seed production, flour and bread production, storage, marketing, local variety farming, overcome the sever lack of animal fodder and promoting the agriculture and livestock value chains. This project will address the key problems related to food security in the rural communities by creation-integrated solutions through the rehabilitation of the agricultural sector (plant and animal) through several complementary activities including supporting of small and mid-size framers and livestock breeders.

Needs

According to key messages of HNO report 2019, the proportion of people who are food insecure at national level is 5 percentage less compared to the 2017 HNO. This can be attributed to the significant scale of food and livelihoods/agriculture assistance provided by Food and Agriculture Sector partners that accounted for 5.1 million people being reached on average on a monthly basis with food, and 2.4 million people reached with livelihoods and agriculture assistance from January to October 2018. The agriculture sector is a major contributor to GDP with more than 25 to 30 per cent of Syria’s GDP reliant on this sector, and it is also producing food for more than half of the population. For these reasons, saving, restoring and protecting agricultural and related livelihoods and value chains will be critical to increase the self-reliance and strengthen the resilience of Syrians. On the other side, Unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods has led to irreversible coping strategies. In addition, livestock production is also further constrained due to a reduction of livestock number, in comparison to the last comprehensive livestock census conducted in 2010. According to the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform, sheep numbers fell by 45 per cent, goat numbers by 30 per cent, cattle numbers by 40 per cent, and poultry numbers by 55 per cent during the first three years of the crisis. The main challenges continue to be high fodder prices and insufficient coverage of veterinary services.

Implementation of activities

This project aims to address key problems related to the agricultural sector (plant and animal) through several complementary activities. 1-Support Plant production cycle through the followings activities : • Supporting of wheat farming ( distribution of wheat seeds, fertilizers and supplementary irrigation ), sufficient for 5 dununs of each BNF• Intercropping with winter vegetable crops (fava beans, chickpeas and peas) sufficient to farm 3 dununs for each BNF• Home- gardening (summer and winter vegetable): both the host and IDPs HHS will be supported with seeds and fertilizer to plant 0.2 dunum for summer and winter home gardening activities • ToT training on the best agricultural practices for wheat, legume and home gardening activities In order to revitalize livestock activities, Ihsan looks to provide livestock kit for small and mid-size livestock breeders (Fodder, vaccination through veterinary mobile clinic ) - To confirm sustainable cycle between livestock and plant production in the targeted communities, Ihsan will support farming of two types of forage crops (barley and vetch). This support will include distribution of seeds and fertilizers of both vetch and barely to plant 5 dunums of each crop. - Providing technical training on the best practices for livestock breeding.

Protection Risk Analysis

This project aims to preserve and restore the livelihoods of affected communities while stimulating economic recovery through supporting of agricultural and livestock activities. Poverty, food and livelihoods insecurity, economic inequalities and conflicts over the control over resources exacerbate the risk and vulnerability environment for GBV. Livelihoods and protection can be linked in practice, both at the level of analysis and action. The main risks that may be occurred during the implementation of this project are gender-based violence and sexual exploitation. One of the key activities in addressing GBV is creating awareness sessions about the issues surrounding it. Gender training, information and communication campaigns are crucial factors to raise awareness of gender-specific issues in humanitarian interventions – including access to food, agricultural and livestock activities. Interventions in support of information and gender awareness raising may include: information dissemination and basic training amongst community and opinion leaders about issues of gender, power and GBV to raise their understanding of and responsiveness to the different needs of women, men, girls and boys; and training in roles and responsibilities amongst NGOs, their partners, refugees, IDPs, and host communities to promote the respect of accountability principles.
