Project Summary

This project will target communities in Yunusari and Geidam local governments that are severely hit by acute malnutrition in Yobe State. The primary focus of the project is to promote the prevention and management of SAM cases through community mobilization to improve the health and well-being of children under five years, pregnant and lactating women and reduce morbidity and mortality associated with global acute malnutrition. This would be achieved through building the capacity of community members on IYCF practices at the community level and at the heath facility, also the community nutrition mobilizers will support in identification and referral of SAM cases to OTP and Stabilization centers for treatment. The project will also support to build the capacity of community members on the mother MUAC approach in terms of knowledge and skills to use the MUAC (mid-upper arm circumference) tapes to correctly screen, timely identify and appropriately refer SAM/MAM cases for treatment.
This will also engage and empower the communities with an enabling structure through the support groups that allows them to increase control over their own health by continuously monitoring, identifying and referring new cases of acute malnutrition among those vulnerable groups for treatment. Also, the project will support capacity-building for government health care providers and the provision of personal protective equipment to health facilities to promote effective service delivery curb the spread of Corona Virus.

1 - Needs

The protracted north-east conflict continues to directly worsen the affected population’s nutritional status, especially the most vulnerable and those in inaccessible locations. About 1.5 million people in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states are in need of nutrition assistance out of which 0.6 million are women and 0.69million are children (Humanitarian Response Plan, 2021). Poor nutrition in the first two years of a child’s life can lead to stunted growth, an irreversible condition that is associated with impaired cognitive ability and reduced school and work performance and productivity. Malnutrition during the first five years of a child life increases the risk of exposure to non-communicable diseases in adulthood (UNICEF 2021).Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months are on the increase across the BAY states when compared year-on-year: according to the ninth round of the NFSS, the GAM prevalence in Yobe is 13.6% as of October 2020. However, a recent survey by the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance (NFSS) Round 10, in September 2021, indicates a higher combined prevalence of global malnutrition (using WHZ, MUAC and edema) in Yobe state, which is between 14.7 to 18.5% (above WHO emergency threshold), while the prevalence of severe acute malnutrition is between 3.7% to 5.6%, with higher trends among boys (NFSS R10 Data sheet, 2021). Most specifically according to the current data by NFSS R10, the GAM rate for Geidam LGAs is between 16.0%-24.1%, while Yunusari has between 16.5%-24.4% The crisis has also exacerbated pre-existing conditions of malnutrition, driven by a number of inter-related factors. These include weak health infrastructures; poor infant and young-child feeding (IYCF) practices; limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; the prevalence of water-borne diseases; poor hygiene conditions; and seasonal food insecurity (humanitarian needs overview 2021). Data from the Nutrition PiN and target 2021 reveals that the population of children with SAM burden are as follows- Geidam (10768), and Yunusari (4423). While the population of pregnant and affected women (PLW) affected are as follows- Geidam (16,965), Yusufari (13,744). This pre-existing malnutrition situation is largely attributed to inter-related factors such as the weak health infrastructures; poor infant and young-child feeding (IYCF) practices; limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; the prevalence of water-borne diseases as seen this year within the rainy seasons in Yobe state; poor hygiene conditions; seasonal food insecurity and reduction in essential nutrition service coverage (due to COVID-19) since 2020. This is substantiated by the Nutrition Pin and target data 2021 highlighting IYCF burden as follows-Yunusari (10308), Geidam (11197).

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Advocacy visits and engagement with key stakeholders in the community (State and Local Government Officials, State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA), Department of Primary Health, Local Government Nutrition Officer, Traditional and Religious leaders. CCDRN carryout advocacy visits to relevant stakeholders to introduce the project and seek for collaboration and partnership. Activity 2: Training of 60 Community Nutrition Mobilizers, 10 Health workers, and 30 lead mothers/fathers on MUAC screening, SBCC-strategies, and community mobilization, to promote and support IYCF services at health facility and community levels.The CNMs would be jointly selected by the LGA Primary healthcare department and transition to Community Health Influencers and Promoter and Services (CHIPS). Activity 3: MUAC screening to identify and refer malnourished children (6-59 months), pregnant and lactating women. MUAC screening will be conducted by community health workers at facilities and CNMs through regular house-to-house screening and Family/Mother MUAC approach. Activity 4: Targeted supplementary feeding programme including Tom Brown and Porridge mum approaches: Distribution of nutrition food supplements/nutrient-dense ingredients-tom brown at community/health facility for children 6-23 months with MAM.Tom brown -maize, groundnut, soya bean, Dry fish, Cornflour, Baobab leaves, groundnut, millet, dates).This would be given accompanied by food preparation demonstrations to educate on preparation methods. Activity 5: Establish 60 community support groups for IYCF services (57 Mother support groups (MtMSG) and 3 father support groups (FtFSG) across Yunusari and Geidam LGAs.These groups would include mothers, fathers, caregivers and ensure the inclusion of persons with disability. Activity 6: Promotion of Family/Mother MUAC approach: 1800 caregivers trained on family MUAC approach, IYCF while integrating Covid-19 messages so caregivers are able to self-screen their own children and refer appropriately. Activity 7: Hold bi-weekly meetings with Mother-to-mother and father-to-father support groups, with messages on maternal and child nutrition, hygiene promotion, immunization, for 7 months in Yunusari and Geidam LGAs of Yobe State with support from Lead mothers/fathers and CNMs.Conduct monthly cooking demonstrations for support groups for 5 months, using nutrient-dense supplementary foods made from locally available ingredients. Activity 8: provision of small-scale support to 150 mothers and fathers on backyard gardening/kitchen gardening to promote homestead food production, enhance household food security and dietary diversity.Inputs such as millet/sorghum/maize, groundnut, and assorted vegetable; hoes and spraying cans would be provided, with training, mentorship, and monitoring for a period of 3-6 months. Activity 9: Procurement of personal protective equipment (PPEs) including masks, gloves, protective gears, hands-sanitizers, soap, and hand washstations for frontline GBV caseworkers to 6 supported health facilities.This includes 5 packs each of face masks, hand gloves, hand sanitizers and soap per health facility. Activity 10: GBV prevention: Provide integrated training on GBV prevention for CNM, Health workers, lead mother/father; Integrate GBV prevention awareness during IYCF and Support group activities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CCDRN is committed to the safeguarding and protection of children, women, men, boys, girls, and persons with disabilities. As its protection approach is guided by the humanitarian principles of Do No Harm, and the SPHERE Core Standards and Protection Principles.Being that this project involves the engagement of children, women, men and persons with disabilities, as our standard practice, all staff, volunteers and vendors engaged shall undergo training on CCDRN code of conduct, child safeguarding, protection, prevention of SEA and all forms of GBV, with all signing their commitments to the organization's code of conducts. CCRN would ensure activities are conducted in safe locations that do not pose a threat/endanger the lives of the target beneficiaries. The following strategies will be adopted to ensure the core principles of Protection are mainstreamed throughout the project: 1. CCDRN will identify suitable venues to be used as distribution sites where it can be easily accessed by the beneficiaries,2. Priority will be given to the most vulnerable individuals and groups throughout the targeting and distribution of assistance3. Appropriate complaints and feedback mechanisms will be set up in the communities such as the complaints desk, toll-free lines, through which affected populations seek for clarification or lay complaints, other structures already established in the community such as the Project management committees, Food community, the support group will also support.4. The capacity of community members, community health workers will be strengthened through training MUAC screening, referral, GBV, COVID-19 prevention to promote resilience prevent and mitigate GBV, and by enabling survivors and those at risk of GBV to access care and support.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

AAP is an integrated approach that facilitates the accountability of donors and partner organizations to the affected population. It also has great potential to improve efficiency and effectiveness of the humanitarian response by creating greater transparency and a platform to facilitate full engagement of the affected population. This will create a better understanding of problems on the ground and the formulation of humanitarian actions and plans that respond to real needs and build the resilience of affected communities.Accountability is part of CCDRN’s core values and is the main component of the organization’s quality framework and management operating standards. CCDRN shall ensure accountability through the following mechanisms:• Conduct advocacy and sensitization to the targeted communities and wards throughout the project’s implementation, ensuring information is shared on project activities, requirements, procedures, target beneficiaries etc.• More so CCDRN shall hold quarterly town hall meetings with community stakeholders to get feedback on project activities and recommendations.• CCDRN shall establish feedback and response mechanisms across the LGAs-communities through (a)the use of help desks during crucial activities to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries and concerned community members (b) the use of CCDRN toll-free hotline (08034241293) which will be managed by CCDRN CRM focal persons to receive feedback/complaints/recommendations; provide resolution or referrals as appropriate. This will be made available to community members and posted in each community, to enable the community present complaints and/or feedback.• Information from the accountability feedback systems is analyzed on a monthly basis and shared with the senior management team for discussion and decision making to adjust program activities in response to the local context, proposal development and knowledge generation.
