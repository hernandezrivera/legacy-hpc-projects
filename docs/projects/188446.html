Project Summary

the situation in NE of Syria remains risky, over 70% are considered people in acute need of life-saving assistance. With the COVID-19 pandemic, economic crisis and ongoing hostilities, the number of people relying on humanitarian aid to address survival and basic needs is increasing.
Long-term damage to civilian infrastructure has led to a direct negative impact on communities’ access to services. With the economic crisis and devaluation of the SYP, over 90% of the Syrian population are living under the poverty. Access to livelihood opportunities and income generating activities are limited, especially for vulnerable people living in rural areas and IDP sites.
Additionally, the water levels of the Euphrates River remain critically low, limiting the access to water for people living in eastern area such Raqqa and Der El-Zoor. Low rainfall and damages to water stations and networks have further aggravated the reliable access to water for households and communal use.
in response to longer-term solutions to the provision of water supply, and other critical sanitation and hygiene services of the affected populations in North-East Syria to reinforce the most urgent life-saving interventions, Amal Organization aims to enhance the long-term WASH infrastructure. This intervention targets both residents and IDPs in host communities in NE of Syria which is considered a highly prioritized and underserved area that is facing multiple challenges in light of unseasonably low levels of rainfall as well as sanitation services. thus, the planned intervention will respond to the needs of 170,512 individuals (90,712 IDPs, 79,800 residents) through the following activities:
- Rehabilitation and maintenance of 4 Water Stations in Raqqa and Der-Ez-Zor ex. (Hora, Mansora) stations and linkage with water network and connecting the water station to the public electrical grid as well as installing solar power system which will contribute to reducing the monthly operational cost by approximately 45 percent, rehabilitation and maintenance of irrigation water canals This will benefit a population in NE of Syria 170,512 individuals (90,712 IDPs, 79,800 residents).
- Establishment of 3 wastewater treatment plants (Anaerobic Baffled Reactor ABR), 1 in Der-Ez-Zor and 2 in Raqqa This component will benefit the 3 communities with 60,000 HH in total by reducing water-borne diseases which will be improving the public health situation also the treated wastewater for irrigation purposes for the local farmers. In consequence, the whole community will benefit from this component.
- Expansion and rehabilitation of sewerage networks in NE of Syria, to ensure safe access to sanitation infrastructure for the whole community members both IDPs and host communities.
- Supporting the regular solid waste management through operation, and maintenance (O&M) for current vehicles, distribution of garbage bins, and cleaning equipment. This component will benefit the target communities.
- Rehabilitation and maintenance of existing latrines, showers as well as heating of bath spaces using the solar system for the target collective centers which will be used for lighting too, in addition to providing water tanks for the IDP's families residing for assumption collective centers.
- Control of the spread of COVID-19 through RCCE and provision of soap for handwashing for the most vulnerable and subjected to infection focusing on PWD, elderly, PLW, women-headed households, and those who have chronical diseases and children in the schools who mainly transmit the infection to their families.
Consequently, the aforementioned components will help the affected population in target communities to avoid adaption of negative coping strategies in terms of having access to safe water and other critical sanitation services, and they will directly feed the WASH cluster objectives through support to water, sanitation/sewage, and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services affect people.

4 - Needs

he scale, severity, and complexity of the humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain extensive. This is the result of continued conflict, and new and protracted displacement, according to HNO 2022. 13.2 M people in Syria are in need of HA. The same source reported that "Water trucking services are the only source of water for 73% of IDP site residents and only 24% HHs are connected to sewer networks, indicating a continued need for medium-term investment in IDP sites infrastructure to provide more sustainable, affordable, and safer way to supply water and dispose wastewater." All the above mentioned may lead the BNFs to adopt negative coping mechanism in order to meet their water needs, in addition, to increase of water-borne diseases and increased risk of COVID19, thus the HHs will be forced to continue relying on water trucking where the cost might reach to 10% of the monthly HHs income. So, the proposed action’s components will complement each other’s to have comprehensive WASH services to the most affected communities in NES and prioritized by WASH cluster. On the one hand, this intervention relies mostly on ensuring sustainable and long-term solutions through installing solar power systems which will highly decrease the operational costs, and construction of wastewater treatment plant that will enhance the public health of community members and help farmers to irrigate their lands in light of global water crises, and these activities are in line with WASH cluster.

5 - Implementation of activities

Initially, Amal will announce vacancies to attract expertise to optimally achieve the project's objectives. After employment, the terms of the MoU will be agreed upon and signed with the local councils. Amal will receive work sites to begin preparing for the next stage. The technical specification, drawings, other documentation will be organized for the tender announcements, and after the bids are opened and the most appropriate supplier selected and signed the contract with him, the worksite will be handed over to the winning contractor, to start work under the supervision of the engineers in addition to the monitoring of MEAL team to ensure the optimal completion of the work for all activities. A plan for closure and withdrawal from the community will be prepared after the project ends without leaving negative effects on the community as a result of the withdrawal, and the local council will hand over all the sites where the project activities have been implemented and develop a plan with them to ensure continuity.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Amal will work to generalize the four principles of protection, the aim of which is to avoid, reduce and mitigate the risks associated with the provision of humanitarian aid. Amal organization will work to: -Giving priority to safety and dignity and avoiding harm: Amal will ensure that the services provided are inaccessible and safe places for all age groups. Amal also worked on analyzing the protection risk matrix for all project activities to ensure that individuals are not harmed during or during the implementation of the project activities. Amal will also employ its teams of both sexes and will encourage women to apply for the required vacancies to ensure and observe the customs and traditions in the local community. - Adhere to an organization’s code of conduct to protect people from sexual exploitation and abuse: Amal has strict policies and procedures related to sexual assault and harassment, child protection policy and code of conduct. Amal will work to train all its teams on these policies and circulate them to them and ensure that all their teams sign the codes of conduct and ensure that it’s is not violated. -Accountability: Increase transparency, secure feedback and complaints mechanisms, reach it easily, and encourage people to reach it Consultation with all segments of society to reach the marginalized. -Participation and Empowerment Amal will ensure the participation of all segments of society in its programs from the beginning of the design until the closure.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The organization has strict policies and procedures related to sexual assault and harassment, child protection policy and code of conduct. The organization will work to train all its teams on these policies and circulate them to them and ensure that all their teams sign the codes of conduct and ensure that it’s is not violated. Emphasis will also be placed on the necessity of educating the beneficiaries and reporting cases of the exploitation of women or children by humanitarian workers during distribution operations. We will also work to activate the complaints channels well and train the BNFs on them. The employees will be assured that there are strict procedures in the event of any kind of exploitation, and this matter will never be tolerated.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions 1: The security situation remains stable in the targeted area. 2: Needed materials and equipment are available in the local markets with good quality and reasonable prices. 3: The land of the plant is available and free from HLP risks 4: the needed materials for the water station rehabilitation are available in good quality and at reasonable prices. 5: Amal has skilled staff who can manage such components. Risk1: Closure of Boarders: the majority of items and materials in the local markets mainly for water system rehabilitation are imported from Turkey. Mitigation1: Amal Organization has previous experience in such procurement processes and will ensure to commence the procurement immediately after the signature of the agreement. Risk2: Failure in construction of the intended ABR due to lack of the needed experience Mitigation2: Amal has a comprehensive technical study for the intended activity, and has the technical staff who have implemented similar projects. Risk3: HLP is not clear of the land where the plant will be constructed Mitigation3: Amal has already got a letter from the water unit that claims to offer clean land for free that belongs to the local council. Risk4: Escalation in Military Operation will lead to suspension of the activities Mitigation4: Close coordination with the S&S department to develop a contingency plan. Risk5: Theft of equipment like the generator and solar systems: Mitigation5: there will be a fence and guards.
