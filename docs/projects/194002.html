Project Summary

Through this project, FPDO will be providing life-saving assistance and protection in localities that have received additional refugees through secondary displacement from other localities around Sudan.
The project will continue addressing the needs of refugees who had already been residing in refugee camps/gathering areas of the targeted states prior to the crisis through the provision of emergency protection services and referrals; NFI kits, emergency shelter (family tents, plastic sheets, upgrading of collective shelter and cash). To address the food insecurity faced by the refugees and their host communities (out-of-camp), the organization will provide them with food assistance (in-kind or cash) in addition to maternal and young child nutrition services and Infant and Young Child Feeding practices among under five children scaled-up interventions for children and women. The project will also provide WASH support in the form of emergency latrines/showers (maintenance), hygiene items to families and at risks groups, hygiene promotion interventions and water resources maintenance. Capacity building and refresher training
(remotely as necessary) for GBV risk mitigation and response interventions to support the established humanitarian response infrastructures.
The project intends to target 10,365 as per 2,677South Sudanese Refugees in White Nile and 1,148 South Kordofan; 4,640 Ethiopian Refugees and host communities in Kassala and Gadaref and1,900 other Refugees in Central Darfur.

1 - Needs

Sudan hosts one of the largest refugee populations in Africa, with 1.14 million refugees and asylum seekers in 2022. The fighting in Sudan has increased vulnerabilities of refugees and asylum seekers across Sudan. It has caused secondary movements of refugees and asylum seekers, including South Sudanese, Eritreans, Ethiopians, Syrians and other nationalities from Khartoum to White Nile, Kassala, Port Sudan, Aj Jazirah, Gedaref and other states. Breakdown in national infrastructure and service systems exacerbates pressure on local resources and available services, contributing to growing needs in locations hosting refugees and registering new refugee arrivals fleeing conflict hotspot areas. The primary affected locations include Aj Jabalain, As Salam / Ar Rawat, White Nile; Reifi Wad Elhilaiw, Kassala and Galabat Ash-Shargiah and Al Fashaga, Gedaref.Out-of-camp refugees in some of the targeted states, especially in Azum ,Central Darfur, are unable to pursue secondary movement due to lack of resources or other vulnerability with few options of available relief.

The project is supporting safer methods of disposal through the establishment. The project is supporting safer methods of disposal through the establishment of proper waste pit disposal. 6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

FPDO practices safe programming across all its projects through training of project staff to be able to engage with communities in identifying protection issues for the different gender and age groups. FPDO`s Do No Harm approach ensures the principle of non-discrimination is strongly implemented within all projects. The community-based approach will ensure beneficiary selection criteria is clear, and will prioritize the highly vulnerable. FPDO will also ensure protection is mainstreamed within implementation through community FGD and consultation with key female community leaders on their needs and concerns, and ensure equality of access for those with mobility constraints or special needs. Through participation and consultation, FPDO capitalizes on people’s knowledge of the risks that they face and their capacity to address these risks. This leads to the development and reinforcement of the self-protection capacities and resilience of communities, mitigating risks of future harm.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

FPDO will implement the project on participatory and community based approaches. Regular meetings and information sharing activities will be delivered to community to inform about project services. Project will highly consider best interest of women and children when handling, sharing data and information about identified cases. Through M&E team will evaluate and adapt interventions to mitigate SEA/GBV risks and ensure that SEA/GBV risks are not being created or exacerbated through the implementation of response activities. FPDO will organize meetings with community leaders and other relevant stakeholders to present the activities and explain the accountability and feedback measures. FPDO will present the different ways to communicate issues (special complaints during distribution, direct contact with FPDO staff, visit FPDO office, complaint boxes, etc.). When a complaint is received by FPDO, it is recorded by the M&E officer and sent to sector managers or Country Director (according to degree of severity) to review. Following internal review of the information FPDO ensures to close the feedback loop and provide (where possible) the community member or group with a clear response and outcome.
