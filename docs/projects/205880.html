Project Summary

The recurrent instabilities in most parts of South Sudan both political and inter-communal conflicts, coupled with migration of refugees and returnees in addition to the effect of floods including the limited food security and livelihood opportunities result in the displacement of communities and the economic regression exerting much stress on already limited WASH services across South Sudan.
PAH experience in successfully implementing both static and rapid responses various sectors will use its strong WASH expertise to robustly respond with emergency lifesaving-assistance to vulnerable communities across South Sudan by improving access to portable water, sanitation services and the knowledge and capacity for the affected communities for a better wellbeing.
PAH will prioritize the vulnerable people affected by high level of WASH vulnerability especially in locations faced with IPC levels 3, 4 and 5, displaced people due to a variety of factors, returnees and host communities with over-stretched resources due to migration of IDPs, in locations with outbreaks waterborne disease such as cholera, HEV, and where refugees and returnee refugees have settled or in transit to their resettlement destinations. To improve the access to clean and safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene plus other domestic uses, PAH will conduct repair/rehabilitation and construction of water points mainly boreholes, water yards, shallow wells, rain water harvesting systems etc in the affected communities as well as in institutions especially nutrition and health facilities for integration of the response. PAH will prioritize sanitation at the institutional level, while at community level, PAH will encourage community participatory approach at community and household levels for sustainability as well as exit strategy. To enable a positive behaviour change of the affected population and the sustainability of the operation and maintenance of the WASH infrastructure, PAH will design a community led hygiene and health promotion messaging, combined with protection, health, nutrition and other cross-sectoral topics are integrated in the messaging. PAH will chose from the affected population people who will be trained as hygiene promoters, water management committees, sanitation committees, pump mechanics etc and engage them within the community.
PAH will ensure accountability to affected population (AAP) is key in its programming by ensuring that the beneficiaries are aware of the services at all stages and can report complaints through established complaints and feedback committees established within their community. Recognizing the cross-cutting nature of WASH, PAH will integrate the WASH with other sectors by providing dignity kits and NFIs, Protection mainstreaming etc to ensure that exploitation of the beneficiates especially the women and girls is minimized.
PAH, were feasible, will implement flood resilient infrastructure construction, such as elevated platform boreholes, community latrines and will encourage construction of flood resilient household level latrines, with use of local materials.

1. Needs

Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes persist across South Sudan despite the start of the green harvest and some stabilization in cereal prices in unimodal areas, albeit at above-average levels. This persistence is driven by ongoing sporadic conflict, underlying poor macro-economic conditions, limited income-generating opportunities, slow livelihood recovery prospects, and localized poor harvests. As PAH responses in both static and mobile modality the submitted proposal refers to the general needs of the country due to possibility of mobile responses in multiple locations. According to the latest HNO - 2024, more than 8.5 million people need multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance in South Sudan.In the northern counties bordering Sudan, the influx of South Sudanese returnees continues to drive high humanitarian needs, putting additional burden on the host households who are sharing scarce resources with the newly arrived. Canal/Pigi and Fangak of northern Jonglei, and Fashoda and Panyikang of Upper Nile remain of high concern, where some households are likely still in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in the latter two counties due to multiple years of asset and livelihood erosion exacerbated by the arrival of South Sudanese returnees and conflict-related disruption to trade flow with Sudan. Sporadic attacks and road ambushes continue in several areas of South Sudan, leading to disruption of livelihood activities and trade flows, as well as loss of lives in some incidents. late August and into early September, inter-communal counterattacks were reported in Gogrial East of Warrap, as well as road ambushes along Torit-Kapoeta road in Eastern Equatoria. Key informants have also continued to report new incidents of looting of household food stocks and assets in Central Equatoria, notably in Wudabi payam of Morobo County; Mugwo, Otogo, Lasu, and Tore payams of Yei County; and Wonduruba and Mukaya payams in Lainya County. The killing of a trader in Yambio County of Western Equatoria and the attempted killing of a local chief in Lobonok payam of Juba County in Central Equatoria are likely to prompt retaliatory attacks and tensions within the communities, disrupting household mobility and trade flows. Meanwhile, rising community tension over land resources and looting of cattle The depreciating SSP coupled with the continued high prices of staple food are affecting household access to food and non-food commodities.

2. Project activities

1. Rehabilitation of existing water points. PAH will target water points at the community level, with special attention to point in Nutrition facilities. In grazing areas water points will be designed in a manner to ensure safety for people and animals. In areas with high food insecurity PAH will assess the irrigation practices and possibilities and may give instructions and encourage people for sustainable and safe solutions for watering the crops. This will provide synergy between WASH and food access. 2. Establishment of new water points. New water points development will be considered in case of communities, whose water points are overcrowded, there is no water point, however the community is willing to stay in this place. New water points will also be established in permanent/semi-permanent IDP location depending on the need and population. The water points may be developed/upgraded to solar powered water yards. For both new and rehabilitated points the water user committees will be trained and equipped to ensure sustainability and maintenance cost will be instituted. Pump mechanics will be trained locally to ensure urgent repairs in case of break downs. The water points will be raised to avoid inflow of contaminated water from floods. 3. PAH will consider construction of new or improving existing sanitation facilities at local institutions, at crowded settings. PAH will establish gender and disability segregated sanitation facilities provided with proper doors and inside locks to enable privacy and mitigate risk of GBV. For a sustainable sanitation approach in IDP, host community, refugee and returnee settings, PAH will form and train sanitation committees selected from across the villages. The provision of the sanitation kits will be accompanied with awareness on the importance of latrine use, the dangers of open defecation etc. The sanitation facilities will be handed over to the institutions management and monitored to ensure sustainability. 4. Flood resilient constructions solutions will be implemented for both new/rehabilitated boreholes and sanitation facilities. PAH has designed mentioned facilities, including household level latrines to be constructed with a use of local material. 5. Providing WASH NFIs. Where possible, the cash based intervention modality will be implemented. As a member of CASH working group and JMMI initiative PAH will monitor closely the market situation. Provision of solar lamps, dignity/hygiene kits and soap will be ensured for those most at risk of GBV. PAH will provide vouchers for soap which will cover three months’ supply. Depending on availability, items will be procured from the local markets in the project locations. The market and risks assessment will be conducted to later decide if vouchers modality can be used. 5. Community awareness on hygiene, sanitation, domestic hygiene etc. will be delivered to the community at the HH level, including monitoring of implementing the measures, with special focus on mother and care-taker of severely malnourished child. For sustainability of water treatment. PAH will provide protection training and awareness raising to community hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committees’ members, women groups and others. PAH will work closely with Nutrition/Health and FSL partners to combine the project with their activities. Pah will provide trainings for Nutrition staff. 6. Distribution of WASH NFIs. PAH, relying on core pipeline and local market for cash voucher assistance will distribute core WASH NFIs. ensuring meaningful access - separate queues for elders, PWD and pregnant women. Carriers will be considered for people with limited mobility. 7. PAH will conduct WASH assessments with protection analysis using risk equation in cooperation with clusters and NGOs to provide platform for gap analysis and needs.
