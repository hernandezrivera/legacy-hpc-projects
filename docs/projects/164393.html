Project Summary

The project aims to deliver sustained assistance to the most chronically food insecure. It will focus primarily in Raqqa city, informal IDP settlements in multiple governorates while also increasing the provision of assistance to conflict affected areas along the Euphrates River in Deir-ez-Zor Governorate. This will be done in coordination with other food security partners in NES. The project will consist primarily of two main modalities of food security assistance dry food rations and food vouchers and will also include a complimentary activity comprising the provision of agricultural vouchers.

4 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in many parts of North East Syria remains dire . In terms of food security, an average of 42% of the population in North East Syria has been identified as severely food insecure and with limited access to livelihoods coping strategies (agricultural inputs, home-based food production and income-generating activities). The 2020 HNO for Syria reports that the number of people who are food insecure is increasing and is currently at 7.9 million people. Almost a decade into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are conflict and protracted displacement, loss or lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity and economic sanctions on the country. These factors have resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, soaring prices and inflation, contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous livelihood depletion for the most vulnerable. This is evident from the recent market trends which indicate that the national average price of a standard reference food basket at USD 77 based on formal exchange rates is more than 8 times higher than pre-crisis levels. There are still large gaps in coverage amongst INGOs, particularly amongst long-term IDP communities (in urban Manbij, Tabqa and in rural informal camps) and recent returnees in Raqqa city. Despite scaled-up food security assistance in Raqqa city, recent surveys by REACH show that there are still large unmet needs and that food insecurity is on the increase with basic food items being unaffordable to a growing number of people. There are a number of reasons for this. First, the continued depreciation of the Syrian Pound (SYP) against the dollar has dramatically reduced spending the same time, there has been a significant increase in the number of returnees to Raqqa city, including new IDPs as a result of the October 2019 Turkish military offensive.

5 - Implementation of activities

Improving food security for vulnerable households and IDPs through two food assistance modalities: food vouchers, general food rations (GFRs) and will also include two essential complimentary activities the provision of basic agricultural inputs in the form of in-kind agricultural kits and agricultural vouchers . Food rations will be distributed to both newly displaced IDPs, and extended IDPs residing in hard to reach areas where a voucher modality cannot be used. We will aim to support IDPs in informal settlements for 8 months, while newly displaced IDPs who are still moving will receive the same for one month. Additionally, we will also distribute food rations to households with an agricultural background who receive an agricultural kit containing seeds, fertilizers, basic drip irrigation systems and other necessities for up to 3 months between planting and harvesting. IDP and returnee households in areas with access to functional markets will be supported with a minimum of eight months’ food voucher distributions. This intervention will primarily be implemented in Deir-ez-Zor and Ar-Raqqa Governorates. Support may also be continued in Aleppo governorate particularly in the instance of new displacement. We will target IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities using our extensively tested food voucher household assessment tool; 100% of households receiving assistance will be assessed. The NGO uses a multi-vulnerability assessment, in line with guidance of the FSS. The assessment tool uses a points based vulnerability scoring system (examining household size, gender of head of household, income sources, number of young children, disability, food consumption scores, negative coping strategies, productive assets, status of shelter etc.) to select the most vulnerable families. The value of the food voucher will be set against the minimum survivable expenditure basket (SMEB) and fully in line with FSS and Cash Working guidance.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Based on the analysis of the FSS sector of the numerous risks and potential threats linked to the implementation of the sector strategy, the NGO similarly identifies two key protection risks around distributions themselves and the possibility of causing tension between IDP and host communities. Mitigation measures are spelled out below: In order to mitigate the security/protection risks for all beneficiaries during distribution, they will be carried out early in the day to ensure beneficiaries can come and go with daylight. Women and men have separate lines at distribution points that are staffed by male and female field workers. In camp settings, multiple distribution points are established to help keep the number of people at one point to a minimum and to reduce waiting times. For out of camp distributions, the duration between assessment and distribution is kept to a minimum to avoid large distributions and to help deliver assistance to beneficiaries as soon as possible. Voucher distributions take place by mixed-gender teams at the household level. Whenever possible and appropriate staff from the area will carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive informational networks and good community acceptance. It ensures conflict sensitivity as our staff have an awareness to the social, cultural and political dynamics of the area and are representative of the program participants. The NGO also holds consultations between host and IDP communities prior to activities. Additionally CRM is in place and reporting methods communicated clearly with beneficiaries at all distributions and in all activities. Participants in all our activities can directly provide feedback or complaints about projects, staff and systems via WhatsApp, email, telephone, by a CRM focal person, and are in line both NGO's commitments under the Core Humanitarian Standard, and with the requirement of NGO Code of Conduct and Associated Policies.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There is a broader risk of more widespread resumption of conflict between the SDF, GOS, TAF and FSA, especially in border districts such as Manbij, Kobane and Tabqa. In October 2019, the Turkish Military incursion resulted in the establishment of a “Buffer zone” from Talabied to Ras Al Ain, causing widespread displacement. Further escalation of the conflict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: IS resurgence particularly in the wake of reduction in support from coalition or redirection of military efforts to other conflict areas; tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict. In these instances, partial or complete access to project areas could be jeopardised. Assumptions -The security situation will remain the same or improve enabling an expansion of activities across NES. - There will be no significant shifts in control over key locations in NES– This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly Mitigating Measures This organization has developed, in coordination with other NGO actors and the NGO forum, its own scenario analysis and contingency-planning. In the event of wider political instability, insecurity or resumption of conflict this organizations response would depend on the severity of the scenario. In the event of localized insecurity/access restrictions/power-changes in one or several locations.
