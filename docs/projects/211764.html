Project Summary

For 2024, War Child (WC) and its consortium of 12 Syrian partner organizations will provide an education response to 23,932 highly vulnerable boys and girls including those living with disabilities (240) PWD, (11,966 boys and 11,966 girls)in North West and North East Syria (Idleb, Aleppo, Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor & Al-Hasakeh governorates) across a 12-month action. All targeted sub-districts are severity 3, 4 and 5. Recognizing the interconnection between protection and education, the action will increase access to non-formal education and strengthen the protection of vulnerable girls and boys who are out of school or at risk of dropping out, to build their resilience and provide a pathway back into a safe and protective environment in school. The action will include non-formal education interventions including Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) for out-of-school of vulnerable boys and girls including those living with disabilities of the ages 6-17 who have missed more than two years of school. The action will also include guided self-learning for informal settlements with no access to learning spaces to keep vulnerable boys and girls including those living with disabilities connected to learning, and for youth mainly boys who joined the working force to support their households and girls who are not sent to school and run the risk of early marriage. For schooled vulnerable boys and girls including those living with disabilities with learning gaps, remedial and homework support will be offered to limit the risk of dropping out. War Child's integrity framework fully incorporates (PSEA) strategy. Throughout the project cycle, we ensure that all our partners' staff receive comprehensive training and awareness sessions on PSEA, emphasizing its critical importance. An effective teacher professional development through a coaching system based on INEE’s TICC and WC’s Core program to ensure teachers’ and vulnerable boys and girls including those living with disabilities wellbeing, will have coaches build the capacities of 200 teachers receiving incentives under the action. The coaches will be trained and supported by WC education coordinator based on teachers’ needs, to help teachers obtain skills and methodologies for the creation of a safe and quality learning environment for vulnerable boys and girls including those living with disabilities. For the guided self-learning modality, teachers will be trained to support caregivers with guidance and tips to support their vulnerable boys and girls including those living with disabilities learning and provide them with a safe learning environment at home. The action will adopt an integrated approach through developing the capacity of teachers on child protection and enhancing the accessibility and safety of the school environment. Teachers will be trained on safe identification and referral for CP concerns. This prevention and response approach will address immediate and urgent identified risks to access to education while contributing to long-term change. WC will be delivering its Core program, which combines scaffolded psychological care with real-time, individualized classroom support to teachers. Teachers’ competence and skills progression is monitored through WC developed follow-up tools. WC will pilot social emotional learning (SEL) as an integrated approach that complements WC’s evidence-based Deals PSS programs. Teachers will be trained to identify, and work with vulnerable boys and girls including those living with disabilities to develop their life skill and learning competencies in the safe learning environment of the classroom. WC is incorporating the digital learning application Can't Wait to Learn (CWTL) into their System of Care. CWTL supports education, psychosocial support, child protection, and specifically aids vulnerable children, including those with disabilities, in crisis areas.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

WC will enhance learning environments through school rehabilitation, including WASH facilities and provision of school supplies. A needs assessment in NWS and NES is planned for 2024 to tailor WC's response to community needs. This will include an inclusion checklist to ensure accessibility, participation, inclusive development, and engagement of marginalized groups in monitoring and evaluation activities. The MEAL plans are set out collaboratively with partners as part of the design phase. The War Child MEAL team works with partners to ensure appropriate indicators and tools are measured at the right time. The information is channeled through secure channels such as War Child Microsoft Servers or the in-house database that is used widely for all War Child programs. There is always a focal point from each organization that is supported to produce their work effectively. War Child requests data on indicators, participant monitoring, and FCRMs from all partners monthly. War Child’s MEAL team supports partners in the production of information, analysis, and capacity building consistently throughout the partnership. The monitoring cycle begins with developing a comprehensive project framework, delineating key areas for evaluation, such as project outputs, outcomes, and impact. Subsequently, specific evaluation questions are formulated to guide the data collection and analysis process. War Child and partners will regularly be monitoring activities, outputs, and outcomes. These are facilitated by tools such as monthly IPTT progress reports and the database, as well as the utilization of feedback and complaints mechanisms. Finally, War Child employs a third-party monitoring service providers to conduct evaluations and assessments in areas of implementation.
