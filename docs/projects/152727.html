Project Summary

Help Restore Youth (HeRY) aims to enhance food production for 10,000 vulnerable households through provisions of inputs, and vaccination and animal diseases’ surveillance. This project targets vulnerable communities in Aweil East and West of Former Northern Bahr El-Ghazal and Buma (Pibor) of Former Jonglei State, whose access to agricultural inputs: seeds, farming and fishing tools, and vaccinations and animal drugs limited. This is to alleviate risk of sliding into famine or loss of livelihoods, according to IPC September 2018 report. The project looks at increasing production ability, supplement dietary intakes and diversified livelihoods for the vulnerable households. Inputs distributions, vaccination and surveillance as well as vegetable and fish preservation training skills will be provided by HeRY. HeRY will also be from time to time conducts crops and livestock production and diseases monitoring and reporting in the target areas.

The war has contributed to vulnerable to be mostly affected, especially, women, children and elderly. These vulnerable people are likely to face famine in Aweil West, Aweil East, and Buma in Jonglei. According to food security phase classification (IPC) report, September 2018, about 60% of the country's population will face a severe food shortage. In fact, there is a terrible hunger in 2019. In addition, war and other natural catastrophes contributed eminently to loss of livelihoods and property to majority across the country. This project will enhance protection of livelihoods and promotes adoption of diversified livelihood activities to boost households’ food stocks and dietary intakes.Boma communities in Greater Pibor have limited access to adequate agriculture inputs and vaccination services since 2010 to date, due to conflicts. These crises did not only cripple livelihoods sources but also cut-off the area from humanitarian services, depriving them of the basic necessities. Besides, with the promise of peace in the country, these areas would likely witness significant return of displaced families, who require integration packages into their native homes. HeRY will help facilitate the integration of this category of households to enhance integration process. Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

HeRY had earlier discussed the intervention in the aforementioned target areas for their inputs and to bless it. This Project will also prior to implementation inform the primary beneficiaries through sensitization, mobilization, stakeholders orientation meetings, and community gatherings on matters pertaining to project. The local community leaders and local project management committees will through out implementation work along with HeRY's project team. Tokens will distributed to beneficiaries and mini-banners indicating entitlements display at the distribution centres. Participatory project monitoring will be conducted to keep abreast stakeholders including local communities and beneficiaries such that keep pace with the project implementation. Radio talks over local FM radios will be conducted to educate the location population about the project.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The barriers to access were identified and planned for. In Buma, the population are scattered and there lives three tribes who are sometimes in conflict to each other. HeRY with the help of local authorities will ensure necessary security measures, through local police are put place. Separate distribution points will be established for every community. HeRY will also preposition the inputs before the wet seasons for easy access.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project address the inequality and protection issues. The most vulnerable people are children women and elderly since they do not have the ability to work in the government positions and there are no or limited laws to protect them. They do not have the equal rights like other people and they are in danger of violence also. HeRY through local chiefs and local authorities advocate for prioritizing child and female headed households as well as elderly. Protection is another issue that we will address since sometimes, food provision can be a source of conflict with the communities, and we are very careful to raise awareness within the community, especially women about the dangers of violence over aid assistance.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

With the help of local authorities and chiefs, HeRY will ensure the distance travels by a beneficiary to the project site should less than one hour. Local radios also will be used to convey information about distributions or vaccination schedule.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project measures the safety of beneficiaries by targeting them at their safety zones. This helps avoid exposing beneficiaries to risk, especially in areas where insecurity is an issue. In addition, HeRY’s monitoring tools capture information on distance covered by a beneficiary to distribution site and whether it is safe for them to travel the road. The monitoring recommendations will help will HeRY to fine the approach where appropriate.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity of beneficiaries is observed when targeting is not in exchange of sex, beneficiaries targeted within appropriate distance and not left stay in queue waiting for assistance for longer hours.
