Project Summary

Sorouh is aiming to provide continues, improved, equitable and sufficient WASH services in the IDPs camps and complexes in Salah Al-Din, while also focusing on creating an enabling environment for correct sanitation and hygiene practices in 30 primary schools in the returnees areas, And focusing on breaking the taboo around menstrual hygiene and providing gender-sensitive facilities ( a private and safe with sufficient clean water and hygienic receptacles that are ecologically sound ) to assure the well-being and dignity of teenage girls in 25 high schools in the returnees areas in Salah Al-Din.
The project will cover IDPs camps and complexes in Tikrit, Al-Alam, and Shirqat ( Al-Shahama-Karama camp, Qadissiyah, and Dream city complexes, Al-Alam camps, and Basateen camp ) with nearly 14800 IDPs individuals settling in these camps, the project will provide daily O&M of water system ( including Zamzam projects, RO units, water tanks, water network, and water taps ), and daily water quality monitoring, the project will also provide daily Sanitation facilities maintenance, with garbage collection and desludging services, the project will also form community-based WASH committees to increase community participation and feedback, and the project will support the local authorities in decommissioning of water units to be re-used in other locations.
The project will also create an enabling environment to practice best hygiene practices in 30 schools in the returnees areas in Baiji, Tuz, Balad, Al-Fares, and Samarra, by rehabilitating the WASH facilities in these schools, distributing school cleaning kits and conducting awareness-raising activities with special focus on handwashing, water conservation and best hygiene practices.
The project will also increase awareness on Menstrual Hygiene Management within teenage school girls in 25 high schools in the targeted areas through training 3 teachers from each school and the trained teachers will provide MHM sessions to the teenage girls.

Needs

In Conflict-affected and vulnerable communities, the disruption of familiar hygienepractices can contribute to an increased risk of disease transmission andepidemics. The most common source of disease is human waste, the movement ofpathogens from the feces of a sick person to ingestion by somebody else cantake many pathways, these are fluids, fingers, flies, fields, floods, and food.Barriers can stop the transmission of disease. These can be primary, such aspreventing initial contact with the feces, or secondary, such as preventingfeces from being ingested by a new person, the barriers can be controlled bywater, sanitation, and hygiene interventions.Poor hygienepractices, lack of adequate sanitation, and unsafe or limited water suppliescan contribute to the spread of preventable diseases such as diarrhea, choleraor typhoid. Breaking the chain of infection transmission at home, at school andin the community by promoting good hygiene practices can make an importantcontribution to increased well-being and healthSince 2016, IDPs have settled in Salah Al-Din camps and complexes, According to our recent statistics and CCCMs updates, there are currently 2958 IDPs families ( 14,800 Individual ) settled in Al-Alam, Al-Shahama-karama, and Basateen camp, in addition to Qadissiyah and Dream city complexes, these remaining IDPs families are unable to return to their AoO mainly because their homes are destroyed, or there is no security clearance for them to return, it's expected that these families remain in IDPs camps up till mid 2019, these IDPs are in need of continued, improved, equitable, safe, sufficient and appropriate water supply, sanitation services and improved hygiene practices. Those IDPs young children, Children, adolescents adults, and elderly people are in need of safe water, sanitation services, and hygiene raising messages. On the other hand, the return movement in Salah Al-Din started in late 2017, and according to the latest IOM update, there are 36,679 families ( 219,462 ) in Tuz, Baiji, Dujail, Samarra, Balad and it's surrounding areas. There are highly vulnerable returnees in these areas are still in need of basic WASH services and enabling the environment to practice best hygiene practices, especially in schools.

Protection Risk Analysis

-Hard access to gender-segregated WASH facilities in IDPs camps will cause serious protection risks to IDPs girls and women.- Lack of basic services provided in the female WASH facilities ( water, lights, cleaning tools ) will also cause protection risks on women and girls.

Mitigation Measures

-Sorouh will ensure that both female and male WASH facilities are easy to access by both genders at all ages- Sorouh will also ensure that all of the WASH facilities will be maintained regularly and fully functional at all times.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Sorouh has demonstrated a high commitment to accountability to affected populations by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into all programmes, Sorouh will form community-based WASH committees in IDPs camps that will consist of IDPs from different ages and gender to provide feedback and to participate in implementing the project activities. Sorouh will also provide hotlines for complaints and will provide a complaint box that will be managed by both Sorouh and the community members. Sorouh will also conduct briefing sessions, focus group discussions with teachers, students, parents and community leaders in the returnees areas to ensure community participation in implementing and monitoring the activities.
