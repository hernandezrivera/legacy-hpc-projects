Project Summary

South Sudan remains to be among the countries with a high prevalence of gender-based violence where half of the girls marry below the age of 18. Reports of physical violence, rape, and other forms of GBV towards women and girls are pervasive. Societal norms, values, practices and gender related power imbalances are key drivers of the various forms of GBV women and girls face, even not related to conflict.
With current conflict in Sudan, the number of people in need of protection/GBV services has significantly increased in transit and reception areas. The disruption of community networks and loss of family assets have heightened the risk of violence, exploitation and abuse, particularly for women and girls. According to UNHCR-IOM joint report 20% of returnees plan to reach the Greater Bhar el Ghazal area (Northern Bhar el Ghazal, Western Bahr el Ghazal, and Warrap states), with Aweil serving as the central hub for new arrivals ( (Protection cluster May 2023)
Therefore, it is imperative that humanitarian interventions are sensitive to this reality, given that they may both cause and exacerbate the incidence of GBV cases.
The overall objective of the HRP is to reach to 39,760 individuals (17,494 women, 17,097 girls, 2,783 boys and 2,386 men) with GBV prevention, risk mitigation and referral services including awareness raising on gender based violence types, its harmful consequences and the available services; building the capacity of frontline staff and volunteers on GBV topics and PFA; implementation of psychosocial activities; distribution of dignity kits, and facilitating Safe and confidential referral for specialized services to specialized services. Action against Hunger will implement these activities integrating them in the multisector projects to be implemented in Warrap, NBeG and Jongle States. ACF has already conducted gender analysis in its operation areas of Warrap and Jonglie and the evidence will be used to guide the activity implementation. Besides, key gender questions will be integrated during the need assessments conducted for the multisector projects to complement the implementation

1. Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) threatens the overall well-being of South Sudan’s population, particularly women and girls. Studies indicate that some 65 percent of women and girls have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime. According to UNICEF (2021), 50% of women have suffered from intimate partner violence (IPV). Child marriage, sexual abuse, rape/harassment, and/or harassment of women and girls, including by armed force, were key protection issues raised across the country. The elongated flooding and frequent inter-communal conflicts have increased the women, men, boys, and girls’ risk of exposure to different types of violence, including rape, murder, and torture. GBV can be detrimental to the nutritional and health status of women as well as their children if present, given that she is denied decision-making and purchasing power: this causes deterioration of physical health and mental wellbeing. According to a study by UNICEF, there is a clear and positive relation between IPV child malnutrition. Women who experienced any form of intimate partner violence are more likely to give birth to stunted children. Having little control over one’s sexuality and pressure on women to bear additional children increases the stress on household food and nutrition security and limits the ability to breastfeed. The gender analysis conducted by Action against hunger in Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazel in 2018, revealed that those who experience SGBV are often ostracized, excluded from valuable income-generating opportunities. Girls with disabilities were reported to be at risk of coercion into sex in exchange for support, and as a result, vulnerable to unwanted pregnancy and sexually transmitted disease. People living with disabilities are less likely to disclose or report violence due to shame, fear of family members, who are usually the perpetrators and lack of awareness. These factors make women and girls with disabilities an easy target for different forms of violence. Moreover, men’s lack of knowledge and engagement in nutrition and health programs often lead to their restricting women’s movement to services in favor of attending to household needs, a reality that inhibits the access of women to critical basic services. Further, the stigma around GBV, especially sexual exploitation and abuse, has precluded many from seeking treatment, exacerbating the physical health and nutrition effects of the attack. The above facts call for coordinated and standardized efforts for the delivery of gender-responsive and do no harm approaches.

2. Project activities

Awareness raising on gender equality and GBV topics: Action against Hunger will conduct awareness-raising and information dissemination sessions using the different platforms to disseminate information on GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and available services for women, men, boys, and girls. The Mother-to-Mother Group discussion forums and the Men group discussion forums will be established at nutrition sites as one way of reaching out to community members with key messages. The programs will ensure the participation and inclusion of different gender and people with disabilities working closely with existing associations and grass root level organization representing these groups. Existing tools and guidelines will be developed to facilitate contextualized sessions in the intervention areas. Capacity Building of staff and gender Focal points: Action Against Hunger will continue to train its entire staff on gender, GBV and inclusion concepts to establish a universal commitment to the values of gender equality, inclusion and GBV awareness-raising. The trainings will also highlight perspectives on inclusion of people with disabilities, especially women and girls. Feedback and Complaints Mechanism: Supporting the implementation of prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) policies within the organization and within the community, and promoting awareness of complaint mechanisms.Dignity Kit Distribution: Dignity kits will be distributed for GBV survivors, highly vulnerable women, and girls at risk to ensure their safety and dignity Gender Analysis: We will conduct a rapid gender analysis in each of Action Against Hunger’s areas of interventions to assess gender and GBV dynamics that affect or undermine current humanitarian intervention strategies. This will inform any recommendations and changes in approach for our organization as well as for cluster partners. Safety Audit: Action Against Hunger will conduct Safety Audits at nutrition sites and in the communities, surrounding those sites using validated tools to assess protection risks in and around nutrition facilities and suggest measures for risk prevention and mitigation. The findings will enable us to mitigate the potential risks posed through different sectors for example ensuring the availability of gender-segregated and disability friendly WASH facilities at nutritional sites, reducing waiting time for beneficiaries coming to the sites ensuring FSL cash distribution centers are close to beneficiary homes to reduce long distance movement. ACF will conduct safety audits at each nutrition sites and surrounding villages at least once a year. Recommendations from the Safety audits will be used for improving programming approaches. Provision of Psychosocial support: Action Against Hunger will capacity building activities for its staff and the frontline workers in areas GBV basic concepts and psychological first aid (PFA) to improve their skills so as to provide group and individualized psychosocial support for vulnerable women, men, boys , girls and people with disabilities in affected areas. Selected recreational activities and skill-building activities will be identified and facilitated by PSS workers and trained community volunteers. Facilitate Safe and confidential referral for specialized services: Action Against Hunger will conduct mapping of the available services in the locations and will train all site and community level staff in the areas on referral mechanisms and how to safely refer survivors of GBV, as well as raising awareness among program participants on such pathways. As part of this effort, we will work with the GBV sub-cluster to gather information on available services and the latest referral pathways.
