Project Summary

The project seeks to improve water and sanitation services for Palestine refugees living in 10 refugee camps in the West Bank, in the context to COVID-19 outbreak. In order to continue providing services in such a challenging environment, UNRWA needs additional daily paid sanitation labourers who can replace staff who has been diagnosed with COVID-19 or need to isolate or quarantine. In order to ensure sanitation labourers can carry on their duties in safety, the necessary PPE and equipment will be distributed to all front line staff.

1 - Needs

According to the WASH Cluster Vulnerability Map 2020, ten refugee camps in the West Bank are particularly affected by the risk of floods and/or waste accumulation impacting around 73,299 refugees. COVID-19 has led to increased pressure and created additional challenges on water and sanitation services across refugee camps in the West Bank, due to movement restrictions and closures. Due to COVID-19, UNRWA solid waste management service is challenged by an increased amount of mixed waste which is dumped in the open, including infectious waste due to low levels of segregation at source. Waste accumulation is becoming a health hazard for the camp residents, especially next to schools, HCFs and public spaces creating a general unhygienic environment. Accumulated trash attracts stray dogs and cats, as well as rodents – all possible vectors of diseases - and contaminates ground water resources. Furthermore, refugees in camps are exposed to the risk of flooding during the rainy season, as well as the overflow of storm-water facilities and sewage pumping stations, due to the poor status of the sanitation infrastructure. Inadequate and inappropriate handling of healthcare waste may have serious public health consequences and a significant impact on the environment. Due to the new guidelines for handling waste under the COVID-19 pandemic, additional capacity and resources are required to maintain compliance for proper waste management.

2 - Activities

In response to the COVID-19 pandemic in the West Bank, UNRWA environmental health teams will provide increased solid waste management services and additional sanitation campaigns in 10 refugee camps, namely: Dhesheh, Beit Jibrin, Aida, Al Fawwar, Aqbat Jaber, Qalandia, Shu'fat, Al Amari, Jalazone, Deir Ammar. UNRWA plans to enhance the collection of solid waste to ensure that critical water and sanitation services remain available. The enhancement of these services requires the provision of PPE and additional daily paid staff. Through the project, 42 supplementary sanitation labourers will be hired to cover the increased demand for maintaining cleaning and sanitation in camps. The additional staff will perform solid waste collection and critical operations and maintenance to prevent/respond to the risk of flooding. Extra staff will mitigate shortages in case of infection or need of isolation for some of the labourers. The additional workers will be equipped with PPE, including protective clothing, environmental cleaning tools and materials. Finally, UNRWA’s sanitation laborers will provide solid waste management for the community led isolation centers and conduct monthly cleaning and disinfection campaign in the public spaces around the camps in partnerships with the different Camp Service Committees and CBOs which will be involved during the activities' design and implementation.

3 - Indicators

In addition to the indicators proposed by the clusters, UNRWA will measure the following indicators: # of refugees living in camps are benefiting from improved access to solid waste management services during COVID-19 (target 73,299);# of tons of waste are removed from 10 targeted refugee camps in the West Bank (target 5,000); % of sanitation labourers equipped with adequate protective gears (target 80%)

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In UNRWA Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritization of safety and dignity is explicitly addressed as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff. Further, UNRWA Medium-Term Strategy 2016-2021 explicitly prioritizes the "do-no-harm" principles by stating that UNRWA will address protection challenges that arise from its programme service delivery. This will include focusing on instances when UNRWA, through its actions or policies, unintentionally undermines the realization of specific rights such as the right to live in a safe and clean environment. By addressing such concerns through "do-no-harm" and other approaches, UNRWA service delivery promotes and respects the rights of beneficiaries and ensures the security and dignity of beneficiaries.
