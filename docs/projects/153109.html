Project Summary

The Rescue Mission (TERM) will conduct life-saving WASH activities aimed at meeting the critical WASH needs of vulnerable people in South Sudan affected by conflict, natural disasters, disease outbreaks, and areas of high malnutrition rates.
This project aims at providing timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and IDPs, and conflict affected populations, cholera hotspots and mitigating GBV resulting from inadequate WASH services increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases targeting IDP’s, host and the returnees. Interventions will include safe water provision through water point rehabilitation (hand pumps, Water yards water quality monitoring and treatment of contaminated water source, provision of household water filters, depending on the needs); provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management); hygiene promotion (hand washing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain and jerrycan cleaning campaigns); capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, school's hygiene clubs), and distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, water purification filters, PUR or others).
Total targeted group is 30,000 beneficiaries. The project will target communities with poor access to safe water supply, unsafe sanitation and hygiene practices, poor sanitation coverage and support nutrition, health and education centers with WASH facilities (i.e. GAM rates of more than 27%).
The intervention will include detailed needs assessment to identify the needs, affected gender, age and diversity groups, the vulnerabilities of the targeted community and coping strategies employed by the affected population. Based on the assessment report, interventions will be designed accordingly with TERM capacity, response with other partners will be planned. The main priority is to provide timely WASH response necessary to reduce morbidity and mortality caused by WASH related diseases and limited access to water.
TERM will focus on supporting other sectors as Health, Nutrition, Education with WASH facilities like soap, hand washing facilities, improvement of waste management facilities, access to latrines and capacity building of community health, nutrition workers on WASH related disease preventive measures among others.
Centralizing protection to promote principled access to basic services for the displaced and other vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to affected people and addressing and mitigating GBV risk resulting from WASH related needs . TERM will implement capacity building of the community to reinforce coping mechanism to manage current shocks and sustain livelihoods, strengthen linkages with development action to support resilience.

Needs

South Sudan humanitarian crisis has continued to intensify and expand due to the compounding effects of the widespread violence and the deteriorating economic situation, the ongoing fighting and surges of violence has forced people to flee their homes, many of them multiple times. Violence and rights violence continue unchecked and have become a persistent reality for civilians as internally displaced people’s access to services has eroded with insecurity and economic decline. Various types of gender based violence (GBV) are pervasive but go largely unreported, this aggression is faced mostly by women and girls in a situation where undertaking daily survival tasks such as collecting water and firewood places them under threat. Particularly vulnerable groups such as children, people with disabilities and elders suffer the most intense consequence of sustained displacement, violence and lack of access to services. Continued economic decline has undermined people’s access to basic resources The ongoing violence throughout the country still causing displacement of community and the economic downturn which has put a further strain on already limited WASH services. Based on TERM recently conducted assessment in Kajo-keji on the 17/10/2018, Lainya (Wonduruba) and Dollo payam of central Equotaria state Findings shows that,1. more than 80% of the areas assess are IDP’s Returnees and host community leaving with poor or no access to water with no WASH facilities,2. Most water points are broken down with community fetching water from the swamps and streams with no treatment practice,3. Locations like Kajo-Keji (Korijo) IDPs have completely no access to WASH services whereby there is no single water point spotted during assessment in4. The quality of the water is very turbid and smelly as it’s a surface water collection of running rain water perused to be contaminated due to the open defaecation 5. The population does not treat the turbid surface water nor practice boiling as its look at time wasting6. Lack of water points brings risks for women and girls, who are often exposed to violence when walking to remote water sources. In urban areas, people struggle to access safe water due to ever-rising prices. 7. Sanitation remains a big challenge, community practise open deification which is at high risk of water borne disease outbreak8. Institutions like Health/Nutrition centres, Schools have no WASH facilities existing 9. The affected population have poor Knowledge on hygiene on HH level, hand washing at critical moment is not a practice10. Hygiene practice among the population is observed very low, no hygiene items like for dental, hair, baby hygiene at HH level11. The affected community have limited IDP’S have limited WASH NFIs as observed at HH level and at water points as most end up sharing 5litres container with 3HH for water collection and storage. In addition, significant movement of returnees has been ongoing to areas destroyed by conflict early April 2018 as in Wonduruba, Current report provided by OCHA estimated 63,000 IDPs returned home from Minkaman IDP settlements. Humanitarian partners are planning to provide support to sustain the returnees as most WASH facilities are destroyed and not in use. This adds pressure on existing facilities in towns with limited urban planning and capacity. As more returnees arrive, individual and household coping mechanism is stretched, particularly in the sector of food security and WASH. Such situation exposes people to a high risk of diseases outbreaks and increase in morbidity and mortality rate due to lack of sanitation, access to safe water and high rates of malnutrition.

Activities

TERM will be focusing on implementing the live saving WASH activities as described below. 1. Conducting WASH assessments (either, TERM’s own, IRNA and/or coordinated with partners) in areas with chronic WASH needs, disaster and conflict prone areas, including gender specific needs. 2. Provision of timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected population and those at risk of disease outbreak through water points repair and rehabilitation within community and at institutions like Health, Nutrition centres and schools to prevent malnutrition and WASH related diseases. 3. Provision of water purification items (PUR and Aquatabs) and Distribution of WASH NFIs.4. capacity building in terms of preparedness through (training of pump mechanics and provision of borehole spare parts, training of Water Management Committee for resilience) water quality tests, both at water points and community level. TERM will be using sustainable solutions, such as installing water sources propelled by solar power, where feasible. feasible.5. Increase knowledge of appropriate Hygiene practices among IDPs, and conflict affected population to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases through promotion activities particularly hand washing for displaced and vulnerable communities and institutions; Nutrition/Health centres will be targeted for hygiene promotion.6. Enable vulnerable and affected population to practice safe sanitary and hygienic living through secure dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services, construction of sanitation facilities to Nutrition/Health centres, schools, with consideration for gender separated latrines and provision of latrine digging tools to the affected community as an exit strategy.7. Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities
