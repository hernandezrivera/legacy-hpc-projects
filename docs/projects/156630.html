Project Summary

This project aims to promoting cohesion and recovery by reducing shelter vulnerabilities of residents, host communities, and returnees, and where appropriate IDPs. who is living in substandard shelter conditions in (Khan Shaykun, Bara, Rami, Hawash, Hayyan and kafr Bssin) of sub districts (Khan Shaykun - Ehsem - Madiq castle - Haritan) in (Idleb, Aleppo and Hama) governerates, by providing rehabilitation for 560 houses (moderate damage) for 560 HHs (3472 beneficiaries), each house approximately costs 1000 $, the total cost of rehabilitation is 560,000 $ and distribution 2025 NFI kits (5 blankets & 5 mattresses) to 2025 HHs, 12555 beneficiaries, each NFI kit costs 160 $, the total cost is 324,000 $, so Ataa will be reaching to 2585 HHs, 16,027 beneficiaries (52% female - 48% male) according to vulnerable families.
In order to achieve that the project will include:
1- Establishment of community working groups (CWGs) to ensure a high level of community engagement and avoid any tensions with the communities especially in selecting the beneficiaries as these groups will provide a list of all possible beneficiaries.
2- Prepare profiles and conduct a technical assessment to all beneficiaries provided by CWGs to assess the rehabilitation needs of each house, identify the current occupant of the house, and the owner of the house. As well as provide information pertinent to their conditions including specific vulnerability categories.
3- Provide HLP training to CWGs and members of local councils to respond to any immediate or future HLP issues such as identifying the relationship between the current occupant and owner of the house.
4- Making requests for quotations in the target communities to select the contractor to implement rehabilitation activities under Ataa supervision.
ATAA will conduct FGDs with targeted community to inform them about goal, outcome, and, activities of the project. Brochures and banners will be used to share information with the community.
ATAA will set a community – based mechanism to make sure that community members can submit their complaints and feedback. feedback tracker will be prepared on monthly basis.
ATAA Accountability team will set the appropriate complaint channels as (Hotline, WhatsApp, Viber, Email, Observation, Meetings, and Field visits)
ِAll received complaints will be recorded and tracked on monthly.”

Needs

According to SNFI_HNO_2019, as a result of the evolution of the crisis over the last 12 months, there has been a slight improvement in the overall NFI situation, but a worsening of the shelter situation.4.7 million people are in need of shelter support in Syria due to inadequate conditions in existing shelters, and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters. Over the course of the crisis, shelter and infrastructure damage has occurred on a massive scale, with the scale of rebuilding and reconstruction needed which goes beyond the capacity and remit of the SNFI sector and the humanitarian response in general. Against the backdrop of increased self-organized returnees in 2018, there are increased needs related to the repair and rehabilitation of damaged shelters and infrastructure as people seek more durable solutions. It is likely that these needs will further increase in 2019, 4.4 million people require NFI assistance, a 6 per cent decline from the 2018 HNO, and the second year in a row that the total number of people in need of NFI assistance has reduced. However, within the situation in some areas, such as the northwest, is in fact deteriorating amidst and increase in people in need. Access to NFIs has generally become more stable over the last year, with 14 per cent of communities reporting a deteriorating situation, compared with 24 per cent last year. However, needs remain substantial, both in terms of humanitarian life-saving needs, particularly those among the high numbers of newly displaced IDPs requiring core, supplementary and seasonal assistance, as well as longer-term needs, among those communities requiring resilience-oriented support to reduce dependence on external assistance and enhance the ability of families to withstand future shocks and stresses. In 2019, these needs will continue to compromise the ability of NFI partners to tailor responses based on the intersection of individual needs and vulnerabilities with the localized capacity of markets and supply chains.According to the results of RNA conducted by Ataa in sub districts of (Khan Shaykun - Ehsem - Madiq castle - Haritan) including the destructed houses with no possible repairs, the severity of damage in the targeted communities as following:1- Khan Shaykun: the total of houses: 6500, the total numbers of IDPs: 2500 HHs, the houses with considerable damage: 2600 and the houses with Moderate damage: 20802- Bara (Ehsem): the total of houses: 3000, the total numbers of IDPs: 1370 HHs, the houses with considerable damage: 60, the houses with Moderate damage: 1403- Rmi (Ehsem): the total of houses: 3081, the total numbers of IDPs: 231HHs, the houses with considerable damage: 65, the houses with Moderate damage: 2304- Hawash(Madiq Castle):the total of houses: 900, the total numbers of IDPs: 160 HHs, the houses with considerable damage: 342, the houses with Moderate damage: 2615- Hayyan (Haritan):the total of houses: 1800, the total numbers of IDPs: 100 HHs, the houses with considerable damage: 400, the houses with Moderate damage: 5006- Kafr Bssin (Haritan): the total of houses: 700, the total numbers of IDPs: 95 HHs, the houses with considerable damage: 10, the houses with Moderate damage: 35Moderate damage: Under 30%, slight wall damage, moderate fire damage, repair possible (walls repairs, windows, doors, walls covering, painting,…..).Regarding the interviews which had been conducted with KIs, almost 79% of them clarified that there are a lot shortages in NFI sector and these shortages are affecting everyone, but they are not life-threatening. Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Beneficiaries might be exposed to risks if attending badly planned, organized and conducted distributions in connection with poor safety and security planning and/or lack of adequate consideration for vulnerable groupsMitigation 1:- Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set up, postponed or cancelled- Ensure that NFIs distribution are completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight- Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk- Communicate intensively with national and local authorities, stakeholders and community leaders- Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women- Set up a door to door distribution for the very vulnerable HHsRisk 2 : Poor coordination of type of aid delivered among agencies might lead to creation of double standards and different quality/quantity of NFIs distributed to neighboring communities (or even among the same community to different groups)Mitigation 2:- Constantly liaise with SNFI Cluster, submit 4Ws and planned activities report, attend Cluster meeting and seek Cluster support prior to the implementation of the activities- Harmonize the assistance delivered as per SNFI Cluster instructions and SNFI guidance- Coordinate with local councils in the targeted areas as well as being active in field coordination groupsRisk 3 : Tension between host community and IDPs due to lack of context/needs understanding or poor communication and/or tension between included and excluded IDPs might lead to conflict between target groups and complaints against the humanitarian organizationsMitigation 3 :- Clearly communicate selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of the results of the verification/selection exercise- Make effort to target a 20 % of vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs- Establish feedback mechanism and conduct follow up visits if needs be- Involve the local stakeholders throughout the processRisk 4: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitatedMitigation 4 :- Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster- Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP DD- Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed- Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment formRisk 5: Risk of conducting rehabilitation of building with poor structural integrity and/or not fully cleared from land mines and ERWsMitigation 5 :- Conduct a thorough shelter assessment and infrastructural technical assessment of the buildings that are aimed to be rehabilitated- Coordinate the interventions with local authorities that might be aware of security issues in the neighborhood- Ensure that ERWs clearance is conducted if needs be- Check with other actors working in the same area to understand what kind of issues they encountered
