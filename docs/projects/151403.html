Project Summary

The proposed WASH intervention will improve the health conditions of the targeted communities in Hirshabelle, Galmudug, Banadir, and Nugaal through increased access to safe water, use of latrines, hygiene promotion and increased adoption of safe hygiene practices. Cash programming, in the form of conditional transfers and vouchers, will form a core pillar of the proposed intervention.
Key activities will include:
- Design and deployment of mass media hygiene promotion campaigns
- Distribution of water vouchers to the most vulnerable beneficiaries to bridge access to safe water until durable solutions are established
- Training and deployment of hygiene promoters to households
- Training of latrine construction artisans
- Registration of cash for work (CfW) teams to construct household latrines and engage in environmental sanitation micro-projects
- Construction of new water points and rehabilitation of existing water points

Needs

Despite improvements in the humanitarian situation, there are still acute water, sanitation and hygiene needs especially in IDP settlements and displacement affected communities in urban areas. Two in five Somalis remain acutely food insecure, more than 2.6 million people are internally displaced and the seasonal assessment results indicate poor access to safe water and improved sanitationamong IDPs. Disease outbreaks such as Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD)/cholera and measles continue to lead to preventable deaths across Somalia. The ongoing conflict, knock-on effects of the drought, evictions, and natural disasters continue to undermine the resilience of communities, trigger displacement and impede crisis-affected civilians’ access to available, yet limited basic services as well as humanitarians’ access to those in need. According to the WASH Vulnerability Map, March 2018, released by the WASH Cluster, Hiraan, Banadir, Galgaduud, and Mudug rank as Extremely Vulnerable. This assessment is based on AWD/cholera risk, rainfall forecast, malnutrition rates, IDP numbers, IPC levels, and water and sanitation coverage. From assessment data collected in Hiraan, Mudug, Nugaal, and Banadir, RI found that IDP communities have less access to WASH facilities and a poorer level of hygiene than their host community peers. This leads to increased risk of AWD/cholera and more prevalence of open defecation.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

There is a crisis of displacement in Somalia that has been exacerbated by the prolonged drought, the pervasive conflict and knock-on effects of both. This has led to separations and child protection issues. The project specifically targets displacement-affected communities, including IDP settlements. Based on an awareness that displaced families are less likely to have access to water and sanitation facilities and commodities to maintain hygiene, the hygiene promotion activities will particularly target IDPs, including door-to-door visits to households in IDP camps. In areas where WASH facilities are not available, water trucking and/or water vouchers will cater for the IDP population. In areas where IDPs are integrated in host communities or in near proximity to water points, RI will rehabilitate/construct water points. RI also acknowledges the protection issues that are inherent in accessing WASH services. RI will work to ensure that women and children are within a reasonable distance to water points, that rehabilitated/constructed latrines will be gender segregated with lockable doors. RI conducts Post Distribution Monitoring, enabling us to verify that targeted beneficiaries have been reached with services assistance. Data collected by RI at the community and individual level is disaggregated by displacement status wherever possible.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

RI acknowledges that there are differential risks for women, men, girls and boys and across the age cohorts. The project is designed to ensure the specific needs of women and girls are met in recognition of the particular challenges they face. For example, all latrines will be built according to a woman/girl-friendly design. In addition to the economic and contextual factors faced by girls in general, adolescent girls across Somalia are strongly encouraged to drop-out of school to marry and begin to manage household responsibilities. This also exposes women and girls to adverse exposure for protection concerns as they will likely take on the majority of water fetching activities. RI will work to ensure that the distance traveled are reduced through emergency water provision and the rehabilitation/construction of water points. RI will also encourage strategies to mitigate against protection concerns such as traveling in groups to fetch water as opposed to traveling individually to mitigate against adverse exposure. In training of water management committees, RI will insist on 50/50 gender spilt amongst participants as much as possible, recognizing the importance of female representation in water resource management. Community sensitization campaigns, in which water management committees will play a leading role, the protection concerns for women and girls, boys and men will be emphasized. As mentioned above, Post Distribution Monitoring exercises will be conducted to ensure that no targeted beneficiaries are being excluded from receiving services on the basis of gender or age. All data collected by RI at the individual level is disaggregated by gender, and further by age where appropriate.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

RI recognizes conflict as a key factor affecting access to WASH services in Somalia. Conflict causes migration of entire households and of individual children, which can reduce the ability of families to pay for water/access water resources and sanitation facilities. People who have experienced or fled a conflict situation are likely to face more challenges in accessing WASH services: violence and upheaval may have a negative impact on psychosocial wellbeing and often leads to a higher likelihood of poor coping strategies such as domestic violence and increased financial indebtedness. The approach of this project is therefore tailored to meet the particular needs of these beneficiaries. A key approach is in the training of water management committees and the hygiene promoters to ensure that those most affected by armed conflict and violence are targeted for service provision to improve their access and utilization of these services. RI has a dedicated team of local staff and strong remote management systems in place to ensure services can be delivered in challenging environments. Where communities experience challenges accessing services due to active conflict, RI will be supporting the provision of emergency water provision, which will also be meeting the needs of beneficiaries who have fled conflict. An aspect of the water management committee training will include aspects of conflict management.
