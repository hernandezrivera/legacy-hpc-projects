Project Summary

The proposed program will target vulnerable, conflict affected persons in Northwest Syria, to deliver a quality integrated protection program. The intervention will include integrated Mental Health (MH), GBV and Child Protection (CP) Case Management, focused Psychosocial Support, Group Work, Skill-building, Community Outreach, trainings for community members on mental health and protection topics, Awareness activities and Protection Mainstreaming activities. Case management services will provide; ongoing support tailored to the individual and their family or caregivers needs, information, problem solving, advocacy and referral to other services. Psychosocial support will help beneficiaries to improve their coping mechanisms, manage stress and find solutions to problems in their lives. Group work will provide a space for people with similar issues, problems or circumstance to come together, if they feel comfortable, for mutual support. Outreach services will spread information on positive mental health and protection risks, as well as reaching the most vulnerable in communities and making appropriate referrals to services. Trainings will target both community members and front-line service providers to increase their capacity to help and support others. Awareness raising activities aim to decrease stigma encourage people to be more aware of the protection risks faced by the population and what can be done to support people in times of need. Service provision will be closely integrated with health and physical rehabilitation services, though not directly a part of this program, in order to improve access and ensure strong referral mechanisms and identification of those community members and IDPs most in need and at-risk.
The organization will work to improve holistic response to the most vulnerable by targeting a range of humanitarian actors who might encounter or assist those in need in accordance with IASC Guidelines on MHPSS and GBV and Child Protection Minimum standards. These actors will be engaged to increase attention to Protection or psychosocial risks, to mitigate and prevent those risks where possible, and to respond within relevant capacities to community member’s needs, provide referral to specialized services, keeping with client based or survivor-centered support principles. Capacity building training and supervision on case management, psychosocial support and brief psychological interventions, child, women and community engagement and empowerment and caring for child survivors delivered to strengthen local capacity in prevention and response of protection risks and psychosocial support issues. The organisation will deliver GBV core concepts and guiding principles training to reduce the risk GBV and to strengthen the referral pathways. Activities will be available for all community members and IDPs and will specifically target children and women assessed to be at high levels of risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect or violence, children associated with armed groups, unaccompanied/separated children, persons with mental disorders, the elderly, chronically ill and the disabled.

The intervention will aim to strengthen the overall protective environment and individual resiliency of both internally displaced people and host community members as well as targeting the most vulnerable with services tailored to their needs, including people with mental disorders, psychological distress, psychosocial problems, female, male and child GBV survivors, at-risk or abused children and persons with disabilities. The project will target specific geographic locations where a high level of need has been identified in both Aleppo and Idleb Governorates. Through the project the organization will target at least 30,700 beneficiaries over a 12 month period. Services, though targeted toward specific groups, will be available for all community members, both host and displaced, regardless of age, gender or impairment.

Needs

After more than seven years of conflict in North West Syria, a vast number of the conflict-affected population have been deprived from their basic rights, including more vulnerable groups such as women, children, the disabled and elderly. As the situation worsens due to continuous clashes and absence of any political solution, protection risks for the population continue to be very prevalent. GBV for women and girls in particular has continued to be a high risk due to the conflict, displacement, breakdown of law and the erosion of social and support structures. Sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria. In addition, the changes in traditional gender roles and responsibilities within the families resulted often in an increasing of stress and tensions within the families leading to an increase of GBV incidents (HNO 2019), harm to children and increased levels of stress and psychological distress. Destitution and high levels of stress contribute to negative coping mechanisms, while informal employment often leads to exploitation, discrimination, and harassment. Information received from the organisations case management teams indicates that parents’ interest in protecting daughters from multiple risks also contributes to increased incidence of early marriage. According to cases reported to the organisation in northern Syria where the Protection program is implemented in Aleppo and Idleb, the main protection concerns included: domestic violence (41.6%); psychological / emotional abuse (33.3%); and sexual violence (16.6%). Children are continually forced to leave schools and work to support their families. In addition there is a growing number of children being orphaned due to the ongoing conflict and high crime rates. Based on the findings of the CP HNO analysis, in 81% of assessed communities, child labour commonly prevented school attendance, affecting adolescent boys (40%) and Adolescent girls (34%). In 46% of assessed communities, family separation was an issue of concern with death or divorce of caregivers, economic difficulties and early marriage the most common cause. In 45% of assessed communities, adolescent girls (56%) and Adolescent boys years (36%) were considered affected by early marriage. In 30% of assessed communities, recruitment of adolescent boys for by armed groups (67%) was reported. MHPSS needs are increasing, with an estimated 27,635 people in need of mental health services through health centers. WHO estimates that one in thirty people living in Syria suffers from severe mental health conditions (such as severe depression, psychosis, or disabling anxiety) and at least one in five suffers from mild to moderate mental health conditions, all of whom are at are at increased risk of protection concerns. Rapid Assessment conducted by the Organisation in 2018, indicate the need for different levels of psychosocial support services. The causes of PSS needs identified are acute, resulting from the recent ongoing conflict and displacement, and chronic as a result of the protracted conflict which affects peoples’ lives and psychological wellbeing in many ways, as well as persons who have mental disorders dating from before the onset of the conflict. Children with developmental disorders and delays are among the most vulnerable, and as such, far greater efforts are needed to provide both children and their parents/care-givers with adequate support in order to strengthen resilience at the individual and household level. People with disabilities and those living with chronic health conditions are at a higher risk of protection concerns and of developing mental disorders.

Implementation of activities

Integrated Case Management will be provided in order to provide ongoing support, information, referral and follow up, advocacy, develop safety, care and risk plans and develop the beneficiaries social support system. Case management services will be integrated into Primary Healthcare Facilities and Community Centers for ease of access and referral, confidentiality and will pay attention to appropriate entry points, existing women’s groups, CFS, schools and hospitals, with strong referral mechanisms in place other services in the locality such as Clinical Management of Rape and specialised Mental Health Services. PSS interventions will be provided for Children and Parents, GBV survivors, people with mental disorders and psychological distress. This will include stress management, coping skills, problems management skills, self-help techniques, counseling with regard to specific needs or concerns such as shame or guilt with GBV survivors, coping with aggressive children or parenting skills in child protection cases. Group Activities will be facilitated in order to promote wellbeing for case management beneficiaries and their parents/care givers. Group Activities will aim to enhance functionality and positive coping, promote empowerment of positive wellbeing, improve social skills, promote women’s empowerment, strengthen the role of participants in the family and the community by providing them with an opportunity to increase their functioning and social interactions through exercise, mutual support, skills-building activities, psycho-education and coping techniques. Structured non-focused psychosocial activities for children, supporting resilience and positive socialization with their peers and youth empowerment programs for youth that include psychosocial support activities, life skills, and skill-building activities. This initiative will aim to promote resiliency, limit the psychological effects of the conflict on the wider community, reduce protection risks and stigma, provide an avenue for both host community members and IDPs to collectively and collaboratively provide support for one another. Integrated Protection Outreach teams will be mobilized and will provide PFA, including identification of Protection and MHPSS concerns and other needs and referral to appropriate services immediately in the event of any influx of new IDPs. THE NGO will provide trainings to build capacity, raise awareness, improve referral mechanisms and strengthen coordination. Trainings provided will include Sphere Standards, protection mainstreaming, GBV and Child Protection Guiding Principles, PFA, PM+, Self Help and Coping Skills. Supervision will be provided for all staff to ensure continuous professional development and that skills learned in trainings are incorporated properly into their work. The organization will implement protection mainstreaming activities to ensure that the protective and overall impact of aid programming is maximized by building the capacity of humanitarian actors and national/ local authorities to improve the quality provision of services. To measure and evaluate the degree to which mainstreaming protection improves the quality of services in target areas, protection team will utilize Protection Mainstreaming Monitoring System, developed by the Global Protection Cluster. Based on the findings of the ProMMS, protection mainstreaming team will develop an action plan in collaboration with designated focal points to advocate to enhance the response to protection risks. All activities are monitored and evaluated using beneficiary satisfaction survey, access to services assessments, individual client functioning and wellbeing assessments, tests for training participants, focus group discussions with community members and close supervision of activities by supervisors and the management structure.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Exploitation, sexual exploitation and abuse is seen as a possible risk when providing individual services such as case management and psychosocial support for vulnerable people, especially women, children and persons with disabilities and mental disorders. Likelihood: Low – moderate, based on prior experience, high stress levels and difficult life conditions for staff and beneficiariesMitigation Measure: The organization maintains a ZERO TOLERANCE policy towards Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and follows internationally established protocols and standards for beneficiary and staff safeguarding. The organization’s and partners’ staff are required to sign a code of conduct and receive training in child safeguarding and SEA protection with clear protocols on reporting potential SEA of beneficiaries, staff and children. The organization has an established community based feedback and response mechanism to including a PSEA hotline to report suspected cases. All community centres and facilities have clearly visible signs with guidance on how to register complaints including reporting protection concerns anonymously. The organization considers maintaining a gender balance in its outreach teams and at static centres, in order to ensure that beneficiaries are comfortable with the services provided. Risk: Risk of violence/harm toward staff from beneficiaries, community members, persons with mental disorders when providing case management, psychosocial support, community-based activities, group work.Likelihood: Low, based on prior experience, stigma toward MHPSS and Protection issues is factor for community backlash. Mitigation Measure: Staff do not work alone, either inside the clinic or in the community. Security guards will be employed at each PHC/PSS Center. Staff providing outreach, emergency outreach and community activities and events will work in pairs and/or full teams. Each staff member will have a mobile phone report and check in with their team leader regularly. Risk: Case Management, group work and psychosocial support services pose a risk of worsening mental disorders and psychological distress for persons who have experienced trauma or dealing with severe levels of stress Likelihood: LowMitigation Measure: All staff providing such services will receive weekly/bi-weekly supervision sessions with their supervisor in order to discuss difficulties with beneficiaries, the staff capacity to support the case and ensure that Do No Harm approach is incorporated into all areas of work. An experienced, clinical psychologist will also be available building the capacity of supervisors, facilitating case discussions and presentations and will be on hand to discuss any emergency situations (including suicidal cases) and provide support to protection staff to support beneficiaries with more complex needs. Risk: Appropriate referral pathways are not found for beneficiaries of case management, psychosocial support and outreach activitiesLikelihood: Moderate, based on the organisations and clusters knowledge of the lack of appropriate services required by the population and especially it’s most vulnerable members, such as specialised mental health, legal assistance and protection, secure living conditions Mitigation Measures: Staff will conduct service mapping in each area we conduct activities, including emergency outreach and have on hand all available services and supports for those in need. Conscious of the conditions in North West Syria and lack of all required services, psychosocial support will also focus on supporting beneficiaries to cope with their situation in the most positive way possible and target manageable problems to find the ways that they can make some improvements in their lives that they can take some control over, and cope positively in what they can’t, with a focus on valued living and finding meaning within difficult circumstances.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions• The organization continues to have the approvals from local authorities to continue to provide services in targeted locations• The organization can maintain access to targeted locations to deliver the proposes assistance• The security situation remains stable enough to implement the programs on a day to day basis• Non-interference by groups in control of geographic locations in delivery of proposes assistance.• Local community is willing and participates in mental health, GBV and Child Protection activities Risk: Security situation remains volatile where inter armed group violence, criminality, military operations could lead to destabilization of the current status negatively impacting safety and security of staff and beneficiariesMitigation Measure: The organization has developed an extensive security management structure, encompassing a security manager, as well as security officers, assistants, analysts and guards who establish valuable community based contacts enabling acceptance and a steady flow of information. The organization undertakes full Security Risk Assessments of all activity locations identify threats, estimate impact, and assess risks. Staff have emergency contacts and safe houses in Syria in case of emergency; receive regular security updates; are able to receive treatment at NGO supported clinics and medical evacuation in organizations supported ambulances; and only enter an area if they feel comfortable doing so.Security training for staff limit/lessen exposure. Support evacuation/withdrawal of local staff to alternative locations to continue managing program or revise targeting as per changing context. Consider supporting affected staff through Duty of Care, including financial compensation support for treatment of injured staff, for families of staff that may be killed due to conflict activity and severance pay for staff that no longer continue with the organization. Risk: Potential program suspension/termination due to take over by Government of Turkey or Government of Syria; leading to restrictions in implementation of activities without specific approvals (in the case of GoT) or loss of cross border access (in the case of GoS)Mitigation Measures: The organization will explore options to continue support the program through local partner NGOs registered in Turkey in the case of Turkish takeover to maintain support to targeted facilities. The organization will withdraw support in case of GoS takeover in order to protect the safety of the staff as cross border operations are deemed illegal by GoS. In the event of a foreseeable gap in service provision, a contingency plan will be in place for such as event, where the most vulnerable and in need beneficiaries including those receiving Case Management and PSS services will be provided with prior training on personal or family wellness plans, designed with beneficiaries and caregivers/parents for personal follow up care and maintenance of wellbeing.
