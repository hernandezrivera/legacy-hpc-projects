Project Summary

Project Summary
This project aims to provide integrated emergency food security and livelihoods assistance to 60,000 (10,000HHs) vulnerable women, men, boys and girls including IDPs/host communities/ returnees in the IPC crisis and emergency phases in Jonglei and Upper Nile States. This project is in line with the South Sudan Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster Objectives, CO1 Saves lives,CO2. Enhance Protection and CO3 increase resilience and access to services. By providing emergency food security and livelihoods assistance through direct distribution of agricultural inputs, livestock vaccination and treatment services and training on improved agricultural production techniques, this project shall improve access to food, increase vulnerable households food production, strengthen households and community livelihoods and coping capacities, and, consequently, support early recovery and resilience among the targeted households in Jonglei and Upper Nile States. African Leadership Skills Initiative (ALSI) has been operational in the Greater Upper Nile for over a decade with competent and experienced staff that will be rapidly deployed to respond to the needs. The organization currently has implemented FAO-funded food security and livelihoods project (Emergency Livelihood Response Programme-ELRP) in Ayod County for more than seven years. ALSI has built very good rapport and excellent community goodwill, local state ministries linkages, strong local networks and coordination with the FSL Cluster. ALSI shall employ multi-sectoral approach to promote vegetable and crop production, reduction in post-harvest losses and safe guarding animal health, restoration and support of fishing activities, focus on restoring and strengthening livelihoods alongside more multi sector integrated actions (e.g. WASH, Nutrition & Health), advocacy for women’s needs, voice and rights, capacity building and skills transfers, livestock vaccinations and treatments, and increase efficiency and informed assistance through robust assessment and post distribution monitoring. Through inter-cluster synergies, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, protection including Gender Based Violence/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, Disability inclusion, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

1 - Needs

NeedsAs of December 2021, an estimated 5.3 million people were reached with some form of humanitarian assistance. However, to enable the response to people impacted by flooding, humanitarian organizations were compelled to the re-program in-country support, which resulted in a reduction of food assistance to internally displaced people, refugees and communities in acute food and livelihood crises. These unmet needs increased vulnerability for populations in 2022. More people are likely to experience severe food insecurity in 2022. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). This represents a 7 per cent increase from the 7.7 million in 2021. The counties of most concern are in the states of Jonglei, Lakes, Unity and Warrap (specifically Greater Tonj), with the most extreme case being Fangak County in Jonglei State, where the situation is comparable to Pibor County in 2021. Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. In 2022, the precarious situation is expected to be further exacerbated by the country’s increasing humanitarian caseload, the expected increase in the duration of needs in the most affected areas and the projected further reduction in humanitarian assistance across the country. Food assistance is expected to decrease by 10 to 20 per cent from the already reduced levels in 2021. The current economic crisis is impacting people’s ability to access markets, while conflict, flooding and seasonal effects are limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services. An estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be at risk of acute malnourishment. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent was reported in people in twenty-six counties in six states (Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Eastern Equatoria). The leading cause of morbidity is malaria, followed by acute respiratory infections and acute watery diarrhea. In 2022, morbidity rates are expected to increase proportionally for each of these causes as they did in 2021, compared to 2020. People are at constant risk of an outbreak of infection. 2 - Project activities

Support vulnerable and food insecure households with crop and vegetable kits, and agricultural tools and fishing kits; Train community leaders, lead farmers and extension staffs on improved agricultural production techniques including the management of Fall Army Warm(FAW); Train fisher folks on improved fishing and fish preservation techniques; Train farmers on vegetable utilization and preservation; Vaccinate livestock against endemic livestock diseases; Treat livestock heads against endemic diseases; Provide refresher training for CAHWs; Provide new training for new CAHWs; Train new stock persons/AHAs; Provide refresher training to existing SP/AHAs on cold chain managements and monitoring and CAAP; Conduct Participatory Disease Search sessions for emerging livestock diseases; Install and maintain solar fridges for vaccine handling at the field; Procure and distribute new CAHW kits; Capacity Building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production training( farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation(business skills), vocational training etc., among targeted beneficiaries; Support value chain addition e.g. through milk and fish preservation, and marketing among targeted beneficiaries; Train communities on improved natural resources management practices; Identify and support DRR projects which target youth; Conduct post-distribution, post-planting and post-harvest surveys; Conduct monitoring sessions and compile reports.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ALSI will seek feedback from affected populations in order to address any problems that may arise during program implementation and to improve how activities are designed and carried out. Beneficiaries will be informed about how they can register complaints or provide feedback in a variety of ways. For example, during distributions, a helpdesk will be set up where beneficiaries can provide feedback or register complaints with a trained staff member. Beneficiaries are then notified about availability of such services. The staff member at the help desk will be a member of the monitoring and evaluation (M&E) team, rather than the distribution team, in order to ensure adequate separation of duties. The helpdesk staff member will have a standard form for receiving and recording feedback/complaints. At each of the distribution sites, there will also be a complaint box where community members can submit written complaints if they prefer. In addition, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) committees will be established and trained in each community to receive and submit to ALSI any complaints/feedback community members bring them. Beneficiaries will be informed about the availability of these complaint and feedback channels on a regular basis (i.e. during community meetings, training sessions, before and during distribution exercises) so that they are well informed.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project beneficiaries will be informed of their right to file complaints, including reports of SEA and sexual harassment through established reporting mechanisms before commencing the project activities. ALSI highly safeguards safety, confidentiality, and dignity of the reporting beneficiary and handles the matter with utmost respect and in a non-discriminatory manner. Project-specific complaints are directed to the PSEA focal point in the head office. Any report of SEA or sexual harassment at the field level is reported immediately to the Head of Field Office who will communicate with ALSI’s Project Manager and Executive Director, per the ALSI’s Whistleblower Policy, for an appropriate response and/or investigation. According to ALSI’s Whistleblower Policy, the PSEA focal person will enlist support from various individuals to ensure a prompt, diligent, and confidential investigation appropriate to the complaint.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

ALSI has identified the following protection related risks and analysis: Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services (Likelihood is low and impact is high). To mitigate this risk, ALSI will ensure that registration is conducted and assistance provided as close to the beneficiaries as possible. ALSI will work with existing community structures and local authorities to ensure that appropriate locations are identified for project activities so that beneficiaries do not walk for long distances for registration or to receive assistance. Activities will be implemented in line with the seasonal calendar to ensure accessibility to project locations. Inputs will be distributed and trainings conducted prior to the rainy season during which some locations are either cut off or inaccessible. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities (likelihood is low, impact is high). As a mitigation measure, ALSI will adopt participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches throughout the project life-cycle from the onset of the project including consultations during identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. During the identification and registration of beneficiaries, ALSI’s project team will adopt the FAO’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS, Survivors of GBV etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms (likelihood is low, impact is medium). To mitigate this risk, ALSI will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns. ALSI will strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria and how to provide feedback or complains to ALSI. This will be done through regular meetings with the target communities and use of IEC materials to disseminate information about the CRM. ALSI upholds a code of conduct that is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Beneficiaries will be informed of their right to file complaints, including reports of SEA and sexual harassment, through established reporting mechanisms including setting up hotlines. ALSI highly safeguards safety, confidentiality and dignity of the reporting beneficiary and handles the matter with utmost respect and in a non-discriminatory manner. Program-specific complaints are directed to the PSEA focal point at the head office. Any report of SEA or sexual harassment is reported immediately to the Executive Director who will communicate with programs team, per the whistleblower policy, for an appropriate response and/or investigation. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources (likelihood is low, impact is high). ALSI's mitigation measure for this risk entails developing and sensitizing the local authorities and community groups (youth and women groups) on the beneficiary selection criteria. The beneficiary selection process will be monitored by ALSI staff to ensure objectivity and impartiality. PDMs will be conducted to ascertain the objectivity of the beneficiary selection process and appropriate corrective measures will be implemented in case of any issues identified.
