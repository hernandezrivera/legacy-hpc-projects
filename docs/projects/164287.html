Project Summary

UNRWA estimates that 418,000 Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance in 2020. Food insecurity continues to negatively affect the Palestine refugees who depend on UNRWA assistance to meet their minimum food needs. To mitigate the impact of poverty and food insecurity, the Agency will provide in-kind food assistance to up to 135,000 Palestine refugees including those belonging to the following categories of extreme vulnerability: families headed by a person older than 60 years; families headed by a person with disability and people with disabilities; families headed by a female; and orphans. Palestine refugees affected by acute emergencies, including newly displaced persons and those directly impacted by new or increased hostilities, and families and individuals spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas that were severely affected by the conflict and/or lacking basic services will also be eligible for in-kind support based on needs. The Agency will continue to monitor the availability of food on the markets and will retain operational flexibility to adjust to a changing context as appropriate.

4 - Needs

Of the 438,000 Palestine refugees currently estimated to be in Syria, two-thirds have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict. An estimated 40 per cent of them continue to be displaced within Syria, with Yarmouk, Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps, previously home to more than 30 per cent of Palestine refugees in Syria, destroyed by the hostilities. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns in 2020 will increase. According to UNRWA observations, many of those who have returned are suffering a secondary displacement, affecting their ability to sustain basic needs, including food. A socio-economic assessment of living conditions conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in absolute poverty (less than US$ 2 person/day) prior to receiving UNRWA cash assistance. It also found that old age, female-headed households, and households headed by persons with disabilities are three of the most vulnerable Palestine refugee groups and suffer from high unemployment rates and poverty. Rising inflation, increasing prices of basic commodities, destruction and damage of houses, high rental costs, limited livelihood opportunities, and a depreciating local currency continue to severely undermine household resilience and coping capacities, contributing to households low purchasing power as they struggle to meet their daily food needs. Prolonged displacement and loss and lack of livelihood opportunities Palestine refugees are facing, coupled with the high inflation and soaring currency depreciation are therefore making them particularly vulnerable to food insecurity. It is expected that a majority of Palestine refugees will continue to rely on UNRWA assistance to meet their most basic needs in 2020. Families' inability to meet those needs can increase their vulnerability and could force them to adopt harmful coping mechanisms, affecting physical and mental well-being.

5 - Implementation of activities

To mitigate the impact of poverty and food insecurity, UNRWA will provide the most vulnerable Palestine refugees and a few additional identified vulnerable cases with in-kind food assistance through three distribution rounds during 2020. The Agency will continue to monitor the availability of food on the markets and will retain operational flexibility to adjust to a changing context as appropriate. Ready-to-eat food baskets will also be provided to Palestine refugees affected by acute emergencies, including newly displaced persons and those directly impacted by new or increased hostilities. Families and individuals spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas that were severely affected by the conflict and/or lacking basic services will also be eligible for in-kind support based on needs. Monitoring and Evaluation: UNRWA has a dedicated Area Support Team and a SFO based Programme Support Team. Monitoring will be guaranteed through regular field visits and feedback from implementing staff based in the field and their respective programme. The Programme Support Office has a Monitoring and Evaluation system in place that closely follows up on and provides feedback and donor reports for all projects.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Beneﬁciaries face exploitation or abuse travelling to and from distribution points where they receive food items.o Likelihood: medium o Impact: medium Mitigating measures: - In order to minimize problems in accessing distribution points, UNRWA allows ﬂexibility within the families as to who collects the assistance and a delegation system allows individuals of families to nominate a trusted person to collect the assistance on their behalf. - UNRWA also provides lists of what beneﬁciaries received to facilitate their travel while carrying in-kind assistance. Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of GBV and sexual harassment, particularly at locations with high numbers of beneﬁciaries. o Likelihood: medium o Impact: medium Mitigation Measures:- UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time. - UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming ensuring principles such as safety and dignity and access are incorporated into distributions - UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a regular basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. Risk: Explosive remnants of war (ERW) are recognized as a protection risk for Palestine refugees across the country, including when accessing UNWRA services. o Likelihood: low o Impact: high Mitigation Measures: - UNRWA has prioritized awareness raising and training to staff, community members, including children, on reducing exposure to risks related to ERW. - UNRWA sends text messages to beneﬁciaries to advise them on changes to services in order to minimize unnecessary movements.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A mix of humanitarian and post-conflict interventions: Whilst Damascus, Rural Damascus and most of the major urban concentrations in Homs, Hama, Latakia, and Dera’a experienced relative calm throughout 2019, hostilities continued in other areas of the country and the overall situation remained tense and unstable. In 2020, the Agency anticipates that a mix of humanitarian and development approaches that are supportive of post-conflict rehabilitation will be needed to respond to the different needs of Palestine refugees across Syria. High levels of humanitarian needs especially amongst the most vulnerable Palestine refugees: A socio-economic assessment conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in poverty. Depreciation of the Syrian pound, rising inflation, increasing prices of basic commodities, destruction and damage of houses, and limited livelihood opportunities continue to undermine resilience and coping capacities, particularly for those most vulnerable.Increase in returns; a large proportion of Palestine refugees in Syria will remain displaced: the levels of displacement are foreseen to remain high among Palestine refugees, as access to Yarmouk is still restricted and the level of destruction there is significant. Returns to Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps, which have sustained major destruction, remain slow, due to the level of damage to basic infrastructure and the lack of public services. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns from out of Syria in 2020 will increase, as will HLP and CD needs. Secondary displacements is also anticipated among Palestine refugees due to their legal status. Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to face serious protection challenges: It is anticipated that protection risks will persist and may intensify in newly accessible areas where spontaneous returns, both from within and outside of Syria, are expected to increase.
