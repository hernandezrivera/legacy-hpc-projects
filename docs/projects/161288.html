Project Summary

Community Action Organization (CAO) is a national NGO working in the locations of Jonglei, Upper Nile and Warrap, offering humanitarian and recovery aid in the sectors of nutrition, WASH, protection, and livelihoods in partnership with UNICEF, WFP, FAO and UNDP. through the Provision of WASH services as keys to mitigate the effects of malnutrition, food insecurity, and disease occurrence among U5s, PLW, PWDs, and the most vulnerable communities in Fangak, Canal/Pigi, Panyikang, Nasir and Twic Counties.
WASH needs continue to emerge despite humanitarian assistance offered in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Warrap mainly due to issues surrounding access, lack of functional markets, lack of adequate WASH infrastructure, flooding, displacement and poor HH WASH practices as a result of attitudes and dispositions towards acceptable WASH standards. Integrated Phase Classifications for the first half of 2020 indicate a likelihood of a worsening food insecurity situation mainly affecting Jonglei and Upper Nile.
In line with the Humanitarian Response Plan standards, objectives of physical & mental well-being, living standards, recovery & resilience, CAO targets a total of 75,000 persons adhering to cluster objectives and activity packages that include; Integrating WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package; Integrating WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level; Provision of timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees and mitigate WASH-related gender-based violence .
Out of the 75,000 targeted, 19,000 will be in Fangak, 12,600 in Pigi, 20,200 in Panyikang, 16,000 in Nasir/Luakpiny and 7,200 in Twic county, 45% of these IDPs, 15% returnees, 40% host communities 65% female, 5% elderly.
CAO has extensive knowledge about the local customs and its strong links with communities and local authorities has gained acceptance of its presence in relatively insecure areas. This project activities will be monitored in line with CAO policies, Cluster and joint results-based Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework taking into account the project log frame and work plans as well as agreed standards and cluster guidelines.

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian situation persists in Jonglei and Upper Nile due to the effects of conflict and current flood challenges access, service delivery, and adverse weather conditions. Communities in the locations of Fangak, Canal/Pigi (Jonglei), Panyikang, Nasir/Luakpiny (Upper Nile) and Twic (Warrap State) continually experience unacceptable living conditions due to poor access to clean and safe water, sanitation and hygiene services; Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 1.25 million people, followed by Upper Nile state with 845,000 people. With joint populations exceeding 427,399, (Fangak – 193,053, Pigi – 99,781, Panyikang – 64,879, and Twic - 262,546), more than 50% of these communities fall under IPC 3 and 4, 70% in Pigi, 60% Fangak, 50% in Panyikang and 20% in Twic County. 14 counties are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in 2020. Jonglei State is expected to have the highest number of people estimated to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at 1.18 million, followed by former Upper Nile State, with 765,000 (55% of these female). Poor WASH conditions contribute to high levels of malnutrition and food insecurity affecting mostly under 5s, pregnant and lactating women, elderly and PWDs. High GAM rates are linked to poor access to safe and clean water, sanitation services hence leading to high cases of water-related diseases. GAM prevalence exceeded the WHO emergency threshold in Jonglei (19.4) and Upper Nile (16.3). REACH and FSNMS surveys indicate that over 78% of the communities in the locations lack access to a secure source of water, with over 86% using surface water for both domestic and personal use. Open water sources are most times contaminated due to poor fecal disposal as a result of a lack of latrines. Latrine usage is below 20% in the locations, with majority of those with no access to safe and secure latrine are women and girls. Latrine HH surveys indicate a low latrine coverage especially in Jonglei and Upper Nile; a similar trend observed in Twic County. Over 80% of the communities reported practicing open defecation. The insufficiency of water points has further been worsened by increased concentration of IDPs in remote secure locations, increase in returnees due to stability, breakdown due to over usage and lack of access by humanitarian actors to carry out routine rehabilitation and repairs. An analysis of the Food Consumption Score (FCS) indicates gaps in dietary intakes among HHs, with low dietary diversification, coupled with grim figures on the household hunger scale. Poor WASH standards continue to contribute to high levels of malnutrition in Jonglei – 21.5% GAM; Upper Nile – 18.2% GAM and Warrap – 16% GAM leading to increased incidences in child mortality. Torrential rains in the latter part of 2019, leading to sustained floods have made access to water points difficult and contributed to the destruction of latrines and bathing shelters. OCHA reports indicate over 600,000 people are affected by floods, exposing entire villages to water-related diseases. Protection issues are closely linked to access to basic needs such as water, sanitation, and hygiene. Women bear the brunt of collecting water, majority from distant water points thereby exposing them to gender-based violence and SEA, with girls having less time for school and other livelihood activities. More time spent at water points reduces opportunities for women and girls to access other services. Women and girls lack access to dignity and hygiene kits, resulting in protection issues. CAO implements Nutrition, WASH, protection and livelihood projects in the locations of Pigi, Fangak, Panyikang, and Twic has been instrumental in the response and enhancing coordination mechanisms in these locations.

2 - Activities

Amongst other WASH cluster recommended activities, CAO proposes the following activities in the locations targeted: Activity: 1. Provision of safe, clean, adequate and equitable water to the vulnerable population through rehabilitation/repairs of the non-functional water supply facilities such as boreholes, hand-dug wells. Activity: 2. Distribution of household water treatment tablets including PuR and Aquatabs on a case-by-case basis to HHs and communities while ensuring proper usage, sustainable solutions to water treatment and adequate supply for communities without adequate access to clean and safe water. Activity: 3. Distribution of WASH NFIs including buckets, soap to enhance WASH conditions of communities. These will include MHM/Hygiene kits for women and adolescent girls. Activity: 4. Provision of appropriate and safe sanitation services while considering people with special needs including people with disabilities, women, and girls through construction/rehabilitation of gender-appropriate communal latrines at displacement sites, supporting and promoting household latrine constructions, construction of institutional latrines for schools, nutrition (OTP/SC) centers and health facilities. Activity: 5. Training of community WASH stakeholders comprising of hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water management committees with at least 55% female representation. Training packages will range from safe water handling techniques at HH and communal level, sanitation practices, hygiene promotion, WASH in Nutrition, health, protection packages, WASH minimum standards, as well as cross-cutting issues such as environmental protection, HIV/AIDs, and SEA. Activity: 6. Carry out hygiene and sanitation awareness promotion in the locations targeted, in a language the communities understand using several approaches comprising of house to house visits, focus group discussions, mass campaigns at water points, and public gatherings, school hygiene clubs, special needs associations. Activity: 7. Strengthen community feedback mechanisms and coordination in the locations targeted through the establishment of community feedback structures and reporting desks for protection related issues connected to the WASH services provided.

Self-replenishing structures for inputs such as hand pump spare parts, soap, NFIs will be strengthened to ensure continuity and sustainability.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

CAO has a risk management policy, which is periodically communicated to the employees and volunteers as appropriate to ensure that all risk management processes and practices are carried out in accordance with it. The policy includes statements as to risk tolerance and risk appetite which help the organization decide what actions to take if any for risks assessed at different levels i.e extreme, high, medium or low. Risks are assigned to nominated risk owners (senior management) who are then accountable for managing them. Risk assessments are carried out at inception level and will be carried out before any WASH facility setups, through risk identification, risk analysis, and risk evaluation. A risk matrix will be developed, risk levels determined, a risk profile developed and risk ownership assigned.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback mechanisms are in place, with messaging to be conducted in the local dialects, using megaphones and community radios. Several channels will be in place to collect maximum feedback from the stakeholders including interviews with KIs, focus group discussions, suggestion boxes, the establishment of hotlines and PDM exercises. PWDs will be attended to specially, as well as the elderly. The project has created a feedback and complaints mechanism. This followed the recent meeting with the small group focused discussion, group discussions and CAO’s team realized that some of the beneficiaries did not want to be known as having complaints. This then forced us to come up with an anonymous way of reporting in such a way that the beneficiaries do not have to indicate their names. However, this only applies to people who can write. We have staged a complaints' box outside our compound for this. We are still looking into ways of having illiterate people report their complaints.
