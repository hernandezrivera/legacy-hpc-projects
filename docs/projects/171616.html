Project Summary

The Gu rainy season flooding in 2020 directly affected 548,339 individuals and displaced 279,108 people in nine regions across the country. The floods have worsened the humanitarian health situation at a time when responders are overwhelmed by COVID-19 responses. The COVID-19 has added to the mental suffering of the affected population and aggravated gender and interpersonal violence (GBV/IPV). The Displaced population, mostly women and adolescent girls, face significant barriers to access reproductive health and malnutrition services during this flooding season as compared to other groups. In 2021, 1.3 million boys and girls and pregnant and lactating women will suffer from high acute malnutrition, with 180,000 children under 5 affected by life threatening severe malnutrition, thus requiring emergency nutrition services. Children under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women and girls (PLWGs) are among the most vulnerable people in the event of humanitarian crises, The proposed intervention will scaleup the provision of health services in Benadir, Galmudug and Hiiran regions for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and vulnerable host communities. The project will support 4 health centres in Benadir, 2Galmudug and one health center Hiiraan with an aim of targeting 63,025 people. Integrated essential lifesaving preventive and curative services will be provided through outpatient consultations for children under-five and those above age five, in addition to laboratory and pharmacy services, reproductive health, basic emergency obstetric services, immunization, and community health education. The IRC aims to increase access to quality life-saving GBV services for women and girls, while enhancing GBV prevention in host and IDP communities. IRC will provide GBV training to the psychosocial first aid (PFA) volunteers. The proposed intervention will support provision of maternal health care and newborn care including safe deliveries, emergency obstetric (BEmONC & CeMOC), and SRH services (including management of STD/HIV treatment and SGBV particularly in geographic areas. The Critical patients attending the mobile clinics and pregnant women with obstetric complications will be referred and managed at the health facilities. Pregnant women in need of Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Care will be referred from the PHCC to tertiary hospital. Community dialogues will target different age groups within the communities and at different times. the proposed intervention will address immediate needs i.e. mitigating the spread of COVID-19 and supporting the public health consequences of the pandemic in target locations including Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) including awareness raising through media and training more community health workers on case definitions as well as to scale up hygiene response in the targeted areas.
Creating access to services: The facilities will receive drugs, pharmaceuticals to accommodate various morbidities.
Ensuring quality of services: The IRC will continue to work to improve the reproductive healthcare through the provision of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP), limited to basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care (BEmONC) services, especially targeting ANC, PNC, family planning/birth spacing, syndromic management of sexually transmitted infections and referral of high-risk complected pregnancies.
Increasing demand for services: This will be done through health promotion, community engagement The IRC will also continue building the capacities and skills for the CHWs to enable community health staff to provide a rapid, effective response in case of a health emergencies disaster like in the current situation, COVID-19. The CHWs will work to dispel rumors, fears and misconceptions received/heard in the community about COVID-19 using the existing community structures and channels of influence, including religious leaders and other respected community leaders.

Project Activities

IRC will provide free primary health care including Reproductive health non-communicable diseases by providing free outpatient consultations to 63,025 drought affected people both IDPs and host communities (including 15,756 women, 18,908 girls, 18,908 boys and 9,453 men) including 15% of people with disabilities. Through the proposed intervention, the IRC will support 5 fixed healthcare facilities (4 in Benadir region and 1 in Dhusamareb) and 1 mobile outreach clinic in Beletweyne. The services offered will be included Communicable Diseases treatment of pneumonia, malaria, diarrhea, measles, skin conditions, injury care in children and urinary tract infections, sexually transmitted infections; Reproductive Health, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CEmONC), Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) ; and Mental Health. In addition, community health worker will Identify, refer complicated deliveries to the health facility. IRC will provide standby ambulance from IDPs to health facilities/hospitals and vice-versa in the effort to reduce maternal and child mortality rate in Somalia. IRC will coordinate closely with the MOH and UNICEF in providing child health and ensure all children below 5 years have been immunized to ensure a great proportion of children under 1 year receive Penta3 and measle vaccines at IRC-supported facilities; further screening all beneficiaries for COVID-19; and providing specialized reproductive health care for pregnant and lactating women that includes skilled deliveries to increase proportion of skilled birth attendance. Furthermore, IRC will provide Clinical management of rape services (CMR) and GBV / IPV services and referral, psychosocial counselling to the stressed and establish referral pathways for different conditions and ensure immediate movement of emergency and critical patient to secondary level of care. In summary, the following specific activities will be undertaken: 1) Provision of outpatient consultations and treatments (both communicable and non-communicable diseases) with access to essential medicines adhering the standard treatment protocols for men, women, boys and girls within the targeted districts.2) Mobile outreach health promotion and preventive services through a network of gender dis-aggregated community health workers on Malaria, Diarrhea, Pneumonia, Vaccine preventable diseases, IYCF, safe motherhood, GBV. 3) Secondary or tertiary referrals for the cases such as obstetric complications and Severe chronic diseases 4) Immunization services (Measles and Polio) to increase the coverage of infant and child vaccinations. 5) Provide integrated Basic Emergency Obstetric and New-born Care (BEmoNC) services, by skilled health workers. 6) Conduct facility-based identification, provide psycho-social support and prompt referral for appropriate services for people with disability and mental health issues 7) Identification, counseling, management and prompt referral for survivors of Gender-based Violence 8) Capacity building of frontline health workers (CHWs, nurses , clinicians) on IMCI service protocols and distribution of guidelines. 9) Provision of procurement of essential drugs and clinical supplies for health facilities. The IRC will also conduct community dialogues (targeting different community social and age groups) during and after outreach to identify key health issues in communities and to address barriers to access; conduct health awareness, community mobilization, education and advocacy activities on MHPSS, disabilities; provide Clinical management of rape services (CMR) and GBV treatment and prevention; distribute PPE to HCWs and community health workers for COVID19 mitigation; and rehabilitate health facility latrines and handwashing stations to promote patient and provider safety and prevent the spread of diseases.

Mitigation Measures

Ensure robust community engagement through sensitization session with all community segments encouraging communities to adopt inclusive representation in community committees and groups.Establish inclusive selection criteria that provides especial emphasis to women, children, people with special needs/disability and marginalized groups.Strengthen the IRC client response mechanism to provide enough room for community engagement to prevent lack or poor inclusion of women, children, people with special needs/disability and marginalized groups in the project.Provide regular awareness raisings at community and during community outreach activities to improve community role in the project activity implementations. The IRC’s client responsive feedback and complaints mechanisms, which are designed through multiple channels to be anonymous and accessible to all groups, including women, girls, IDPs and people with disabilities, collect and analyse data on clients’ perspectives on the quality and relevance of project interventions, and are used to inform timely responses, guide decision-making, and ensure that the program does not negatively impact the communityThe IRC implements and monitors programs through the inclusion of clients and other stakeholders in the feedback mechanism on its delivery performance. Room will be given to clients to report any IRC related misuse of resources, lack of effectiveness, timeliness or responsiveness. A communication structure is in place in collaboration with Complaints Response Mechanism and includes guidance on how to handle complaints and report them to the IRC Management. The community health committees established will serve as forums where feedback on program implementation can be shared and ideas on further improving activities generated. An exit interview will be conducted within the project period to get the feedback from the communityThe main barriers to accessing healthcare in these areas include lack of quality health services including lack of emergency obstetric and neonatal care and minimal staff capacity. In addition to the above, the recurrent conflicts have disrupted routine health programs leaving a large sector of the population without access to health services. Hence IRC will recruit and engage qualified service providers to provide services and outreach responses that create more access to health services.The proposed project supports the gender theme in that it promotes health service provision to all sections of the population including men, women, girls, and boys, without discriminationBuild strong working relationships with respect local authority and line ministries to ensure no potential influence that can alter the project design and resource allocation among target areas.Maintain the employment of the IRC values and transparent mechanisms during the implementation to suppress stakeholder expectationsBuild close collaboration and working relationships with the IDP and host communities to ensure fair project resource distribution based on participative selection criteria that informs the vulnerabilities.
