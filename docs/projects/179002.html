Project Summary

The project aims to enhance the resilience of the most vulnerable people in Aleppo and improve their livelihoods, as it targets six communities affiliated to Azaz district in Aleppo northern countryside.
Aleppo – Azaz – Azaz – Niddeh (C1572) 75 HH
Aleppo – Azaz – Azaz – Nayara (C1573) 75 HH
Aleppo – Azaz – Azaz – Shamarin (C1566) 50 HH
Aleppo – Azaz – Azaz – Salama (C1561) 200 HH
Aleppo – Azaz – Azaz – Kaljibrin (C1575) 100 HH
Aleppo – Azaz – Azaz – Yahmul (C1571) 100 HH
Based on needs assessment, the project has been designed according to the following:
 Increasing food security and enhancing the livelihoods of IDPs and host communities in Aleppo Governorate (Azaz sub-district) by supporting olive value chain as follows:
- 600 households (100% host communities) in Shamarin, Kaljibrin, Salama, Niddeh, Yahmul, Nayara, will be provided with agricultural input coupons to enable them to care for and service 2,400 dunums of the total number of olive trees (4 dunums for each farmer), at least 20% of them will be women. In addition, RF will support marketing the olive oil by purchasing 600 cans of olive oil /16 kg per one/ from the beneficiaries.
- 500 households (100% IDPs) in the same areas will benefit from cash for work for harvesting and pruning the olives.
- 600 IDP households will be supported by distributing one olive oil can to each household once in the project areas.
The main interventions include:
- Provide coupons to purchase the agricultural inputs accompanied by relevant guidance services.
- Capacity building targeting the farmers and members of the local community.
- Supporting IDPs by creating job opportunities in the harvesting and pruning olive trees.
- Supporting the marketing of olive oil by purchasing olive oil from the beneficiaries to help the farmers market their products.
- Distribute olive oil cans to vulnerable IDPs in the project areas.
- Follow up the project with the integrated management of olive tree service.
 RF plans to implement the project by providing one type of compound fertilizer coupons, and two types of pesticide coupons, as well as to support the olive harvesting and pruning, support supplementary irrigation, in addition to support the marketing of olive oil.
 RF will hold awareness sessions with merchants and all beneficiaries to explain the coupons redemption mechanisms and the list of authorized agricultural inputs.
 RF is also planning to increase the awareness of the host community and IDPs regarding Coronavirus risks and the need to take the preventive measures. In addition, RF will distribute free medical masks and hand sanitizer to 500 workers during the harvest and pruning in the fields.
On the other hand, agricultural training will be conducted throughout the project to educate the farmers about the correct scientific methods to be considered in the agriculture in the farmer school established for all 600 targeted farmers, so all technical issues related to olive cultivation will be covered and the farmers will be able to apply the correct practices. In addition, brochures will be distributed to farmers to raise awareness and guide them to the correct scientific methods to be applied. Thus, the project provides integrated services to olive farmers, so that they are guided and assisted in all stages of the season to achieve integration of the project and ensure results.
RF will identify and verify the target beneficiaries through the local councils according to the following eligibility selection criteria:
• Households owning olive trees land, with an area of at least 4 dunums.
• Olive pruners who have the experience and skills.
• Shortlist will be determined based on the vulnerability criteria.
• Female-headed households (widows, divorcees, victims of gender-based violence).
• Large households (more than 5).
• Households with handicapped members.
• Low-income households (less than 50 $)
• Households with elderly (men - women)

4 - Needs

Before the crisis, about 100,000 Syrian households were employing for olive production. Syrian Arab Republic produces nearly one million tons of olives annually (about 200,000 tons of olive oil), making it the fourth largest producer in the world. Now Aleppo governorate has the largest number of olive trees, with 23 million trees.Key areas of supporting the value chain include providing productive inputs, creating income-generating opportunities, and strengthening community services and markets.There is an urgent need to support olive farmers due to the significant increase in the prices of production inputs, the decrease in the marketing value of agricultural production, especially in light of Covid-19 epidemic and the devaluation of Syrian pound, which has worsened the situation for olive farmers, and therefore most of them do not provide good agricultural services to olive trees.Targeted sites are considered to be of high priority to support olive farmers, because the annual production of most of them has been affected. The large decrease of production, lack of markets, the decrease of the market value of production, in addition to their struggle to secure their livelihood prompted many farmers to cut down the trees and use them for heating. Cutting olive tree is considered as the elimination of the household’s productive assets completely, leading to a deterioration in the household’s nutritional status in the future, especially in light of the lack of other sources of income.On the level of IDPs, there are a large number of IDPs who are neither work nor have any sources of income. Therefore, finding alternative job opportunities for them will contribute to reduce unemployment rates in northwest Syria in general

5 - Implementation of activities

According to RF policy and procurement procedures, agricultural input vouchers will be distributed to the beneficiary farmers, and they will be instructed on how to use these vouchers in addition to increasing their awareness of the project's objectives.1- Agricultural inputs will be distributed through the vouchers, where each farmer will receive agricultural inputs that include fertilizers, pesticides and agricultural tools, at a value of 150 $.2- Supplementary irrigation: each farm will receive an irrigation service by contracting with a service provider, at the rate of 10 water tanks for each beneficiary. This amount is enough for two supplementary irrigations, and covers 80% of the water needs of olive trees during the summer.3- Cash for Work (CFW): 200 unemployed workers, especially IDPs, will be employed during the olive harvest, and each worker will receive 10 $ within two months (60 days). (60 days * 10 $ * 200 = 120,000 $).4- Crushing and squeezing olives wages will be supported for the farmers, as each farmer will be paid a wage for squeezing 500 kg of olives, and this process will be controlled by distributing vouchers to the beneficiary farmers. As the expected production of 2,400 dunums is approximately 300 tons of olives.5- 5% of the olive oil produced will be purchased from each farmer and then distributed to 300 displaced households.6- Farmers School: training sessions on good agricultural practices for olive trees will be held through theoretical workshops in addition to practical practices in the fields.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Depending on the protection principles, these guidelines will be adhered to in order to ensure the safety of the beneficiaries, as all project staff will sign a code of conduct and many measures will be taken such as avoiding large gatherings, maintaining the confidentiality and coding of user data, respecting the privacy of the beneficiaries when documenting project activities, in addition to avoiding taking pictures that violate the dignity of the beneficiary.The project provides equal services to the targeted groups, with prioritizing women and elderly, and will enhance the resilience of these groups through conducting awareness sessions to claim their rights and enhance cooperation between them.In addition, do-no-harm principles will be emphasized by not discriminating against any specific population group (both the host community and IDPs are deliberately targeted) and by being aware of the existing dynamics.During the distribution of agricultural materials, RF will establish separate waiting and entry lines at registrations, distributions and exhibitions for vulnerable individuals such as pregnant women, people with children, unaccompanied children, elderly and sick, in addition to assisting people with disabilities.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Security hampers access of staff, volunteers, service providers, and supplies and services to project locations. RFs' has a security management plan and security protocols that all staff is trained on and required to adhere to. RFs' conduct a continuous assessment of the situation; security protocols are frequently updated and adapted to ensure risks are minimized. In cases where prolonged security affects operations or the project as a whole, RFs' will update OCHA and discuss scenarios for continued or adapted implementation. - Perception of breaching humanitarian principles: Carry out a monthly evaluation against RF’s red lines with critical analysis of impartiality, voice, and safety. Ensure implementation of the needs-based program serving the most vulnerable communities. - beneficiaries data protection is compromised: Conduct awareness sessions to staff, volunteers, and partners on the legal compliance with the regulations seek beneficiaries consent before sharing their info - Government approvals: delays in project approvals leading to delays in implementation. RFs' has designed its project work plans to allow for potential delays in approvals. - Tension between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. RFs' uses a participatory community approach to develop the vulnerability and eligibility criteria and the selection of beneficiaries to ensure transparency, acceptance and accountability to different stakeholders. - Inability to receive funds in the country due to sanctions: Open accounts with different banks to diversify transfer routing and corresponding banks, Increase foreign cash reserve within the country and balance the pool of suppliers/ contractors to include local and international companies.
