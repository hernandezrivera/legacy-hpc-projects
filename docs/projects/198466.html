Project Summary

Under this project, IOM will scale-up its provision of shelter assistance targeting internally displaced people (IDP), returnees and host communities. IOM will:
(1) Save the lives of displacement affected people through the provision of different types of emergency shelter solutions and household items in camps and camp-like settings;
(2) Sustain lives of displacement affected people by providing medium-term shelter solutions in support of decongestion of camps and camp-like settings;
(3) Build the lives of displacement affected people through protection-informed transitional and durable shelter solutions;
shelters are conducted for continued safety and comfort of the affected population.
(4) Promote local knowledge, locally-adaptable technologies, and innovative response modalities (cash, voucher, and hybrid) to provide medium- to long-term shelter solutions.
IOM’s shelter/NFI activities will be implemented in three states of Northeast Nigeria i.e. Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, targeting 460,440 individuals. IOM ensures that all shelter activities conducted are accountable to the affected population and that the specific needs of project beneficiaries are considered, including those related to gender. IOM will ensure that kits and other shelter solutions are harmonized at sector level and coordinated with partners.
The project will also include the continuation and scaling-up of Non-Food Items (NFI) support for people in camp and camp-like settings, in host communities, including for new arrivals. To ensure the timely response to the emerging needs in case of influx of new arrivals and locations that has the risk of being affected of heavy rains and/or fire outbreaks, IOM will preposition NFI kits in or close to critical locations, where IOM has established agreements with partner warehouses.
IOM will ensure the participation of women, men, girls and boys to capture their needs in the project design and implementation. The design of activities and services will address specific needs of each gender and different age groups, and address women's, girls' and boys’ specific needs, especially in terms of
protection and access concerns. To illustrate, (1) the content of NFI kits will be tailored in accordance to the needs of different age and gender groups (2) All activities related to this project will equally target the different age and gender groups and will be delivered on a sustainable and equitable basis. Regarding assessment of gender specific needs, monitoring and evaluation and to measure the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations and set-up communication channels with them where possible, and all activities will be conducted based on Do-No-Harm principles.

1 - Needs

The situation in Northeast Nigeria is caused by several interrelated issues such as terrorism, banditry, violent killings, kidnappings, farmer and herder resource conflict, continued unrest, separatist agitations, environmental pollution, erosion, flooding and drought. The Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO- Feb 2022) indicated an estimated 8.4 million people in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states in acute need of humanitarian and protection assistance. According to IOM’s DTM (Round 41 of 2022), there were a total of 2,197,824 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) that were living in 290 camps and camp-like settlements, while some were living among host communities in 2,075 locations. Similarly, there were 1,983,130 returnees recorded and more than 336,000 Nigerians have taken refuge in Cameroon, Chad, and Niger. Whereas conflict and environmental factors are major drivers of migration in Nigeria, desertification has forced nomadic herders to change their migration patterns, resulting in conflict over increased trespass on farmlands, particularly in the country’s Middle Belt. The farmer-herder conflict and related unrest has further spread to other affected locations with the increase in climate-related pressure. According to UNHCR (2022), Nigeria hosts 73,000 refugees from Chad, Niger and Cameroon, many of whom are elderly, pregnant women, children, and people with disabilities, and are particularly vulnerable to health and protection concerns when they undertake long journeys during migration, mostly on foot. The joint multi-sectoral assessments undertaken in Shelter/NFI/Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) sector and partners in BAY states in 2022 indicate that the evidence-based unmet needs for shelter are 301,428 households, whereas the unmet needs for NFIs are 230,793 households. This caseload comprises of IDPs living in camps and host communities, and non-displaced host communities that are hosting IDPs in overcrowded circumstances.

2 - Activities

Shelter Solutions: (1) Conducting shelter needs assessments, with all groups including the most vulnerable groups in the community (IDPs and Host Communities); (2) Provision and pre-positioning of emergency shelter kits; (3) Construction of emergency shelters, (4) Rehabilitation and partitioning of damaged public buildings for accommodating IDPs; (5) Reinforcing and repairing damaged emergency shelters; (6) Upgrading emergency shelters; (7) Construction of improved emergency shelters; (8) Construction of transitional shelters; (9) Construction of mudbrick shelters; (10) Provision of emergency shelter assistance through cash or voucher programming; (11) Provision of cash for shelter repair for returnee households with damaged buildings; (12) Provision of rental subsidy assistance to IDPs; (13) Cash assistance to small traders of construction materials; (14) Reinforcing and repairing damaged improved emergency shelters; (15) Reinforcing and repairing damaged transitional shelters; (15) Provision of shelter repair kits in return areas; (16) Provision of maintenance materials; (17) Shelter improvements for persons with disabilities; (18) Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) for distribution of shelter kits, repair kits and cash assistance; (19) Conduct Post Construction Monitoring for constructed emergency and transitional shelters NFI assistance and consolidate reports. NFI assistance: (1) Conduct NFI needs assessments with all groups including the most vulnerable groups in the community (IDPs and Host Communities); (2) Procurement of NFI kits that are culturally appropriate, basic and improved kits as approved and endorsed by the sector and / or vendors selection for voucher-based response; (3) Prepositioning and stockpiling of basic NFI kits in or close to areas of expected influx of new arrivals and in locations likely to experience heavy damage from storms in the rainy season; (4) Distribution of basic and improved kits to new arrivals in both displacement sites and host communities; (5) Distribution of improved NFI kits to IDPs living in camps and non-camp settings; (6) Sensitization of communities on the use of NFI kits; (7) Conduct Market assessment in locations with market availability; (8) Distribution of improved NFI kits with a cash component to the most vulnerable IDPs in camps and host community households; (9) Conduct Focus Group Discussion with the communities, ensuring the involvement of women, girls, and youth through all stages of the intervention; (10) Conduct PDM / Beneficiary satisfaction survey.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The HDP nexus offers the opportunity for actors from different sectors to learn from each other through the lens of comparative advantage. It offers collaborative approaches that allow humanitarian actors to support the work of development and peace actors. However, the interventions of Humanitarian, Development and Peace actors working through the nexus must be informed by the context. By leveraging on its vast operational footprint, IOM remains committed to supporting the local and state Governments in the pockets of stability to find a durable solution for IDPs, returnees as well as the local host population affected by the conflict, with a community driven, holistic and integrated approach. Some of the key project activities in support of the HDP nexus will entail the sustainable integration of IDPs and durable shelter and livelihood solutions for returned IDPs in north-east Nigeria.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM plans to guarantee the participation of women, men, girls and boys as equals to ensure that the special needs of each group are taken into consideration. The design of activities and services will address specific needs of each gender and different age groups, and address women's, girls' and boys’ specific needs, especially in terms of protection and access concerns. To illustrate, (1) the content of NFI kits will be tailored in accordance with the needs of different age and gender groups (2) All activities related to this project will equally target the different age and gender groups and will be delivered on a sustainable and equitable basis. Regarding assessment of gender specific needs, monitoring and evaluation and the measurement of the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations during missions and set-up communication channels with them where possible, and all activities will be conducted based on Do-No-Harm principles. The respective distributions will be conducted by a team of IOM staff. The team will include IOM Shelter staff to verify the kits provided and offer technical advice as to the use of the kits and shelter repair; IOM Psychosocial Support staff responsible to support the identification of vulnerable beneficiaries and ensuring safe, secure and equitable access to the assistance distributed and services accessed. In addition to IOM staff,the Organization will seek to promote participation from community leaders in monitoring the distributions IOM also provides continuous prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) trainings for all IOM and partner staff hired. IOM has due to its longstanding presence in Northeast Nigeria developed contextualized capacity in the area of protection mainstreaming and supported the establishment of agency as well as inter-agency referral mechanisms to support protection outcomes throughout programming.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons with Specific Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision making process.IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project. IOM will utilize the Community Based Participatory Planning (CBPP) approach throughout the period of the project. This approach entails involving the community at every stage of the project to contribute to making important decisions towards the project implementation and giving them a sense of belonging/ownership of the project activities. Community engagement meetings will be held before the beginning of every key activity like project activity design, activity planning, beneficiary registration and project activity monitoring and evaluation. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. IOM has already established complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFM) in many sites across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States. For any distributed items, IOM will ensure the community receives proper briefing on the selection methods and the date, time and place of distributions to take place ahead of time. During distributions, demonstrations and sensitization is done on the usage of the items and the cash assistance and sampled post distribution visits. During distributions, IOM also has a separate Complaints and Response desk for receiving instant feedback.
