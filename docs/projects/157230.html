Project Summary

The project is designed through addressing the life-saving objective and 3 outcomes of the FSL sector in the HRP 2019 through interventions aiming at reducing acute food and livelihoods insecurity by providing emergency assistance. It targets three states of BN (Damazine of a population of 317,906 and FSL target 21,268, Roseries population 161,295 and FSL target 21,581), SD state (Shargjabal Marra population 14,785 and FSL target 2,746), SK state (Abujubaiha 201,095 FSL target 13,056, Talodi population 41,336 and FSL target 17,529) as indicated in 2019 HNO. These 5 localities are prioritized as highly in need of FSL interventions in the HRP 2019.
The project targets directly 23,100 conflict affected people (11,600 men and 11,500 women) in these areas to reduce mortality and morbidity rates related to food deficiencies and livestock diseases by increasing the capacity of the IDPs and most vulnerable host communities to produce their own food. Different interventions were designed to address both farmers and livestock owners by providing agriculture and livestock emergency inputs packages in kind and using a voucher and cash transfer method which helps mobilize the local market as well and building the capacity of individuals and increasing their skills and knowledge; training of small scale farmers on fodder production and storage, conduction of vaccination campaigns and extension sessions by providing emergency livelihoods training and start up kits such as cash transfer for income generating environment friendly interventions. The interventions will benefit children nutrition status by providing access to adequate food from own production, which also contributes in reducing malnutrition from the nutritious food aspect. The project also considers gender and environment as important cross cutting issues.

Needs

Nowadays, Sudan is facing acute economic crisis that affects the food security of the people in general and in particular the vulnerable war affected communities. As indicated in the FSL sector HRP 2019 “Rising prices, compounded by limited access to livelihoods opportunities, have been identified as the main drivers of food insecurity and poor food consumption, which have led to a severe decline in the purchasing power of the most vulnerable communities including returnees, refugees, IDPs, as well as vulnerable host communities and residents”.According to the IPC phase 3 and phase 4, as indicated in the HRP 2019 of FSL sector, a number of 159 out of the 171 assessed localities by the IPC in October 2018 are classified as food insecure at crisis (IPC Phase 3) and emergency levels (IPC Phase 4). Among these localities the 5 localities of Damazine and Roseries in BN, Shargjabal Marra in SD, and Abujubaiha and Talodi in SK are targeted in the project. The target areas lack most of the basic services in particular FSL, and the need for FSL interventions was identified by NIDAA in the past and still is existing since the number of IDPs has increased due to the new conflict last year in the surrounding areas in particular in Sharg Jabal Marra locality as a newly access area same as in BN and SK targeted localities. acute food insecurity persists among IDP households in areas worst-affected by conflict. Internally Displaced Persons, or IDPs, and host communities have been using a variety of coping mechanisms to deal with food insecurity. Families have reduced the number of meals they consume to just one per day. Women-headed households are mostly affected, besides the other vulnerable groups as elderly, children and disabled people. Other common copings mechanisms used include feeding on wild fruits, leaves, and roots; families with livestock are selling or slaughtering their animals. Malnutrition is common among all ages, particularly for children below five years of age and women who are pregnant or lactating. beside that environment is harshly degraded and IDPs depends mainly on natural resources such as trees for wood and water. Inflation and scarce of cash has affected severely the purchasing power of people.

Activities

1\ Conduct a voucher for agriculture inputs and services for 4,000 HHs (20,000 peoples including 10,000 women) IDPs (new/old) and war affected in 4 localities of SK and BN localities. Voucher is equivalent to 50 USD per each beneficiary covering inputs and services costs(land rental)). NIDAA conducted voucher for agric inputs in two SHF projects in 2014 and 2016 in three states of SK, WK and BN. The cash voucher cost is decided based on previous experience and consultation with the local communities and comparing it with WFP and FAO standard kits. NIDAA will contract the local suppliers’ to compete as markets are functioning in the 4 localities and accessible. This will mobilize the market and provide the beneficiaries with a freedom of choice. Also will enable IDPs rent land as they have no land and cannot afford it.2\ Train 200 people (150 men and 50 women) in agriculture and veterinary extension and environment conservation and equip them (with inputs through cash transfer) to carry on emergency extension in all the target 5 localities benefiting 1,000 people per locality (5,000 50/50 M/W).3\ Support extension sessions about agriculture intermediate methods and modern techniques and veterinary extension and environment conservation for 2,500 (1,250 women) from all the 5 localities.4\ Train 200 small scale farmers (M/F 50/50) in all 5 localities about fodder production, processing and preservation and supplementary feeding for their kept animals which they brought with them to reduced food losses and strengthened post-harvest techniques through improved food and crop storage and processing facilities.5\ Provide cash grants for 200 people (50 in each locality) to promote income-generation, self-reliance and equitable access to resources and decision-making and promote usage of drip-irrigation techniques. project committees will verify beneficiaries based on a vulnerability criteria prioritizing women. NIDAA sub-offices will follow up with beneficiaries.
