Project Summary

The people of Tribal Districts Orakzai and Kurram have seen a protracted state of displacement in the aftermath of military operation. In mid-2015, because of the improved security situation and a more conducive environment for returns, the GoP initiated the return process of IDPs to different Tribal Districts, based on de-notification notices issued by the FATA Secretariat. Since 2010 about 438,657 families have been facilitated by the Government and humanitarian partners to returning process.
Assessments show that most of the health infrastructure in return areas is damaged, while the functional health facilities have limited functionality due to inadequate staff and resources. There is urgent need to address the health concerns of the returned families with objective that returned men, women, boys, girls, elderly and Person with Disabilities, have access to essential lifesaving health services that contribute to reduction in morbidity and mortality. The proposed project on the basis of health needs of the returned families in Tribal Districts(Orakzai and Kurram) will have multiple activities to provide primary health care services in 6 Health Facilities of Tribal Districts(Orakzai and Kurram) particularly rehabilitation of 6 health facilities, provision of necessary furniture & fixtures, medical equipment, solarization, free treatment and medication (OPD, EPI, MNCH, RH, Health Education etc.) and referral/ambulance services to patients to secondary as well tertiary healthcare service providers. Through this proposed grant, KAMORE will provide Primary Health Care services to a total of 52,000 individuals including 13,520 men, 15,080 women, 11960 girls and 11,440 boys. all 6 health facilities will be provided with gender balance teams of human resource for smooth Running of each Health Facility comprises of :01 Medical Officer, 01 Medical technician, 01 LHV, , 01 Dispenser, 01 Midwives, , 01 Vaccinator, 01 Naib Qasid, 01 Daye, 01 Chowkidar and social mobilization team for orienting communities on basic health hygiene messages. 2000 clean delivery kits (CDK) will be provided to the identified pregnant women for safe deliveries and 2000 baby kits (BK) will be given for the newborn babies to save their health and protect from infections in 6 health Facilities of two selected tribal districts.
The project will integrate various approaches. KAMORE will follow principle of Participation's in its programing and community notable will be taken in confidence for conducting all field level intervention (hygiene sessions, outreach as well free medical camps and de-worming campaigns of children specially for school going children). Social mobilization teams would deliver program introductions in the catchment areas to ensure participatory approach by involving the community and strengthen outreach mechanism. Social mobilization teams will form 12 health management committees (6 male 6 female) in the catchment area to ensure community participation and develop the sense of ownership, responsibility and community based monitoring. Under the proposed grant a total of 14000 individuals in the catchment area will be provided with hygiene promotion sessions to educate male, female and children on best HH practices.
For efficient delivery of the services to meet the health needs, the targeted health facilities will be renovated and provided with necessary equipment, medicines and supplies. KAMORE will sign an MOU with health directorate/District Health Office for the sustainability of interventions and a clear understanding that health directorate will deploy its staff in the due course of the project implementation to take over the responsibilities of carrying forward the interventions and service delivery to the catchment population.

Needs

The Directorate of Health Tribal Districts in collaboration with Khyber Medical University Institute of Public Health & Social Sciences (KMU-IPH&SS), Peshawar, Pakistan and World Health Organization Pakistan has successfully completed health facilities assessment “Health Resource Availability and Mapping (HeRAMS)” in Tribal Distract. The assessment was commissioned in November 2017 to assess the health infrastructure and services in all government health facilities in all seven Tribal Distract and six Frontier Regions (FRs), and was completed in April 2018.The overall security situation in Tribal Districts and FRs has markedly improved. Over 1.6 million people who were initially displaced in nearby Districts and other provinces. About 98% TDPs have voluntarily returned to their homes, while only 2% remain in displacement. According to the assessment Out of the total 753 health facilities (providing services based on the availability of patient records at the time of assessment) in the Tribal Distract, 300 (39 %) health facilities have damage to the infrastructure. Among this 256 (85%) are partially damaged while 42 (15%) have extensive damage to the health facilities infrastructure. The most affected Tribal District terms of fully damaged Kurram and orakzai.According to HeRAM Access to health and other social services in the return areas are poor as a result of damaged Health Facilities, schools and water facilities. inadequate Human Resource lack of equipment’s, stock out of medicines, poor motivation of the staff, inadequate community awareness of services availability at the facility, poor accessibility of the health facility and poor attitude of the service providers was also a big challenge in many of the health facilities. For instance only 46.7% of the health facilities had refrigerator, 22% of health facilities had safe delivery kit, 19% of health facilities had sterilization equipment, 23.7% of the health facilities had delivery table. 47% of the health facilities in Tribal districts have the capacity for limited waste management like safe collection, segregation and disposal. 33% of the health facilities in Tribal Distract use an unsafe source of water. At health facility level it is worth mentioning here that some districts like Orakzai and Kurram were more affected as compared to other Tribal districts.Preliminary findings from Tribal Districts Vulnerability Assessment exercise revealed that 64% of the population in Tribal Distracts faced difficulties in accessing health care facilities. The mean distance travelled by the population to the nearest health facility in Tribal Districts is 7 km and in Frontier Region (FR) Areas is 19.5 km. Out of the women who had a child in the last three years not received any vaccination against infectious disease, 24% reported that they didn’t seek any medical help or services (ANC, PNC.TT) during their pregnancy. A significant proportion (40%) reported delivery at home, mostly with the assistance of traditional midwife (Dai). Availability of EPI services was poor in all regions around 20% of the households from Tribal Districts and 15% from FR areas reported that their eligible children were not vaccinated during routine EPI.Around half of the respondents 51% in Tribal Districts reported that during the last three months, they were unable to seek medical help even it was needed. Key reasons include the unavailability of relevant staff, service and financial constraint.Majority of health infrastructures in return areas are severely damaged by the crisis and remain non-functional. Many of the health staff who were displaced especially the female health workers have not resumed work. This has negatively influenced the provision of Reproductive Health, Mother & Child Health care services in Tribal Districts.

Activities

Provision of life saving medicines and supplies covering PHC and MNCH services in the revitalized 6 HF in districts Orakazai and Kurram .Implementation PlanKAMORE will directly implement the project as it has the capacity, previous experience, experts and health professionals, having medical professional in Board of Directors (BoD), Panel of Experts and the senior management team. Prior to implementation the on board field project staff will be oriented on the project objectives, targets process, target areas and beneficiaries. Before stepping in in to the field, coordination will be made with stake holders (UNOCHA , WHO, Agency government line departments, humanitarian actors and beneficiaries) of the project to ensure smooth implementation. Two teams will be made and deputed to the areas for services delivery. All necessary arrangements will be made in the facilities where from the technical experts will deliver services. The arrangements will include rehabilitation of the health facilities and separate waiting areas for female to honor cultural values and avoid conflict. The Project Review Committee will be established, which will review and assess the project achievements, make recommendations for future. This strong consultation mechanism will ensure complimentary, local ownership and coordination. No Objection Certificate (NoC) will be obtained from FDMA/PDMA. In case the NOC issuance is delayed, KAMORE will approach and apply to UNOCHA and will request for changing the project start/end date covering the delay period. KAMORE will establish a Project Management Unit (senior management) at its head Office to have close oversight on the project implementation and will make sure its timely completion with quality achievements.KAMORE will hire the project staff following the HR policy through a transparent manner and provide them training on the project objectives, targets and methodology, UNOCHA guidelines, code of conduct and reporting. The resources will be mobilized and field office will be established / equipped accordingly. The project team will devise Detail Implementation Plan based on the ground reality and contextual based. After conducting the Broad-Based Community Meetings, LQT and local health volunteers with adequate representation from all segment of the society (M&F) will be formed, signing ToRs and orientation on their roles and responsibilities in project implementation and monitoring. OPD will be operationalized, medicine will be given to patient and the provision of following PHC services as the minimum requirements:Out Patent Department (OPD) will be operationalized, Appropriate treatment of common diseases and injuries. Provision of essential drugs. Education concerning prevailing health problems and the methods of preventing and controlling them. Maternal and child health care, including Family Planning. Immunization against major infectious diseases. Prevention and control of locally endemic diseases.Management of Minor injuries, Management of Insect /Snake bite etc will be dealt in the health facilities. A strong referral mechanism will be developed with AHQ and THQ hospitals and referred cases will be given ambulance services. The Hygiene Promoters will provide health and best hygiene education to the patients and their accompanying and in the catchment area. Midwives will be identified and trained. TBA kits will be provided to them to ensure the quality neonatal services to the pregnant women and reduce mother/infant mortality.KAMORE has experience in managing projects in security threatened areas and will have fool-proof security arrangements for office and field staff. KAMORE will ensure the information sharing with all relevant stakeholders (agency administration, line departments, sister organizations, clusters, ERF /UNHCR and the beneficiaries to avoid any confusion, conflict and duplication/overlapping and ensure transparency through meetings, data, plan and reports sharing.
