Project Summary

In order to build capacity, increase ownership and clarify responsibility for the management and maintenance of WASH infrastructure, GOAL is in the process of implementing the UNICEF WASH FIT framework for adoption across the health and nutrition facilities. WASH FIT is a: “practical guide that provides a description of a risk-based, continuous improvement framework and associated tools for undertaking water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) improvements as part of wider quality improvements in health care facilities. WASH FIT extends beyond water quality to address sanitation, hygiene, health care waste and other aspects of environmental health and health care facility management and staff empowerment. The guide contains ready to use tools to help implement WASH FIT and step-by-step instructions for each stage.
GOAL Sudan will develop plans together with the SMoH to introduce the WASH FIT approach gradually or pilot it, in our supported health and nutrition facilities. If the WASH FIT framework is not deemed fully suitable, GOAL will adapt the approach to suit the needs of the facilities.
GOAL Sudan take a holistic approach to WASH in our areas of operation. Quality water supply, hygiene promotion, sanitation facilities, training and management, and environmental waste are all elements of our WASH program, which is implemented as part of a multi-sectoral response. Nutrition, WASH, and Health activities are all linked.In GOAL-supported health (including nutrition) facilities, clinic staff are responsible to for the management of waste including biological waste. GOAL has constructed incinerators in all GOAL supported health facilities in order to ensure proper disposal of medical waste; these are operated and managed by the clinic staff. In addition, during the project period GOAL will conduct universal precautions training for MAs, Pharmacists, MWs and vaccinators. GOAL also provides safety boxes in all health facilities to keep used hazardous materials including needles to be disposed in the incinerators.

1 - Needs

GOAL has worked to progressively expand access, particularly by improving water access in IDP camps and some health facilities, but needs remain especially in rural areas. Recent assessments by GOAL have identified over 16,000 people near supported clinics still relying on open or unprotected water sources, and in 2019-2020 GOAL aims to continue addressing these areas. An additional clinic, Damrat elSheikh, lies in an area with scarce groundwater, and thus all the water for this clinic is brought from another water point in a different location with a strong aquifer. Damrat elSheikh is not suitable for a borehole, but in the clinic the current water storage and distribution system needs rehabilitation, including a new tank, in order to improve cleanliness and reduce risk of contamination of the water once it arrives to the clinic. where groundwater is scarce and water needs to be brought from a different location, was identified as needing an upgraded tank and water distribution system to improve cleanliness and reduce risk of contamination. While water usage in the assessed villages is below SPHERE standards, GOAL worked with the water committees in Kassab and Fata Borno camps and WES and finalized the handover process of operation and daily management of water systems in the two camps. In 2019, GOAL will continue to provide technical advisory support and water quality testing to ensure that the water committee sustain the water supply schemes in the two camps benefiting a total of 30,226 IDPs. GOAL is also progressively working to improve sanitation in urban and crowded areas where sanitation, particularly solid waste management, is poor and a risk factor for diseases such as diarrhoea, infections, and malaria and other vector-borne diseases. Many public and IDP areas of Kutum are crowded and highly unsanitary, and GOAL started in 2018 to work with the locality authorities to put in place an improved solid waste management system. This work will continue in 2020 but still needs effective garbage collection points across the high-traffic areas of the town, especially around the market and areas vulnerable to flooding in the rainy season. In South Kordofan, GOAL’s assessment team found that despite several past humanitarian water supply projects in Talodi, currently NGOs have stopped support and communities have not fully taken ownership or responsibility for water points, which have fallen into permanent or irregular disrepair. In Rashad, the situation is similar, and at least 25 hand pumps remain non-operational, and 8 out of 18 water yards are non-operational due to lack of spare parts and funds. Rashad’s groundwater situation is difficult due to the geological nature of the area being without major aquifers and reaching water through drilling requires a high level of precision and can still yield low-productive boreholes. During the assessment, it was observed that many communities depend entirely on unsafe sources of water supply such as digging small pits in the dry beds of the seasonal streams to get their drinking water, even though livestock use the same sources. In both of these localities, the targeted health facilities are lacking direct access to clean water points, and are required to bring in water from long distances away; this includes Talodi Hospital which has a broken-down water system. Across the areas assessed by GOAL, the cost of a drum of water (220 litres) is SDG 20 in most towns and reached SDG 40 in the rural areas, comprising a significant barrier to regular clean water access not only for vulnerable households but also health facilities, who may lack the operational funds to regularly have clean water. Transporting water from far away in barrels also increases the risk of contamination during transit, and the inability to test the quality of water at health facilities means they may be using contaminated water without being aware, putting patients at further risk of infections.

2 - Activities / Outputs

In South Kordofan, the main activities will be• Train Garbage/EH committees on management and key hygiene messages • Carry out 2 Clean Up Campaigns in Talodi town in and around the hospital and market, including providing garbage collection tools (shovels and wheelbarrows) to the locality administration to facilitate more sustainable sanitation. • Provide 15 garbage disposal containers to Talodi town for market and other unsanitary public locations • Work with locality, MoH environmental health department, and WASH committees to develop Community action plans for solid waste management and disaster risk reduction• Vector control campaigns to include awareness raising in communities and support for prevention of outbreak strategies (Malaria, Dengue, AWD/Cholera) developed in coordination with WES/MOH/ WHO (Insecticides managed by WHO / WES) • Repair of 6 hand pumps in Rashad and Habila localities; many broken-down pumps were identified during the needs assessment, and GOAL will identify priority locations with assistance from the communities near supported health facilities, reaching a total of 3000 people with improved water access• Upgrade one mini water yard wtih submersible pump to a hybrid solar- diesel powered mini-water yard including a new elevated water tank in Atah Barah neighborhood, in Talodi town, improving access to clean water for 3,000 vulnerable people in this area• Rehabilitate and upgrade one existing MWY, including installation of a hybrid solar-diesel pumping system and connecting the Health facility, in Kou al Mango village in Rashad locality to improve clean water access for one rural health facility • Provide training and techncial support to water user committees for the MWYs in Rashad and Talodi town• Conduct regular water quality testing in the two MWYs and health facilities, including procurement of water quality testing kit and supplies;

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

GOAL Sudan incorporates protection principles in every aspect of programming and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian action while making sure that response is participatory and that it is accountable to the people GOAL seeks to assist. For GOAL Sudan, protection mainstreaming is crucial both internally and externally to ensure that extremely vulnerable groups including children, women, people with disabilities and the elderly can access services and are protected from both intentional and unintentional harm. GOAL has ensured that all staff and partners are aware of protection issues through training, and protection mainstreaming tools have been used in programming and internally to ensure the safety of the extremely vulnerable groups. Throughout its intervention GOAL is promoting gender equality and promoting beliefs and norms that foster respectful, non-violent gender norms to help prevent and mitigate GBV. Safety, respect, confidentiality and non-discrimination in relation to survivors and those at risk are vital considerations for GOAL.
