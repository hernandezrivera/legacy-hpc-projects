Project Summary

The project is based on cooperation with DOH in Hama to rehabilitate and activate the function of two health governmental centers in southern rural of hama to provide primary health care in these area, including these villages (Hurbenafseh, Deir-Alfardees, Taqsees, Jerneyah) which has been affected badly by the crisis, have a large number of IDPs and returnees to this area.
So our the purpose of the project is to support DoH in Hama and provide the suitable health care.
the targeted number of the project according to SCS-Hama & DOH-Hama is about 14000 patients.

4 - Needs

All population groups are affected by the crisis in Syria and have a right to receive health care.Although all people have been in some way affected by the crisis in Syria, certain groups are exposed to particular risks which render them especially vulnerable. As such, the Health sector will prioritize the provision of assistance to people living in hard to reach areas; people exposed to high intensity of hostilities and areas contaminated with explosive hazards;IDPs and spontaneous returnees living in last resort where freedom of movement and/or access to services remain limited and living conditions below standard; newly-displaced populations,and over-burdened communities hosting a large concentration of IDPs and/or spontaneous returnees.Beside the affect of COVID-19 outbreak and the lack of awareness about this pandemic.Access to health care remains poor, especially in hard to reach areas due to a lack of functioning facilities; shortages of health staff, drugs and medical supplies in higher prices.Referral systems for trauma, emergency obstetric care, and surgical services are insufficient, while there remains limited capacity for mobile clinics to reach out to fluid temporary settlements and host communities.The maintenance of medical equipment is also a core challenge, compounded by restrictions related to international sanctions on Syria, which prevent partners from importing critical spare parts. According to the field survey carried out by SCS-Hama in cooperation with the Directorate of Health in Hama DOH during the current period, most residents in current period, all residents in eastern rural of Hama are suffering from the shortage of food, medicine, health services, water, sanitation..

5 - Implementation of activities

Improving access to primary health care facilities by providing integrated health services through 2 PHCs with an emphasis on internal medicine, pediatric, obstetric, gynecological and health education services, by the following activities:-Provide primary health care services, including reproductive health care and general health care and pediatric health care-Refer patients with serious illnesses to secondary level health care facilities for further treatment-Community outreach by CHW for health education, health screenings and referrals-Raising awareness about COVID-19 pandemic and personal protective Procedures-Participation in vaccination campaign with DoH-Strengthen capacity to investigate and detect disease outbreaks-Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community HCWs to provide essential health services though: health staff trained/re-trained on mental health topics including mhGAP, PFA, GBV first-line response including Clinical Management of Rape, infection prevention and control.Monitoring and evaluation procedures: SCS will ensure project is being correctly implemented, bottlenecks are relieved and changes are addressed in a timely manner. SCS’ responsibilities are: monitoring the quality of care being provided as well as the administrative work being conducted, verifying that all documentations and reports are being conducted according to required standards. SCS will use MEAL toolkits to monitor the activities like (Filed visit and User satisfaction surveys). SCS will provide 4Ws and monthly information on the progress of the project.SCS will evaluate the project twice during the implementation to see if it meets the expectation by using evaluation checklist and indicators tracking.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Conflict settings place women and girls at acute risk of poor health outcomes and sexual and gender-based violence. Health services are often the first, and, on many occasions, the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance. Health facilities and providers are therefore an entry point for survivors to receive life-saving care. Mitigating measures (MM): Health staff should be trained on providing trauma-sensitive, survivor-centered first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals as applicable to their respective role. 2. Inequitable access to health care which could increase tensions within communities; forced into challenging ethical dilemmas and triage scenarios that healthcare providers are confronted with due to limited resources. MM: health actors are encouraged to avail themselves of mechanisms for visibility, advocacy and accountability.3. The outbreak of Covid-19 pandemic disease makes a huge risk in similar communities, that has lack of awareness or access to primary health care. MM: Health actors should expand duty of care efforts for their frontline staff. This includes sufficient supply of personal protective equipment and ensuring access to care should health workers become ill. 4. Poverty and lack of sources or livelihoods, high prices of drugs or health services could lead to any outbreaks of diseases or conflicts among people in this area. MM: Provide free essential primary health care, consultations drugs and awareness in the targeted areas.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risks:1. Exploitation of patients by healthcare workers and other staff2. Exploitation between patients Risk mitigation and prevention measures:⁃ Train staff on PSEA- Require that all healthcare workers and other staff in clinics sign a Code of Conduct establishing minimum standards for acceptable behaviour⁃ Promote a zero tolerance policy to violation of the Code of Conduct and encourage disciplinary action⁃ Clinics should have separate latrines for males and females and for patients and staff.⁃ Sensitize patients and visitors that all services are free⁃ Establish complaint and feedback mechanisms in clinics for affected communities capable of handling complaints of SEA⁃ Make affected communities aware of their rights and available complaints mechanisms⁃ Encourage reporting of suspected cases of SEA through established channels.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Delays in implementation of activities due to delays in obtaining necessary approvals. Likelihood: High. Mitigation measures:-Liaise and consult with local governmental authorities to highlight the needs of the population-Follow up when undue delays are experienced2.Aid diversion: If the resources at the disposal of SCS are perceived as being diverted to unauthorized persons, then Integrity of project partnership agreement is compromised, resulting to reduction in donors' confidence; thereby limiting SCS operational capacities. Likelihood: High. Mitigation measures: -Establish harmonized monitoring systems-Establish mechanisms for communicating with communities-Raise awareness among staff on fraud prevention3.Outbreak of COVID-19: If precautionary measures are not observed by staff and beneficiaries, then COVID-19 Pandemic would spread among SCS staff and among beneficiaries' communities. Likelihood: High. Mitigation measures: -Provide personal protective equipment for staff who are in direct contact with clients.-Ensure being up-to-date COVID-19 outbreak guidance’s.4.Financial Risk stemming from currency fluctuation affecting ability to deliver on planned targets, Likelihood: High. Mitigation measures:Prices need to be monitored over the course of the project.Due to the sensitive nature of GBV service provision there is a risk that inexperienced staff will cause further harm to GBV survivors. To mitigate this risk, partner technical capacity will be strengthened and appropriate systems put into place in order to ensure safe access and do no harm principles are respected.Ensure that confidentiality and privacy are respected in all forms of consultation, counseling and personal information sharing.
