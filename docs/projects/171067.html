Project Summary

This project is designed to ensure that most vulnerable crisis-affected children and youth - including children living with disabilities - have access to safe, protective, inclusive and continuous learning opportunities in Gedo and Lower Juba Regions of Somalia. In achieving this, the intervention will implement a set of integrated actions aimed at increasing access and retaining these vulnerable learners in school whilst ensuring they are able to access lifesaving learning within a protective environment. The 10,000 learners (5,000 girls, 5,000 boys) will primarily be targeted from IDPs and vulnerable host communities. Of these targeted beneficiaries, the project will deliberately target 1,000 children living with disabilities, 50% of whom will be girls. The project proposes a set of integrated activities consisting of: construction/rehabilitation of learning spaces with child friendly and inclusive WASH facilities, provision of 5,000 litre water tank to schools, Enrollment awareness campaign, cash voucher assistance for vulnerable learners, provision of teaching and learning materials, incentive payment for teachers, emergency school feeding and provision of sanitary kits for female learners. Disability mainstreaming will also be targeted, to ensure CwDs are able to access inclusive education. In order to strengthen the capacity of education actors to deliver effective and coordinated EiE preparedness and response in Lower Juba and Gedo regions, the action will train teachers on inclusive, protective and socio-emotional teaching and learning and psychosocial support; CECs on school management, child protection and school safety. MoE staff at State and District level will be trained on emergency preparedness and response planning and coordination. In Gedo and Lower Juba, protection related issues have been identified as one of the hindrances to safe access to protective learning environments. These include the presence of armed personnel at checkpoints on the road to school, as well as at the school premises. GBV on the way to school and back is also another concern, in addition to lack of fences and gender sensitive WASH facilities, which particularly impacts girls’ access to education. Cultural practices e.g. FGM, early marriage and child labor also causes children to not enroll in schools or drop out prematurely. Marginalization and discrimination of children is prevalent and access to education for children from marginalized groups, CWDs and girls is being hampered. To respond to these challenges, the project will establish referral system in schools and community-based Child Protection Mechanisms for quick and efficient referrals of children with protection needs. This will include training, mapping of services and focal points within community and school (community child protection committees, School Management Committees, school Child Protection focal points); Provision of community and school based PSS to the affected children and their families through CFS, school clubs, community groups activities, procurement of recreational and dignity kits.; Case management – for Unaccompanied and Separated Children through documentation, immediate care, referrals and family tracing and response to GBV survivors & capacity building and life-saving messaging on prevention of family separation, child recruitment Risk Education, and other protection concerns- for teachers, community members, CP committees. The action will provide and support safe and free education that will help mitigate the impact of conflict and disasters by giving children and families a sense of normalcy, stability, structure and hope for the future, especially post-COVID. Disability mainstreaming will also be ensured in the action, by deliberately increasing efforts to identify barriers, risks and enablers for CwDs, and will disaggregate data by disability; consult closely with CWDs and address the additional risk they face in accessing education.

Mitigation Measures

The project targets the poor host communities, returnees with specific focus on the IDPs. The IDPs are more vulnerable to protection and education inclusion since they are mostly affected by the environment they live in. Women, girls and boys from the internally displaced communities are exposed to sexual violation and abuse with no/minimal referral system in and out of schools. Insecurity and clan conflict is now one of the main causes for displacement. Drought, floods and cyclone are also the root cause of the displacement in Somalia. Somalia faces a dire humanitarian situation, having endured drought and famine as well as continued fighting and unpredictable heavy rains and floods; this is now compounded with recent COVID 19 outbreak, all of which have aggravated the conditions of IDPs and poor host Communities. People denied the rights of the IDPs as human being and they are facing evictions, rape and looting due to the fragility of the jubaland state government who was under their responsibility to protect them. Most of the IDPs left behind their livelihood assets and displaced to the big towns with empty hands. Thus, the IDPs have the right to receive necessary service, such as protection, education and other services in order to save their lives. Unfortunately, conflict sometimes is preventing humanitarian agencies from delivering assistance to the IDPs. In this project, ADRA/WI will address protection concerns and supporting education retention and access activities for children already in schools e.g. through provision of safe drinking water in schools, provision of emergency school feeding, hygiene promotion in schools, school supplies and construction of temporary learning spaces among other related interventions is thus vital in ensuring retention of learners. The project will also establish referral system in schools and community based child protection mechanisms for efficient protection needs.

Please provide further explanation

The project proposes to work with the community in identifying lasting solution and management of the schools. Training of Community Education Committees will enable the communities manage the schools. Temporary learning facilities will be handed over to the community at the end of the project with the entire infrastructure used during the project. The community will be mobilized before the project and all the beneficiaries will be informed of their entitlement and services they will receive. Contract agreement with the donor as well as local administration engagement will outline all the services and entitlement the target beneficiaries will receive. ADRA/WI will hold at the inception of the project, community mobilization, and sensitization meetings with all stakeholders including the local authorities to officially launch the project. During the sensitization meetings, the project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement and roles and duties of each stakeholder including M&E roles will be discussed and agreed. The project will develop elaborate beneficiary complain and feedback tool, project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be given 1 day workshop on how to use the complaint /feedback system to enhance transparency and accountability. Community ownership of the project and keeping the sustainability of the Education infrastructure after the face out of the project will be done through formation of structures such as Beneficiary Representatives, CEC Committees, and Community Hygiene Promoters with equal share of men and women. In order to meet the Education needs of the affected community, the project will involve the target community through its leaders and engendered committees in the first stage of planning of the project to ensure community participation. The organization will establish engendered committees (women participation will be strictly adhered) that will have hand-in-hand working relations with the project team and beneficiary communities; both sides will exchange any information related to the management and implementation of the project activities. Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will also be used to make sure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with services provided and complaints can be channeled to the right direction. ADRA/WI will adhere the principles of "do no Harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project beneficiaries.
