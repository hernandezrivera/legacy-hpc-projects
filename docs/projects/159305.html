Project Summary

The main problem that this project seeks to address is “Limited access of breast cancer patients to timely, well-designed and high-quality breast cancer care”.
The project aims at improving the accessibility of women at risk of having or developing breast cancer to vital preventive, diagnostic and treatment services, leading to an improved 5-year survival rate. This is to be achieved at all levels of service: primary, secondary and tertiary, as follows:
Primary care: this begins at the community level and covers awareness of early signs and symptoms, through conducting organized health educational workshops at the community level and also other awareness activities that include disseminating brochures and releasing media materials.
Secondary care: this involves advanced diagnosis and staging for women referred from the communities and women visiting health centers (mainly the biopsy, and histopathology) in coordination with the partner PRCS through its hospitals.
Tertiary care: treatment, follow-up, and rehabilitation. Treatment with medications will be provided in coordination with the Ministry of Health. Rehabilitation and Post-op care services will be provided by PRCS including Physiotherapy and PSS activities. Distribution of services will take place in a way that geographic coverage will be maintained.

1 - Needs

UNFPA report (2018) “Pathway to Survival: The Story of Breast Cancer in Palestine. Analytical Mapping of Breast Cancer Care in Palestine” indicated the following:₋ Breast cancer is the most common cancer occurring in Palestinian women and the third most common cause of cancer death in 2016 and leading to the death of 1072 Palestinian women and representing 10% of the total number of deaths in Palestine.₋ The five-year survival rates are estimated as low as 40%.₋ Out of the 1072 breast cancer deaths in 2016, 643 (60%) have actually died prematurely and could have been saved if effective screening, early detection, and referral pathway was put in place. ₋ Limited number of oncologists is available at the governmental sector with their high overload of cases (70-80 cases per day). According to Gaza’s Central Drug Store by Sep. 2019, 646 items ( %44) out of the essential medications were reported at zero stock levels in the Central Drug Store (CDS), 635 items (28%) out of the medical disposables in the essential list, were reported at zero stock levels in the Central Medical Disposable Store (CMS). For Cancer’s drugs and chemotherapy, 36 items out of 65 items were reported at zero stock levels. Cancer patients were referred to receive treatment abroad (8500 referred cases annually) due to the serious gaps in service provision in Gaza including shortage of drugs, lack of radiotherapy services, lack of palliative care, and lack of specialized services. 56% of cancer drugs were at zero stock - less than one month's supply on shelves. The Gaza Strategic Health Plan indicates there are serious problems with the cancer services available in the GS with shortages in human resources, inappropriate infrastructure, and equipment for oncology services, deficiencies in necessary drugs and supplies, and an absence of clear guidelines and policies for referrals. The problem analysis is based on the triangulation of findings from literature review, 8 focus groups with women including WWBC and 8 key informant interviews with representatives of breast cancer service providers (governmental and NGOs) in the five governorates of Gaza Strip. Consequences this problem is causing in the context include but not limited to the followings: ₋ Low Five-year survival rate (40%) among women diagnosed with breast cancer in GS.₋ Depression and severe mental and emotional effects.₋ Isolation in family and community. Intimate partner violence and abuse.

2 - Activities

Conducting awareness activities: about 70 sessions will be conducted by a specialized team with the partner PRCS. Brochures and other materials are to be produced and distributed at the community level. Media materials (e.g. Radio spots, videos … etc.) are to be released and published Advanced diagnosis procedures including Biopsy and histopathology tests are to be executed targeting 250 women. Medications are to be supplied for the Ministry of health, covering the needs of about 1400 WWBC for a period of about 12 months. Post-op care, rehabilitation, and physiotherapy sessions are to be conducted targeting at least 150 WWBC received Onco-surgeries. This activity will be executed by the partner PRCS at their centers. Psychosocial support activities (PSS) are to be executed targeting 700 men, women and family members of WWBC. PSS activities include distributing artificial silicon breasts, bras, and artificial hair … etc. for 100 WWBC by the partner PRCS at their centers. Note: QRCS contribution for the project will be 500,000 USD in the form of procurement of chemotherapeutic drugs and medications
