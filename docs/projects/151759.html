Project Summary

Through this project proposal IOM will provide humanitarian Shelter/NFI Assistance to 372,000 newly displaced and protracted IDPs and host community. The geographical areas of intervention will be Lower Jubba, Gedo, Bay and Benadir regions for static interventions. The pipeline prepositioned emergency light kits will be distributed countrywide wherever the needs arise and are endorsed by the cluster. The target is to have 70% of the beneficiaries stated in the proposal reached out through cash-based interventions, whenever this is feasible and appropriate. IOM will contribute to the delivery and piloting of cash-for-rent scheme in the Municipality of Mogadishu for 2,000 households with a clear exit strategy that relies on the financial stabilization of IDPs through the access to livelihood opportunities. The quality programming of this response will revolve around a solid approach to accountability to affected population by promoting community participation in programme implementation and accountability to inform analysis and future response.

Needs

Somalia currently has an estimated caseload of 2.6 million of internally-displaced population (IDP) resulting from conflict and climatic shocks. The 2017 drought and conflict added 1.5 million to the already existing protracted caseload. Despite humanitarian assistance having slightly addressed some of the conditions suffered by IDPs, mainly around food security, the majority continues to lack access to life-saving and life-sustaining basic services. A large population of IDPs residing in urban and peri-urban area suffer secondary or multiple-displacement as a result of evictions, exacerbating their vulnerability. Lack of unclear plans for durable solutions and self-reliant approaches undermines the capacity of those displaced to improve their living conditions. The Joint Multi-Cluster Needs Assessments (JMCNA) 2018 showed that 48% of the households interviewed highlighted shelter as a priority; 80% of the IDPs live in makeshift shelters, temporary emergency shelters, tents or in the open; and 80% of IDPs reported lack of core Non-Food Items (NFIs). Evictions remain at alarming levels in the country. More than 80% of the IDPs are reported to be tenure insecure with Benadir region as most affected with 75% of evictions in 2018 taking place in Mogadishu City. Baidoa is the second most affected area. Factors underpinning evictions are multiple with general tenure insecurity as the prime issue. This project will contribute to addressing the following needs: i. Provision of emergency Shelter/NFI assistance to newly displaced IDPs through prepositioned supplies; ii. Provision of Shelter/NFI assistance to newly displaced IDPs through cash-based interventions whenever feasible and appropriate; iii. Provision of Shelter/NFI assistance to protracted IDPs through Cash-Based Interventions whenever whenever feasible and appropriate and; iv. Provision of short-term and medium-term shelter solutions for IDPs at risk of evictions in urban and peri-urban areas.

Activities

Under the cluster Objective 1, IOM will provide Shelter/NFI assistance to newly displaced populations, with specific focus on IDPs, host community and those affected by natural hazards. The response under this objectives will have two components. Firstly, 12,500 households will receive life-sustaining NFI and 10,000 households life-saving Shelter assistance through static interventions in Bay, Gedo, Kismayo and Benadir Regions. These activities will be implemented through cash-based interventions whenever it is feasible and appropriate. Secondly, IOM will keep building up the preposition pipeline of supplies in Mogadishu and, potentially, in secondary hubs with a total of 15,000 emergency light kits to provide support to IDPs affected by climate and conflict shocks anywhere in the country. The distribution of these emergency kits will be purely in-kind, used as a last resort when other modalities are not feasible, and accessible to all cluster partners. Under the cluster Objective 2, IOM will provide Shelter/NFI in order to facilitate the improvement of living conditions and to contribute to local reintegration to the protracted IDPs caseload. On one hand, IOM will provide life-sustaining NFI assistance through cash-based interventions whenever it is feasible and appropriate to 12,500 households in Bay, Gedo, Kismayo and Benedir Regions. Likewise, 10,000 will receive life-saving shelter assistance linked to land tenure in Baidoa city. On the other hand, IOM will collectively contribute to the efforts of providing cash-for-rent solutions in Benadir Region to 2,000 households in coordination with the Shelter/NFI and the Protection Clusters as well as municipality and regional administration stakeholders. Overall, IOM will ensure the mainstreaming of Objective 3 in all the activities by ensuring quality programming by promoting community participations in program implementation and accountability to inform analysis and future response.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

All the different activities included in these proposal have a solid contribution to durable solutions and resilience. Cash-Based Interventions by nature enhance the resilience of affected populations. Through active engagement with traditional formal and informal leadership structures, traders, and women and youth groups, effective partnerships will transfer expertise and knowledge to improve their living conditions and equip the community with skills that they can use in the current location or wherever they move, including if they return to their areas of origin. IOM will maintain a commitment to accountability to affected populations, including through transparent identification and notification of the project, inclusive and active engagement with the community during project design and implementation through focus group discussions and key informant interviews. Through multiple and continuous discussions, IOM will engage directly with the community to better understand their intentions in recognition of the converging crises. The community will play a central role in almost all aspects of programme implementation, proving a crucial partner in the process of selecting traders, sensitizing beneficiaries and the wider community, responding to feedback/complaints and organizing the skills trainings. Allowing the community to assume increased responsibility for the improvement of their living conditions will restore their dignity and strengthen the self-sufficiency of the affected population. This applies to distributions for Shelter and NFI assistance for both newly and protracted populations but also for those benefiting for cash-for-rent initiatives. In order to ensure cash-for-rent activities are sustainable and do not create dependency, IOM will liaise with durable solutions stakeholders, including IOM’s Recovery and Durable Solutions unit, to guarantee all the beneficiaries of rental subsidies are subject to stabilization of the financial and living conditions by supporting household coping mechanisms and self-sufficiency. In this scenario, advocacy to refer the families to livelihood, skill training or microcredit is recommended in order to facilitate beneficiaries’ resumption of a normal life. The medium and long-term solution to the evictions problem has its foundations in strengthening the capacity of local authorities. The current institutional mechanisms are weak and are not empowered or rely on a non-existent legal backup. This intervention will has a strong emphasis in investing and reinforcing in the current mechanisms and creating new ones if it is necessary. The committee dealing with evictions put in place by the Benadir Regional Administration has proofed to be successful, nonetheless cannot cope with the magnitude of the problem. IOM will support in providing a scale up of the infrastructure and human resources and also legal advice, which all together will strengthen the institutional and community long-term resilience.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk 1: Marginalized or underrepresented groups (MUG) of IDPs risk exclusion in community consultations due to prohibition of local leaders, inability to access location for consultations, lack of structure or proper representatives within these groups, and weak information sharing mechanisms of organization.Likelihood & Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1.) S/NFI staff should be representative of all groups within the community respecting age, gender and diversity (AGD) and should be given protection and AAP mainstreaming training.2.) Have prior understanding of target group including power dynamics and basic conflict analysis of area and community by liaising with protection partners and desk review of most recent reports.3.) Raise awareness of local leaders and communities on mandate of IOM on mainstreaming protection and AAP and importance of humanitarian assistance delivered inclusive of AGD.4.) Identify MUG within IDP community and request for a representative based on an understanding of power dynamics within the community and do no harm principle.5.) Understand best ways to communicate with these communities and operationalize approach.6.) Regularly monitor, prior, during and after project implementation that consulted MUGs have not been subjected to threats, retribution, or further marginalization as a result of their inclusion. Risk 2: MUG of IDPs risk exclusion on active participation during project implementation such as during cash-for-work activities, or when hiring casual laborers during in-kind distribution.Likelihood & Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1.) Mitigation measures 1-5 & 6 from above is applicable.2.) In consultation with MUG, identify their strengths and weaknesses, and how special measures can be adopted by IOM to ensure their participation respecting equal representation and do no harm principle e.g. Person with no community link can be considered to work as a casual laborer with endorsement of the rest of the community.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk 1: Age and gender needs are at risk of being excluded when designing S/NFI interventionLikelihood & Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1.) S/NFI staff should be representative of all groups within the community respecting age, gender and diversity (AGD) and should be given protection, gender and AAP mainstreaming training.2.) Raise awareness of local leaders and communities on mandate of IOM on mainstreaming protection, gender and AAP and importance of humanitarian assistance delivered inclusive of AGD. 3.) Mainstream tools and approach with protection, gender and AAP e.g. needs assessment should have a specific focus on identifying shelter needs based on gender and age (e.g. households with adolescent girls may need separation within shelters)4.) During consultations, female and male staff should respectively be in-charge of leading FGD and KII for females and males, especially for adolescent girls and adolescent boys.5.) Have prior understanding of household power dynamics and who has decision making power on specific SNFIs to inform intervention on how optimize response and best cater to AGD needs of beneficiaries e.g. The male adult may have the decision making power of mosquito nets and should be primarily considered during awareness raising initiatives on mosquito net use, care and maintenance.6.) Understand AGD roles within community especially during participatory activities such as cash-for-work activities and in-kind operations, and provide equal opportunities for women, elderly and PSNs respecting do no harm principle e.g. PSNs with physical impairment can work as enumerators.7.) Understand best ways to communicate with these communities respecting AGD, and operationalize approach.8.) Regularly monitor, prior, during and after project implementation ensuring consulted and involved community members have not been subjected to threats, retribution, or further marginalization as a result of their inclusion.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Risk 1: Beneficiaries are at risk of being exposed or targeted to armed conflict and violenceLikelihood & Impact: Mitigation measures: 1.) Mainstream protection on tools and approaches to have a protection-informed interventions e.g. safety audit tools, conflict analysis assessment tools, etc.3.) Regularly monitor, prior, during and after project implementation ensuring consulted and involved community members have not been subjected to threats, retribution, or further marginalization as a result of their inclusion.
