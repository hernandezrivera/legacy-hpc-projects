Project Summary

Africa Development Aid intends to provide quality protection services to crisis-affected children and their caregivers in Pigi/Canal and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State, Malakal, Renk, Nasir and Panyikang Counties of Upper Nile State. Emergency Response to 180 (90 boys, 90 girls) Unaccompanied and Separated children through family tracing and reunification services. Emergency response to 7,000 (2500 boys, 2500 girls, 1000 men and 1000 women) adolescent children and caregivers through structured and non-structured community-based mental health and psycho-social support interventions, conduct parental sessions on child protection issues affecting children in the community, this will enhance the psychological well-being of children and their caregivers facing trauma as a result of conflict and flooding. 1000 (500 boys, 500 girls) children with different protection concerns will receive case management services through material support and 400 (200 boys, 200 girls) most vulnerable children through cash based initiative. This will support them in accessing their basic immediate needs. Conduct 150 awareness raising sessions on child protection issues, Protection risks, counselling and positive parenting. Formation of community based child protection committees to identify key protection concerns and help in advocacy on how to solve issues affecting children in the communities, this is to help the communities understand changes required on harmful practices against children. Establishment and update of Child Protection service mapping and referral pathways to enable smooth implementation and referral of cases during and after project implementation periods. ADA will also identify and train a total of 100 people from different groups of women and men e.g (community leaders, humanitarian and government social welfare staff) on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) and these are in-line with Protection cluster 2025 objects on response plan to child protection issues for both returnees and host communities in South Sudan. ADA will engage the local communities of all different project target locations by involving them in project design, understand their immediate needs, install suggestion boxes and have group sessions with them to ensure that their suggestions are incorporated into project implementation, different community committees will be formed and trained on key child protection issues to help them sustain and address the key protection concerns during and after the facing out of the project. A total of 8,500 people (4500 Males and 4500 females) will benefit from this project. Out of this, 5,000 will be children (2,500 girls; 2,500 boys) and 3,500 will be adults – 1,500 males and 2,000 females of which 15% will be persons with disabilities. However, out of the total beneficiaries 30% will be from the host community while 40% will be from Returnees and 30% from IDPs.

1. Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP) 2024, South Sudan is currently experiencing one of the world’s worst humanitarian crises, with 9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in 2024, and the people of South Sudan will continue to require significant support from the humanitarian response. The deteriorating humanitarian conditions in South Sudan could result into elevated, extreme deprivations of basic services such as food, health, protection, water, sanitation, and hygiene. Following the onset of armed conflict in Sudan between RSF and SAF, in April 2023 to November 2024, UNHCR reported over 75,000 individuals have crossed into South Sudan via Renk County adding extra burden on the already fragile situation especially in areas with high number of returnees and refugees. Moreover, according to the IPC rating, (October 2024 to March 2025), a total of 38 counties are classified in IPC Phase 4, 15 counties are in Phase 3, 19 counties are in Phase 2, and 8 counties are in Phase 1. 1 - Acceptable 2 - Alert 3 - Serious 4 - Critical 5 level. The IPC, AMN Classification Phase remains similar in 76 out of the 80 counties, among the counties classified in Phase 4, 24 of the 38 counties are located in Greater Upper Nile which include Jonglei state, Upper Nile and Unity State where ADA is intends to respond in nine Counties namely;(Fangak, and Pigi/canal), in Jonglei State and (Nasir, Renk, Malakal and Panyikang Counties) Upper Nile State. Several IRNAs conducted FGDs and KII visits in the assessed and confirmed the presence of IDPs and returnees from different counties targeted above, assessment teams’ observed the numbers of affected populations from incident and flood situation including returnees from different countiries e.g Sudan and and Ethiopia were facing different protection needs, during this assessments, concerns of early/child marriages, child labor, child abuse, violence and cases of exploitation were recorded among children. In Fangak about 2,377 HHs approximately 14,262 individuals were displaced into and within the Old Fangak Payam centre, additionally, there are 352 returnees’ households in Old Fangak while new Fangak registered more than 20,000 IDPs from submerged Payams and Returnees from Sudan. From the all different assessments conducted in different counties eg. Nasir, Panyikang, Malakal, Fangak, Pigi/Canal and Renk, Child Protection concerns including families separation, child abduction, gender based violence, cases of early/child marriages, child abuses, psychological distress among others were noted. According to Protection cluster analysis update published in june 2024, case of forced families separation, GBV eg. Early/forced marraiges, exploitation, trauma, violence among other protection concerns with Panyikang County falling under catastropic while other targeted counties e.g Fangak, Nasir, Renk among others falls under extreme and severe needs.

2. Project activities

ADA will response to children’s emergency needs through case management services which includes both cash based initiatives and provision of in-kind assistance, These includes children with different protection concerns such as Child with disabilities, Orphan children among other concerns. Unaccompanied and Separated children will be supported via family tracing and reunification services. Emergency response to children, adolescents and caregivers through structured and non-structured community-based mental health and psychosocial support interventions, conduct parental sessions on child protection issues affecting children in the community. Awareness raising on child protection issues, formation of community based child protection committees to identify key protection concerns and help in advocacy on how to solve issues affecting children in the communities. Establishment and update of service mapping and Child Protection referral pathways and training of women and men (community leaders or humanitarian and government social welfare staff including ADA staff) trained on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE). ADA will Recruit and capacity build 54 CP staff (27M, 27F) including existing staff from Renk, Fangak, Pigi, Panyikang, Nasir, Malakal Counties and train them on revised CPMS, CPiE, CPIMS+, PSS and Case Management SOPs. Case workers will identify; document; assess the protection needs of children; plan and implement interventions on the identified needs including referral to other actors for services, and follow up of cases using global child protection case management minimum standards. PSS facilitators will be trained on basic skills like how to communicate with children, signs of children facing distress or other forms of abuse, psychological first aid, planning and management of mobile & static Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) activities that are age, gender and culturally appropriate. Provide psycho social support activities including parental sessions and also select and train community-based child protection networks and foster parents on CPiE for all the targeted locations. Strengthen case, management approach including provision of cash-based assistance. 1000 most vulnerable children (500 boys & 500 girls) will be supported with cash-based Assistance (CBA) as part of case management support. Identification will be done using priority list from Standard Operating Procedures for case management. The well-being of all children with critical protection concerns will be monitored through follow up visits to ensure new protection needs and risks are prevented or responded to in good time. 180 UASC/missing children will be identified/registered, documented and referred for family tracing. Provide psycho social support (PSS) services to 5,000 children and some of their 2000 traumatized caregivers through provision of mental Health and Psycho Social Support Services e.g., recreational activities, livelihood services and parenting skills sessions via mobile & static response. Conduct 150 awareness sessions on prevention of family’s separation, and harmful traditional practices prevention to 8,500 adults and children, these will be the same targeted parents and children for PSS. Identify/train 100 community volunteers (CBCPN & Foster Parents) and other actors on CPiE in Pigi, Panyikang, Malakal, Renk, Nasir, and Fangak Counties. Establish strong referral pathway in nine targeted counties, this will include service mapping to know which partners is doing what to ensure children access other services that the project will not provide, eg. child survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) will be referred to GBV actors etc. ADA intends to target 8,500 people (4,250 Males and 4,250 females) with this project. Out of these, 5,000 will be children (2,500 girls; 2,500 boys) and 3,500 will be adults.
