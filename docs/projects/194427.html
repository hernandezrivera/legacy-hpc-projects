Project Summary

This project aims to enhance integrated responses targeting Kapoeta South, Akobo, Ayod Mayendit, Tonj North, Canal/Pigi counties to provide both static, mobile and outreach durable solutions to protection gaps, scale up protection activities and strengthen resilience.
The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO_Feb_2022) OCHA reaffirmed the deteriorating humanitarian conditions in the Counties; Kapoeta South, Akobo, Ayod, Mayendit, Tonj North & Canal Pigi. Severe flooding entered the third consecutive year and in May through December 2021, the floods caused dire humanitarian situation including General Protection needs. Most of the affected social groups are; women, children, older persons and other persons with disabilities /physically challenged persons1
Conflict and Insecurity equally continued to disrupt livelihoods and caused mass displacements. This is a major trigger of protection concerns especially among women and children. There is an increasing vulnerability caused by the prolonged crises the Counties of; Canal Pigi, Ayod, Tonj North and Mayendit. The South Sudan Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Dec 2022-March 20203 projection indicated acute food insecurity, high levels of malnutrition and mass loss of social capital. This led to increase in protection concerns among the vulnerable Populations.
Literature review of assessment reports indicate, due to compounding shocks; floods, sub-national violence, armed conflict, revenge killings, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. This has exposed the community to different protection-related risk and suffering2
HAA plans to scale up her mobile and static responses in the above Counties to provide critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas
The project aims to reach a total of 34,653 crisis affected individuals’ with the general protection services. The following activities will be implemented
Below are the planned project activities
• Provide Psychosocial support (PSS) to women, men, girls and boys to ensure individual, group, specialized PSS and other related general protection services (Psychosocial support and access to documentation)
• Conduct awareness on peace building, and awareness so that people of concern are reached through peace building awareness raising activities
• Conduct trainings to people of concern on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building
• Conduct General Protection awareness to women, men, girls and boys reached through general protection awareness raising activities

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO_Feb_2022) OCHA flagged out the deteriorating humanitarian conditions in the Counties; Canal Pigi, Ayod, Tonj North and Mayendit Counties. And the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) Oct 2022 indicated that, the main drivers of these phenomena are climatic shocks (Floods and dry spells) that have submerged households and depleted assets. Conflict and Insecurity equally continued to disrupt livelihoods and caused mass displacements and affected functionality of social services. This has grossly eroded social stability. There is limited peace across these Counties.There is an increasing vulnerability caused by the prolonged crises the in the Counties of; Canal Pigi, Ayod, Tonj North and Mayendit. The South Sudan Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Dec 2022-March 20203 projection indicated acute food insecurity, high levels of malnutrition. Half of South Sudanese girls are married or promised in marriage before the age of 18. Women tend to experience greater food insecurity given their cultural and social roles as caregivers of children and older persons, which leads to others being prioritized and served food first. Female-headed households are more commonly found in rural areas. In armed conflict, both women and men are subjected to sexual violence, rape, murder and torture. Men and boys are likely to face the risk of recruitment by armed forces. Men and boys tend not to engage in activities such as collecting firewood, water and food, often fearing they will be targeted by armed actors to join conflicts. As such, women and girls tend to take responsibilities for such activities which places them at the risk of sexual and gender-based violence, while they carry out the tasks. Women and girls have a higher risk of domestic violence, which in some parts of South Sudanese society is viewed as acceptable.People with disabilities face greater challenges to access services, with greater challenges experienced among women and especially children with disabilities (75 per cent perceive having less access to services, food, and education) and among the older persons. This is also triangulated by the 2021+FSNMS+ qualitative findings that indicate that people with disabilities were considered to face some of the highest protection risks, according to interviews held with host communities, IDPs, returnees. Women and girls with disabilities were reported to be especially at risk of sexual exploitation and harassment Literature review of assessment reports indicate, due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. The Inter-Agency Needs Assessment (IRNA) report 13th - 15th March 2022 Canal Pigi County flagged out the deteriorating Humanitarian situation triggered by floods that resulted into an upsurge of WASH induced diseases; Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea, Acute Respiratory Infections, Skin infections, and trauma injuries on account of either snake bites, Accidents, and Gender-Based Violence (GBV). The semi-permanent health infrastructures collapsed as such the static health facilities are dysfunctionalREACH Situation Overview Report for Jonglei State between April and August 2022 highlights humanitarian needs as particularly high in Ayod, and Canal-Pigi counties, which were severely affected by flooding that resulted in widespread displacement and food insecurity. In August, reporting of people leaving their settlement as a consequence of flooding was particularly high in Ayod (100%), and Canal-Pigi (100)

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community consultation and Inclusive Participation: Community Participation and Inclusion: During the project's design, Health Action Africa (HAA) will conduct and facilitate a consultative forum/workshop in each selected County. The participants will be constituted from the local stakeholders comprising the local authorities such as the office of the County commissioners representatives, The County Relief and Rehabilitative Commissions representatives (RRC), County Health Department (CHDS), community representatives, youth groups (males and females), representatives of persons with special needs, and humanitarian partners. This forum/workshop allows women, men, boys, and girls from various backgrounds to participate in meaningful programming on common issues affecting the vulnerable persons facing persistent health insecurities. Communication, Information sharing and transparency: The project will ensure all persons of concern, women, men, boys, and girls of diverse backgrounds, have access to timely, accurate, and relevant information on (i) their rights and entitlements and (ii) the project activities. HAA will also communicate and share information in languages, formats, and culturally appropriate media for and accessible to all community groups through health promotions and campaigns. Feedback and response: HAA will adopt the tools from the Community engagement and Community working group for effective feedback mechanism. These tested tools are the community score cards. The Community scorecards will moderate reviews, allowing people of concern to provide formal and informal feedback. Information will be received and responded to systematically, taking corrective action as needed. Health Action Africa (HAA) will maintain effective feedback systems (including comments, suggestions, and complaints), using a variety of communication channels accessible to all persons of concern and appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback. HAA will allocate human and financial resources to ensure that input from persons of concern is systematically collected, acknowledged, assessed, referred, and responded to in a timely, confidential, and effective manner and collaborate with partners in feedback referral and response processes (wherever appropriate). Organizational learning and adaptation: Interventions, planning, priority setting, course corrections, and evaluation are informed on an ongoing basis by the views of persons of concern. HAA will learn from continuous engagement with communities of concern and adapt interventions and programs in response to new knowledge gained through community participation and feedback, both in the short and long term

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Prevention: through awareness and good practice, ensuring that all staff minimize the risks of any form of sexual exploitation and abuseAll project staff will be familiarized with the reporting mechanism for PSEA to ensure that they are clear on the steps to take where suspicions or concerns arise regarding allegations of Sexual Exploitation or abuse of adults in vulnerable populations. HAA will take immediate action /response to identify and address reports of sexual exploitation and abuse and ensure the safety and well-being of the sexually exploited or abused.PSEA (Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse), Do No Harm Policy: Health Action Africa (HAA) has adopted the PSEA policy from the UN (United Nations) core humanitarian Standards, The HAA management will ensure project staff receive a PSEA refresher training to ensure compliance with the protection of anyone, whether direct or indirect beneficiaries, while protecting project staff from all forms of inappropriate sexual conduct. Prevention of Sexual Violence: There will be safety audits, protection mainstreaming, and crowd control during every project activities/distribution sites to ensure safety, confidentiality, respect, and non-discriminatory delivery of services. The protected services will be appropriately documented. The project will pay for incentives to build temporary protection corners and cover costs of data protection folders for Information management.Communication and transparency: The project will ensure all persons of concern, women, men, boys, and girls of diverse backgrounds, have access to timely, accurate, and relevant information on (i) their rights and entitlements and (ii) the project activities. HAA will also communicate and share information in languages, formats, and culturally appropriate media for and accessible to all community groups through health promotions and campaigns

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Organizational learning and adaptation: Interventions, planning, priority setting, course corrections, and evaluation are informed on an ongoing basis by the views of beneficiaries / persons of concern. HAA will learn from continuous engagement with communities of concern and adapt interventions and programs in response to new knowledge gained through community participation and feedback, both in the short and long term. There will be quarterly reviews, progress monitoring and learning. This feedback will be used to inform project design of the humanitarian assistance to address the context and relevant needs of the beneficiaries
