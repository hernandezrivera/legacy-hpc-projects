Project Summary

The proposed project aims at providing integrated Nutrition treatment and prevention services for drought affected under five boys, girls and caregivers in Daynile, Kahda, Dharkenley of Benadir region and Afgoye corridor of Lower Shabelle region. It will contribute to reduction of nutrition related morbidity and mortality rates. SOYDA intervention will target pregnant and lactating mothers together with children aged below 5 years among the host communities, IDPs and returnees. Active case finding for malnourished cases will be done through mass screening of children aged below 5 years and pregnant and lactating mothers for rapid identification of malnourished cases. Moderate acutely malnourished children and pregnant and lactating mothers will be referred for enrolment in the SFP programs run by other actors in Benadir and Lower Shabelle Region The project will support a network of 11 fixed Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTP and 8 mobile sites fully equipped with OTP commodities including Ready To Use Therapeutic Foods (RUTF), essential medicines for treatment of communicable diseases, vaccines, trained OTP nurses, vaccinators and hygiene promoters. The trained OTP nurses will treat severe acutely malnourished children in the static and mobile OTPs according to IMAM protocol. Mass screening will be done by trained health workers deployed in the 8 mobile clinics and trained 30 CHWs each assigned at least one village in the areas covered by the project. Immunization status of all children screened will be checked by both static and mobile OTP staff and children who are eligible for vaccination and do not have updated immunization status will be immunized by the vaccinators and assistant vaccinators in the mobile OTPs. Severe acutely malnourished children with medical complications will be referred by the mobile clinics and the CHWs to the stabilization Centre in Afgooye and Benadir hospital for nutrition rehabilitation. Hygiene promoters will during the activities of the mobile clinics disseminate hygiene promotion messages to the communities to prevent outbreak and spread of communicable diseases like AWD/cholera and measles. During the same time, mothers will be counseled individually and in groups by trained health workers and Community Health Workers (CHWs) on appropriate maternal infant and young child feeding as part of efforts to prevent malnutrition. The project will also provide micronutrient support by distributing multiple micronutrients (MNPs) and Vitamin A supplements through the static and mobile clinics. Staff dedicated to the static and mobile OTPs will be subjected to proper trainings on infant and young child feeding, integrated management of acute malnutrition and nutrition, health and hygiene promotion. Efforts will also be made to link households with children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) conditions and suspected cholera cases to WASH interventions and food security/ livelihood programs. There will be regular coordination meetings at the field level between WASH, Health and Nutrition clusters to foster synergy, avoid duplication of efforts/ resources and share information on response efforts. SOYDA will keep the nutrition cluster, Ministry of Health and UNOCHA regularly informed on progress of activities through sharing of progress reports and also share any challenges encountered for rapid assistance.

Needs

Despite improvements in the first half of 2018, major humanitarian needs in Somalia persist, particularly among IDP populations, due to residual impacts of drought, ongoing displacement, conflict and marginalization. 5.4 million people are still in need of humanitarian assistance throughout Somalia, including 2.8 million children; although this number may decrease in the second half of the year following the impact of above average Gu rains and sustained humanitarian interventions. Malnutrition rates across Somalia remain among the worst in the world. In total, about 1.2 million children under-5 are projected to be acutely malnourished in 2018 including, 232,000 expected to be suffering from severe acute malnutrition. According to FAO-FSNAU, an estimated 294,000 children are acutely malnourished, of which nearly 55,000 are severely malnourished. The surveys show that the overall level of acute malnutrition is serious (median GAM of 14 per cent), which is an improvement from critical compared to the 2017 post-Gu assessment (median GAM of 17.4 per cent). Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) prevalence is critical (more than 4 per cent) only among Mogadishu IDPs. Of major concern is the increasing crude death rate (CDR) of over one per cent in three out of 33 population groups surveyed, as well as the under-five mortality rate (U5MR). The situation is predicted to worsen between August and October due to the limited availability of public health and nutrition services. Disease outbreaks such as AWD/cholera and measles continue to represent a major threat to children with 6,979 suspected measles cases (72 per cent being children under-5) and 5,704 suspected cases of AWD/cholera with 39 deaths reported in 2018. The project will address all above issues and contribute the reduction of malnutrition in our target areas through provision of integrated live saving nutrition package.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project beneficiary may face different level of risk based on the contentious displacement in most target areas such risk include, cultural and religious barriers that might led discouragement from using health services such as MNPs, immunization,vtamin A supplementation among other nutrition service that is necessary for immediate treatment of severely and moderately malnourished children, confidential or privacy problem, poor nutrition outreach and facility based design that would not provide the beneficiaries the required attention and well as the confidence for caregivers (male and female) separation coupled with lack of information documentation among the most vulnerable population specific needs in the target location.SOYDA organization shall however intends to mitigate such risk through improving the community information sharing on the nutrition needs and services as well as improving the design, confidentiality/privacy and coordination mechanism in working with all actors as well improving the distance and time needed for immediate access to basic nutrition service packages(BNSP) by creating mobile teams that will provide services closer to these disable and marginalized communities in both rural and urban IDPs set up in Mogadishu and Afgooye.SOYDA will also ensure to provide effective community mobilization and sensitization to avoid any child to missed most important vaccination/immunization ,it shall also improve community engagement to review the challenges and barriers of nutrition services on both local leaders religious leaders, women group and youth for better health services uptake.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

In implementing the nutrition service provision in highly risk and unpredictable areas there might be different factors that might led the risk of exclusion based on age and gender in circumstance such exclusion may include sexual exploitation among the female health/Nutrition employee, exclusion on job opportunities duty discrimination against minorities, physically challenge person, poor representation among the affected people, poor feedback mechanism as well as lack of code of conduct, monitoring and reporting channel, such risk factors might hinder the actual program implementation purpose thus led it to ineffectiveness in reaching the affected people.To mitigate this SOYDA organization intends to established clear feedback mechanism for complaints during such instance on sexual exploitation as well as poor nutrition staff conduct that calls for unprofessional behaviors in doing so, SOYDA will provide the project beneficiaries with hotline/call center that shall be responsible by a compliant officers who is going to directly report to executive director for immediate action this will further ensure the accountability to the affected population, it will also create reporting and monitoring system that shall ensure properly documented project information, challenge, lesson learnt as well as ensure all nutrition staff signed code of conduct.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Due to the porous security situation in most part of the country Somalia, there might be many conflict related risk towards the beneficiary such are military occupation of the health and nutrition facilities, untargeted stray bullet to beneficiaries, cross fire violence that might erupt between the militia group and the government forces as well as sexual abuse among the vulnerable girls and women in the IDPs set up hence this might directly inflict more harm to the beneficiaries in the target location To mitigate this, SOYDA shall ensure the risk of beneficiaries are minimized during the displacement by putting health and nutrition facilities far from military bases to enable avoid any stray bullet and cross fire that could have otherwise danger the already displaced beneficiaries, it shall also ensure to provide psychosocial support to its project beneficiaries to cater for the need of the sexual gender based violence (SGBV) as well as establish coherence reporting and coordination pathways that will enable the beneficiary to seek immediate life-saving and life-sustaining intervention.
