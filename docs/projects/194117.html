Project Summary

The aim of the project is to improve access to quality and lifesaving nutrition services including screening, treatment, referral and preventative measures of acute malnutrition among IDPs and vulnerable crisis-affected communities. The intervention will also aim to reduce mortality and morbidity among children under two (U2), who face the highest risk of malnutrition, mortality, and morbidity, exacerbated by the current military conflict between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Response Forces (RSF) that commenced on 15 April 2023. The conflict has had a significant impact on humanitarian needs, with 19.9 million people classified as food insecure, 11 million people in need of health services and 4.6 million in need of nutrition services. The project is in line with the Nutrition Cluster’s Objective 1: Life-saving preventive and protection intervention focusing among under-two and Objective 2: Treatment of Severe and moderate among under-fives and PLWs. The project will provide a minimum package of nutrition services through the health facility in Daba Naira Camp, Ag Geneina, Al Fao, Al Galabat Al Gharbyah- Kassab and Port Sudan as well as other locations affected by the ongoing conflict. The multi-sectoral approach with Health, Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), and Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) elements will maximize the impact of prevention of malnutrition, by providing basic and life-saving assistance to the vulnerable target population. The project covers basic and minimum package of nutrition including community outreach; outpatient treatment of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and severe acute malnutrition (SAM) without medical complications under community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) protocol; referral of SAM and MAM cases to higher level of care at Stabilization Centers (SC) or hospitals. The project also provides counselling and health education at the community and facility level, such as optimal infant and young child feeding in emergency (IYCF/E) message dissemination, promotion of exclusive breastfeeding and continued breastfeeding, close monitoring and reporting of BMS code violations and critical early detection of malnutrition.
IOM has the technical and operational capacity to provide quality nutrition service in the support of, and in coordination with, UNICEF, WHO, SMoH and other sector partners. IOM will coordinate with local authorities, field office, UNDSS, to obtain timely information and mitigation measures to ensure the continuity of service provision if security risks arise and humanitarian access to target locations are hampered.

1 - Needs

Before the current conflict, Sudan’s overall global acute malnutrition (GAM) remained at 13.6 per cent, with the prevalence of GAM varying significantly across the localities with 15 per cent or above reported in 64 localities in Sudan. Drivers of malnutrition including food insecurity, deterioration in the provision of Health and WASH services, an economic crisis, and suboptimal caring and feeding practices for children under five (U5) are likely to contribute to the deterioration of the nutrition situation in the country. Acute malnutrition is associated with high risks of mortality and morbidity, children under two (U2) need to be particularly prioritized since they face the highest risk of malnutrition, mortality, and morbidity. The current conflict has induced displacements of households favouring drivers of malnutrition and risking the lives of the most vulnerable including compromised access to food due to inflation, sub-optimal supply of water and sanitation facilities, coupled with increased infections and communicable diseases. The Nutrition Cluster reports that an estimated 30 per cent increase in the number of children with acute malnutrition in hot spot areas with a 15 per cent increase in IDPs in hosting states and a 10 per cent increase in other localities. Pregnant and lactating women are also at risk of increased malnutrition with pre- and post-natal care not readily available and household feeding practices have been disrupted by displacement and instability of food sources. A comprehensive multi-sectoral approach is needed to prevent and treat acute and chronic malnutrition and its subsequent complications in the hotspot areas.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will provide nutrition services, ensuring the multi-sectoral approach, between health and nutrition. IOM will also coordinate and integrate its activities with other clusters such as Food Security and Livelihoods and WASH, making sure to address the cause of malnutrition comprehensively, and maximizing the impact of the intervention. The project will provide the basic minimum package for nutrition suggested and agreed by the Cluster which is life-saving prevention, and protection interventions for malnutrition prioritizing U2 and treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition. This will be achieved through the IYCF/E minimum package including nutrition counselling for pregnant and lactating mothers, formation of breastfeeding Community groups and the critical implementation, monitoring and control of marketing of breastmilk substitutes including reporting of such violations. Additionally, the project will encompass outpatient treatment of MAM and SAM without medical complications and CMAM protocol. To ensure continuum of care, the project will be keen to refer SAM cases with medical complications appropriately for quality treatment. The intervention will further include vitamin A supplementation per child (two doses), iron, and folic acid during pregnancy, early detection of malnutrition among U5, and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) through MUAC screening at facilities or mass MUAC screenings and referrals at community level including regular household visits, deworming of U5 (12 to 59 months), growth monitoring and promotion among U2 (0 to 23 months). Necessary equipment, supplies, furniture will be provided or mobilized for service provision, for instance a scale that measures weight and height will be requested from UNICEF. For the nutrition supplies and medicine, IOM will approach and coordinate with UNICEF and WHO, through Nutrition Sub-Cluster in intervention areas. IOM will also mobilize the healthcare cadre, in consultation and in coordination with SMoH, including community health workers (CHWs) and community midwives (CMs). CHWs and CMs will take a role in community outreach, as well as follow-up of mother-to-mother and father-to-father support groups. The healthcare workers will also be trained on CMAM, IYCF/E, micronutrient supplementation and Growth Monitoring and Promotion.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

IOM will try to minimize the negative environmental impact as a result of the project. For example, IOM will advocate for the mothers to bring empty sachets of RUTF/RUSF to the health facility, which can be exchanged with additional items. The collected sachets will be disposed of through proper waste management process according to WHO and SMOH standards. IOM will also provide key messages that raise awareness among the target communities about environmental health risks. The awareness campaigns will focus on issues such as preventing the spread of infectious disease through vector control, proper waste disposal, and personal hygiene, which will reduce malnutrition-related risks.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will address the protection needs of IDPs, women, and other vulnerable groups such as IDPs, returnees and other affected communities through strengthening the capacity of the community to create a protective environment. The project will pay special attention to women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women, children under the age of five and persons with disabilities who do not have adequate access to essential health and nutrition services and vulnerable to large scale epidemiological events and disease outbreaks. The planned interventions will focus on the development of women’s capacities, strengthening women’s participation and direct interaction with communities as well as health providers and addressing negative health seeking behaviors to promote gender equality and reproductive health rights. For the protection against gender-based violence, targeted women, girls and persons with disabilities, the project will strengthen the coordination between the supported health facilities and GBV Sub-Cluster, by facilitating the capacity building of the healthcare staff for clinical management of rape (CMR), psychological first aid (PFA) and being aware of the way to maintain the confidentiality and referral pathways. GBV key messages will be also shared through community outreach services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected IDPs and local communities to the implementation of the interventions. Efforts will be made to engage in particular women including PLWs, children, and youth. The project will empower these groups by encouraging direct participation in community-based health awareness activities. As part of capacity development, CHWs will be trained as mentors and key messengers addressing negative practices as well as protection threats such as harassment, sexual exploitation, and abuse, targeted at women, girls, and persons with disabilities. Accountability will also be promoted through the capacity development of CHWs and health providers as well as NGOs/CSOs in the health sectors as duty bearers to inform Cluster partners in health, nutrition, and protection if protection threats were reported. Applying IOM's experiences enhancing the protection of migrants through the provision of direct assistance services, IOM will provide direct assistance through psychosocial support, counselling, and medical assistance to survivors in coordination with the sector partners as needed. Through previous interventions, IOM and NPO established the trust and linkage with the community in the camp. IOM will continue to coordinate with local authorities, field office, UNDSS, to obtain timely information and mitigation measures to ensure business continuity and service provision if security risks arise and humanitarian access to the camp is hampered. For example, IOM will ensure sufficient stock of supplies in the facility in Daba Naira, in case of a disruption of the transportation from the capital.
