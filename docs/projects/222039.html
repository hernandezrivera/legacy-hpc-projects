Project Summary

In the lean season projection period of April to July 2024, the food security situation will
deteriorate and an estimated 7.10 million people (56% of the population) will likely face Crisis
(IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 79,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC
Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County (11,000) of Greater Pibor Administrative Area,
Aweil East County (40,000) of Northern Bahr el Ghazal State and 28,000 South Sudanese
returnees that will be spread across the country.
The levels of severe acute food insecurity in the country remain high, necessitating urgent scale
up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent the total collapse of
livelihoods in the affected counties, especially those with a high share of populations in
Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity as well as for South
Sudanese returnees.
The most food insecure populations are in locations that have been significantly affected by
frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency
depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the
conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in
humanitarian assistance. As resources are limited and FSLC partners cannot meet all
needs, so rather than scattering our forces, the impact of FSL
cluster interventions will be greater if we combine and coordinate
complementary interventions. Integration of COI/CO2/ C03
activities will achieve the desired strategic long-term solutions to
resilience building within vulnerable households rather than
distribution of Food and agricultural inputs alone. The impact will
be much greater if complimentary interventions are combined
through appropriate response packages as well as increased
local capacity that will enhance food security. To reduce the
number of people in need of humanitarian assistance by 2026,
the FSLC will ensure that CO1, CO2 and CO3 interventions are
closely coordinated in the same location and for the same
beneficiaries

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread floods, conflict and insecurity. Inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phases 4, and 63,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5(Catastrophe). The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. an estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. As per Integrated Food Security Phase Classi cation report published on 18th of November 2024 and its projection for April – July 2025, 57.2% of the population will be in phase 3 or above, who need food assistance. When reviewing this by State, the two States seriously affected because of the combined effect of flood and communal conflict are Unity State, affected 71.6% and Jonglei State, affected 62.4%. Considering the resources scarcity and other partners contribution, from Unity States, Mayom and Pariang Counties are targeted, who has got 81,000 and 75,000 people in need respectively and from Jonglei State, Bor South and Pibor Counties are targeted, who has 88,000 and 200,000 people in need. The main drivers of severity of food insecurity are floods, dry spells, in ation and high food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence and household stressors (such as death of the bread winner, gender-based violence, and morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services), which are greatly affected by the continuous effect of shocks. In South Sudan, 95 per cent of livelihoods depend on traditional rain-fed agriculture, crop farming, pastoralism and animal husbandry. Climate induced shocks (floods events, localized dry spells and conflicts for grazing areas and farming) have increased violent conflicts among agropastoral communities and have led to an increase in livestock mortality and a decline in the agricultural productivity. Among the most affected people are child headed households, persons with disabilities, older people and women headed households. South Sudan ranks amongst the top ten countries in the world most vulnerable to climate impact, including droughts and flooding. 2. Project activities

ADA will consider the following activities in accordance with the FSLC guidance and cluster objectives and according to HNRP 2025 strategies: 1. 1. Selection, registration & verification of the beneficiaries for unconditional food assistance in IPC4 + locations not reached by WFP (through cash response) and in-kind food assistance (including GFD (cash -25%) and voucher-10%) to 500HHs, vulnerable host, IDP and returnee communities (275females and 225 males), consisting of men, women, boys, girls; 1.2. Emergency Livelihood: Support to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary nutritious vegetable and crop seeds/tool, fishing and support to 4,500HHs, vulnerable host, IDP and returnee communities (2,450 females and 2,050 males), consisting of men, women, boys, girls, elderly people and people with disabilities. And as such distribution of dry season agricultural kits (VK, FK), this is planned for 4,500 vulnerable individual households to provide each of them for the upcoming dry season, the livelihood support package;1.3. ADA will procure the five types of the highly nutritious vegetable seeds; 20-gram amaranth, collard, eggplant, onion, tomato and 30-gram watermelon, pumpkin; 50-gram okra including at least one agricultural tool: maloda (shovel), and rake; 1.4. ADA will procure the fishing kits (FK) to support 500 fisher folks to provide them with two boxes of hooks (100/box), one coil of monofilament, and two spools of twine. All the procurements will be advertised on all the media (ADA website, NGO Forum and others) and all bidders will be subjected to a thorough, fair and transparent bids analysis and the best bidder will be awarded; 2.1. Training on Good Agricultural practices, Integrated pest management, value addition and marketing; 50 individuals will be selected and trained from each county to serve as lead farmers in the kitchen garden; 2.2. Training on fish processing; 200 fisher folks will be trained on fish catching, processing, value addition, marketing and preservation, and management in which 50 fishermen will be selected and trained from each operational County. Result 3: Resilience Support With overall aim of reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs support, 3,000 individuals from host and returnee communities will have an improved ability to absorb shocks with various resilience building interventions. Dyke work support and maintenance; 3.1. Training of community-based extension Workers (CBEWs); 20 extension workers (10females and 10 males) will be selected from the community with set of criteria and will be trained for three days on sustainable agricultural and improved practices in farming so as to rollout the same training and support to their communities and project beneficiaries when the project exits; 3.2. Develop & implement Integrated Pest Management strategies, addressing issues such as Fall Armyworm (FAW), desert locusts, and pesticide use; this is area specific strategy developed by project team and County MAFS expert in close consultation of the community, which will be used during this project period to mitigate the crop loss that comes from effect of pest. And of course, the project beneficiaries selection, registration and verification of 500HHs will be conducted while mindful of the gender and inclusivity concerns and utmost emphasizing the accountability to the affected population and their participation in the project implementation; Formation of the project management committee(PMC) and accountability to affected population committee(AAP) will reflect gender and persons with disabilities representation; Conducting monitoring, evaluations and learning and reporting of the project activities as well as formation and training of AAP and PMC committees, and conducting safety audits and GBV, conflict sensitivity, risk analysis and finally conducting the baseline surveys (FCs and Coping strategies) in all the project locations.
