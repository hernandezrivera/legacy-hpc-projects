Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan still remains dire amidst the revitalized peace agreement signed in Khartoum by the government and opposition parties. Several ceasefires have been undermined with continuous clashes between the government and opposition parties across the country hence forced about 1.5 people to flee their homes and took refuge in the neighbouring countries - Uganda, Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), Kenya, Sudan and Ethiopia while 1.9 million people were displaced within the Country with many more hiding in the bushes and swamps in remote areas. The almost five years prolonged conflict and an entrenched economic crisis have caused severe human suffering in South Sudan with one million in brink of starvation. With the progress of the peace signed in Khartoum, it is strongly believed that the millions of South Sudanese displaced to the neighbouring Countries like Uganda, Kenya, Sudan, DRC, etc including internally displaced persons (IDPs) will voluntarily return to their areas of origin hence high influx of returnees is possible. It is on this background that the 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan for South Sudan projects should hugely increase in the number of people in need that include IDPs, returnees and the host communities whose resources have been depleted or stressed. Nearly 1.9 million people have been internally displaced, 85% of whom are women and children besides millions that have fled the country as refugees to the neighbouring countries. SPEDP proposes a-12 month project targeting at 25,000 vulnerable people(6100 women, 5248 men, 7109 girls and 6543boys) with emergence shelter, life-saving non-food items and cash based intervention responses to address the different needs of vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in Greater Yei Counties. SPEDP is very familiar working in these Communities without any access constrains. SPEDP will engage with joint mobile response teams including , Lacha Community and Economic Development (LCED), Concern and TiTi Foundation to address shelter and NFI needs to the affected populations in assessed areas in the project locations. SPEDP will work in close collaboration with above-mentioned partners to establish synergies among the similar initiatives and avoidance of duplication and overlapping. Among the activities include needs assessment, beneficiaries verification and registration, distribution of shelter and NFI materials, Cash-Based Interventions (CBI) as SPEDP has a good reputation in Korijo IDP Camp where 600 emergency shelters were constructed through CBI programme. Market assessments will be conducted in stable project sites like Yei, Morobo and Lainya to establish items available in the market for the cash intervention. Post distribution monitoring will also be conducted to find out whether beneficiaries are satisfied with the items distributed. SPEDP will assume the role of distribution together with mobile and static partners that may be present in the respective project locations. The proposed project will focus on women headed households, child headed households’, people living with disabilities, elderly people, etc incorporating gender dynamics and specific needs of different groups into needs assessment and response recommendations. SPEDP will equally engage with stakeholders concern and will form a committee that will oversee beneficiaries’ selection criteria of women headed households, child headed households, people with disabilities and the older people. For the safety of the cash, SPEDP will use its previous approach of contracting money transfer agents or be so flexible by doing it directly with the help of identified and contracted vendors.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan still remains dire amidst the revitalized peace agreement signed in Khartoum by the government and opposition parties. Several ceasefires have been undermined with continuous clashes between the government and opposition parties across the country hence forced about 1.5 people to flee their homes and took refuge in the neighbouring countries such as Uganda, DRC, Kenya, Sudan and Ethiopia while 1.9 million people were displaced within the Country with many more hiding in the bushes and swamps in remote areas. The almost five years prolonged conflict and an entrenched economic crisis have caused severe human suffering in South Sudan with one million in brink of starvation. Greater Yei is the worst hit with continuous clashes between the government and opposition parties since the July 2016 skirmishes. More clashes still take place in some parts of Yei, Kajo Keji and Lainya Counties to date. Over 60,000 people from Yei, Lainya, Morobo and Kajo Keji fled their homes to the neighboring Uganda and Congo for refuge. Tens of thousands are seeking shelter in Yei and Lainya towns with many more hiding in the bushes, in Mukaya of Lainya County alone there are 7,858 IDPs being hosted in Yei town with many more hiding deep in the villages. Most of the population displaced lost all their household belongings and have limited access to shelter according to several assessments conducted by partners in Yei and SPEDP Initial Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) report, which was conducted in October 17, 2018 in Korijo village of Kajo Keji County. Humanitarian access remains restricted to many areas of greater Yei with a high vulnerability of IDP and host communities in need of humanitarian assistance. The priority needs for this intervention are based on life saving assistance to protect and reduce risks of diseases and human suffering, which is highly registered among children under the age of 5 years and the elderly above the age of 60 who are highly affected by the conflict. The summary of proposed intervention include: - provision of emergency shelter to protect displaced persons from harsh weather conditions and protection risks, provision of life-saving NFIs to contribute to better health of IDPs and returnees, enhance nutrition, protection and better hygiene and CBI to support the priority shelter and address NFI needs of the vulnerable IDPs and returnees.

Activities

The key activities to be addressed across the four Counties of Greater Yei are include: – 1. Engaging in joint needs assessments with partners like LCED, Titi, ARD-A and Concern to better understand the emergency context and identify the extent of needs among the affected populations before planing any joint response.2. Engaging in joint verification exercise to confirm presence and needs of IDPs, returnees and host Community in all the targeted counties of Greater Yei done in close collaboration with the concerned authorities, partners and beneficiaries who will provide more information besides available data to verify exact figures that should benefit from the intervention.3. Conducting beneficiaries’ registration in collaboration with the community leaders, church leaders and community representatives to aid distribution of the identified needs.4. Conducting distribution of shelter and NFIs e.g. tarpaulin sheets, cooking sets, mat, blankets and mosquito nets to the targeted households.5. Engaging in market assessments to verify available goods in the market to support the cash based intervention programme.5. Supporting construction/repair of shelters and provision of NFI to the vulnerable female headed households, elderly people, sick people and child headed families through cash based intervention programme. This intervention will only be done in peaceful locations with access to market e.g. Lainya, Morobo and Yei.6. Conducting post distribution monitoring to evaluate the effectiveness, appropriateness and coverage of individual responses, and thus accountability to beneficiaries.All the activities will be coordinated with partners like LCED, Titi, ARD-A and Concern World Wide in order to complement on existing gaps as well as reducing duplication of activities in the targeted project locations.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The communities will be continuously consulted on the process and contents of interventions conducted; local support enumerators, including males and females, will be recruited on a temporary basis to support these activities and ensure they are locally appropriate, sensitive and relevant. This project will inform the beneficiaries about the assistance through project inception and community mobilization meetings with the relevant stakeholders both primary and secondary stakeholders by sharing the project objectives, project expected out puts and the deliverables.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The messages are tailored for gender and age groups through involvement of women, men, girls and boys including the community leaders. This helps SPEDP to figure out the priority needs of affected populations and give due consideration to their perceived needs.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Distribution centres will be located at central points for easy access by all the beneficiaries and distributions organized early enough to mitigate cases of sexual exploitation and abuse. Women and girls will be advised to move in groups during distribution to avoid cases of rape and other GBV incidences. Women and girls will be provided with temporary shelters to minimize cases of sexual and gender based violence (SGBV).

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The consultations will bring on board the local authorities, community leaders, church leaders and community representatives from women, youth and people with disabilities to voice their different opinions or needs for effective and relevant provision of shelter and NFI deliverables.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The complain mechanisms will include formal and informal mechanism due to different literacy levels of the community. The informal mechanism will include members from the community to access project managers contact numbers for sharing any complain related to the project. The formal complains shall be gathered through setting up information centres, complain desk and mobile teams seeking ongoing feedback. However, the targeted population will also be consulted to give their views how they prefer to channel their complains to SPEDP project team without any obstacles.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The community will be engaged to participate in project monitoring and evaluation through an established committees that comprises of relevant authorities, community leaders and community representatives to enable them know whether the project is progressing well and meeting the set targets including lessons learnt. Post distribution monitoring will be carried out in conjunction with the community representatives to ensure that the assistance provided is in line with the needs beneficiaries preferences.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SPEDP will ensure protection concerns are mainstreamed in all activities under this project and this will be through working in collaboration with protection actors including GBV and child protection sub clusters to strengthen the capacity of the project implementation team. SPEDP team will review the context for conflict sensitive and HLP issues, and referral pathways, in order to improve understanding of the context dynamics and therefore decrease harm on the community. Safety audits will be conducted to understand risks of every gender. This will enhance so greatly in having number of protection concerns analyzed and protection standards maintained. SPEDP will also closely monitor the identification of protection cases in order to adequately serve groups at risk, such as the youth, female headed households and the most vulnerable.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SPEDP will prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: ensuring that SPEDP delivers services and assistance in ways that preserve the physical integrity of all population groups, that are culturally appropriate and that avoid any unintended negative consequence. Staff will work with protection actors to closely monitor the potential risks associated with conducting distributions in particular areas, and in the identification of protection cases in order to adequately serve them with NFI and shelter materials, such as unaccompanied minors, pregnant women, and the elderly. Given the specific security dynamic in Greater Yei counties and surrounding areas, SPEDP will continue to ensure that protection of beneficiaries, humanitarian staff and assets are at the forefront of planning and implementation throughout the project period. Based on the fact that there are still armed groups that never signed the revitalized peace agreement may posse threat in accessing the beneficiaries. However, SPEDP will coordinate its operations with local Government authorities, leadership of the opposition parties, community leaders and partners to enable easy access of shelter/NFI materials to all the targeted population groups.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The community members i.e. women, men, girls and boys, people living with disabilities, other minorities will be consulted on power dynamics as perceived by them and how equity can be achieved in their perspectives. The project will ensure non-discrimination and meaningful access to project assistance and services. The concerns of safety and dignity of children, youths, women and men will be central in this project. Special attention will be paid to ensuring the participation of, and consultation with women and girls who may often be excluded from decision-making processes. SPEDP will continue to provide special shelter and NFI needs to vulnerable population groups.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities seek to address inequalities and protection threat through equal participation of all vulnerable age groups and people with disabilities and protection mainstreaming in all the activities. During distribution, people with disabilities, elderly and pregnant lactating mother will be given priority to receive their items. Distribution centres will be located at central point for easy access by all the beneficiaries and distributions organized early enough to mitigate GBV and other protection cases.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project develops and adopts a monitoring, evaluation and reporting (MER) framework to assess the project performance. At the start of the project a clear MER workshop will be organized involving SPEDP national and field staff. SPEDP Centre will conduct periodic monitoring visits to target communities; develop and submit narrative and financial reporting; and ensure procurement, monitoring and stock taking of commodities. Direct beneficiary reach will be monitored by Field Staffs during trainings, workshops and community meetings. The project will adopt measures to enhance access of beneficiaries to project resources and services through post distribution monitoring report, Focused Group Discussions (FGDs) with the beneficiaries and collection of success stories from the project beneficiaries.The project will involve the beneficiaries monitor the project activities when they participate in its implementation.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring will measure the safety of beneficiaries through rapid monitoring, post distribution monitoring and Focused Group Discussions with the affected population. Likewise, SPEDP has a potential advantage of being a NNGO with its operational base in the target Counties. SPEDP through its security focal person will constantly monitor the security threats and developments in project areas to determine safety of beneficiaries and staff. The organization also has protection policy and PSEA policy which all staffs sign and adhere to.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Post distribution monitoring will be used to measure the level of beneficiaries dignity through the shelter and NFI materials distributed by checking on the appropriateness, equity and the quality of loose items distributed to the beneficiaries.
