Project Summary

Recent reports and HRP prioritize the need for scaling-up interventions to contain the economic deterioration affecting livelihoods, agriculture sector and food security as well as tnd establish protection services especially among the IDPs in camps and informal settlements. AHF has conducted a rapid needs assessment in 7 sub-districts in Idleb and has reported the need for strengthening farmers’ livelihood, providing life-saving assistance, and supporting self-reliance of affected IDPs especially those in the camps AHF is designing a 10-months project in FSL three stages and The number of direct beneficiaries is 25,000 :
1) Supporting wheat value chain in Maaret Tamsrin.
2) Purchasing farmers' wheat production(600ton)in Maaret Tamsrin.
3) Produce bread and distribute it to the 18 target IDPs camps in Dana sub-district
AHF is already implementing bread distribution in the same camps for two years and currently seeking for new funding to continue providing life-saving assistance to the PiN through sustaining the bread distribution activity. the selected beneficiaries from the target IDPs who will receive food assistance(bread) a
Support wheat value chain with share in the cost of fertilizer irrigation harvest and purchasing wheat production from 300 farmers (2 tons from each farmer at a price of $400 per ton) in Maaret Tamsrin.
Distribution of agricultural inputs (wheat seeds) to farmers for the coming season (200 Kg/farmer) with total beneficiaries’ of 1,000 farmers in Maaret Tamsrin.
Bread production at AHF existing bakery & distribution to 18,500 individuals (250g per person of daily bread for 78 days) in the 18 target camps in Dana.
Cash-for-work to 28 individuals for 3 months to support wheat value chain at stages 1&2.
Cash-for-work to 31 individuals for 3 months through baking and distribution activities at stage 3.
Over the past year, the project has been fully implemented, and currently we have the same plan, and we do not have secure financing

3 - Needs

Based on HNO The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent of the total population. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, and 2.9 million people areestimated to be at risk of food insecurity, an alarming increase of people at risk who could shift to become food insecure during 2023 if the crisis continues at same worsening levels.Syria’s multiple crisis have severely affected the agriculture and agri-food sectors and supporting its resilience has become an urgent national priority. It is important to note that the sharp decline in crop production and the entrenchment of food insecurity during the 2021-2022 cropping season was not only driven by weather anomalies, but also a result of issues with water scarcity and availability,specific needs assessment for this project was conducted during the in 3 sub-districts including 7 communities and Dana camps from the most affected by the previous floods and there was a need to intervene in supporting wheat farmers in the aforementioned areas.
