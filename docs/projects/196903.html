Project Summary

The conflict in Syria has generated a protection crisis in which children are vulnerable to violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. After more than ten years of conflict, displacement and loss, Syrian children face severe needs which are exacerbating their protection concerns and risks. The worsening financial circumstance in Northwest Syria has negatively affected individuals attempting to get their fundamental necessities including food and medicine. This has added to expanded feelings of anxiety among communities. The brutal day to day environments in packed and crowded camps in Northwest Syria and absence of foundation are on the whole factors in individuals facing psychosocial distress, when a child is out of school, the impact is not limited to their education, but also can drastically affect their wellbeing, safety, protection, and future life chances and opportunities. The lack of access to schools can increase their anxiety and stress levels which may cause long-term harm to a child’s physical and mental wellbeing. Being out of school increases the hazard of domestic violence, child labor, child marriage, and recruitment with armed groups. Supporting children to overcome the boundaries to education can be complex due to overlapping emergencies and constantly changing situation of poverty, And it needs cooperative and integrational efforts of different sectors (such as education, protection and MHPSS). Furthermore, schools are frequently used as safe spaces where children meet their needs and linked with other services after their vulnerabilities can be identified. To reduce risks on children and to get them back to education safely, there must be an integrated case management and MHPSS approach both at a specialized level and at the community level. Girls and boys have different psycho-social support needs and face different barriers (limited access, limited services, limited capacities in understanding children’s needs...etc.) to reach mental health and psychosocial support, boys and girls who are not in school are at substantially at higher risk of being involved in child labor (particularly boys). Boys are also at risk of being recruited by armed groups. Girls are at a higher risk of being married, which then leads to early pregnancy and domestic violence in most of the cases. With higher rates of suicide among children alarming all the humanitarian workers to integrate their services, through the project Hurras aims to mitigate the burden of war on children by supporting the most vulnerable (child labor, child marriage, psychosocial distress, violence, neglect, and exploitation) with assistance focusing on their needs toward their protection from violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect and other protection threats, and help them integrating in their communities through implementing the peer support program facilitated in their schools and in communities through youth centers and committees, as well as, increasing their resilience and coping mechanisms and enhancing their mental health and psychosocial wellbeing due to living in the current war conditions toward their improved wellbeing by individualized MHPSS (for tier 3 to receive diagnosis and individual specialized support) and child protection services, and empower communities to support their children and children to support each other, the project also aims to strengthen the capacity and increase the engagement of key committees’ members to facilitate peer to peer children support and to do community-based child protection initiatives towards enhanced awareness of MHPSS and CP in their communities after receiving training of psychosocial support and peer to peer support and self-care, HRS has been doing similar activities for the past 9 years and all the M&E tools are already developed, in addition HRS will be committed to report to the CP cluster through on the 4Ws, and the project will be applying tools of accountability to affected people.

3 - Needs

The protracted conflict in Syria has severely impacted children’s education, leaving one third of school-aged children out of school and one in three schools either completely or partially not functional; thus, disrupting their learning and jeopardizing their hopes of a better future. Deteriorating living conditions, frequent exposure to violence and multiple displacements have profoundly impacted children’s psychological well-being, while COVID-19 pandemic and increased transportation costs are limiting their access to services, Girls and boys continue to be exposed to multiple child protection risks. Grave violations against children -including risk of being killed, injured, recruited, used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted and sexually abused- remain a significant concern. Insecurity, economic hardship and Covid-19 continue to exacerbate child protection concerns including harmful coping mechanisms such as child labor, child/ early marriage putting adolescent girls and boys at risk. Separation from caregivers and absence of alternative care options is common across Syria. High psychosocial distress prevails among children, while there are over 6 millions of children in need in Syria with 28% of them having disabilities, the CP AoR aims to support 2.5million children and their caregivers in need of care and protection, so Priority will be given to the most vulnerable, including those at risk of or have experienced violence, neglect, and abuse.

4 - Implementation of activities

Through the proposed HRS is aiming to reach Girls and boys affected by the war to show better resilience and wellbeing by receiving Psychosocial support from peers and community, and those in need to receive quality child protection case management services complimented with Individual Protection Assistance as well as focusing on raising the community's awareness on children’s mental health and psychosocial support and needs through the proposed activities: -Response for children: conducting PSS program with children and adolescents in and out of school, and conduct Youth Resilience-related program with youth along with community engagement program, and offering MHPSS service to children who are identified according to the specified criteria, in addition to establishing an internal referral mechanism to identify and refer the most vulnerable, linked with established cluster referral pathway and update service mapping regularly, and support the vulnerable children with case management and IPA. -Response for adults: conducting parenting skills program, offering awareness raising to communities and PFA, Peer Support and other relevant CP and MHPSS training to members of CP committees, and supporting them to conduct initiatives in their communities to raise awareness on CP and MHPSS children’s needs in their communities, and supporting adolescents to have initiatives/ vocational training/ towards supportive communities of children, and establishing CFS in community.
