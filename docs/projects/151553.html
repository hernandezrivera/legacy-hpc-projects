Project Summary

The goal of the project is to provide emergency assistance and ensure the non-discriminatory access to quality essential services to at least to 3335 people (incl children 0-5, girls 1,9%/ boys – 0,6%; children 5-17 – girls 3,8%/boys – 7,5%; adults 18-59 – 16,9% women and 12,2 % men; older adults 60+ – women 41,1 % and men – 16%) in need and improve the resilience of the conflict-affected population, prevent further degradation of humanitarian situation and promote early recovery and social cohesion in line with the Humanitarian response plan for 2019. It complies 1) Shelter repair component: 600 households (1380 beneficiaries) with light building repairs (550 HH) and emergency relief measures (50 HH) and 265 households supported with medium-scale building repairs (about 610 beneficiaries). 2) NFI component: 750 households provided with solid fuel (coal) for winterization support (about 1725 beneficiaries). Project will provide ability for vulnerable categories people, living in affected areas, to have satisfied dwelling conditions. The preferences will be given to most vulnerable groups (elderly without adult children to care for them, pregnant/lactating women in HH, extended families, people with oncological diseases, victims of gender-based violence, households with low income). When relevant the project will take into account also additional criteria. The project activity will increase quality of housing in stable, government controlled areas, that are not covered by shelter assistance of other humanitarian actors in accordance with Shelter Cluster coordination activity.

Needs

Damages. According to Shelter cluster, over 50.000 of buildings have been damaged or destroyed since the beginning of conflict, which 20.000 in GCA. Of which, 59% of houses incurred light damages, 34% of houses incurred medium damages, 6% incurred heavy damages and 1% incurred full destruction. And new damages are still recorded. According to Shelter Cluster assessment 5,300 HHs live in damaged accommodations along the contact line, there are still huge needs in shelter activities and significant piece of assistance should be focused on acute emergency support and light & medium types of repair. ADRA rapid needs assessment based on field monitoring visits and meetings with local authorities also shows a significant number of damaged households which are still not covered by humanitarian actors. Specifically, 4% of assessed HHs have heavy damages, 60% of assessed HHs have medium damages and 36% HHs have light damages. The population of concern is people living along the approximately 500-kilometer-long contact line and internally displaced persons. 250,000 civilians are dwelling in the active war zone in GCA and 600,000 civilians in NGCA. Majority of them are the most vulnerable because ideally, they should have fled these areas. Additionally, around 1 million out of 1.6 million registered IDPs are living in GCA and a significant part is dwelling in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts. There is also a negative trend in international funding while humanitarian needs arise. In its turn, the underfunding has forced some humanitarian partners to readjust the programmes, cease planned critical activities or even halt their intervention Winterization still remains the most acute concern and the main life-saving activity, especially when it comes to solid fuel distribution in the areas along the contact line. Between 23,464-30,270 households are in need of fuel on both sides of the contact line. Additionally, 12,800 IDPs and their households in Donetsk GCA and 5000 IDPs and their households in Luhansk GCA can’t afford basic goods and are applying negative coping mechanisms. Moreover, IDPs, which previously lived on the contact line locations, are returning to their homes as their resources have exhausted. ADRA also received information about partners’ winterization plans and it shows that most partners are cutting on the amount of winterization support. Acute emergency support. Non-displaced, returnee and internally displaced people, living in areas along the contact line, are at particular risk of conflict-related incidents. According to the Shelter Cluster, agencies should preposition on both sides of the contact line 400 shelter emergency kits in 2019, based on the trend registered in the first half of 2018 in Co-ordination with UNHCR and ICRC (HNO 2019). Beneficiaires. Due to HelpAge data elderly women is one of the vulnerable category and it prevails among people affected by the conflict. That's why it is the biggest group in the project proposed (elderly women 60+ 41% and men – 16%). The accent is made also on children (incl children 0-5, girls 1,9%/ boys – 0,6%; children 5-17 – girls 3,8%/boys – 7,5%) and mothers. Extended families, pregnant and lactating women, single parents (more often women) and victims of gender-based violence are the categories of priority in ADRA activities. Other vulnerable categories are people with oncological diseases, households with low income. Our beneficiaries have special needs and restrictions due to physical and peculiarities, which is taken into consideration during project planning. The repair work is provided together with materials to assure that the shelter problem is solved. Also winterization assistance is done in in-kind manner – the coal delivery is effectuated directly to beneficiary’s house.

Activities & outcomes

Result 1(relates to HRP Shelter/NFI Objective 3): Adequate permanent shelter and wintering assistance for the most vulnerable conflict affected population provided ADRA will continue providing construction materials alongside repair services. Our experience shows that most beneficiaries are older people who are unable to utilize the provided materials. ADRA has even observed that the materials provided by some organizations went unused for a period up to a year. MoU with all local authorities is signed in the target locations. This helps to increase partnership responsibility and involve local authorities in the identification of the most vulnerable households; creating awareness among local population; and cooperation in repair activities including warehouse support. ADRA is one of few organizations which hire locals as construction workers. This helps to contribute to the local economy and reach heavily accessed areas where construction suppliers are not easily available. Additionally, this approach will help contribute to the capacity building of the local population in repair activities. The project will put a part of efforts on covering suburbs of urban settlements and small communities in remote areas which are usually not covered by any humanitarian actor. ADRA will continue to practice home visits which are done by monitoring and technical teams. Our experience shows that information provided by local authorities should be verified and assessed as in some cases, it may not be consistent with the selection criteria. Result 2. Adequate wintering assistance for the most vulnerable conflict-affected population provided Winterization aid will focus on providing solid fuel to the most vulnerable households. Each household will receive 2 tons of solid fuel (coal) which was the minimum recommended by Shelter cluster as minimal HH support for the winter period. Due to the limited amount of support, the list of target households will include only those which meet several vulnerability criteria.
