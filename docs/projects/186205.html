Project Summary

WASH is the top priority need expressed by affected populations in assessed villages in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon according to MSNA conducted by OCHA in August 2021. Also according to Cholera outbreak Situation report N° 06, of November 2021 published by the Regional Delegation of Public Health for the South West, on 12/11/2021, 7 IDP host communities and 2 hospital registered deaths suspected of cholera in Kombo Itindi, Bakassi Health District with potentials to spread to other coastal IDP host communities in Bakassi and the Ndian Division.
The ongoing Cholera outbreak is further increasing the vulnerabilities of IDPs and their needs to access WASH services in the Bakassi health district. This project aims to provide WASH services particularly skills and awareness to toilet management committees in the Bakassi peninsular as well as construct some more emergency toilets to reduce open defecation as well as reduce the prevalence of Cholera among vulnerable IDPs and host communities in the Bakassi Peninsular and other coastal communities of Ndian Division as a whole. The project aims to reach 50,000 people for a period of six months. Main interventions will include construction of 5 water points, construction/rehabilitation of emergency toilets, and distribution of hygiene items.
The project will also include a component relative to training of existing and potential toilet management committees in Bakassi. These trained committees should be capable of managing their toilets sustainable when support from donor funds ends.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The humanitarian situation in the North-West (NW) and South-West (SW) regions of the country continues to appear to be worsening with frequency incidents reported among the non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and the military. The Multisectorial Need Assessment conducted in August 2021 reveals that drinking water is the second in rank as first priority need expressed by affected population in assessed villages. This situation has worsening compare to Dec 2020 where drinking water was still at 2nd position with 24.3% of assessed villages. Also according to Cholera outbreak Situation report N° 06, of November 2021 published by the Regional Delegation of Public Health for the South West, on 12/11/2021, 7 communities which are hosts to hundred of IDPs and 2 hospital registered deaths suspected of cholera in Kombo Itindi, Bakassi Health District with potentials to spread to other coastal IDP host communities in Bakassi and the Ndian Division. Therefore the ongoing Cholera epidemic is adding to the vulnerabilities of the host communities especially IDPs in the Bakassi health district of the Ndian Division.As far as sanitation is concern in the Bakassi, more individuals continue to defecate openly at beaches despite the construction of toilets by EPDA since 2019 and 2020 with support from UNICEF. One of the issues regarding unsustainable use of sanitation facilities is linked to cultural practices and another is due to the fact that the project to construct the toilet was an emergency response and so could not consider adequate time in training the local toilet management committees that were put in place in proper management of the toilets. Another problem is due to the fact that many communities still remain unreached because of limited resources. Regarding hygiene, sensitization efforts need to be stepped up to change behaviours. Access to water is very limited in Bakassi and boroughs of coastal communities which facilitates the outbreak of diseases such as Cholera. On the 27/10/2021 in the South West region, the Regional Delegation of Public Health reported a cholera outbreak. Few weeks later, the health district of Bonassamba in Littoral region also reported cholera cases in 2021. Thus, this situation is not good considering the presence of COVID-19 pandemic and frequency of cholera outbreaks in the South West region. On 25 November 2021, 84 cases and 17 deaths with a CFR of 4.92% were reported and only South West and Littoral regions were stills active. Last figure from South West region reveals that 209 people have been affected, 9 deaths reported and a CFR of 4.3%. sex ratio female /male is 1:2. Based on these needs, UNICEF and implementing partners plan to provide integrated WASH services using minimum WASH standards of SPHERE and/or national standards. An integrated package of WASH services will be provided to communities and institutions, with due consideration for solutions that are resilient climate and gender sensitive. Other transversal issues to consider include Protection, GBV and Accountabilities to Affected people. The project plans to sensitize, train and provide WASH kits to beneficiaries.
