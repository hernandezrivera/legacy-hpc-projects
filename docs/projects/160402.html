Project Summary

Panyikang, Raga, Kajo Keji Ayod and Fangak counties are among the other counties in the country where WASH needs are demanding, particularly among host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. This is attributed to by the impact of inter communal clashes and war experienced in some parts of the region in the recent past that led to vandalizing of the already limited existing WASH infrastructures. Seasonal floods in Aug to Oct 2019 wreaked havoc in many parts of Jonglei and WBeG states with more than 72,000 already vulnerable people being affected and rendered with no access to safe drinking water, basic sanitation and hygiene services and therefore being prone to cholera and acute watery diarrhoea and malaria which calls for unhindered humanitarian support and close monitoring.
Relief Corps Organization (RCO) proposes a viable holistic project that respond to the prevailing WASH gaps affecting the vulnerable populations in most complicated emergency zones – using its past experiences in emergency. RCO will to target 3,000 households (18,000 beneﬁciaries) made up of 3,000 men, 5,000 women, 5,000 girls and 5,000 boys including elderly (> 60 years and those with disabilities/ special needs) amongst the most vulnerable emergency affected IDPs, returnees and host communities directly affected by the floods & conﬂict with a With a WASH/GBV, WASH/NUT & WASH/Health integrated response in line with 2020 HRP WASH cluster strategic objectives through addressing and provision of acute WASH needs among beneﬁciaries at risk of gender and sexual based violence, those at risk of malnutrition as well as those at risk of contracting water-borne diseases whilst addressing WASH needs .
The project will address unmet WASH gaps and acute WASH needs at PHCC/PHCU health facilities by dealing with the root causes of active transmission of WASH related diseases including where it will aim to contribute to the decrease of the transmission rates. The project will in addition address malnutrition cases by providing WASH services at OTP/SC centers and address protection especially among IDPs, women and girls who face GBV, a key protection concern while they trekking for long distances in search for water among others.
To ensure this succeeds, the project will train WASH/Nutrition/Health hygiene promoters on core WASH’Nut, WASH’Health and WASH’GBV competencies including referrals and case reporting and they will in turn train and follow-up the mothers and caretakers of SAM affected children on the importance of improved hygiene coupled with Mass hygiene promotion campaigns. The project will then plan and review the needs together with beneficiary members of each activity site, technical assessment of target WASH facilities suggested by beneficiary members for repairs. Boreholes and hand dug wells will be rehabilitated and drilled by the trained pump mechanics and Water Management Committees who will be flushing out to ensure they (bore holes & hand dug well) are well cleaned and removed turbidity by air compressors and treated with chlorine for disinfection and improve water quality as well as replacing the worn out parts. RCO will address the sanitation situation both at the communal level and institutions like OTP/SC and PHCC/PHCU health centers through rehabilitations of gender/disability segregated latrines with doors and locks as well as installation of a hand washing facilities at each latrine.
The project will synergize multi sector response by coordinating with counterpart agencies implementing Protection, Health, Nutrition among others to ensure effectiveness and referrals of other cluster objectives in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of AAP principles, protection including GBV/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conﬂict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

1 - Needs

In general, there has been a significant reduction of internal conflicts and displacement as a result of the 2018 signed peace deal in South Sudan, and accordingly communities have enjoyed relative peace for the last year. However, parties from either sides of the agreement have expressed obstacles that have slowed down the implementation of the peace accord. This has led to tensions as the political contexts are fluctuating, the prevailing humanitarian context has increasingly remained overwhelming; at the same time, the 2020 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview predicts a more complex wave of humanitarian challenges, with many counties of Upper Nile, Jonglei and WBeG States indicating unprecedented levels of severity from a combination of other factors. WBeG, Unity and Upper Nile are still experiencing protracted conflict compounded by cyclical waves of intra and inter-communal violence, territorial, generational/age-set and inter-ethnic violence which is often triggered by spats of tit for tat revenge killings, children and women abductions, cattle raiding. This has been exacerbated by the current seasonal flooding which has caused multiple displacements of people. These events have been further complicated by the already unprecedented levels of food insecurity with consequent malnutrition-IPC findings of August 2019 which estimates 6.35 million people estimated to be extremely food insecure; and an estimated 6.35 Million South Sudanese women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2020. Worst still majority of the population in both rural and urban locations are struggling to survive due to the accumulative effects of the conflict, which has hurt livelihoods, and coping mechanisms. (IPC_South%20Sudan_Key_Messages_August_2019). This situation continues to challenge humanitarian actors with the wrath of matching limited resources and increasingly humanitarian needs of possible IDPs and Returnees. In WBeG, Jonglei and Upper Nile states for example inadequate bore hole and untreated surface water has been attributed as the main cause of water borne diseases; in addition, poor sanitation coverage and lack of hand washing facilities, poor knowledge and practices on personal and environmental further fuel the situation which constitute to nearly 50% of the total AWD and cholera cases. (South Sudan Situation Reports on Cholera 2017/18). Latrine coverage in Jonglei and Upper Nile states remains critically low (3%); thus open defecation is predominant in these areas and considering the current flooding, water sources may get contaminated by faecal matter hence potential for water borne disease epidemics such as cholera. The available limited facilities are not gender disaggregated and do not take care of the needs of those with disabilities. Community Knowledge Attitudes and Practices related to water safety, cholera preparedness/control, hygiene and sanitation practices still has a long way to go, with open defecation, lack of hand washing during critical times, poor disposal of solid waste etc being widespread. (FSNMS round 22). Deteriorating WASH conditions are further exacerbated by increased cases of protection concerns among girls, boys, men and women with subsequent conflict related sexual violence and abuse. Women and girls have been particularly vulnerable as they move long unsafe distances in search of water and other social amenities (Protection Trends 2019; Protection Cluster Updates). The above scenario is predominant in conflict/floods prone counties such as Panyikang, Pibor, Raga, Ulang, Fangak, Ayod among others; where only a few schools, markets and health centers have functional sanitation facilities that are not gender/disability tailored. Should these conditions persist, the food security and WASH situation is expected to deteriorate further. This calls for immediate and unhindered provision of humanitarian assistance and close monitoring.

1. Carry out risk analysis with girls and women and RCO female staffs in the community, schools, OTP/TSFP centers and health centers and market centers and rehabilitate 60 dysfunctional boreholes and water points to ensure fully functional and equitable access to safe and suﬃcient water to vulnerable and affected 18,000 beneﬁciaries in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Western Bahr el Ghazal States. 2. Carry out risk analysis with girls and women and RCO female staffs in the community, schools, OTP/TSFP centers and health centers and market centers and rehabilitate of 120 safe and lockable latrines and equipping them with hand-washing facilities to ensure 18,000 targeted beneﬁciaries practice safe, sanitary, digniﬁed and gender appropriate. 3. Sanitation - Carry out gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Western Bahr el Ghazal 4. Provision of WASH NFIs, including WASH/Hygiene kit to 5000 parents of children admitted for SAM and discharged at OTP/SC till the household level5. Mobilize and train 300 hygiene promoters/ Health/Nutrition workers/volunteers trained on the WASH minimum package of activities 6. Carry out hygiene promotion campaigns among 18,000 beneﬁciaries on appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases. 7. Mobilize and train 200 health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within handwashing with soap at critical times at their household level8. Provide timely WASH minimum package of interventions targeting 4000HHs on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the speciﬁcities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Western Bahr el Ghazal states. 9. Train 60 Water Management Committees to manage and maintain the 60 rehabilitated water points. 10. Train 30 Hand pump mechanics on repairs and installation of bore holes and wells. 11. Distribute WASH NFIs including Pur, Satchets, MHM kits (to women and girls at adolescent stage) and jerry cans to the identiﬁed vulnerable communities in the targeted states. 12. In schools, WASH clubs will be formed and the patrons will be trained to ensure sustainability of the WASH activities in schools using Child to Child approach. 13. Training of adolescent girls on Menstrual Hygiene management by female WASH staff as well distributing of menstrual hygiene kits both at household and institutional level. 14. Establishment of hand washing stations both at the community and institutions to enable hand washing practices are adhered to at critical times. The hand washing facilities will encourage change of attitude and behaviour towards hand washing and hence improved hygiene in general.15. Conduct project monitoring and GBV risk analysis sessions, in the target counties with GBV partners on ground and female and male beneﬁciary members at each project site to enable target population recover from crisis, like displacements and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses and in order to support coping capacities.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

As with all the other past projects implemented by the organization, RCO has always used its expertise and experience with effective and quality programming that recognizes the community’s rights including dignity, capacity, and ability for self-determination. RCO will adopt the same through a consultative approach in the implementation of this project; by including ensuring a two-way communication and dialogue throughout the project cycle. Views and opinions of the affected communities (through floods and conflict displacement) will be incorporated at the start, during the project to ensure relevance and accuracy. Beneficiaries will be dis-aggregated and involved -equal representation - taking into account the different roles and needs of boys, girls, women and men); local leaders among other relevant authorities in decision making as well and also ensuring a meaningful participation in implementation, monitoring and especially evaluating the project. All project activities will be aimed at obtaining full dignity and respect for the rights of the beneficiaries and communities. For example, RCO staff will consult with women and girls particularly, on best locations for distributing dignity kits, and rehabilitating bore holes and latrines.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

RCO understands that the proposed project will be implemented in an emergency context where there are predominant cases of displacements caused by conflicts and currently by floods and will therefore mainstream protection principles into project activities. As the IDPs, returnees and host communities are the primary beneficiaries of this project. RCO will involve affected people right from the assessment in the process of needs identification and activities will be designed to ensure meaningful access to services provided based on needs of the displaced people. To ensure that services are provided to the targeted beneficiaries, RCO will also use people from within the affected people to deliver assistance and implement the project activities. The Do-No Harm principle will be maintained to ensure that project activities do not expose the displaced populations to further harm. Safety and dignity of beneficiaries including the IDPs will be a key priority of the project in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’. RCO will ensure nondiscrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly, the disabled and the marginalize people with special needs. This we be achieved through establishing a complaint mechanism, consultative problem identification and solving through dialogue.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

First the project will have an in-built capacity strengthening component through coordinating, collaborating and networking with other partners working in the same locations these partners will include INGOs, NGOs, CBOs among others. The Government counterparts like RRC, Ministry of Water and local authorities will also provide us with technical advice and will be working closely with our locally recruited staff to be mentored and trained on WASH needs as a sustainability strategy. The project will also train 300 hygiene promoters who will be equipped with skills and knowledge to address Hygiene needs and how to deal with them this hygiene promoters will intern remain in the communities after the project to ensure continuity in hygiene promotion and thus sustainability. RCO will also ensure the project is implemented with involvement of all beneciaries and key stakeholders who will remain in place after the end of the project. The project will not create new or parallel structures which are unsustainable in the long term. The project will endeavour to support sustainable and catalytic interventions strengthening the supply and demand side of WASH needs, thereby stimulating renewed commitment. Water management Committees and hand pump mechanics from the payams and sub payams (Bomas) of the community will be selected and trained on management of water points and simple repairs respectively using the spare parts provided from the core pipeline, this will build a sense of ownership and ensure there is knowledge and skills transfer in WASH interventions and direct community participation being emphasized throughout the project life cycle to ensure that the program responds to the needs of the target community and acceptance by the community thus contributing to sustainability of the project. Coordinating and collaboration with partners in the target area will ensure the smooth transition with partners or communities taking over the project.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

RCO through this project will mainstream protection principles into all activities. The Do-no-Harm principle will be maintained to ensure that project activities do not expose communities to further harm. Safety and dignity of beneficiaries with be a key priority of the project in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’. This we be achieved through establishing a complaint mechanism, consultative problem identification and solving through dialogue. WASH infrastructure and NFIs distributions will be done in safe locations which will be consulted first from the women and girls and then communicated to beneficiaries well in advance and if possible around their households to enable female double up in routine domestic chores and to ensure their protection from risk of sexual and gender based violence when travelling to and operating in far distant locations. Finally, RCO will consult all groups including people with disabilities, women and children, especially girls on the location of WASH infrastructure (latrines, water points) and share relevant information to ensure they are aware of their rights, can claim their rights and lodge complaints when aggrieved. RCO will also work with WASH and protection actors (GBV , Nutrition, CP & GP) to collectively identify actions to reduce GBV and child protection risks and improve accessibility for all people as well as create referral pathway and train our Staff on how to receive, refer and report protection concerns safely and ethically. RCO will continuously identify key protection concerns affecting beneficiaries and report to various Protection/GBV/Nutrition/FTR desks for referrals.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

RCO will ensure that all groups are consulted on their needs and capacities, together and in separate groups, on the basis of gender and age, by same sex facilitators, during the initial assessment, our program team will then ask all groups, together and in separate groups, on how they want to access and design the CFRM as well as how they want to communicate with Us. RCO will then establish a CFBM that include formal and informal mechanism to respond the different literacy levels of the community. The informal mechanism will include community members who have easy access to RCO staffs’ contact numbers for sharing any complaints related to the project. The formal complains shall be gathered through setting up information centers, complaint boxes and mobile teams seeking feedback. RCO will also share the project information with the beneficiaries to ensure they give feedback and make complaints whilst ensuring our staff have suﬃcient knowledge on how to receive, refer and report protection concerns confidentially, safely and ethically and adhering to our policies to ensure Protection from SEA including SOPs, investigation and response mechanisms as well as training the community on what SEA is and how to report it. The organization through our existing SOPs that includes PSEA will then analyze all concerns/complaints from the CFBM raised and respond to them according to the SOPs including consulting with the GBV and Protection actors in the field site on the referral pathway to investigate and respond to the claims raised. During Monitoring the project beneficiaries will be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process and the quality of services to measure their overall satisfaction. Project management will reflect on the information generated to enable on-going program improvement and create evidence base and platform for providing feedback to the project beneficiaries.
