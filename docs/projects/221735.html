Project Summary

YEDA will target 16,958 individuals (10% residents, 42% returnees, 48% IDP) in Pibor County (GPAA) with in-kind assistance and CVA, tailored to the most urgent needs of the flood-affected and conflict-affected population.
In line with the SNFI cluster strategy, YEDA will focus on life-saving interventions, comprising the distribution of emergency shelter material and NFIs to newly displaced persons;
The choice of aid delivery is informed by YEDA’s previous experience, and the need to scale-up CVA as an avenue to facilitate reintegration and self-reliance, while revitalizing the local economy. YEDA has a full operational office and static presence in Pibor town, which allows assistance-delivering to the targeted locations via road, as well as item prepositioning.
Beneficiary targeting will be guided by the SNFI Cluster criteria of multiple vulnerabilities; PLWs, children <5, the elderly, PWN/PWDs (15%), high number of dependents, combined with multiple displacement, lack of ties with the local community/purchasing power, GBV and health risks associated with SNFI needs.
The in-kind response, implemented in locations with no functional markets, will include ES/NFI distribution to 13,958 individuals. For in-kind response, pipeline items will be requested upon approval of SNFI analysis report by the SNFI Cluster coordinators, in areas without access to local market/shelter material. The transportation from Juba to the field warehouse will be covered by the SNFI Cluster core pipeline, while YEDA will cover the last mile transportation costs.
The CVA approach will be used in areas with functional markets, for the procurement and construction of shelter materials and repairs, allowing 500 vulnerable HHs (3,000 individuals; HH@6) to repair their homes, and ensuring appropriate shelter, protection and dignity, and NFI replacement. Since in remote areas framing material is not accessible due to protection concerns, the project will include a small CFW component targeting 100 individuals for training in shelter construction by the YEDA local shelter expert, with secondary benefits for host communities (financial empowerment and conflict risk reduction). Wherever possible, shelter materials will be procured locally, using the same system. HLP and CCCM partners on mobile response will be engaged to minimize conflicts over land and shelters, in areas with high concentration of IDPs and returnees, where there might be potential conflicts with the local community (e.g. occupied shelters/land; conflict between households receiving shelters and those not prioritised).
Financial service providers will be mapped out in the project sites, and distribution facilitated by YEDA staff. Female heads of household and PWDs will be prioritized, to ensure financial empowerment of intended people and GBV risk reduction. In line with this, YEDA will conduct 1 consultation with different actors/stakeholders on Housing, Land and Property with the purpose of supporting recovery and resilience building through interventions that build assets and capacities of affected population to recover from shocks and restore HLP rights, with particular attention to supporting durable solutions.
Coordination with the SNFI cluster coordinators and partners on regular basis, and with other humanitarian partners utilizing humanitarian hubs in Pibor to take part to joint responses, will minimize duplication, and promote integrated interventions, gender and protection mainstreaming, GBV referrals, risk mitigation, and incorporation of health and GBV/PSEA prevention messages. As SFP for GPAA, YEDA will lead humanitarian coordination and refer need/gaps to ICCG/ICWG.

1. Needs

According to the South Sudan projections for 2025, 9.3 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2025, from 2.2 million in 2024, of which 4.2 million will need be in need of SNFI assistance (OCHA, Nov 2024). This is mainly caused by multiple shocks related to conflict, natural disaster (floods) and diseases, coupled with high influx of returnees from Sudan. As of October 2024, flooding has affected over 890,000 people across South Sudan, with about 226,000 flood-displaced people. Additionally, since the beginning of the conflict in Sudan in mid-April 2023, 56 per cent of the South Sudanese returnees received assistance to reach their final destinations, mostly in Upper Nile, Unity, Jonglei/Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) and Central Equatoria. At their final destinations, the conditions of the returnees remain precarious, while arrivals put pressure on already constrained resources. These are compounded by already existing vulnerabilities in host communities, induced by floods and intercommunal violence (OCHA, Humanitarian Update, Feb 2024).In fact, Jonglei/Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) is one of the states where communal violence, floods and food insecurity are recurrent challenges, together with Upper Nile and Unity State. Persistent flooding, endemic insecurity (involving the Dinka, Nuer, and Murle communities) and unstable markets have created a state of emergency, contributing to livelihood disruption (REACH, 2023). Since May, floodwaters have damaged homes, crops and critical infrastructure, disrupting education and healthcare services and heightening the risk of disease outbreaks. Many communities have become isolated, with impassable roads cutting off access to markets, livelihoods and vital humanitarian aid (South Sudan humanitarian snapshot, August 2023). All Relief and Rehabilitation Commission county offices in GPAA have reported displacements due to floods (OCHA South Sudan: Flooding Situation Flash Update, Oct 2024).Shelter remains one of the priority needs, as majority of IDPs and returnees are sleeping under the trees, with increased negative effects on their health, protection and psychosocial wellbeing. Those include lack of privacy and protection risks, particularly for women and girls who normally collect the increasingly scarce construction materials form the bushes, and increased morbidity rates, mainly due to malaria and exposure to extreme weather events, and easy transmission of airborne diseases as a result of congestion (REACH, 2024). Looting, destruction and displacement also create a lack of life-saving NFIs, especially flood kits and basic items that can protect the most vulnerable people from vector-borne diseases and extreme weather.Affected people in GPAA, in particular Pibor County, urgently need access to emergency shelter and NFI materials to prevent further deterioration of their living conditions and of resilience against recurring hazards. For those living outside IDP camps, there are few natural resources available, depleted by multiple displacement and climate-change related disasters, and often inaccessible to most vulnerable categories.At-risk groups include Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), children under 5, the elderly and Persons with Special Needs/With Disabilities (PSN/PWDs), due to their higher vulnerability to environmental hazards related to lack of access to essential SNFIs, inability to collect shelter material, and higher vulnerabilities to protection and GBV risks, especially for women and girls. This justifies YEDA’s targeting criteria of most vulnerable households, considering such groups who cannot afford to access shelter materials and cannot afford to pay community members to collect it (labour rates are average $8/day for unskilled labour and $10 for skilled). Finally, findings from various FGDs revealed that the extent of damage on S/NFI differs, which will guide the cash voucher value.

2. Project activities

YEDA will use cash and in-kind modalities in order to target the most vulnerable individuals (24% women, 53% children, 15% PSNs) with ES/NFI assistance. For in-kind assistance, activities will include;(1) S/NFI analysis, including protection risk analysis (at least 2 S/NFI partners, one GBV partner, and one health partners) and conduction of community consultations (total duration 2 weeks) and sensitization on disaster risk reduction. The analysis will also include analysis of the existing land rights systems in place and risk of conflict over HLP issues, through discussion with local authorities, host community leaders, HLP and S/NFI partners. (2) Community consultations and establishment of CFRM (3) Formation and training of Village Representation Committees (VRCs, including women/youth/PWD representatives, IDP and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs; 50% female). Training will be on their role and responsibilities during activity implementation, including beneficiary selection, distribution, CFRM, and mediation in cases of disputes, especially over land and property, using the appropriate channels.(4) Beneficiary registration and verification, including CFRM. (5) Request and transportation of loose items/kits from S/NFI cluster core pipeline (6) Local procurement of framing material (wooden poles, bamboo bundles) and roofing material (grass), where deemed feasible(7) Secondary transportation and distribution of pipeline items and shelter material** (8) M&E and reporting: Rapid monitoring at the time of distribution, to record feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries concerning the distribution modalities and type/quality of items received, and PDM within 4 weeks after distribution.***(9) Documentation and reporting to the cluster.For the cash modality, activities will include; (1) Community consultation including protection risk and context analysis, establishment of a CFRM and needs/market analysis, including Covid-19 preventive measures* and sensitization on disaster risk reduction.(2) Beneficiary identification (3) Market/Price monitoring – to update the prices due to currency fluctuation (4) Mapping of service providers for CVA (cash voucher modality) (5) Community sensitization on cash modalities (vouchers, CFW) supported by community selected volunteers on source of funding, intended purpose, transfer value, CFMs(6) Provision of conditional (Cash For Work-CFW) and unconditional cash voucher assistance through the selected service provider and then YEDA staff**(7) Training in shelter construction(8) Shelter construction and reinforcement/rehabilitation, combined with CWF modality,(9) M&E; Rapid monitoring after cash distribution, M&E (after 1st instalment) by the M&E staff through household visits and FGDs with beneficiaries and later after a month (final instalment) a post distribution monitoring conducted to measure impact and effectiveness***(10) Documentation and reporting to the cluster (YEDA M&E data flow chart), exit activities (case studies, photos, documentaries). NOTES:* At all stages, the VRC members, volunteers and YEDA staff will all be trained/sensitized about epidemics preventive measures (Covid, EVD, cholera, etc.), also with the support of IEC materials in local languages. Gathering of people will be limited to the essential and any activity that involves that will be conducted following the WHO prevention guidelines for Covid-19, including hand-washing stations**Each distribution will incorporate beneficiary sensitization on disaster risk reduction as well as GBV and PSEA sensitization, in collaboration with GBV/protection and health partners in the project locations.***M&E: YEDA may use third-party monitoring/evaluation, by involving a cluster partner, as well as an external consultant to measure effectiveness, efficiency and impact of the intervention, ensuring transparency and quality reporting
