Project Summary

This project will reduce risks posed by explosive hazards, enabling the affected population to safely access productive land, natural resources and basic services. Additionally, the project aims to provide assistance to landmine/ERW survivors for socioeconomic reintegration. Landmine/ERW contaminated areas will be surveyed and cleared, securing access to 10,000,000 square meters of priority land for safe movement of refugees and IDPs and/or productive use by the local populations. 300,000 vulnerable populations will learn how to adopt safe behaviors in landmine contaminated areas. 250 landmine/ERW survivors and other persons with disabilities (PWD) will be empowered by gaining psychological support, physical mobility and means for livelihood. Through this project, the United Nations Mine Action Service (UNMAS) will support the Government of Sudan to meet its commitment to the Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention (APMBC) by strengthening the institutional capacity of the National Mine Action Center (NMAC). In this endeavor, the project will continue to build the national capacity in mine action by providing training to national partners including NMAC and non-governmental organization (NGO) staff members.

1 - Needs

The presence of landmines and ERW poses a great threat to the population to sustain livelihoods and gain access to basic human needs such as education, water and health services. According to the official records in the Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) more than 207 dangerous areas reported to be contaminated with landmines or ERWs covering 26.4 million square meters of land. The IMSMA also indicates that South Kordofan and Blue Nile states have explosive hazards contamination at the size of 23.4 million m2 and 1.5 million m2 respectively. Considering the current political situation and the ongoing peace talks, opening roads is crucial to protect the expected influx of people between the Government controlled areas and oppositions controlled areas. Nevertheless, the actual level of contamination is not yet fully known; the IMSMA database is based on the general mine action survey conducted in 2013, and most hazards are located in and around the opposition-controlled areas. Since 2017, a huge influx of IDPs was observed in the South Kordofan and Blue Nile states moving from the opposition-controlled areas to government-held regions in search of food and basic services. That trend of displacement continued in 2018 and 2019 without improvement. The danger that IDPs, returnees, host communities and refugees face is imminent as they walk through the contaminated land without knowing the risks of explosive hazards. With limited mobility due to landmines, people are faced with persistent barriers to secure livelihoods, food and other social services that satisfy their basic needs, such as education and health. Deploying mine action in the area will not only remove the existing barriers and increase safe access to basic livelihood for vulnerable population, but also enable timely humanitarian assistance and pave the route for development and also facilitate responses to the affected people's need regardless of their gender. In Darfur, five states were severely affected by civil war, including vast areas of land contaminated with UXO. UNAMID is present in Darfur since 2007 with a mandate on protection of civilians, facilitation of humanitarian assistance, and mediation among the parties to the armed conflict. In accordance with Security Council Resolution 2429, UNAMID is going to withdraw from sectors East, West, South and North, handing over its tasks to the UNCT, including UNMAS Sudan. To fill in the operational gap during the transition, UNMAS Sudan is undergoing a process to extend its current mandate to cover the Darfur states. The highest number of UXO accidents in Sudan was observed in Darfur in the last few years, with increasing number of accidents in recent months, what is believed to be caused by the negative implication of the forced arms collection campaign. Men, boys, women and girls are affected differently by crisis and by ERW. Men and boys make around 88% of the killed and injured in Sudan based on IMSMA system, but, compared to men, injured women and girls are disadvantaged when it comes to assistance: less likely to have access to immediate healthcare, so more likely to die as result of injuries, less likely to have access to information about the kind of assistance and opportunities available for survivals, to find a partner and job opportunities, while more likely to face isolation and stigma.

2 - Activities / Outputs

- Identify and record areas with landmine and ERW contamination through NTS and TS. - Conduct verification and/or clearance of routes suspected to be contaminated by landmines and ERW.- Conduct survey and removal of ERWs through EOD spot tasks.- Release 10 million sqm of land previously contaminated by landmines/ERW through survey and clearance operation.- Conduct risk education (RE) through direct presentations and community-based RE to 100,000 persons in mine/ERW affected communities.- Conduct risk education (RE) through indirect presentations including through radio and TV to 200,000 mine/ERW affected people in the restricted or remote areas.- Print RE material for distribution during RE sessions.- Advocate with relevant authorities and institutions to support and endorse the Sudanese Strategic Framework for Victim Assistance and multi-year work plan to provide direct provision of services to PWD including victims of landmines and ERW.- Provide necessary physical and psychological supports including prosthesis and assertive devices, rehabilitation and counseling to 250 survivors of landmine/ERW accidents and other PWDs.- Deploy survey teams to collect and register victims data particularly including the identification of under-reported cases.- Provide income generation support to address socioeconomic issues of the survivors that include vocational training, macroeconomic workshops and provision of business start-up material.- Promote positive perceptions and greater social awareness towards PWD through awareness campaign and advocacy with government entities.- Coordination and monitoring of MA operations in Sudan.- Regular participation of the Sudan’s delegation in International Meetings and Events.- EOD level 2 and level 3 training.- Provide the required support to strengthen the capacity of NMAC in management and coordination.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNMAS will ensure that mine action activities are carried out in compliance with the National Technical Standards and Guidelines (NTSG) and International Mine Action Standards (IMAS) for what concerns environmental preservation. UNMAS and NMAC will identify and assess relevant environmental aspects and determine appropriate and effective measures to mitigate adverse impacts of explosive detonations on soil, air and immediate surrounding environment. This will include the inclusion of environmental requirements in the selection of implementing partners and their incorporation in SoP, proposals, and quality assurance monitoring plans.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Mine action is basically a protection intervention aiming to protect people lives from the threat of landmines and explosive remnants of war, MA sub sector ensures equal participation, consultation and inclusion of affected populations in the planning and implementation of mine action programmes as well as ensuring that the different exposure to and knowledge of risks related to explosive ordnance among women, girls, boys and men from diverse groups is incorporated into risk reduction efforts, including clearance and risk education. It will also facilitate and support multi-sectorial assistance to victims of explosive ordnance that is responsive to the needs of women, girls, men and boys from diverse groups, and advocate for the mainstreaming of gender and diversity considerations in national mine action policies, institutions and programmes. Additionally mine action implementation partners are encouraged to ensure they “do no harm” to the environment or livelihoods, and avoid increasing the long-term vulnerability of affected communities.. Therefore, this project is considering protection mainstreaming through all phases of the project starting from the project concept throughout project design, implementation and closure of the project.
