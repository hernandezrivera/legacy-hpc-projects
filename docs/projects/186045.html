Project Summary

In this project, IOM seeks to prioritize the provision of life-saving protection services, addressing gender-based violence (GBV), and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) through prevention, response and risk mitigation activities as well as mainstream protection through provision of assistance in a safe, timely, and dignified manner.
1. IOM's MHPSS activities will consist of services to populations informed by assessment of their needs to provide a comprehensive response from a community-based approach, including lay counselling, basic emotional support, life-skills development, language classes, and socio-relational and cultural activities. IOM will ensure that a multi-layered response system is established that integrates affected populations, government and stakeholders to improve access and provision of quality services; community members' knowledge, socio-relational capacities, and practices are strengthened towards peaceful co-existence and community stability; and people with mental health disorders and needs are referred to the appropriate mental health services and facilities. IOM will implement activities using a holistic approach to design preventive and risk mitigation models to respond to the drivers of MHPSS and protection risk factors that are already identified, with the long-term focus on building local capacities.
2. In close partnership with the NAPTIP, IOM will continue to build the capacity of counter-trafficking stakeholders with the intent to localize and increase ownership of the counter-trafficking response in north-east Nigeria. IOM will also seek to improve prevention and response services targeting at-risk sub-groups of the crisis-affected populations and victims of trafficking.
3. To support GBV survivors consenting to access services in the camp and host communities, IOM's Psychosocial Mobile Teams will provide the following array of services: survivors of GBV will be provided with psychological first aids (PFA) (Level 1 and 3 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid) to cover their acute needs; targeted survivors of GBV will be provided with structured psychosocial counselling, either as a stand-alone service or to complement other specialized services (Level 3 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid); With their consent, survivors of GBV will be referred to specialized services for case management and dedicated follow-up (Level 4 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid); Families of GBV survivors will be provided with structured psychosocial counselling and family support sessions (Level 3 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid). In addition, IOM will ensure that safety, confidentiality, respect, and non-discrimination principles are adhered to protect survivors' rights and dignity and promote resilience. IOM will focus on system strengthening to ease GBV survivors' access to medical, legal aid, safety services, and vulnerable individuals' access to services. To mitigate the risk of GBV and other protection risks in camp and host communities, IOM will conduct context specific sensitization and awareness campaigns on GBV and protection threats in the targeted camp and camp-like settings.
4. IOM will jointly work with the IDP safety committees to conduct safety audits and mapping to identify and highlight protection and safety concerns within the camps, disseminate protection-focused messages, and increase awareness of protection risks.
5. IOM will continue to enhance accountability to the affected population by strengthening Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and feedback mechanisms.
6. IOM will build the capacities of humanitarian actors, including IOM staff, as well as community- based structures in the areas of MHPSS, Protection, GBV and counter-trafficking with the overall aim of mainstreaming protection principles in programming and strengthening GBV risk mitigation.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in north-east Nigeria is characterized by deliberate and widespread attacks on civilian populations, including sexual violence; abductions, especially of girls and women; disappearances; child and forced recruitment; forced displacement; and the use of human bombs. Female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children, and adolescent boys and girls are particularly at risk of GBV, child and forced recruitment, and human trafficking, while adolescent and adult males face threats of harassment, arbitrary arrests and detention, and serious maltreatment during detention. Displaced populations face grave protection risks and abuse such as exploitation, family separation, restrictions on freedom of movement, and insecurity. Vulnerabilities of the crisis-affected population are exacerbated by pre-existing, and emergency induced problems faced by the affected population, notably for the more vulnerable sub-groups of the population, including the elderly and chronically ill, persons with disabilities, female and child heading households, unaccompanied and/or separated children, adolescent boys, and girls, pregnant and/or lactating women and people returning from captivity. In addition, the population displaced due to the conflict is facing additional stressors created by the displacement itself. Stress factors include family separation, violence, difficulties and sometimes denial of access to services, limited livelihood opportunities, breakdown of family and community support mechanisms, potential stigmatization, secondary and repeated displacement.Human trafficking by armed groups manifests itself in brutal forms such as the use of children in combat roles, the sexual enslavement of women and children, forced marriage to combatants of Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs), suicide bombers, and other ancillary activities such as cooking, cleaning, and farming. Various data sources reveal that Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities in captivity are being exploited in several forms, including, but not limited to forced labour, forced begging, organ removal, sexual exploitation, and forced marriage. Victims who are released or escape from these trafficking situations suffer from stigma and possible rejection from the community upon their release, as well as insufficient attention, care, and support.Meeting their medical, psychosocial, and other needs is a significant undertaking needing intensified and targeted support. Failure to do so may result in re-trafficking. According to the Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2022, more than 4.2 million men, women, boys, and girls in the north-east will require specialized protection services in 2022. Despite the fact that some IDPs have started to return to their villages of origin, living conditions for conflict-affected populations remain dire, particularly for vulnerable individuals such as women and children who are at high risk of abuse. As the situation evolves, IDPs are being relocated to areas of origin, and there might be increased risk that may predispose women and girls to protection concerns, including GBV. Moreover, for the population who will be able to return to their areas of origin, there will remain many challenges. The conflict's impact continues to affect the physical and mental wellbeing, living conditions, and capacity for resilience and recovery of millions of Nigerians in the BAY states. This is compounded by the fact that the social fabric, including the supporting mechanisms and institutions, has been deteriorated and cannot provide protection to the most vulnerable such as the elderly, women, and children. The programme will continue strengthening the resilience of the most affected population, as well as to build a more durable and sustainable multilayer system of response to promote synergies with the cultural existing resources of communities and stakeholders.

2 - Activities

Activity 1. Establish psychosocial mobile teams engaging local stakeholders in project locations, including hard-to-reach locations in Yobe, Borno Adamawa, Kaduna, Nasarawa, and plateau states. Activity 2. Provide culturally appropriate mental health and psychosocial support activities targeting women, girls, men, and boys across all the four layers of the pyramid of intervention. Activity 3: Strengthen the MHPSS/Protection response coordination among partners and stakeholders to include: developing or integrating existing tools to have pathways working systemsActivity 4: Equip Community members with knowledge and skills through community-based MHPSS/Protection services and activities.Activity 5: Provision of material assistance to vulnerable individuals, including Victims of trafficking, GBV survivors, women, and girls of reproductive age to address their needs, such as livelihood opportunities and enhancing their socio-relational skills to contribute towards reintegration .Activity 6: Strengthens the capacity of social frontline workers (such as MWASD, Ministry of health, NPF, NSCDCs, and NAPTiP) to enhance their responses to the Protection, GBV, and psychosocial needs of the affected population in host communities.Activity 7: Organize orientation sessions and deliver protection-focused messages by increasing awareness of different protection concerns.Activity 8: Identify prioritized areas and conduct research (assessment including safety audits and safety mapping exercises) to adjust the MHPSS and protection response to the relevant needs of the affected population. In coordination with the relevant stakeholders, implement recommendations to improve MHPSS /Protection response. Activity 9: Engage professional networks and institutions in coordination with the government partners to support data-driven research. For example, the American university In Yola, Adamawa, and the University of Maiduguri, Borno, could be partnered in this regardActivity 10: Establish an information tracking system to analyse the determinants, risk factors continuously, and MHPSS / Protection emerging needs of the affected population in the region, in coordination with the sector Activity 11: Create community stakeholders' collaborative use of data, in line with IOM Data Protection Principles, and corresponding approaches for addressing known risk factors. Activity 12: Provide protection training (MHPSS, GBV, PSEA, and Protection) for relevant partners and stakeholders, including local authorities and committees on their roles and responsibilities, on topics such as , how to handle a disclosure, and facilitating safe and confidential referrals of GBV survivors, in Activity 18: Conduct preventive activities such as community dialogues, including dissemination of Protection, focused messages, and holistic community engagement strategies such as Engaging Men through Accountable Practice (EMAP). Improved radio programs, plays or dramas, etc., to address and influence harmful gender social norms, CT, etc.
