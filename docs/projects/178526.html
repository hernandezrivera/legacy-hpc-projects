Project Summary

The objective of the project is to address the basic needs of most vulnerable communities through the provision of NFI kits and shelter rehabilitation grants. Both in-kind and cash modalities will be used in order to ensure a quick and flexible response to immediate needs of IDPs and vulnerable host community members.

4 - Needs

Conflict continues to be the principal driver of humanitarian needs, with the civilian population in many parts of the country, including project locations, exposed to significant protection risks which threaten life, dignity and wellbeing on a daily basis. The humanitarian situation is incredibly complex and constantly evolving, with population movements difficult to predict. Thousands of families in project locations face protracted and multiple displacements. Acute winterization and summarization needs remain high for IDPs and families living in damaged houses, requiring large scale timely response to address their immediate needs. IDPs living in open areas, where displaced people are staying without covered shelter for a period of longer than one week, are likely to face a further layer of vulnerability in the absence of adequate shelter and services. Needs are especially acute during the cold winter and the hot summer months, where people’s survival is directly at risk in the absence of an immediate shelter solution and assistance. The number of IDPs residing in IDP sites has increased by 20% per cent since January 2020 and the severity of shelter needs has increased slightly across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2020 by 4% to 5.88 million people in 2021. Shelter conditions for IDPs remain poor with most households residing in improvised shelters which will need replacement/repair in the next year. Conditions for IDPs living with host communities are no different, most shelters require repair or rehabilitation while services are strained.

5 - Implementation of activities

Both in-kind and cash modalities will be applied in order to ensure a quick and flexible response to immediate needs of vulnerable HHs. This decision is based on various assessments and PDM conducted by DCA, during which beneficiaries expressed strong needs for both in-kind (NFI) and Cash support. The locations selected to receive support will be based on analysis of gaps and needs in coordination with local authorities (HAO and Kumin) and WGs. Specifically, the project is aiming to achieve the following outputs: *Vulnerable HHs have essential needs met through the distribution of NFI kits. The contents of the kits will be agreed with sector WGs and follow Sphere standards. The NFI kits will be distributed in informal settlements and/or urban settings prioritizing vulnerable HHs that fulfil one or more of the following criteria: Recently displaced; Houses hosting IDPs; Large HH size; Elderly or female headed HHs; and HHs caring for one or more PwD. *Vulnerable HHs living in damaged and substandard accommodation will receive cash grants to enable rehabilitation. In line with WG recommendations, MoUs will be signed to ensure beneficiary rights are respected and there is no change in rental conditions for 12 months. DCA will assess the HLP rights of every HH, and in case the beneficiary does not have a valid document, DCA will provide an MoU to state the right to property. A monitoring plan will be developed at the onset of the project, based on the workplan, to capture both the individual efficacy of each activity and the integrated impacts of the project. All data will be collected using tablets equipped with mobile data collection software and fed directly to DCA’s MEAL Coordinator.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Increased risk of intercommunity tension due to varied assistance packages across members. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation: 1. Agree to minimum assistance package across sector; 2. Assess and validate any changes before they occur; 3. Identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. Protection Risk: Housing Land and Property issues (Eviction of tenants after completion of works, confusion between owner and occupant) Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium Mitigation: Shelter Rehabilitation interventions will be done in coordination with the local authorities who will be in charge of verifying the ownership of the shelters in which the rehabilitation works are carried out. Through the signature of an MoU (between the project, local authority, occupant and shelter owner – if applicable), the responsibilities of all parties will be agreed upon before any rehabilitation work begins. The MoU notes that should the occupant of the shelter be a tenant, the owner commits to allowing the tenant to remain rent free for at least 3 months and in the shelter for at least 12 months with no increase in the rent.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members or partner staff members. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints box will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Assumption: Access to project locations remains safely accessible for suppliers and staff. Mitigation measures: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security SOPs (Standard Operating Procedures) and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training and briefing for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place. Should the security and access situation deteriorate, the project will remain flexible and will shift locations to areas where it is safe for staff and beneficiaries. The project will temporarily revert to remote management modality should this be required. Risk: Local authority-imposed COVID-19 curfews in NES prevent project staff from moving and/or working, and/or local authority-imposed curfews in NES/KRI prevent hawala agents from transferring money for distribution and salaries.Assumption: Project locations will remain accessible and DCA will continue to have permission to operate (through modified activities to protect beneficiaries and staff), and Hawalas will be able to continue transferring money. Mitigation measures: DCA staff will follow local authority-imposed curfews and restrictions related to COVID-19. DCA will regularly communicate with hawala agents to ensure supply chains are operational. DCA will communicate with other NGOs and NES Forum should selected hawala agent not be able to transfer funds.
