Project Summary

The proposed HRP 2022 interventions will be implemented in Pibor, Nyirol and Akobo West which have been classified as IPC 4 and 5 facing deteriorating humanitarian conditions, worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. Protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. The proposed intervention will contribute to the HRP 2022 Protection Cluster, General Protection Objectives: Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience; Enhance protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response; and Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas. The main objective of the proposed intervention is to increase safe, equitable and dignified access to essential basic protection services reducing exposure of affected vulnerable populations to protection risks, including GBV in Pibor, Nyirol and Akobo in Jonglei State. To achieve this, the proposed intervention has the following specific objectives: SO1 To undertake effective and timely protection assessments in targeted areas for timely and robust humanitarian and protection decisions, influence and actions; SO2 To strengthen community-based protection mechanisms for risk reduction and promotion of local peace and security for women and youths; SO3 To provide Individual Protection Assistance to address priority protection needs for vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in targeted areas; SO4 To support ‘safe programming’ (protection mainstreaming) including safeguarding in all interventions in all the targeted locations. Targeted population are Host Communities, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and Returnees consisting of women, girls, boys and men. The intervention will be contributing to the Protection Cluster target of 105,320 women, men, girls and boys provided with individual, group, specialized PSS and other related general protection services. This project will be implemented by Oxfam and its local partners. The expected number of people reached through Oxfam intervention is 6,600 (across all locations 60% women, 20% girls, 10% boys, 10% men): Pibor HC-1000, IDPs-800, Returnees-400; Akobo HC-1000, IDPs-800, Returnees-400 and Nyirol HC-1000, IDPs-800, Returnees-400.

2 - Project activities

SO1 To undertake effective and timely protection assessments in targeted areas for timely and robust humanitarian and protection decisions, influence and actions• Activity 1.1. Conduct protection risk and trends assessments in each of the targeted Counties (Akobo West, Pibor and Nyirol) to inform prevention and response programming: Protection risks and trends assessments will be regularly conducted in each of the locations to identify risks and develop risk mitigation plan and actions plan. • Activity 1.2. Mapping of GBV services to assess gaps & establish/strengthen existing services/ coordination in Akobo West, Pibor and Nyirol• Activity 1.3. Strengthen referral mechanisms for specialized services alongside protection and GBV partners: Oxfam will support monthly meetings for Protection and GBV partners in order to strengthen referral coordination and this will include revitalizing and communicating service maps and encouraging 'help seeking behavior' among women, men, girls and boys affected by the risks including GBV. SO2 To strengthen community-based protection mechanisms for risk reduction and promotion of local peace and security for women and youths: • Activity 2.1. Train existing Community Protection Committees (CPCs) on the basics of protection and local advocacy using Oxfam CBS Module: Strengthening Community based protection (of which Oxfam already has established Community Protection Committees (CPCs) in Nyirol, Pibor and Akobo west – and will contribute to 'prevent, mitigate, and respond through enhanced preparedness and risk reduction.• Activity 2.2. Protection sensitization/awareness raising on services, rights & related issues (GBV, early marriages etc: Oxfam has Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials that will be reviewed to ensure that it is context appropriate and in consultation with various stakeholders, key protection messages will be developed.• Activity 2.3: Community dialogues around GBV and other harmful traditional practices: Oxfam will identify and train 30 facilitators (15 females and 15 males) in Pibor, Akobo West and Nyirol conduct community dialogues in various locations around SGBV and other harmful practices. SO3 To provide Individual Protection Assistance to address priority protection needs for vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in targeted areas• Activity 3.1: Identify persons at risk including SGBV survivors and provide them with Cash for individual emergency protection assistance and/or protection NFIs and/or Dignity kits: • Activity 3.2. Capacity building training (Protection and SGBV) of existing service providers including local authorities & traditional community-based structures: Oxfam aims to provide capacity building training and support 180 individuals 60 per county. • Activity 3.3. Establish and Support Safe Spaces for women and children affected by protection related issues and train focal points to manage the safe spaces: Oxfam will establish 3 Safe Spaces (1 per county) for women and girls in each of the targeted locations in Akobo West, Pibor and Nyirol. • SO4. To support ‘safe programming’ (protection mainstreaming) including safeguarding in all interventions in all the targeted locations in Akobo West, Pibor and Nyirol• Activity 4.1. Conduct safe programming and gender mainstreaming trainings for Oxfam staff, partners, service providers, volunteers and local leaders• Activity 4.2. Establish/strengthen community feedback and complaint mechanisms and train community focal point persons in selected locations in Akobo West, Pibor and Nyirol: Oxfam will establish a fully integrated community feedback and complain mechanism in selected field locations. • Activity 4.3. Information dissemination, awareness raising and campaigns on the feedback and complaint mechanisms and include protection messages including PSEA: Information dissemination, awareness raising and campaigns on protection messages including GBV will carried out.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Scaling-up of cash transfer programming (CTP) to respond to the largest protection needs of affected communities. The complexity of the crisis and the resultant needs of women, girls, men, and boys are immense. The Cash Voucher Assistance or transfer programming (CTP) is vital for the rural Women’s Protection and Empowerment (WPE), access to services by GBV survivors as well as other protection cases are life-threatening and require unconditional cash programs in Pibor, Rumbek, and Terekeka. The aim is to use cash transfers as a tool to build women’s resilience towards gender-based violence through meeting their basic needs and targeted protection services

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

To understand local market dynamics, Oxfam conducted a Market assessment in Pibor in January 2022 which consisted of 6 focus group discussions (FGDs) with consumers, 5 FGDs with trade unions, additional informal key informant (KII) interviews with traders, local authorities and humanitarian partners, as well as direct observation. Directly assessed marketplaces include Pibor among others. These locations were chosen because of their importance to the overall market system in the region, and to adequately represent various sub-systems that exist within the region. Findings from the assessment indicated functionality in markets in Pibor centrally accessed by nearby locations

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The findings from complaint and feedback and other sources of information such as exit interviews and post distribution monitoring will be used to inform the project activities and flexibly refined activities to address the concerns. Also, findings may be used for future programming, this is essential to ensure that future programme are designed with understanding of past understanding of needs and challenges

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Oxfam programming follows a non-discriminatory approach in which all people regardless of race, gender, affiliation, and disability status. The programme are designed to reach the most vulnerable groups within the population including PLWDs and others. The CFM mechanisms are robust including face to face interface with staff, words of mouth, written. The complaint and feedback mechanisms will be implemented accordingly with intention to reach all sections of affected population.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Oxfam has integrated complaint and feedback mechanism which includes complaint related to safeguarding. The process of handling safeguarding concerns such as sexual exploitation, harassment and abuse is different from the traditional complaints and feedback on project direct activities. Safeguarding is particularly concern about people and their interaction in their day to day activities with people we work with including beneficiaries, partners, volunteers, government etc. Oxfam has different mechanisms for reporting complaint related to safeguarding including international safeguarding desk, whistleblowing, web based anonymous reporting and trained dedicated safeguarding focal persons at various field locations.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The information will be provided during project inception or awareness stages, at distribution site and through community level meetings. Information on awareness on gender and protection pathways will also be made available to ensure beneficiaries can access such services in case of GBV and protection related concerns. Mechanisms such as help desk and exit and PDM interviews will be conducted to gather feedback from the different sources.
