Project Summary

Samaritan’s Purse has been implementing multisectoral interventions in nutrition, health, and Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) in Mayendit, Mayom, and Maiwut Counties in Unity and Upper Nile States, South Sudan. With over six years of operational presence in these areas, the organization is well-positioned to continue addressing the critical needs of conflict-affected populations. In alignment with the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan, Samaritan’s Purse will scale up these interventions to meet the growing demand for nutrition services and improve resilience and nutrition outcomes among vulnerable populations.
Samaritan’s Purse proposed WASH intervention aims to reach 36,000 conflict- and famine-affected individuals ([M: 18,000 F: 18,000] [Host community: 27,572; Returnees; 4,500; IDPs: 3,748]) in Mayendit, Mayom, and Maiwut counties. The project is designed and will be implemented in collaboration with the County Water Directorate (CWD) and County Health Department (CHD), and other implementing partners in WASH, health and nutrition sectors, to improve access to clean and safe water and sanitation facilities and build resilience in these populations. Additionally, the project will strengthen existing 24 Community Health Club (CHC), 52 water user committees and 52 hand pump mechanics to support the implementation and sustainability of the high-impact WASH interventions effectively. The CHC will work along with Boma Healthcare Workers to align their activities in promoting health and hygiene messaging.
The WASH interventions in the targeted locations, include drilling new boreholes and rehabilitating non-functional existing ones, constructing Institutional, Communal and household (HH) VIP latrines to reduce open defecation and related disease outbreaks. These facilities will be provided particularly in areas with a high concentration of IDPs and returnees, and higher grounds that are less prone to flooding as well as Health and Nutrition facilities, including MMU sites. Additionally, solid waste disposal pits will be constructed and Community Health Club (CHC) approach will be employed to promote good hygiene practices in the proposed locations has done over the past two years of programming. Furthermore, WASH Non-Food Items (NFIs) will be distributed to children under five who are identified as severe acute malnutrition (SAM). Also, communities affected by disasters like floods or conflict-driven displacements will be supported with WASH NFIs.

1. Needs

Despite progress in Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) interventions, significant gaps remain in Mayom, Maiwut, and Mayendit counties. In 2024, Samaritan’s Purse targeted areas show that only 27%, 52%, and 41% of households in Mayom, Maiwut, and Mayendit, respectively, collect water from improved sources. While per capita water usage in Mayendit (18.7 liters) and Mayom (15.64 liters) meets Sphere standards, Maiwut falls drastically short at 7.02 liters per person per day, largely due to limited water points and inadequate water storage containers. Access to functional latrines is also inadequate, with only 34% households in Mayom, 42% in Maiwut, and 26% in Mayendit having access. Open defecation remains prevalent, practiced by 58% in Mayom, 44% in Maiwut, and 30% in Mayendit. Safe solid waste disposal is practiced by only 25% of households in Mayom, 18% in Maiwut, and 9% in Mayendit. Additionally, hygiene knowledge is insufficient; 35% to 71% of households are unaware of critical handwashing times. The WASH situation is further exacerbated by flooding, conflict, displacement, and economic challenges that limit access to sanitation materials and hygiene products. The influx of returnees places additional strain on already limited resources.The WASH needs in South Sudan continues to persist in an aggravating manner due to the increase in the number of conflict-affected populations yearly. The investments by key donors and Samaritan’s Purse in addressing WASH needs in Maiwut, Mayendit and Mayom Counties, the over the years have resulted in some notable progress. However, significant WASH gaps persist; only 52% 41% and 27%, of HHs in the new areas targeted by Samaritan’s Purse for WASH interventions in fiscal year (FY) 2024 in Mayendit, Mayom and Maiwut Counties, respectively, collect water for domestic use from an improved water source. The water uses per capita in Mayendit and Mayom and recorded at 15.64 liters and 13.82 liters, respectively, indicate some progress realized in HH water sufficiency in 2024 compared to 2023. However, the prevailing severe flooding across these counties has impeded access to clean water sources by the affected communities, given that most boreholes in the areas are inundated. In Maiwut County, at 7.02 liters, the water use per capita is way below the recommended Sphere standard of 15 liters/person/day, attributable largely to limited water points and a shortage of water collection and storage containers. In the new areas targeted by Samaritan’s Purse for WASH support in FY 2024 in Mayendit Mayom, and Maiwut Counties, only 42%, 34%, and 26% of HHs, respectively, have access to a functional latrine. The practice of open defecation (OD) is still prevalent among 30% 58% and 44% of HHs in Mayendit, Mayom, and Maiwut and respectively. Moreover, only 9% of HH in Mayendit, 25% in Mayom and 18% in Maiwut practice safe solid waste disposal. Hygiene practices among HHs in the three counties require strengthening, as some 31%, 71%, and 54% of HHs in Mayendit, Mayom, and Maiwut, respectively, still lack knowledge of the most critical handwashing times. The WASH situation in these counties is aggravated by conflict, insecurity and flooding, along with related population displacements; increasing economic difficulties hindering access to sanitation construction materials and hygiene products by the affected populations; and the continued returnees’ influx, which is putting more pressure on the already limited WASH resources. The need to scale up WASH interventions in the four counties remains imperative.

2. Project activities

Samaritan’s Purse plans to implement WASH interventions aim to meet the most urgent WASH needs and enhance the resilience of conflict- and famine-affected populations in South Sudan, particularly IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities, through the provision of potable water, and improved sanitation and hygiene services, integrated with health and nutrition services to improve the health outcome and enhanced nutritional status of targeted vulnerable population. The proposed intervention will be implemented in most vulnerable communities in Mayendit, Mayom and Maiwut through collaborating with County Water Directorate (CWD), CHD and local leaders. Samaritan Purse proposes to drill new boreholes and rehabilitate non-functional existing ones, particularly in areas with a high concentration of IDPs and returnees, and higher grounds that are less prone to flooding as well as Health and Nutrition facilities, including MMU sites without water sources. Samaritan’s Purse will use village drills in Mayom and Mayendit counties to drill and develop new boreholes. The borehole platforms will be raised from the surface ground in order to protect surface water and flood contamination. The borehole drilling and sanitation infrastructures will be carried out considering safety, PWD and gender matters. Water quality monitoring will be conducted for the drilled and rehabilitated boreholes based on the Sphere standards in order to ensure the water is safe for human consumption. Whereas a mechanized drilling rig will be used in Maiwut county as the water table is relatively deeper. being Institutional, Communal and HH VIP latrines will be constructed to reduce open defecation and related disease outbreaks. The institutional latrines will be sited at health/nutrition facilities that lack functional sanitation infrastructures in order to improve access to sanitation facilities by patients and health care workers. Building on gains from the Community Health Club (CHC) approach employed over the past two years of programming, Samaritan’s Purse will scale up this approach in a bid to promote good hygiene practices in the proposed locations. Samaritan’s Purse will engage communities in construction of solid waste disposal pits through collaboration with local leaders and the CHCs, in a bid to promote proper solid waste management in the proposed locations. Communities will be supported with cleaning and waste pit digging materials. The project will construct waste disposal pits at Health and Nutrition facilities, including MMU sites using metallic drums to promote safe disposal of medical wastes. WASH Non-Food Items (NFIs): Samaritan's Purse will work closely with the CHD and Health and Nutrition partners in the proposed locations to intentionally identify children and women enrolled in CMAM programs for support with WASH NFIs. Also, communities affected by disasters like floods or conflict-driven displacements will be supported with WASH NFIs. Samaritan's Purse will carry out Safety Audits and AQA in the operational areas.
