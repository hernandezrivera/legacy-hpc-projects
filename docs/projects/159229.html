Project Summary

The project intends to provide an integrated response to better alleviate critical health and WASH needs of populations affected by the conflict in Eastern Ukraine. First aid assistance and essential WASH services will be provided on EECPs. Population’s access to quality healthcare will be improved through the following:
- Decrease of associated costs (voucher for free access to drugs/medicines);
- Fuel for transportation provided to health facilities;
- Improve quality of care by provision of essential medical supplies;
- Develop health staff education/skills through supportive supervision and capacity building (including MHPSS);
- Improve health education of local population;
- Improvement of WASH-in-Health conditions in HFs.
Morbidity linked to poor water supply systems will be decreased through repairs/rehabilitation of water supply networks.
The project intends to multiply the effects of this combined WASH and Health intervention by increasing the geographical consistency between all its components and working with an integrated approach, based on the needs. The project sites will be in the 0-5km (for GCA and NGCA) and 5-20km (GCA) zones, where needs are critical, but actual referral pathways will be taken into consideration when selecting SHFs to implement the voucher program.
Lastly, the project seeks to both answer emergency needs (by supporting essential services at EECPs, primary healthcare in health facilities and by restoring access to water supply) and support middle/long term solutions. This is to ensure communities and stakeholders have sufficient capacities to cope with the effects of the conflict (capacity building of health staff including when it comes to the health reform, MHPSS, WASH-in-Health, etc…). The activities will be The described needs were already partially covered in 2019, however continuous activities like EECPs intervention and vouchers distribution will have to be re-funded in 2020, in order to allow sufficient time to alleviate the consequences of the protracted conflict and allow communities to build their own resilience mechanisms.
In NGCA, PUI will work with Donbass Development Center, a local NGO that has been partnering with PUI for more than 4 years. DDC has excellent knowledge of the difficulties in the targeted areas and solid capacities to implement the e-voucher program. Additionally, this will facilitate the acceptance of the project by the de-facto authorities, who are often reluctant to let INGOs operate directly.

1 - Needs

Conflict-affected areas of Eastern Ukraine suffer from disruption of basic services. In terms of healthcare, access to services is limited because of real out-of-pocket costs (categories such as single mothers-headed HHs struggle to cover health expenditures) and security, distance to services/isolation of the communities. Issues are manifold: lack of essential medical supplies, disruption of pre-conflict referral pathways, work overload in HFs, lack of professional development of staff, lack of appropriate WASH infrastructure, among others. According to REACH (Protection Assessment, 2019), 50% of HHs reported engaging in negative coping strategies in relation to health such by either not seeking medical assistance or by self-medicating, due to high costs associated with obtaining such services. In addition, female headed HHs were significantly more likely to reduce essential health expenditures as a coping strategy (55%) than were male headed HHs (38%). For HHs within 20km, the costs of medicines was the most frequently reported challenge while for those in isolated settlements physical access was the main issue. Elderly population and those with disabilities were the most affected by both cost and physical access to healthcare. Cost of travel to a facility was reported as a challenge for 55% of HHs in Donetska Oblast. For people with disabilities (PwD), lack of quality of healthcare services was considered a major point of concern. In PUI’s PDM surveys (2019), 31% of respondents in GCA and 35.97% in NGCA reported not having enough money to cover their most basic needs (e.g. costs for food, medicines, clothes etc.). The majority, 86% (GCA) reported various coping strategies, including reduction of the essential health-related expenditures, including medicines purchasing, doctors’ visits etc. (PUI PDM survey). Psychological stress of those living along the contact line were also highlighted due to the fear, instability and uncertainty created by the conflict – 80% reported feeling a periodic or constant threat to their life during the night (REACH, 2019). Elderly were reported to be of particular concern due to their increased loneliness worsened by isolation and immobility (REACH, 2019). Additionally, children have now been subject to many years of cumulative stress, potentially leading to long-term impacts. Despite the high need, only 27% of HHs reported being able to access MHPSS services. In PUI’s 2017 Hard-to-reach settlements assessment, MHPSS care was the most requested form of assistance for all gender and age categories. WASH conditions at health facilities are also impacted by the conflict, with systems in isolated settlements being rarely maintained or checked for water contamination (REACH, 2019). Additionally, 35% of HHs in isolated settlements reported experiencing water shortages, thus increasing the risk for waterborne diseases. In PUI’s HF assessment (2019), the majority of FMAPs and 50% of ambulatories did not have access to drinking water for staff nor patients, or where it was available, its quality could not be confirmed. A need for access to potable drinking water was identified as well as WASH rehabilitation works, in particular for FMAPs. According to the WASH cluster and REACH, many settlements and municipalities continued to highlight water supply as the most pressing need for 2019. Quality of water is of high concern due to the lack of chemicals necessary to treat water and pollution risks, as well as is the capacity of the centralized heating systems to function normally in the winter. Lastly, WASH and health risks continue to exist at EECPs, though local authorities improvement works (both in GCA and NGCA) are ongoing. While conditions have improved, long lines at the EECPs remain a major concern. Health risks affect all gender and age groups among the population, specifically older adults and young children. Concerns are related to harsh weather and conditions of WASH facilities.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: The access to first aid and essential WASH services at EECPs is maintained. A1.1 Provision of first aid services and essential WASH services/infrastructure at EECPs-GCA: Mayorsk, Mariinka, Novotroitske, Hnutove. Government’s current improvement of services at EECPs will be taken into consideration to focus on critical gaps to complement the government or other stakeholders’ plans. The activities will benefit all categories of populations crossing at EECPs without any prioritization by age/gender, as needs are considered critical and affecting everyone, and the proposed activities provide life-saving assistance. Automatic defibrillators will be donated to one of the state services operational at EECPs to enable them to perform life-saving operations out of PUI working hours. A 1.2 Emergency intervention at Zolote EECP (GCA) if opening. A 1.3 Support to the local actors providing first aid services at EECPs-NGCA: Mayorsk, Mariinka, Novotroitske, Hnutove. Already deployed teams of Medical Service of Emergencies Ministry of de-facto authorities will be supported with essential medical and non-medical equipment and medical supplies to provide first aid to the population queuing and prevent harmful health conditions given the existing waiting conditions. Automatic defibrillators will be donated to enable them to perform life-saving operations and support referrals of emergency cases. Outcome 2: Access to and quality of healthcare are improved in conflict-affected areas A 2.1 Distribution of electronic needs-based vouchers-for-free medicines in 4 secondary health facilities and related primary health care centres (2 in GCA and 2 in NGCA) to decrease the costs associated with access to health care. The system will include monitoring of the medicines distributed and the quality of prescriptions. The system will benefit the most vulnerable: CU5, PLWs, people in need of emergency care among all age and gender groups (injuries/trauma, surgery) with specific focus on mother and child health. Individual feedback/monitoring mechanism will be enabled through questionnaires administered to voucher recipients at health facilities plus a hotline for complaints. A2.2 In-kind support to PHCCs for transportation of vulnerable patients in need of referral to SHC-level. The system will be implemented in 2 PHCСs targeted under this intervention (in GCA), taking into consideration actual referral pathways, including referral to SHCs located beyond the 5km-zone in order to maintain the continuum of care and not disrupt access to essential medical care. Beneficiaries will be contacted for phone interviews to assess the quality of the intervention. A 2.3 Capacity building of basic health staff in PHCs (2 ambulatories and 4 FMAPS) and emergency health staff on first aid, quality health practices and healthcare provisions, medical protocols (in link with the health reform) and health education at community level, and MHPSS/mhGAP when applicable. This activity will be provided through training of health staff and supportive supervision from PUI team. Attention will be put on addressing and taking into consideration the needs of different age/gender groups in provision of healthcare and health education. GBV IEC materials will be placed at project sites. A 2.4 Provision of essential drugs, basic medical equipment and consumables to at least 2 ambulatories and 4 FMAPs (GCA and NGCA). A 2.5 Critical WASH-in-Health rehabilitations in 5 FMAPs (4 in GCA and 1 in NGCA). Standard gender & age requirements will be taken into consideration.. Outcome 3 –access to sufficient safe water, and minimal levels of sanitation provision A3.1 – Water supply and technical needs assessment in villages along the contact line. Assessment will reflect the needs of different age and gender groups among the assessed communities. A 3.2 - Repair/maintenance/replacement of pipes, tanks, pumps etc of existing decentralized piped water supply systems
