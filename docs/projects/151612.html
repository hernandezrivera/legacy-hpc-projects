Project Summary

Medair will implement an integrated WASH response in partnership with four local partners: DAWA, SAACID, SomaliAid and Zamzam. The proposed WASH action is a continuation and extension of an ongoing project intervention that currently supports WASH infrastructure in supported primary healthcare facilities, hygiene promotion at household and community levels, and distribution of soap and water treatment tablets. These activities will be implemented in Mogadishu (Karaan, Hawlwadag and Daynille districts), Cadale, Afgooye, Balcad and Kismaayo and Badhaadhe districts of Somalia targeting 16,000 beneficiaries.
In Cadale town Medair will install a small solar water system for the community and WASH installations in the health facility (placenta pits, latrines, septic tanks, soak pits and incinerators). A WASH Management Committee will be established and its members will be trained comprehensively on administration, operation and maintenance to ensure the sustainability of the water system. Community members will also be sensitized, mobilized and fully involved into decision making regarding the project and the selection of the committee members. Through public meetings, decisions about the project, its procedures and mechanisms, will be communicated and explained.
In all other areas, Medair proposes to continue with hygiene promotion through existing community networks. Medair will provide training for 39 health workers and 999Community Health Workers on hygiene promotion topics including hand washing and proper disposal of waste. These, in turn, will deliver this education directly to beneficiaries attending the health facilities and in the community surrounding their own households during the bi-weekly household visits.
Medair has been using the Care Group model to disseminate Behavior Change Communication (BCC) messages in all its urban programming areas with positive outcomes, and proposes to continue the same in this project. Using the Care Groups, information is first collected from the community about cultural ideas around hygiene. Medair will convey messages to build community understanding about disease transmission, importance of safe water, personal hygiene and sanitation, and encourage behavior change to prevent sickness. Topics will include the importance of hand washing with soap or ash at five key moments, personal hygiene and safe disposal of human excreta. The trainings involve practical examples of the hygiene risks and how behavior change can prevent sickness, with particular attention for people's capacities to make these changes. The Care Group model will mainly target women, who are usually those most in charge of hygiene in the household. The men will be reached through community sessions, especially when outbreaks take place.
In the rural areas, hygiene promotion will be done through the networks of the Integrated Community Case Management (iCCM) CHWs.
Medair will continue to address water quality as part of hygiene messaging. Many people collect water from open sources generally unsafe for human consumption. Donkey carts, pots and jerry cans used for collection and storage are potential sources of water contamination. Medair will promote household water treatment, and distribute Aquatabs and jerry cans where people are lacking clean water, mainly during outbreaks. 67mg Aquatabs per 20 liters will be used for non-outbreak conditions and 2x67mg Aquatabs per 20 liters for outbreak areas (AWD/cholera).
Over the years Medair has developed accountability mechanisms that reach across all stakeholders, including but not limited to actual beneficiaries. Complaints, compliments and other propositions are routinely received, reviewed and carefully addressed.
Medair is an active cluster partner at local (sub-cluster), and national level. Medair will continue to actively participate in these meetings, often seeking guidance to determine specific areas of intervention to avoid task overlap and duplication.
Medair has developed an M&E system that will improve and facilitate the process of data collection and analysis from health facilities, from community workers and from the Medair supervisors. Data will be disaggregated by age and sex to allow for more careful analysis of how the program engages these groups. This will help Medair to monitor its progress against project objectives, activities and indicators by utilizing streamlined data collection tools in line with methods already in place at the health facilities and communities. Community input will also be included through community meetings, and assessment of key public health indicators will occur in KPC household surveys. At the end of the project cycle, Medair will carry out an external evaluation with a full review of achievement of indicators, implementation of activities, beneficiary feedback, lessons learned and recommendations for future projects. The purpose of the review will be to determine the outcomes of the project, and the effectiveness and the efficiency in achieving those objectives.

Needs

Somalia received above average Gu rainfall in 2018 which resulted in flash floods and riverine flooding resulting in displacements, and damaging infrastructure and cropland further aggravating the needs of an already vulnerable population. According to FSNAU/FEWSNET, open defecation rates stand at 60 per cent in rural areas and 39 per cent on a national level. The practice of hand washing is often focused on religious rituals rather than effective hygiene to reduce disease transmission, thus diminishing its effectiveness; only 10% of the population has access to a hand washing facility with soap. Open defecation, combined with low hand washing practice has resulted in high prevalence (24%) of diarrhea among children under five s and has also led to a sharp increase in disease outbreaks with a total of 6,464 AWD/cholera cases being reported (since Dec 2017). Medair’s assessments in October 2017 and May 2018, confirmed lack of access to clean water has contributed to increasing episodes of diarrhea. This also contributes to general poor health and nutrition status.There are few or no latrines in the districts hence the threat of outbreaks is still high. Most households in the displaced settlements access water from open shallow wells which have higher risk of contamination. The prevalence of diarrhea was 33.5% and 30.1% of assessed households had soap and water at handwashing stations (Medair’s 2018 KPC survey for the combined districts). The cycle of drought followed by flooding has vastly affected the communities’ capacity to respond to shocks and disasters, the identified WASH needs will need to be addressed in order to reduce WASH-related mortality and morbidity amongst the targeted populations including the IDPs, the disabled and the people who are living in drought affected areas who are vulnerable for AWD/Cholera.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Medair project will target both the IDP and vulnerable host populations and everyone in the community regardless of their age and gender. Medair will aim to provide services as close as possible to the targeted populations in order to minimize movement as well as contribute to the reduction of rural-urban migration. CHWs will relay hygiene promotional messages at the household level hence ensuring all households have been reached. Medair will give beneficiaries the opportunity to evaluate services received, primarily through exit interviews at health facilities. Medair records number of IDPs in the supported locations as well as those reached by services and uses this information to assess whether the displaced have had access to quality WASH services. Though some causes of displacements are beyond Medair’s control, Medair will deploy its ER team to verify and assess the needs of these populations, share the findings with the cluster and possibly respond to the emergency subject to funding.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

In all the supported health facilities, Medair has rehabilitated separate latrines for men and women. The latrines have been fitted with a lockable door, source of light and a handwashing station. Medair’s supported health facilities have also been fitted with ramps to enable the elderly and the disabled easy access to the services. To ensure privacy and confidentiality at the supported health facilities, Medair will ensure that there is privacy by screening of the examination areas and confidentiality in handling and storage of the patient’s information and records. Medair has ensured that most of the staff at the health facilities are female in order to encourage more women to utilize the services. At the Community level, Medair’s CHWs (majority of whom are women), will disseminate hygiene promotional messages at the household level on a bi-weekly basis. Men in these households will also be reached by behavior change communication. CHWs will also help identify households of elderly people, persons with disabilities or other special needs through their visits at households. In consultations with local partners and Community Elders, Medair will select beneficiaries of WASH items and select distribution points that will not pose a risk to the beneficiaries who will be recieving these items. Beneficiaries will be identified and recorded in a register which will be used for accountability purposes. Distributions will be done during the day and the disabled, the elderly and women headed households will be prioritized. Medair will ensure the established WASH Committee in Cadale district includes representatives from all age groups (including school going children) and gender.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Medair recognizes that provision of services that only target specific people in the community can result in conflicts. Medair will target both the IDPs and host population in this project and ensure services are easily accessible. During distributions of WASH supplies, Medair will collaborate with its local partners as well as Community Elders to ensure that only the most vulnerable receive WASH supplies. The Communities will also be notified how the process was carried out and given a platform to air their views. Medair recognizes women and children are among the most vulnerable groups during conflicts. Women will be empowered to bring desired change in their communities through their role as CHWs. The Community networks will provide assistance to women in the community through dissemination of behavior change communication at the household on a bi-weekly basis. To minimize conflicts at work, Medair keenly vets employees and has put measures in place to ensure staff are hired from different clans so as not to be perceived to be favoring certain clans. Most of Medair’s staff are Somalis, who can easily access project locations and carry out supervision visits and on-job trainings.
