Project Summary

According to recent data on the population of Cameroon population1, it is estimated in millions, at about 27.9 inhabitants in 2022 with an average annual rate of change in the population, in percentage, 2020-2025 estimated at 2.5. The population aged 0-14 is approximately 42%, those aged 10-24 is 33%, and between the ages of 15-64, the estimation is 56%.
Each year around the world, 12 million of girls are married before the age of 182. Child marriage disproportionately affects girls and deprives them of their basic rights to health, safety and education. Girls married before the age of 18 are more likely to experience violence from their intimate partner, as well as sexual abuse, physical abuse, psychological pressure and emotional abuse from their in-laws.
The project's goal is to strengthened national capacity for women’s empowerment, prevention and response to GBV, harmful social and gender norms and practices particularly FGM and child marriage, including in humanitarian settings and public health emergencies through; the reinforcement of the capacities of youth-led and women-led organizations to initiate and implement GBV prevention and risk mitigation actions in their communities and improve delivery and access to quality multi-sectoral responses services to vulnerable women and girls including survivors of GBV.
To meet the objectives of this project, IMC aims to; identify and train youth and women led organizations on GBV core concepts, Gender (this will include women’s rights including SRHR, promoting gender equality, women’s empowerment, leadership and participation in decision making), Advocacy and, community mobilization techniques for the elimination of GBV; train law enforcement agencies (FMO) and legal authorities on GBV core concept and their implication in the referral pathway and support local organizations to initiate and lead community engagement for promotion of women’s rights, gender quality and elimination of GBV.
Rehabilitate/construct and equip women and girl’s safe spaces (WGSSs); facilitate group psychosocial support and empowerment activities in WGSS to support the implementation of information sessions, socio recreational activities and skill building activities; support the set-up of VSLA as part of women’s economic empowerment to improve the implication of women in decision making and management of resources; provide case management services for GBV survivors and support GBV coordination meetings for a better coordination of GBV interventions between partners under the lead of MINPROFF/UNFPA.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In Cameroon, women enter their first union earlier than men3. Among women aged 25-49, 13% had already contracted their first union before the age of 15, 36% before the age of 18. By comparison, among men aged 30-49, these percentages are respectively less than 1% and 5%. When at the age at first sexual intercourse, women begin their sexual life at an earlier age than men: among young women aged 15-19, 12% had already had their first sexual intercourse before reaching the age of exact age of 15, compared to 6% among young men in the same age group. Women in the East (15.7 years), Far North (16.2 years) and North (16.2 years) regions begin their sexual life earlier than those in other regions. Additionally, Cameroon has been affected by crises in several neighboring countries, which have led to insecurity and left Cameroonian communities in a state of vulnerability, especially women, children and the deprived, many of whom live in border areas. As of September 31, 2021, Cameroon was host to 467,485 refugees of which 315,031 Central African refugees were hosted in the Eastern, Adamawa and the North regions, including 232,568 off-site and 82,463 in developed sites, in addition to 119,552 Nigerian refugees, and 2,462 from other nationalities. At the epicenter of the crises are women and children, who are disproportionately affected by the loss of livelihoods, a lack of social services, and protection violations. In fact, 52.17% of the total number of refugees are women and girls, while 54% are children. Moreover, 74,262 of the total population of Central African Refugees are Persons with specific needs including 9,311 women at risk. In this climate of insecurity and poor access to basic services, GBV is widespread and is a threat to the health and well-being of women. In the East, Adamawa and North regions, sexual violence and exploitation, particularly against women and young girls, is of particular concern because 259 cases of GBV were recorded in 2021, including 91.12 % of cases recorded in the East and Adamawa regions. It is specifically noted that of these 259 cases of GBV, 30 are cases of rape, 47 physical assault and 119 cases of denial of resources, opportunities and services.Women's participation in community self-management structures remains low. Although there is a slight growing acceptance and tolerance for women's leadership in communities, young adult women aged 18-30 years old still feel excluded. Similarly, women in committees do not most of the time hold leadership positions and there are not opportunities for refugee women to be integrated into the local associations set up by the women of the host community. However, combined with the traditional, religious, social and cultural barriers which are in large part at the origin of protection risks, the drastic reduction in resources is a serious problem faced regarding refugee empowerment needs. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains widespread and one of the main rights violations affecting women and girls in the regions covered due to the crisis as well as the patriarchal system and cultural barriers. Moreover, this situation has been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic due to restrictive measures such as confinement and the closure of schools, leading young girls and women to have reduced access to protective services. Adolescent girls aged 10 to 17 are particularly at risk and can face multiple protection violations simultaneously including GBV, exploitation, and early or forced marriage. The coordination mechanisms for a holistic approach for case management of GBV survivors through security and legal services remains weak.
