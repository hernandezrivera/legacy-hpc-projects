Project Summary

The project goal is to provide community based and protection oriented assistance to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and conflict-affected population, including returnees and persons at risk of arbitrary displacement living in the areas along or in close proximity to Line of Contact (LoC) in the Government Controlled Areas (GCA) of Ukraine.
The project will be implemented through seven offices at Svitlodarsk, Toretsk, Chasiv Yar, Avdiivka, Mariinka, Maiorsk and Volnovakha in Donetska oblast and three offices in Luhanska oblast established in Shchastia, Stanytsia Luhanska and Zolote. Interventions will include:
● advocacy for access to rights at national and regional levels;
● protection monitoring including individual case management, protection counselling, information sharing, referrals, and assistance for implementation of UNHCR cash programme;
● mobilization and empowerment of local communities through community support initiatives; and
● promotion of peaceful coexistence by small-scale infrastructure support for communities with procurement of certain services, materials, and other supplies.
● Social help to residents of line of contact settlements, where the Ukrainian government does not have the capability to fully provide its services
● Psychological support and counseling for residents of line of contact settlements

Needs

At national level, Ukrainian policy, practice and law continue to be in contradiction with international standards. The government’s efforts to address the protection needs of IDPs and the conflict affected persons are of poor quality and often violate the human rights of beneficiaries. The government efforts to regulate issues facings IDPs and the conflict affected resulted in an increase vulnerability. On regional level, there are often discrepancies in implementation of regulations affecting IDPs in different regions. Capacities of regional and local administration is often limited and the level of services provided to communities living close to the contact line is not sufficient. In some of line of contact settlements Ukrainian government does not provide its services, so the need of social help is high.Forced from their homes or living along the line of contact, IDPs and conflict affected people in Ukraine face protection risks. Main concerns are denial of registration, denial of payments, limited access to services, arbitrary deprivation of land, homes and other property, and direct exposure to the effects of armed conflict. According to sex and age analysis of previous project`s cases of Proliska, 44% of PoCs with such needs, are male, 56% are female. Among them 4,5% of age 0-4, 14,5% of age 5-17, 61% of age 18-59 and 20% of age 60+.When Proliska at own expense purchased houses for vulnerable families who lived in the areas along the contact line they willingly moved to safe areas. That detects a serious need in housing in safe locations of people living on the contact line whose lives are constantly in danger due to shelling. 35% of people who had such need were male, 65% were female, 20% of age 0-4, 25% of age 5-17, 35% of age 18-59 and 20% of age 60+, as many of them were single mothers of many children.As consequences of conflict and severe economic crises, many IDPs in Ukraine could not relay on existing traditional support mechanisms such as extended families, friends, neighbours and other networks. This has added to other life struggles they have experienced and contributed to reducing their sense of control over the lives. Communities living close to the line of contact are in very specific situation, marked with security concerns, isolation, lack of the access to basic services, and under constant threat of displacement. Their capacities to organize themselves and to use their own strengths are significantly weaken. 44% of them are male, 56% are female. Among them 4,5% of age 0-4, 14,5% of age 5-17, 61% of age 18-59 and 20% of age 60+.Many of community infrastructures in the areas where Proliska is operating are in bad condition. There was no opportunity to the authorities to rebuild the infrastructure due to frequent conflict escalations. Those living in remote locations with imposed restriction of movement are not properly covered by the government provided services. Due to the protracted nature of the crisis, people’s ability to cope decreased drastically. The ability to access services deteriorated and livelihood opportunities become more limited. Thus, humanitarian actors are still the main source of the protection and assistance provided to conflict-affected population residing in areas close to the contact line. Settlements in this zone are often composed of people of different background: while many are long-term residence, certain number of IDPs and returnees also live there. In some cases host communities have raised concerns that IDP presence is making additional pressure on already limited community resources, services and infrastructure.

Activities & outcomes

It is critical to continue advocating for the rights of beneficiaries vis-à-vis the Ukrainian government, to keep these issues on the radar of humanitarian and of international stakeholders, and to keep advocating for the rights of beneficiaries at the community level. Advocacy interventions will be conducted by Proliska on two levels: national and regional. Often Proliska staff members are the only source of information, humanitarian assistance or social support present in the area along the contact line. Proliska's staff conducts individual case management for vulnerable individuals with protection risks and provides assistance for displaced and other conflict-affected people of concern with the emphasis on people with specific needs. It is expected that the assistance will be get by 44% of PoCs who are male, 56% female. Among them 4,5% of age 0-4, 14,5% of age 5-17, 61% of age 18-59 and 20% of age 60+. 500 PoCs will be reached by individual psychosocial support and counselling. Being present in the communities allows Proliska to trace the population movement dynamics and to identify current protection trends and concerns. Proliska will carry out the protection monitoring and will collect, verify, and analyze information obtain through various sources (field monitoring visits, interviews with PoCs, contacts with local authorities, etc.) in order to identify human rights violations, protection risks encountered by IDPs and other affected populations.Immediate response will include individual protection counselling (individual consultations), sharing of information (group consultations), as well as referrals to appropriate service providers. Besides, PoCs will be provided by direct primary legal aid.In the cases of emergency (direct damages of houses caused by shelling, forced displacement, etc.), Proliska will assist affected populations with the UNHCR provided non-food items and shelter materials for acute emergency repair. Purchasing houses in safe locations will allow to move and to avoid the risk of life vulnerable categories of people who can`t take care about themselves. As many of them are single mothers of many children 35% of people who will get such help will be male, 65% female, 20% of age 0-4, 25% of age 5-17, 35% of age 18-59 and 20% of age 60+. Proliska is aware of the legal issues related to the tenure of the purchased houses, and commits to consult with other partners and working groups with specific expertise. Proliska is also aware of the sensitivity of this activity and of the importance of a proper selection of the beneficiaries, based also on the do-no-harm principle.Both UNHCR and Proliska will identify and measure trends and changes in the protection situation over time, and will have the information necessary to inform specific protection interventions. IDP access to appropriate services will be increased. Through implementation of community support initiatives communities living in the areas close to the line of contact will be supported to develop knowledge and capacity to identify their concerns and design response to these concerns, peaceful co-existence projects will support communities who established self-management structures, who are able to properly articulate their needs and who can establish connections with local authorities and other relevant organization in their area. 44% of them are expected to be male, 56% female. Among them 4,5% of age 0-4, 14,5% of age 5-17, 61% of age 18-59 and 20% of age 60+. As a result, the quality and availability of local social/ community infrastructure will be improved.As AAP mechanisms Proliska will use complaint boxes situated in each headquarters, the keys of which will have only the head of the organization who will regularly personally deal with the complaints, also Proliska will have “hot-line” and a newspaper in each edition of which there will be all the contact information for people to be able to give some feed-back.
