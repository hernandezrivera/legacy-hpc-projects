Project Summary

As foreseen by the priorities of the HRP and identified gaps and needs in the selected areas, the project aims at increasing access to basic services as well as improving livelihood opportunities and resilience of vulnerable communities in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor. All areas fall under Severity Scale 3-5, with the exception of two SS 2 sub-districts that have been deemed priority nonetheless in complementarity with other ongoing interventions.
In line with the Obj.1 of the ERL HRP and the priorities identified with local authorities on the ground, WW-GVC will focus on access to basic services where infrastructure has been severely affected by the conflict and is still in need of comprehensive intervention to regain functionality. The main focus will include educational facilities as well as water network and pumping stations. Equal access to services has been deemed essential to enhance and ensure the impact of livelihoods programming, to avoid that vulnerable communities resort to expensive private services or negative coping mechanisms, as well as to increase community cohesion and to reduce tensions in areas where divisions and conflicts still affect the road to recovery. Kindly note that activities related to rehabilitation of basic services are not included in indicators and budget as they are already included in HRPs for other Sectors.
Furthermore, in line with the Obj. 2 of the ERL HRP, the Action will promote income generating opportunities for vulnerable HHs. In effort to ensure an integrated approach with our Education Action, WWGVC will prioritize HHs and communities with a high number of OoSC or children at risk of dropping out. Since economic reasons (child labour) are the first cause of non-attendance (HNO 2023, MSNA), the support to the creation and improvement of income generating opportunities for the HHs with OoSC is favoured to directly improve the enrolment and retention of children in formal or non formal learning pathways. Within the Action, beneficiaries will receive training and will be provided with knowledge and tools as well as the opportunity to take part in an internship or a short-term job opportunity to gain first-hand experience. This is to favour the re-insertion in the job market, boosting the local economy and increasing the sustainability of the action. At the same time, short-term job opportunities will be created in fields benefitting the Education system (eg. producing PPE for students, small renovations of NFE centres, building furniture for schools, etc). This will generate income for the HHs, increase skills in useful fields, while at the same time increasing safety and protection of learning spaces and benefitting the community as a whole.
Beneficiaries will be identified through a two-step selection process that aims at evaluating the need and vulnerability as well as the motivation and the interest in undertaking vocational training and actively seeking employment or self-employment opportunities. The starting point for the selection will be the database of HHs with OoSC that WWGVC has built through multiple years of campaigning and raising awareness on school enrolment and retention in vulnerable communities. Vocational trainings will be implemented under the umbrella of the DoE, lasting between 2 and 6 months, and covering topics chosen on the basis of a market study and FGDs/KIIs at community level. Trainees will be provided with individual professional kits to allow them to seek self-employment if they wish.
In an effort to implement the Nexus approach, linking the provision of assistance and rehabilitation works to the development of sustainable and resilient solutions, the Action is therefore aiming to target both basic services and the promotion of income-generating opportunities, paving the way for a longer-term strategy that puts the focus back on the agency and the strengths of the communities.

3 - Needs

The economic crisis, coupled with the health emergencies such as COVID-19 and Cholera/AWD, brought a worsening in the availability of services as well as a decrease of the ability of HHs to cover basic needs with the income (HNO 2023). In GoS, an estimated 77% of the population reported that their income is not sufficient to cover basic needs, even though 49% report more than 1 HH member working. Basic infrastructure has been damaged or destroyed during the conflict (eg. in DeZ city around 75% is in need of rehabilitation). As highlighted during KIIs, the population faces significant difficulties to access electricity and other services, as 73% of HH have electricity for less than 1 third of the day. The increasing number of IDPs and returnees (16% of the population, as Aug21) will put pressure on the system, which could lead to conflicts without an effort to restore services for the population as a whole. In both Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor, the economic crisis has led to an increase in CP risks, such as child labour. Specifically, according to the Education HNO, economic reasons are the 1st cause for children to be out of school, with families citing that they cannot afford school-related expenses as well as basic necessities and they need for children to work. Data collected by WWGVC during the BTL campaigns confirms this and found a high number of OoSC in the areas (1500+ in Ashara, 2000+ in rural Aleppo).

4 - Implementation of activities

Quality programming, sound management, monitoring, and evaluation mechanism are essential throughout the program cycle. WWGVC’s MEAL Department is structured to allow continuous and regular control on the implementation of monitoring activities at the field level as well as a quality check on the monitoring of indicators. In particular, the MEAL team is composed of a Manager and a MEAL Officer based in Damascus, and two MEAL Officers based in each targeted governorate. Provided that the required authorizations are granted, the MEAL Manager will perform regular field visits to ensure the correct implementation of monitoring activities. The MEAL Manager will also be in charge of conducting initial training to field officers on data collection and data entry. The MEAL system will ensure: -Routine M&E activities based on a systematic reporting activity and implementation of the M&E plan. -Development of data collection tools, establishing information management mechanisms leading to efficient use of data and informative reports aiming at strengthening internal decision-making process, external and internal accountability, including the management of the database of OoSC which will serve as a starting point for the selection of beneficiaries. -Inclusion of qualitative information and data triangulation to monitor indicators, and the achievement of outcome and results. -Monitoring and coordination with the DoE’s and/or SARC’s volunteers for activities directly implemented by them.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WWGVC has a child safeguarding policy aimed at ensuring that children’s rights are recognized and respected and that children are protected from any form of violence. The policy contains a set of standards that all WWGVC staff must take into consideration, including: • placing children’s safety well-being above any other considerations • reporting any concerns cases of suspected child abuse or concerns about the welfare of a minor • planning and organizing project activities whilst minimizing risks for children • avoiding culturally inappropriate physical contact with children WWGVC is committed to create and maintain an environment where children’s well-being is promoted in every aspect of its operations. Preventions measures include: • AWARENESS RAISING: All new staff is introduced to the Child Safeguarding Policy. • TRAINING: WW-GVC commits itself to conduct periodical training in relation to this Policy to adequately inform the staff, raise their awareness and enhance their capacity in preventing, and dealing with possible events. • RISK ASSESSMENT: WWGVC endeavors to reduce these risks to a bare minimum. Regular risk assessments are carried out in order to identify, evaluate and mitigate risks throughout the organization’s operations. • SAFE PROGRAMMING: WWGVC takes proactive measures at every stage of project implementation to avoid causing harm inadvertently whilst mainstreaming best practices.
