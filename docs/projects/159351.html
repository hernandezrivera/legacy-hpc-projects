Project Summary

This is an urgent emergency response to reinforce the capacities, resilience and protection of vulnerable Palestinian communities in Area C, H2 and East Jerusalem from natural and manmade hazards. The action includes the rehabilitation and upgrading of shelters and provision of capacity building training. An intervention that will will reduce the exposure of targeted beneficiaries from both man-made/natural disasters, weather conditions, risk of water borne diseases and health problems, by enabling them to live with adequate standards, and improve their preparedness. The action aims to mitigate the threats against communities and support resilience building to protracted threats and vulnerability while responding to their needs in terms of shelter and protection, and indirectly WASH. Providing adequate shelter will contribute to mitigate the effects, vulnerabilities and risks associated with the coercive environment, especially for women, children, elderly and PWDs. Shelter rehabilitation and upgrading activities, coupled with the implementation of the community participation methodology will allow the fulfillment of the objectives of empowering communities in terms of coping capacities, ownership and expertise as well as, enhancing their resilience and capacities to respond to protracted crisis and preparedness. The action is fully in line with 2019 HRP Strategic Objective 2/3, complies with the minimum shelter cluster standards for adequate housing, gender, and adequate access to housing by PWD. This integrated approach ensures that communities are sufficiently protected and equipped with both tangible and intangible assets, in order to prevent their forcible transfer.
The beneficiaries of this action are among the most vulnerable households in terms of socio-economic and protection vulnerabilities which are connected to the physical and political-related characteristics of Area C and East Jerusalem. Families with PWD who needs shelter/WASH facilities adaptation and families who suffers settlers violence in H2 area. Residents of these communities are highly affected by settler and IF violence, lack of access to basic services, inadequate shelter, and movement restrictions among many other hindrances. The action will target 25 communities in six governorates. WW-GVC is present in 23 of these communities as focal point within the WBPC and MAAN has been actively working in the rest of the communities in shelter rehabilitation. Shelter rehabilitation will be done through different techniques (rehabilitation of concrete shelters with composite roof, rehabilitation of concrete shelter with asphalted roof with internal renovation works; provision of PVC cover and rehabilitation of composite shelters). In addition, the partners will conduct capacity building to the communities on self-implemented house renovation techniques to improve the shelter conditions. The partners will support the communities who suffer from inadequate shelters, with activities and sessions to contribute to self- recovery, reduce the risk of GBV and other protection-related risks, privacy inside shelters, WASH facilities and hygiene, and PWD minimum living standards. These high-priority interventions will be implemented in coordination with the PA bodies. The action is part of a multi-year action which aims at covering the total assessed needs in three years. The partnership will capitalize on the partners’ presence and experience in shelter rehabilitation as well as outreach capacity to the communities. The action responds to communities’ needs and looks beyond the mere material needs to the broader issues of safety and dignity The action aims to support gender and age responsive protection as means to meet specific material and safety needs, ensure gender and age assistance and equitable access to humanitarian services, prevent and mitigate potential negative effects of the action on different gender and age groups and ensure adequate participation.

1 - Needs

As part of the Community-based Protection Approach (CPA), in the framework of the ECHO West Bank Protection Consortium, WW-GVC has been collecting quantitative and qualitative data in the targeted communities since 2015 to collect protection related information on vulnerabilities, threats, capacities, and coping strategies. The protection analysis focuses on community engagement. The last round of assessments carried out in February 2019, highlighted the persisting difficult environment, continuously challenging protection threats, restriction of movements, and restricted access to basic service (including shelter). WW-GVC has participated in the OCHA multi- sectorial assessment in H2 in June 2018, and the shelter assessment led by the Shelter Cluster completed in May 2018. This data, along with the joint assessment carried out by WW-GVC and MAAN during the proposal elaboration, highlighted the following issues: presence of old shelters in urgent need of rehabilitation, including severe electrical damage, persistent dampness, leaking and caving roofing, and many other critical needs; incapacity of the shelters to guarantee proper summer insulation and proper hygiene conditions; need for installation of doors and windows to prevent the entrance of animals, as well as to mitigate the harassment from Israeli Forces and settlers; need for privacy (especially for women who suffer from lack of privacy in their rooms and in the WASH facilities inside their homes and for boys and girls to facilitate their homework); unhealthy and unhygienic WASH conditions due to old sewage systems in their shelters; proliferation of bacteria and presence of snakes, scorpions, and centipedes into the shelter; prevention of work on the site by Israeli DCL citing security reasons. The assessments shows that the inability of the community to improve structures is due mainly to economic deprivation. Data analyses conducted by WW-GVC show that specific groups are extremely vulnerable and exposed to these risks, particularly new couples, households lead by women, and pregnant women, as well as children, elders and PWD. Women represent a vulnerable group, given the patriarchal culture that stresses women’s role in the domestic sphere, they specifically suffer from improper and unsafe living conditions and they are more vulnerable to poverty and diseases. The same applies for children, elders and PWDs. During the design of the project the different needs of men, women, boys and girls collected though the above assessments and related with the rehabilitation of the shelters have been taken into account. The identified activities will be further appraised through technical assessments and community consultations in line with the applicable humanitarian standards and the parameters of the Shelter Cluster, also factoring social acceptance factors to insure gender and age-appropriate solutions. Furthermore, social teams will study the situation of the shelters with all family members specially women and girls to define the main problems related to health, hygiene and women/girls privacy, and transfer this information to the technical staff, to define the suitable and accepted solution with the family members and access to PWD. These will be taken into consideration in the final design, drawings and BoQ to make sure to adapt them to the indications of the end-users. Women will be involved in designing the rehabilitation of the houses, based on the needs they expressed through the brainstorming meeting with field staff. The project will place particular attention on the vulnerable families (families with people with disabilities and families headed by women) as these are spending a great deal of time at home, according to the social customs and tradition of the selected communities

2 - Activities

The action will entail the provision of winterization assistance to vulnerable households at risk of exposure or affected by natural or manmade hazards (e.g. winter storms, flooding), Shelter upgrading through providing cash or materials assistance and technical training, shelter and WACH facilities adaptation for PWDs, and protective assistance to shelters affected by settlers violence; Community capacity building and awareness raising; Technical assessment will be conducted prior to the intervention to design interventions and formulate the MOUs, specifying the materials delivered for each HH and labor work allocated for each HH.Based on the Community Implementation’s methodology, rehabilitation/upgrading will be completed by the beneficiaries themselves, whom will establish local project committees, one in each community committees (at least 30% of the members are women), that will be in charge of the implementation of the activities. MOU signature and handover is foreseen with the local committees, which assume the responsibility of the implementation of the work. Rehabilitation of shelters, will guarantee proper ventilation, decreased water leakages, improved hygienic conditions, and privacy, in compliance with shelter cluster standards. Households whose currently shelters do not comply with minimum shelter cluster standards and shelter minimum standards will benefit from renovation activities which will include proper tiling, plastering and painting of the shelters, ensuring proper accessibility for people with or without disabilities, adequate insulation, ventilation, prevention of rain-water leakages, improvement of the WASH connections and facilities, replacement of doors and windows. The renovation work will include also, when needed, WASH infrastructure in the shelters, with improved health conditions and the water, sanitation and hygiene management and preparedness for the hot and cold temperature (e.g. through summer/winter isolation when relevant). The partners will provide technical support and supervision to the shelter upgrading through weekly field visits conducted by WW-GVC, MAAN to monitor the smooth implementation of the Intervention.Constant coordination with the Shelter Cluster, and stakeholder: Governors offices (based on signed coordination MOU) and Hebron Rehabilitation Committee (HRC) Palestinian DCO, HRC, OCHA, ICRC and OHCHR will be ensured. The specific technique selected for each community depends on several factors (among other, the status of land ownership) and include: the rehabilitation of concrete shelters with composite roof. They are comprised of steel framing covered with metal sheets, insulated, and include a wooden floor framework treated with fireproof material; rehabilitation of concrete shelter with asphalted roof with internal renovation works; provision of PVC plastic cover (supporting the structure of metal bars that the community members already own). WW-GVC and MAAN will conduct capacity building to the community members on self- implemented house renovation and techniques to improve the shelter conditions, including protection from natural hazards and climate, and to ensure privacy and safety. GVC and MAAN will support the targeted communities who suffer from inadequate and overcrowded shelters, with activities and sessions to contribute to self- recovery, to reduce the risk of GBV and other protection-related risk, and to sensitize the communities about privacy inside shelters, WASH facilities hygiene and privacy, PWD minimum living standards (2 sessions per community). The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming targeting vulnerable families through a response to their immediate needs, while increasing their protection and resilience to manmade and natural shocks, integrating risk reduction and coping strategies to improve the provided emergency assistance and reduce the beneficiaries’ exposure to forced displacement.

3 - Indicators

The purpose of this action is to address, by assisting families in restoring their shelters,the communities’ humanitarian and protection needs, while improving their living andhealth conditions and contribute to safety and dignity. Specifically, the action will lead tothe achievement of the following indicators: 100% of assisted HH living in upgraded unitin accordance with minimum standards; 100 % of assisted families reported improvementin living conditions; 190 household / 1326 individuals drop in emergency caseload due toprovision of durable climate protection. As part of the monitoring system, specificassessments are designed to capture indicators related to the shelter sector, which will becollected giving consideration of the impact on women, children, people with disabilitiesand the elderly. The interventions are guided by protection analyses. Context-adaptedresponses and accountability to affected populations (including gender-age sensitivity)are ensured thanks to active beneficiary engagement throughout all stages of the projectcycle, starting with participatory protection assessments and response planning. Thecommunity protection approach is specifically designed to support adequate beneficiaryparticipation and to involve groups traditionally excluded from decision making (women,children, PWDs). Specifically, the achievement of the indicators will be measured asfollows: A technical assessment is conducted prior to the technical implementation toinvestigate thoroughly each of the community’s protection, through focus groupsconducted with men and women, girls, boys, children, elders with and without disabilities(separated for males and females) to evaluate indicators (such as ventilation, number ofrooms, separation between girls and boys, size and reason of seasonal movements)related to shelters conditions as baseline After the completion of the intervention, anoverall evaluation will be conducted, including quantitative data collection (Post Specificsurveys) and qualitative analysis through separated focus groups (women/girls andmen/boys) focusing on Displacements / Seasonal displacements / Family Separation /Safety / Dignity and Integrity. Finally, a feedback mechanism is assured through concretecommunity engagement in decision making related to intervention’s design andcommunity implementation, which enhances the ownership of the activity by thecommunity. The action responds to communities’ needs and looks beyond the merematerial needs to the broader issues of personal safety, dignity and protection. The newshelters will ensure: an improvement of the privacy of all family members, especiallywomen and girls; good ventilation and subsequent improved health conditions of allfamily members especially children and elderlies during cold and hotseasons; increased security and safety of all family members, especially of women andgirls who will be able to carry out their duties in a safe place. The proposed shelterintervention will significantly decrease the risk of displacement for the most vulnerablePalestinian populations, especially women and children. Reduced seasonal displacementwill provide protection for vulnerable families (families with people with disabilities andfamilies headed by women) as these are the beneficiary families have members spendinga great deal of time at home, according to the social customs and tradition of the selectedcommunities. Improved shelter standards will provide environments with improved WASHstandards, allowing for pregnant women to spend more time at home before and aftertheir delivery, decreasing separation from their families and community. The separation ofspaces for boys and girls will allow increased sense of privacy and dignity for men andwomen. In compliance with shelter cluster standards the shelter rehabilitated will provide betterlighting system improving the life of youth in the community.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Through Community Protection Approach all members of the community will be involved throughout all the project phases. A thorough assessment will be conducted with each community to further investigate and understand the needs, risks and vulnerabilities of the diverse social groups, assuring the compliance with the Do No Harm principle, so that the renovation works are based on the preferences and needs expressed by the members of the HHs, considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women and men of all ages and with or without disabilities. Additionally, interventions in the shelters will be designed to comply with protection principles, such as meaningful access and accountability towards beneficiaries, taking into account the diverse needs, roles and dynamics of women and men, age and disability status, in each community. Furthermore, household members will work in their shelter rehabilitation, so the sense of dignity and ownership will be improved.the field team participated in the protection cluster GBV training for improving their capacities in detecting and referring cases. moreover, the field team also participated in a training with War Child to better detect and refer children violence. Complying with the “Do No Harm” principles, communication between the PA’s legal counterparts and NRC, ensures that legal awareness sessions are implemented to strengthen the target communities’ knowledge of their rights, risks, and procedures to be followed.WW-GVC is now mainstreaming the AGD and PWD toolkits in the community based protection methodology.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

WW-GVC is adapting the Community Based Protection methodology "CPA" which is based on WW-GVC operational interpretation of the IASC definition of protection. An environment fully conducive to protection is obtained when multi-sector humanitarian and development needs of individuals and communities are met in a dignified and safe manner. As such, multi-sector needs are identified and ranked on the basis of the contextual protection risks of communities and individuals. A right-based analysis is used as entry point to elaborate local protection strategies to be used as programming framework for the definition of needs-based responses.The CPA has been developed according to IHL, Humanitarian, do not harm and the following fundamental principles 1. the safety of affected population (both of groups and individuals); 2. restore the dignity of affected population putting a stop to emerging or established patterns of violations (and/or alleviating their immediate effects)3.Place the empowerment of communities at risk at the core of any action to enable self-protection and realize their rights. The use of focus groups, random interviews and transect walks, ensures the involvement of the population in terms of location, age, gender, ethnicity, diversity, profession and power. According to the access to the population, the level of trust built and other do not harm considerations, one or multiple specific techniques apply to get deeper insight (bias reduction and/or data gap filling) of a particular group, or of a particular information and/or of particular household.Throughout the assessment and planning phases, the needs of all community members will be taken into consideration. The proposed shelter and protection intervention address the urgent need of the targeted communities, accounting for non-discriminatory practices and considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. Providing a voice for each member of the community and requesting their direct action in the project’s implementation, it preserves the dignity of each beneficiary and fosters community empowermentThe distribution of the CRM cards in the communities ensures the accessibility to all beneficiaries to provide their feedbacks and complaints about the implemented project.
