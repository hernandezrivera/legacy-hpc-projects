Project Summary

To provide safe and child friendly environment for the affected children (returnees and stayees) who are unable to attend schools due to the damages of school infrastructure, the project will establish 5 pre-fabricated structure classrooms having all necessary education facilities including solar system and WASH facilities at damaged school site. Project will also carry out minor repair in the selected schools. Each pre-fabricated structure will have two classrooms along with verandas and washroom which will accommodate at least 86 students per classroom. Space for the pre-fabricated structure classrooms will be identified/finalized in consultation with DEO and TIJs members.
In order to control drop out and maintain retention in school, project will provide cash stipend to 70% students especially girls of the selected 60 schools upon 85% attendance. Criteria for the cash stipend will be devised in close consultation with the education department.
The proposed project will provide educational learning materials to 60 schools (55 girls and 45 boys). Thus, the support will cover 4730 students (2,129 boys and 2,602 girls) in functional schools. The project will support 45% boys and 55% girls. Similarly, the project will also provide standard school-in-a-box (Teaching materials) to all the targeted schools (2 in each schools).
The proposed project will provide training on child friendly teaching, psycho-social support, mine-risk and DRR. The training program will focus on development of teacher’s knowledge and skills to best use various tools for creating of CFS, inculcating life skill and DRR in students and providing effective psychosocial support services to the students. The project will organize 5 days 5 training workshops for 120 (55% female and 45% male) teachers. Prior to the training workshops, approval from District Education Office will be granted. The training will be planned in such manners that student’s educational activities are not affected.
In order to strengthen the capacity of Taleemi Islahi Jirgas (TIJs), HOPE will conduct training to the 60 targeted schools (55% girls and 45% boys). HOPE will conduct 2 days’ workshops (22 workshops) to 480 (216 males and 264 female) members on various topics. These topics will be selected in consultation with District Education Office according to the needs of the TIJs.

Needs

As per UNHCR fact sheet, October 2018, in 2017-18, some 60,121 families have returned to their areas of origin in five tribal districts of KP. This translates into a population of 162,326 children in age-bracket of 3-18. As per Primarily findings FATA Vulnerability Assessment 2017 report the erstwhile Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) Education Census Report (2015-2016) states that 4,949 (2,930 boys; 2,019 girls) out of 5,994 government educational institutions are functional in FATA. Of the 4,949 functioning institutions, 3,842 (2,219 boys; 1,623 girls) are primary schools. This indicates an apparent under-supply of secondary and higher secondary educational institutions in FATA. The Rehabilitation and Reconstruction Unit, FATA Secretariat, reports that currently 868 educational institutions are damaged (209 completely and 659 partially) in the five IDP returning tribal districts (Khyber, Orakzai, Kurram, NWA and SWA). The overall dropout rate from Nursery (Kachi) to Class-5 in government primary schools over a cycle of 6 years is 68% in which 64% is of boys and 74% of girls. Some 500,000 children in the 4-9 age group are reportedly out of school in FATA. The fresh return to these tribal districts which put further pressure on existing facilities and thus require additional immediate resources to provide opportunities for all school aged children in their areas of origin. The FATA Vulnerability Assessment exercise, shows that primary schools are available in many areas, with 87% of boys and 69% girls having access to them. Around half of the respondents were dissatisfied with the quality of education due to lack of basic facilities, lack of teachers, teaching materials and damage to school infrastructure. While a small improvement in enrollment was observed compared to 2016, approximately half of girls and a one third of boys are currently out of school. Key factors preventing attendance at schools include fear of an unpredictable security situation, the distances to schools and lack of affordable transportation (specifically for girls) and children’s involvement in household chores and other work. Despite many challenges, families in FATA tend to prioritize the need for children’s education above many other competing priorities. Direct observations on the ground indicates that when schools are accessible and furnished with teachers and basic equipment, the demand for education is high.The available data indicates the need to increase girls’ access to education in Tribal District North Waziristan. This will require a good understanding of the various barriers to their access including, but not limited to, construction of temporary and permanent schools with adequate security and basic WASH facilities, ensuring the regular attendance of teachers, supplying minimum school equipment and assisting with transportation and other costs. Parents and teachers may benefit from sensitization and training on the psycho-social impact of displacement to develop protective teaching, learning and caring skills.
