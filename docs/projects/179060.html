Project Summary

AHF through this project will Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health services for the most vulnerable and in need; strengthen health sector capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver a timely response to disease outbreaks, including COVID-19 and strengthening health system capacity to support continuity of care.
Through the proposed intervention AHF will support its facilities on different levels to continue the provision of PHC, SHC and THC services through its facilities as follows:
- PHC services through: Kafr Bonni PHC with attached mobile clinic, Sijjo PHC, Leen PHC and Atareb PHC, with EPI supervision project, the estimated number of beneficiaries from PHC services is 199,745.
- SHC through Sarmada Children's Hospital in Sarmada, Alsham pediatric hospital in Sijjo, Alsham surgical hospital in Tal Elkarameh and Alsham ambulance system in many locations across Idleb and Aleppo, the estimated number of beneficiaries from SHC services is 193,835.
- In addition provide specialized health services includes rehabilitation services to the people with disabilities with in 2 centres in Tal Elkarameh and Sarmada, and 1 Dental mobile clinic in A'zaz, the estimated number of beneficiaries from the specialized health services is 12,580.
- Covid-19 response will be maintained through Alsham Isolation hospital in Dana, our 2 CCTCs in Maar tmisrin and Harim, and our emergency COVID-19 ambulances, the estimated number of beneficiaries from AHF's Covid-19 response is 8,490.
The mentioned facilities are also putting into consideration the easy access to people with disabilities through the prepared infrastructure of those facilities to make sure of full access.
As always, AHF will have the previous interventions fully coordinated with all health actors through the Health Cluster whether through the data in 4Ws, HeRAMS and DHIS2. or through its constant participation and active membership within the TWGs like SRH, PHC and Non-Communicable Diseases, SIG, Rehab, referral, Supply Line and COVID-19 TF and its related groups, and on the ground AHF constantly coordinates with HDs.
Before the project implementation, M&E team will make a regular collecting the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. Monitoring and evaluation of the quality of the service provision through analyzing the collected data based on the Log-Frame, the project will be monitored and evaluated through three levels:
1) The operational, for which the project manager is responsible.
2) The technical, for which the technical team are responsible.
3) The objectives, for which the MEAL team is responsible.
Monitoring tools:
•Performance Indicator Tracking Tables (PITTs), issued in monthly basis.
•Satisfaction survey among beneficiaries and stakeholders will be analyzed and reported to stakeholders.
•Project Evaluation Report, by the end of the project-term.
The project activities will be coordinated with other actors including partners through the Trauma TWG meetings and health cluster meetings, in addition to the regularly meetings with IHD, so we have a full coordination with all parties working in the same field, and for the community within project areas we are coordinating with the local councils through regular visits will be conducted by the ambulance system administration manager.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2021: "with the Syria crisis approaching its 10th year, the health system remains heavily disrupted. While attacks on health care in 2020 fell by 67%1 compared to 2019, the COVID pandemic, coupled with economic downturn in the country, has pushed the health system to the brink. By the middle of 2020, 130 subdistricts – home to 11.97 million people – were below emergency standards across three critical population level health system indicators: number of healthcare workers, functional primary health centers and availability of basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care (BEmONC)2; while 52 districts with 5.52 million people were below emergency standards for hospital beds.According to HeRAMS only 396 of the total 577 health facilities are currently functional in NWS, including only 56% of those in Idleb and 39% of those in Aleppo, in addition to a shortage of qualified health workers at the HFs with less than half of the SPHERE standard.The health sector remains focused on five key vulnerable groups: displaced persons, children under five years (U5), WRA (15-49), older persons (60+), and people with disabilities. Among the 12.43 million people in need of health services in 2021, 1.41 million are children U5 while 3.32 million are WRA, including 498,480 women, are expected to become pregnant in 2021. An additional 536,753 older people are likely to require health services and those with early-onset NCD, which is estimated to account for 45 percent of all mortality in Syria.351 In Syria, the prevalence of disability is estimated at 25 %,352 nearly double the global average. (HNO 2021)

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will provide health services through 3 levels in addition to COVID-19 response as the following: - Providing consultations through out patient services in 3 PHC centers.- Providing pediatric inpatient services through 3 hospitals and surgeries through another hospital.- Providing advanced ICUs and specialized services through 3 hospitals.- Providing physical therapy sessions and prosthesis through 2 rehab centers.- Provide dental services through one MC.- Provision of COVID19 and non-COVID19 referral services through 20 ambulances.- Provide HBI services for COVID19 cases with severe symptoms through one isolation hospital.- Provide admissions for COVID19 cases with mild and moderate symptoms through 2 CCTCs.- Procurement of PPE, medical and non medical Supplies for the HFs.- Provide capacity building program to staff members (625 medical and non medical) of the facilities covering many topics like IPC, PFA, COVID19 case mang., NRP, NICU, BLS, ACLS, ATLS etc.Coordination of all the activities continues with IHD for Idleb, and with Turkish health Authorities in Afrin and Azaz, technical coordination with Health cluster, WHO, the Central ambulance system and with partners working in the same areas.Through the project implementation, AHF's Meal unit will contribute to improving decision making and management, by keeping the project on track towards achieving the outcomes and environmental and development objectives and by integrating lessons learnt into planning.The field MEAL team is in charge of collecting and providing information on project activities and implementation. To ensure accessibility to all gender groups, including women, children, people with disabilities, and other vulnerable people.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

AHF is an active member in PSEA network, AHF has updated PSEA and child safeguarding policies and procedures early 2021, including all levels from prevention, reporting, investigation and survivor support. AHF has male & female PSEA focal points in Syria and in Turkey offices, they support the implementation of AHF PSEA strategy, and they carry out the following tasks: - Ensure that employees are aware of their obligations related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. - Equip employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Response to Both. - Create mechanisms for reporting protection from sexual exploitation and abuse at the community level, including establishing contact points, according to the location and settings for reporting and posting. - Engage with communities and key stakeholders in strengthening community complaints mechanisms, including determining the provision of victim assistance services in all targeted locations. - Conduct regular awareness-raising activities on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, policies, standards and codes of conduct related to protection from sexual exploitation. - Provide specific awareness sessions to target groups such as women, girls, boys and men in reception centers, camps, sites for displaced people, host communities and society. - Ensure all project personnel sign the Code of Conduct for PSEA.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AHF will coordinate closely with the Nutrition cluster, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustment is needed. AHF has the access and qualified program team, the Equipment are available, the place is available, the fiels staff is available, the required supplies and medication available, the electricity is available by functioning electrical generators.Risk 1: Conflict related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors and shifting alliances (L)Response: Postponing the services till safety is back or relocation if the project's activities can't be provided in the same locations. Risk 2: Change of control in the services area, access constraints will continue to be the most significant threat to humanitarian operations. (L)Response: A comprehensive security and safety plan will be prepared so that the staff and the patients will be secured and we will coordinate to relocate the services depend on the new needs. Risk 3: The number of patients exceeds the capacity of the HFs (M)Response: In this case, we will coordinate with the cluster, Donors, local authorities and the partners on the ground to fill these gaps. Risk 4: Finance: cash transfer to Syria and within Syria with the absence of a banking system. (L)Using PTT for all money transfer to Syria either for operational costs, or vendors and contractors. Risk 5: The end of COVID-19 pandemic or becoming under full controlWe'll reconvert our services to its modality as before, so the ambulances will return to cover the gaps in ambulance services as before, the isolation hospital will be converted to an internal medicine hospital etc..
