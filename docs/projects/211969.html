Project Summary

Needs were assessed through direct observation conducted in current and closed projects in targeted locations, KII, and secondary data review. Identified needs encompass access, quality of education and educational community resilience. Barriers to accessibility are physical, economic and socio-cultural, including lack of learning spaces/furniture, low quality of facilities, economic inability of families to cover education-related expenditures, high cost of transportation for teachers to reach remote areas. Beyond leading to high dropouts and/or number of OoSC, the situation impacts the quality of education, already threatened by the low availability of trained teachers in target areas. Vulnerable categories are CWDs and girls, whose needs are often not addressed in educational communities. The same scenario applies to Vocational Training schools and opportunities, whose difficult access, retention, and low-quality bring youth and communities to loss of livelihood.
The proposed intervention target 83,870 beneficiaries, with an estimated budget of 4,362,041 USD. Target locations are in 9 districts in Aleppo, Deir-Ez-Zor and Ar-Raqqa, having all a severity scale from 3 to 4. By integrating soft and hard components in the intervention, the proposal aims at contributing to increase access to a quality, protective, and inclusive educational environment that can foster school retention and reinsertion. The main activities are rehabilitation of classroom and facilities, distribution of material, NFE, support to teachers and PTA members. The intervention is complemented by the activation of TVET, to alleviate economic barriers to school access.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

With the aim to increase access and quality of education, the action promotes a quality, protective, and inclusive educational environment that can foster school retention and reinsertion. The proposed intervention targets 15,000 of the most vulnerable children (50% boys, 50% girls, 5% CwD) in the areas of Aleppo, Deir-Ez-Zor and Raqqa. To ensure inclusive access to schools, 80 classrooms in pre-identified schools will undergo rehabilitation after a more in-depth technical assessment is conducted by WWGVC engineers in coordination with the MoE. WASH facilities will be renovated following a gender-sensitive and inclusive approach, which is mainstreamed in the whole school rehabilitation. Upon identified needs, the schools will also be furnished ensuring quality standards. Additionally, solar panels will be installed in 6 double-shift schools, to ensure provision of education services to children also in winter. Access will be improved also by tackling the school costs that burden families and cause increases in dropouts, by providing children enrolled in targeted schools and/or NFE centres with school supplies and school bags. Access support activities will include non-formal education through SLP and RCs to 15,000 children. During the NFE program, three cycles will be ensured, and each child will follow at least two of them. Children learning progress will be tracked through a robust evaluation mechanism, in line with MoE standards. OoSC to target with the interventions will be identified through BTL campaigns, that will also provide information on households with youth in highly vulnerable situations. 250 of these individuals (age group 5-17, 18-24) will be offered with Non-Formal TVET education to increase income and favour OoSC re-enrolment. To ensure schools are open in remote areas, transportation allowance to qualified teachers will be provided. Teachers working in NFE centres will moreover be provided with training in active learning, inclusive education, classroom management, pedagogical skills, child protection, life skills, and psychosocial support. Trained teachers will conduct sessions on PSS and life skills in informal settings. Recreational activities such as drawing competitions and sports galas will be organized using provided materials to alleviate trauma experienced by the children. This will highly contribute to improving the quality of education. The action also keeps a focus on building educational community resilience and behavioural change. First, PTAs will be established in targeted schools as per the guidelines of MoE and 100 parents and teachers will be accompanied in creating a joint action plan to tackle commonly identified educational-related problems and mobilize community resources. Secondly, awareness campaigns through IEC distribution and FGDs will be conducted with community members on the importance of inclusive education and other relevant topics raised also by PTAs. The project targets 83,870 beneficiaries.
