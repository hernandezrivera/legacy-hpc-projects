Project Summary

Following a decade of unfathomable violence and conflict, the humanitarian situation in
Syria, specifically in Northeast Syria (NES) has resulted in the displacement of millions of
Syrians and in worsening the socio-economic situation as well as handicapping the basic
services in the region such as electricity, water, access to health and education.
The proposed project aims to provide non-formal education services (Accelerated
Learning Program) to 1000 students in 2 sub-districts: Deir Ez-Zor and Thiban. The main
aspect is the need to fill a significant education gap for many children (out of school). According to Education Sector Findings HNO 2022, 25% of school-aged children are
less likely to attend any learning form.
The project is planned to take place through two cycles each four months, including 250
students in each area. In addition to the education components, PSS activities for children
and caregivers across the 2 targeted areas will be delivered including CP case management and awareness-raising sessions as AFH is part of the CP sub-working group. Furthermore, the project will focus on supporting 60 youth in the targeted regions through providing soft skills, computer literacy
programming, and encouraging youth-led community initiatives.
The proposed project is very relevant in addressing the identified problems as follows:
increase capacity building the number of trained teachers in the area; the delivery of ALP
programs to help in reducing the educational gap that many children are experiencing at
the moment after having missed school for many years and, bringing them to the
appropriate academic level compared to their age group will allow them to benefit to a
higher degree from the classes they attend in school.
provide PSS activities to at least 1000 children and 800 caregivers across the 2
communities through specialized PSS workers and CP case management for those cases
that need one-to-one psycho-socio support through specialised case manager.
Develop and conduct awareness-raising activities for 3500 children, youth, caregivers, and
key members of the community on child protection and education, available services,
prevention of family separation, and psychosocial self-care, in addition to PSEA and child
safeguarding through centres and mobile teams.
The awareness sessions in schools will be held to discuss issues such as child marriage,
child labour, and domestic abuse with students and their parents through appropriate
private consultation where needed aims to shed a light on the negative coping
mechanisms of children, prevent the perpetuation of the offences and support the children
in overcoming the psychological damage of the violence that they have experienced.
Youth support intervention, which is one of our proposed activities will target 60 youth
Males and females through a customized program composed of different components
aiming to build the capacity of youngsters and reinforce their role in society as active
participants. The intervention will be conducted in Jiah and Eastern Jarda following 3
cycles consisting of 2 months each cycle, each cycle will be targeting 20 youths aged
between 15 to 25 years old. These youths must be residents of Jiah and Eastern Jarda,
have at least a pre-secondary certificate (9th grade), come from vulnerable families, and
demonstrate the ability and commitment to attend the sessions. each group will be 100%
consisting of females or males for context-specific considerations.
The activity will be based upon providing computer literacy classes to attract the
participants and then provide them with life skills training and support them to adapt a
community initiative that will lead to making a positive change in their respective areas.
In the event that there are children with special needs, transportation will be provided for them to bring them to school. Health facilities will be provided within the schools, especially for people with special needs.

3 - Needs

The Syrian crisis has left more than 6M children with little or no access to education, the situation is very critical in the Deir Ez-Zor Governorate due to the full or partial destructionof educational facilities, lack of qualified teachers and weak capacities, and the verylimited resources of the local Education committee. Just after the security situation in Deir-Ez-Zor relatively improved, and the Education Committee focused its efforts on rehabilitatingschools and re-admitting children into the education system, regardless of their age oracademic level, the children were put into lower grades due to their level of education, andthe years they have missed because of the conflicts, thFurthermore, the education quality provided was very mediocre due to the lack of qualified teachers in the area. According to Education Sector Findings HNO 2022, 25% of school-aged children are less likely to attend any learning form. These factors have resulted in many children dropping out of school because of poor education quality or studying with much younger age groups which often creates frustration among these children. Consequently, children being out of school are more exposed to negative coping mechanisms and protection risks such as child labour, early marriage, neglect, and abuse. AFH is proposing this intervention out of its belief that the right place for children in school and that no child must be deprived of this universal right regardless of the causes.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Learning facilities might be attacked by parties of conflict or by communities. Attackers may arrest teachers or students, intimidate school staff/students or military use of the school, arson, theft, or looting. Formation of a local committee within each school composed of community leaders in the area in addition to the school's neighbours, the school principal, and some teachers. Their mission is to protect the school's property from theft and fire, in addition to appointing guards for the school from the same area.2- Acts of sexual harassment conducted by boys on young girls in latrines or unsafe places. AFH will ensure to rehabilitate sanitation facilities in a way that provides privacy and gender segregation to protect girls from any SG or harassment risk. 3- Services not matching the needs of people with disabilities. in this regard, AFH will make sure that all the facilities are PWD-friendly including classrooms and sanitation facilities.4- Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.) Unsafe learning spaces and insufficient/non-existent WASH facilities contribute to children not attending learning facilities. In summer, provide ventilation in all classrooms. In winter, safe heating is ensured in all classrooms. In addition to ensuring the cleanliness of sanitary facilities and the availability of water therein.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•The beneficiaries might reject the education services and have fewer children to benefit from our program. The mitigation plan is to reach out to target children and children who are out of school by holding meetings with students' parents and community leaders, introducing the project, explaining the advantages, and encouraging them to join the schools and the ALP program.•Security risks, the contingency plan in case of a minor security threat includes the temporary suspension of the program and the temporary closure of the school until the security situation goes back to normal, during which an emergency plan will be developed based on the delivery of distance education through WhatsApp groups.•Withdrawal of American troops in the SDF-controlled area, a contingency plan in case of an imminent advance of GoS in the areas where the program operates involves, closing the AFH once, and facilitating the evacuation of the staff that wants to leave the area, despite the high impact of the risk the accordance of the event is low.
