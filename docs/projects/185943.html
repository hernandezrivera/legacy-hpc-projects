Project Summary

The project dubbed WASH response to female-dominated households and affected host communities in the South West and Littoral Regions of Cameroon aims to improve the level of humanitarian response in the conflict-stricken North West and South Regions of Cameroon in the WASH sector. I focuses on addressing the burden set on female-dominated families and host communities as women in general bear the brunt of water scarcity, gender based violence related to their day to day activities.
The specific objective of this project is to Improves access to WASH services by 30,000 vulnerable individuals in female-dominated households and host communities in South and Littoral; construct 50 (100 cubicles) gender segregated to reduce open defecation and improve community sanitation, Sensitize over 30,000 individuals on hygiene practices to control and prevent the spread of cholera and COVID-19
The activities that have been earmarked by this project include: rehabilitation of boreholes and water points, distribution of dignity kits to women, distribution of WASH kits to affected households and host communities, construction of emergency gender segregated latrines, training of water user and toilet management committees.
The project targets affected female-dominated households in IPD host communities in the South West and Littoral Regions of Cameroon. Overall the project will benefit women, girls, boys and men and persons with disabilities. The project will be implemented in the South West (Buea, Limbe 1, Limbe II, Limbe II, Limbe III, Tiko, Kumba 3,and Mbonge subdivisions,) and in the Littoral (Loum, Njombe-Penja and Dibombari subdivisions), North West (Tubah, Santa, and These communities host affected female-dominated families and individuals who who at potentially at risk from cholera, covid-29 and access to limited WAS services.
The Environmental Protection and Development Association (EPDA) is a national NGO registered and legalized in Cameroon since 2010. EPDA deals in three thematic areas namely WASH, Environment and Food Security.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The North West and South West Regions continue to experience serious socio-political confrontation between non-state armed groups (NSAGs) and government forces characterized by intense shooting. This gun battles have increased the level of insecurities both at local and urban settings resulting in increased displacement of individuals and families. More displacement trends have been observed from areas of high insecurities to areas of relative safety including the Fako Division (Limbe 1, 2 and 3, Buea, and Tiko) and the West and Littoral regions. As individuals and families displace themselves, it is observed that the majority of those at risks are women and girls who in most cases outnumber the men and boys run into host communities with existing WASH challenges. As the population in the host communities increases, the needs for water, sanitation services and hygiene increase with a greater burden on women and girls. Limited access to WASH affects the dignity of women and girls in addition to the burden it places on them travelling distances to carry water which often is not safe and sometimes at night increasing their vulnerable to gender-based violence (GBV). According to WASH Cluster - Situation Report Oct. 2021, on about 25% of targeted people benefited from WASH items and hygiene promotion activities whereas about 95% of targeted people still lack access to safe drinking water point and basic sanitation services. Also according to OCHA SITREP of November 5, 2021, insecurity in the North West and South West regions displaced over 4,724 people in the NWSW, and to the West and Littoral regions, and 573,900 IDPs within or displaced from NWSW. Added to the humanitarian crisis in the NWSW regions is the global health crisis of COVID-19 pandemic as well as the cholera outbreak in some specific parts of the South West and Littoral regions with potentials to spread to many other places if concrete response actions are not undertaken on time. The SITREP on cholera in November highlights that the total cases were 79, 6 deaths: 6 (CFR 7.6%), a cholera attack rate: 8.52 per 1,000 people, Sex ratio Female/Male 1:2. The barrier measures to cholera and COVID-19 require washing of hands regularly with running water and soap.In the South West and Littoral regions especially in IDP female-dominated households and host communities, need for improved access to WASH requires rapid response to ensure the dignity and safety of female-dominated families and host communities by providing dignity kits to affected women and girls, rehabilitating water points, constructing boreholes and emergency gender segregated latrines as well as supporting host communities with WASH kits to improve hygiene practices at all times. This project will be implemented for a duration of nine months (3 quarters) starting from March to November 2022. This will then allow the project team to train water user committees as well as toilet management committees to ensure effective use and sustainability of the response.
