Project Summary

For HRP 2022, IOM will contribute to the cluster objectives namely: CO#1: Ensure that persons affected by conflict and natural disasters have protection from the weather and privacy through provision of shelter and NFIs; and CO#2: Vulnerable displacement and disasters affected populations have access to transitional or durable shelter, enabling safer and more dignified living standards and preventing recovering communities from slipping back into humanitarian need; through provision of: (1) NFI support to 10,000 newly displaced households through in-kind (through contingency stocks – 5,000 kits) and Cash-Based Interventions (CBI)); (2) Emergency (3,000 households (HH)), transitional (1,000) and permanent shelter (250) support through mix methods of in-kind and CBI; (3) Mainstream risk and resilience into project activities through infrastructural support and site de-congestion activities (500 HH). Interventions will focus on Baidoa, Dollow and Kismayo.
Somalia is at the crossroads of mixed and complex migration flows, often resulting in internal and external displacement due to ongoing conflicts, climate-related shocks, communicable disease outbreaks and weak social protection mechanisms. It is estimated that the pressing need for shelter has persisted and worsened into 2021; the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview for Somalia estimates that 3.6 million people are in need of shelter and NFI assistance with 2.7 million of these categorised as displaced population with 573,000 being newly displaced with an increasing trend of migration to urban centres related to climatic shocks, insecurity and better livelihood opportunities.
For the 2022 HRP, IOM will focus on providing 60,000 individuals with NFI support through a mixed method of in-kind and CBI support through contingency stocks and direct implementation respectively. A further 24,000 individuals will receive shelter support either emergency, transitional or permanent shelter using in-kind or CBIs. The unit cost for durable shelters is 3,000 USD. Finally, 3,000 individuals will receive infrastructure support under umbrella of risk reduction activities or site decongestion.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The cash or vouchers provide beneficiaries with flexibility to opt for the items that are needed to complement their existing resources, hence providing them with option of making choices that suit to their circumstances.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

IOM cash-based interventions are preceded by market assessments that determine the market functionality, availability of items locally, cost and safety of beneficiaries to receive cash support. During a market assessment, a minimum of 10 individual interviews with local service providers are undertaken. These providers are informed during the interview that participation does not guarantee that the interviewee will receive a contract offer. These interviews capture data such as whether they have access to a Somali bank account and government approved documentation; from where are supplies procured, modes of transport, links to trader associations or other traders; current stocks; and capacity for working with a commodity-voucher scheme where delays of up to three weeks are expected.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance: Mitigation measures: (1) train distribution teams on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse; (2) rotate distribution teams so no one team constantly visits the same settlements; (3) provide clear information to distribution actors and affected populations explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services / favours to anyone in exchange for receiving SNFI assistance, ensuring that this information is communicated to women and girls; (4) active complaint and feedback mechanisms for affected populations to make sure that issues are heard and acted upon throughout the implementation of the program Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards in targeted project area:Mitigation measures: (1) take into account the land tenure arrangements of IDPs, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land and inheritance rights; (2) conduct land and property due diligence and verify land tenure status using multiple sources, including local authorities, neighbors, elders and local residents; (3) advocate with land and property owners and local authorities to avoid eviction or disproportional rising in rent; (4) obtain permission before using or building on any land or property, in writing where possible; (5) avoid community tensions by providing the local/host community with access to camp services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of non-food items; (6) consult both displaced and host communities about shelter and NFI needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment, or conflict between the two communities; (7) keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use – apply effective data protection measures.
