Project Summary

The project aims to provide WASH services to 11,000 HHs of approximately 55,000 individuals (11,950 m, 13,250 w, 14,900 b, 14,900 g), most vulnerable IDPs, and host communities in Idleb and Aleppo governorates in communities and IDP informal camps.
The main intervention would be concentrating on the following:
15,000 individuals will have access to safe drinking water through the rehabilitation and maintenance of Sab Wiran water station, which feeds the Kundurli community and part of the Qabasin community, in addition to the camps affiliated with the Kundurli community, including operational support in the communities/camps, installing solar power feasible, and ensuring that all community utilities are connected to the system (schools, health facilities, etc.) in addition to developing the cost recovery system and forming the relative water committee in the targeted areas.
20,000 individuals will access safe drinking water in the (Al Omareen, Al Taawun(Kelly), Aladala Wa Altanmia, Ataa Alrahman, Khair, Al_Diaa 8, Bayrakdar(kelly) camps (Maaret Tamsrin subdistrict) by Providing 40/capita/day chlorinated drinking via water trucking to IDPs and providing the needed galvanized water tank.
Support systematic and relevant water quality indicator monitoring and reporting in high-risk areas. Chlorination and sterilization of water, daily FRC tests, and water distribution and quality monitoring. Conduct periodic water tests to ensure the validation of water for drinking and checking the sources and testing it, where Provided water will go under quality test. Periodically for the water tanks to ensure their safety.
40,000 individuals will have safe access to a clean environment and vector control through Solid waste management, including rehabilitating communal landfills and providing/maintaining the garbage containers / 100 L size garbage containers for every 50 individuals / in communities and solid waste removal in camps.
20,000 individuals will have safe access to proper sanitation through the Construction and rehabilitation of latrines and showers, including construction, plumbing, and electrical works, with all that is necessary and cleaning the latrine daily by recruiting volunteers from the camp residents. In addition, to ensure safe excreta disposal in at-risk IDP camps and other high-risk areas by dislodging septic tanks and treatment septic tanks with concentrated doses of chlorine. Septic tanks will be treated daily with two doses of concentrated Chlorine within the camps where cholera cases occur. All septic tanks dislodging tanks will be sterilized with a total amount of chlorine after each shift.
40,000 individuals within camps and communities (15% host community and 85 IDPs) will have Hygiene promotion awareness sessions during the distribution time for the targeted beneficiaries. This session will be through a trained team of males and females to organize the HHs sessions in each targeted location and provide printed awareness brochures about hygiene and how to use hygiene materials, water conservation, and rational consumption. The family will be trained during each session. We will use the IEC materials issued by the WASH and Health clusters and RCCE; the Cholera prevention procedures IEC will be explained, printed, and posted.
10000 individuals will have good hygiene practices by receiving hygiene kits.
Conduct capacity-building activities with WASH focal points and staff working at treatment centers and healthcare facilities on WASH interventions during cholera outbreaks.
SDI's plan for the 2025 budget is around the same amount as the 2024 budget and targets two water stations serving two communities and 10 adjacent camps. The areas will be identified based on gaps, needs, and severity determined during the 2024 HRP. Additionally, there will be maintenance and expansion of water and sanitation networks in the targeted communities and camps, as well as SWM, conducting hygiene promotion campaigns, and distributing HKs

0 - Project Summary (extended)

WASH HNO 2023 report highlights the significant reliance of IDPs on humanitarian aid, particularly in informal settlements where WASH conditions are often dire. Newly displaced populations exacerbate WASH needs, with limitations in infrastructure affecting all community groups. Overburdened communities with high IDP ratios and areas of high returnees face particular challenges due to inadequate and damaged infrastructure. Moreover, populations underserved by public services suffer from water supply shortages, poor quality, sewage issues, and lack of waste management services, further exacerbating their vulnerabilityThe project will commence with thorough coordination among stakeholders. Staff and contractors will receive training on PSEA and GBV. IDP representative committees will be established, ensuring diversity and participation across gender and disability lines. Technical assessments will be conducted, and procurement processes will adhere to transparent policies. Upon completion, the project will be transferred to camp management, with prior coordination with the WASH cluster to ensure sustainability. Activities prioritize age, gender, and disability considerations, addressing protection concerns and ensuring facility accessibilitySDI plans to disseminate reporting contact information widely through banners, social media, and messages to ensure accessibility. They will provide PSEA training to staff and contractors, emphasizing a zero-tolerance policy. Awareness sessions will be conducted for beneficiaries and children, encouraging reporting and dispelling fears.
