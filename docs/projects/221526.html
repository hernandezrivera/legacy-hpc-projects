Project Summary

The ongoing crisis in the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions of Cameroon has resulted in the displacement of over 74,607 households, with children constituting a significant proportion of the population, and severely affected the mental health and well-being of children, with over 19,000 children separated from caregivers due to displacement (MSNA, 2024). Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) rates are alarmingly high, reaching 30.3% in the North West, and 27% in the South West (UNICEF & SCOC CP Needs Assessment, 2024). Most of these children have been exposed children to traumatic experiences, separation from caregivers, and gender-based violence (GBV). This project leverages the power of sports as a tool to provide Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), foster social cohesion, and identify vulnerable children for targeted case management and birth registration services. Additionally, 65% of children and caregivers show symptoms of trauma from displacement and conflict, underlining the critical need for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) interventions.
This project, to be led by Street Child of Cameroon in partnership with TeenAlive Association and FOMCAM, aim to use sports as a dynamic tool to provide MHPSS, identify vulnerable children for case management, and facilitate birth registration. TeenAlive and FOMCAM are both local organisations with capacity in providing MHPSS to children. TeenAlive, a Strategic Advisory Group member of CPAoR, for the past three years has implemented projects mainly focused on providing MHPSS to children, funded by UNICEF. They have also played pivotal role in strengthening the capacity of CPAoR partners on MHPSS and Adolescent Life Skills Program. While FOMCAM besides her daily provision of MHPSS, provide capacity building opportunities to CPAoR partners.
By leveraging community engagement through sports, the project aims to support the most vulnerable children, improving their mental well-being, ensuring their protection, and strengthening their legal identity through sustainable, localized solutions.
Findings from the MSNA Round 10 (2024) reveal significant gaps in MHPSS services, access to case management, and legal documentation for children in the NWSW regions. Key identified needs include:
1. Trauma and Psychosocial Distress: Displacement and conflict have severely affected children's mental health.
2. Separation and GBV Risks: Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and adolescent girls face high exposure to exploitation and abuse.
3. Lack of Legal Identity: Thousands of children lack birth certificates, limiting their access to basic rights like education.
4. Community Disconnect: Fragile social cohesion and weakened community-based protection mechanisms (CBCPMs).

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Addressing Trauma and Psychosocial Distress: The project aims to address the critical mental health and social needs of children in conflict-affected regions through sports-based Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) activities. Leveraging structured games, creative play, and community sports events, it fosters emotional healing, resilience, and social connections. Children in the North-West and South-West regions experience severe stress and trauma due to conflict and displacement, with 32,374 internally displaced persons (IDPs) in these areas and an additional 21,145 IDPs in the Far North. Many fall within the vulnerable 6–13 years age bracket (27.31%), a crucial stage for emotional and cognitive development. Sports like football and obstacle courses provide stress relief and an outlet for emotional expression, while trauma-informed sports camps offer relaxation exercises such as mindfulness, building emotional stability and trust in safe environments. Addressing the Lack of Legal Identity: The project also tackles the lack of legal identity, which prevents many children from accessing education and healthcare. In the North-West and South-West, 20% of children aged 6-12 are out of school, often due to missing birth certificates. Community sports events incorporate awareness sessions for parents on the importance of legal documentation, creating pathways for birth registration. By engaging children and families in these programs, the initiative identifies undocumented children and connects them with legal services, ensuring their inclusion in education and healthcare systems. Expanding Access to Services for UASC and GBV Survivors: Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and gender-based violence (GBV) survivors also face significant challenges, with limited access to case management services. The MSNA (2024) highlights that 26.2% of IDPs in the South-West have returned to unsafe environments, exposing them to heightened risks. Sports activities provide safe spaces for UASC and GBV survivors, offering structured play alongside integrated psychosocial services. Peer support groups formed during sports programs foster emotional and social support, while trained facilitators link at-risk children to specialized care, ensuring both immediate and long-term protection. Strengthening Fragile Social Cohesion: The project addresses the breakdown of social cohesion caused by displacement. Armed conflict is the primary cause of displacement for 95.7% of households, and only 30.6% of returnees feel their areas of origin are safe. Community sports events and team-building activities aim to rebuild trust and foster unity, while family-inclusive sports days strengthen bonds within households. Team-based sports like football and handball reduce divisions among participants from different backgrounds, creating stronger networks to support child protection efforts in the long term. Integration of Life Skills: Additionally, the project integrates life skills development into sports activities, teaching problem-solving, leadership, and conflict resolution to equip children with tools to navigate challenges and build resilience. Inclusivity ensures equal participation for children with disabilities and marginalized groups. Sustainability is promoted through capacity-building workshops for local coaches and community leaders, ensuring continuity of MHPSS activities. Monitoring and evaluation track the impact on participation, emotional well-being, and community dynamics. By addressing trauma, legal identity, access to services, and social cohesion through sports-based activities, this project directly tackles the assessed needs of vulnerable children. It offers a holistic approach that combines psychosocial support, community engagement, and skill-building to foster resilience and long-term well-being in affected populations.
