Project Summary

In line with the CCCM cluster strategy for 2025, Acted will continue its work in addressing the needs of displacement-affected populations in South Sudan. Through a combination of static, roving, and mobile responses, Acted will focus on providing quality, information-driven interventions that support people in adapting to shocks and transitioning toward self-reliance. The target areas include camp and camp-like settings, informal settlements, and Transition Centers (TCs), with a particular focus on newly displaced populations, such as refugees and returnees from Sudan.
Acted’s CCCM teams will maintain flexibility and capacity to respond to the diverse needs of displaced populations in both cross-border and internal displacement contexts. The mobile responses will be particularly critical in addressing life-saving needs in hard-to-reach areas with limited service provision and partner presence. This mobile and roving approach will be essential in the immediate response to the ongoing conflict, especially in locations like Renk, Joda, Manyo, and Aweil East.
Acted’s proposed intervention in South Sudan is strategically grounded on the CCCM Cluster’s objectives for 2025 SO1, SO2, SO3 and SO4.
Acted will provide CCCM services in Renk, Joda, Manyo, and Aweil East (Majokyinthio border point) and in other displacement locations as the context demands. The first element of Acted’s approach centers on enhancing site management and coordination. Building on its presence in Upper Nile, and in complementarity with Acted’s currently secured funding to build a Reception Center in Aweil East, Acted will continue ensuring that displacement sites are managed efficiently, providing a platform for effective coordination among humanitarian partners. Acted will establish or strengthen site management and community committees, foster collaboration among service providers, and ensure that services are accessible to all populations. Close cooperation with local authorities, service providers, and the community will ensure that assistance is provided equitably, while also upholding the safety and dignity of all arrived population, especially vulnerable groups such as women, children, and persons with disabilities.
Community empowerment is another core component of the intervention. Acted will facilitate active participation of transiting individuals in decision-making , allowing them to voice their needs and be directly involved in addressing them. This will be achieved through the establishment of community-led governance structures, regular consultations, and feedback mechanisms, ensuring that displaced populations are actively engaged and their concerns are incorporated into the response, from the assessment phase to the closure of the project. Acted will also focus on facilitating durable solutions for IDPs. The intervention will work in partnership with local governments, humanitarian organizations, and the private sector to assess and implement solutions that are contextually relevant, whether through voluntary return, local integration, or relocation. Acted will ensure that adequate services and protection mechanisms are in place to support these solutions, promoting a smooth transition from emergency relief to long-term recovery. A significant emphasis will be placed on building the capacity of local actors, particularly community-based organizations and government authorities. Acted’s approach will enhance the operational capacity of these stakeholders, ensuring that they take ownership of the response. This aligns with CCCM’s broader localization goals, focusing on empowering local actors at every stage of the response. Strengthening local leadership will ensure that solutions are sustainable and contextually appropriate.

1. Needs

According to the 2024 HNRP for South Sudan, an estimated 9.4 million people are projected to require humanitarian assistance, with escalating needs driven by ongoing conflict, food insecurity, natural disasters, and recent refugee influxes. Key areas of need include Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity, Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria, where the compounding impacts of prolonged conflict and climate-related shocks have created extreme vulnerabilities.Moreover, eighteen months into the ongoing war in Sudan, fighting has displaced more than 800,000 people to South Sudan, moving to these already burdened displacement sites through the numerous crossing points, exacerbating overcrowding and straining existing resources. Whereas IOM has continued to provide onward movement services to returnees, site congestion remains a critical challenge at the crossing points reception sites. To ensure the provision of basic services remains adequate in the face of this escalating influx, it is imperative to proactively plan and allocate resources. Collaborative efforts between UNHCR, Acted, and other stakeholders in 2023 and 2024 have been crucial to mitigate the potential strains on existing infrastructure and services in both Renk Transit Centre, Joda Reception Centre and Renk town, but also for the neighboring populations in Manyo, on the opposite bank of the White Nile from Renk, that is targeted by limited humanitarian intervention. In these areas, the evolving nature of the displacement crisis calls for a shift towards a semi-static CCCM model. Acted’s flexible CCCM model is designed to support the medium- to long-term resilience of local services and infrastructure in areas hosting displaced populations. The transition to semi-static services is not only about responding to the immediate crisis but also preparing for sustainable management of displacement sites as populations remain in South Sudan for an extended period.While, from April 2023, the vast majority of displaced individuals, roughly 80%, have entered South Sudan through Renk County, the northernmost border crossing in Upper Nile State, new entry points have emerged at the norther border in South Sudan, particularly in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) State. Increased fighting in Sudan’s Kordofan region has exacerbated displacement, with over 56,000 individuals counted as having entered NBeG in 2024 alone. This shift in displacement patterns has made the Majokyinthio border point in Aweil East County the second-most trafficked border entry point in South Sudan. These sites also experience significant protection risks, including gender-based violence (GBV), child exploitation, and general insecurity, particularly affecting vulnerable populations like women, children, and individuals with specific needs. Overcrowding, coupled with inadequate access to hygiene and sanitation facilities, increases the potential for communicable disease outbreaks and hinders community resilience.

2. Project activities

A critical priority is the continued support to TCs in Renk and Joda, where Acted is active since 2023, and at Majokyinthio border point (Aweil East), where Acted, from October 2024, is working with partners to establish and coordinate a Reception Center. This will enhance living conditions and ensure that displaced populations have access to basic services such as water, sanitation, and shelter. Acted’s approach involves a combination of site management, community engagement, and infrastructure support, with an emphasis on flexibility and adaptability to respond to both ongoing and emerging needs, especially in hard-to-reach areas.These interventions will include: Site Management Services, effective monitoring of services, community engagement, and coordination among operational entities. Site/area-level coordination will ensure that services are accessible, equitable, and provided in accordance with international standards. A site-specific referral system will be implemented to ensure that individuals have timely access to appropriate services, such as healthcare, protection, and other life-saving assistance. Safety Audits will be conducted at each site, including new displacement locations, to assess and improve security conditions for all site residents. Multisectoral DRR Contingency Plans will be developed to ensure preparedness for potential shocks or new displacements. Support to TC) will be provided, including site planning, ground works, and the construction of basic site infrastructure and communal facilities to improve living conditions. Community Governance and Leadership Structures will be further promoted by supporting inclusive and representative community committees and leadership structures, empowering displaced persons and local populations to take active roles in site management and decision-making processes. Capacity Building for Local Actors will be conducted for community leaders, local authorities, and camp management staff to equip them with the skills and knowledge required for effective site management. Promotion of Local and National Participation, including meaningful involvement of local organizations, civil society groups, women-led organizations, and IDP/returnee-led groups in decision-making processes related to site management and service delivery. Cash for Work (CfW) Interventions, where possible, will be implemented to engage community members in site maintenance, community center construction, and infrastructure projects, ensuring that activities are aligned with identified community needs. MPC and NFI Distribution will be considered, particularly for protection cases, in line with UNHCR guidelines. Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms to promote transparency and accountability will be ensured. Acted’s approach will also bridge the gap between emergency response and longer-term self-reliance. By focusing on the capacity of local actors and fostering partnerships with government bodies, Acted aims to transition from immediate relief to sustained, locally owned management of displaced populations. Acted's CCCM response also focuses on area-based mobile teams which can rapidly respond to sudden displacement shocks, set up, and create sustainable management structures for site or informal site management, and hand over responsibility, with follow-up spot checks, once new arrivals numbers decrease. These teams work in a static/mobile modality, working in static sites in Greater Upper Nile and Greater Bahr el Ghazal until new displacement requires them to respond in their areas of responsibility. Deployment occurs in coordination with UNHCR, IOM, the CCCM cluster, and other relevant coordination actors to ensure non-redundancy, and comprehensive response. Teams also work with national and international static partners, where present, to ensure coordinated services that Acted does not offer, such as health and nutrition screening, in areas of intervention.
