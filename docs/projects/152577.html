Project Summary

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), an estimated 5,712,000 South Sudanese women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2019. GBI seeks to provide a 12 months Integrated & Sustainable, Adequate and Improved access to WASH facilities for vulnerable and affected populations in South Sudan (Jonglei : Ayod, Twic East & Pibor, Central Equatoria :Juba and Eastern Equatoria: Ikotos. In line with the call by the WASH cluster and the 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), GBI is proposing to scale up an integrated WASH response in these locations through supporting existing nutrition sites, health centres and schools’ sanitation, hygiene and water facilities as well as rehabilitation of dysfunctional ones and passing on critical messages. The project will support a total of 13,500 beneficiaries (men, women, boys and girls) targeted directly with lifesaving WASH and Nutrition interventions, along with sanitation and hygiene promotion activities at household level. The project will target IDPs, Host communities, Returnees and other vulnerable members of the community that may not fall under these categories, broken down as (3645, 5400, 3720 and 735 respectively).
The overall objective of this project is to contribute to the reduction of mortality, morbidity and impacts of acute malnutrition due to poor sanitation and hygiene amongst children under five and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) through management of acute malnutrition as well as building their resilience. This project is designed to reduce vulnerabilities and risk associated with the current food insecurity and poor WASH conditions in the proposed counties, and it also will complement existing WASH, Health and Nutrition activities that implementing partners are doing in the proposed counties. Increase access to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene for all, paying attention to the needs of malnourished children, women, adolescent girls and those in vulnerable situation. The project will require $ 445,000 to provide the intended services and activities throughout the intervention period.

Needs

Water is a necessity, and an important resource for sustaining life. The decline in water quality endangers the health of humans as well as the ecosystem. Clean drinking water, hygiene, and sanitation play an important part in maintaining health. Contaminated water causes many waterborne infections like diarrhoea and serves as a carrier for vectors such as mosquitoes spreading epidemics. Open defecation means no sanitation. It fouls the environment, and spreads diseases. In Eastern and Central Equatoria States, the spread of cholera across the area occurred in the late dry season (November-May), a time where the areas pastoralist communities are getting ready to leave seasonal grazing lands toward their home settlements. A lack of information on cattle migration patterns impeded access of lifesaving assistance to many cattle camps and pastoralist communities (REACH 2018). The lack of access to improved water and sanitation and poor hygiene behaviours is a principal cause of water-related diseases such as diarrhea and cholera, infection from guinea worm, and contributes to malnutrition. Water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) is also associated with educational access and nutritional outcomes. Access to water and sanitation can be the reason why girls are kept out of school and improved access to water and sanitation at schools has been shown to increase school attendance among girls (USAID 2018). Increased rainfall throughout the first quarter raised the risk of incidence of waterborne diseases and brought along the concern of a potential re-emergence of cholera, which has been officially contained since February (REACH 2018). The most vulnerable members of the population are in the greatest need of WASH support, particularly the internally displaced. This includes the severely malnourished and food insecure, who are prone to water-borne and water-related diseases (HNO 2018). Activities to improve the WASH behaviours of individuals, households, and communities, focused demand creation and promotional activities will be applied for the four key hygiene practices: hand washing with soap, use of appropriate latrines, the safe storage and treatment of water in households at the point of use, and food hygiene by this project. While there are several NGOs working in these locations, GBI still sees deficiency and gaps in the service offering. We shall use our local presence to identify and provide specific WASH needs to the vulnerable populations.

Activities

1) Restoration of broken-down community water points and construction of new water facilities (Boreholes, wells) to increase access to safe water for vulnerable and affected people. 2) Construction of cost friendly public latrines for health facilities and schools in project locations distributed according need as contribution to the intended integration of WASH cluster with health and education.3) Community mobilization and training on hygiene promotion to provide regular hygiene messaging at household level and public (to encourage people to take action to protect their health and make good use of facilities and services provided).4) Water User Committees formed to ensure the functionality/sustainability of existing water points for adequate, timely and improved access of safe water for the vulnerable and affected population. 5. Distribution NFI kits to vulnerable and affected communities.
