Project Summary

Idleb governorate has a population of 2.379.649,5, of whom 1.125.233,5 are IDPs. In Dana subdisrict of Harim District, people in need of health is estimated to be 388.352 for 2019 with a severity score of 4. The number of IDPs in Dana (estimated 378.023) are three-fold of the residents (118.888 ). Many IDPs live in last resort sites, including “self-settled camps” that are “independent of assistance from the government or humanitarian community.” Sarmada town of Dana hosts such camps, AIDoctors has a primary health care unit (PHC Unit) located in Al Kamouneh Camp with a catchment area of nine camps around it. The area hosts 6106 IDPs in 1079 tents.
One of the the HRP Health Sector Objectives for 2019 is to “increase access to humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health services for those most vulnerable and in need.” IDPs living in last resort camps, sites, collective centres and/or living in open areas are identified as one of the priority vulnerable groups in HNO 2019. Our objective is to increase accessibility of quality primary health care services by all the IDPs living in these camps with specific interventions adjusted to their age, gender, and disability conditions. Preventive and curative services will be provided by the PHC Unit. In line with the cluster activities, the activities will include outpatient consultations, trauma consultations, mental health consultations, referral for specialized treatment, immunization according to the national programme, training/retraining of health care workers and commynity health workers.
The activities will try to cover the needs assessed through direct observation, interviews with key informant and IDPs taking into considerations identified by the health sector. The prioritized needs are diagnosis and treatment of common health problems, availability of medicine and medical supplies for acute or existing conditions, outbreak prevention, improved referral services, improved physical accessibility for the disabled people to the health care services, and availability of reproductive, maternal, newborn, and child health (RMNCH) care.
Protection risks including gender based violence (GBV), Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), lack of duty of car efor the staff and lack of engagement and participation of community will be mitigated using recommendations of the The Whole of Syria (WoS) Health sector. Risks that will affect the implementation of activities are identified as violence against our facility and our healthcare providers, mass influx of new IDPs to the area, mass displacement from the area, and increased violence in the area between parties in conflict. Mitigation measures will aim at ensuring the safety of our staff and the IDPs and continued provision of services with Do No Harm principles.

Needs

Equitable access to preventive and curative essential primary health care services is the overarching need assessed in the project area. It is vital to ensure the most vulnerable people can receive health care.IDPs living in last resort camps, sites, collective centers and/or living in open areas are identified as one of the priority vulnerable groups in HNO 2019.The project will be located in a self-settled tent camp and serve to the IDPs with compromised basic needs from shelter to hygiene.Within this group, some subgroups are more vulnerable and need special attention including infants and young children, women at reproductive age, the elderly and people with disabilities. The identified specific needs are: 1.Diagnosis and treatment of common health problems:The suboptimal living conditions and lack of basic services (nutrition, WASH) increase the risk of communicable diseases, especially in children under 5. Case management is essential for the recovery of the patients and prevention of outbreaks. The people over 50 years old account for 6,4 % of the camp population and many suffer from chronic illnesses that can be managed in the primary health care level such as diabetes and hypertension.People with injuries due to violence and conflict need first aid and follow up. 2.Medicine and medical supplies for acute or existing conditions: As the livelihood opportunities are extremely limited in the camps, people cannot afford medicine.Medicine and medical supplies for primary health care are required to be provided. Ensuring the availability of safe and quality medicines with reliable supply and storage system vital for effective health care provision. 3.Outbreak prevention: Outbreaks of measles, acute bloody diarrhea, typhoid fever and leishmaniosis were reported in various areas of the country throughout 2018. Routine immunization is essential to stop spread of vaccine-preventable diseases. Vector control such as cleaning the latrines and waste collection areas and preventive measures such as increased availability of hygiene products were vocalized as needs by the IDPs. 4.Improved referral services:Ambulance service for emergencies is required, especially at night when the vehicles face security problems. For the non-emergency cases, the transportation cost is prohibitive for many vulnerable patients even if they are referred to a certain facility by the physician and they need financial support.5.Improved physical accessibility for disabled people to health care services: 107 people have been identified with a physical disability, excluding the elderly with reduced mobility. The people with disabilities may have reduced mobility that impacts their physical access to the services. Actions such as the provision of assistive devices, home visits, making the center accessible will ensure PWD can receive health care.6.Availability of Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, and Child Health (RMNCH) Care: More than one-fourth of the camp population are the women at the reproductive age and as of January 2019, 130 women were pregnant. Lack of delivery with a skilled birth attendant as well as antenatal and postnatal care can increase maternal and infant mortality and morbidity. Early marriages take place as negative coping mechanisms, usually leading to adolescent pregnancies. Awareness raising activities on early marriage and pregnancies and available family planning materials are required. Awareness of GBV increase and help-seeking for the survivors should be promoted. 9,1 % of the population is children under five years old. Preventive health care (exclusive breastfeeding, immunization, hygiene, nutrition) and curative health care for communicable and congenital diseases are needed. The needs assessment sources:1.Direct observation 2.Key informants interviews-camp manager, doctor, and health care staff.3.Interviews with the affected population through home visits-women, pregnant and lactating women, men, adolescents, elderly, and PWD.

Implementation of activities

Activity 1. Outpatient consultations: The health care unit will be open 5,5 days a week. Patients first will be taken to the triage zone and be prioritized according to the urgency. Among non-emergency cases children under 5, pregnant women, the elderly, and the people with disabilities will be prioritized. One general physician will conduct consultation with the help of the nurse for vital measurements. Case management of communicable diseases and management of the existing noncommunicable diseases are expected to consist of the majority of the consultations. Medicine prescribed by the doctor will be handed to the patient by the pharmacy technician. The pharmacy will make sure never to experience out-of-stock essential medicine and medical supplies through stock-keeping and timely procurement and delivery of medicine and supplies. RMNCH care including family planning, antenatal and postnatal care and integrated management of childhood illnesses will be provided in the center. Health promotion will be conducted by each health care provider to increase awareness on key health issues.Activity 2. Trauma consultations: Both hostility-related and non-hostility related trauma cases will receive first aid, dressing/bandage application and changes. Severe cases will be referred to higher level health facilities. Conflict-related cases will be recorded and reported separately.Activity 3. Mental health consultations: Psychological first aid and MHPSS counseling by the psychosocial worker will be conducted for people suffering from mental trauma or distress including GBV survivors. Severe cases will be referred to higher health facilities.Activity 4. Referral for specialized treatment: Cases that need further investigation for diagnosis or advanced treatment such as people living with chronic disease who develop complications and the wounded with complex traumas will be referred to higher level hospitals, namely to the Aqrabat Hospital and Bab al Hawa Hospital. Pregnant women will be referred to Atma Hospital for deliveries, obstetric and newborn emergency cases. GBV survivors will be referred there for comprehensive management after clinical management of rape in our health unit. Non-emergency patients who cannot afford the transportation cost to those facilities will be supported through payment of the transportation fee. Emergency cases will be referred to other facilities utilizing ambulances from Idleb Health Directorate ambulance system.Activity 5.DPT or equivalent pentavalent vaccine for children under 1: Vaccinations will be carried out in the center and through home visits by the nurse. Each child will have a vaccination card and tally sheets will be filled to keep records of the vaccinated children. CHWs will follow up the routine vaccination schedule of children. Activity 6. Training/retraining of health care workers: Inservice training, re-training, and capacity building of doctors, nurses, midwifee, and psychosocial support staff with task shifting when possible will be done by the organization and through participation to the training by the health sector. Activity 7. Training/retraining of the community health workers: CHWs will attend training by the health sector and the organization to increase public health awareness in the community and conduct referrals to the center. Training for both the health care staff and CHWs aims to increase their capacity in case of dysfunction or inaccessibility of the center due to the occurrence of a Force Majeure.

Protection Risk Analysis

1.Risk: Gender Based Violence (GBV). Survivors may seek help in the center.Likelihood:Medium, Impact:HighMitigating measures;- MISP services to prevent and manage the consequences of sexual violence will be provided through training of the health care providers, availability of the medicine, medical supplies, and educational materials; awareness raising sessions in the facility and in the community.We will collaborate with community leaders and other providers.Intimate partner violence will be included in both prevention and management activities.-We will train our staff on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. -We will reach out to secondary level facilities and protection sector to identify safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - We will have female health care providers to increase accessibility.One of them will be identified as in charge.2. Risk:Given the position of power and close contact of health staff with the beneficiaries, the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) exists.Likelihood:Medium Impact:High Mitigating measures:- All staff will be trained on the organization's "zero tolerance" PSEA policy that forbids any verbal or physical sexual exploitation or abuse.A mechanism for reporting any misconduct for accountability as well as actions for victim assistance will be established. Continuous training on the topic will ensure the implementation. -The community awareness sessions will inform the people about the organization, it's values and principles, the project's deliverables, and expectations from the team. A complaint mechanism will be put in place including a complaint box and WhatsApp line. Given the sensitivities of the women and girls in the community, female staff will be part of the activities. Victims will receive the required support.3.Risk:Given the health care staff are Syrians experiencing physical and mental difficulties, protection of them and provision for their safety,security, and wellbeing are essential. Likelihood:High Impact:High Mitigating measures:-We will establish a duty of care policy and share it with the staff. The policy will include timely payment of the salaries, the actions to be taken in an abrupt termination of their contracts in force majeure to compensate their loss, as well as their rights. We will incorporate their feedback on the policy.- We will ensure that the staff has access to psychological counseling through agreements with psychosocial support providers. We will conduct regular sessions to discuss the wellbeing of the staff and encourage seeking professional support when needed.4.Risk: Without the engagement and participation of the community in design, implementation, and monitoring of the program, we cannot serve the needs of the population.Likelihood:Low Impact: High Mitigating measures:- We will recruit staff from the community to increase direct communication.-We will have feedback mechanism through WhatsApp/box in the center/and face-to-face interactions and inform the community on the services and the feedback mechanisms through CHWs.5.Risk:Vulnerable groups may have problems with access to health services.Health sector identified vulnerable groups as children under 5, women of reproductive age, people living with disability PWD, over 50 years of age, IDPs and returnees.Likelihood:Medium Impact:High Mitigating measures:-We have disaggregated data by age and gender including vulnerable groups. We included them in the analysis and design phase through face-to-face interviews and will continue to receive their feedback to ensure equitable access-We will have special services to cover the needs of those groups (SRH care, assistive devices, NCD management, immunization etc)-We will include the needs of these group in emergency plannings

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Risk: Violence against our facility and our healthcare providers: The health care facilities and providers have been subject attacks. In 2018, 134 attacks were recorded; 47 of them were in Idleb. Although there have not been security incidents towards our health unit, the risk exists. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigating measures: -We de-conflicted our health facility. -We will be impartial and neutral when providing services. It will help us not to be seen as part of the conflict by any party. Our staff will be visible (coats, usage of logo etc.) or reduce visibility depending on the situation to increase their safety. -We will establish good communication and trust with the community that may support our protection. 2.Risk: Mass influx of new IDPs to the area: Idleb accounted for the majority of displacements in Syria in 2018. Between January and November 2018, 435,462 people moved within Idleb, and 217,246 IDPs came to Idleb from different governorates. Violence as well as other reasons such as flooding may cause new displacements, as it did before. The need for health care would increase directly due to the increase in population and indirectly due to the pressure put on already limited existing resources affecting health status such as shelter and WASH etc. Likelihood:Medium Impact: High Mitigating measures:- We will have a contingency and emergency response plan to expand our services to serve more people, including increase in human resources and materials respecting Do No Harm principles.3. Risk: Mass displacement from the area: The people living in the camps may leave due to push factors from the existing camps (security, worsening physical conditions, discontinued basic services etc.) or pull factors in other areas (improved basic services). Likelihood: Low Risk: High Mitigating measures:- We will have a mobility plan to relocate the center with the IDPs if required while continuing the services at the center for the remaining IDPs with downsizing the scale.4. Risk: Increased violence in the area between parties in conflict: Although an agreement between Turkey and Russia prevented an expected major offensive to Idleb in 2018, the uncertainty on Idleb's fate remains for 2019. Violence among between armed opposition groups in Idleb have occured before and likely to happen again. Likelihood:High Impact: High Mitigating measures:-We will have an evacuation/relocation plan that will be activated when the security situation requires it. It will include provision of services as much as possible. The activation for evacuation will be based on reports from the field staff.
