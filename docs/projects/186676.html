Project Summary

The project will be aimed at supporting and improving the GBV knowledge and build life skills of adolescents girls who are affected by the insurgency in the northeast.
Through the project activities The Big Smile Foundation seeks to support, protect, and empower adolescent girls in humanitarian settings. Program has been designed to help contribute to improve prevention and response to violence against adolescent girls in humanitarian settings by providing them with the skills and knowledge to identify types of GBV and how to seek support services if they experience or are at risk of Violence and also improve their basic skills which girls need in life.
While girls need to see their daily realities reflected in program content, they also need to be able to envision themselves as empowered individuals. In many places, adolescent girls are not encouraged to have ambitions, so life skills programs can be an opportunity for them to discover new aspirations for their present and future personal and professional lives. Women and girls should be portrayed in life skills programs as confident, capable and empowered to handle the difficult situations that life brings them and to pursue their goals. Contents related to human rights, gender equality and power relations are critical to transformative programs.
There are six core topic areas which the Program focus to improve/develop the basic skills girls need to survival and develop and assets necessary to decrease risk and increase safety for girls:
Trust - This module helps lay the foundation for the group to be able to move on to more challenging topic areas, while also addressing some immediate issues related to safety.
Social & Emotional Skills - This module provides the foundation for building girls’ positive sense of self and their ability to successfully interact with each other and their communities.
Health & Hygiene - This module gives girls the opportunity to learn critical information related to their bodies, during a period when they are going through significant changes. They can explore issues related to hygiene, puberty, and sexual health in a safe space, where they are provided accurate information.
Safety - This module focuses on the concepts and skills girls need for identifying, preventing, mitigating, and responding to GBV.
Solidarity - This module helps girls to start preparing for the end of the program cycle, and strengthen their support networks so that they will continue to nurture the skills they have learned and their support network once the life skills curriculum finishes.
Visioning - This module is designed to strengthen the social and emotional skills related to perseverance, as well as to create hope and support girls in concrete planning for their futures.

1 - Needs

The ongoing crisis in North East Nigeria is primarily a protection crisis, which has exposed civilians to violence, exploitation and abuse, including gender-based violence. Gender based is widespread, and has been aggravated by displacement, limited access to services, disruption of livelihoods as well as harmful socio-cultural practices. An estimated 1.3 million people are in need of GBV support across the three worst-affected states of Borno, Yobe and Adamawa. Over 1 million of these are girls and women, and over 756,000 are children. These statistics show the particular vulnerability of female headed households and adolescent girls, in particular, to sexual exploitation and abuse - including survival sex, early marriage and forced prostitution. Women and children who have been abducted by armed groups may have endured rape, sexual abuse or forced marriage, and often face high levels of stigmatisation after their release. Children associated with armed groups (CAAG) and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) are therefore particularly vulnerable. Stigmatisation can also contribute to economic marginalisation, making it difficult for survivors to rebuilt their livelihoods and reintegrate fully into the community. Despite the particular vulnerability of women and girls, it is important to note that all population groups - girls, boys, women and men from host community, IDP and returnee communities are at-risk and in need of support. All forms of GBV, experienced by all types of survivors, are chronically under-reported due to stigmatisation and marginalisation as well as the lack of effective reporting and response mechanisms. Hence the importance of awareness creation and community engagement that encourages prevention and access for support for victims of GBV. There are significant gaps in the humanitarian response to GBV. There is a general lack of GBV services available, and where services are available the quality (including making of timely referrals and follow-up of referrals made) is often low.

2 - Activities

The Big Smile Foundation will provide 1200 adolescent girls this will include children at risk, survival, out of school, in school and children with disability, through the engagement of girls in workshops session to improve their life skills and knowledge on Gender based violence and including the distribution of dignity kits, counselling sessions for survivors. Survivors will also be supported with livelihoods support through a tailored package of vocational and business skills training, mentoring and cash grants to enable the survivors to access sustainable income-generating activities and promote their socio-economic reintegration. At the community level, TBSF will build capacity of community-based protection and response structures. This will include awareness-raising activities and training of community-based committees on GBV prevention and response principles. Community-based GBV monitoring and support groups will be established as forums for peer support and mentoring, empowering community members to facilitate awareness-raising, prevention and response activities to challenge harmful gender attitudes.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

TBSF considers protection of all persons affected and at risk when planning humanitarian response and protection is central in TBSF’s preparedness and life-saving activities throughout the duration of project. The population at risk would be identified considering the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as internally displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities, and persons belonging to sexual and other minorities. Primary consideration will be given to TBSF’s accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their own protection measures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed action is defined by daily interaction and collaboration with affected communities, including support to community-based structures and their participation in public awareness-raising, referral processes and collaboration in identifying and implementing initiatives which contribute to improving the protective environments. Furthermore, TBSF ensures independent consultations with specific groups, including women, girls and children, to ensure their voices are heard and ideas are incorporated into all activities. As part of the emergency response activities, beneficiaries will be consulted through regular monitoring visits and post-distribution monitoring exercises, during which individuals will be consulted on matters including the quality and relevance of assistance received. TBSF will engage community and stakeholders in parodic focus group discussions receives complaints, reports and feedback from communities, analyses the data and shares with relevant sections in TBSF for action as reports and complaints regarding other services handled by different agencies come in, they are shared with the Sector to take action. Each case is followed up to provide feedback to the community members who laid complaints.
