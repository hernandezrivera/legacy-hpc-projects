Project Summary

The facilities are not self-sustaining and nor do they receive full support from the government; thus are dependent on shrinking support received from humanitarian partners
UNHCR will continue to provide emergency shelter (ES) materials and non-food items (NFI) to people victims of newly displacedment. In situations of protracted displacement, UNHCR will provide ES/NFI support in case of new shocks, including natural disasters, floods or incidents of violence/attacks. Through policy advocacy with social protection stakeholders, primarily the Ministry of Security and Social Development and Ministries of Social Welfare, UNHCR will promote the integration of vulnerable IDPs in the social protection systems of Sudan, and the removal of legal or material discriminatory barriers to their access to essential protection services

1 - Needs

Due to the current armed clash in the Jebel Mara area, Central Darfur will continue to witness new displacement. In fact, UNAMID strategic review team from New York has decided to make UNAMID’s Darfur Head Quarters in Zalengie, Central Darfur. Although some locations of UNAMID have been closing and will continue to go through rapid withdrawal, following the extension on the Mandate to one year. It is expected that UNAMID team sites and the two States, West Darfur and East Darfur are expected to be closed in mid-2020. Further to this, there will be locations where humanitarian agencies will newly access for emergency response. As per Sudan’s Emergency Response Framework, newly accessed IDPs will be treated as newly displaced population when designing a response plan. Given new displaced population and newly accessed IDPs are in dire need of ES-NFIs response, UNHCR will engage in addressing t unmet needs identified by Area HCT. With Security and land tenure usage situation improving and since the government’s top political priority is closing IDP chapter in Sudan through solutions, large numbers of IDP returnees have been verified and reported in 2018 and will continued in 2019. While 2018 HNO figures indicated that 386,000 IDP individuals have returned home, the authorities in Darfur are reporting over 900,000 returns (not confirmed yet though if these are seasonal or permanent returns). In this part, whenever return is reported, the first initiative is for UNHCR to conduct voluntariness of return verification mission which also consist of assessing safety conditions and if indeed return is permanent. Based on such verification report document, IOM will conduct registration. In an agreement with stakeholders and relevant authorities, an inter-agency assessment mission will be carried out to identify protection and essential basic service’s needs. UNHCR will participate in such inter-agency assessment missions to identify Shelter/NFI needs. From the missions conducted by UNHCR in 2018-19 clearly informs that essential services and infrastructures are not in place in majority of the places of return. Further to the needs in strengthening essential services, provision of durable shelters and NFIs needs to be provided for the vulnerable IDP returnees. UNHCR will further work closely with community-based structures and identify projects which compliment in the empowerment and mobilization of such structures to mitigate social protection issues. As indicated in the above section where prioritization is identified, given the return movement and urbanization plan will continue in 2020, UNHCR will engage through the existing inter-agency coordination forum to identify and address unmet needs associated with protection and ES-NFIs. Response to the urbanization planning will primarily constitute in what is agreed with HCT and relevant authorities.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Key activities under include assessments, verification with partners and government counterparts, planning, and dispatch of NFIs, implementation and monitoring through PDMs. In addition to procurement and managing the NFI Core Pipeline. UNHCR as the Sector lead of the S/NFI Sector will also conducting trainings and workshops for community leaders, service providers and local authorities; advocacy and policy through relevant forum on plans for durable solutions. All activities will consider the different protection needs of Women, men, children and People with Special Needs.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

According to 2019 HNO Sudan has estimated 1.86 million IDPs living across Darfur, Kordofan and Blue Nile states as well as in other parts of the country. The vast majority are in a protracted displacement situation in Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile states with around 24,000 new displacement recorded during the course of 2018 from Jebel Mara region in Darfur, and parts of South Kordofan under SPLM-N controlled. IDP registration and data management is undertaken by IOM in Sudan and, as such, UNHCR is not involved in population data management for IDPs. The overall national IDP figures are compiled by HAC and OCHA that form the HCT official figures. According to the 2019 HNO, Darfur is currently having 1.6 million IDPs while an estimated 324,000 IDPs have voluntarily returned to their places of origin. IDP returns are being verified for confirmation as well as assistance purposes. The interagency voluntariness of return verification missions undertaken in 2018 and 2019 by RRR Sector, UNHCR and relevant authorities, have confirmed voluntary returns in areas where security and land issues have been resolved. According to 2019 HNO, over 324,000 IDP have returned to their places of origin within the last five to six years. The 2019 HNO has further confirmed an additional 235,000 IDPs in West and South Kordofan, and Blue Nile States. While security has improved significantly in areas affected by internal displacement, IDPs continue to face risks to their physical safety and integrity. The continued nature of displacement, lack of lasting solution of the root cause of the conflict and presence of military or militias expose IDPs, returnees and local residents to physical violence. Women and girls are particularly exposed when moving to work on fields, collect water or firewood, or going to markets, while men and boys risk arbitrary arrest and beating. Cases of kidnapping continue to be reported, as well as extortion or looting. IDPs attempting to go back to their home areas and cultivate their land have also been exposed to threats and violence from members of other communities, or are only allowed to access and work on their land under unfair terms of use. While the number of inter-communal violence has decreased, localised armed violence takes place, most frequently between sedentary farming and nomadic-pastoral communities, as well as between nomadic communities, clashing over access to, use and management of resources, especially land and water.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The Office will build from its work in 2018 and 2019 to establish a robust PSEA response across the operation. This includes strengthening the culture of accountability across the operation through the integration of PSEA actions within protection functions and broader Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) strategy. This will be achieved through regular PSEA training for UNHCR staff, partners and contractors; regular SEA risk assessment and development and implementation of annual PSEA action plans for field offices; and collaboration with operational partners to establish inter-agency and community-based complaint mechanisms accessible to IDPs, returnees and newly accessed in all locations with an active IDPs, returnees and newly accessed response. The Office will continue maintain its active participation in- and contributions to other inter-agency PSEA fora, including the RC/HC’s PSEA Network.
