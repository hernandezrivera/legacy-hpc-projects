Project Summary

The project intends to provide integrated emergency WASH response to host communities and IDPs in the communities and the in the camps and informal settlements north Syria.
WATAN will continue support the IDPs in 50 camps. A continuation of the emergency WASH to IDPs living in 50 camps, where WATAN response has started since June 2019. In addition to expand in this emergency response by adding new 50 informal settlements have been established in January 2020 with the expensive displacement of approximately 400,0000 IDPs from south and east Idleb.
Through this project, WATAN will provide water trucking, solid waste management, desludging latrines septic tanks, constructing new latrines for persons with disabilities and persons without disabilities, operation and maintenance for the existing and new constructed latrines, distributing garbage containers, hygiene kits distribution and hygiene promotion.
As for the host communities, in four communities, WATAN will rehabilitate water systems, provide operation and maintenance for the rehabilitated water systems, repair sewage systems and extend the coverage of the sewage to response to the new IDPs, support the operation and maintenance for the solid waste in the targeted communities.
The project subscribes to WATAN strategy of alleviating the suffering of people in need
from Syria in order to maintain a healthy life and reduce the transmission of water-borne
diseases.

4 - Needs

These camps need diverse WASH-related services in terms of water for different uses, solid waste management, hygiene activities and enough sanitation facilities, according to SPHERE standards taking into account the needs of different age groups, gender and considering of cross-cutting issues (do no harm principles-accountability), through the following;- Providing safe water according to WASH cluster recommendation in the camps for drinking and personal and domestic hygiene focusing on chlorinating the water and monitoring the quality.- Construct latrines for IDPs taking into account the gender and the persons with disabilities needs.- Provide or continue providing solid waste management including distributing garbage containers.- Hygiene activities: Distribute hygiene kits and conduct hygiene sessions to raise awareness of IDPs including creating recreational activities for children.- Rehabilitate the water station in the targeted communities and providing diesel and oil for operation and maintenance, taking into consideration establishing a cost recovery in the targeted communities.- Extend the water network to cover the new area of constructed houses by pumped water.- Improving the water quality by providing chlorinated water, and monitoring of the FRC.- Support solid waste management operation and maintenance in the targeted communities.- Rehabilitate the sewage system and extension of the sewage line to cover the new constructed houses including the new IDPs. WASH Needs Assessment Camps, Informal Settlements, Communities North Syria By WATAN February 2020.

5 - Implementation of activities

The implementation will start with coordination with the clusters WASH and CCCM to avoid overlapping with other NGOs providing the same type of WASH response. The second layer of coordination with the local authorities (local councils and camps management), and signing MoUs. The second half of February WATAN will raise the purchase requests and advertise the tenders for the planned activities in camps and communities. The water trucking will start first as it is the most urgent due to the current displacement situation, and it is expected to be started during February, the solid waste management and desludging will start the second half of February. Latrines construction will start during March with the rehabilitation of the WASH infrastructure in the communities. The emergency WASH in the camps will continue until the end of 2020, and monitoring activities will accompany the emergency WASH in camps, the latrines construction will last for three months started from March 2020, while the rehabilitation of the WASH infrastructure will complete within four months. O&M for solid waste activities and water station will continue for four months starting from March.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Hygiene Kits; Risk- Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV.Mitigation - Promote Establishment of adequate management plan; Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partner staff.Emergency water supplyRisk - Movements of water trucks in congested, camp like settings.Mitigation - Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points, delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; Conduct safety training with truck drivers/operators.Risk - Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standardsMitigation - Ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities or local councils.Ensure and comply with agreed context /SPHERE standards (minimum humanitarian standard at 15 litre/person/day) and establish water quantity tracking & monitoring system involving beneficiariesRisk - Water distribution not safe to access or not accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distributionMitigation - Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design and implementation phases.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

-Loss of cash or assets (operational): the risk owner: the team leader in each site - This continues to be a threat to all areas of operation inside of Syria - mitigation measures: daily inventory, control the treasury better, involve many people in this process to extend the circle of suspicion.-Unforeseen Changes in the Conflict- Risk owners: Senior Management Committee - each office - Conflict is such that organization cannot safely access locations, staff gets besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. Mitigation measures: Unlikely Moderate Stable Local information shows an increase in conflict levels, as we as direct reports from staff on the ground. “Continue to gather information from a range of sources. Develop contingency plan to work in alternative location if conflict continues. Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space.-" Kidnapping / Abduction - risk owners: Security Management/Program Management/Individual - risk mitigation measures: traveling in the day time - transportation in convoys - high coordination on movement tracking - deterrence though local authorities. -Exposure of sensitive information - Risk owners: "Security Management/Program Management/Individual Responsibility/Executive reinforcement of policies" - Communications with anyone inside of Syria are very likely compromised and we must assume that leakage of electronic data will occur. Results will include a Threat to the detention of individuals, reputational harm and compromise of community members or partner safety. Mitigation measures: strict procedures on staff in case there was information exposed. Less visibility of WATAN in critical environments.
