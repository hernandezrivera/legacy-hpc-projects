Project Summary

After more than 10 years of deadly conflict, explosive ordnance continues to jeopardize the lives of millions of people, obstructing not only the repair and access to socioeconomic infrastructure but also the delivery of crucial humanitarian aid. The operating environment is extremely unpredictable in Borno State, where all major supply routes have become dangerous due to the potential of attacks by non-state armed groups (NSAGs) and unexploded munitions, particularly improvised explosive devices (IEDs). In February 2022 (UNMAS) 85 explosive events were reported. Between 2016 and February 2022, there were 2,370 injuries and 8,575 deaths, with 1,387 civilians injured and 802 civilians killed. The use of explosive ordnance in civilian-populated areas is widespread in Borno state, but there was a significant increase between January and March 2022, with 163 casualties documented. The increased use of IEDs by NSAGs is due, in part, to the re-opening of the Maiduguri-Damboa road in February and the removal of the requirement for armed escorts along the Dikwa-Ngala highway, where attacks have increased since the beginning of the year, as well as NSAGs' prioritization of asymmetric tactics in response to BSG advances. (Protection Analysis Update April 2022). Under the proposed project, YAF would execute humanitarian mine action activities in Banki, Damboa, Monguno, Ngala, Gujba, and Michika LGA of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States through its technical unit, to improve the protection environment for impacted populations in the region. This intervention will concentrate on Explosive Ordinance Risk Education (EORE), which aims to mitigate and prevent injury caused by EO through effective awareness creation. The initiative would benefit a total of 5300 beneficiaries. 3550 farmers, scrap metal collectors, wood collectors, Community Stabilization Committees (CSCs made up of women leaders, community leaders, and Religious and youth leaders), and 1750 direct in-and-out of school children in IDP camps and host communities in the activity locations.

2 - Activities

The Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) intervention aims to ensure preventive measures by sensitizing and informing vulnerable people about the potential threat posed by explosive ordnance (EO) and providing them with information and knowledge on safe behavior as well as whom to event that EO is discovered in their communities. YAF proposes to provide direct EORE training to 35 adult target groups referred to as Community Focal Persons (CFP) involving farmers, scrap metals collectors, wood collectors, Community Stabilization Committees (CSCs composed of women's leaders, bulamas, and religious and youth leaders) and 175 in-and-out-of-school children referred to as Community Peer Educators (CPE). The Trained Community Focal Persons will be responsible for creating a cluster of 70-100 members for each respective target group (cluster of farmers, scrap metal collectors, etc.) within their community and conducting EORE sessions. The trained community peer educators will deliver peer-to-peer sessions to at least 10 peers within their community. The primary goals of this activity are to build local capacity to prevent incidents and to detect and report any potential dangers that may harm a community. All activities will be conducted through participatory facilitation by YAF's teams. 1. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

YAF will ensure that women, girls, boys , and men are mobilized separately to ensure sufficient absorption of information being relayed. Women and men will also be separated in sessions to ensure active participation while ensuring participants are comfortable sharing among members of their own gender. YAF in line with cultural sensitivity will hold separate gender meetings to strengthen acceptance by communities. YAF will carry out sessions in the community to ensure everyone can access and use community volunteers to carry out door-to-door mobilization to ensure all community members have access to information. YAF mainstreams protection throughout its programming, putting the “Do No Harm” principle at the center of its interventions. YAF has a robust protection mainstreaming package which it adopts for all projects, including training for all staff on protection principles and protection mainstreaming and the implementation of sector-specific checklists that support staff to monitor the extent to which the elements of protection mainstreaming are considered in each stage of the project cycle. The distinct roles and activities that are undertaken by women, girls, boys, and men in the communities affected by ERW determine the various level of exposure and the risks faced by each sex and age category. To be able to identify the pattern of risks faced by the various sex and age groups, YAF first conducts a baseline survey in the affected communities to understand the existing knowledge, attitudes, practices, beliefs, and norms of the affected population with regard to EO.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

During field activities, the YAF team creates awareness and sensitization about CRM, beneficiaries are informed how to access, what type of complaints to be raised, and the various feedback mechanisms available. YAF will maintain a database where all records of all complaints are registered, categorized, and escalated to the various stakeholders for quick resolution. The objective of the complaint and response mechanism is to ensure that complaints from beneficiaries are heard, recorded, and resolved in an effective and professional manner within a determined period of time, and any lessons learnt are incorporated to improve programming, ensuring effective and efficient delivery of assistance. In addition to rolling out the various feedback mechanisms, YAF will be meeting with various community heads or focal point to discuss and analyse the effectiveness of the complaint and response mechanism and the impact it has on our programs. Any concerns e.g., safety of the mechanism and accessibility can be addressed at these meetings with clear action plan and timelines. The beneficiaries are also informed about the Code of Conduct Reporting mechanism which allows for a range of different ways to report suspected misconduct by YAF staff. Reporting suspected misconduct may even be done anonymously. The complaint and response mechanism is based on values and guidelines that conform to the core principles of administrative investigations conducted by humanitarian organizations. The core principles include: The right to due process, the right to confidentiality, the right to protection against retaliation, the right to notification of status, the right to have a third -party present during an interview and the right to respond to the Report of Suspected Misconduct. YAF has a strict zero tolerance approach with regard to sexual harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse and also Fraud, bribery and corruption.
