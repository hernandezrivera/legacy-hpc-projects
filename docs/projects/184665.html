Project Summary

Somalia continues to experience effects of protracted crisis, with displacement of populations due to recurrent conflict, drought, COVID-19 pandemic and flooding, leading to concurrent shocks across the country stretching the already limited resources available & exacerbate sectoral needs in the IDP camps. The IDPs in the camps face challenges ranging from poor living conditions, overcrowding, inadequate camp design, disproportionate access to humanitarian aid, inaccessible sites, difficulty in leaving the sites at night especially for women and girls, and the shocks from COVID-19 effects and new displacements. Vulnerable groups namely women, elderly, refugee returnees, minority groups, youth and people living with disabilities (PLWD) are the most acutely affected, with PLWD facing barriers in access to services in the camps. Boys and girls face disenfranchisement in accessing essential services, including inadequate ground for playing and child-friendly spaces in the camps, further creating deteriorating and worsening situations for IDPs, with their protection, compromised and human rights violated. Poor coordination of the camps by the village relief committees or the camp leaders with inadequate capacity to manage the population in the camp, affecting the quality of service delivery. The returns of IDPs to their home areas of origin are extremely limited due to lost livelihoods and reliance on humanitarian services.
DKH and Implementing Partners (IPs) in Somalia, will continue supporting identified humanitarian needs for IDPs by establishing camp coordination structures at the site level that improve living conditions and protection of IDPs and ensure equitable access to services and assistance by all the affected communities through supporting camp coordination and camp management (CCCM) interventions in the IDP camps in Banadir, Bay, Hiraan, Galgaduud, and Mudug regions which continue to experience increases in new IDP arrivals in districts and camps that are already seeing living conditions below acceptable standards. The project enhances protection and improves the living conditions of IDPs in the camps, ensuring equitable access to services and assistance by the vulnerable and poor host communities affected by the humanitarian crisis. Newly arrived IDPs living in the target locations with be prioritized through a consultative approach with the other humanitarian actors and cluster members in the affected areas ensuring the most underserved areas are targeted and existing gaps in access to humanitarian services. At least 50 sites from the targeted locations and 30,000 people will be identified based on needs, in consultation with CCCM cluster members. DKH and IPs will prioritize activities such as services mapping and improved coordination provision of humanitarian assistance in IDP camps, site planning, preparation, coordination and management, community sensitization/mobilization on COVID-19 and promotion of participation of vulnerable groups (women, girls, youth and PLWDs), formation and strengthening capacities of Camp Management Committees (CMC), and establishment of community feedback mechanisms (CFM), and assessments as agreed upon with the cluster. Inclusion of PLWDs and minorities will be prioritized and identified through assessments and collaboration with stakeholders. The project will harness effective coordination between the host communities and IDPs; and reduce barriers to assistance, ensure access to basic services, enhance the accountability to affected populations, mitigate risks of SGBV, fire, and flooding, through consistent engagement with the CMCs and enhanced coordination with the existing authorities to negotiate for land tenure for the locations where evictions are rampant. DKH will coordinate with the CCCM Cluster member while administering activities that fill gaps in operationalizing durable solutions for IDP in camps. DKH will regularly document and share experiences with other stakeholders

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of cash enhances the decision-making and dignity of the beneficiary as they can make an independent choice, with the flexibility provided by cash, to purchase what they need. The project will utilize the cash for work (CfW) modality targeting vulnerable households to participate in the maintenance of the informal settlements ensuring hygiene, a clean and safe living environment while at the same time accessing livelihoods. While cash enhances the beneficiaries' income level, it also contributes to strengthening local market functionality with the injection of cash into the local markets. CVA are conditional to participating in camp cleaning campaigns. Further, cash transfer is quicker and more cost-efficient than providing in-kind commodities, in addition to the items purchased by households being culturally appropriate and suit their specific needs.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project has considered market functionality through tagging the CVA amounts distributed being dependent on the minimum expenditure basket, ensuring (MEB) values per region of interventions, as defined by the Somalia Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU). DKH will implement the project in an integrated approach where market assessments conducted and seasonality analysis under the food security and (FSL) sector among others will inform the CCCM project implementation decisions, especially the CVA approach. Under the FSL, DKH will consider the use of EMMA tools to enable detailed market analysis to support the market systems and to contributing to improving response analysis and programme implementation; and to better understand, accommodate, and make use of market systems. The use of these tools will therefore support consideration of market functions in the cash decision. The cash provided based on MEB will increase the beneficiaries' purchasing power to access livelihoods from the existing markets thereby supporting the local economy. The amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market.
