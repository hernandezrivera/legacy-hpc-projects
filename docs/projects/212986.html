Project Summary

In 2023, Syria faces a severe rise in malnutrition among children under five, exacerbated by inadequate diets and consistently elevated anemia rates. The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has tripled over the past five years, reaching 4.7%, with some governorates reporting emergency-level rates. The burden of wasting in children under five has surged by 40%, necessitating urgent life-saving treatment for over 500,000 children. Stunting rates have also risen, affecting approximately 650,000 children, with some governorates reporting unacceptably high rates exceeding 20%. Alarmingly, areas within certain governorates report catastrophic levels of stunting exceeding 30%. Children with disabilities face heightened risks of malnutrition and related health issues.
Exclusive breastfeeding rates have improved overall, but certain regions have seen a decline, with significant formula usage reported by caregivers. The dietary patterns of children aged 6–23 months have deteriorated, with only 1 in 10 consuming a Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD). Anemia affects at least 1 in 4 children, highlighting the impact of sub-optimal diets lacking essential nutrients. Diarrheal episodes among children have surged, with one in four affected, exacerbated by outbreaks of AWD/cholera and measles.
Teenage pregnancy rates are concerning, with one in five teenage girls either having children, being pregnant, or lactating. Wasting levels among women and girls vary across Syria, with some areas reporting prevalence above 5%, contributing to increased risks during pregnancy. Anemia prevalence is high among women, especially teenage girls.
Syria has remained within serious levels on the Global Hunger Index for over five years, ranking second within the region. Key drivers of malnutrition include drops in exclusive breastfeeding rates, suboptimal diets, limited access to health and nutrition services, increased morbidity, food insecurity, rising food costs, poor hygiene, and sanitation practices, and unfavorable environmental conditions. Addressing these factors is crucial to mitigate the malnutrition crisis in Syria.
The project aims to decrease acute malnutrition levels among vulnerable groups, improve treatment and recovery rates for Acute Malnutrition cases, and enhance the coverage and participation of the target population in nutritional programs. The Project will also contribute to improving children's feeding practices to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality among infants and young children in NWS & RATA by ensuring access to safe, adequate, and appropriate preventive and curative nutrition services. The program will focus on sub-districts with high GAM rates, identifying beneficiaries through 10 OTPs and 10 RRTs and using the CVA approach to reach the most vulnerable households according to the most recent CVA operational guidelines. The project will directly benefit 140,000 individuals, particularly vulnerable groups, pregnant and breastfeeding women, adolescents, and children aged 6-59 months.
Cross–cutting themes: Activities will prioritize gender equality, women’s empowerment, and social inclusion throughout the project cycle. The project will adhere to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Gender mainstreaming strategies, ensuring safe identification and referral of cases. Staff will receive training on gender mainstreaming strategies, enabling them to implement these principles effectively. WV maintains a zero-tolerance stance against Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA). Support will be provided for PSEA mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and caregivers, including technical assistance, policy review, and development with partners. Training will be conducted for frontline workers and field focal points, along with awareness-raising initiatives using child-friendly materials. Regular reporting on PSEA activities and incidents will be coordinated with other clusters and the PSEA network. Child safety concerns

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Through this project, WV and its partners will run 10 OTPs and 10 RRTs where the following nutrition activities will be provided:Therapeutic activities: - Admit boys and girls (6 - 59 months) having SAM without medical complications for treatment-Admit boys and girls (6 - 59 months) with moderate acute malnutrition for treatment-Admit PLWs with moderate acute malnutrition for treatment Preventative activities:-Provide micronutrient supplements (MNP) to children (6 - 59 months) for four months-Provide adolescent girls with micronutrients, including iron, folate, and Micronutrient tablets for six months-Provide PLWs with micronutrients including iron, folate for six months -Provide children 6-23 months with BSFP-Provide PLWS with CVA to enhance their nutrition diversification and consumption (according to the CVA 2024 operational guidelines) -Provide PLWs with skilled individual IYCF counseling Capacity buildingTrain relevant staff on the CMAM and IYCF topics * As this project covers the RATA area, therefore, the following are some risks that will be considered when designing the project: Risk 1: WV is perceived as supporting the Turkish government's plan to relocate Syrian refugees to NCS by providing services there, contributing to demographic change in NCS. As a mitigation action, WV will clearly communicate to all relevant stakeholders its commitment to humanitarian principles of providing assistance solely based on needs and will distance itself from any geopolitical agenda executed by the Turkish government or other actors. Risk 2: The project does not maintain gender equality due to a lack of women willing to participate in project activities, a lack of community acceptance of women's participation in specific activities, and exclusion of women from active participation in specific activities. To mitigate this, women will be consulted on the most appropriate location for the communal locations for project activities to help reduce SEA risk and increase their participation in decision-making within each of these sites. Risk 3: Health activities do not meet the technical standards due to a lack of qualified health staff in NCS. To mitigate this, WV will work on an additional option that consists of recruiting Syrian staff from Türkiye to operate in NCS. WV with its partners will assess this risk very early on in the project to take mitigation measures in a timely manner Risk 4: There is another risk when implementing a nutrition project is the RATA area which is the difficulty in securing and delivering nutrition supplies to the field through cluster support as the RATA area isn’t known to be under the mandate of cluster coordination. To address this, WV will ensure to intensify its coordination, communication, and advocacy to supply the area with lifesaving nutrition supplies.
