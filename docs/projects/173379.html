Project Summary

The project will support the returnees and host communities in Elfashir / Mellit and Um Kadada localities to support public health/nutrition services to fill the gap by rehabilitation of one health Clinic in Um Kadada, provision of medical supply, equipment’s, furniture, and solar system in addition to emergency materials, nutrition supply.
Returnees and IDPs communities in El-Fashir rural, Melit and Um Kadada has gap in health services and all of them has one main hospital in Elfashir, there are small public health centers, where all devastated during the past conflict hundred preset population displaced either to Elfasher or Korma IDPs camps. The population started return back to the village since 2017 after stability of security situation in the state due to the many factors of Land owners and most of them are farmers and nomads.
NPO through The International Organization for Migrants – IOM and WHO started to support the returnees and IDPs in the target localities rehabilitated and provided health & Nutrition services to the public health in Birka, Sarafya, Kuwim and Alabasi , that provided rehabilitation, medical equipment, furniture, provision of solar system and emergency materials.
Primary Health Care units to provide health services for returnees, IDPs and catchment population and nearby communities. Most population in the the target localities classified as returnees, IDPs and local communities, they are vulnerable and living under poverty. Birka, Sarafya, Alabasi and Kuwin PHC units are already maintained and well-equipped, but the need for qualify medical cadre , medicine with provision of nutrition facility for under-five and provision of reproductive health in addition to public health services. In addition to rehabilitation of PHC in Um Kadada with provision of health services.
The target localities in need of minimum basic service include immunization and treatment of malaria and pneumonia, providing medical supplies and drugs to returnees and local communities through PHC to deliver management and to control infection, in addition of OPD consultation, STI management, ANC and Postpartum care. The project also will support health education, to improve good practices and enhance health seeking behaviors to address malaria, pneumonia, diarrhea disease, measles, pregnancy related medical problems, vaccination services at health facility and acceleration campaigns in collaboration with SMOH.
The project will increase the awareness of EPI services and protective equipment including biological waste disposal boxes, incinerators, and gloves. The promotion use of iron rich foods and vitamin A and counselling supplementation provided to contribute the prevention of micronutrient.
The other part of the project will provide nutrition services starting by nutrition assessment to ensure of treatment and referral cases to conduct MUAC screening campaign to support OTP and TSFP with critical cases in nutrition centres in weekly basis. Providing of therapeutic foods, appetite test, admission and the child/mother to attend OTP and monitoring the management of children 6 -59 month and Pregnant Lactating Women -PLWs with CMAM protocol. The project will focus on sensitive nutrition activities as OTP, mother to mother support , Infant Young Children Food – IYCF , Therapeutic Supplementary Feeding Program - TSFP for children and Pregnant Lactating Women -PLW.
NPO is not part of national Health & Nutrition clusters, but working actively and part of Health and Nutrition sub-national coordinator in Elfashir. NPO committed to engage with National Health cluster and link with Nutrition cluster by participating actively in the clusters to attend sector meetings, and share NPO information which will support in coordination and to learn from cluster participation. NPO committed to report on 4Ws and accelerate efforts with cluster colleagues to achieve the strategic goals.

1 - Needs

Human health considers as the most valuable thing in life, on the basis of well health, the human will be able to do all activities, thinking contributions, efforts, and even the worship depends on the health and well-being, for that the project importance was raised. Especially in North Darfur State as the part of the war affected area because of Darfur conflicts, that has led families to be displaced from their villages and their habitat searching for shelter, food and medical care, which prompted many voluntary organizations to rush into Darfur States to provide health and relief services, that the Patient Helping Fund was one of them, and played a prominent role in providing relief contribution and health care.After the voluntary return since 2017 Elfashir rural, Melit and Um Kadada communities now returning to their origin side, by restoring health services that encourage many families to come back specially the situation now secure and their land is there will be cultivated again. Poverty affects negatively the primary health care of the children and the overall living standard in North Darfur generally and especially in Elfashir, Melit and Um Kadada localities. The target location Abassi B IDP camp , Birka, Sarfya, Kuiwm and Um Kadada Public Health Centres are classified as substance economic, this is due to people live in such situations as IDPs , returnees and host communities with low income and unable to meet their basic needs. This situation affected on children and women health in addition to high rate of mortality among under 5 years children. Abassi B , Birka, Sarafya, Kuiwm and Um Kadada Primary Health Centres PHCs aimed to provide adequate Public Health care services for both IDPs, returnees and settlement communities. The five facilities running by State Ministry of Health SMOH North Darfur providing basic Health services for about 40,247 individuals male and female in addition to provide nutrition to more that 24000 children This information according to survey done by NPO last in (November 2019). According to the study the common diseases reported by the two clinics as (malaria, pneumonia diarrhoea, typhoid, cold), there is nurses, medical assistance, pharmacist and midwives in place, but still there is a lack of medical doctor to support the clinic and serious lack in medicine, laboratory services, referral system for emergency cases although Elfashir town far from the proposed project location 40 - 110 kilometer. In addition to that Um Kadada PHC in need for fence and store for medicine also need a iron shade as reception for vaccination.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project main three outputs supporting by below activities to achieve Project objective : Increase equitable access to humanitarian lifesaving and life-sustaining basic health services for those residing in high risk areas and vulnerable population groups in North Darfur. Outputs : 1. Improved health at communities levels practices and enhanced health seeking behaviours. 2. Community outreach activities increased the service uptake, follow defaulters and problem cases and conduct screenings for malnutrition, screenings for vitamin A supplementation. 3. Promotes use of iron rich foods and vitamin A supplementation to contribute to the prevention of micronutrient deficiencies. The promotion and counselling activities will be provided at the entry point of PHCs. A. Health activities : 1. Provision basic health service package and secondary referral systems.2. Provision of medical supplies and basic need drugs.3. Conduction of training for community midwives on Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care. And training for hygiene promoters volunteer to disseminate hygiene promotion message.4. Conduction of health /nutrition awareness sessions addressing malaria, pneumonia and diarrhoea diseases. In addition to vaccination, important of EPI services and use of iron rich food.5. Establishment of mother to mother support groups.6. Provide personal protective equipment including biological waste disposal boxes, incinerators and gloves. 7. Compilation of weekly and monthly statistics to ensure good follow up.8. Support medical waste management and infection control at five PHC units.B. Nutrition activities:1. Conduct nutrition assessments in Kuiwm, Um Kadada & Alabassi communities, and use data for treatment and referral cases. 2. Support OTP and TFP and refer critical cases to Stabilization Centre service. 3. Support the transport of therapeutic foods and other supplies to Birka, Sarafya, Kuim, Alabassi and Um Kadada PHC units .4. Avail drugs, supplies and equipments necessary for CMAM program in coordination with SMOH. 5. Carry-out nutrition and health education sessions will be provided to project beneficiaries. 6. Provide training for CHWs and nutrition workers on malnutrition treatment protocols.7. Support CHWs to provide CMAM including prescription and distribution of therapeutic feeds to those who meet the criteria and scheduling a follow up for the well-being of the patients.8. Organize and conduct two health, hygiene and nutrition promotion campaigns for the target communities.9. Conduct MUAC screening for children aged 6-59 months and PLW and refer to TSFP if they fit the appropriate criteria at the target localities.10. Running the CMAM program, all identified cases will be immediately referred for treatment. 11. Promotion of good feeding practices to contribute to the prevention of malnutrition. 12. Distribution of health and nutrition messages through IYCF practices

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Mainstream protection throughout the planning and delivery of assistance to returnees and host communities. Through community-based protection approaches that put returnees and host at the centre of decision-making partners will promote community engagement throughout the Refugee Response. The annual Participatory Assessment process promotes participation through structured dialogue on the specific protection risks and assistance issues that different groups face. Gender inequalities are persistent across Sudan. The protracted nature of the conflict, particularly in Darfur states continues to reinforce pre-existing gender inequalities, with women having lesser opportunities. The exits records worrying rates of gendered violence especially sexual violence. The efforts to prioritize gender across the humanitarian response are in line with the government commitment to gender equality, visible through the advancement of laws such as the Criminal Act and the National Civil Service Act. These laws are related to the criminalization of the FGM and the right of abortion in the case of rape. The recent amendment is indicative of the possibility of a Sudan with more gender-equal laws and a better future for girls and women in the country. Taking lessons from the 2020 response, in 2021 will make efforts to address the needs of women, girls, men and boys through strengthening capacity on preparedness and making investments on systems and institutions which support gender inclusion. In 2021 humanitarian operational will continue efforts to ensure that the standards and commitments of the IASC Gender Policy are reflected in the response. Gender will be part of regular advocacy and key messages.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will ensure an effective response to refugees in Sudan the response plans will follow an Age, Gender and Diversity approach by using participatory methodologies to promote the role of women, men, girls, and boys of all ages and backgrounds as agents of change in their families and communities. Specialized mechanisms need to be strengthened in 2021 to address Protection from of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) to handle sensitive complaints.The plan will advance a collective in-country prevention and response approach to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) in addition to supporting individual entities evaluate and enhance their PSEA programmes. The PSEA Network will establish clear linkages with key sectors and actors (including affected populations) to implement the Joint Framework of Action for PSEA to include:• Ongoing capacity building and development of PSEA network members and focal points on specific measures and resources to enhance awareness amongst staff/personnel on PSEA; create and maintain an environment which prevents SEA;foster a common understanding on reporting. • Advocacy for stronger management and coordination of PSEA within agencies including through: (a) Incorporating PSEA focal point roles and responsibilities into work plans and performance evaluations; (b) Establishing codes of conduct where they do not already exist; (c) PSEA trainings for staff/personnel on PSEA. • Work closely with the GBV and CP sub-sector/actors to ensure that support for victims of SEA is integrated into GBV response services and fully accessible to them. Protocols and guidance will be developed to outline how agencies respond to victims of SEA and referral system.
