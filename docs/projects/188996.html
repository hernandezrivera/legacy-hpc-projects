Project Summary

This project will support flood & food insecure affected 100,000 beneficiaries (Men 15,000, Women 25,000, Boys 27,000 and Girls 33,000) in Jonglei state counties (Fangak, Canal/Pigi) and Unity state Counties (Leer, Rubkona and Guit) with improved essential healthcare services. HFO will under take responsibility to implement PHC services activities to achieve health cluster and Ministry of health sectoral objectives. The response will focus on providing basic essential health services in line with national and international humanitarian standards and is based on the epidemiological evidence of disease outbreaks, disease burden seasonality and trauma-related emergency issues. Prevention, preparedness, response readiness and mitigation activities will be supported with special attention given to providing vulnerable groups including women, children and person with disabilities. HFO in collaboration with the Cluster at the national, state Ministry of Health, County health departments and other implementing partners will support different intervention modalities suited to the needs of affected people, including the COVID-19 appropriate interventions. HFO and its health staff will continue prevention, preparedness and response readiness activities addressing Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) in Bentiu. HFO will link static with mobile health facilities and community-based healthcare systems (Boma health initiatives) to serve communities who do not have access to health care facilities (>5KM) as part humanitarian development Nexus (HND) during e,mergency that can wider coverage in Fangak County. HFO plans to further strengthen health technical staff, capacity and field coordination support to CHD, Boma health workers. HFO as a developmental partners will collaborating with development partners HPF and UNICEF partners who are providing regular health services through static health facilities in crisis affected areas by identifying communities that are still not reachable and providing services through 2 MTs in each county as well as strengthening 2 PHCUs/county and 1 PHCC in Fangak with improved access to essential health services will contribute to the overall reduction of the high maternal mortality rates and under-five mortality rates in women and children and mitigate the transmission of vaccine-preventable and other infectious diseases. HFO will involve affected communities and regular national, state and county health promotion, prevention, and control programs led by county health system in the planning and organizing of emergency health services. Our coordination mechanism at the national, state and County levels will be utilized to coordinate, collaborate, liaise with partners and authorities to implement, monitor and review the response activities. Mnanagement of SGBV as part of CMR and Referral pathways for mental health and SGBV survivors for psychosocial support. Maternal Neonatal and child health emergencies will be streamlined and strengthened. Referral pathways will link the community and facility-based health care system to provide continuity of care such as BHIs lingkage and engagement. HFO will encourage and support infection prevention and control (IPC) and WASH in health facilities including COVID-19 appropriate preventive measures. COVID-19 is expected to continue impact health services in the country for the foreseeable future. Frontline partners coordination mechanism at all levels will work towards comprehensive integration and collaboration with FSL, Nutrition, Protection and WASH partners on grounds. HFO targeted populations is informed by the intersectoral prioritization information analyzed, the health sectoral severity analysis and a review of disease infection risk by county. We will monitor and evaluate all these actvities through IDSR, 5Ws and DHIS2 to CHD, Cluster, WHO and MoH. HFO gets IEHK from WHO, MoH, CHD and PPE from IOM and WFP to support the response.

1 - Needs

South Sudan ten states’ health system depends mainly on humanitarian assistance (88%) and is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide primary essential life-saving health services. Poor health facility functionality and capacity, and limited coverage and hard access to existing system, have made it difficult to meet additional health needs posed by floods, food insecurity and frequent conflicts. Women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to healthcare services. Government contributions to health services was just 1.9% of 2021 national budget allocations with an under performing economy also contributing to poor health outcomes. Fangak County is inhabited by a population of over 167,230 people (NBS 2020). The entire County is affected by floods which also took up some airstrips including Old Fangak Airstrip. People have continued to struggle in making local dykes to prevent the remaining airstrips from being engulfed by the flood waters. Only 2 Primary health care centers (Keew and Old Fangak) of the 16 health facilities are providing the full package of BPHNS and the rest of the facilities are moderately functional causing inadequate access and unavailability of some health services to some communities in many parts of the county (DHIS reports 2021). 58% of the Bomas in the county are still not having Boma health teams (BHWs). Only 46% of the population can access health facilities at <5 KM radius or 3 hour’s time while 80% reported barriers in accessing health service exist. Fangak is among hot spot counties for Cholera that is already threatening due to damage of WASH facilities. Extremely low immunization coverage (<45% Penta 3 coverage) Maternal Mortality Rate remain a threat in the county because of lack of Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric neonatal Care CEmONC facility in the County. Canal/Pigi has only 2 PHCCs and are not fully functional and have lack of training health workers the facilities are operated by local staff with limited training no referral pathway for emergency cases. Diel population over 50,000 has been depending on a PHCU throughout two years (IRNA August 2021). Rubkona, Leer and Guit Counties have the same needs of flood and community violent where all villages and payams are flooded people have moved to small high ground (230,000 IDPS), health facilities are not coping with populations’ health needs; all these have made children, women PLDs more vulnerable to preventable diseases. Malaria is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 52.6% in 2021 according to MSF's reports on camps A, B, C D and E IDPs sites. Leer and Rubkona counties are now classified as cholera hot spot with a high risk of new outbreaks. Further, Hepatitis E cases rose to over 1,800 cases with 11 deaths and still remained above epidemic thresholds in 2021 (WHO/MoH-IDSR reports). While no new cholera outbreak has been declared since the end of 2017, the number of reported AWD cases are increasing across the country and 20 Vaccine Derived Poliovirus 2(cVDPV2), (59 cases reported since the outbreak began in 2020 of which 42% are female) and the threat of infectious disease outbreaks like COVID19 and EVD. Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, with 789 per 100,000 live births Only 39% women accessed Four+ Antenatal Care Services during pregnancy and skilled birth attendance are low at 12.3% and 14.7% (South Sudan SARA 2018 report). Access to BEmONC is very low and contributes to pregnancy related and new-born morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable populations. Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 yrs. Access to Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) remains inadequate Research study shows 41% of PTSD in Unity and Jonglei states. Gender inequality and gender-based violence are widespread Sociocultural norms and armed group presence.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

HFO multi-sectoral approach helps in ensuring that we meet most of the needs of the targeted beneficiaries including protection, inclusion and equality. Healthcare Foundation Organization during the initial stages of the projects conducts a general risk assessment and once the project is assessed to cause risks to both the beneficiaries and the organization, mitigation measures are always employed. In previous projects, mitigation measures such as clear and open communication with the beneficiaries will help in protecting the beneficiaries from being exploited, choosing locations for registration and distribution which are close and safe for the beneficiaries and an inclusive decision making process with the beneficiaries. HFO also works with the government and the community leaders to ensure that adequate information reaches the beneficiaries and the community leaders usually take responsibility of managing the crowds as the community members listen to them more. During distributions and registrations, vulnerable groups such as sick, disabled, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, children and the elderly are prioritized so that they spend little time in waiting. Confidentiality is also key to protecting the beneficiaries and HFO normally conducts thorough training for the staff before any activities in the field which includes data protection policies etc. 1. During consultation of OPD outreach, ANC, Immunization services, protection activities have to be considered and ensrureed by the team to keep an eye on the vulnerable groups and they are given high priority to get services under registration and treatment. It is very important no one is left under attended for. 2. By mitigating this risk, it important for the project to make this clear from the start that people with disability are part of decision making and any person with diability is identified and assisted to reach where services are or can be reached by services wherever she or he is, this can be done through outreach sessions and their representatives have to have information for where they can be reached easily. 3. The project will work collaboratively with all protection and GBV partners to mark referral pathways and ensure every survivor has access to the services. HFO will have to work with law enforcement agencies; such as police and local leaders to ensure everyone's right to services adhered to and observed for the purpose of accessing huamnitarian services. 4. Marginalized groups are the ones that bear the burden of humnitarian crisis where they are left under served and their problems are always ignored or dismissed. This project will ensure at the community levels women are at the front and during employment, both females and males will be both encouraged to participate fully during the implementation of the project. In so doing, the most marginalized person will definitely be in the picture. And the issues of marginalization shall be minimized.
