Project Summary

The aim of the Emergency Livelihood Response Programme (ELRP) is to improve food security in Luhansk oblast, among the communities most affected by conflict, including those affected by October’s 2020 wildfires, to enhance households food production, reduce the degree and frequency with which households depend on external food assistance, and strengthen their capacity to absorb shocks.
The wildfires intensified the impact of the conflict in the communities, resulting in a decrease of food production compounded by the additional challenges of economic recovery.
The ELRP will set the foundation for enhancing the long-term adaptive capacity of communities and strengthens the synergies among FAO’s short- and long-term strategies to build resilience; Linking the ELRP with FAO’s wider resilience programmes in Donetsk oblast will contribute to the strengthen of the East region adaptive capacity.
The ELRP is developed in accordance with the “New Way of Working”, acknowledging calls for integrated, context-specific, multi-partner actions led by local actors, as well as the adoption of joint programming with United Nations agencies wherever possible.
The project will target 3 000 households (6 840 people) from Luhansk oblast’s conflict affected villages located within the area of 0-5 km from the contact line, including those affected by the wildfires.
Households targeted to benefit from emergency livelihood support will be those facing from food insecurity that possess basic knowledge and skills in crop and vegetable production and have sufficient access to land and water. Priority will be given to the most vulnerable, including women-headed households and households affected by the wildfires.
• FAO Technical capacity
FAO food security and agriculture experts, emergency response officers, monitoring and evaluation specialists and area coordinators will ensure beneficiaries receive inputs that are tailored to their needs and agro-ecological zones.
• Results framework
Through the implementation of the ELRP, FAO aims to protect the livelihoods of vulnerable communities and enhance food production while strengthening their resilience. This will be achieved through the following outcomes: (i) protect the livelihoods of vulnerable households and enhance their food production; and (ii) strengthen absorptive capacity and build resilience at household and community level.
• Humanitarian–development nexus
FAO’s emergency response programme is part of the Organization’s broader resilience-building efforts, bridging the divide between humanitarian and development outcomes. Recognizing the role of humanitarian livelihoods interventions in building a foundation for resilience, the ELRP seeks to go beyond input provision where it is stable enough to do so and where partners are on the ground to deliver. It is expected that this will contribute to a longer-term vision for food security in selected locations, including the creation of better linkages between short- and long-term interventions to enhance impacts and contribute to longer-lasting outcomes. Community engagement will be enhanced to improve participatory planning, thereby increasing beneficiary ownership of the activities and strengthening FAO’s commitment to accountability to affected populations. This is expected to streamline needs identification, targeting and key messaging down to the local level.
• Implementing Partners
FAO, as the specialized agency of the United Nations for agriculture and key expert in food security, will implement the Project with the support of the local implementing partners (IP). Based on the established selection process, FAO will identify and select the local NGO to to organize supply of inputs, undertake community mobilization activities in the field, ensuring gender-sensitivity of the providers of community mobilization activities. For community-based trainings, FAO will procure services from academia institutions to ensure high quality of outputs.

1 - Needs

Six years after the beginning of the armed conflict in Eastern Ukraine, the situation still faces challenges to food security. The 2020 Joint Intersectoral Analysis has identified 1.5 million people remain in need of food security and livelihoods assistance in Eastern Ukraine, with females to be prevailing in those in need of assistance (70%). The impact of the conflict coupled with the adverse effects from the Covid-19 pandemic, that broke in the beginning of 2020, resulted in further deterioration of the food security situation and reduced resilience of the conflict-affected population, more vulnerable to unemployment followed by limited access to basic services and value chains. Markets are distant and inaccessible for most of the population living along the line of contact. This results in further destocking and in the deterioration of the already precarious food and nutrition security situation at household level.The wildfires that broke in September 2020, in the rural areas of Luhansk oblast, have worsen this situation and damaged or destroyed 600 houses, 950 household facilities and burnt more than 900 hectares of forest and dry grass, according to the State Emergency Service of Ukraine. This has affected the fragile food security situation of the population, now deprived from their core productions and income sources of berries and mushrooms, as well as pasture areas for livestock. According to reports produced by UN OCHA, wildfires have further caused animal losses, killing over 630 heads of livestock and 451 heads of poultry, impacting the farmers capacity maintain their domestic productions and harvest.The wildfires intensified the impact of the conflict in the communities, resulting in a decrease of food production compounded by the additional challenges of economic recovery. These effects have particularly impacting food security status of the rural population, for whom small-scale backyard farming continues to be a significant source of food. However, ongoing military operations, limited availability of transportation and broken logistics network makes it impossible for citizens in distant rural villages, especially those located within 0-5 km from the contact line, to replenish their agricultural inputs. This results in further destocking and deterioration of the already precarious food and nutrition security situation at household level. In this regard, provision of assistance in the form of agricultural inputs is urgently needed in order to safeguard the survival of the most essential livestock assets and to sustain households’ agricultural and food production. Further, to provide a stronger foundation for food security and resilience, it is critical to reinforce and build households’ and communities’ ability to absorb natural and man-made shocks and stresses, such as impact from the ongoing military conflict and seasonal wildfires.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1: Protect the livelihoods of the most vulnerable households and enhance their food productionFSLC Objective 2: Improve food security through the provision of emergency and time-critical agriculture assistance. FAO will continue its efforts to safeguard livelihoods and provide support to the most vulnerable rural population. Under Outcome 1, the Organization aims to support the most vulnerable to maintain or resume food production. Activities will revolve around the timely provision of inputs necessary to engage in crop, vegetable production and protect livestock assets as well as actively inform decision-making through community trainings, assessments, analysis, monitoring, and coordination. Output 1.1: Provide emergency livelihood kits to support vegetable/crop productionFAO will provide livelihood inputs according to seasonality, supporting crop and vegetable production. The implementation modality will be direct distribution of seeds of selected crop and five types of vegetable seeds and seedlings (e.g. eggplant, onion, tomato, cucumber, watermelon, pumpkin, potato, cabbage), allowing farmers to enrich their diets, improve their backyard farming and generate income from the sales of produce, contributing to keep the market supplied thus stabilizing prices. Output 1.2: Safeguard animal assets to enhance food and animal securityFAO will provide rural households with poultry kits, up to 30 heads live chickens, dual-purpose breed, and 1 package of concentrated specialized feed to intensify breeding for eggs and meat, to enhance domestic productions. This will be complemented by clear information packages about breeding best practices and basic prevention for common diseases. Chickens provide an important source of animal protein and can be raised in situations with limited feed and housing resources. In intensive poultry production systems, they are valuable contributors to the improvement of household’s food security and increase of the family’s incomes. The nutritious value of both eggs and poultry meat are vitally important for the nutritional security of families, especially for women (pregnant or breastfeeding) and children. Poultry breeding sustains maximum production for minimum costs at minimum risks. Outcome 2: Strengthen absorptive capacity and build resilience at household and community levelFSLC Objective 3: Enhance resilience of vulnerable people affected by conflict and/or COVID-19 through restoring of non-agriculture livelihood and promote resilience to shocks (including wildfire, unemployment, higher prices, and other hazard) at community level. In providing a stronger foundation for food security and resilience, it is critical to reinforce and build households’ and communities’ ability to absorb natural and man-made shocks and stresses. By enabling households to employ preventative measures and appropriate coping strategies, their exposure can be minimized to avoid permanent, negative impacts to their food security. Over the Project implementation period, FAO seeks to strengthen local preparedness and support communities’ production capacity in order to protect and improve agricultural livelihoods. Output 2.1: Increase local preparedness and resilience buildingInvesting in local systems – particularly in supporting local capacity – is key to ensuring long-term positive impacts interventions. FAO will provide community trainings on sustainable practices for crop production, vegetable production and livestock and poultry breeding feed and fodder production, trainings on extension and advisory services focusing one agricultural practice, to all targeted communities. Capacity building will support agriculture production and build community resilience to external shocks, in the longer term. Trainings will promote the understanding of how provided livelihoods assistance can be used as a foundation for resilience building.

FAO will continue to promote an accountability to affected populations framework that will engage seven commitments: strengthening leadership and governance to embed good practices; greater and more routine transparency; feedback and timely response; fair and representative population; accountability to affected communities mainstreamed in design, monitoring and evaluation; prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse; and collaboration with partners. This approach calls for transparent targeting which involves community engagement through participatory rural appraisal approaches to allow beneficiaries as well as non-beneficiaries to understand why they were or were not targeted. It is expected that this will help to reduce potential internal conflicts. Gender. Community resilience, as well as the elimination of hunger, cannot be achieved without closing the gap between women and men in agriculture and food production, as well as ensuring the protection of vulnerable women. Conflict, displacement, and the deterioration of infrastructure and basic services have helped increase the inequalities between men and women, resulting in increased labour of women. The ELRP seeks to help women as they help their families manage the consequences of crisis and food insecurity, providing opportunities for income generation and increasing access to nutritious food sources. Protection. Protection is mainstreamed throughout FAO’s Project, ensuring at-risk groups’ specific needs are taken into consideration and risks mitigated where and when possible. Those at risk include not only women and girls, but also men and boys, elderly, unaccompanied children, disabled, minorities and displaced persons. FAO contributes to the protection of individuals from gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse as part of its efforts to restore and promote sustainable livelihoods, such as mitigating gender discrimination in participation, decision-making and access to productive resources. Feedback mechanisms are shared with beneficiaries to enable channels for raising issues.Furthermore, Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training is provided to implementing partners and PSEA is embedded in its Letters of Agreement, and PSEA focal points have been nominated in all FAO offices to offer support, awareness creation on PSEA to partners, monitor and report cases.
