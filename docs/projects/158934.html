Project Summary

The proposed action is based on the specific objectives of the Food Security Sector: “The resilience, food security and productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected” and “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs.”, aiming at achieving the strategic objective 2 “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under International Humanitarian Law” and strategic objective 3 “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued” of the 2018-2020 HRP. Based on recent ACTED assessments, extensive consultations with key stakeholders, and following the recommendations of the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), Food Security Sector (FSS) and Cash Programming Working Group. The project will aim at providing emergency support to the most vulnerable HHs of Rafah, Khan Yunis and Middle Area, including ARA. Specifically, ACTED will restore the productive capacity of 185 vulnerable farmers, with a specific focus on the ARA, whose livelihoods which were damaged during the incidents occurring at the border since the Great March of Return in March 2018. The project will contribute to: improve and restore farmers’ productive capacity, households’ access to food security and contribute to increase communities’ resilience capacities, through cash-for-work implementation (555 workers) and cash-for-asset; increase access to food security of 185 vulnerable households located in the same communities, through the provision of emergency one-off cash assistance to meet their basic needs and improve their livelihood; strengthen 3 existing Community-Based Agriculture and Protection Committees (CBAPCs), established under former projects, through capacity-building. In total, 185 farmers and their households, 555 workers and their households and 21 Committee members will benefit from the action (4,238 individuals). The project is based on needs identified during an assessment conducted in September 2019 which has confirmed the urgent needs that ACTED will be addressing in the proposed project, thus contributing in resuming production activities of most vulnerable farmers in the ARA and increasing food security of their households. Eventually, the project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with specific focus on women and vulnerable groups, and a strong Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) component. In particular, beneficiaries will be heavily involved in the action through community-based empowered committees, and a comprehensive Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) will be deployed. Gender principles will be mainstreamed during the action and Committees will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases with a particular focus given to women and children issues such as related to GBV. ACTED Protection Officer will be focal point of the committees to receive and follow-up on cases. ACTED will implement this activity with its partner, the Arab Center for Agricultural Development (ACAD). The partnership between ACAD and ACTED has proven to be successful in projects implemented in recent years, and this project is based on strong experience from ACAD and ACTED in Gaza in rehabilitating water and land resources. In addition to this strong and effective partnership, the project will rely on extensive experience from both actors in the food security sector and in rehabilitating agricultural infrastructures and assets in the West Bank and Gaza.

1 - Needs

Multiple episodes of war, loss of lands, irrigation systems and agricultural assets, in addition to existing restrictions imposed on cultivable surfaces and import/export, lack of financial capacity and reluctance to investment led to the output of Gaza’s agricultural sector shrinking to below 5% of the GDP (World Bank, 2018). The ARA has been targeted over time to prevent or discourage access, and farmers are therefore unable or reluctant to invest in land in ARA. Around 95% of this land is arable but inaccessible due to Israel's "open fire" policy. The agricultural damages have affected the ability of farmers to produce their own food and their ability to furnish the local market with fresh produce, thus increasing the need for imports and the price of fresh products available. The scale of damages, high level of poverty and policies restricting the importation of raw material and agricultural equipment have prevented most of the reconstruction efforts. From March to September 2018, the Gaza Strip has witnessed significant increase of casualties and damages in agricultural lands located in the ARA. In consequence, many farmers, whose agricultural assets and lands were damaged or destroyed, lost their means of livelihood. Losses of the agricultural sector in Gaza had a significant impact on women’s employment, due to their limited share of agricultural holdings and limited economic opportunities to generate income. Even though female farmers constitute only 6.1% of agricultural landholders and 8.5% of livestock holders in Gaza (HNO 2018), there are structural barriers to agricultural land ownerships by women (inheritance rights, slow land reform, social attitudes). Therefore, land ownership does not reflect women role in the agricultural sector. Indeed, PCBS (2011) data indicates that 18.2% of economically active women work in agriculture and constitute 30% of agricultural employees in Gaza, against 8.5% of economically active men work in agriculture. In 2018, ACTED conducted a market survey and a qualitative assessment which confirmed constraints encountered by farmers: increase of food prices, poor irrigation and/or water quality and scarcity resulting in lower variety and quality of vegetables, need for rehabilitation of water ponds, lack of access to inputs and services reducing plant protection, production and profitability. In September 2019, ACTED conducted a new needs assessment in Rafah, Khan Yunis and Middle Area which demonstrated that those needs mostly remained uncovered. The assessment was conducted through qualitative/participatory approaches, it was led by ACTED AME Officer, with ACTED’s Protection Officer. Three FGDs with farmers (10-12 participants per FGD, in total 4 female farmers) were held in al-Shoka, Wadi al Salqa and Abasan al Kabira, while three KIIs were conducted, one with the Head of Plants Protection Unit at the Agricultural Directorate in Rafah, one with the Head of Operations in UAWC and one with the Head of Board of Directors of Rafah Farmers’ Association and the Public Relations Manager. The assessment indicated that small-scale farmers suffer from many challenges: high level of water salinity limiting crops production, reducing the life of the irrigation network and causing weak harvest; high cost of water transporting, lack of good irrigation water and high cost of transporting water due to the monopoly; increased numbers abandoning their lands increased due to the monopoly of few big farmers; crops damages; destruction of land and property during Israeli military operations and land incursions; lands impacted by chemical sprays/herbicides; low purchasing power of the consumers due to the political division that resulted on cutting the employees’ wages to around 50% of their monthly wage over the last two years; high prices and low quality of pesticides, fertilizers and seeds; lacks of financial support and high debts, lack of awareness and knowledge in the using of agricultural inputs.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 Identification of project beneficiaries through vulnerability and technical assessments. Beneficiaries will be identified through a call for application that will be widely advertised in public spaces across targeted areas, including supermarkets, administrative buildings and mosques, with the support of existing or established CBAPCs. After, ACTED and ACAD will set up selection criteria in order to prepare a list of farmers eligible to conduct the assessment. The level of vulnerability to food insecurity will be the primary selection criteria to prioritize most vulnerable groups (female-headed HHs, HHs including many children and/or people with disabilities). Vulnerability and technical assessment will be then conducted in the selected HHs to identify most vulnerable farmers and most urgent needs of rehabilitation. Once beneficiaries are selected through this vulnerability analysis, the project team will crosscheck the information obtained from the Ministry of Agriculture for the final validation. Activity 2 Urgent rehabilitation of agricultural water networks. ACTED and ACAD will launch a tender for the rehabilitation and maintenance of water assets in the selected farmers’ lands. In total, rehabilitation of 35 water ponds and 4 water wells to store water and minimize farmers’ dependency on main water sources will be implemented by hired contractor. Urgent rehabilitation of irrigation networks will be carried out in case of significant damages. Integrated solar systems for all ponds and pumps will be installed to minimize the impact of the electricity crisis. In total, 35 farmers will benefit from water ponds rehabilitation and 120 from wells reparation. Activity 3 Urgent rehabilitation of 370 donums of land and existing agricultural assets. 185 farmers will be supported in the rehabilitation of their land (2 donums/farmers) through a mechanism of cash assistance: - Cash for assets: this cash assistance will be conditional and restricted to the purchase of agricultural inputs needed for rehabilitation of 2 donums (small irrigation network components, fertilizers, seed). -Cash for works’ implementation: each farmer will receive cash for work of 312 USD (2 unskilled and 1 skilled workers per farmer) to support land rehabilitation and informal employment of community members. The amount has been calculated based on the equivalent cost of the kit installation by the Cash for Work modality, in line with the agreed FSS and CPWG rates. Cash-for-work modality will enable farmers to determine their own priorities and needs and will stimulate the local economy by creating short-term work and supporting local businesses through the purchase of materials. Workers will start rehabilitation works in the lands under direct supervision of the farmers and CBAPCs, with the support of ACTED and ACAD. Farmers will have to fill Site Attendance Control Sheet to be signed by the workers to confirm they received their wages. The final sheets will be validated by the CBAPCs. Activity 4 Emergency assistance via emergency cash. 185 selected farmers’ households will receive a first emergency one- off cash assistance (unconditional and unrestricted) of 300 $ to meet their immediate needs and support their living conditions. This cash assistance can be used to cover part of their debts or to purchase essential items or material to be used at household or livelihood levels. This cash assistance will particularly help women, including female-headed households’, to support living fees related to children education, including primary needs (food, clothes) but also education material. Activity 5 Capacity-building of CBAPCs. 3 CBAPC (21 members, 9 female and 12 male) will be strengthened through training on protection principles, food security, livelihood and SPHERE standards, best agricultural practices. Women involved in farming activities have been highly encouraged to participate in the CBAPCs.

3 - Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects and anticipate and mitigate any challenge. ACTED AME Unit will be in charge of measuring project outcomes related to the specific project objective falling under the 2018-2020 FSS HRP goals: “The resilience, food security and productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected” and “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs.” ACTED will measure the following indicators: #of farmers, herders receiving inputs or services; # of donums made productive or with improved production capacity; # of water wells rehabilitated or provided; # of m3 of water storage rehabilitated or provided; # of KW of renewable power supply system; # of beneficiaries supported through non-agricultural livelihoods; # of individuals benefiting from Cash for Work; # of individuals benefiting from other conditional and unconditional cash support; # of people reached by non-technical awareness sessions; % of female members participating actively in the community based agriculture committees (Target: 40%; Means of Verification: CBAC’s ToR and list of members, Training attendance sheet). In particular, ACTED Project Team will conduct a baseline and end line assessment to measure progress against pre-identified indicators, as well as beneficiary satisfaction, and draw lessons learned from the project. Those activities will enable gathering and reporting detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneficiaries and measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups (people with disabilities, female-headed households). ACTED will share the findings and results with the FSS so as to contribute to the wider humanitarian community’s understanding of food security challenges and issues in the ARA of the Gaza Strip, and disseminate best practices. Satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), managed by the AMEU, will enable to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM complying with HAP standards and ACTED’s SoPs. During the project, the CBACs will be responsible for monitoring the works and ensuring that protection standards are upheld and concerns are addressed or referred to the ACTED Protection Officer. Following the completion of the project, the CBAPCs will be responsible for monitoring and reporting any technical or protection issues in regard to rehabilitation of land and assets to ACTED, to ensure the sustainability of the project’s outputs and outcomes. In addition, ACTED Protection Officer, with the support of the CBAPCs will be in charge of monitoring the dissemination of protection principles as well as their concrete application during the implementation process through regularly visiting all beneficiaries to ensure that protection concerns are being respected and addressed through the works. Finally, ACTED Programme, Project Development and Finance Department will closely follow-up project activities and ensure the action is implemented in conformity with the HRP guidelines, IHP, as well as locally defined Food Security Sector recommendations. Using ACTED’s global Project Cycle Management tools, project team will report monthly to the PDD, on project implementation, outputs and data to ensure that all indicators are on track, delays are prevented and any difficulties are identified and recorded. Similarly, the Finance and Audit department will ensure a constant follow up of all procurement, expenses and transactions. Those monitoring activities will ensure the highest level of compliance and transparency.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneﬁciaries and communities will be ﬁrstly ensured through ACTED’s Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM), implemented in all ACTED countries of intervention, including oPT. The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. All beneﬁciaries of the action will be explained their right to provide complaints in conﬁdentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance, and will provided with small pamphlets summarizing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. It is worth mentioning that CRM relies on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, ﬁeld paper forms, compliant tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanisms, as well as mandatory timely feedback (within 15 days) to the complainants. This CRM will enable ACTED to identify and solve any issues reported by beneﬁciaries and non-beneﬁciaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanisms features several channels, including a dedicate hotline and frequent visits by ACTED Protection Oﬃcer to ensure that feedback is received from beneﬁciaries and, when preferred, conﬁdentiality is ensured and respected. Engagement and acceptance of community members is key component of the design, implementation and monitoring of ACTED projects. Beneﬁciaries’ satisfaction is continuously monitored by ACTED’s AME Unit, that regularly visits and interviews beneﬁciaries regarding ongoing activities. The AMEU will report any issues brought to its attention. ACTED has been implementing projects in the targeted governorates since 2010 and beneﬁt thus from a high level of acceptance in local Gazan communities. The project will be implemented in compliance with ACTED security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented if needed.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. Strong Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) and continuous communications with stakeholders, including community leaders and local authorities (with whom ACTED works with as well under other projects) as well as beneficiaries, are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict. It is worth mentioning that the project Protection mainstreaming relies on existing and solid ACTED’s policies such as Child Protection Policy and Policy against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by the staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms) allowing to sensitize all the stakeholders of a project and to ensure that the field teams maintain a respectful dialogue with the beneficiaries; implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams; identify risk factors on an ongoing basis and in particular throughout the cycles of the programs. All team members involved in the project implementation will undergo prior extensive training in Protection to ensure they engage with the beneficiaries and take into account the specific needs of all household members. Members of CBAPCs will receive refreshing training on protection and all beneficiaries will be informed on the correct usage of the assets received, in particular items which could expose beneficiaries to risks if misused. ACTED has been working in the targeted district for several years and is well accepted by the community members, which ensures smooth, easy and safe access to the area of intervention. In Rafah governorate ACTED is currently implementing an emergency-preparedness project “Increasing and strengthening emergency response and response of Rafah community” funded by OCHA. Throughout this project and former shelter, agriculture projects, ACTED developed and strengthened an approached based on community participation, engagement and empowerment, essential to build inclusive intervention and ensuring that “no one is left behind”. This community-based approach has been proven efficient based on former ACTED projects’ evaluations. Community-based Protection Committees established by ACTED under former projects play a key role in establishing a trustworthy relationship with the community-members and with liaising with the community for projects’ implementation and monitoring. ACTED and ACAD staff involved in the direct implementation of this project will be continuously based in Gaza reducing the risk of permits issues. ACAD has been working in the locations for several years and is well accepted by the communities, which will ensure smooth and easy access to the affected households. Therefore, no access constraints preventing the implementation of the project are foreseen.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize its effects on beneficiaries, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have different needs and assistance will be adapted to beneficiaries’ needs. ACTED’s Protection Officer will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages, solid family and community support networks, accountability through accessible Complaint Response Mechanisms, adequate referral to other organization for individual protection assistance.An additional effort will be done to refer special cases to other organization working in health, mental health, awareness raising and protection. Women involved in farming activities in the targeted areas will be approached and highly encouraged to participate in these CBAPCs, as they represent a significant but underserved fraction of the agricultural workforce in the target areas. Committees’ members trained in protection principles, standards, prevention and response and SPHERE, accountability to affected population. CBAPCs will be involved throughout the whole project to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level and guarantee a better compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups (female headed households, disabled, elderly). Committees will be tasked with hosting awareness raising sessions in the targeted communities to disseminate information given during training sessions, which will contribute to ensure the assets rehabilitated through the action are efficiently and safely used and maintained, in particular the fertilizer, which could expose beneficiaries to risks if misused. This “waterfall” capacity building approach empowers the committees to replicate their training and transfer their knowledge on maintenance and protection to the beneficiaries. Vulnerable groups such as women and people with disabilities will be prioritized during the selection process. During the beneficiaries’ selection and registration, beneficiaries will be actively involved and ACTED’s Protection Officer will ensure that people with disabilities’ needs are prioritized and will identify the priorities for protection throughout the whole project. In order to ensure that all the needs of each household member, especially the most vulnerable individuals such as women, children, elder people and people with disabilities, will be taken into account during the rehabilitation works and throughout the whole implementation , ACTED Protection Officer will conduct additional visits to ensure that the responses to specific needs, regarding gender, age and disability, are integrated and the needs of female members are properly take into account. CBACs will also be responsible of mainstreaming and guaranteeing the inclusion of protection principles during the work of land rehabilitation, especially when related to female-headed households’.
