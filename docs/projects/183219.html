Project Summary

The onset of the political and economic crisis combined with the COVID-19 outbreak and general lockdowns, further deepened vulnerabilities of all populations residing in Lebanon (Syrian/Palestinian refugees, migrants, Lebanese), affecting a much larger population, including those living outside previously recognized most vulnerable localities. The deepened vulnerabilities and rapid impoverishment of families, combined with lack of access to basic social services, social safety nets and livelihood opportunities amongst displaced populations and vulnerable Lebanese, contributed all together to an alarming deterioration of the protection situation of children and women.
As a general objective, UNICEF, together with its partners, aims at increasing access and quality of CP/GBV services through strengthening the community-based protective and supportive environment free of violence, exploitation, neglect and abuse.
Based on the above, UNICEF will work in collaboration with line ministries, UN agencies and partners to address the psychosocial needs of more than 40,000 affected girls, boys, women and their families, using a multi-layered approach through the provision of an integrated package of services, including community-based and more focused psychosocial support activities, case management and access to specialized services, such as mental health psychosocial support services.
Moreover, UNICEF will provide emergency cash transfer to approx. 20,000 individuals, primarily children, living in the poorest and most vulnerable areas of the country. The programme will be linked to broader services that vulnerable households will need, particularly child protection. Extensive community engagement, outreach and communication on the programme, efficient payment channel, and clear grievance and accountability mechanisms will be crucial.
All services will be provided through face to face and remote modalities.
UNICEF will also include timely and consistent AAP mechanisms (Accountability to affected population) to ensure that the programme responds to girls', boy's and caregivers' needs.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

To address the worrying CP/GBV trends, and within the scope of the Lebanon Emergency Response Plan, UNICEF, with support from its implementing partners, and in close coordination with the Child Protection working Group, SGBV Task Force and the Case Management taskforce will: - ensure provision of sustainable, quality, and integrated continuum of social services and actions to address specific CP and GBV concerns among the most vulnerable girls, boys and women including dissemination of referral pathways, psychosocial support, case management, and referral to specialized services including legal, medical, mental health, etc.; - ensure the presence of a strong multi-sectoral referral system that aims to maximize outcomes for children and women, including provision of emergency cash transfer to approx. 20,000 individuals, primarily children; - promote agency of girls, boys and women to better protect themselves and address social and behavioral drivers within communities. The envisioned activities will ensure outreach to the most vulnerable populations residing in Lebanon, including migrants/migrant workers and their children, Syrian/Palestinian refugees and Lebanese not previously targeted through LRP, and equal access to comprehensive CP/GBV services to girls, boys, women and men without any exclusion criteria.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

UNICEF is an active member of the National PSEA TF and has set up a number of mitigation measures to ensure the prevention against SEA. Under this project the following will be ensured: - Review of internal complaints handling procedures to ensure existing feedback mechanisms are functional and can respond to reported issues- Ensure affected communities, especially women and girls, are consulted on changes to complaints and feedback mechanisms, while ensuring that changes are communicated to affected communities in appropriate formats.- Promote safe, confidential complaints and feedback mechanisms with affected communities, making increased efforts to ensure those who are particularly at risk are aware of and have access to safe and confidential means for them to report concerns over sexual exploitation and abuse and other types of misconduct.- Continue roll-out of mandatory PSEA trainings for UNICEF staff and for partner staff and ensure adherence to PSEA standards and guideline.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

To strengthen its AAP efforts, UNICEF systematically incorporates processes for affected people to promote participation and equitable, two-way communications between communities and service providers. Moreover, UNICEF builds on existing planning, monitoring and evaluation capacity in development, humanitarian preparedness and response work, before, during and after a crisis. By leveraging local, national and international partnerships aimed at expanding the reach and scope through coordinated responses, UNICEF continuously supports vulnerable and affected people to strengthen their resilience, the capacity of service providers and authorities to respond effectively to affected populations needs. More specifically, under this project, through its outreach work UNICEF will increase access to timely, accurate and reliable life-saving information to the most vulnerable population in urban and rural areas and strengthen community engagement as agents in their own survival and recovery.
