To Scale-up and sustain minimum food consumption needs through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance among conflict-affected households (HHs) living in Northwest Syria (NWS)

Basic Info

Start Date

Project Summary

The project's purpose will be achieved by distributing Monthly Cash for Food Assistance, Food Baskets, Free bread to the most vulnerable HHs in NWS, in addition to bakeries rehabilitation and Agricultural inputs distribution to farmers. Through providing food security assistance, emergency food assistance, free bread and Agri inputs, and rehabilitation of food production facilities, the intervention will improve food security and enable current and newly displaced HHs and vulnerable communities to meet their immediate multifaceted food needs with dignity and flexibility, and will further support the overall intervention goal by addressing life-saving needs and promoting resilience.
SR will enable 120,000 vulnerable individuals (20,000 HHs) to meet their basic food needs by providing Monthly Cash distribution (cash for Food) over 8 months. Each HH will receive $75 per month (as per FSL cluster recommendation). Due to the deteriorating status of both IDPs and host communities, SR will target both groups. To ensure that the most vulnerable HHs selected, SR will conduct 100% door-to-door registration and verification and select the HHs according to SR vulnerability scoring system. SR will undertake secondary verification of a random sample of the selected HHs.
Alongside the monthly cash distribution and where the markets are not functional, SR will target 60,000 vulnerable individuals (10,000 HHs) with Food Baskets for 8 rounds. Each FB will cost 80$ (approximately 1650 Kcal per person/day, 70-78% of the daily food intake per person/day). SR will follow its procurement policy to purchase the FB needed quantities. To ensure that the most vulnerable HHs selected, SR will conduct 100% door-to-door registration to both HC and IDPs, and select the HHs according to the SR vulnerability scoring system. SR will undertake secondary verification of a random sample of the selected HHs.
In addition, SR will contract with bakeries to provide approximately 1,800,000 bread bundles with a weight of 1000 gr and will distribute to 30,000 IDPs individuals free of charge for 6 months based on the minimum daily needs (330g bread/person/day). On the other hand, 2,000 farmers will benefit from the distribution of agricultural inputs; the inputs will include seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, irrigation costs and equipment.
Besides that, SR will do a rehabilitation process for 50 bakeries in the most server areas, where no functioning bakeries to support the people in meeting their bread daily needs.

3 - Needs

According to HNO_FSA_Final draft, The food security crisis in Syria is indeed ”a protracted crisis”, and remains characterized by very complex drivers and contributing factors, from economic worsening and weakened local currency, to drought-like conditions and severe agro-climatic fluctuations, water scarcity and limited energy supply and population movements. The food security situation of Syrian families continues deteriorating which will lead to a possible increase in the number of food-insecure households in 2023.Substantial harvest losses combined with chronic economic deprivation and displacement have contributed to mounting food insecurity and associated negative food and livelihood coping strategies for vulnerable Syrians. According to MAAR, the total area planted with wheat in 2021/2022 was 1.2 million hectares (down 25% compared to 2021). Furthermore, the harvested area is less than 60% of the total cultivated area.In north-east Syria, according to the MSNA, a total of 79 per cent of the surveyed households reported having insufficient means to meet basic needs. Nearly half (43 per cent) of the households surveyed mentioned a lack of income or access to income as being the main barrier to meeting their basic needs.

4 - Implementation of activities

SR will provide the assessed food insecure people with monthly food assistance through the distribution of 8 rounds of Cash for Food (75$ per HHs) to 120,000 individuals, 20,000 HHs, 8 rounds of Food Baskets (approximately 1650 Kcal per person/day, 70-78% of the daily food intake per person/day) to 60,000 individuals, 10,000 HHs, Supply 1,800,000 free bread bundles approximately (1000g) that will be produced by contracted bakeries and distributed to 30,000 individuals, 5,000 HHs (330g bread per person/day according to the FSL minimum needs recommendations) for 6 months, distributing of agricultural inputs – The inputs will include seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, irrigation cost and equipment; along with related training to 2000 farmers, and light rehabilitation of 50 bakeries in the targeted locations.SR organization will follow its standard operating procedures and policies to implement the proposed activities; SR field team will register all the families and implement a verification process to ensure all potential beneficiaries are in line with SR eligibility and vulnerability criteria. In addition, all recruitment and procurement processes will be according to SR policies that will ensure transparency and equal opportunity for all project stakeholders. SR MEAL team will be involved in all the response stages, from registration mentoring activities and beneficiaries verification to baseline/endline surveys and PDMs reports.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

SR will do many actions to mainstream protection and gender across all project stages as following: SR conducted a rapid needs assessment by using Focus Group Discussions (FGD) with males and females separately as well as the most vulnerable groups such as disabled and PLW to ensure the program design meets the exact need for beneficiaries to avoid doing harm in the implementation stages. Female staff will be hired for distribution/redemption and MEAL to ensure equal access for all beneficiary categories including females headed of household, girls and widowed. Beneficiaries will be clearly informed that all distributed value-based vouchers are free and that cash distributed is provided with no obligations on their end. Options for home delivery of the needed food commodities for vouchers which will be redeemed on behalf of vulnerable persons (e.g., PWD who cannot access the markets, elderly, etc.) confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system will be designed and beneficiaries will be clearly informed where to refer or report incidents or violations. The day, time, and location of the distribution of the vouchers will be discussed with different community members and leaders to determine the safest most accessible location to allow all individuals including women, children, and the elderly to access the service with dignity and without exposure to further risk of harm; moreover, the list of contracted markets, allowed food commodities, and the redemption


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risks: 1-SEA risk against beneficiaries especially women 2-GBV Risk against beneficiaries 3-Not equal access for services from marginalized groups such as disabled and women (Bias and discrimination in distributions process) Mitigating Measures: All staff and vendors will be trained, acknowledge, and sign a code of conduct for sexual exploitation and abuse, moreover, SR has zero-tolerance against SEA. PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (PSEA posters will be distributed to beneficiaries, PSEA banners will be placed at the distribution site, PSEA messaging/videos and hotline information will be shared with beneficiaries through SMS/WhatsApp). All project staff will be trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies, to ensure full adherence with the Do No Harm principle and to stress that PSEA and GBV issues will be dealt with firmly and expeditiously. Female staff will always be present at the distribution/ redemption places and involved in the distribution process All markets populated by beneficiaries will have access to educational posters/banners for the appropriate use. Ensuring safe and equal access to inclusive, accessible, and non-discriminatory list of markets to get their food assistance. Protection cases including GBV will be referred to the respective focal point NGOs in the respective locations and sensitive data will be handled and kept confidentially with the PSEA field FP.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Operational R: A-Markets are not functioning which may affect the voucher redemption, Likelihood: M, Impact: H B-Exchange rate fluctuations between USD and TRY and SYP which affects the price in NWS, Likelihood: H, Impact: M C-Lockdown due to Spread of C19 pandemic in NWS. Likelihood: M, Impact: H. Mitigation measures: 1-Regular price monitoring and markets assessment to ensure this intervention will not cause harm to the local markets 2- Monitor REACH Report for SMEB price monitoring 3- All voucher value will be loaded in USD and all contracts with service providers will be signed in USD 4- SR will apply precautionary measures which are in the FSL guidance about COVID-19 as well as WHO recommendations for staff and contractors in terms of hygiene practices, use PPE, and maintain physical distance for safety. Food quality R: Poor Food Quality, Likelihood: M, Impact: M. Mitigation measures: SR will do a lap test on samples of all goods provided by the supplier to make sure that the food items specifications are in line with Sphere and other humanitarian standards, and relevant UN Cluster guidelines.
