Project Summary

In order to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in line with cluster objective 2 (CO1). The project will use Cash & voucher-based transfers to address food security/ nutrition objectives. The project will provide conditional cash transfers based on participation in investments in productive assets, training, or communal work groups and planning committees. Conditional food assistance through food for assets (FFA) modality will also be used.
In line with cluster objective 2 (CO2), the project aims at enhancing and sustaining emergency food production. It provides complementary main season crop seeds and tools, vegetable kits and fishing kits that promote production for own consumption and sale and improve access to food. The project will utilize the humanitarian pipelines for crops, vegetables and fisheries kits from the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). The project targets will also provide of timely food security information. The project targets returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host community especially female, child & male headed households.
Cluster objective 3 (CO3) ensuring household reduced dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks through seed security and seed multiplication interventions, seed fairs and supporting agriculture value chain addition. The project will also aim to train and capacity building of on-farm production such as farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots, income generation (business skills), vocational training in addition to natural resource management trainings. On farm training will focus on improved agronomic practices, crop and livestock pest and disease control such as FAW and desert locusts and reduction in postharvest losses and introduction of appropriate preservation technologies (e.g. hermetic storage bags). To ensure diversification & restoration of livelihoods small grants for petty trade; village saving & loan schemes & micro finance interventions will be implemented. The project targets an estimated 10,500 HHs mostly returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host community with the interventions.
All agricultural and fisheries activities will be implemented in collaboration with the State Ministry of Agriculture in order to ensure that government extension workers benefit from and also contribute to the technical components that will be offered by the project by so doing building capacities that will continue to be utilized after project closure. Community based extension workers such as lead farmers will be trained to continue supporting other farmers during and after project closure.

1 - Needs

• The political crisis has resulted in massive fighting among rival military groups and inter communal conflict causing insecurity countrywide, massive displacement of men, women and children and loss of livelihoods assets.• According to the IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis for Dec. 2020, an estimated 6.35 million people (52.6% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 2.1 million people faced Emergency (IPC 4) acute food insecurity. • The States with the highest number of counties in Emergency (IPC 4), projected at the peak of the 2021 lean season are Jonglei and Upper Nile. Akobo, Aweil South, Pibor and Tonj North counties have pockets of populations in Catastrophe (IPC 5) acute food insecurity at one or more periods between the end of 2020 and mid-2021• Fighting in Lainya, Kajo-keji and Morobo in Central Equatoria led to suspension of activities, ambushes of humanitarian convoys, looting of critical supplies and violence against humanitarians. • Malnutrition needs are greatest in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Lakes, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazel states where number of acutely malnourished children under-five and PLW caseloads are highest, also with more than 50 per cent of their respective population facing Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity.• The direct and indirect impact of COVID-19 and related pressure put on markets and the movement of goods; an upsurge in sub-national violence, especially in Jonglei; desert locust invasions in the east of the country; recurring flooding in areas still recovering from the 2019 floods; a protracted macro-economic crisis; higher food prices; and a devaluating local currency are all threatening the food security of people. Other factors that will influence the food security situation include the seasonality of livelihoods, grain stocks, prices, climate, movement of livestock, nutrition status of people, and ongoing conflict.• The counties of greatest food insecurity severity are found in locations where the compounded shocks are exceptionally amplified due to intensified sub-national violence, two consecutive years of widespread flooding, indirect effects of COVID-19, and a protracted macro-economic crisis resulting in record high food price spikes which have severely impacted people’s livelihoods and access to food from markets, livestock and farming.• Low purchasing power of households has been due to depreciation of the South Sudan Pound (SSP). South Sudanese Pound weakened and touched the record rate at 750 per US$ on 14 Oct 2020. The abrupt depreciation of the South Sudanese Pound contributed to the above normal increase in prices of food items across markets, especially for exclusively imported items such as wheat flour, sugar and vegetable oil.• Low crop production caused by prolonged dry spells, floods, crop pests and diseases, and shortage of seeds and agricultural tools as well reduced access to livestock products as animals migrate in search of water and pasture• Chronic pattern of food insecurity has eroded livelihoods, disrupted social safety-nets and negatively affected the resilience of affected populations. This is compounded by already existing factors of low seed and tools access, monoculture, flooding, dry spells, low area under production, lack of land use planning, weak state institutions and poor governance.• Deterrents associated with limited and lack of means for income generation and limited access to markets, have been worsened now due to insecurity and poor road conditions• As a way to cope, households (mainly female-headed HHs) engage in negative strategies such as reducing number of meals, eating less preferred foods, sending children to beg and selling livelihood assets. Income generating options are scarce and unprofitable due to inflation and most vulnerable HHs engage in casual jobs, selling, petty trade and firewood/charcoal further adding to environmental degradation

Prior to the rainy season in locations bordering constant water sources or during main season interventions. • Emergency food & livelihood support such as fishing kits and vegetable kits that are fast maturing to the displaced and returnee populations. • Poultry & goat/sheep restocking through livestock voucher fairs • Market monitoring – biweekly collection of market data on prices, supply and availability on key food and non-food commodities/rainfall data/livestock and crop disease outbreak and pest outbreak informationCO 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19• Training on the use of the vegetable and crop kits received• Training on vegetable production including low cost cultivation techniques, post-harvest management and market linkages• Fisher folk training in good fishing techniques, preservation, processing and marketing and on the use of fishing kits received • Female-headed HHs and fisher folk supported with cash grants to start IGAs. Some markets in Jonglei, Unity, Warrap and Northern and Western Bah rel Ghazal are functional and NRC will target this market-based activity in these areas - during the hunger gap• Supporting small scale farmers on group formation, value addition to their produce and linking them to markets and agro-input dealers and other value chain actors • Cash for Work to support community assets, such as secondary dyke rehabilitation to control and prevent floods to address land degradation and siltation of rivers and streams, feeder access roads, fish ponds etc.• Crop disease control including training of farmers and surveillance on Fall army worm (FAW) and Desert Locusts control through Community based surveillance and Early Warning• Train land, water, forest and range users in sustainable land and water management practices and introduce alternative resource-based conflict resolution mechanisms• Training farmers on grain/seed harvesting, storage and support through traditional granaries and hermitic bags• Facilitate formation of VSLA groups for training and supporting with start up capital/kits

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

NRC conducts market functionality assessment prior to designing any cash or voucher interventions. Cash and vouchers will only be considered where local markets are functioning and able to meet demand. NRC also conducts security risk assessment and price monitoring in addition to market functionality assessments prior to any cash programming interventions. In locations where there is no market integration and where markets are not functioning NRC will not conduct cash/voucher transfers in the locations. NRC has comprehensive cash based intervention standard operating procedures with guidelines on decision making process for cash interventions. Teams work with protection staff to conduct a comprehensive protection risk analysis prior to any cash programming, especially to vulnerable people in meaningfully accessing distribution sites (including safe return from distribution points, and ability to safely store cash). Protection analyses, based on consultation with affected communities and especially vulnerable people, will seek to balance the needs to target the most vulnerable, especially women, with the risks of offering them cash resources. NRC teams and other protection partners will work with communities and beneficiaries to establish plans to mitigate and address protection risks.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The proposed intervention will use cash and vouchers where local markets are functioning and able to meet demand. Cash transfers serves as an appropriate and powerful alternative to in-kind direct distributions of food and non-food items. Cash and vouchers are also more dignified and flexible form of assistance, since beneficiaries have the freedom to choose what they need. Cash/voucher can also be delivered in a timely and cost-effective assistance to crisis-affected populations, while at the same time supporting the local economy.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

NRC takes prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse very seriously. All NRC staff engaged in the project will be trained in PSEA; further, staff will be trained in PFA and identification of potential survivors of SEA, and receive training and mentoring in how to respond appropriately and with empathy. All communications with affected populations will stress their right to receive services without coercion or exploitation. NRC will work with communities to develop confidential, appropriate complaints and feedback mechanisms, and respond to any complaints promptly and transparently, to the extent that we are still able to assume both the survivor and perpetrator of their safety. NRC will strive to recruit gender-balanced teams and take every effort to ensure that vulnerable women are able to work directly with competent woman staff. NRC field staff will also provide awareness sessions on humanitarian principles and NRC code of conduct especially focusing on the prohibition of sexual exploitation and abuse amongst the beneficiaries and community at large.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NRC complaint and feedback mechanisms that is designed to use appropriate and accessible communication methods that older women and men, including those with disabilities, will be used to analyse the service gaps and whether the humanitarian services are safe and accessible. Some of the specific unique features with the complaints feedback mechanism to ensure accessible for people with disability could include:• Consulting older women and men, including those with disabilities, using accessible communication methods on their needs, service gaps and whether available humanitarian services are safe and accessible• Establishing community-based complaints and feedback mechanisms that uses a variety of accessible communication methods. • Analyse and use feedback from older women and men and PWDs on a regular basis, for example, as part of monitoring, to support adaptive programming and to redesign interventions that are inaccessible or inappropriate for older people and PWD’s needs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The NRC response approach strengthens multi-sectoral planning and delivery, mainstreams protection activities across the strategic objectives and focuses on strengthening AAP. A robust inter-sectoral approach has been put in place to ensure that targeted populations and beneficiaries feel informed and consulted throughout the entire humanitarian programme cycle. Through a targeted community communication and engagement plan, the project aims to protect vulnerable communities in high-risk areas from sexual exploitation and abuse. Population groups targeted to receive assistance should know and use established complaint mechanisms throughout the entire programme cycle. In addition, project grant opening/ project inception meeting with staff, key stakeholders, government and the donor (FAO or WFP) to launch the project and create common understanding of set outputs and monitoring systems. The project will ensure all beneficiaries and relevant stakeholders, including the most marginalized access information about assistance to be provided in such a way as to maximize meaningful participation and consultation. Information shared will include the scope of the proposed interventions, entitlements of those involved, clarify any targeting of vulnerable groups, and information essential to accessing companion services, as well as other relevant information. NRC will consult with communities about the optimal way to share information, and provide information in forms accessible to those with limited literacy and in local languages, to the extent that it is possible. This will involve using different communication channels and forms taking precautions to make the project as transparent and inclusive as possible will both service to make the program more inclusive and contribute to a sense of local ownership, as well as mitigate possible negative consequences of targeting or prioritizing vulnerable persons and minority groups. The project intervention disaggregates needs, interventions and reporting by gender, age, disability or population group

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will offer several complains and feedback mechanisms, depending on the needs of the community. In initial assessments, NRC will consult with targeted persons about the best approach to establish CFMs and strive to be responsible and flexible in our implementation. NRC will establish information provision and community feedback and complaints response mechanisms such as pre-activity meetings with reduced participants for each meeting, and a toll-free hotline to allow communities to voice their feedback and complaints to NRC regarding the implementation of the project. Targeted communities will be sensitized on the utilization of the established complaint mechanisms. The community feedback accountability mechanisms will be managed by the project M&E Officer. On a monthly basis, an “accountability report” will be generated for all complaints received and will seek solutions to those complaints from the relevant staff. The project staff will also be required to prepare a monthly response to the actions taken to address the issues raised from the previous report. Analysis and use of this complaints and feedback mechanisms on a regular basis, for example, as part of monitoring, to support adaptive programming and to redesign interventions inaccessible or inappropriate for older people’s needs.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

NRC field staff will provide awareness sessions on humanitarian principles and NRC code of conduct especially focusing on the prohibition of sexual exploitation and abuse amongst the beneficiaries and community at large. Beneficiaries will be made fully aware on where to submit gender related concerns and issues through the complaints and feedback mechanisms such as suggestion box and complaints desk for support and referral.To mitigate against the risk of beneficiaries not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms, NRC will employ a range of communication modalities which will be made available -as relevant and appropriate- to targeted communities, ensuring communication channels are easily accessible, taking into account the specificities of the local context and culture, as well as project resources and beneficiary vulnerabilities and preferences. While the most commonly used modalities are complaints desks (including dedicated accountability staff), phone lines and direct engagement with NRC office and M&E staff in the field as possible channels. Multiple communication means ensure that vulnerable people – especially those with limited mobility such as the elderly and people with disabilities – are able to provide feedback, while guaranteeing total confidentiality of information shared. To avoid risks associated with unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries. NRC already have data protection and safeguarding procedures that will be observed together with the following ethical standards or safeguards in data collection and information storage, pertaining to beneficiaries and other stakeholders:• Ensuring the information gathering activity is necessary and justified• Adoption of informed consent and voluntary participation procedures, including written and oral consent• Adopting appropriate confidentiality procedures sensitive to the needs of the target groups and more specifically children, in case they are also participating• Being sensible and prepared in terms of understanding and being mindful of cultural, religious, gender and other significant considerations within the communities in planning, conducting and reporting findings.• Ensuring that all staff and external parties contracted to undertake specific M&E tasks are adequately briefed of the above ethical issues and they sign to indicate willingness to adhere and be held accountable in case of a breach.• Ensuring that all enumerators are carefully trained in data protection and all internal protocols are closely followedTo mitigate against risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries, NRC will engage in high-level advocacy with relevant government officials and coordinate with partners to promote good practices and consistent messaging with government and donors. NRC will also provide awareness sessions on humanitarian principles and international humanitarian law, respecting NRC’s operational independence and safeguarding the organization’s reputation and credibility as well as the safety of the people we serve and our staff to government partners and other stakeholders

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

NRC in collaboration with WFP will provide food assistance to ensure assistance to the most at risk populations according to the IPC acute or emergency phase classification, specifically population classified under IPC 4 & 5. Returnees, IDPs and host community populations that are classified in IPC 3 and 4 that have access to land for farming will be targeted with crop and vegetable production, as water is readily available to support production. Farmers will be trained on vegetable production, irrigation techniques, and making compost manure and fruit tree production. NRC will promote horticulture through extension support, farmer field schools, and demo plot approaches. Households will be encouraged to begin vegetable gardening around their homestead during the rainy season. Men will be encouraged and consulted to ensure that they will support their women counterparts in nursery management and vegetable gardening. NRC intends to leverage experience on integrated pest management interventions, including the current and previous projects to tackle newly reported desert locusts’ infestation. NRC will introduce short maturing fruit seedlings (pawpaw, mangoes, bananas avocado, and passion fruits) to beneficiaries. On the other-hand the fisher-folk will be supported with fishing gear composed of twine, hooks and monofilament in addition to training on fish preservation, processing and hygiene. Livestock farmers will be supported with livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns as well as capacity building and installation of the cold chain for the Community Animal Health Workers

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

To enrich complementary feeding for children between 6-24 months from locally available foods, food preparation and cooking demonstrations will be integrated into the care groups and the farmer field school activities, This activity will demonstrate how to prepare diversified nutritious meals particularly for children aged 6-24 months, pregnant/lactating women and other vulnerable groups. The care groups will as well be supported with seeds and tools to engage in kitchen garden vegetable production which will include training on improved agronomic practices. PLW as well as households with malnourished children will also benefit from poultry as well as goat and sheep restocking. The intervention will ensure the malnourished children consume eggs and other livestock products improving their dietary diversity and nutrition
