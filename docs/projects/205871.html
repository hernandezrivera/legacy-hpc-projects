Project Summary

The proposed project (USD 1,000,000) aims to support 50,000 individuals (15,300 men, 15,300women, 9,700 boys and 9,700 girls) including vulnerable category groups (elderly and Persons with Special Needs, internally displaced, returnees and host communities) in fashoda, panyikang, Ulang Melut and Manyo counties (Upper Nile State) and Twic county (Warrap State). WV will Implement a hybrid approach of in-kind in-line with cluster strategy 2023-2025. The proposed project will contribute to the SNFIs Sub Cluster's objective, (1). % of the targeted population are able to access SNFI material in situations of emergency and transition, (2). Enhance the community’s dignity by increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and building on existing skills.
WV will use both the mobile and static teams to undertake multi-sectorial approach in serving most vulnerable people with shelter and Non-food Items. There is greater probability that this approach has high impact to the affected populations as sectors like Health, FSL, WASH, Protection will be integrated with SNFI sector to address community needs. Through this project, WV aims to use approaches that contribute to durable long term community solutions by ensuring effective environment impact assessments is conducted and mitigation measures are adhered to and incorporated during the project cycle with a Lense for minimizing the impact of assistance on the environment, proper coordination within the National Cluster and local State focal points systems and stakeholders at community level. Beneficiaries of the project will be involved from assessment all the way to the end of the project cycle. Local community structures such as, local chiefs and faith-based institutions will be used to enhance community participation.
Local committees will be formed and trained to sensitize communities on coping mechanisms, Housing Land and Property (HLP) issues and climate mitigation measures. They will also be enlightened on local resource mobilization for other community projects. Additionally Focus Group Discussions for consultative community meetings will be facilitated, a complaint Feedback Mechanism will be in place from project inception to enable WV to gather feedback from project participants. WV will align with the SNFIs cluster guidelines and standards during implementations at national and state levels. WV will collaborate with government authorities and partners, UNHAS, UNOCHA, IOM/pipeline, local and international organizations to realize durable solutions for emergency shelter and non-food items. Existing local community structures would be strengthened, where they are non-existent, WV will facilitate formation of these structures at project sites in collaboration with project participants.
The SNFIs emergency interventions will improve population with access SNFI material in situations of emergency and transition, which will be integrated in other sub cluster sectors, among them Health, Nutrition, Food Security and Livelihood, WASH and Child Protection/ GBV.

1. Needs

In 2023, 3 million people (49.8% male and 50.2% female) in South Sudan will need SNFI assistance. These numbers include 1.3 million IDPs, 629,830 returnees and 1 million host community. Approximately 13% of people needing SNFI assistance are PWD. However, with ongoing political tensions coupled with the continued floods, economic instability and disease outbreak, South Sudan will continue to register more people requiring SNFI support. The lack of flood-resilient shelters, continued depletion of natural resources and destruction of markets further compounds vulnerability, most households remain in a cyclical pattern of emergency need without the opportunity for recovery. Communities from high-risk areas experience conflict related to housing, land and property ownership among the displaced, returnees and host communities. As a result of these compounding factors, the affected people have limited coping strategies. Some adopt harmful coping mechanisms, such as engaging in exploitative work, commercial sex, stealing and living under trees or in open shelters, exposing them to health and protection risks. Timely provision of SNFI to at least 3 million people across 79 counties remains critical to enable them to live a dignified life. OCHA (October 2023) South Sudan Response to the Sudan Crisis indicates the country continues to face multiple compounding shocks and stressors. Insecurity, sub-national violence, regional and national macroeconomic challenges – intensified by the global economic downturn, the impact of climate change and the ongoing Sudan conflict – all adversely affect the food security situation. As at 16 October 2023; 317,993 people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since the onset of the conflict in April. Overall, half of the arrivals are women and children below the age of 18. South Sudanese returnees account for 88.5% of all arrivals. New arrivals continue to report harassment, intimidation, torture and extortion of money during their journey to South Sudan, including by security personnel along the route. The proportion of refugees among new arrivals from Sudan is increasing, with Sudanese refugees accounting for 40 per cent of new arrivals since the beginning of October, in comparison to 9 per cent in September. 37,066 refugees and asylum-seekers have sought asylum in South Sudan, with 61 per cent biometrically registered. More than 9,643 refugees and asylum-seekers are in Aweil, 8,600 in Juba and 8,500 in Maban.

2. Project activities
