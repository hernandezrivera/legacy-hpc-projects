Project Summary

As of 2023, the security situation in Burkina Faso remains a significant challenge. Since 2018, there has been a drastic deterioration, marked by the expansion and intensification of activities by non-state armed groups (NSAGs). These groups exploit pre-existing inter-communal tensions, and the situation escalated further in 2020 and 2021, resulting in a higher toll on human lives and vulnerable populations. The NSAGs are fragmented with multiple internal players, and external players linked to regional dynamics, particularly in the Central Sahel countries.
The consequences of this insecurity include massive population displacements and severe impacts on already vulnerable populations. As of October 2021, over 1.4 million people have been displaced, with new displacements reported daily. The security situation has had repercussions on the overall food and nutritional security of the country. The food security and nutritional situation have been adversely affected by the growing crisis affecting vulnerable populations.
During the 2022 lean season, an unprecedented humanitarian emergency left 2.6 million people in need of assistance, according to the Cadre Harmonisé. As of November 30, 2022, the number of registered internally displaced persons (IDPs) is 1,810,105, hosted in 299 communes spread across all 13 regions of the country. Additionally, the Analysis of the Cadre Harmonisé of November 2022 indicates that 3,533,220 people, or 16% of the total population, would be food insecure. This includes 572,382 children under 5 years of age, concentrated in regions such as East (19%), Centre-North (18%), Sahel (17%), North, and Boucle du Mouhoun (10%). These areas also correspond to localities with high security challenges.
Humanitarian access to vulnerable populations is severely restricted due to threats posed by NSAGs, counterterrorism operations, the presence of Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) planted on roads, and the damage or destruction of key road infrastructure, especially bridges targeted in attacks in the Sahel region. In response to these challenges, UNHAS aims to provide a safe, effective, and efficient passenger air service to the entire humanitarian community and their implementing partners, including NGOs, UN agencies, the Red Cross/Red Crescent movement, donor organizations, and diplomatic missions. UNHAS plays a crucial role in delivering assistance to hard-to-reach areas where people are the most vulnerable. UNHAS aims to provide a safe, effective, and efficient passenger and cargo air service to the entire humanitarian community and their implementing partners, principally NGOs, UN agencies, the Red Cross/Crescent movement, donor organizations, and diplomatic missions. The Logistics Cluster and UNHAS will coordinate all necessary efforts to deliver assistance to these areas where people are the most vulnerable.
