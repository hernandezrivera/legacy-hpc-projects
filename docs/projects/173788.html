Project Summary

Good nutrition is the bedrock of child survival and child development. Well-nourished children are able to grow and learn better, they participate in their communities, and also are resilient in the face of disease or disaster (UNICEF, 2019).
Natural and man-made disasters, including floods, droughts, earthquakes, and armed conflicts have a great impact on the nutritional situation of affected communities and individuals (Bahwere, 2014). During such situations, food production and harvesting of farm products are disrupted, while access to food from the market is limited by security threats, reduced household income, and the collapse of food distribution and marketing networks. At the same time, food preparation practices and food safety are compromised in a context in which healthcare and sanitation services have collapsed. Such disruption to normal life results in an increased vulnerability to food insecurity and to malnutrition, with the ability to cope depending on the type and severity of the disaster and the gender and age of those affected. Cameroon is host to significant numbers of refugees and displaced populations; this situation heightens chronic food and nutrition insecurity and can overwhelm services (Ngwenyi et al., 2019)
In Cameroon, the number of children with malnutrition is increasing. The prevalence of stunting increased from 24.4% in 1991 to 32% in 2014. Likewise, the prevalence of wasting in children under 5 increased from 3% in 1991 to 5.2% in 2014, while the proportion of underweight children increased from 13.6% to 14.8% during the same period (UNICEF, 2017). By the end of 2019, it was estimated that up to 7,000 children between 6-59 months with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) can be expected in the two regions (OCHA, 2019). This has significant impact on the nutritional status of children living in these areas couple with the ongoing Corona virus pandemic, we are hypothesizing that the nutritional status must have gone worst.
The purpose of this intervention is for atleast 11,000 boys and girls between 6 to 59 months benefits from screening and management for all forms of life threatening acute malnutrition. Our targeted populations are people living in hard to reach communities and are being affected by the ongoing sociopolitical crisis. This will be achieved via community door to door and community mobilization, sensitization, screening, establishing a rapid response mechanism and mobile clinics in those affected health districts. All forms of malnutrition within 6 to 59 months will be managed accordingly, SAM with complication will be referred to recommended health facility and followed up until recovery.
All boys and girls will have equal rights. Data acquired will be shared within the Nutrition cluster, the Regional Delegation of Public health and the various health districts.
