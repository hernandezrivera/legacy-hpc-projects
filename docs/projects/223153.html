Project Summary

To address pressing threats to communities' physical safety,
dignity, freedom of movement, and access to humanitarian
assistance, DRC will deploy two highly mobile Multi-Task Teams
(MTTs) to carry on with the clearance and Explosive Ordnance
Disposal (EOD) in the affected communities of South Sudan.
Additionally, DRC will deploy 3 Teams focused on delivering
Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) to conflict-affected
communities to mitigate risks of explosive hazards further.
The proposed project aims at:
• Providing immediate lifesaving assistance to address the urgent
protection needs of the most vulnerable (including women,
children, elderly, disabled, and survivors of ERW in conflict) in mitigating risks and strengthening resilience
• Enabling durable solutions for IDPs and other affected
populations
Tasks that will support the humanitarian response in the country
will be prioritized through the following key activities:
• Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and Technical Survey (TS) – The
purpose of the Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and Technical Survey
(TS) is to prioritize and target clearance based on humanitarian
needs. Key informant interviews and household questionnaires
with the local populace and authorities will be used in the
surveys. While evaluating the effects of these risks on
susceptible groups in each location, NTS will ascertain the
density and probable locations of ERW contamination in target
areas. When a high concentration of explosive hazards is
identified, TS (using detectors) will be used
• Manual Mine Clearance (MMC) / Battle Area Clearance (BAC) -
the MTTs will work as MMC / BAC depending on which task is
being undertaken since the team has the same capacity for MMC
& BAC. The team will be performing static clearance activities
across larger areas which are identified through Non-Technical
Survey (NTS) and Technical Survey (TS). In the event of concerned
hazard areas, the team will be directly deployed based on the
tasking order from UNMAS.
• Explosive ordnance disposal (EOD): Once NTS has determined
the threat's nature, the teams' technical capacities will be
deployed to remove and destroy identified hazards.
• Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) – all teams will
contain the capacity to deliver risk messaging, and raise
awareness about how to recognize dangerous items. as a risk
education will emphasize safe practices that should be followed
and high-risk behavior that should be avoided concerning mines
and explosive remnants of war.

1. Needs

The Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) presents the strongest opportunity for peace and post-conflict recovery in several years. However, South Sudan continues to experience unprecedented humanitarian needs. In 2022 an estimated 8.3 million individuals need protection assistance and decades of conflict have left South Sudan heavily contaminated by landmines, munitions, and unexploded ordnance (UXO). On the other hand, in the 2024 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Response Plan, the protection cluster highlighted that 9.18 million people are estimated to be at protection risk with the need for protection assistance, and the predictive model during the planning phase indicated that approximately all the 9.18 million people in the affected population are exposed to at least one of the top five protection risks.According to The UN Mine Action Service (UNMAS) records, all known contamination currently amounts to over 22 million m2. Much of this contamination affected the land that is primarily used for livelihood and economic activities in the affected communities, and the primary return routes for refugees in neighboring countries. Although South Sudan's general security situation has improved, tensions still exist. Cattle raiding and localized ethnic conflicts are still common, and power struggles among leaders make the citizens live in fear of threats of hostility. To address the threats of being exposed by war remnants in the affected population, DRC will put in place a roving response capability that will quickly deploy to areas where ERW poses a serious risk to community safety and hinders land use to make sure the land is safe for use by the people who need it the most. Through mapping and prioritization mechanisms that are intended to target the most vulnerable communities, the DRC Mine Action team will work closely with the DRC Protection team to target displaced and conflict-affected populations. Furthermore, DRC will be able to quickly deploy to priority locations determined by the Protection Cluster.

2. Project activities

The overall goal of this proposed project is to prevent and mitigate the physical risks posed by landmines and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) through clearance, and disposal of ERWs to enhance safety and resilience through risk education (EORE) messaging. To contribute to protection cluster objectives, DRC proposed intervention activities under this project as follows; To support the protection cluster objective 1, the proposed intervention will directly engage in removing the physical threats posed by the presence of mines and war remnants contaminations in the affected community, this will be done through clearance. The intervention will also support protection cluster objective 2, through coordinated EORE awareness activities with other implementing partners in the affected community, and DRC through this intervention will activity participate in the monthly coordination meeting. The EORE activities will focus on safety behaviors to enhance community-level resilience against explosive hazards.To support protection cluster objective 3, DRC will prioritize land clearance activities in the affected communities to eliminate the physical presence of mines and war remnants. The prioritization of the activities will be based on the community's needs and by targeting known contaminated areas that could support local integration, returns, and access to land use for productive livelihood activities for the community. The intervention also proposed to work with a local partner and the stakeholders to uplift their capacity.To support the protection cluster objectives, DRC proposed to deploy 2 clearance teams and one 1 Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) team to focus on either enabling recovery or improving humanitarian access, depending on needs at any given time or operates as a static team in confirmed hazard areas hindering access to land for viable economics activities, 3 EORE teams will compliment clearance responses as necessary, by targeting surrounding locations that have not yet received a clearance response. During the intervention, DRC proposed that the teams will undertake the following activities: 1. Ensure the provision of critical assistance to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in hard-to-reach and/or priority geographical areas.1.1:Carry out desktop research and engage with other stakeholders to identify populations affected by conflict and by landmines or ERW, including restrictions on access to humanitarian assets or service delivery.1.2:Conduct a Non-Technical Survey (NTS) in the affected community to inform clearance activity.1.3:Survey infrastructure and transit routes through NTS to facilitate the timely, effective, and safe delivery of humanitarian assistance by other stakeholders.1.4:Conduct meetings with humanitarian agencies to prioritize locations in terms of humanitarian access/service delivery, including warehouses, compounds, distribution sites, and/or schools. 1.5: Conduct refresher training for the DRC project staff on NTS, technical survey / TS (using detectors), and clearance methodologies. NTS training includes strategies designed to ensure participation and active engagement from women and children, recognizing the obstacles presented by societal norms, and gender / age-specific daily routines and movement patterns. 1.6:Conduct (mobile) Explosive Ordnance, Disposal (EOD) / (static) Battle Area Clearance (BAC) / Manual, Mine Clearance (MMC) to remove and destroy dangerous items. 2. Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. 2.1. Conduct EORE targeting at-risk women, men, boys, and girls within displaced and host communities to promote greater awareness and safer practices concerning landmines and explosive remnants of war. 2.2: DRC will monitor gender and age-specific attitudes towards explosive hazards, with the ability to develop tailored risk reduction strategies where required.
