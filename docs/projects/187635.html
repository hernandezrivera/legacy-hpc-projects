Project Summary

Like all sectors working inside Syria, FSL has suffered serious setbacks since the beginning of the current crisis in 2011.
During all these years of conflict, the Syrian population has decreased as a result of emigration and mortality, and the labor force participation rate in all sectors in this reduced population is estimated to have fallen from 43 to 41 percent. Nevertheless, because of the even greater reductions in productivity in other sectors of the economy, most recent estimation states that 5 million people are hit by unemployment and that nearly 80% of the population in the most affected areas are without a regular income, which makes it impossible for men as traditional breadwinners to provide for their families’ daily needs. High food prices are eroding the purchasing power of families, limiting access to basic food items leaving Syrians increasingly dependent on relief items and services provided by humanitarian actors.
Moreover, the current crisis has devastated the previously flourishing agricultural lands by the loss of cultivated land, the movement of farmers away from insecure areas, the destruction of farm machinery and irrigation structures, shortages and high costs of farm inputs and fuel, a severely damaged infrastructure, and compromised power supplies.
The conflict in Syria has developed into a humanitarian disaster of “apocalyptic dimensions” and the humanitarian situation is rapidly deteriorating in large parts of the country. Hundred thousands of Syrians are in need of urgent assistance, including people displaced inside their own country, half of them children, struggling to survive and cope with the disaster. Furthermore, the big percentage is of women and children.
Rahma Worldwide is proposing here a project of distributing food kits and ready to eat meals for the displaced families in camps and host communities
The intervention aims to ensure that lives are saved and human suffering is reduced through provision of food security production inputs, to targeted beneficiaries, in order to ensure immediate access to inputs and subsequently to food for the most vulnerable populations. This food security support project is a response to assist 7000 vulnerable HHs which is almost (43,750 beneficiaries).
The concept of the project can be summarized as follows:
- Distributing 36,000 food and RTE kits along (2022/2023) for 12,000 HHs (almost 75,000 beneficiaries)
- The process of distribution will be every other month (6 distributions per year) for 6,000 HHs in each year.
- Each 3,000 HH will receive the assistance for three times during 6 months and then the beneficiaries will be replaced by new others.
- Each year, 18,000 kits will be distributed for 6,000 HHs and the total is 36,000 kits for 12000 HHs (almost 75.000 IDPS).
- The project will mainly target the IDPs in camps in Idleb.
- The main locations of distribution are in (Jisr-Ash-Shugur) sub-district/ (Bdama-Darkoush-Janodiyeh) districts.
- The kits components will be consisting of a ready to eat items in addition to the main items of the food basket that will be enough for 2 months.

4 - Needs

According to the latest need assessment that was conducted by Care and other FSL partners and HNO reports, the results showed that Based on the Household surveys, high cost of food in the market is the primary challenge of households in accessing food, followed by lack of resources to buy food.Other challenges identified by the respondents were security issues that restrict movement and affect in getting food.All respondents also mentioned that children under five (5) years old usually eat three times daily. However, 73.3% of respondents mentioned that there are children in this age group who do not eat every day. Almost all respondents (93.3%) also confirmed that they do not keep stockpile food, beyond the normal weekly consumption. When we asked if food is limited, who is prioritized to access it among the members of the household? 59.9% said children, 27.7% the members with disabilities followed by 9.09% who said there is no difference between family members and only 4.5% who mentioned elderly people.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Every effort will be made to recruit female staff locally in the project areas. Female staff will be involved in engaging women in project activities at all stages, including assessments, planning, implementation, and monitoring by inviting them to plan and review meetings, project site visits and meet them individually. This approach enables RRW to target the most vulnerable and ensures that RRW is meeting the needs of the female beneficiaries in a manner that is acceptable considered appropriate by them. RRW will ensure that project sites for female beneficiaries are approved by them and that distributions take place in a culturally appropriate manner.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Rahma Worldwide will target female led households with difficult conditions. In order to do this, Rahma Worldwide will ask women’s center with these areas for eligible cases. Additionally, Rahma Worldwide will hire at least one women field officer and/or supervisor in each of the target areas in order to make distribution as comfortable as possible whom will also attend the distribution point. For those restricted by society or culture norms, Rahma Worldwide will compile a list and will deliver house to house. All field distribution staff will be trained, oriented and accounted on PSEA mechanisms.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The level of insecurity is extremely high in Syria, the growing diversity and complexity of different armed groups both regime and opposition contributed to the instability of the current situation which could impose a direct impact on the feasibility of the project. The main risks identified in the area of intervention are bombing, shelling, kidnapping. The security situation is getting more complex with regards to the number of different local and foreign armed groups which could restrain the area of intervention. This is not expected to change in the coming months. RRW is committed to implementing the project in the targeted location due to the high humanitarian needs and lack of food and livelihood. RRW has set up specific standard operating procedures for identifying risks in order to limit the staff and beneficiaries’ exposure as described.
