Project Summary

Currently, DRC programme is focused on children and child survivors providing case management and community-based protection in camps 6,7 and 2W for children and child survivors, filling a gap that is not being served by GBV programs. While the majority of survivors are girls, DRC also provides case management activities for boy survivors. Furthermore, DRC's program is based on the Best interests of the Child applies a survivor centered approach to case management. DRC applies safeguarding principles of its Code of Conduct and the Global Protection Sector guidelines on case management and child protection including minimum standards for child protection in emergencies and adheres to Case Management SOP's.
DRC provides supports community-based child protection mechanisms consulting with an extensive network of Rohingya volunteers and committees including separate women and men's adult and adolescent groups and a children's club to identify and refer vulnerable children and protection risks, and provide suggestions for relevant activities and feedback on DRC child protection activities during monthly meetings. 45 volunteers conduct all community awareness sessions and activities offered in two Child and Adolescent Multi Service Centers in camp 6 and 7, and protection monitoring at night. Community awareness sessions deal with child protectioin risks affecting children and work as prevention against communities stigmatizing child survivors. Additionally CBCPC members will meet with families to prevent child marriages whenever possible. Child and Adolescent Multi Service centers in Camp 6 and 7 offer adolescents practical skill-building sessions for both girls and boys as well as literacy training and organized PSS activities for children. Adolescents groups identify skills they want to learn and with the help of Rohingya volunteers and CBCPC's, a Rohingya instructor is identified to lead the group.
DRC will replicate its child protection program in host community Unions of Rajapalong and Palongkali where DRC implements existing GBV, commuity-based protection programs, and community centers. This will allow DRC to have a comprehensive protection program in targetted host communities and fill the gap of protection for children and child survivors. In this project, DRC will work with Libraries without Borders to expand its Ideas Box pilot project in Rajapalong to Palongkali, Camps 6, 7 and 2W. The Ideas Box is an innovative way to provide static and mobile access to informative, educational and cultural resources adapted to the Bangladeshi context and the Rohingya refugee camps. These activities will be open to both host community and refugees living in the area. The ideas box creates a natural learning context where integration and community cohesion can be fostered through joint learning opportunities. DRC employs Do no Harm principles to identify and build on community strengths and not exacerbate community tensions through its programming and thus contibuting to social cohesion. The project will serve a total of 50,070 beneficiaries including 26,650 children (15,660 girls and 10,990 boys) and 23,420 adults (14,010 females and 9,410 men).

Needs

The Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) Joint Needs Assessment (JNA) the top risk identified for girls by camp residents is child marriage (46.5%), and risks of trafficking, sexual harassment, physical and sexual violence also scored high. The top risks for adolescent boys are engaging in dangerous and exploitative labor, addiction and transport of illegal drugs, radicalization, increased criminal activity, and recruitment into violent factions within the camps. Memory of violence before movement was listed as the main cause of distress for both boys and girls (78.3% and 66.2 % respectively). For girls, exposure to sexual violence and witnessing violence are amongst the top three reasons for behaviour change and distress (44.1% and 41.2%). 10.1% reported that exposure to sexual violence was a driver of distress among boys. In terms of child labour, the JNA highlighted that girls’ involvement in sexual transactions was at a rate of 14% and boys’ involvement was 3%. According to UNICEF, children with disabilities are more than 1.7 times more likely to be abused than children without disabilities and much less likely to access health services, especially facility-based services. Despite evidence of past and ongoing child protection issues, according to the Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS), only 4% of total cases received by GBV actors are children and no cases of child sexual abuse have been evident in GBVIMS reports. While prevalence data is notoriously difficult to determine, given the demographics of the camp and the known child protection risks, it is clear that child protection incidences are underreported, resulting in children failing to receive specialized case management services. Factors affected reporting and response include access to specialised services: As of November 2018, only 43% of minimum service coverage has been achieved for urgently required GBV case management and psychosocial support for children and adults, and the majority accessing services are adults. However, since recently establishing its child-focused GBV programming, DRC has received a significant number of child sex abuse cases including rape or attempted rape of minors. Continuing DRC's activities in Camps 2W, 6, and 7 and expanding services to the host community will address this existing gap in serving this most vulnerable population.

Activities or outputs

1. Strengthen exisiting community-based child protection mechanisms in camps 2W, 6 and 7 and 2 host communities in Rajapalong and Palongkali to identify and mitigate child protection risks, identify and refer children and child survivors and other extremely vulnerable children to case management services, and provide community awareness sessions. DRC community awareness sessions will deal with child protection risks affecting children and will work as prevention against communities stigmatizing child survivors. Additionally, CBCPC members will meet with families to prevent child marriages whenever possible. In case management and all DRC activities, the safety of survivors is prioritized as well as the best interests of the child. Relocating a family or placing the child in alternative care is only used as a last resort.2. Case management for child survivors and other extremely vulnerable children including trafficking, physical and sexual abuse, sexual exploitation and Worst Forms of Child Labor (WFCL) in camps 2W, 6 and 7 and 2 host communities in Rajapalong and Palongkali.3. Improve access to quality survivor centered services by responding to individual needs, preventing and mitigating of GBV risks, and empowering women, girls and survivors of GBV in Rohingya refugee camps and targeted host community sites, in particular in camps 2W, 6 and 7 and 2 host communities in Rajapalong and Palongkali4. Implement structured psychsocial support, life and portable skills trainings in multi-service centres, commuity-centers in host communities for adolescents through Idea Box facility for adolescents, in camps 2W, 6 and 7 (50%) and 2 host communities in Rajapalong and Palongkali (50%).5. As part of the DRR contingency planning, the Child Protection team will ensure risk mapping exercises and community solutions planning during monsoons to mitigate risk, in addition to awareness raising about risks like Dengue, flooding, separation in camps 2W, 6 and 7 and 2 host communities in Rajapalong and Palongkali and Lost Child Care Centers in camps 6 and 7. This DRR activity would ensure separate risks mapping done with younger children and boys and girls groups. 6. Under this project, DRC intervention builds child protection capacity of Rohingya volunteers, community-based committees as well as national NGO's working in Camps 2W, 6 and 7. DRC already has a collaborative relationship with national child protection actors in these camps and will build the capacity of existing organisations such as BRAC and CODEC in implementation of case management for survivors and using commuity sensitization on abuse and neglect, exploitation, and violence as prevention. In the host community, DRC is currently conducting partner mapping and plans to select partners to handover the activities in the second part of the year. The activities are targeting adolescent girls and young girls, but will also target boys. DRC aims to lead case management activities for boys survivors and adolescent boys groups and will provide pre-vocational skills the adolescent groups are interested in. In addition, structured PSS support will be provided for both girls and boys, in different groups. DRC has been a regular participant in the PSEA network and ensured that a PSEA focal point is in place. Allegations of PSEA against DRC staff or volunteers can be channelled through the DRC code of conduct (CoC)reporting mechanism mentioned above which includes phone, email and in person options. For Code of Conduct concerns, staff, volunteers and other stakeholders have access to DRC code of conduct reporting mechanisms which include phone, email and, for persons accessing the office, in person availability. Staff and volunteers are systematically trained on the DRC Code of Conduct, including how to report suspected misconduct. In camp locations, participants are also regularly informed of the Complaint, Feedback and Report Mechanisms within Site Management services.
