Project Summary

Access to education remains as a significant challenge in South Sudan in the context of protracted conflicts. The entire education system, which was already under-resourced, with a low percentage of children enrolled in schools, is further weakened by the long-lasting conflict, which has pushed hundreds of thousands of children out of school. One in three schools has been damaged, destroyed, occupied or closed since 2013. South Sudan has at least 2.2 million out-of-school children who are exposed to protection risks and may need psychosocial support. Based on projections, this number could easily increase to 2.4 million by 2020, if no action is taken.
Therefore, in view of the above this project seeks to provide safe, protective and inclusive education to conflict affected boys and girl in South Sudan in alignment with HRP Strategic Objectives 1, 2 & 3 and Education Cluster Strategic Objectives 1, 2 & 3.
World Vision proposes to implement the project in the counties of Baliet, Fashoda, Melut and Renk (Upper Nile); Tonj South, Tonj East,Tonj North (Warrap); Yambio, Nagero, Tambura (Western Equatoria) and Juba in Central Equatoria which are geographical areas affected by conflict, displacement or facing acute food insecurity levels. WVSS continue to give focus to emergency affected rural and urban areas with significant IDPs presence. It is worth noting that WVSS has presence in these locations and is able to deliver quality education programming. Majority of the locations are part of UNICEF Education PD. However, not all schools are covered by the project, so there is room for collaboration. WV is willing to share information with UNKEA.
In line with the Education Cluster Response Plan, WVSS will ensure that through this project, 33,000 girls and 22,000 boys have access to quality education and 1,300 teachers (780 female and 520 male) and PTA’s will be trained and supported with supplies and materials. WVSS will also prioritize establishment of learning structures with gender-segregated WASH facilities, and other supplies/materials/kits that will be supportive and will enhance motivation of children to go to and remain in school with a particular emphasis on girl’s education. In addition, recreational opportunities will be established and implemented to support the recovery of children and youth and promote their overall wellbeing. All learners will be given equal access to education despite gender, disability or ethnic background.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has one of the highest rates of out of school children (OOSC) in the world, an issue which is compounded by the late entry to primary school. Nearly 75 % of six-year-olds and 59 % of seven-year-olds are not attending school. OOSC rates steadily increase as children reached adolescence, ages 12-17. There are multiple barriers that exclude children from education, such as lack of qualified teachers and schools in remote areas, destruction of school infrastructure, scarcity of schools and poor infrastructure, high pupil-teacher ratios, socio-cultural belief and practices, including those that devaluate education for girls or put children to work at a young age. South Sudan’s OOSC are being excluded from education due to complex, inter-connected environmental, supply and demand-side, and quality related factors. Moreover, many teachers have been displaced from their homes coupled by limited and irregular teacher salary payments whether by the government or donor-funded programmes, forcing teachers to seek alternative livelihoods. In addition, regardless of age, boys and girls have an unequal chance to attend school with the proportion of boys attending school being higher above that of the girls across all age groups (and most pronounced at ages 16-17). Girls also tend to reach secondary school later than boys (while 10.6% of boys were in secondary school at age 16, only 1.3% of sixteen-year-old girls were in secondary school; at age 17, 8.7% of boys were enrolled in secondary and 2.6% of girls). According to the UNICEF, at greater risk of being out of school in addition to girls are IDPs, children with special needs or disabilities and pastoralists5. A large number of students leave school without a minimum mastery of literacy and numeracy skills. As pointed out by the World’s #WritetheWrong Campaign on 21st September 2019 demanding the recent UN Conference, the campaigners urged donors, UN agencies, and governments to do more and urgently support early learning/pre-primary schools. In a similar campaign, the campaigners also highlighted that child soldier numbers are increasing in South Sudan. Taking in to account such challenges, the education cluster also believes that firstly, early learning for age 3-5 children provides opportunities to school readiness and linkages to primary and secondary education, and also protect them from all forms of violence. Secondly, investment in early learning, formal and non-formal education protects targeted communities and children from being involved in activities causing humanitarian crisis.

2 - Activities

Under objective 1, key activities would include;1) Rehabilitation of existing learning spaces and/or construction of Temporary Learning Spaces(TLSs) where there is influx of new IDPs and returnees; 2) Establishment or rehabilitation of wash facilities; including latrines (gender segregated), water points and provision of soap for hand washing, provision of dry chlorine, 3) Construct/rehabilitate 28 TLS and/or semi-permanent structures with temporary latrines and water and hand washing facilities (GUN: 8, WEQ: 8, Juba: 6, Warrap: 8); 4) Learning spaces will also be provided with benches/desks/chairs and chalk boards; 5) Provide food and water to 8,500 children in schools and 6) Training of teachers. Under objective 2, key activities would include; 1) Provision of textbooks, learning and teaching materials; 2) Provision of hygiene kits for girls to support their menstruation, 3) Provision of school furniture and blackboards; 4) Provision of recreational materials; 5) Regular follow up and support through monitoring and supervision of learning activities in schools; 6) Equip Technical and Vocational Education Training (TVET) the following supplies: student kits (GUN: 85, WEQ: 85, Juba: 60, Warrap:85); recreational kits (GUN: 85, WEQ: 85, Juba:60, Warrap: 85); ECD kits ((GUN:85, WEQ: 85, Juba: 60, Warrap: 85); school bags (GUN: 5600), WEQ: 5600, Juba: 2800, Warrap: 19, 037); sanitary kits; teachers’ kits; and life skills and psychosocial support training materials as well as hygiene kits/sanitary to adolescent girls ; 7) Conduct life skills and peace building training for teachers; 8) Train teachers and other stakeholders on courses in intensive English, curriculum deliver, basic pedagogy, life skills and classroom management. Under objective 3, key activities would include; 1) Training of PTAs/SMCs on PSS; 3) Training of SMCs and county education officials on school management, record keeping and monitoring of education activities; 4) Creating school hygiene clubs to support hygiene messaging; 5) Provide awareness on cleaning and safe use of latrines; 6) Conduct training of Caregivers/Facilitators on classroom management, teachers’ code of conduct, lesson planning/scheme of work, teachers’ role in managing children with disabilities (CWD) and use of the ECD kit.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The PTA will be key factors contributing to the project’s monitoring and evaluation process. Child Help Desks in schools and complaints collection templates will be designed and filled in by beneficiaries during supervisory visits.
