Project Summary

The project aims: Firstly, to provide health care and prevention services to women including: detection and primary care of gynecological problems; ultrasound examination; advice and treatment for menopausal women; testing during pregnancy.
In addition to provide family planning services to women and adolescents including information and counselling regarding all possible contraceptive methods: natural methods, condom, injections, pill and IUDs, in order to facilitate an optimal and responsible free choice. Meetings and workshops on reproduction and family planning will be organized for various target groups with special focus on adolescents besides provide ante and postnatal care to women: Pre-natal counselling services including ultrasound examinations, will be offered to women. Pre-delivery courses will be offered to women in the last quarter of pregnancy consisting of suitable physical exercises, relaxation and breathing techniques and health education sessions regarding pregnancy, delivery, breast-feeding, baby-care and family planning. Post-delivery counselling and services will be offered for women who have recently given birth and who will receive information on breast-feeding, family planning, baby care and conduct physical exercises.
Secondly: capacity building for frontlines staff from the health specialized actors o self- care and safety, provision of PPEs to the medical teams as well as training on prevention and control of COVID -19., health counselling and contingency planning, GBV identification, follow up and referral mechanisms, PSEA measures indicated in UN Secretary-General’s SEA Bulletin ST/SGB/2003/13, complaint and response mechanism and providing strategic directions to health and nutrition programmes and advocacy
Third: Procurement and distribution of PPEs to the medical teams/health facilities engaged in the project as well as procure medical laboratory tests, hygiene and sterilization materials and other needs of medical supplies and disposable.
All project's activities will be delivered FREE of charge

1 - Needs

As of 14 October, 4,175 cases have been reported in Gaza. Rapid increase of people with the virus and the number of deaths is of grave concern. Pregnant and lactating women and children < 5 are considered most vulnerable and they may not be able to receive essential healthcare in response to the crisis because of the re-prioritization of health services. Gendered inequalities in the oPt further limit freedom of movement, decision-making power, and access to health, education, and basic services and resources differently for men, women, boys, and girls. Also, socially and economically disadvantaged women, male/female youth, and marginalized populations (i.e. PWDs) are more disproportionately affected. When overlapped with existing gendered disparities, the response to both the protracted crisis and C-19 becomes increasingly complex. Global trends indicate that women and girls are bearing specific burdens of the C-19 crisis such as: increased household responsibilities and family care, restricted mobility, and rising levels of GBV.CARE’s assessment (April 2020) noted that significantly fewer female respondents (58%) than males (86%) have safe access to health facilities inside and outside of their community, which they attributed to a lack of cash and inability to travel. The prevalence of male medical and security staff also creates barriers for women accessing health care, mental health support, basic hygiene, as well as the right to privacy and comfort, given the taboos and norms related to gender mixing. Between 15-19 Oct, 2020, Red Crescent Society for Gaza Strip (RCS4GS) held 4 FGDs and 15 phone KIs with RCS4GS staff and volunteers (women and men 24-54 years), (2) Zoom meetings with health institutions and CBOs/NGOs, WhatsApp groups with 50 women with breast cancer, and with 50 women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating, and mothers of children <5 years; it also utilized the helpline and had (25) phone KIs with most vulnerable women/ men 18-59, with persons >59 years, girls/ boys<18 years, persons with disabilities (PWD with reduced mobility including physical disabilities, elderly, the bed-ridden and with non-mobility related disabilities including blind, hard of hearing, intellectual), breast cancer survivors, SGBV survivors, refugees, IDPs and FHHs from Gaza North (Jabalia, Beit Hanoun, Beit Lahia, Siafa) and Gaza (Gaza City, Shati' Camp, Mughraga, Juhr Dik) and ARAs. The assessment findings reveal critical needs, in alignment with 2021 HNO. All participants expressed their fears about the rise in transmission of COVID-19 (C-19) in local communities and amongst healthcare workers in Gaza Strip. RCS4GS staff and volunteers asked RCS4GS for monthly PPE kitsF, and hygiene and sterilization materials for their facilities (Dr Haidar Abdel Shafi Medical Center- Gaza and Women’s Health Center- Jabalia). The frontline health staff are in need of self-care capacity building training for frontline staff in health and nutrition, and also noted the need for PSEA, complaints/response mechanisms, and remote health counselling. Women of reproductive age and pregnant/ lactating women noted the lack of quality maternal and nutrition services in governmental health facilities, primarily access to SRH, family planning, prevention and referral of SGBV, screening, diagnosis, treatment and rehabilitation, medical lab tests; individual and group PSS, couple therapy, specialized mental health treatment, social/legal counselling; home visits, dignity/hygiene kits and community awareness raising. Participants with cervical and breast cancer indicated the need for screening, diagnosis and rehabilitation. Women and girls with physical disabilities noted the need for assistive mobility devices (mobile chairs, walkers), while those with hearing disabilities lack assistive audio devices. SGBV cases lack integrated case management services, and prevention/ referral services. Finally, a broad advocacy is needed to raise the voices of women and girls about their critical health gaps/needs. HNO (2021) indicated that redirection of resources from provision of SRH, obstetric care and emergency nutrition services affected an additional 80,000 vulnerable women and 210,000 children, including 25,000 neonates. Currently,1800 patients visit the Gaza center monthly to receive medical and healthcare services, out of whom 150 are pregnant and lactating women. Rafah Medical center receives 800 patients, of whom 100 are pregnant and lactating. The majority of women (18-45yrs) visiting UHCC for SRH services, are the most vulnerable; 10-15% are HHS, lacking source of income, health awareness and healthy behaviors.

2 - Activities

The project aims: to improve and enhancing the sexual and reproductive healthcare of pregnant and lactating women including with disabilities, breast cancer survivors, GBV survivors, IDP, refugees, and FHHs taking into account the local impact of current COVID-19 pandemic.The planned activities were guided by the HNO 2021, HNC Objectives and activities mainly Objective: 1 Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt, and Objective: 2 Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises. It also goes in line with the accountability to affected populations (AAP) with respecting the rights, dignity and protection of all segments of affected women within the targeted communities. The activities are closely coordinated with HNC, health providers, local CBOs, while being aware of what is offered in the targeted locations. The activities will be implemented in partnership with Red Crescent Society and Union of health Care Committee who will be responsible of the technical implementation at the field level (provision of health services). CARE will be responsible of the overall quality management and will act as the main contact for donors and stakeholders. The project activities will be as follows: Activities for O#1: comprehensive Women health care services: Provide health care and prevention services to women including: detection and primary care of gynecological problems; ultrasound examination; advice and treatment for menopausal women; testing during pregnancy; provide family planning services to women and adolescents including information and counselling regarding all possible contraceptive methods in order to facilitate an optimal and responsible free choice; awareness sessions on reproduction and family planning will be organized for various target groups with special focus on adolescents, provide ante and postnatal care to women: Pre-natal counselling services including ultrasound examinations, will be offered to women. Pre-delivery courses will be offered to women in the last quarter of pregnancy consisting of suitable physical exercises, relaxation and breathing techniques and health education sessions regarding pregnancy, delivery, breast-feeding, baby-care and family planning. Post-delivery counselling and services will be offered for women who have recently given birth and will receive information on breast-feeding, family planning, baby care and conduct physical exercises.Activities for O#2: Capacity Building for medical team: Conducting capacity building for frontlines staff from the health specialized actors on self- care and safety, provision of PPEs to the medical teams as well as training on prevention and control of COVID -19 for 80 medical staff, health counselling, GBV identification, follow and referral, PSEA measures, complaint and response mechanism. Additionally, awareness activities on COVID 19 will be implemented taking into account that RCCE materials will be in accordance with HNC RCCE plans/recommendations Activities for O# 3: Procurement Procurement and distribution of PPEs to the medical team’s/health facilities engaged in the project as well as procure medical laboratory tests, hygiene and sterilization materials and other needs of medical supplies and disposable. At the beginning of the project, at the preparatory phase; CARE and the Partners will reach out to the targeted communities to explain about the project's objectives and activities (services). during this phase, meetings with different actors (MoH, local CBOs, community representatives/leaders, women groups) to share the information about the project. At the same time description about the project will be posted on the main locations in the communities like Mosques, CBOs, municipalities, etc). more over there will be announcements about the on the social media, etc

Protection will be mainstreamed in the project delivery (including respect for principles of non-discrimination, do no harm, etc.); and cross-cutting issues, including gender, age, environment and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). CARE is committed to assuring accountability to the communities we serve and stakeholders in all aspects of the project. the design of the project took into consideration the different needs of the different groups in the communities (sex and age), where the communities informed the design of the project activities, the composition of the teams, frequency of the services, etc. Monitoring will have carried out through on sight monitoring of distributions and activities; monitoring system will be designed to include segregation of data by sex and age. Partners will follow the same monitoring guidelines and through reporting provide information. A confidential complaint and feedback mechanism will be designed, allowing targeted population to reach out to CARE. CARE's complaints mechanism: in order to ensure that the interests of the beneficiaries are continuously addressed and in line with CARE's 'Do No Harm' approach and Humanitarian Accountability Framework.
