Project Summary

The problem to be addressed is that the livelihoods of communities are being negatively affected by a combination of weakened local currency, the knock-on effects of the C-19 pandemic, a poor agricultural season, climate change, and water crisis impacts. To reduce the negative impact on the affected population, on markets and services, a resilience portfolio has to be adopted through a variety of key Livelihood Economic Recovery interventions that will assist to make the vulnerable household more self-reliant and reinforce their access to livelihood opportunities. Taking into account the real protection needs of women, girls and GBV survivors through merged them within the livelihood's intervention through referral mechanism.
The project targets 1,400 HHs (8,400 individuals) to reinforce and enhance better access to income-generating opportunities and to have community resilience strengthened. 300 farmers (25% women, 5% PWDs) access ways affected by years-long conflict, will be slightly rehabilitated to improve access to farmlands. 600 women (5% PWDs) with previous experience will provide with SMEs and skills training, then deliver cash grants. Moreover,500 CFW workers (semi-skilled & Unskilled) (10% women, 5% PWDs) will implement activities related to rehabilitation of the access ways, public infrastructures such as public bakeries, irrigation canals, water, sewage, and drainage services. CFW can foster social cohesion and community ownership in addition to supporting markets through communicating clearly how the CFW benefits the whole community, even if there are more IDP participants, Hire skilled workers from the community as CFW supervisors and trainers and pay them at labor market wage rates commensurate to their expertise, Consider incorporating into the CFW design apprenticeship schemes targeted to the local youth among the most vulnerable resident HHs, Conduct town hall meetings as part of community engagement, circulate key messages through pamphlets, posters, social media, mobile messages.
The priority should be given to the most vulnerable groups including, women, Girls, C19 patients, elderly people, GBV survivors, and people with disabilities. Especially if the project activities aim at the same time to empower the women, thus affecting the family situation. So, work will be guided by protection principles to design and plan the activities.

3 - Needs

MEAL team carried out a Household Economic Profile in Nov 2022 through which we will seek towards the realization of the commitment to putting poor people first, particularly women and girls and leaving no one behind, by ensuring to reach the most vulnerable population. Where we met community members of women, men, disabled, elders, and young through KII and FGDs to reach for actual needs.A total of 100 surveys were conducted. In summary, the study reveals the continued degradation of the economic situation. The economic situation significantly worsened. Due to the scarcity of available jobs and the harsh economic climate, respondents predominantly reported taking up poorly-paid and irregular daily-wage employment. CFW scheme is preferred, where beneficiaries are linked with the dominant agricultural value chains activities, and so create short-term work opportunities. Focusing on building the resilience of vulnerable people with previous experience by strengthening their livelihoods through SMEs. Cash modality allows for a more normalized market environment and protects beneficiary dignity. The priority should be given to the most vulnerable groups including, women, Girls, C19 patients, elderly, GBV survivors, and people with disabilities. Especially if the project activities aim at the same time to empower the women, thus affecting the family situation. So, work will be guided by protection principles to design and plan the activities.

4 - Implementation of activities

The action will provide structured support to 600 SMEs in the target locations. For manywho do not have access to land or livestock assets. For those who have had to relocateand have left behind assets, or where there is increased demand for their services but donot have the ability to restart due to a loss of capital, the action will provide conditionalcash transfers (CTP). 4 Business training will be delivered to BNFs under C-19 precautionswhich will particularly focus on (a) basic profit/loss accounting and (b) marketing and (c)preparation of the monthly turnover report. Bahar will conduct 1 SME training, where all theparticipants from the VT will be invited to attend SME training for 5 days which isprovided to enable BNFs to prepare business plans that include all aspects of managingthe expansion or establishment of small businesses. Also, provide temporary employment(CFW) to 500 HHs run by unskilled/semi-skilled workers, who will be assigned toworkgroups. CFW will include green water harvesting techniques, agro-access road rehab,and primary irrigation canals. 450 unskilled workers, employed for 20 days, supported by50 semi-skilled (Agro & construction technicians). Labor wages be harmonized with otheragencies and the local market rate and are anticipated to be 5-8 USD per day. coordinationand harmonization, especially at the area level with other aid providers via clusters gapanalysis figures and associated communications channels (skype groups).

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Women and girls being excluded from the program design, implementation, andevaluation of activities. Mitigation: As per our protection mainstreaming policy needsof women and girls are constantly considered, with specific dedicated staff, questions,and action/responses targeting the most vulnerable and the gender sensitivity. Risk:Boys and girls (under 18 years) are excluded from the design, implementation, andevaluation of activities. Mitigation: Needs of boys and girls are constantly considered,with specifically dedicated staff (part of Bahar child protection mobile team) withadapted assessment tools and action/response targeting the young and the gendersensitivity. The CRM is adapted for under 18 years, ensuring the feasibility of a safeand secure feedback mechanism, as well as adapted follow-up to address the issues.Risk: Older persons and persons with a disability (PWD) are part of the design,implementation, and evaluation of activities. Mitigation: Program staff is regularlytrained in inclusion and adequate program response tailored-made for PWD. Risk:Increased sensitivity between host communities and IDPs. Mitigation: Based on acommunity approach, social activities are conducted based on communities’initiatives, ensuring inclusive participation of all groups. Bahar ensures the involvementbetween the host community and internally displaced persons once the project startsin order to mitigate any tension within the community.
