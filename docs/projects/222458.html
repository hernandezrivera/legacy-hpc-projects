Project Summary

Samaritan’s Purse has been implementing multisectoral integrated intervention in health, nutrition, Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), sectors in the counties of Mayendit, Mayom, and Maiwut in Unity and Upper Nile States, South Sudan. With a strong operational presence spanning over six years in these counties, Samaritan’s Purse has the organizational capacity to continue addressing the pressing needs of the conflict-affected populations. Samaritan’s Purse will continue to scale up its multisectoral integrated interventions in alignment with the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan to meet increasing demands while improving resilience and health outcomes among vulnerable populations.
The proposed health sectors’ project will improve access to essential health services for 68,396 conflict- and famine-affected individuals ([M: 33,473; F: 34,923] [Host community: 52,985; Returnees; 9,145; IDPs: 6,266]) in remote and hard-to-reach areas in Mayendit, Mayom, and Maiwut counties; and will strengthen the health system resilience for service provision and delivery. The project will prioritize most vulnerable population, including children under five (CU5), Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women and Girls (PBWG), People living with Disabilities (PWD) and Elderly.
Samaritan’s Purse project objectives will be achieved through a collaborative and an integrated health services provision and delivery approach, with the State Ministry of Health (SMOH), County Health Departments (CHDs) and other implementing partners in WASH and Nutrition Sectors. The intervention approach will include establishing and deploying context-specific Mobile Medical Units (MMUs); Temporary Health Post (THP) where static health facilities are non-existent, and supporting existing community-based health structure, the Boma Health Initiative, a South Sudan’s formal community-led health system structure that bridges the gap between health facilities and communities by providing an integrated package of health promotion and disease prevention activities with the help of Boma Healthcare Workers (BHW). The MMU/THP will be staffed by skilled medical professionals recruited from the local communities, working alongside the BHWs from the same catchment areas.
Samaritan’s Purse will also support strengthening the health system by recruiting and training of healthcare workers (HCWs) including boma health workers (BHWs); ensure regular availability of essential pharmaceuticals to deliver essential life-saving healthcare services; and support the Ministry of Health (MoH) in disease surveillance and reporting for outbreak-prone diseases. In an event of disease outbreaks or large population displacement, an emergency response will be implemented during the acute phase of such emergencies, to significantly reduce excess crisis-related mortality and morbidity. The response will include rapid establishment of a Temporary Health Post (THP) at the outbreak locations or within displaced population settlements.
During the life of the project, Samaritan’s Purse will deploy will four (4) MMUs (one in Mayendit, two in Mayom, and one in Maiwut); establish one THP in Nortikur Boma, Kuerbone Payam, Mayom County; recruit and train healthcare workers (HCWs), including 70 BHWs and 30 skilled HCWs; and provide timely response to at least one public health emergency.

1. Needs

South Sudan's health system remains heavily reliant on international aid, with limited funding resulting in poor primary healthcare coverage, reduced functionality, and frequent closures of health facilities (HFs). According to the 2024 ISNA, only 57 per cent of the population can access the nearest HF within an hour, while 44 per cent identify long distances as a key barrier. Severe flooding has worsened these challenges, disrupting services at 154 static HFs nationwide. Mayendit, Mayom, and Maiwut Counties are among the areas hardest hit by flooding and the influx of returnees from Sudan, further straining healthcare services. The most recent WHO/UNICEF estimates of measles and Penta 3 coverage is 72 per cent and 73 per cent respectively. Due to this low coverage, there have been multiple measles outbreaks across the country since 2022. Malaria remains endemic and the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, followed by acute respiratory infections (ARIs), and diarrhea. Recurrent outbreaks of measles, yellow fever, poliovirus, meningitis, hepatitis E and cholera, combined with emerging threats from Mpox and Marburg virus in neighboring countries, present significant public health risks. Maternal mortality rates at 1,223 per 100,000 live births, and the under-five mortality rate at 98.8 deaths per 1,000 live births, remain alarmingly high. A recent GBV health analysis conducted in July 2024, reports challenges with access to early intervention for GBV, especially at lower-level facilities due to limited staffing and poor infrastructure, limiting referrals. Only 28 per cent of health facilities provide GBV care, with 43 per cent offering services.

2. Project activities

Samaritan’s Purse will support four (4) MMUs and a temporary health post, across 18 payams in 3 counties in both states, including ensuring regular availability and prepositioning of medicines, supplies and equipment recommended for a Primary Health Care Center (PHCC). The MMUs/THP will provide essential health services following the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services for South Sudan, including outpatient consultations for communicable and non-communicable diseases alongside disease surveillance; care for injuries and trauma; antenatal, postnatal, neonatal and women’s health care; the expanded program on immunization (EPI); care for survivors of sexual and gender-based violence; mental health and psychosocial support and referrals; screening for malnutrition and community health education activities. The BHWs will focus on delivering the Boma Health Initiative Service Package for South Sudan in a bid to efficiently provide an integrated package of services at the boma level. Services include integrated community case management for childhood illnesses (iCCM), health promotion and disease prevention messaging, and support Care Group (CG) members /Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) with malnutrition screening services. The BHWs will align their health and hygiene messaging with that of Community Health Clubs Volunteers established by the WASH sector in the respective communities. Additionally, Samaritan’s Purse will provide transportation support to the most vulnerable groups, particularly, children U5, PLWs and PWDs to access referral services whenever needed. The referral services include, complicated labor, medically complicated Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and illnesses limited to severe form of malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea in children U5. The support is in form of financial reimbursement of a fixed amount for transportation cost incurred in accessing the referral services. In the event of upsurges in health service needs due to disease outbreaks and large-scale population displacements within the proposed counties, an emergency response activity will be implemented during the acute phase of such emergencies. This response will include the rapid establishment of a Temporary Health Post (THP) at the outbreak locations or within displaced population settlements. Services will be provided through an integrated approach that includes surveillance, vaccination, case management, infection prevention and control (IPC), and risk communication and community engagement (RCCE), integrated with other WASH services. The duration of each response will be limited to maximum of three months and subsequently handed over to the MoH or suitable implementing partners. The MMU/THP will be staffed by skilled medical professionals recruited from the local communities, working alongside community healthcare workers (CHWs) from the same catchment areas. They will be trained and mentored on the major causes of morbidity and mortality; disease surveillance and reporting; dispensing essential practices, including pharmaceutical management, proper storage, and usage documentation of medical supplies and provided with Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and treatment guidelines. Technical support and routine monitoring will be conducted using a standardized supervision checklist to ensure proper use of these tools and to enhance service delivery. A context-specific feedback mechanism such as hotlines, suggestion boxes, and community feedback committees will be established to monitor service effectiveness, while community representatives help ensure transparency and accountability.
