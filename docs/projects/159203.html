Project Summary

The public health system in Somalia has significantly under performed for many years because of longstanding conflict and instability and is further overburdened by the impact of climate shocks such as drought and flooding. Somalia has the world’s highest child mortality rate with the risk of childhood mortality highest between birth and one month, accounting for approximately 45 per cent of death among children under the age five of years. Nearly 54 per cent of health facilities do not provide an EPI services driving coverage below 50 per cent, leading to a rapid accumulation of children susceptible to vaccine preventable illnesses and exposing communities to the risk of communicable diseases. Furthermore, one in three people is or has been affected by mental illness in Somalia, a rate higher than other low-income and war-torn countries. People with disabilities and special needs also face profound vulnerability due to a lack of services and support. The proposed areas are among those with the highest number of IDPs and communities affected by droughts and conflicts. To support and provide these vulnerable communities with essential health services, CPD is proposing health facility based as well as community based services provision through mobile teams in hard to reach areas to realize the dream of the recently launched Universal Health Coverage (UHC) by paving the way for ‘health for all, by all’ by improving access to basic health facilities and reducing financial hardships associated with health care in these areas. The project will directly target to reach 112,962 affected people with these essential services. The services to be provided will include outpatient consultations for both males and females (boys and girls included), child health services, immunizations for both children under five and WCBA; BEmONC services to include skilled birth attendance, ANC and PNC; Health promotion activities and referrals for complicated cases. CPD is already implementing health services in these areas and seeks a continuation of these essential services to leverage on the gains already made and have a healthy community at the end of the project including seeking the availability of mental health care services within the integrated essential services packages at primary care level. CPD is also separately proposing for nutrition activities in the same target locations and this will be integrated with the health activities both at facility and community levels.

Mitigation Measures

1. 1b. Protection of civilians: To ensure that people are not displaced in search of humanitarian assistance, CPD will do the following;• Liaise with communities and their leaders to identify the areas deemed safe closest to them. These are areas where the static facilities will be situated.• In as much as possible, security allowing, services will be taken to them. CPD will initiate mobile/outreach health services so that the community is not disadvantaged in terms of geographical coverage.• To prevent community tensions, by providing the local/host community with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. CPD will consult with both already displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities.• CPD will strive to provide integrated projects to ensure that not only health is available for communities but by integrating it with Nutrition, WASH and FSL.• CPD will conduct community awareness and sensitization activities to build the capacity of the communities and inform of their rights so that they can be informed and that services will be available in their areas of residence.2b. Exclusion and marginalization:• The project will enhance gender equality as the services will be provided equally across the general population for men, women, boys and girls. However, some interventions are age specific such as maternal and child health• CPD will ensure that beneficiaries know of available health services and assistance where/how to obtain it. Communicate information through various means to ensure communication reaches the broader community and consider literacy levels (eg. health community workers, door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms).• We will identify the power dynamics in the area. Who has access to health care? Who manages facilities? Who is able to influence decisions on provision of health services? In this case women, boys and girls will have a different forum for discussions during design and implementations such as FGDs etc. this will help in ensuring their voices are heard and respected. • CPD will ensure to respect fairness and respect the local cultural and religious sensitivities. For example, female personnel will be deployed to areas where women, boys and girls are served. In this regard, during design phase, female employees will collect data from women as culturally women open up to their fellow women; BEmONC services will be conducted by female nurses/midwives while health staff with skills and experiences working with children be employed.• CPD will involve and consult all the target groups appropriately. In this case, consultations with women, men, boys, girls, and other interest groups will be held to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for health centre location, design and services.• Where available, CPD will engage with women and children organizations and other interest groups to get their ideas and how best to serve them.3c. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection:• Ensure the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe by identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. • CPD will ensure that patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private with the provisions for separate examination rooms for males and females. Daily security briefing will be conducted by the security focal point to ensure the area is safe before work starts and in the outreach areas, community leaders would be consulted before the team leaves for the site.• Health facilities will have exterior lighting as well as the pathways to ensure that clients are safe at night.

Please provide further explanation

The health project will be undertaken in both existing health facilities within the public domain in each of the indicated areas to enable access and capacity development. The proposed health programme, which is in line with National Development Plans (NDP) and UNICEF Strategic Plan is strategically designed to significantly address the need for quality health delivery system to help address preventive and curative approaches to disease management, all the while contributing to the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), World Health Assembly resolutions and Global Nutrition Targets for 2025. The project facilities shall be handed over to the local Health management Committees in charge of the health facilities which will be linked to the health management structures at District, Regional and Federal Levels. The project will be linked to existing large scale health interventions to complement their work. Key beneficiaries of the health provision as planned by the project will include IDPs and rural vulnerable populations who cannot manage the high cost of private health services. .
