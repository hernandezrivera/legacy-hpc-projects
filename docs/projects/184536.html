Project Summary

Pastoralist and agro-pastoral communities and IDPs in Somaliland have lost most of their livelihood assets including loss of livestock, farms, pasture, and other socio-economic impact related to the effects of recurrent drought, Epidemic disease of COVID19 and Desert locust, which means they are mostly under IPC3+ according to September FSNAU report, on the other hand, it worth mentioning that the current security situation of Somaliland regions remains stable but project target communities in Sool, Sanaag, Awdal and Togdheer regions needs to be supported through enhanced food security, nutrition, WASH and protection interventions for the most vulnerable communities and IDPs.
However, anticipated La Niña conditions are associated with an elevated likelihood of consecutive below-average rainfall seasons, and Somalia faces a high risk that drought conditions will be developed by early 2021 while desert locusts could exacerbate crop and pasture losses. As a result, a high proportion of the population that is projected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2) in October-December 2021 could deteriorate to Crisis (IPC Phase 3) in early 2022. The Food Security Cluster projected an averagely more than 3 million Somalis (1.4 M in Somaliland) to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) thoroughly in 2022, with projected below-average rains for the coming months.
Against the above Situation, the purpose of the proposed project is that the food security situation of vulnerable and affected households in Somaliland is stabilized and households are more resilient towards drought, infestations of desert locusts, and other shocks. In addition, IDPs and rural communities in Somaliland being the most vulnerable ones, are having challenges related to access to resources, justice and limited knowledge on their basic protection rights. In addition, the existence of GBV related issues is common with the absence of an effective referral mechanism.
On the other hand, due to their increased vulnerabilities as a result of previous successive droughts, Covid-19 and Locust invasion, other challenges encountered by target communities include challenges related to water scarcity which resulted in women and children traveling a long distances and spending more time to fetch water which has protection concerns, other WASH-related challenges experienced by most of Somaliland agro-pastoral communities and IDPs include poor hygiene, lack of garbage collection points, limited knowledge on hygiene and sanitation protocols and lack of WASH committees which collectively contributes/triggers diseases and conflicts
HAVOYOCO will focus on supporting crisis-affected communities in Sool, Sanaag, and Togdheer where it currently has an active presence, experience as well as good relations and reputation with all stakeholders mainly communities and government institutions.
HAVOYOCO designed a life-saving humanitarian intervention that aims to build the resilience of target communities
Speciﬁcally, the proposed project will contribute to three objectives including;
1. Improving access to food through conditional and unconditional transfers with more focus on locust and rehabilitation of other communal infrastructures and as well as Provision of seasonal livelihood inputs support (agriculture and livestock). These will be achieved through conditional and unconditional cash transfers, and agricultural inputs support,
Other planned interventions include building the capacity of target communities to reduce their vulnerability related to climatic shocks through disaster risk management, and effective response mechanism through the development and implementation of DRR plans as well as mainstreaming covid-19 prevention mechanism thoroughly

1. - Projects activities

The project aims to assist 11, 360 individuals consisting of women, men, boys, and girls in disaster-affected communities in Las-anod, Burao, Erigavo, and Buhodle districts through integrated life-saving humanitarian interventions including improving access to food security and livelihood, as well as strengthening the protection of most vulnerable communities. For Food security and livelihood, HAVOYOCO planned the Provision of conditional and unconditional cash transfer (mobile money) for 850HH and 600HH respectively • Provision of agricultural inputs (seeds, farm tools, and tractor hours) for 800HH, GAP, post-harvesting and fodder management training for 400 HH • Livestock management (Training 40 CAHW and equipping them VET kits) for • Establishing and training Village saving loans associations with 200 HH • Development and implementation of Community early warning systems and DRR plans for 9 communities •

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

According to the result of assessment conducted by HAVOYOCO in target communities, Cash transfer programming is preferred according to community needs to help HH to get easy access to their immediate food security, also the modality of delivering cash relief is easier than an in-kind, in addition, the transfer mode is safer as we can use mobile money transfer for easy access purpose while accountability measures will be maintained throughout the implementation of the project through effective monitoring and evaluationCurrently HAVOYOCO is using Telesom and Somtel cash transfer system to support households in food crisis in Somaliland regions, The use of cash transfer recognizes that that at any given time the households that are affected by crisis experience multiple needs. Beneficiaries are best placed to decide what their immediate household needs and the cash gives them the flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities. Beneficiaries targeted for Food Access through the conditional and unconditional cash transfers will be vulnerable households- with members who are chronically ill or have disabled members, Elderly-headed households or child-headed households or female-headed households, IDPs, unsupported returnees and host communities and destitute pastoralist households.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

HAVOYOCO considered several factors that affects market functionality with project beneficiaries including 1. Are food-insecure households well connected to local markets? Physical access to markets is a prerequisite for a successful cash transfer; this question was integrated into household-level data and surveys. 2. How will local demand respond to transfers? Estimating the potential increase in demand is also relatively easy provided data from multiple sources can be synthesized and identified that markets are supportive in terms of allowing food and other necessary commodities availability to the beneficiaries- Emergency market mapping assessment will be done to review any changes in the market. 4.6 - Mitigation Measures

HAVOYOCO project staff will ensure that the planned activities are implemented with the respect of maintaining Covid-19 prevention mechanism as well as protecting the rights and dignity of beneficiaries, risk that beneficiaries are exposed to GBVs during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. Mitigation - Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. - Identify central locations. - Avoid placing distribution points near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, or violence. Weapon bearers should not be present inside distribution points. - Consider the frequency of distributions – the greater the time between distributions the larger the quantities distributed, which puts beneficiaries at risk of attacks and pillage. - Ensure the ration size is appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them. 2. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Mitigation -Consider separate waiting areas for males and females. -Have a proportionate number of female health staff. If female doctors/nurses are not available, consider a female doctor rotation between locations. In this case, women must be adequately informed of which days a female doctor will be available. -Employ health staff with skills and experiences working with children - Install lights outside and on the access route to the facility 5. The risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measures - Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups -Identify individuals who lack documentation to prevent exclusion from services. -promote active participation and consultation with target beneficiates including women, girls and disabled persons during project planning, implementing and monitoring. - Provide information about services in both verbal and written form -Make facilities and infrastructure disability-friendly.Training and sensitizing target communities with HAVOYOCO CRM with free hotline toll, whereby they can raise complaints, suggestions and receive timely responses
