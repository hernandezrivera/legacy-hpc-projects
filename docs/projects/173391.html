Project Summary

ETS will continue to manage the Common Feedback Mechanism (CFM), a toll-free, country-wide number that affected communities can call to obtain information on the COVID-19 pandemic, humanitarian assistance programs, submit their feedback and get referrals to the humanitarian organizations that are best-suited to handle their issue(s). The CFM also enables the humanitarian community to collect feedback directly from the affected populations, better understand the needs on ground, and take better informed programming decisions. The CFM was launched in February 2020, and will continue to enhance its services, referral processes, reporting and partnerships throughout 2021.

1 - Needs

An important way in which the humanitarian community supports community awareness and engagement, as well as enabling communities to provide information on their preferences and perspectives, is through the Inter-Agency Common Feedback Mechanism (CFM). Moreover, in emergencies, the best practice recommends using a single phone number as an entry point for queries from affected populations, and a coordinated mechanism to later channel these queries to individual mandated organizations. This single number enables members of the affected population to have easier access to information about the response and assistance available to them, speeds up the resolution of issues and allows greater access for affected population to decision makers within NGOs, UN and the government, thereby improving aid delivery efficiency and accountability to affected populations. Since its establishment in February 2020, the ETS-managed CFM call centre has answered more than 14,400 calls by the end of September 2020. While numbers were modest in the initial days, given limited awareness, calls sharply increased at the end of March when it was officially requested by the National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) to serve as one of the official national channels for COVID-19 information – going from 50 calls per week in mid-March to 2,400 calls the week after. The call centre continues to play a crucial part in the health response to prevent further spread of the virus in Libya. Since March, the average number of calls has stabilized to around 600 calls per week. However, there is still a need to improve the way that the humanitarian community engages with affected communities and create avenues for affected communities to provide information in relation to their needs and preferences. Results from the MSNA survey showed that of respondents who reported not receiving assistance in the last six months, but needing it, also reported they did not know how to access assistance in their area.

2 - Activities

ETS will work closely with its partners to improve the referral systems and ensure callers’ cases can be addressed in the most efficient and effective way. In addition, the ETS will continue to engage with partners to enhance the way calls are reported – including statistics on geographic location, age and gender, and also the most frequently asked topics or issues received – to enable partners to have a better insight into the needs and issues faced by affected populations and so that programmes can be adapted to best suit the needs on the ground.
