Project Summary

A core component of Camp Management roles and responsibilities is to engage with displaced populations to:
a) ensure access to assistance, protection and services in camps and camp-like settings.
b) Inform, consult, mobilize and support community structures in a location, such as camp leadership or women’s groups, to address barriers to participation, particularly for underrepresented groups, so they can participate in camp activities and decision-making processes at every stage of the camp lifecycle.
However, throughout 2018 there has been a rise in the occurrences of protests in camps – sometimes exacerbated by miscommunication with humanitarian responders. At the same time an overreliance on camp leadership for engagement does not consider their actual representative value.
Instead for effective implementation of project to take place we need to develop a comprehensive understanding and engagement of:
• Relations between leadership and community
• Existing/organic and imposed/inorganic leadership structures
• Who the local social influencers or decision makers are
• The Information flows within and between settlements
It is widely recognised that it is vital for recipients of humanitarian assistance to feel that they have a voice and agency in determining the assistance that is needed. In addition, it is also recognized that with strong community engagement strategies, recipients of humanitarian assistance can provide relevant and timely feedback to improve the humanitarian response. However, it is also highly likely that protest instigated by a minority of the population can hinder an effective humanitarian response and that a feasible mitigation strategy is to effectively and efficiently engage the community-at-large in conversation and feedback about their felt needs.
Therefore, to overcome disputes and engagement challenges that are sure to arise in the humanitarian context in South Sudan, this proposal presents a comprehensive package of innovative Community Communications and Engagement (CCE) activities, led by the development of both a physical and virtual (radio-led) town hall meeting system involving host and IDP communities with humanitarian agency representatives. This will ensure a coordinated engagement strategy with all parties, that provides full accountability to all affected populations and mitigates risk of conflict between the two groups.
This project will build upon Internews’ expertise in CCE and Communication with Communities (CwC), particularly the pre-existing networks of listening groups, focus groups and broadcasting systems (both BBTT and radio), in addition to, a systematic and formalized town hall meeting system. This will be implemented in Malakal, Juba, Wau and Bentiu and their surrounding collective sites, and once implemented they will provide clear avenues for all humanitarians in these locations to further engage and communicate with the communities they serve.

Needs

The heart of this response will be the creation of structured town hall meetings that allow all trained stakeholders, previously selected and representative of all sections of the community, along with other relevant stakeholders to be informed and take part in the discussion process. These meetings will not be a free discussion but rather a very structured and facilitated dialogue with all participants receiving training in CCE before they can take part in the meeting. Within the meeting itself, only agenda items will be discussed, in systematic manner. In effect, the primary purpose of these meetings will be to make sure that information is shared between all sides rather than a place for decision-making. They are designed to supplement and consolidate community engagement rather than be any form of decision-making process. The town hall meetings will serve as an effective and direct way to communicate and receive feedback from the community. By having a continuing series of facilitated town hall meetings with representatives from different sectors of the community at each meeting, including women’s groups, religious leaders, teachers, persons with disabilities, etc., thus the risk of information gate-keeping is mitigated. For example, leaders and mobilisers would be informed with relevant and accurate information to share with their audiences. Further, the town hall meeting will provide an open forum for Camp Management and humanitarian partners to engage directly with the community, as a first step towards promoting community-ownership of sites. In addition, the town hall meetings will be recorded so that information can be disseminated to a wider audience through Internews’ humanitarian radio station or Boda Boda Talk Talk network of mobile speakers. This will further ensure that accountability to affected populations is met. To close the feedback loop and provide relevant and timely data about reactions and feelings within each location, Internews will leverage their existing feedback collection mechanism and listening group meetings to ensure that conversations within the town hall meetings truly reflects the needs of the affected population. The project will also build on Internews’ experience with rumour tracking, using this mechanism to track dangerous rumours spreading within the camps and collective centres. Feedback and rumours tracked through the community feedback mechanism will be shared with the humanitarian community through weekly information bulletins and regular presentations. Town hall meetings will also be held with trained stakeholders in locations outside of the camp. By involving host and camp communities, they can encourage deeper engagement between communities to build peaceful links between the two communities, with the encouragement and inclusion of the humanitarian community. Town hall meetings outside the camp will engage the camp radio stations to provide clear and transparent communication between host and camp populations. These town hall meetings will also provide a crucial point of direct engagement between UNMISS, humanitarians and the displaced community. These meetings will support information sharing so that IDPs have the confidence to make informed choices about their voluntary and dignified return to places of origin. By engaging with more diverse sections of the affected populations and allowing communities (especially marginalised groups) to provide input in a constructive manner, CCCM partners will have more reliable and accurate information to tailor their service provision to better meet the needs to these groups and mitigate protection risks.

Activities

1: Development and implementation of a localised Town Hall methodology Internews will hire a CCE expert to work with CCCM partners to customise the town hall methodology previously implemented by Internews in other contexts. The adaptation of the methodology will be based on CCCM partners’ needs and the local capacity of each location. The expert will be supported by two dedicated staff at each location. The team will manage the planning and implementation of the town hall meetings. 1.a this will include the organisational planning and recruitment of participants (specifically making sure that vulnerable groups including women, elderly and children) who will be given proper training under activity two. 2: Training in community engagement for local leaders from all civil society groups so that they are equipped to; represent their own communities or groups at the town hall meetings, develop constructive relationships with humanitarians. 3.Training NGO staff to fully participate in CCE activities including in the town hall meetings and effectively utilise the CWC methodology in their daily activities. 4. The recording and broadcasting of all town hall meetings with specialised follow-up programming (radio shows and programs) via BBTT/humanitarian radio stations including targeted programming representing each participating cluster and working group. 5. The continued collection of listening group data including homogenous listening groups working with vulnerable populations (such as women, children and the elderly). 6. The construction of sheltered areas to conduct town hall meetings and hold mobile CFM desks with audio recording so that as many opportunities as possible exist for people to record their feedback. 7. Distributing 1,000 radio sets in and around Malakal and 2,000 sets in and around Bentiu to increase access to radio programming 8. Creation and dissemination of programming for residents outside camps through the installation of fixed speakers and engagement with returnees through special town hall meetings.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The purpose of the project is to provide beneficiaries with access to humanitarians information and to humanitarian decision makers by facilitating a structured public discussion between the two. Additionally information provided during this activity will be distributed to the wider community through Internews radio stations and mobile speaker systems. It will also be distributed to the humanitarian community through written bulletins

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Information will be given in real-time in response to public questions in a public forum. In POC sites, such as Malakal, Internews has been working with other agencies that focus on youth and women’s programming to help alleviate the barriers to access to information experienced by these groups. Internews will rely on its established relationships with existing organizations working with adolescents and women in the POC and harness its existing youth and women listening groups to discuss who and how people should be engaged in the project. The project will use its dedicated women staff to address issues related to girls and women in the camps and make sure that their voices are heard. Regular Round table discussion in the Listening Groups are produced, where efforts are made to bring youth and women to the table to put their concerns, issues and question on difficult issues directly to the respective NGO. These round tables in particular work as ways of 'by-passing' the leadership structures and giving voice to a broader range of people. Through our Information Needs assessments we collect and analyse gender-disaggregated data to than analyse the impact of the crisis on girls, boys, women and men and what this entails in terms of information access. We work to make sure that women, men, boys, and girls can access information equally. Through its presence both at the field level clusters and in Juba, Internews works to ensure coordination and gender mainstreaming in all areas of our humanitarian operations.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

All Internews staff will be trained in awareness towards sexual exploitation, its prevention and will be required to undergo compulsory anti-harassment training on an annual basis. They will understand the correct referral pathways to direct protection cases if working in the field or generating information on the topic. However, in relation to the implementation of this project, its primary purpose will indirectly impact on humanitarian action in protection from sexual exploitation. By capacity building within clusters this project will improve the channels of community engagement for the most vulnerable which will directly impact humanitarian efforts in the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse issues.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Involving those who are excluded from traditional mechanisms is a strength of Internews’ Humanitarian Information Service (HIS) projects in South Sudan. This project will strengthen humanitarian information flows – providing a platform between the clusters and communities to directly empower affected communities, particularly reaching the most vulnerable populations. Internews projects in South Sudan, via its radio stations and BBTT project talks to more than 7000 people a year, face to face, of which less than 20% come from community leaders and usual constituencies.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Internews has led in the area of AAP through the implementation of its Humanitarian Information Services set up specifically following this conflict in South Sudan. The HIS has set up an independent communications feedback loop using more than 200 listening groups across 4 POCs, thus providing a voice for communities and a platform for discussion between all actors. On a yearly basis Internews collects more than 4000 complaints and feedback, using almost 100% of them to inform agencies about services adjustments, or to provide more information to IDPs about specific issues. On average around 60% of the feedback reported to humanitarian organizations by Internews is resolved within a month. The services actively support agencies to deliver with broader impact; reaching those without access to information and allowing them to know what services are available, who is providing them, and, in the absence of these services or a shortfall in standards, who to talk to. This mechanism has significantly increased trust between agencies and affected communities. It is the intention of Internews that the project being proposed would use this approach to support a better response at the macro-level.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The Internews approach to accountability is local and engaging. It uses multiple continuous channels of communication, and puts the community’s need for information at the centre of our conversations. It ensures a two-way dialogue and wider representation for people affected by the crisis. Not only is there an exchange of information, but agencies are assisted to tailor their interventions based on the feedback they receive from all communities in an affected area. The conversation shifts from an NGO-led need to share information and a camp leadership claim to represent a majority, to an information exchange driven by all peoples’ need for information. Our 5 Star CWC Rating tool, shared with the cluster and the CCE WG of NGO’s in South Sudan, helps to move CCE/AAP/CWC strategies from an ‘NGO conversation’ to a ‘Conversation with Communities’ using multiple channels of communication that reinforce each other and adapt their programs to the reality on the ground, strengthening the impact of their interventions. To support skills training, Internews has AAP/CWC technical advisors to train in the implantation of this tool and to build the capacity of partner NGOs operating in the various humanitarian contexts. Internews has led advocacy and coordination amongst NGOs and UN agencies on CWC and accountability to communities

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project is based on continuous assessments of the protection risk posed to the most vulnerable groups in society due to a lack of access to information to make informed decisions and a lack of access to decision makers to inform their provision of humanitarian assistance. On a weekly basis Internews collects an average of 20 feedback related to protection topics which accounts for 12% of overall feedback.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Internews continuously works to survey the needs and concerns of people far beyond that of tradition leadership structures. By actively recruiting stakeholders from across power dynamics and vulnerable groups in the community this project will empower the voices of those who often go unheard in communities. By providing a forum for their concerns and questions to be addressed the town hall meeting system has the potential to embolden the voices of vulnerable groups and empower them towards greater participation.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The proposed methodology will take place in a very controlled environment where trained stakeholder, who represent all section of the community, will facilitate the answering of community questions by humanitarian partners. Due to the requirement to submit questions in advance, any subject areas which could pose a protection threat or provoke an adverse reaction from the community will be discussed with the appropriate partners. E.g. protection partners will advise on protection issues and security partners will be able to advise on politically sensitive questions and mitigate against potential threats. Internews' projects in the last 5 years have demonstrated how when people have relevant, accurate and timely information, tensions are reduced. CCCM and camp managers in Bentiu, Malakal and Juba asked the programs to be played during times when tensions were likely to be high such as during food distribution and registration. This was evidenced numerous times including for registration processes when the projects worked closely with agencies to deliver information in simple colloquial language that demonstrated peoples 'rights' and built trust - helping to avoid a 'them and us' mentality.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In addition the collection of feedback data by Internews is highly valued by protection partners as it provides a source of continuous information about protection needs on the ground that may not be available any other way. Community engagement officers are given conflict sensitivity reporting training and understand the importance of sharing all sides of a story to come to a fair report of the situation on the ground. The project operates through a do no harm lens and correspondents are given training to help them see themselves as mediators in the site.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Internews will actively recruit stakeholders from across the community placing particular focus on the representation of women, youth and special interest groups. In addition monthly targets will be set with regard to feedback collection in order to ensure that questions discussed in the town hall meeting are also representative. Over 40% of all feedback will be from women, 5% from people over the age of 60 and at least 2 focus groups with special interest groups will take place once a month. By putting these measures in place in addition to public broadcasting of information it is hoped that former barriers to access to information will reduced. It is the intention of Internews to conduct rapid information needs assessments on a minimum of a biannual basis to assess access to beneficiaries within PoC in which the organisation already works and to enable partners to do the same across all sectors. By actively canvassing the opinion of people outside of the current structures of camp leadership, Internews seeks to understand the barriers to access to information of the most vulnerable and often unseen sections of the community.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Internews identifies the most dangerous risk when conducting CCE activities, against which to mitigate, to be negative behaviour or poorly informed beneficiary decision making as a consequence of poor communication, misinformation and unequal provision of communication channels. By mainstreaming tried and tested methodology from Internews and opening up contributions from the community in the field this project will support information provision across the sectors and put in place impact monitoring controls to allow for course correction. As previously mentioned the control mechanisms embedded in the methodology of the town hall meetings should mitigate against any threats to beneficiary safety while participating in this activity.Through follow up Internews listening groups, which monitor the impact of humanitarian information provision on beneficiaries, including the comprehension of the information by the community on a weekly basis. By continuing to survey beyond current structures of camp leadership Internews will mitigate against the risk of over-representing the views of some groups while more vulnerable sections of the population go unheard. This will be done by setting monthly targets of communicating with at least 40% women, 5% people over the age of 60 and by conducting at least two focus group with special interest groups across all locations. In addition to this, Internews continues to contribute to an ongoing beneficiary satisfaction survey through the CCCM cluster which measures the safety concerns of beneficiaries living within the PoC.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

In line with the grand bargain, APP is core to this project. By continuing to contribute to an ongoing beneficiary satisfaction surveys through the CCCM cluster Internews can monitor beneficiaries’ perceptions of how the level of information provision and representations is meeting their needs in the PoCs. The feedback mechanism already employed by internews in PoC's in Malakal, Bentiu, Juba and Wau will monitor the effectiveness of our communications and engagement with affected communities.
