Project Summary

This project will support the monitoring of population movements and conduct site profiling across non- Government-controlled areas in north-west Syria, aiming to provide reliable and verified data for an efficient and coordinated humanitarian response. An online monitoring dashboard will provide humanitarian actors with the latest information per community level, regular/monthly overviews, and trend analysis will be shared with the CCCM Cluster and ICCG members. Additionally, ad-hoc emergency updates will be produced and shared when required.
Technical guidance will be provided to the CCCM Cluster’s members to support data collection.
Protection Risk Analysis
Risk 1: Physical and sexual exploitation and abuse.
Mitigating measures: UNHCR ensures that partners have a Code of Conduct (CoC) in place, supports developing and reviewing PSEA prevention materials, organizes internal and external CoC/PSEA sessions, and follows-up on PSEA issues on a regular basis. PSEA focal points are identified in the operation whom individuals can contact and/or report misconduct by humanitarian staff.
Risk 2: Assessment fatigue among beneficiaries and the Cluster’s members.
Mitigating measures: The purposes of the monitoring and assessments are clarified to beneficiaries without raising expectations. The tools and questionnaires are developed to be as clear and light as possible.
PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Strategy:
As a strategic priority, UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV sub-Working Group and PSEA network to ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do no harm” principle. In addition to mandatory trainings to its staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who fail to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist cannot renew their partnerships with UNHCR in the following year.
Risk, Assumptions and Mitigation measures:
Risk 1: Security and lack of access
Mitigating measures: Partners are supported to develop and implement Safety & Security and Duty of Care policies. UNHCR’s programmes provide flexibilities for partners to suspend activities when the security of their personnel is threatened.
Risk 2: Interference by local authorities
Mitigating measures: Establishing and sustaining coordination and communication channels with local authorities to raise awareness on the rules and responsibilities of humanitarian actors as well as the de facto authorities on the ground.
Risk 3: Interference in humanitarian assistance by armed groups
Mitigating measures: Open communication lines and relationship are maintained with partners on local developments and specific incidents, and IA coordination is informed about (major) incidents to ensure unified and effective messaging to Armed Opposition Groups (AOG).

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Needs:Humanitarian organizations operating in north-west Syria require comprehensive and accurate data on displacement trends and conditions at site for internally displaced persons, essential for effective humanitarian intervention planning.Throughout 2023, the CCCM Cluster tracked over 291,230 individual movements from January to December. On a monthly basis, the Cluster documented an average of 26,475 population movements, with significant spikes due to the earthquake early in the year and a surge in armed conflict in the final quarter (with 108,906 movements in February and 97,172 in October).This system provides a snapshot within 24 hours that includes disaggregated data (by age, gender, vulnerability, and shelter type) on the number of IDPs and identifies their primary needs at the community level. The Cluster also collaborates with specialized organizations to gather data on population movement, needs, and gaps, ensuring broad geographical coverage and technical expertise.Through its robust data collection and reporting mechanisms, including the monthly IDP Site Integrated Monitoring Matrix (ISIMM) and the bi-annual ISIMM Plus, the CCCM Cluster offers detailed profiling, with disaggregated figures and data, of more than 1,510 IDP sites in north-west Syria. This data aids the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) in coordinating service delivery, preventing service duplication, addressing urgent needs promptly, prioritizing vulnerable populations, and advocating for equitable access to services, protection, and durable solutions for displaced persons. Implementation of Activities:To manage data collection efficiently, the CCCM Cluster has implemented an advanced inter-agency system that allows for the coordination, triangulation, and when possible, verification of data.UNHCR’s partner, Muzun for Humanitarian & Development (MHD), will cover nine districts hosting 1,510 listed IDP sites (ISIMM December 2023 Update) and other spontaneously established locations that could be identified in the future as part of the coverage area. The areas to be covered are Jisr Al-shoughour, Jabal Saman, Jarablus, Al-bab, Azaz, Afrin, Ariha, Idleb, and Harim. MHD has established a five-month realistic work plan that includes all types of data collection outlined by UNHCR. Therefore, it will contribute significantly to the CCCM Cluster’s response considering the strict timeline of the Cluster’s data collection activities of IDP Site Integrated Monitoring Matrix (ISIMM), ISIMM Plus and ad-hoc reporting.ACTED/REACH, a partner of UNHCR, uses the Rapid Displacement Monitoring (RDM) system to quickly capture the current locations of displaced individuals. ACTED/REACH will track the movements of IDPs in more than 1,100 communities in north-west Syria, using a continuous displacement monitoring system. It will also carry out movement monitoring of communities experiencing large displacement on a needs-basis.
