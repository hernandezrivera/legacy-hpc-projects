Project Summary

According to the FSNAU-FEWSNET-2021- Post Gu assessments findings, nearly 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis or worse (IPC phase 3 or higher) outcomes from now and through at least December 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. In addition, the latest HNO 2022 report predicts approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five years, which is total acute malnutrition burden in whole of Somalia, who will likely be facing acute malnutrition between August 2021 and July 2022 if urgent treatment and emergency nutrition support/assistance are not provided. The increase in the trajectory in the number of children in need nutrition assistance in 2022 could be attributed to lose of households purchasing power, high fertility rate, recurrent perennial environmental shocks, conflicts, economic deterioration compounded with the COVID-19 pandemic. The report paints gloomy picture for the rural communities in Somalia who tend to face the highest levels of deprivation of access to services, including preventive and curative nutritional services.
The proposed project aims at providing integrated Nutrition treatment and prevention services for drought affected under five boys, girls and caregivers in Daynile, Dharkenley of Benadir region and Afgoye district of Lower Shabelle region. It will contribute to reduction of nutrition related morbidity and mortality rates. SOYDA intervention will target pregnant and lactating mothers together with children aged below 5 years among the host communities, IDPs and returnees. Active case finding for malnourished cases will be done through mass screening of children aged below 5 years and pregnant and lactating mothers for rapid identification of malnourished cases. Moderate acutely malnourished children and pregnant and lactating mothers will be referred for enrolment in the SFP programs run by other actors in Benadir and Lower Shabelle Region The project will support a network of 6 fixed Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTP and 12 mobile sites fully equipped with OTP commodities including Ready to Use Therapeutic Foods (RUTF), essential medicines for treatment of communicable diseases, vaccines, trained OTP nurses, vaccinators and hygiene promoters. Severe acutely malnourished children with medical complications will be referred by the mobile clinics and the CHWs to the stabilization Centre in Afgooye and Benadir hospital for nutrition rehabilitation. Hygiene promoters will during the activities of the mobile clinics disseminate hygiene promotion messages to the communities to prevent outbreak and spread of communicable diseases like AWD/cholera and measles. The project will also provide micronutrient support by distributing multiple micronutrients (MNPs) and Vitamin A supplements through the static and mobile clinics. Staff dedicated to the static and mobile OTPs will be subjected to proper trainings on infant and young child feeding, integrated management of acute malnutrition and nutrition, health and hygiene promotion. Efforts will also be made to link households with children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) conditions and suspected cholera cases to health, WASH interventions and food security/ livelihood programs.
SOYDA will ensure that people with disabilities participate in the implementation of project activities by ensuring that they are represented in all community meetings. During project implementation, segregated focus group discussions will be held with the community to identify gender inequalities, discuss all the genders' needs, and understand the risks faced by people with disabilities in accessing nutrition services.
There will be regular coordination meetings at the field level between WASH, Health and Nutrition clusters to foster synergy, avoid duplication of efforts/ resources and share information on response efforts.
