Project Summary

MedGlobal ’s intervention will contribute to improving access to health care services and to increase the well-being of the conflict-affected population in Idlib and Aleppo Governorates. This project will focus on ensuring the provision of health services and quality of care for at-risk health groups with limited access to the health care they need.
Name of the Health Facilities:
Tal Elhajar PHC in Tal Elhajar/ Aleppo
Kansafra Camp PHC in Burdaqly/ Idleb
Catchment population area : 70,000
Target Population 30,000
The conflict has impacted individuals across NWS, particularly in the proposed communities where the population suffered from being targeted and displaced several times, undermining the resilience of individuals with need for specific care and services, such as persons with disabilities and older people, by limiting their access to continuity of services and their normal support systems. The Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme estimates that up to 30% of the households in NWS include a head of household with a disability and that at least 27% of the population (aged 12 years and above) in Syria have a disability. This is disproportionally high number compared to the global average of 15%. Moreover, Landmines, Explosive Remnants of War, Airstrikes, Improvised Explosive Devices and violence in general continue to put civilians at risk, leading to countless death, physical and psychological injuries related to the conflict.
MedGlobal will directly provide a package of Primary Health Care, SRH, and MHPSS services to conflict-affected communities through the PHC Centers locations which have been directly affected by the returned wave of hostilities and IDP influx.
MedGlobal PHC Centers will respond to the health needs of IDPs and host community. Strong links with the local community have led MedGlobal to develop the proposed project, responding to the priorities and needs identified by the community itself as well as by the need assessment done by MedGlobal Medical Team. The project has also been designed in close coordination with the Health Cluster members and Idleb Health directorate IHD, and humanitarian actors in the area to ensure that integrated and relevant services are provided to the targeted population.
Medical services will be provided to all groups and ages. As per EHPS issued by Health Cluster, and based on targeting 50000 population, Medical capacity meet with PHC Center classification to be applied in this project.
Medical team consists of 4 GPs Doctors, 1 pediatrician,5 Nurses, 2 midwifes, 2 psychosocial workers, 2 lab technicians,2 pharmacy technicians, and 10 CHW for each PHC will deal directly with the medical needs in the targeted area, through providing essential health services for all population including Mental Health consultations, NCDs, and SRH related topics to reduce mortality and morbidity rate.
As per the project design, MedGlobal health staff and medical supplies could be mobilized for emergency response in other areas of intervention in coordination with other stakeholders. Moreover, due to the volatile context in northwest Syria, the identified location could be modified and additional locations could be added to respond to the most pressing health needs in the area of intervention after coordination with Health Cluster, health authorities, getting their approval

4 - Needs

With medical facilities ravaged by years of conflict, healthcare workers fear they lack the resources necessary to effectively treat patients through comprehensive medical care. According to a report from the International Rescue Committee, the conflict has left just 64% of Syria's hospitals and 52% of primary health care centers functioning. Additionally, 70% of the health workforce has fled the country since the start of the conflict, according to the World Health Organization. Partially as the result of repeated bombardment and the psychological toll of living in conflict, the Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme estimates that 57% of households in Northwest Syria have at least one member with a disability and 24% of those households have multiple members with a disability. Moreover, landmines, airstrikes, and improvised explosive devices continue to expose civilians in Northwest Syria to physical and psychological injuries and death. in this project, and in coordination with HI humanity and inclusion Organization, MedGlobal will ensure targeting disabilities(all groups and ages) by providing iclused services in the PHCs to increase access to health care. all data related will be clarified in DHIS2 and disaggregated accordingly.

5 - Implementation of activities

MedGlobal will directly provide primary health care, sexual and reproductive health, and mental health and psychological support services to conflict-affected communities by operating two primary health care centers( HeRAMS codes B0208026 & G0703198) , and deploying Medical team consists of 4 GPs Doctors,1 pediatrician,5 Nurses,2 midwifes,2 psychosocial workers,2 lab technicians,2 pharmacy technicians, for each PHC will deal directly with the medical needs in the targeted area, through providing essential health services for all population including Mental Health consultations, NCDs, SRH,& Nutrition related topics to reduce mortality and morbidity rate. A network of 10 community health workers for each location will also be recruited, trained, and deployed in order to conduct GBV protection and Nutrition(MUAC measure)activities and refer those cases to the appropriate services.The connection between PHC, and the community health worker network will increase access to and improve the quality of a variety of health services for conflict-affected, vulnerable communities in Northwest Syria. MEAL team will work on tracking the activity performance by the following:1.measuring the beneficiary’s satisfaction by conducting satisfaction survey with a sample of random cases hosted in the Field/Facilities to collect their satisfaction on the services. 2. prepare an IPTT. 3. prepare a specific tools such as “beneficiaries database, referral database, new case tracker, feedback tracker.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

In MedGlobal(MG) Organization protection main principles are mainstreamed into all the Organization’s project cycles and in all humanitarian activities and work aiming at enhancing MG’s programmes quality to enable the most vulnerable members of communities to access the services that MG seek to provide; contributes to creating a safer, more protective environment for the people we serve, ensures that MG is aware of the possible harm that its activities can cause and that MG take steps to avoid this; and empowers staff and communities to support and protect themselves thereby making our interventions more sustainable, this is can be clearly seen through two key underpinning of our integration approach. First, linkages and integration of services (in health, WASH, FSL, and child protection) where feasible and with the establishment of community based referral mechanisms where appropriate to ensure accountability. Second, through the integration of technical training material and resources between the key sectors to raise and up scale the staff capacities . MG’s approach provides an optimal mechanism for the identification GBV cases of the most vulnerable children and women providing referrals pathways mechanisms that are available to the most appropriate service to be tailored to their individual needs. In the proposed project, MG will make sure that within the PHC Center the beneficiaries privacy and their dignity through keeping all medical personal information confidential.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

A key risk is a gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse through humanitarian workers. As Medglobal’s staff will interact with the most vulnerable groups of the community, the power dynamics between staff and beneficiary can be exploited. Mitigation measures 7: MedGlobal trains all staff on PSEA, raises awareness of beneficiaries, and informs on available complaints mechanism. Any complaints, allegations or rumors will be elevated on the country director level and investigated immediately.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Implementation of the project activities is prevented/ stopped by regular clashes, violence, or an upsurge in criminality including theft or kidnapping.Mitigation 1: MG will monitor the security developments and reported criminal instances/ violence in its areas of operation. If an increase in criminality/violence is noted, the project activities will be suspended and alternative areas/activities might be considered under the planned intervention with the approval from health Cluster.Risk 2: staff and patients are at risk due to potential outbreak mitigation measures 2: PPE Kits always are available for staff, regular sterilization for facility and equipment, distribution of surgical masks for patients, and ensure physical distance condition.. risk 3: Loss or damage to project documentation Mitigation measures 3: Maintaining soft copies of documents - Storage of hard copies in safe places and Communication with partner and donor directly Risk 4: Limited access to target areas due to security situation mitigation measures 4: Coordination with community representatives and local authorities, where possible - Presence of staff, offices, and volunteers in target areas Risk 5: Airstrikes or military attacks targeting the project sites Mitigation measures 5: Development of safety and security plan and training - Identification of security focal point in each project team - Establishing communication plan.
