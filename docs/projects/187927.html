Project Summary

In 2021 Human Appeal targeted about 36,500 individuals in food sector in Idlib camps (Ma'aret misrin, Dana, Harem, Kelley). In 2022 The proposed intervention aims at ensuring food security for the conflict-affected Syrian populations in NWS with the focus on the IDPs as being among the most vulnerable groups. Within the targeted population, the interventions largely target those people that have recently been displaced to areas near the Syrian- Turkish borders due to the latest GOS offensive on Maaret Al Numan and Saraqeb cities in early 2020. The referred targeted groups were also affected by the snow storm & floods that happened in January 2022 as well as the consequences of the Covid-19 and Omicron pandemic that largely restricted their ability to find the minimum requirements of life and sustain livelihood. Furthermore, the enormous inflation to the local currency among other conflict-related circumstances also added to the sufferings of the targeted populations.
The proposed intervention aims to target around 8000 HH / 40,000 IDPs with food baskets that will be designed according to the FSL cluster guidelines. Each of the planned food basket will contain 48 kilograms of uncooked/ canned food items (rice, groats, olive, sugar, chickpeas, date, olive oil, flour, lentil, peas, tuna, mortadella) in Ma'aretmisrin area, that is sufficient to reach well nutritional requirements for a family of 5 individuals for a month. this allocation will be for 6 months as a direct distribution in NW Syria camps, with ensure coordinating with FSL cluster and local councils with fully reports.

4 - Needs

Hundreds of tents in Idlib camps affected by the last rains and snow storm, all of their tents requirements were stuck by water as food, NFI, wash items. Three quarters of IDP households report inability to sufficiently meet their household member’s basic needs, citing lack of income as the primary reason (97 percent), and unaffordability of food and essential goods (86 per cent). As a result, the majority of IDPs prioritize access to food and livelihoods support, which is comparable to overall trends. Most IDPs in sites/camps of which have been uprooted more than once (81 per cent) since the start of the crisis. While conflict and insecurity remain the two most important reasons for displacement, the deteriorating economic situation and lack of access to services have become push factors for people to leave and seek improved conditions elsewhere, With an income deficit of 33 per cent as self-reported by the overall IDP population living in sites/camps, IDP households residing in Idleb (39 per cent) indicate even higher financial distress. As a result, a number of segments within the population are in particular need of income generating and livelihood support due to increased vulnerability, particularly female-headed households and heads of households living with a disability who reported the lowest average income across all population groups. For instance, eighteen per cent of IDP households are female-headed.Source: HNO Syria Dec2021

5 - Implementation of activities

To achieve the impact of the aforementioned interventions within the targeted areas of NW Syria, Human Appeal will undertake the following main activities:• Proactively coordination with the FSL cluster to ensure the needs planned to be addressed are in line with the cluster priority locations and modalities.• Ensure proper coordination with the local civil councils and community leaders to promote the facilitated smooth implementation of the planned interventions including the arrangement and execution of the distribution micro plans.• COVID-19 infection prevention and control measures and personal protective measures are in place (social distancing, masks, and sanitizers).• Execute the required procurement procedures (Including open tenders and sampling) regarding the needed supplies, and that is to ensure transparency, competition, quality, and compliance with the applicable regulations.• Build the capacity pf the concerned staff members on PSEA, Child Safeguarding and other cross-cutting issues.• Ensure adequate staffing that guarantees the quality implementation of the planned interventions (This includes staff members that will support the delivery of the assistance to the elderly and people with disabilities).• Ensure that the beneficiary selection criteria are in place ahead of the service provision, and that is for the purpose of reaching out for those most vulnerable and in need.• Undertake the required reporting to the cluster, FTS and platform.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoid causing harm: Concern for the safety and dignity of individuals and avoiding causing further harm is central to mainstreaming protection. The safety of beneficiaries should be the primary consideration in humanitarian action. 2. Ensuring meaningful access to assistance and services: Recognized as a crucial component of all humanitarian interventions, we all must seek to ensure that people have a meaningful opportunity to attain their rights by accessing available services and assistance. Barriers to access can exist in different forms,including logistical and social/cultural factors, insecurity and a lack of information on available assistance and services. 3. Accountability: Setting-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. 4. Participation and empowerment: The humanitarian community should strive to put affected populations at the center of decision-making processes that affect their lives so that services are choice-driven and client-focused. Special attention should also be paid to ensuring the participation of, and consultationwith, the most at-risk and/or marginalized members of society whose voices may not otherwise be heard. 5- Some trainings are given to the field team, and the beneficiaries are instructed to access for note their complaints in the event of any abuse by the field team.
