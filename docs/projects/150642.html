Project Summary

This project targets Palestinian children directly and indirectly exposed to violence and abuse related to the Israeli Occupation, including settler violence, unsafe school environments, home and school demolitions, and detention and/or arrests. War Child Holland (WCH) and its partner will be targeting acutely affected children and families living in Area C (Jordan Valley and Tubas), East Jerusalem (communities outside the West Bank Barrier), and Hebron H2.
This project aims to strengthen the resilience of children, caregivers, schools, and communities in the face of chronic exposure to conflict-related violence by: (1) providing community-based child protection, psychosocial and mental health services to children and their families, (2) supporting parents, school staff, and community-based organizations with psychosocial interventions and capacity building to better take care of and respond to the needs of children in distress, (3) case management and referrals to specialized mental health, social, and educational services, including vocational and remedial education, and (4) addressing IHL and IHRL violations through advocacy initiatives targeting all duty bearers and based on documentation of grave violations.
The outputs are designed to meet HRP 2018-2020 strategic objectives 1: “The rights of Palestinians living under occupation….are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL”; and 2: “providing support to meet the basic needs of the vulnerable population, particularly children”, and the Protection Cluster’s Objective 2 “Protection response mechanisms are in place and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence."
This project aims to strengthen the social safety net around children who have been exposed to conflict-related violence including killings, arrest and detention, injury, and home demolitions. Through the project, children will have access to psychosocial and mental health interventions provided by CBOs supported by WCH, as well as specialized services through case management and referrals. Children acutely affected by conflict-related violence will participate in inclusive structured recreational psychosocial support (PSS) workshops (‘TeamUp”) in and after school. TeamUp was co-developed by War Child, UNICEF and Save the Children in the Netherlands and is now used in multiple conflict-affected contexts. The group sessions are delivered on a drop-in basis, making the activity more inclusive for those children whose circumstances prevent them from regularly attending more structured interventions. Within the sessions, children and adolescents play, learn, make friends and have fun in a safe and protective setting. The activities are designed to allow emergency affected children to explore socioemotional themes in a safe and non-confrontational environment, themes including anger, fear, stress and tension, conflict, respect, bullying, assertiveness, and friendship. The modular, activity-based design of the program allows facilitators to tailor each session to the perceived needs of the particular children attending, including age, space available, physical and sensory abilities. TeamUp also acts as an entry point for children who are experiencing distress or child protection risks to be identified and referred. Children in need of more structured PSS and preventive mental health support will be referred to WCH’s most widely implemented PSS program – the DEALs - to help children and young people at high risk of occupation related violence to develop positive and alternative coping strategies, and engage in regular activities in a safe and protective environment. The DEALs intervention aims to increase children’s wellbeing and resilience by strengthening their social relationships; helping them cope effectively with stress and difficult emotions; and to increase their confidence and sense of efficacy, and has specific curricula for different age groups as well as a dedicated add-on program for adolescent girls.

Facilitators of the PSS activities will be trained in identification and referral of more severely affected children, who will have access to case management from War Child’s case workers. WCH’s caseworkers will have regular presence in the targeted communities, attend the Child Protection Network’s (CPN) monthly meetings in targeted governorates, and will be responsible for implementing community-based child protection activities and case management. In addition to providing timely and ongoing individualized support to the child and family, WCH’s case workers in each location will identify and refer children struggling with child protection and clinical mental health issues to specialized services, in line with the national referral pathway, including the Child Protection Network. Children and families in need of specialized mental health and/or legal services will have access to (EU-funded) individual, group and family counseling and therapy provided by WCH partners Treatment and Rehabilitation Center (TRC), and Palestinian Counseling Center (PCC), and DCIP, under its EIHDR project operating in the targeted locations of this project. Children and their families may also be referred to external mental health and other service providers, (e.g., the Ministry of Health and Palestinian Red Crescent Society), as required. There will also be a particular focus on supporting and enabling young people to re-engage with education (formal, non-formal or vocational) after conflict-related disruptions such as detention, home arrest, and injury through coordination with and referrals to protection and education cluster partners, ensuring that children receive multidisciplinary and holistic support. To ensure efficiency, timeliness, standardized vulnerability and prioritization criteria, and that minimum standards are met, a technical committee made up of WCH, CPN focal persons, and relevant service providers will be formed under this project to routinely monitor and review the referral process, including forms used, information management, feedback on the services provided and address any challenges faced in the referral process and mechanism.
To address the MHPSS needs of caregivers – who are also in need of safe, supportive places to share their worries and challenges, get support, and learn techniques for stress management - parents and school staff will be offered WCH’s caregiver wellbeing interventions designed to help caregivers manage their own stress in order to better support children, and reduce harsh parenting practices. The caregiver support intervention is research informed, and designed based on the identification of common elements of evidence-based stress management programs. It is delivered within communities by community members and para-professionals trained and supervised by War Child. The caregiver support intervention was successfully piloted in Gaza and is now undergoing randomized controlled trial in Lebanon. Referrals for specialized legal and mental health services will also be provided to caregivers, as well as support to those in need of family counseling through WCH’s already established referral network.
Capitalizing on their cohesive network of sources including local media, health workers, CBOs, and community leaders and members throughout the West Bank, partner field workers will closely monitor, document, and rapidly report grave violations against children, including child killings and injuries; settler violence; and recruitment. Strengthened monitoring and reporting of grave violations will yield thematic reports and advocacy initiatives to address protection violations and call for greater accountability on IHL and IHRL by duty bearers.

Needs

This project was designed to complement and fill gaps in community-based child protection and PSS interventions in Area C, Hebron H2, and East Jerusalem, as identified through WCH’s current OCHA project (Improved child protection and psychosocial support service provision for children affected by the conflict in Area C) and EU-funded EIHDR project on prevention, mitigation and rehabilitation for Palestinian children exposed to torture and other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment during arrest and detention. In addition, the target areas were selected based on several needs assessments, including the 2018 HNO, DCIP reports on arrest and detention of children, CPWG mapping of organizations providing child protection services in East Jerusalem, WCH/Palestinian Counseling Center Psychosocial Needs Assessment in Jordan Valley, OCHA household survey of Hebron H2, and direct relations with the communities through implementation of WCH projects. The three targeted areas are exposed to chronic episodes of occupation-related violence, and assessments and reports have identified the need for psychosocial and protection programming to support children and families living under these circumstances. In Hebron H2, according to OCHA’s household survey, 69% of households experienced settlers’ attacks or intimidation in the past 3 years, where a majority reported psychosocial distress as a main result, and 70% said their children have no access to extra curricula activities. In East Jerusalem, home demolitions, detention and house arrest, daily military raids, and restrictions to play and access places of worship have psychosocial and traumatic impacts on children. A study by Palestinian Counseling Center showed that children whose homes had been demolished experience a decline in their academic performance, school drop out, and aggressive behavior and signs of distress. The lives of Palestinian children in Area C are marked by poverty, lack of opportunities and limited access to food, health and education services. They experience frequent displacement, harassment, and constant fear of forced transfer, demolition of their homes and schools, or violence. Behavioral findings from an assessment conducted by PCC in 2017 in Area C of the Jordan Valley showed that 47% of children assessed have behavioral symptoms, the most prevalent of which is constant fighting and violence towards others. 76% of the children showed indications of emotional distress, and 97% expressed feelings of fear. Living under the constant threat of violence, home demolitions, and displacement has demonstrable gendered impacts on Palestinian women and girls and their livelihoods, access to education and health. There are high levels of gender-based violence, and girls are vulnerable to negative coping mechanisms including high levels of child marriage. In the absence of other opportunities, boys in particular resort to physical labor in the neighboring Israeli settlements, and sometimes experience child-labor related illness and disability as a result (for example due to unprotected exposure to pesticides during farm labor). Social norms and harmful practices experienced at home, schools, and in the communities put children at further risk. There is a continued need for advocacy initiatives to promote respect for IHL and IHRL, as there continues to be a little adherence, and no accountability for violations. As reported in the 2018 HNO, 3706 incidents of grave violations against children were documented In the West Bank affecting 31,927 children. While the need for child protection and psychosocial support is increasing, the current care system is overstretched and cannot respond to the protection and psychosocial needs of girls, boys and their caregivers, especially in Area C, H2 and East Jerusalem where access to national services such as the Child Protection Network (CPN) is often hampered.

Activities

Objective 1: Increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations. HRP Activity 2: Monitoring and documentation Grave Violations Against Children.Capitalizing on their cohesive network of sources including local media, health workers, CBOs, and community leaders and members throughout the West Bank, protection cluster partner field workers will closely monitor, document, and rapidly report grave violations against children, including child killings and injuries; settler violence; and recruitment. The result will include thematic reports to expose the violations and advocate for their cessation. HRP Activity 3: Advocacy initiatives that aim to address protection concerns, prevent violations or enhance accountability.Through proven and innovative advocacy strategies and tools, implementing partners work to expose violations against children, increase accountability for violations, and advance rights and protection for children. Through this project the partner will generate content for newswires, photographs, and video clips, and will conduct briefing sessions with high-ranking officials and international groups to address violations against Palestinian children. Objective 2: Protection response mechanisms in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence. HRP Activity 1: Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support and programs aimed at supporting children and families who have been affected by conflict-related violence and violations. • Structured recreational PSS sessions for 1440 acutely affected children aged 6-18, their siblings and classmates, using the WCH TeamUp methodology. The activities will encourage the reintegration of ex- detainee children in their schools and communities, and promote inclusion. The activities will be implemented through partnerships with 6 CBOs, which also serve as a protective space for children – especially girls who have limited space to play. • Group-based psychosocial support and life skills sessions for 360 children, using the WCH DEALs methodology – including She DEALs tailored especially for girls age 15-18, to deal with particular challenges girls face at that age. Sensitization to the caregivers is essential to encourage girls’ participation. • Training of 40 community facilitators on TeamUp and DEALs methodologies, child safeguarding and in identifying and supporting children with special psychosocial and child protection needs – including referrals• Case Management: WCH will coordinate and implement multi-sectoral case management for 100 affected children. WCH community case workers will provide basic counseling, psychosocial support and individual case management for low to medium risk cases, and refer high risk cases to the CPN. The project will ensure that detected children receive integrated quality services, through appropriate safe referral to specialized services in coordination with CPWG, in accordance to the Index of Networking and Referrals of Child Protection against Abuse, Violence and Neglect, and in close collaboration with the CPN at governorate level. Children ex-detainees subject to torture or ill-treatment and their families will be referred to (EU-funded) legal and specialized mental health services provided by WCH’s partners PCC, TRC, and DCIP under its current EIHDR project. HRP Activity 3: Provision of MHPSS for adults and youth.• Caregiver psychosocial support intervention (CSI): 270 caregivers of acutely affected children will be engaged in this MHPSS intervention aimed at helping them to manage their stress and improve their wellbeing, improve emotional availability for parenting/teaching, strengthen their social networks and support, build coping methods for dealing with stress, anger and sadness, and identify alternatives to harsh discipline actions with children. • Training 20 community facilitators on CSI methodologies

Indicators

War Child has an established global monitoring and reporting framework and uses standardized and globally validated means of verification to assess program effects on the wellbeing of participating children and families, and participant satisfaction. Through WCH’s monitoring and evaluation methodology, quality implementation of programming and delivery of care will be ensured. Regular field visits to and supervision of all activity sites will be conducted. Activities and interventions will be further refined and adapted to meet the specific needs of the communities through engagement of CBOs and community facilitators who will regularly assess needs and participant satisfaction through quarterly focus group discussions and using WCH tools for measuring participant satisfaction and through the establishment of a complaints and feedback mechanism with support from WCH. An end-of-project evaluation with participants through focus group discussions will also give children, caregivers, and community volunteers the opportunity to evaluate the various activities.
