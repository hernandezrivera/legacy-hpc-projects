Project Summary

Promote humanitarian access in a complex operating environment, where the security situation remains volatile; (ii) establishing an efficient humanitarian coordination mechanism in Niamey, Diffa, Tillabery, Maradi and Tahoua; (iii) Strengthen civil-military coordination at the regions and capital levels to improve response preparedness, rapid and safe delivery of humanitarian assistance, and foster enhanced predictability of civil-military coordination processes and platforms; (iv) support country-led identification of strategic HDP collaboration priorities/collective outcomes, which will inform the multi-year Humanitarian Response Strategy framework (2022-2027) and the humanitarian, development, and peace action based on a shared understanding of the nexus, and be reflected in the UNSDCF and the National Development Plan; (v) advocate for principled humanitarian action by relevant authorities and sharing its coordination expertise based on global best practices. This will include an enhanced interface and support to the Government of Niger, especially the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and the Prime Minister's Office, capitalizing on their willingness to lead on humanitarian issues; (vi) Enhance partnerships with existing and new humanitarian donors through improved bilateral and multilateral engagement; (VI) Increased accountability of the humanitarian system to meet humanitarian needs.

1 - Besoins

Niger, a Sahel country, is a fragile island of stability in a region in turmoil and is struggling to respond to deteriorating humanitarian conditions. The armed conflicts that affected Nigeria since 2013 and Mali since 2012 had spread to Niger, resulting in significant protection and humanitarian needs. Insecurity is on the rise with increase human rights violations and abuse against civilians. This includes killings, rape, forced displacement, destruction of property, illegal tax collection, destruction of public infrastructure such as schools and health centers, abductions... Combined factors, such as insecurity and violence, weak state authority, causes multiple displacements, chronic underdevelopment, lack of a fragile civilian authority in the departments bordering Burkina Faso, Chad, Mali, and Nigeria. This has led to a situation where the population's needs significantly outstrip the response, leaving significant gaps. The main challenges are the scale and frequency of displacements, providing adequate food security, water and sanitation, shelter, NFI, and health and nutrition and protection services. The situation is exacerbated by the inability of displaced people to maintain their livelihoods of farming in places of displacement. More than 264,000 IDPs and 249,000 refugees have been identified. A total of 549,000 people were displaced as of September 2021 in the four regions of Diffa, Tahoua, Tillabéri, and Maradi. Since June 2021, the Government of Niger instituted a returns process for some IDPs displaced since 2015 who found refuge in 300 villages in Diffa region. The declaration of the State of emergency in three regions (Diffa, Tahoua and Tillaberi) since 2016 and ongoing military operations against various non-state armed groups restrict access to the population in need and restrict the population's access to humanitarian relief, markets, education, health services, and livelihoods. There is also an increase in protection incidents against civilians with more cases of abductions. The number of civilians killed in Tillabéri and Tahoua Regions during the first five months of 2021 already represents 75 per cent of those recorded over the 12 twelve months of 2020. In addition to conflict-related emergencies, Niger is also prone to environmental crises. Climate change impacts will increase affecting agriculture, food security, endemic diseases, and rural poverty. The health situation is characterized by the prevalence of diseases with epidemic potential such as measles, meningitis and the coronavirus pandemic, this increased Humanitarian needs. In such a context, OCHA will have to respond to followings needs: 1) respond to the humanitarian crisis and continue advocating for humanitarian coordination mechanisms to be adapted to ensure that they are fit for purpose to respond to the above challenges. . 2) Also, to strengthen its deep field presence in areas of acute humanitarian needs, if the security and access situation allow. Through its main office in Niamey, OCHA CO will 3) promote and facilitate an inter-agency / inter-sectoral coordination of humanitarian action and response. 4) support the Humanitarian Country Team and the inter-cluster coordination group, 5) organize joint assessments, develop response plans, lead the planning process, and co-chairs working groups, e.g., cash transfer, humanitarian access, etc. 6) OCHA CO will also spearhead public information, advocacy efforts, humanitarian planning and resource mobilization. 7) to support national and local capacities in coordinating humanitarian assistance and advocate for the greater involvement of development actors in addressing structural issues, 8) The need to ensure access to humanitarian workers to provide adequate and timely assistance to those in need.
