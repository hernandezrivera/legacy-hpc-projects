Project Summary

The crisis in NE Nigeria continues to disrupt and limit access to livelihoods of millions of people, undermining opportunities for durable solutions. As a result of the continuous conflict which generated a situation of widespread unemployment further aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic, many households lack sufficient income to meet their basic needs, and livelihood opportunities remain scarce. Although the need for essential life-saving assistance is still widespread, IOM recognizes the urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods and access to essential services, access to justice, to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations, especially girls, youth, women, and the elderly, against future vulnerabilities and shocks. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and restoring social cohesion. Without those pre-conditions, a premature return of displaced populations may lead to secondary displacement, deepen IDPs’ vulnerability, and exacerbate tension and social polarization between IDPs, host communities and Returnees. The inadequate capacity in areas experiencing return is by IOM considered a direct risk to the overall progressive resolution of displacement in the north-east. This project seeks to contribute to the recovery and resilience building of conflict-affected populations through a series of community support interventions. As people continue to return to their places of habitual residence from before the insurgency, there is urgent and significant needs to restore infrastructure, promote early recovery, including through economic and livelihoods restoration, and facilitate a robust resilience building process. Livelihood interventions under this project will include Cash for Work (CFW). Cash-based interventions are a more dignified way of assisting affected populations, as they empower people to determine their own needs and the best way of meeting them. They can also promote peaceful coexistence between IDP, returnee and host communities, as well-designed and run cash- based interventions have a multiplier effect, jointly engaging all members of the community and directly benefiting the local economy. In addition, vocational skills training and skills enhancement activities for youth will be organized as per outcome of a labor market assessment to create human capital as necessary for the early recovery phase, with a special focus on youth. Each targeted beneficiaries will receive business start-up capital to establish small scale business. CFW and skilled labor schemes will support activities such as comprehensive neighborhood upgrades and light community infrastructure rehabilitation to directly contribute to recovery in conflict-affected areas. In addition, this project will work towards enhanced coordination and complementarity between humanitarian, stabilization and development partners, through a people centered approach. To supplement economic recovery interventions, the project will support restoration of social cohesion and local governance mechanisms in the areas of intervention. To this end, the project aims to provide technical and logistical support to community peace committees to address social cohesion concerns. Furthermore, as protection is ensured in recovery efforts, the project will build the capacity of law enforcement bodies, including traditional/religious leaders, to prevent and respond to GBV crimes in compliance with human rights standards and through a victim-centered approach. IOM will align its response plan to the 4 strategic objectives of Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector as follows:
• Support Livelihood Stabilization and income recovered
• Promote inclusiveness and peaceful co-existence among IDPs, Host Communities and Returnees.
• Support restoration of local community governance administration and services
• Increase access to infrastructure and basic social services in areas of return

1 - Needs

According to 2021 HNO, out of 61 LGAs in the BAY states, 39 are in severe need concerning the lack of a sufficient and reliable income. The critical needs for crisis affected groups remain livelihood support and job creation, access to credit and vocational training, support to productive assets and the private sector for value chain strengthening and development, restoration of markets and critical infrastructures, such as hospitals, schools, roads and waste management systems. Severe damage to or destruction of community infrastructure, as well as the lack of access to adequate basic services, ultimately affect the sustainability of return and reintegration. There is an urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods and access to essential services, including access to justice, to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and restoring social cohesion. Specific measures to restore livelihoods include short-term employment creation, providing market-based skills, vocational training, and micro and small enterprise recovery, targeting the recapitalization of small enterprises for youth and women, derived from community consultation. In addition, areas of return and host communities must be conducive to receive returnees in terms of security (including freedom of movement; safe routes; removal of UXOs/IEDs including from housing, agricultural land and market/trading routes) and the re-establishment of civil governance including the presence of police and judiciary.
