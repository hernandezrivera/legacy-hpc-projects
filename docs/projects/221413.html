Project Summary

The Emergency Child Protection, GBV, and WASH Assistance for Vulnerable Populations Affected by the Complex Crises project aims to address critical humanitarian needs in the North west and South West Regions of Cameroon. This project focuses on providing life-saving interventions in Child Protection (CP), Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) to populations affected by ongoing crises. This project will support the provision of essential services in the North-West and South-West regions, targeting Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees, and vulnerable host populations. The project is designed to respond to the urgent needs of children, adolescents, and their families, addressing not only their immediate protection concerns. Multiple protracted humanitarian crises of unprecedented gravity continue to heavily impact Cameroon. According to the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plans HRPs, 4.3 million people in Cameroon require humanitarian assistance to survive, with approximately 53% of them being children. Conflict and violence are the primary drivers of crisis in the region, and the security situation is likely to deteriorate further due to the intensification of military operations, increased presence of armed groups, and political instability. In Cameroon, children are increasingly being targeted by armed groups. The most recent annual report of the Secretary-General on Children and Armed Conflict found that, in 2022 alone, there were grave violations against 156 children in Cameroon.
In addition to ongoing violence, crisis-affected communities also face grinding poverty, school closures, cholera resurgence, and limited access to safe drinking water. Despite the incredibly high child protection needs identified in the Northwest and Southwest, there is still a lack of sufficient protection actors. Plan’s most recent Rapid Needs Assessments (RNAs) and service mappings in the impacted areas show a significant need for child protection response, including psychosocial support (PSS), while addressing barriers such as stigma, cost, and lack of information regarding sexual and gender-based violence.
With escalating activities of Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG), humanitarian actors anticipate a significant increase in the number of Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG) and vulnerable families. In Cameroon, 1.7 million people in the Northwest and Southwest regions continue to face dire conditions, especially in rural and isolated areas. Approximately 500,000 children are out of school due to violence and attacks on schools, teachers, and pupils. In July 2023 alone, violence resulted in the displacement of 3,655 people, with the highest number of displacements recorded in the Meme and Fako divisions in the Southwest and Mezam division in the Northwest. Most community-level child protection mechanisms are disrupted. Furthermore, recurrent cholera outbreaks are common due to unsafe and contaminated water sources. 1500 children will be reached with Child Protection in Emergency interventions, 2500 beneficiaries will be targeted with GBV prevention interventions, and an additional 500 Households will be targeted with different WASH interventions.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Due to the ongoing crisis in the Northwest and Southwest NWSW regions, boys are a primary target for recruitment by non-state armed groups (NSAGs). Children, especially those displaced or separated from their families, face increased vulnerability to forced or "voluntary" recruitment by armed groups as a survival mechanism. Family separation is significantly high in NWSW Cameroon, with approximately 3,000 unaccompanied children and 19,000 separated children. This separation exposes these children to heightened risks of violence, exploitation, and abuse. The project will intervene to reduce the incidence of child protection by; - CPiE I: Increasing Access to Individualized CPiE and GBV Services o Output 1: Provision of Case Management services- CPiE II: Expanding Access to CPiE Services, Resources, and Information o Output 1: Establishment of Safe Spaceso Output 2: Estalished Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms CBCPMsThere is an alarmingly high incidence of SGBV in the conflict-affected NW/SW regions of Cameroon. Of reported GBV incidents, 31% involve rape and sexual assault of women and girls. Among these, 82% of survivors are internally displaced persons IDPs, and 7.5% of survivors are girls under 18. Common forms of GBV in Cameroon include rape, sexual assault, physical violence, Early, Forced and Child Marriage (EFCM), sexual exploitation, denial of resources, and psychological and emotional violence. To address this, the project will inculcate the following activities:- Establishing Peer PSS Groups and Providing Protective NFIs- Community-Based SGBV Prevention and AwarenessAgain, in the Northwest and Southwest NWSW regions of Cameroon, there is a severe shortage of WASH facilities, with an estimated 1.1 million people in need of WASH assistance. The influx of internally displaced persons IDPs has placed significant strain on the already inadequate infrastructure, exacerbating issues for both displaced populations and host communities. The project will address these identified needs through: - Provision of Clean, Sustainably Managed WASH Facilities- Provision of Essential WASH Items and Promotion of Hygiene PracticesTogether, these outcomes create a foundation that supports the overarching theory of change, which posits that by ensuring equitable access to essential services in child protection, SGBV support, and WASH, the project can achieve its ultimate goal of safeguarding lives, reducing suffering, and upholding the dignity of crisis-affected populations.
