Project Summary

In Maiduguri Municipal Council (MMC) and Jere, NRC will provide a mobile site management response in 47 informal sites , including 12 sites in addition to the initial 38 sites where NRC has been implementing MSM since March 2020. NRC will ensure a continuous focus on increasing provision of services through enhanced coordination of partners in those sites that are currently facing significant gaps. NRC will particularly ensure that these sites are being covered by Health, Protection and WASH services and will advocate and coordinate with the relevant actors accordingly. The response will also aim at supporting community engagement and governance structures, in order to increase the meaningful participation of all community groups in decision making. Specific care and maintenance activities will also be undertaken to guarantee that site facilities and infrastructures are functional and safe, in particular to mitigate the impact of oods and winds during the rainy season.Furthermore, in accordance with the DMS/CCCM sector strategy, NRC also proposes to establish a community centre to address out–of-camp displacement gaps through an area-based approach, using the NRC’s Urban Displacement and Out of Camp (UDOC)response. The community centre will be a coordination platform where beneficiaries living out-camp would be able to access services and to be referred to specific service providers.NRC will also respond with a mobile outreach team that will deploy in the different settlements of the catchment area to conduct awareness campaigns, referrals, collect and receive feedback. NRC will particularly focus in building preparedness to prevent the spread of diseases in those sites where very little or no support has been provided.

1 - Needs

Recent IOM DTM reports show that an estimated total of 59% IDPs live out-of-camps, with some residing in spontaneous makeshift structures in hazardous environments as per IOM DTM Round 37. These out of camp displaced populations and their host community are both exposed to increased risk of conflict, natural disaster, communicable diseases, food insecurity and the continuing effects of diseases like COVID-19, cholera, malaria, etc. Ensuring security and protection of IDPs in out-of-camps therefore remains one of the most significant challenges. The 2021 MSNA and related analysis contained in the HNO revealed an increase in needs across the BAY states compared to 2020. The humanitarian community anticipates that the operational constraints that hampered effective response will continue or worsen as the security escalates, humanitarians are targeted, and access is constrained further. It is also estimated that 1 in 4 of the affected is under 5 years of age. Women and children are the face of the protracted crisis, as they constitute 81% of affected population and 87% of the new displacements. The gender and age dimensions of the conflict triggered displacements are having a multidimensional negative impact on survival, social life, protection and safety nets, and overall wellbeing and dignity of affected populations. In Northeast Nigeria, the conflict-affected populations still find themselves in a deteriorated condition, their prevailing needs and rights are not met or restored, especially as duty bearers are often identified as the perpetrators of severe human rights violations. NRC recognizes that the humanitarian crisis in NE Nigeria is fundamentally a protection and human rights emergency. There is a general lack of accountability and responsiveness of duty bearers in providing assistance and addressing concerns of populations. Therefore, it is crucial for all involved humanitarian actors to continue ensuring life-saving assistance in response to identified risks and threats. There risks were further compounded by the pandemic and the continuation of intense armed conflict emerging in scattered locations in NE Nigeria, leading evidently in drastic deterioration of the protection and economic situation of conflict-affected communities and calls for continuous and strengthened support to impacted populations. Through specialized protection programming NRC will seek to strengthen the communities’ strengths and capacities to threats and shocks through community-based protection approach, by building on existing positive community strategies and support them develop new approaches for displacement-affected populations and prevent and mitigate their protection risks. Additionally, as part of the proposed protection programming, NRC will focus on strengthening the protection analyses, through the modality of Protection Monitoring that will focus on identifying and reporting protection risks and trends. The findings from Protection Monitoring will be used to inform other direct provision of services funded under this intervention including Case Management.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

As part of its MSM and UDOC components, the project will ensure access toassistance, meaningful participation and inclusion for all groups of the community. Inparticular, the response will aim at involving these groups in all phases of the project.NRC will build on the traditional or created community representative structuresestablished and/or supported in and out of camps. These structures are representativeof the different demographic groups (gender, age, cultural specicities, specic needsand other marginalized groups) and will serve as a coordination mechanism betweenthe community and NRC, as well as a channel for feedbacks and referrals. NRC willalso strengthen its “Complaint and Feedback mechanism” through the installation ofmobile feedback Desks where beneciaries will have the opportunity to share and toreceive feedback from NRC staffs. NRC will also mainstream protection through itssite improvement and care & maintenance activities. Based on the community groupsfeedback and requests (see above), NRC will implement site improvements in order toimprove living conditions, site safety and reduce protection risks. Hence, NRC willencourage community groups (such as site safety committees, site maintenancecommittees, women committees, elderly committees, etc.) to identify needs and willprovide technical and material support for site improvement activities. In addition toregular meetings with relevant community structures, NRC will also conduct safetyaudits and participatory safety mappings to identify risks and unsafe areas in thecamps. Site improvement works could include, but not limited to: construction of afence to protect latrine area, lightening of latrines, installation of a gate at the campentrance, construction of drainages to prevent ooding risks, rehabilitation ofcommunal building. Site improvement could also be implemented through the cash forwork modality. This modality will ensure that beneciary selection is gender balancedand based on vulnerability criteria, in accordance with the corresponding EarlyRecovery Working Group SOPs. Referrals to specic service providers, such as shelter,WASH, Protection/Legal assistance to address protection risks will also be a keyaspect of both MSM and UDOC components. Referrals will also be conducted throughgender-balanced mobile outreach teams, in particular in UDOC locations, in order toreach remote areas and identify vulnerable cases to be referred.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will develop a CFM mechanism based on the community preferred feedback channels, following a participatory survey. This mechanism should therefore be accessible and adapted to the different demographic groups, including the most vulnerable and marginalized groups. NRC will use both static and mobile approach in order to also reach beneficiaries that have less access to the CFM Desk (in camps) or to the UDOC centre (out of camp). NRC will also coordinate closely with the specific community structures, including representative committees (women, elderlies, persons with disabilities, leadership, etc.) to ensure adequate access to vulnerable population.
