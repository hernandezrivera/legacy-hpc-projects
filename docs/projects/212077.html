Project Summary

The proposed project is ambitious to target 25,000 individuals, aiming to enhance the living conditions of conflict-affected populations through the distribution of essential Non-Food Items (NFIs). The project design includes the provision 3000 items of core NFIs to 15,000 individuals and 2000 winter NFIs to an additional 10,000 individuals, ensuring that the seasonal needs of the beneficiaries are met.
The project will target three subdistricts within the Aleppo governorate in Northwest Syria: the Albab, Bulbul, and Jarablus subdistricts.
In the Al-Bab subdistrict, the total population is 224,833 individuals, 91,603 hosts, and IDPs, with 130,902 individuals and 2,328 returnees. The core NFI severity phase data indicates a critical situation where the entire population assessed is classified under phase 3, defined as severe. The NFI People in Need (PIN) number is 47,508.
In the Bulbul subdistrict, the total population is 35781 individuals, 7465 hosts, and IDPs, where 25792 individuals and 2,524 returnees. The core NFI severity phase data indicates a critical situation where the entire population assessed is classified under phase 3, defined as severe. The NFI People in Need (PIN) number is 9458.
In the Jarablus subdistrict, the total population is 90499 individuals, 45042 hosts, and IDPs, where 43339 individuals and 2118 returnees. The core NFI severity phase data indicates a critical situation where the entire population assessed is classified under phase 4, defined as severe. The NFI People in Need (PIN) number is 20166.
In addressing the critical needs of internally displaced persons (IDP), our project will direct its efforts towards 16,000 individuals in IDP camps, providing them with the necessities to improve their daily living within these settings. Furthermore, recognizing the unique challenges faced by the 9,000 IDPs outside of camps, our aid distribution will also extend to these populations.
Gender balance is a central consideration in our project, with plans to reach 12,000 male and 13,000 female beneficiaries, promoting equity in humanitarian assistance. In prioritizing the welfare of children, our project will cater to the needs of 2,500 young children under five years of age, providing a foundation for their healthy development. An additional 10,500 children and adolescents between the ages of 5-17 will be supported.
Adults aged 18-59 who are pivotal in the sustenance and recovery of their families will constitute a significant focus of our project, with 10,500 individuals receiving support. This aid is vital in empowering them to rebuild their lives and communities in the face of adversity.
Older people, often the most vulnerable in times of crisis, will not be overlooked. Our project will assist 1,500 individuals over 59, ensuring they receive the care and assistance they deserve.
Inclusivity is at the heart of our mission, and as such, 1,750 persons with disabilities will be beneficiaries of our targeted aid distribution. This reflects our commitment to addressing the specific needs of all community members, particularly those who face additional barriers.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project includes distributing essential and supplemental NFIs through two primary activities. Activity 1.1 will reach 15,000 individuals with core NFIs vital for daily survival. Concurrently, Activity 1.2 will provide 10,000 individuals with seasonal and supplementary NFIs tailored to withstand varied environmental conditions. Pre-Distribution Communication and PlanningCommunication with beneficiaries will employ a mix of direct community engagement and widespread announcements to ensure clarity and accessibility. We will work closely with local stakeholders to align our efforts with broader humanitarian initiatives, avoiding overlap and maximizing resource efficiency. Assessments and Item SelectionDetailed assessments will identify the specific NFI needs, ensuring the selected items reflect Sphere humanitarian standards. The distribution modalities—in-kind, cash, or vouchers—will be determined through careful market analysis and consideration of beneficiary security and preference. Beneficiary Selection and Team FormationOur beneficiary selection process will be equitable and participatory, drawing on community feedback to ensure transparency. A robust verification process will prevent duplication. The distribution team will comprise individuals trained in humanitarian distribution protocols and sensitized to the cultural and social nuances of the affected population. Logistics and Site SelectionLogistics will be meticulously planned to secure the safe and timely transportation of NFIs. Sites will be selected based on ease of access and suitability for maintaining orderly distribution while observing social distancing protocols. Distribution ExecutionDuring distribution, the flow of beneficiaries will be carefully managed, ensuring a dignified and efficient process. Security personnel will oversee crowd control, maintaining a safe environment. Complaints and feedback will be actively solicited and managed on-site by dedicated staff. Post-Distribution AnalysisAfter distribution, we will rigorously address feedback or complaints, ensuring accountability and continuous improvement. Post-distribution monitoring will evaluate the impact of the aid provided and collect insights to refine future distributions.
