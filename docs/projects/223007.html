Project Summary

Caritas executes a thorough strategy for protection and gender integration, incorporating age, gender, and diversity considerations throughout every phase of implementation. Furthermore, particular attention is directed towards notably vulnerable groups within this protection mainstreaming framework. The organization utilizes the Protection Mainstreaming Framework and is committed to following the guidance, practical tip sheets, and strategy developed by the WASH Sector. The integration of principles such as Safety and Dignity, Do No Harm, Meaningful Access, Accountability, Participation, and Empowerment is essential for effective response efforts. In this initiative, CB will deliver a thorough Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) package to individuals of concern living in the specified camps. This package encompasses the management and upkeep of facilities, the supply of water resources, the encouragement of hygiene practices, as well as the handling of solid waste and readiness for emergencies.
The national policy for PSEA has been tailored, and complaints will be gathered through various entry points, with direct processing handled by the designated PSEA focal point. A training session focused on PSEA, Safeguarding, and reporting procedures is being arranged for volunteers and staff.
CB works in partnership with various governmental bodies, including the NGO Affairs Bureau (NGOAB), the RRRC Office, the Deputy Commissioner's office, the Camp in Charges (CiC), DPHE as well as other relevant sectors to obtain required approvals and facilitate efficient collaboration. The team regularly share progress reports periodically that include the 4Ws along with a situation report (Sitrep) to different sectors. Furthermore, as a actively participate in WASH Sector and camp coordination meetings at both the Cox's Bazar level and the camp level. CB is committed to actively participating and working together with the Water, Sanitation, and Solid Waste Technical Interest Working Group (TiWG) and SAG as well.
The challenges arise from insufficient strategic emphasis on the operation and maintenance, quality, and sustainable solutions of all vital WASH services, fostering resilience through community engagement. Consequently, CB plans to tackle the outlined challenges to maintain operational maintenance capacity and ensure that facilities continue to function effectively.
In a collaborative assessment on gender issues, young individuals expressed worries regarding their safety while using WASH facilities. Girls faced restrictions on accessing latrines that were shared by both men and women, stemming from inadequate segregation and privacy measures. It was concerning that many households with individuals with disabilities did not indicate that they had utilized an improved water source. CB aims to guarantee inclusive WASH services as well.
Efforts focus on incorporating protective strategies to guarantee secure access to services, uphold accountability, and foster significant involvement in WASH support. The objective is to optimize beneficial outcomes, safeguard rights, and improve involvement and empowerment for the communities impacted.
The initiative encompasses disaster risk reduction elements united into water, sanitation, and hygiene efforts, with a strong focus on maintaining environmental sustainability. CB will incorporate site enhancements, and construction will be carried out to upgrade the WASH facilities. The community members will be encouraged to safeguard against soil erosion by engaging in tree planting initiatives. They will be encouraged to avoid indiscriminate tree cutting and to engage in planting trees at their homes.
The DRR Coordination group, including CRS, Caritas Australia, CAFOD, and Trocaire, developed an Environmental Stewardship tool to promote social unity, encourage proactive dialogue, and involve the community in water, sanitation, and hygiene initiatives.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

As per the ISCG situation and WASH Sector report of the Rohingya Refugee Crisis, to date, the considerable efforts made by the Government of Bangladesh, together with WASH partners, have resulted in 89% of Rohingya refugees/FDMNs reporting having enough water to meet their household needs. 96% of sanitation facilities are also reported as functional. Challenges remain however, especially in hygiene (due to the high cost of supply and inappropriate hygiene practices) and solid waste management. Caritas Bangladesh will continue to promote environmental sustainability including alternatives to plastic materials, long-term sustainability of groundwater supply and water quality in collaboration with relevant institutions and WASH Sector.In 2025, CB will be in line with the WASH Sector’s strategic focus on the operation and maintenance of existing WASH services through cost efficient solutions. The Sector will target the provision of quality water and sanitation facilities, as well as solid waste management provided through infrastructure support and community engagement. In addition, Sector partners will promote adequate hygiene behaviour and distribute hygiene items through a multi-sector approach. UN agencies will continue to lead the supply of WASH items, including soap, and menstrual kits for women and girls produced directly within the camps, for harmonization of assistance, equity, and cost-efficiency. Enhanced monitoring will be crucial to ensure equitable and qualitative WASH services across the camps. CB will also be part of technical priorities that focus removing accumulated, settled solid sludge, reflecting on communal bathing facilities given current, shelter-based bathing habits, operationalization of the new water strategy, and stronger community involvement and ownership in WASH activities (e.g. strengthening WASH committees wherein beneficiaries identify and tackle their own challenges). In host communities, CB will support equitable access to quality WASH services aligned with national development plans and WASH Sector priorities. WASH vulnerability criteria (e.g. lack of improved water and sanitation) will also be taken into consideration while targeting neighboring communities, both directly and indirectly impacted because of hosting Rohingya refugees/FDMNs. CB will ensure and consider environmental issues, gender, age, and disability inclusion, with emphasis on preparedness and response for natural disasters (e.g. disaster-resilient facility design) and public health risks, including disease outbreaks. It will continue to identify the most vulnerable households (e.g. those with persons with disabilities, elderly) to address the specific needs of beneficiaries including women and youth. The CB in collaboration with WASH Sector response will consider environmental issues, gender, age, disability inclusion and accountability, with emphasis on preparedness and response for natural disasters and public health risks, including disease outbreaks.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Caritas is deeply committed to addressing the unique and significant risks to the safety and dignity of Rohingya individuals, with a particular focus on those facing heightened vulnerabilities such as women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, elderly individuals, and diverse groups including LGBTQ communities. WASH facilities are strategically designed and situated to enhance safety, incorporating accessible roads that address challenging slopes and isolated areas while ensuring gender-segregated facilities for privacy and protection. Key priorities in the humanitarian response include Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Safeguarding measures. To enhance safety during nighttime, solar streetlights will be installed along pathways leading to latrines, and solar lamps will be provided to vulnerable individuals at the household level. This approach is rooted in a community-centered strategy that prioritizes active participation, empowerment, and community strengthening to mitigate risks and foster resilience. The Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) structure, along with the Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM), will uphold Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and ensure community responsiveness. The Protection team will employ a comprehensive, protection mainstreamed approach to deliver integrated and inclusive programming, which includes capacity building for staff to uphold and implement protection principles effectively.

2.3 - Age & Disability

In Caritas Bangladesh's WASH program, prioritizing age and disability components is paramount to ensure inclusive and protective services. The organization upholds a comprehensive Child and Vulnerable Adult Safeguarding Policy, fostering a safe and supportive environment. Staff and volunteers are regularly trained in Safeguarding, the Code of Conduct, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Before service delivery, beneficiaries receive orientation on safeguarding policies, and these policies are consistently emphasized in meetings and distributed in Bengali Rohingya to promote widespread understanding.Caritas Bangladesh ensures inclusive programming through sector-specific training for staff and volunteers and develops accessible communication materials. To support persons with disabilities (PWDs) and elderly individuals, porter services, priority queuing, and door-to-door assistance for women and girls with disabilities are provided, along with adapted hygiene materials. Accessibility audits are conducted for all facilities to ensure meaningful accommodations. The program targets PWDs, older persons, and vulnerable groups, addressing their specific needs, such as access road creation. Disability considerations are integrated into assessments. PWDs participate in activities, including committees. Budgeting for improved emergency accessibility, such as evacuation centers, is included.CB’s WASH program ensures inclusivity for PWDs by providing facilities equipped with handrails and ramps and developing individualized plans tailored to their unique needs, collaborates with specialized organizations (HI, CBM, CDD) to reinforce these inclusive practices. Continuous improvement remains a core principle, reflecting Caritas's dedication to adapting responses and prioritizing the safety and well-being of all individuals, particularly the most vulnerable, during emergency situations.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The systemic MEAL mechanism ensures AAP principles, CFRM, and stakeholder consultation, and periodic meetings with stakeholders are designed in the project towards improving the quality of WASH services. CB aims at capacitating staff and volunteers to facilitate cultural programming with a perspective of sustainability and potential replication and to promote the empowerment of the community. The project engages the community in all processes of project implementation. Involvement of community members in needs assessment through Participatory Action Research (PAR) methods. Sharing impact findings with beneficiaries, organizing periodical meetings and consultations, and taking decisions on the ways to ensure quality WASH services. Their feedback will be considered/addressed importantly. The proposed project keeps the door open to community participation through coordinated and inclusive mechanisms.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Caritas Bangladesh is responsible for ensuring that beneficiaries safely access their services, without causing harm, and for promoting meaningful access, accountability, and participation of beneficiaries in the provision of WASH assistance. CB is actively connected with PSEA network and has a checklist that ensures the mainstreaming of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) facilities. It emphasizes the secure and accessible location of water points and latrines, with demarcation and separate entries for men and women. Privacy and security are reinforced through facilities that can be locked from the inside and adequate lighting. The checklist includes provisions for sex-segregated WASH facilities, monitoring of queues for security, and special measures for those with accessibility challenges. Additionally, it addresses awareness and visibility of PSEA, GBV (Gender-Based Violence), and inclusion/disability materials and reporting hotlines, promoting a safe and informed environment for beneficiaries. Staff and volunteers, stakeholders will be trained on PSEA.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the proposed project incorporates Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) elements, as well as measures to address environmental issues and climate change mitigation. Emergency preparedness and response activities are integral, with Caritas Bangladesh (CB) ensuring that each WASH facility has a comprehensive DRR contingency plan in place and adheres to it alongside the community.Capacity building of staff, community volunteers, user groups, WASH committees, and other stakeholders is a core part of the project, emphasizing their active involvement in managing DRR programs. This approach empowers the community by enhancing their knowledge and skills for informed decision-making and implementation, which strengthens resilience and sustainability.Specific measures include the integration of environmentally friendly practices such as using biodegradable materials for the repair and maintenance of WASH facilities. The project will promote tree plantation initiatives to support site protection and contribute to climate change mitigation. Additionally, water source protection, sustainable water management practices, and proper faecal sludge management (FSM), safe solid waste management are emphasized to ensure the long-term sustainability of resources and minimize environmental impact.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

CB through the WASH project incorporates robust Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures tailored for the Rohingya camps, where natural hazards such as cyclones, fire, heavy rainfall, landslide and flash floods pose significant risks. Caritas Bangladesh (CB) employs a community-based approach, actively involving Rohingya refugees in decision-making. This ownership enhances resilience and preparedness. CB has established an Emergency Team trained for rapid disaster response, supported by an Emergency Stockpile of essential supplies like water purification tablets, hygiene kits, and WASH materials. A detailed contingency plan outlines standard operating procedures for emergencies, ensuring coordinated response efforts with clear roles and communication protocols along with WASH sector and Area Focal Agency. Training is provided to staff, volunteers, and local stakeholders in disaster preparedness and response, empowering them to effectively manage emergency initiatives within their communities. The key messages of emergency preparedness will be shared with respective FDMNs through trained volunteers and staff as well. Practical DRR measures (Asssesment and design and implementation), such as tree plantation to prevent soil erosion and using biodegradable materials for maintenance, promote environmental sustainability and resilience of WASH facilities. Regular awareness to prepare the community and Emergency Team for effective response during disasters.Through these EPR measures, CB aims to strengthen the resilience of Rohingya communities in the camps, ensuring they can withstand and recover from natural disasters while meeting their basic needs.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

CB through the project actively engages youth aged 15-24 in the WASH program. We believe that youth and adolescent of this age groups play a vital role in promoting hygiene practices and enhancing community resilience. Through targeted outreach and training, young volunteers are equipped with the knowledge and skills necessary to raise awareness about WASH issues and implement effective hygiene promotion activities within their communities.Additionally, the Cash programming offers young (above 18) people meaningful employment opportunities, enabling them to participate in essential WASH activities, such as the construction and maintenance of water facilities and sanitation infrastructure, as HP volunteers, guards, technicians. This engagement not only empowers youth but also fosters a sense of ownership and responsibility towards their environment. By involving this age group, we aim to build a sustainable future where young leaders contribute actively to improving WASH conditions and promoting positive behavioral changes within their communities.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The WASH Program of CB aims, in essence, that empowers self-reliance and resilience among the refugees through the integration of community-led initiatives and capacity-building activities within WASH interventions. That is, we empower refugees through training in hygiene practices, waste management, and water conservation, hence making them more willing to take responsibility for the environment around them. This is complemented by the community hygiene groups responsible for routine sanitation and maintenance activities, hence inspiring a sense of responsibility and involvement. This project equips refugees with better skills and knowledge to take care of a healthier and cleaner living environment by themselves, which in turn contributes to their long-term resilience and self-sufficiency.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Caritas Bangladesh’s WASH activities in Cox’s Bazar align with district development priorities and planning instruments, including the District Development and Growth Priorities (Dec 2022). By enhancing water supply and sanitation infrastructure, the project supports the district’s goal of improving WASH services while promoting public health and environmental sustainability—key elements in Cox’s Bazar’s development agenda. Engaging both refugees and host communities fosters social cohesion and resilience, strengthening community ties. Partnering with local authorities, the Department of Public Health Engineering, and other stakeholders ensures WASH efforts are integrated into district strategies, addressing immediate needs and contributing to sustainable growth. All WASH interventions follow Caritas Bangladesh’s DRR approach, prioritizing resilience.
