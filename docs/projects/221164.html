Project Summary

The proposed intervention will support two primary healthcare facilities including Abayok PHCU in Renk County and Majok Aken PHCU in Aweil East County, and 18 mobile medical units three per each targeted county to reach the vulnerable population in hard-to-reach locations. Each supported facility and MMU will be equipped with eight qualified health workers including a clinical officer, a registered midwife, a nurse, a community health worker, a laboratory assistant, and two EPI vaccinators to provide lifesaving health care services. Alongside MMU, ARDF will establish Boma Health Initiative structures comprised of Boma Health Workers to undertake treatment of under five top morbidities including malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhea. BHWs will conduct community screening of children 6-59 months for malnutrition using MUAC tape and children defaulted from immunization and refer them for services.
ARDF will procure and distribute essential drugs, medical equipment, and medical supplies to the supported fixed facilities, MMUs, and BHI. MMU teams and BHWs will also be equipped with protective gears including gumboots, and raincoats enabling them to access villages during rain or floods. Metallic trunks will be provided to MMUs and BHWs for storage of supplies. MMUs will be provided with portable furniture, back bags, mats for patients to sit on, and locally made screenings to provide privacy.
ARDF will provide a range of lifesaving primary health care packages comprises of, Curative consultation, testing of malaria and syphilis using rapid diagnostic test kits(RDT), integrated management of childhood illnesses(IMCI), screening of children for malnutrition, referring malnourished children to outpatient therapeutic sites, routine immunization services, antenatal care services, skilled delivery, Postnatal care, PMTCT, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) services, Clinical management of rape, health education, integrated community case management (iCCM/BHI), mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, routine integrated disease surveillance and response, engage BHWs to conduct community surveillance for priority diseases, coordinate with stakeholders and actors at national and subnational to ensure timely response to outbreaks, distribution of LLINs, strengthen infection prevention and control(IPC) and integrate WASH services in the facilities.
ARDF will build the capacity of health providers to improve their skills and quality of health services, particularly on topics; integrated disease surveillance and response, integrated management of neonatal and childhood illnesses, immunization in practice (IIP), Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC), Mental health Gap action program (mhGAP), SGBV/CMR, and use of malaria rapid diagnostic test, Boma health initiative training for BHWs and infection prevention and control.
Monitoring Evaluation Accountability and Learning: ARDF project team will regularly monitor activities through monthly supportive supervision and beneficiary satisfaction surveys at the exit points in the facilities and MMUs using standardized checklists. ARDF will establish an accountability mechanism for the affected population by installing suggestion boxes and conducting monthly community feedback meetings to document beneficiaries’ complaints and feedback where action points are documented and implemented to improve the quality of services. Conduct quarterly program review meetings to review the progress of the project. ARDF will equip all facilities, MMUs, and BHI with Ministry of Health registers for data collection and reporting. Health workers will be trained to use the tools and report the services monthly through activity info and DHIS2, while weekly IDSR through EWARs

1. Needs

About 9 million people in South Sudan need one or more forms of humanitarian assistance, especially in Counties most affected by floods, conflicts, and disease outbreaks (SSD HNO 2024). Upper Nile and Northern Bahar El Gazal states continue to face severe humanitarian crises due to multiple shocks including inter-communal conflicts, flooding, drought, and access impediments to services, placing the population in dire need of health services. The continuous influx of returnees and refugees to South Sudan from the Sudan crisis has further stressed the existing health services (UNCHR Border Monitoring September 2024 Report).In Renk, Malakal, and Baliet Counties of Upper Nile and Aweil Centre, Aweil East, and Aweil South Counties of Northern Bahar El Gazal, the health system is unable to cope with the emergencies, and health services provision relies heavily on humanitarian partners. Despite efforts to support the provision of health services in these Counties by humanitarian partners, vulnerable communities continue to face challenges in accessing health services due to a lack of functional health facilities in hard-to-reach locations, flooding, insecurity, and long distances to reach the health facilities. According to IRNA reports, conducted on 12th Dec 2023, 9th August 2024, and 17th August 2024 in Aweil East, Aweil South, and Aweil Centre Counties respectively, access to health services remains limited due to long distance to reach the facilities, flooding, lack of skill health workers, lack of essential drugs, and medical supplies in functional facilities. Further, the humanitarian Situation Overview (REACH report, October 2024) for Aweil East and Northern Bahar El Gazal confirms that severe flooding and influx of refugees and returnees have exacerbated humanitarian challenges posing both immediate and long-term risks to public health.In Malakal County of Upper Nile state, recurrent inter-communal conflicts continue to affect community access to health services. A recent inter-agency assessment conducted in October 2024 in the western bank of Malakal County confirmed that communities in Lelo Payam and Warajok Boma have no functional health facilities, making them access health services at Malakal Teaching Hospital (MTH), one hour and 30 minutes by canoe. Disease prevalence continues to remain high in Upper Nile and Northern Bahar El Gazal State with Malaria, acute watery diarrhea (AWD), pneumonia, and other acute respiratory diseases being the most communicable morbidities. According to the epidemiological weeks 45 and 46, suspected cases of cholera were reported in Renk, Malakal, and Aweil West, exposing vulnerable populations to the risk of the cholera outbreak. Immunization coverage in Upper Nile and Northern Bahar El Bahar remains significantly low evidenced by the recurrent outbreak of measles in Renk, Melut, Maban, Aweil North, and Aweil South, exposing under five children to risk of vaccine-preventable diseases. Access to maternal health care services is a challenge with low coverage of antenatal care services during 4th visit, skilled deliveries, and postnatal care (DHIS2 Data). Communities in the targeted Counties have limited capacity to manage health emergencies There remain significant protection challenges in Renk and Malakal Counties of the Upper Nile and Aweil East, Aweil Centre and Aweil South Counties of Northern Bahar El Gazal States, exacerbated by the influx of refugee and returnees for the Sudan crisis (South Sudan Protection Monitoring Spotlight Report, March 2024). The report also highlighted that recurrent inter-communal conflicts and the influx of Returnees and refugees from Sudan crises have placed the vulnerable population, especially women, girls, and children in extreme psychological distress, sexual gender-based violence (SGBV), and other forms of abuse.
