Project Summary

Mercy Corps will deliver livelihoods programming for refugees in White Nile, Gedaref and Kassala states, creating market-driven livelihood opportunities in a context where barriers to formal and informal employment severely undermine both the food security and resilience capacities of households. Furthermore, Mercy Corps deliberately designs our humanitarian programs to do better than meet needs on a stopgap basis. While our priority aim is to save lives, minimize suffering, and protect social and economic assets, we will also tackles the proximate and underlying causes of the emergency in order to interrupt repeated cycles of crisis and vulnerability ensuring we are actually mitigating suffering--not just postponing it to a later date.
Building on Mercy Corps existing livelihood projects in Gedaref: El Fashaga locality (Umgargur camp); and Kassala: Khashm Ghirba locality (Girba camp), Wad Sherifey camp and Kassala town; Mercy Corps will strengthen the livelihoods of 4200(White Nile 1020:Kassala and Gedaref 3,180) refugees and 10% vulnerable host community to gradually reduce dependency on humanitarian aid and assistance, with a view to increase the economic capacity of refugee households and support local integration. Efforts will be made to reduce vulnerabilities, increase and diversify sources of income and prevent negative coping strategies. This project will apply a sustainable livelihoods approach focused on gaining productive assets while building capacities and coping strategies of vulnerable households. This approach recognises that individuals are active agents who make rational decisions and choices about their lives, and in response to social and economic change. At the practical level, MC will actively involve and listen to the views of the participants and the wider community during the beneficiary selection. The various training opportunities provided under the project will support people to achieve their own livelihood goals, building where possible on their existing coping strategies. Increasing individuals’ access to financial services will enhance individual’s financial assets and increase their resilience to economic and social shocks and stressors. Importantly, this project recognises the significance of power relations that exist both within and between households and communities, the host and refugee communities. Considering this, MC will ensure that 15-20 percent of all project beneficiaries are host community members. This will increase community and government support for the intervention.
Key activities to realising this objective will include market driven vocational training; business start-up support through cash grants; Village Savings and Loans Association (VSLA) strengthening; and provision of improved vegetable seeds for farming. The program will seek to focus on South Sudanese, Eritrean and Ethiopian refugee groups and surrounding host community.

1 - Needs

White Nile State (WNS) is one of the least developed states in Sudan with an estimated56% of the host community living below the poverty line. Seventy-seventy percent ofSouth Sudanese Refugees (SSRs) in WNS are female headed households forcing womento assume the double of caring for their family and providing an income for their families.While 64 percent of SSR in WNS are children under the age of 17 yearsAdditionally, more than 123,023 Eritrean and Ethiopian refugees are residing in Eastern Sudan, the majority of whom in nine refugee camps across Kassala, Gedaref and El Gezira States. A smaller proportion live in urban settings in Kassala, Girba, Gedaref and Port Sudan (See RRP Planning Framework and UNHCR 2017 Sudan Participatory Assessment). The majority of refugees are Eritreans who fled to Sudan during Eritrea’s war of independence in the 1970s and 1980s, alongside a smaller number of Ethiopians. In 2017, an average of 970 individuals per month arrived in Eastern Sudan, with many Eritreans fleeing forced conscription in Eritrea. Majority of these new arrivals move onwards from Sudan including through human smuggling and trafficking, therefore, exposing themselves to extortion, torture and other violations. White Nile, Kassala and Gederef are among the states classified as IPC phase 3 which recorded highest severity of acute food insecurity. In 2020, a staggering 71,418 Persons in Need (PiN) have been identified in Kassala,over 250,000 in White Nile and a further 30,428 identified in Gederaf requiring Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) assistance and an estimated 46% of camp-based refugees estimated to be living in extreme poverty (2020 Refugee Response Plan (RRP) Needs Overview). From experience, Mercy Corps knows that similar to the host community, the challenges protracted refugees face stem primarily from harsh environmental conditions and limited opportunities to establish meaningful self-reliance. Challenges associated with climate, shortages or fluctuations in rainfall in particular and limited agricultural activity. Approximately 58 percent of households across all states cannot afford a local food basket due to limited purchasing power resulting from inflation and high food prices. In WNS households’ main source of food security for refugees in the camps is general food assistance which is provided on a monthly basis (UNHCR Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) completed in April 2018). In Kassala and Gedaref, 91.3% and 79.1 % people spend their income on food respectively (Sudan Comprehensive Food Security Assessment 2018). Based on information available from the Food Security Monitoring System (FSMS) from WFP (November 2018), around 90 percent of refugees spend 65 percent of their expenditure on food. To cope with the inability to acquire enough food, around one third of resident households adopt food based coping strategies. This includes borrowing money to buy food, limiting portion sizes, reducing the number of meals and reducing adult consumption, favouring smaller children, and eating less preferred food. The highest rates of severe coping mechanisms are adopted in Kassala (29 percent).. In WNS dependency on negative coping mechanism is high across all the camps with between 83.6% to 97% of all SSR surveyed reporting that they were engaged in one or more negative coping mechanisms (SENS 2018)Refugees residing in camp settings typically lack access to paid work. Employment opportunities are mainly open to nationals or require qualifications and certifications that refugees struggle to acquire. Microfinancing opportunities are limited and self-employment options have been further curtailed when some Village Savings and Loans Association (VSLA) schemes were suspended following a drop in repayment rates. Single-headed female households and persons with disabilities will be targeted for inclusion in both activity areas.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Mercy Corps will support new livelihoods in White Nile, Gedref (El Fashaga) and Kassala (Khashm Ghirba and Kassala) through:1.1 Vocational training to provide skills for creation of small-scale income-generating activitiesMercy Corps will provide vocational training to 700 refugees in both Gedaref and Kassala and 300 in WNS. This will include training on soap making, production of fuel-efficient stove, bakery, mobile phone repair and tailoring. Mercy Corps will support 700 refugee households in both Gedaref and Kassala to start small-scale income generating activities (IGA) through business start-up grants to cover business start-up costs. The amount of the cash grant will vary dependent on the nature of the vocational training course completed by the participant and the needs for start-up they have identified in their business plans. following a participatory market assessment to identify the most promising opportunities on the local market. Mercy Corps will train the 700 program participants on basic financial management, bookkeeping and marketing so that they are able to manage their IGAs as profitable small businesses. Then, once participants successfully complete the training program, Mercy Corps will provide Establishment and training Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs)Mercy Corps will establish and train 30 VSLA groups (composed of approximately 10-15 members each) in both Gedaref and Kassala and 30 in WNS on the VSLA methodology. From the onset of the project, Mercy Corps will work to ensure that the VSLAs are self-sufficient and can function without external support through routine mentorship in the initial project stages. To encourage community ownership and an exit strategy each the groups will be self-governed with provision for the beneficiaries to self-select into groups and elect their own governance committee to preside over the group.1.2 Support livelihoods in sustainable agricultureMercy Corps will work with targeted populations to establish vegetable farming, promoting climate resilient crops and train target populations on climate sensitive cultivation techniques. Through a memorandum with the Ministry of Agriculture, Mercy Corps will conduct a ToT training targeting 2,000 female farmers in both Gedaref and Kassala and 60 in WNS. The training will be facilitated by an Agricultural Extension Officer and each farmers group will be comprised of 50 people with one ToT per group. Mercy Corps will provide vegetable sees through vouchers targeting 2,000 (50% Men and 50% Women) program participants in Kassala and Gedaref and 300 in WNS.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARM Process: Mercy Corps (MC) Sudan’s team has established and standardized CARMS, based on our commitment to accountability to the affected population. MC will use CARM to enhance accountability and transparency to the target beneficiaries and stakeholders. CARM allows beneficiaries to raise complains through letter and phone. Community will designate an area to for letters. The received complaints will be documented and evaluated and referred to i.e. those which should be addressed at the field level and those that can be addressed at the national level. Investigated complaints are finalized and feedback will be given to the target population at the field. MC program members will sensitize community members on the available feedback mechanisms and ensure it is included in the activity work plan. This will ensure that context-appropriate communication and feedback channels that are safe, accessible, transparent and confidential for clients are considered. The CARMs will also establish feedback and complaints on MC services as well as seeking information and receiving timely, appropriate responses about MC’s actions and decisions. Complaints received will be analyzed by age, gender and diversity and a dashboard will generate summarized data and information for the field and senior management. Further, MC will engage community leader’s representatives to give feedback on complaints raised and communicate any change in strategy and or implementation. Interactive Participatory Implementation (IPI) – the IPI approach is focused on gaining the optimum involvement of the project’s target stakeholders and target groups. This IPI approach, combined with ‘community empowerment methodology’ will generate the needed involvement of all the segments of the community ensuring the Accountability to the Affected Population (APP). It will also give a sense of accomplishment and create ownership among the target groups as the project activities progresses and achieves its tangible results. MC will involve the target population in the whole project cycle, from design to implementation to close out. • Program Design: MC will engage refugee camp leaders to conduct feasibility assessments and map water sources.• Implementation: MC will continue to engage the community volunteers in program implementation. The program will further ensure that women are also included as part of the water committees. This will further deepen program ownership, participation and eventually enhance sustainability of deployed interventions.• Monitoring & Evaluation: MC will engage water committees to give feedback on water pumps functionality and hygiene promoters in monthly basis. This will be held through focus group discussions. • Close Out: MC will engage the beneficiaries in the final evaluation and handover of the water points to the community.
