Project Summary

In line with the Protection Cluster Objectives 1&2 (ensure provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services; and prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness), the project seeks to (1) reduce suffering of women, girls, boys, and men who have experienced various forms of GBV, (2) provide GBV prevention and risk mitigation measures that address priority needs of women, girls, boys, and men in need, (3) ensure access to livelihood support and economic resources to reduce vulnerability and mitigate GBV, and (4) strengthen risk analysis and vulnerability assessments – reflecting GBV Sub Cluster’s overall strategy and objectives.
The project targets locations in Tier 1 (Twic and Aweil East), Tier 2 (Yambio, Nzara, Renk, Malakal), and Tier 3 (Juba, Fashoda, Tambura and Ulang).
Briefly, project activities include: GBV case management (including specialized, individual, and group PSS), legal advice to survivors to facilitate access to justice, and safe referrals to services (including medical and health care providers for CMR services, safety, access to justice, livelihoods, and other support services). World Vision (WV) will continue to work through existing and establish new WGFS, serving as a self-referral point for services, but also as a space for prevention and risk mitigation activities, in line with South Sudan Guidelines for WGFS. Building on the work of the WGFS, and in line with best practice, WV will continue to move towards and integrate livelihood and income generating activities at WGFS, conducting financial and literacy training to complement the skills learned. Further prevention and risk mitigation activities will include, broad awareness raising, information on access to justice, service mapping, referral pathways, and work with community stakeholders and change agents (community leaders, faith actors, women and youth representatives, protection committee representatives, etc.) who will share messaging further. WV will integrate SASA! and EMAP models more broadly (having started this work in 2019) across interventions to facilitate knowledge, attitude, and behaviour change that is community-led and transformative. Dignity kits will be distributed to women and adolescent girls (prioritizing new displacements, female-headed households, disabled women and girls, pregnant and lactating women, survivors of GBV, among others, as reflective of community demographics and need) alongside messaging on: GBV prevention and response services available, menstrual health and hygiene management, sexual and reproductive health and rights, etc. Finally, interventions will be informed by safety audits and safety perception mapping, to be conducted on an ongoing basis (and particularly as the changing context dictates); such tools will not be exclusive to GBV interventions, but mainstreamed across WV’s interventions, particularly in Food Security and Livelihoods, Shelter / NFI, Health and Nutrition.
The project reflects the unique needs of people in need, committing a gender marker that sees significant contributions to gender equality (including across age groups), prioritizing the needs of disabled populations (reflective of strong protection mainstreaming), and accountability to targeted communities (engaging stakeholders and conducting broad consultations to inform interventions and ongoing monitoring). The project design prioritizes a Do No Harm approach with an explicit intention to address protection issues experienced by women, girls, boys and men, specifically GBV, recognizing that respect for human rights and increasing gender equality are enablers of the South Sudan humanitarian response strategy.
Finally, WV maintains zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, and has a robust Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy and approach to realize this at a national level across South Sudan and globally.

1 - Needs

The protection situation in South Sudan remains dire, and the conflict has only exacerbated the unique experiences of women, girls, boys, and men. In March 2019, UNMISS extended its mandate in South Sudan until 2020, adopting Resolution 2459, which speaks explicitly to efforts necessary to build a protective environment, particularly in light of the continued ‘deterrence of SGBV against civilians’ and widespread human rights violations and abuses. Conflict-related sexual violence and other forms of violence against women and girls has become ‘normalized’ and continues, even after the signing of the R-ARCSS in September 2018. According to GBVIMS 2019 reporting (per latest available quarterly report), physical assaults continue to be the most commonly reported type of GBV (36% of reported cases), followed by emotional abuse (25%), rape (14%), denial of resources and opportunities (14%), forced marriage (6%), and sexual assault (5%). Incidents are most often committed by spouses and partners, confirming that IPV is the most prominent context in which these cases take place (39% of those reported). The next highest context is under harmful traditional practices (13%), speaking to the deeply ingrained patriarchal social norms and cultures that reinforce gender inequalities and power imbalances to the detriment of women and girls. Of the survivors who reported, nearly 20% are under the age of 18. Awareness raising and information sharing continues to be of utmost importance, particularly as 65% of survivors reported within the first 5 days (60% of these reported within the life-saving window of 72 hours); though 35% reported after six days of the incident. Ongoing assessments by World Vision in areas where WGFS are ongoing in 2019 (Twic, Renk, Fashoda, Yambio, Tambura, Juba) indicate the need for economic empowerment and IGAs to be prioritized by partners, and particularly should include diverse activities such as grinding mills, tailoring (including by machine and hand), charcoal, and selling of sandals and clothing (in addition to more common activities of bread making, groundnut paste production, beading, etc.). This is further supported by GBVIMS data which indicates that livelihoods and NFI services are two of the three GBV services not available to reporting survivors (overwhelmingly, safe houses is the most common GBV service requested but not available in South Sudan). Assessments conducted in Melut in Sept. 2019 indicate that greater efforts are required to engage with youth, particularly adolescent girls, who continue to be more reserved and less active in centre activities. A focus on intergenerational activities and group PSS that strengthens self-esteem, confidence, and social cohesions among age groups and across sex is necessary. World Vision began implementing the SASA! model and conducted a baseline in July 2019. Findings indicate that while knowledge of GBV is relatively high (68%), the harmful negative effects and consequences of GBV on individuals, families and communities is not so commonly understood or recognized (50% of individuals surveyed believe children are not affected by violent relationships). Although IPV is the highest context in which GBV takes place, half of those surveyed believe that women should tolerate violence for the sake of the family; a further 63% of those surveyed agree that others should not intervene to support households experiencing violence. Decision-making authority continues to sit with men (63% agree); decisions include how to spend household income, roles and chores ascribed according to culture and gender, and even visiting relatives. Respect is considered to be demonstrated only as long as gender roles are adhered to. Efforts to shift the dynamic and see increased equity within households and communities is perceived by men as hidden opportunities to dissolve unions. It is essential to scale up availability of life-saving GBV prevention, response and mitigation services.

2 - Activities

GBVSC OBJ1 [Comprehensive Case Management, PSS, link to PO1, SO2 and SO1] GBV case management, provision of PSS (individual and group), legal advice (facilitating access to justice), and safe referrals (to medical, including health care providers for CMR services, safety, access to justice, livelihoods, and other support services). As a leading CP actor, WV will ensure the provision of services for child survivors of GBV, including comprehensive case management, strengthening of referral pathways (linking GBV and CP actors), and awareness raising. WV will facilitate trainings in comprehensive case management, including specialized topics such as child survivors, survivors of IPV, case mentorship and management, etc. [WGFS, PSS, Legal Awareness, link to PO1, SO2] WV will provide basic PSS by age cohort, and through inter-generational peer support and mentorship activities at WGFS. Activities stem across basic PSS, referrals to additional services, information sharing and awareness raising, literacy and skills training, including IGAs. More specifically, activities include: structured and unstructured social sessions; information and awareness sessions on SRHR, positive parenting, reporting GBV and CP incidents, laws and rights, available GBV services and how to access these. GBVSC OBJ2 [Legal Awareness, link to PO1, SO2] The project strengthens access to justice by increasing community awareness of legal rights and protections accorded by domestic law, and build capacity of local and traditional authorities in statutory laws, policies and procedures to promote the rule of law which is survivor-centred and rights-based. Where formal justice mechanisms do not exist, engagement with traditional and religious authorities will be prioritized. [Awareness and Prevention–Comm. Awareness, link to PO2, SO2] Lack of awareness of services deters survivors from reporting. WV will map services available, strengthen referral pathways, including printing and distribution of IEC materials. Community protection mechanisms will be established/strengthened, supporting women’s rights committees, and local monitoring, reporting and response mechanisms. Community awareness raising campaigns will focus on increasing awareness of available services, how, where and when to report, and basic information on the harms, causes and consequences of GBV. [Awareness and Prevention–SASA and EMAP, link to PO2, SO2] WV will continue to grow and strengthen prevention initiatives using SASA! and EMAP models, which follow best practices in identifying community-based solutions to prevent GBV, identify power imbalances and gender inequalities, and engage men and youth as agents of change to realize women and girls equal rights in inter-personal dynamics, households, and communities more broadly. Community led dialogues will be supported to focus on attitude and behaviour change, specifically engaging male and female youth, to adopt positive actions that respect women and girls safety, equal rights and encourage positive masculinities. [Prep. and Mitigation–Dignity Kits, link to PO2, SO2] Dignity kits will be distributed in line with GBV SC guidance. Complementary messages on GBV, MHM, SRHR, etc. will be shared. GBVSC OBJ3 [PSS–Livelihood Services, link to PO1, SO2] Building on WGFS interventions and a growing portfolio in gender-transformative FSL, WV will provide literacy and skills training to support IGAs and alternative livelihoods. GBVSC OBJ4 [Prep. and Mitigation–Safety Audits, Mainstreaming, link to PO2, SO2] Ongoing Safety Audits will be conducted as context dictates, including across multi-sector interventions (for example, FSL, Health and Nutrition, WASH, Shelter and NFI). *As WV co-leads the Caring for Child Survivors of GBV TRG, a guidance note on the provision of quality GBV services, particularly for child survivors of sexual assault will be developed; further, WV will be involved in updating GBV SOPs as a contributing member of the Sub Cluster.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

WV's legal awareness and access to justice approach sees thoughtful dissemination, training, and capacity building on national law, policies, and procedures, stemming across both formal and traditional structures of authority. Such awareness and training will contribute to a rule of law that is survivor-centred and rights-based, and recognizes beneficiary and survivor's rights.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Safety Audits and Safety Perception Mapping will be conducted on an ongoing basis (using both observational and interview / focus group discussion tools to ensure that the voices and needs of communities are felt). The design of the project was informed by assessments and surveys (SASA, for example, completed in July 2019) where WV is operational and is proposing activities. Complaint, feedback, and response mechanisms are standard in all WV projects, and include mixed approaches, taking into account, age, gender, ability, literacy, etc., so all people can safely provide feedback and share perspectives.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Project works to strengthen and establish (where none exist) community-based protection committees, that encompass representatives from various stakeholders and influential leaders (including, for example, community leaders, faith leader, women's rights organizations, and youth representatives).

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Project is aligned with the Ministry of Gender, Child, and Social Welfare's National Action Plan (2015-2020) on Resolution 1325 (Women, Peace, and Security), and specifically Objective 1: provide protection for women and girls, including those disabilities, against any form of SGBV, and restore the respect for human rights, human dignity, and equality in South Sudan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

WV has a broad footprint in South Sudan, and is proposing specifically to maintain GBV operations in existing areas and where growth is possible (pending resource gathering), to grow GBV operations in areas where WV has established presence, so that people in need / vulnerable populations have a comprehensive package of multi-sectoral life-saving interventions. As a community-based organization, WV has established community acceptance in the areas proposed, and applies ongoing context monitoring and crisis modification to respond to changes in the environment that would require hibernation or scaling back of activities, prioritizing the safety and protection of WV staff, volunteers, and communities.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project's sustainability will be determined by the successful uptake of key attitude and behaviour change models, namely: SASA! and EMAP. In addition, WV seeks to work with existing protection committees and structures, not duplicating any community-owned initiatives, but further strengthening what exists, where possible. Women's rights organizations, youth groups, and protection committees more broadly are fundamental stakeholders to support sustainable action.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.2 - Women and girls are not included in consultation - WV will ensure that women and girls, and others of various vulnerabilities are equitably targeted for consultation and services by following guidelines reflective of the context in South Sudan: 98% of survivors are female and 20% are children under 18 (case management targets reflect this); for broad awareness raising 60-70% will be adult women; while 12-15% of targeted actions will reach IDPs (remaining, host community). 8.3 - Youth are not included in consultations and decision-making processes - WV has conducted ongoing safety audits and assessments of our work in WGFS, which have indicated that youth, particularly adolescent girls, seem reserved and soft spoken, suggesting that greater efforts are necessary to include their voices and needs in activities. WV will use inter-generational groups and activities to bring adolescent girls, allowing them a space to interact on their own, but also establish friendships and linkages with older women, particularly to discuss sensitive topics. Activities will be crafted around the needs of youth, including preference on timing, scheduling of activities, etc. 8.4 - Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection, for example) - Service mapping and dissemination of referral pathways will be a key component of the project. WV will socialize referral pathways at various centres (for example, health and nutrition centres, WGFS, CFS) and via multiple groups / stakeholders (protection and health actors, protection committees, trusted community stakeholders). 8.10 - Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance - WV has a robust Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy which includes PSEA. Ongoing orientation for new and existing staff and affiliates will be completed. WV adheres to a zero tolerance policy of SEA.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Consultations with communities are ongoing. Household Surveys and Focus Group Discussions are most commonly used, with methodologies targeting specific community members, using sampling methodologies from a representative sample that is reflective of community demographics (considering, sex, age, ethnicity, religious affiliation, ability, etc.). Key Informant Interviews and feedback is collected from Ministry representatives, local authorities, community / traditional leaders, faith leaders, women’s and youth group representatives. Influencing data and feedback is sourced from women, girls, boys, and men from the community more broadly. Further, safety audits (observation and focus group discussions) and safety perception mapping are completed monthly or quarterly (as context dictates / allows) to better understand the general situation of vulnerable populations and people in need, particularly women and adolescent girls (for example, how women and adolescent girls spend their time, problem or challenges in movement), and their safety and security (for example, where women and girls may feel unsafe, a description of the types of violence faced by these groups, how they choose to protect themselves, etc.). Consultations are conducted in a sensitive way, ensuring that facilitators are able to interact with participants and capture information using sex, age, and culturally appropriate modalities.
