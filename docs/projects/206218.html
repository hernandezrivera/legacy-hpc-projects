Project Summary

The effects of Sudan conflict have affected more than 300,000 South Sudanese returnees including Sudanese refugees. Over 500,000 thousand people are expected to cross to South Sudan by the end of December this year (2023). The targeted states are leading in receiving the highest number of affected people include Upper Nile and Jonglei States of South Sudan.
Generally, the food insecurity situation in Jonglei and Upper Nile state has remained poor due to prolonged flood situation, dry spells, existing conflict and insecurity, low agricultural production and the current influx of refugees and returnees from the Sudan conflict. To address the flood & Conflict induced acute food insecurity situation (IPC4) as well as livelihood need of most vulnerable households in the above counties, ADA intends to carry out the following interventions;
a) Food Assistance: Address immediate household food security needs through provision of food ratios to vulnerable household.
b) Climate Smart Agriculture: Through flood adaptive agriculture practices to promote and increase production among the most vulnerable households.
c) Economic Recovery: Through Livelihood interventions for example the Village Savings and Association (VSLA).
d) Cash Based Programming: For immediate access to basic needs.

1. Needs

The influx of returnees and refugees into South Sudan following the outbreak of conflict in Sudan on 15th April 2023 further compounded the already dire humanitarian situation straining resources available for food assistance. The continued fighting and influx meant the resources available remained stretched increasing humanitarian needs requiring additional funding to meet critical food and nutrition needs of the communities. The high levels of acute food insecurity in South Sudan remain worrying, with the most severely acute food insecure populations continuing to be significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks for example floods, inevitable economic crisis (inflation) as well as the insecurity and population influx due to the conflict in Sudan causing forced displacement and unavailability of enough food for household consumption. According to the current IPC analysis, 5.83 million people (46.3 percentage of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). A further 1.64 million people are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). The most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 50 percent of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) are Jonglei State (61.3 percent), Unity State (57.9 percent), Upper Nile State (56.1 percent) and Lakes State (52.8 percent). In the first projection period of December 2023 to March 2024 (harvest/ post-harvest season), the situation is projected to improve marginally as a result of seasonal availability of harvest. An estimated 5.79 million people (45.8 percent of the population) are projected to face IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), including 25,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) and 1.71 million people likely to be in Phase 4. In the lean season projection period of April to July 2024, the food security situation will deteriorate and an estimated 7.1 million people (56.3 percent of the population) will be severely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or above). 79,000 people are likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) while an estimated 2.34 million people are likely to be in Phase 4 (Emergency). Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition including 480,000 million children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.17 million expected to suffer Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). 870,000 pregnant or breastfeeding women are expected to suffer acute malnutrition in this period. An estimated 72 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap.

2. Project activities

Under this project, ADA intends to implement the following activities; a) Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 ) b) Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support; ADA will provide the emergency access to agricultural inputs to most vulnerable to increase the food production in the above counties. c) provide the training on best agronomic practices to increase the food production, reduce the post -harvest damages and building on seed multiplication approaches. The intervention will ultimately increase households’ self-reliance capacity and will reduce the dependency on humanitarian assistance in coming time. d) Cash Based Programming: For immediate access to basic needs. ADA will ensure that both in-kind food distribution 50% and multisectoral MPC 50% are done in the same locations to enhance food security. The list of beneficiaries in all locations of intervention will be shared with other partners to create synergy and complementarity, as well as avoid duplications. ADA will work with WFP and FAO to ensure harmonization of activities in the counties of intervention. Since ADA also intents to do Nutrition, the project will include complementarity with nutrition components targeted by ADA, e.g., MUAC and provision of communication materials for complementary breastfeeding. All these will create bigger impacts of intervention in same communities where ADA will be implementing this project
