Project Summary

BTSC is aimed at continued focus on drought prevention and life-saving humanitarian response by reducing household consumption gaps (primarily food) in 5 most affected rural villages of Burhakaba. BTSC will specifically target populations in IPC3 and 4 especially the newly displaced and worst affected Pastoralist/agro-pastoralist communities.

Needs

Buurhakaba is one of the poorest and most affected by natural disasters district in Bay region an estimated population 165,968 live in urban & rural areas.. Most of them rely directly or indirectly on agro-pastoralism or agriculture for their livelihood. According to the FSNU/FEWSNET 2018 Somalia Post Gu Seasonal Food Security and Nutrition Assessment The estimated number of children under the age of 6-59 months that are acutely malnourished at the time of the assessment in June/July 2018 is 226 300, including 42 500 who are severely malnourished (prevalence estimate). Between August and December 2018 an estimated 294 200 children will face acute malnutrition, including 55 200 who are likely to be severely malnourished (total acute malnutrition burden for Aug-Dec 2018). Data from OCHA indicates that the total population of internally displaced persons (IDPs) is currently 2.6 million. Results from the post-Gu assessment indicate that most of the 13 primary IDP settlements are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), and this is expected to be sustained through December 2018. local farmers are facing several challenges that restricts their production capacities, the major ones being (i) poor quality of seeds; little crop diversity, (ii) lack of adequate farm tools to carry out large size cultivation; (iii) poor roads networks and inaccessibility to main market centers; (iv) floods that destroy crops and irrigation infrastructure. To avoid or minimize the impacts of climatic and other shocks on vulnerable populations in Buurhakaba

Activities

The project will directly support the Food Security Sector`s cluster objective 2 through enabling households suffering from limited of access to food and income opportunities to meet their basic food needs.1. Provision of cash for work activities support to emergency and crisis household headed women and men in Burhakaba District of Bay region. BTSC will ensure gender as key criteria in ensuring all vulnerable men,women,boys and girls are considered in its livelihood program. 2. Provide Unconditional cash assistant to 1500 households in Burhakaba town and rural villages and ensure all vulnerable women household is considered in the program. BTSC will ensure 1500 HH of individuals in crisis and emergency benefited from cash transfer assistance by December 2019. 3. Provision of livelihood input and good agricultural practices training for 2000 people participating in productive livelihood rehabilitation asset through cash for work activities. 4.Organize and conduct capacity building training on resource management, early warning system, disaster risk reduction, good agricultural practices and conflict resolution for the 1500 community, farmers, religious/community leaders, women leaders (60% women) in Burhakaba town and 25 rural villages.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

As an emergency programme the cash provided will be focused on life saving needs and concentrate on addressing the immediate humanitarian challenges facing Somalia, namely: Short-term relapse to large scale humanitarian crises in Feb-May and Aug-Sep 2019. BTSC will be cognizant of the longer term goal of building a cash delivery system that can eventually be adapted as a social safety net system inherited by the government to implement core-resilience and social protection strategies. This will be contributed to through a safety net activity. With this in mind, the cash systems and structures that we design will consider how cash based solutions can address some of the longer term challenges facing vulnerable communities in Somalia, namely food security stress/crises in rural areas that severely weakens resilience to droughts that are likely to resume/occur in near future (rural safety net), as well as extreme poverty of long-term displaced resulting in a lowered resilience to urban stressers that deny a realistic pathway to increased protection, well being and reliable income (IDP safety net). In addition, the probability of drought and other shocks increasing without sufficient mitigation or early action for extreme poor (crisis modifiers). Further the activities proposed under complementary proposals of the participating agencies will contribute to building the resilience of targeted communities, along with the cash based interventions.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

BTSC will view gender in its full meaning, of specific needs and vulnerabilities for men and women. For this reason this action considers these needs, through inclusion of beneficiaries of both sexes, in each step of the design and implementation process. Further parts of this action are specifically designed to address social barriers and injustice towards women and youth. This action directly addresses issues of discrimination and exclusion of women from accessing rights. This holistic consideration of gender aspects will foster assistance that considers the sensitivities of the different needs and capacities of women, girls, boys ,men and those with disabilities by ensuring they participate constructively and benefit from interventions that address their specific concerns.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

BTSC will aim to target the most vulnerable through contextual assessment. In particular, child head hhs and hhs with children under 5; unmarried elderly or elderly people with no children as well as people with disabilities, pregnant women acknowledged to be poor and without source of income or family support networks will be prioritised. These groups are frequently vulnerable as they are labour constrained, have limited support and hence struggle to provide for their households without external support. Men and women, boys and girls are exposed to different risks and their needs are specific and diverse. Families can be separated and the majority of the displaced population is made up of women and children. For men, their traditional role of providers and protectors can be significantly diminished. Men impacted by conflict sometimes use sexual and gender-based violence to address feelings of powerlessness and hopelessness. For women and girls in particular, who in Somalia are primarily responsible for procuring and cooking food in the family, food insecurity in emergencies can contribute to increased risk of GBV.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

It's assumed that there will instances of displacement, due to conflict, natural disaster or evictions. Emergency Preparedness and Response is integrated through separate applications for funding as well as other programmes implemented BTSC to ensure timely response with life-saving assistance. BTSC also build relationships with communities, remain cognizant of clan dynamics, and engage and involve local leaders in programme implementation, in particular informing them of planned activities and targeting, which helps improve security through local acceptance.BTSC has a risk management Policy, Protection through risk identification, Assessment, Mitigation, Implementation and Monitoring of the risk.
