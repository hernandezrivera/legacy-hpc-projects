Project Summary

In line with the UNHCR GBV policy, the Inter-Agency minimum standards for GBV in emergency, the IASC guidelines, the GBV strategic objectives of the HRP as well as the “Call to Action” road map 2021-2025, the project is designed with the three areas of Prevention, Risk Mitigation and Response to address gender-based violence (GBV). The project aims at strengthening protection of women, men, boys and girls internally displaced and returnees including those with specific needs against exposure to risks of GBV and ensuring that all survivors have adequate and timely access to quality services in Borno and Adamawa states.
Additionally, the project will address specific issues related to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) involving humanitarian workers, Security actors and community leaders in the camps and host communities.
In prevention, the project will work to engage all women, men, boys and girls IDPs and returnees to address root causes of GBV such as gender inequality and unequal power dynamics in the community applying community-based approach which entails active participation of community-based structures and innovative approach such as the zero-tolerance village alliance (ZTVA). It is for the purpose to nurture ownership of communities and achieve sustainable localized solutions. Meaningful engagement of men and boys is emphasized throughout, acknowledging their positive power as agent of change toward GBV. Various capacity building will target IDPs and returnees leaders (religious and traditional), peer to peer groups, community volunteers. Raising awareness through mass sensitization campaigns will be conducted in and out of camps on GBV, effective behavior and social changes, sexual exploitation and abuse.
Risk mitigation is a key component to reduce GBV risks of women, men, boys and girls and promote their safety. The project will promote economic empowerment of women through life skills acquisition as a way to reduce vulnerability and mitigate risks of GBV and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Targeted assistance of dignity kits and alternative energy sources (efficient stoves, briquette) will be supported as recommended by the joint NE SAFE assessment. UNHCR priorities the most vulnerable including GBV survivors identified through protection vulnerability screening, protection monitoring and GBV intervention.
For the integrated Response, the project will ensure maintaining quality case management and referral pathways to ensure GBV survivors have safe and meaningful access to safe, timely, confidential and adequate services to immediately address survivors’ needs such as physical safety, health, psychosocial support, legal assistance, material support and economic empowerment in line with the survivor-centred approach. UNHCR manages Women Girls Safe Spaces where women and girls feel physically and emotionally safe, comfortable and able to express themselves and discuss sensitive issues and opportunities for social networking, solidarity-building, confidence-nurturing among fellow community members. The project pays a special attention to women and girls in detention facilities and women and girls previously used or associated with armed group as well as those released or those that have escaped from NSAG abduction. This project will be complemented by the UNHCR “access to justice” project which fight impunity by facilitating survivors’ access to justice for via legal representation, counseling and awareness.

1 - Needs

Protection incidents particularly gender-based violence (GBV) remain a major concern in the north east of Nigeria despite multiple efforts in terms of prevention and response. Widespread insecurity and ongoing conflict occasioned by incessant insurgencies, combined with lack of livelihood opportunities and basic necessities such as food and non-food items continue to increase the risk of GBV. Not only deep-rooted gender disparity but also changes of gender roles in family and community due to displacement further frustrates and challenges traditional gender norms that lead to GBV against women, men, girls and boys. Vulnerability of women and girls has also increased due to the social status of women since most of them are widows or single due to the high number of men killed during the conflict. Extremely limited livelihood opportunity and socio-economic support continue to expose women and girls to risks of engaging in harmful coping mechanisms such as sale and exchange of sex for daily necessities. Women and girls continue to suffer from the humanitarian crisis being at risks of kidnapping, sexual violence and forced marriage by non-state armed group, and face stigmatization, rejection and secondary violence upon return to their family and community. In 2020, UNHCR and its partner received 2,737 reported incidents of GBV in the operational locations through self-reporting and community-based protection monitoring. The analysis indicates that female (97%) and particularly girls under 18 remained most affected. Denial of resources (37.3%), Early and forced marriage (21.7%), Physical assault (19.7%), Psychological abuse (7.9%), Sale/Exchange of sex (6.17%), Sexual assault (3.9%), Rape (3.1%), were the major incidents reported. It is very difficult to determine the real prevalence of GBV, however, we consider it is under-reported due to stigmatization, fear of retaliation, lack of adequate protection services and inaccessibility to services due to the current security situation. With the global pandemic of COVID-19 and following restriction of movement and slowing down businesses, focus group discussions revealed the increase of intimate partners violence and further exposure to harmful coping mechanisms in the IDP camps and host communities. Protection needs for GBV are huge and the inter-agency capacity to respond remain insufficient, poor in quality and challenging in terms of location coverage in the current security situation. End of 2017, UNHCR, WFP and FAO carried out an assessment on «safe access to fuel and energy » within Borno state among IDP’s, returnees and host population. The finding showed that 95% of the interviewed population depend on firewood and charcoal for their daily cooking energy needs and 85% indicated that they face risks of rape, sexual assault, killing and abductions during collection of firewood including. There is an urgent need to address these issues by creating more economic opportunities particularly for women and adolescent girls. Additionally, in many local government areas, there is not yet the presence of civil authorities with almost a breakdown of law leading to impunity for GBV perpetrator in hard to reach areas. For those released or those that have escaped from NSAG, they are transferred in the “rehabilitation centre”. The whole process showed weakness and risk points for GBV and SEA. Many allegations of SEA by security actors as well as influential community members and government agency staff members have been reported by IDP’s. There is an urgent need to take appropriate measures such as capacity building, sensitization and high level advocacy to prevent SEA as well as awareness creation on the reporting mechanisms among the IDP and host communities.

2 - Activities

Establish and build capacity of community-based GBV structures for awareness raising, prevention and referral for specialized services (Youth club, women committee, community volunteers, peer to peer groups, groups of men and boys engaged)• Identify key male leaders in the community (religious, traditional) and engage them in prevention of GBV and SEA and peaceful dialogue • Implement the ground-breaking model of community mobilization using the zero-tolerance village alliance methodology to address root causes of GBV• The project will explore innovative way (promotion of kitchen gardening, referral for unconditional CBI multipurpose) to mitigate GBV/SEA due to lack of condiments and other basic needs. • Implement the SAFE approach to ensure IDP’s and returnees have safe access to fuel and energy by exploring distribution of alternative sources of energy • Life skills acquisition activities will be used to promote economic empowerment of women to reduce economic vulnerability of women and girls • Capacity building for security actors, humanitarian actors and IDPs and returnees on SEA and GBV and GBV mainstreaming• Advocacy at state level for favorable legal framework for protection of women and children • Strengthen PSEA community-based complaints mechanisms from the transit/reception facilities to the camps. 2. RISK MITIGATION• Identify, monitor and document through protection monitoring and vulnerability screening women, girls at risk including unaccompanied and separated children at the transit center, in the camp and in rehabilitation center • Assess, document and sensitize risk points of GBV/PSEA• Identify and assess through vulnerability screening adolescents and women in need of life skills acquisition and livelihood support • Support referrals of girls and women used or associated with armed group• Build capacity of GBV actors in identification and documentation of GBV incidents 3. MULTI SECTORAL RESPONSE• Provide psychosocial support, case management and GBV critical material support at the transit/reception facilities for new arrivals and rehabilitation centre for WAAFG• Manage integrated safe spaces for women and girls in the camps and host community to provide information, educational and recreational activities.• Strengthen Case Management and Referral System• Provide psychosocial support including life skill acquisition and legal support services • Dignity kits support for vulnerable girls and women including survivors living in the camps. • Contribute to the GBV Information Management System.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles:• Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need.• To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls.• If necessary, additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks.• In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures. • Establish measures aimed at preventing and responding to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, bearing in mind the UN zero tolerance policy.• All along project implementation, UNHCR will monitor protection, age, and gender aspects and will address any arising issues. To ensure a comprehensive response, all the findings will be shared with the Protection Sector as well as other relevant Sectors, with whom UNHCR will maintain close coordination.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will strengthen community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Child friendly complaint boxes will be introduced in child friendly areas as well as in educational structures. The existing tollfree number and email will be vulgarized and peer to peer approach will be established to support reporting of complaint. Focus group discussions with people from different age, gender and diverse origin will be conducted regularly to collect feedback from affected population on available services and as well as protection risks. UNHCR will ensure that committees of women, men, boys and girls are in place in order to participate fully in the implementation of activities. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention as well as to the attention of other partners, for intervention. UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the SG’s special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
