Project Summary


The project will aim at providing multi-purpose cash assistance at the MEB transfer value for various populations in Sudan, as well as supporting CWG through information collection, assessments and advocacy. To meet the rising needs identified whilst also factoring in the security and market constraints in especially West Darfur (WD), ADRA proposes to provide lifesaving assistance to vulnerable HHs and communities through MPCA thereby also preventing and reducing conflict between different groups though a conflict sensitive approach. Protection remains at the core of the response in targeted States, ensuring integrated targeting of beneficiaries of the Actions’ MPC and other interventions.
MPC: Based on ADRA’s assessments cash remains the optimal assistance to meet the diverse needs of vulnerable populations in Khartoum, El-Gezira, White Nile and Blue Nile especially as markets remain reasonably functional. It gives HHs freedom to prioritize their needs and even allows enough support for some to flee to safety. ADRA’s assessments show that Bankak, a mobile money service provided by the Bank of Khartoum, is the most appropriate modality for cash distributions at the time of the proposal, despite disruptions in the system and phone network. According to the CWG’s Financial Service Provider (FSP) Matrix, Bankak is the only functional FSP per January 2024. Furthermore, ADRA’s data from Dec 2023 shows that HHs in Khartoum also use Bankak to receive remittances from family members.
In late 2023, ADRA and Al-Massar provided cash assistance to 1500 HHs /1,300 beneficiaries in Kerrari, Elgezira, Sennar and White Nile. The proposed Program ADRA aims to upscale this response and increases the geographical coverage. Through the response, ADRA and local partner will work with emergency rooms and other grassroot actors in selecting beneficiaries and designing successful strategies to navigate the risks and complexity related to cash programming in Khartoum. ADRA will continue this conflict sensitive approach and will ensure that beneficiary lists are not single sourced but supported through different voices and groups. The Action will support vulnerable HHs with three monthly rounds of MPC as per the latest interim MEB shared by the Sudan Cash Working Group (CWG) - i.e. an estimated 200 USD per HH per round (~33 USD per person) depending on the number of HH members), amounting to nearly 173,000 SDG. According to ADRA’s MM from December 2023, this amount will cover 55-60% of the monthly MEB in Khartoum due to price increases and availability issues.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection is not only mainstreamed into all activities but it's also takes a focus in the project. ADRA will ensure that communities are involved across the different stages of the project from development to closing. At the same time, topics of hygiene, education, and protection will be presented in a way to maintain and enhance the dignity as well as benefit the health of the target population. Topics of MGM will be represented in key hygiene messages, as they play an important role in allowing women and girls to participate fully in daily routines without impediment due to lack of supplies, safe spaces, and knowledge of MHM practices and without encountering discrimination that can accompany menstruation. Even though there is very limited available data and information related to conflict issues and trends among IDPs and the host community, a good understanding of the context, including key conflict dynamics and key drivers of conflict, has informed the design of the program strategy and approaches. This understanding has been developed through field presence, work, and interactions with the communities. Experience from ADRA’s current program on community-based protection (funded by the Government of Canada) has provided evidence of main dividers and triggers of tensions/conflict as well as connectors have benefitted the design of the program. During implementation, RAWA aims to “do no harm,” by not exacerbating existing tensions. Conflict sensitivity will be used to ensure a fair distribution of services and inputs, and a meaningful representation and participation of different groups. The project has taken into consideration the power dynamics and potential conflict between men and women, IDPs and host communities as well as internal conflicts within the factions and host communities. Activities and approaches will be tailored to ensure cultural sensitivity and responsiveness to the specific needs of each group, for instance, messages in different indigenous languages and dialects. In addition, RAWA will promote opportunities for jointly implemented activities bringing together members of the two targeted communities. This is expected to enhance mutual understanding and positive interactions among different groups and individuals. ADRA will monitor and evaluate both unintended and intended impacts of the program on the conflict, tensions, as well as power dynamics. Particular attention will be given to monitoring potential backlashes related to tensions over new individuals and groups gaining new authority and independence and others fearing to lose their role and authority. Disaggregation of data on indicators by ethnical/tribal belonging as well as other factors such as age, gender, and disability status will help to ensure that all groups are represented and heard and that specific conflict sensitivity concerns are detected timely. Techniques and methodologies for data collection will also be conflict sensitive.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

ADRA's position of monitoring evaluation, learning and accountability MEAL, enhances the interlinkages between the four elements of programming. The complaint and feedback mechanisms have been designed to allow for quicker and more effective integration of feedback and complaint. The MEAL manager shares with the project manager feedback as is recorded in the CFRM register (described in 4.2 below). The feedback is then followed up on on a weekly basis, and integrated either through pipeline planning or in new projects.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The Beneficiary Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) allows program participants to provide feedback on the design, implementation, systems, and staff of the program, allowing them to influence the decisions related to the program and motivate corrective actions if needed. Depending on the already existing and/or new modes of feedback, the communities will determine to use what best works for them and ADRA will support accordingly and work with the community to provide feedback and corrective action. One of the existing mechanisms that has been successful is the set-up of complaint boxes in centers and health facilities. ADRA also has established hotlines to ensure that complaints that come in through the hotlines are addressed adequately and confidentially. Traditionally, complaints also come through the local leaders, emergency rooms, and community elders, and in this proposed Action, complaints will also come through the CPBNs. ADRA appreciates that the CFRM must be community centered so that community members, especially women and PSNs will have the opportunity to determine any other modalities that suit them best. ADRA’s Protection Assistant (trained by ADRA International Protection Advisor) receives the inputs and logs them into the CFRM log sheet. The sheet follows a traffic categorization system, where red indicates serious and sensitive allegations or feedback (PSEA, Fraud, Corruption..etc), and these are sent directly to the Country Director. The Yellow allegations are also serious but not sensitive and can be addressed by the Project Manager, meanwhile green are for minor complaints or feedback that could be addressed directly by the Protection assistant or involve the field teams. Serious PSEA allegations are often reported to the donor within 48 hours of receiving them and an investigative committee is set up by ADRA Board of Directors and ADCOM (ADRA Sudan administrative Committee).
