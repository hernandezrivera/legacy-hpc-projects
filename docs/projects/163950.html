Project Summary

- The Project is proposed to increase the education chances for children in Syria whom are suffering from war and its effects, Space of Hope NGO will do the needful to basically rehabilitate 20 schools in western rural Aleppo and rural Idlib to accommodate 6000 students (Boys & Girls) for the age range between 6-17 years old in order of Increase safe and equitable access to formal and non formal education for crisis affected children and youth.
1. Increased access to education and psychosocial support for conflict affected children - Provide support to formal general schools and establish Learning Centres providing non-formal education - Conduct BTL campaigns (media outreach, radio programs, awareness raising activities, community mobilization and community outreach) - Rehabilitate classrooms in line with INEE standards - Provide schools with school furniture (desk, chairs, blackboards, etc.) - Provide students with education kits - Provide structured PSS and recreational activities in formal schools and in Learning Centres using SOH’s Activity Handbook that provides a reference resource of basic PSS principles and specific ideas for conducting PSS activities - Provide maintenance and running cost (such as fuel for heating) in learning centres and schools - Construct, rehabilitate or improve gender-sensitive and disability sensitive WASH facilities
2. Enhanced capacity of education actors to provide quality education for conflict affected children - Train teachers and education personnel on child centred and protective pedagogy( gender-sensitive inclusive education, classroom management, codes of conduct and anti-bullying) – Train teachers and education personnel on psychosocial support and referral mechanisms for children with special needs - Train teachers and education personnel on Education in Emergencies (EiE) and national/INEE (Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies) Minimum Standards (MS) - Provide teachers and education personnel with monthly incentives – Provide teachers and education personnel with teaching resources, kits and guides Following needs assessments and programme design,
- holding community stakeholder meetings to provide an open forum and opportunity for the community and individual households to raise concerns, ask questions, and ensure greater decision making in programming. The meetings also explain the use of Complaints Response Mechanisms, empowering target communities to monitor the implementation of the project and promptly flag any concerns.
- recognize and address the different barriers faced by, and needs of, girls and boys in accessing, participating and achieving in learning.
- Recruitment of educational mentors and staff will also consider the need for gender equity and inclusivity and aims to accommodate an equal number of male and female centre staff.
- Community Education Committees consisting of selected teachers, parents and LCs will be established to act as a coordination and decision making body within the school and support all school related activities including assessment of needs
- communication with SOH and education authorities regarding needs, issues and ideas for implementation of education or social related activities within the school or local community.

4 - Needs

In the 1st of December 2019, Space of Hope (SoH) M&E team conducted a needs assessment for education sector in western and northern countryside of Aleppo governorate. The assessment was launched in order to determine the level of special needs of students and out of school children affected by the Syrian crisis, the overall education situations, the percentages of the children drop out of schools, Findings indicate that these areas contains approximately 60-71% IDPs from southern Syria and from the camps located in the northern of Idlib governance , which are in need in several services including Education, 28% (10% girls and 18% boys) of the children are out of schools for a duration of 6 months - two years , and 50% ( 30% boys and 20 % girls) of the students are at risk of dropping out of schools due to the lack of services provided for the schools in these areas. 7 of those areas in the western countryside of Aleppo contains 39 schools, only 10 schools are supported and the others are partly functioning due to the lack of support, which puts the students at a high risk of dropping out.the percentage of CwD at the areas assessed were 3% (both boys and girls), and they are facing a hug need for school transportation, schools well equipped with WASH disability-friendly.According to HNO 2020; 86% of the people in Idlib and Aleppo are in need have acute and immediate need of humanitarian education assistance, especially after the recent displacement of people from areas of western countryside of Aleppo towards NW Syria. The total number of schools in the assessed areas is 637 schools; 624 functional and 13 non-functional Decrease in the salaries of educational personnel and administrators up to 45% for the current academic year 2019/2020 in comparing to the last year Most of the support driven toward first cycle first to fourth grades, and there is a need to support the second third cycles fifth to ninth, and from tenth to twelfth.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Specific vulnerable groups/individuals are excluded from accessing education and psychological support. Mitigation measures: CRM mechanisms, participatory approach and consultation with local stakeholders, conducting community events that help to understand excluded groups especially vulnerable out-of school displaced children. In addition Conflict Sensitive teacher trainings include topics on creating a disability sensitive classroom aimed at facilitating the smooth integration of learning abled-physically disabled children with their peers and to reduce the negative social effects of their injuries, such as stigma and bullying 2-Overstretched education physical infrastructure posing a threat to children’s safety – Mitigation measures: SOH plans to conduct necessary rehabilitation, which will take into consideration gender, age and disability. All schools rehabilitation are conducted in line with INEE standards and schools/learning centers are supported to develop their emergency and safety manuals. The core tenets of SOH's approach to protection mainstreaming include: 1. Rigorous communication and feedback loops to ensure that the most vulnerable are able to voice their preferences and concerns at all phases of programme cycle management; 2. Protection Mainstreaming initiatives to enhance accessibility to assistance of those most vulnerable, including development of new dedicated protection strategy for Northern Syria 3-Behavior of educational personnel threatening children’s well-being and protection – Mitigation measures: to ensure accountability and quality, mentors and center staff sign a Code of Conduct (CoC) and participate in a workshop detailing CoC requirements as well child protection best practices. Training will be also conducted to all educational and SOH staff PSEA policy. Any breach of the CoC will be investigated and result in disciplinary project up to and including suspension from the project without pay

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: attack of school building or surrounding area and general violence Mitigation: move schools underground if this is determined to be a safe mitigation measure, provide safety/security equipment schools and carryout related training with period drills (training supported by the Civil Defense and health professionals); suspend school when its deemed too dangerous for children to attend. Risk: inability to move supplies/materials cross the Turkey/Syria border or on transit roads to project areas. Mitigation: when possible purchase locally and pre stock items when border/roads are accessible. Risk: looting or destruction of warehouse Mitigation: store items in numerous smaller warehouses to reduce impact of any warehouse getting affected; ensure good relations with local authorities and their support of education Risk: fighting will return to the areas of intervention, preventing the project teams from working and the beneficiaries from receiving the services. Mitigation: SOH security focal point will follow the security situation on the ground and will advise staff of any increased risk and of any areas they should immediately leave or avoid. All project teams will carry communication equipment with them at all times and remain in contact with their security focal points or advisers. Project activities will be suspended if the security focal points consider the risks to be too great. If the fighting spreads to the project areas, and if the logistical and security situation allows, the teams may move to new locations and continue project activities. SOH has experience of working under such conditions and will remain fluid and adaptive, allowing for project methodologies and modalities to change, as the situation on the ground demands, also SOH has a duty of care policy that determine suitable mitigation in case of emergencies .
