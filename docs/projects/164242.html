4 - Needs

Amongst the civilians in Syria 6.2m have been displaced, some of them already several times due tochanging front lines (HNO2019). While many households managed to return to their homes, they often need to restart their lives there from scratch. In this fluid, uncertain situation children and women bear the burden of the consequences of the conflict. Many families live in dire needs and parents struggle to cover their most basic needs, because of lost livelihoods or unemployment. Savings are long depleted after several years of conflict and poverty is on the rise due to this prolonged crisis. Inflation of the Syrian pound further undermines the purchase power of Syrian families. Salaries in Syria are low and income options are few. So even if there is a breadwinner that provides for the households the little they earn loses a lot of the value to procure daily needed items. On the other hand agricultural production in Syria has also not recovered and is not yet producing basic foods at the level needed to feed the own population. As a result there are high numbers of people in need. As for the target areas of Dorcas' livelihoods interventions they are as follows: In Aleppo livelihood needs are at the severity level 3 with 517,117 people in need of support (2019). In Homs livelihoods needs are at a severity level 3 with 250,989. From among the high needs in these urban locations Dorcas will select vulnerable households that need limited cash- or equipment support in order to restart their own income generating activities and no longer depend on handouts. Dorcas additionally selects needy farmers from northern rural Homs communities to ensure that their food generating activities sustain the livelihood of the family.

5 - Implementation of activities

Dorcas plans to select vulnerable breadwinners from Returnees, IDPs and host communities in Aleppo and Homs and support them with either cash or in-kind provisions for needed inputs so that they can once again generate income for their households themselves. Support interventions reach from covering gaps in service provision of shops, book stores, carpentry, car repair shops or farms. Each household being required to support their request with a business plan that shows income and expected improvements based on the request. While the primary focus is on urban needs, also farmers will be supported through agricultural inputs on one hand to secure and sustain their family livelihood, on the other hand to enhance the food production efforts in Syria.
