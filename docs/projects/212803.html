Project Summary

In 2024, the Syrian Arab Republic enters its thirteenth year of crisis, as families and communities continue to suffer from violence, displacement, and socio-economic deprivation along with further health risks resulting from epidemics. In 2024, 16.7 million people within the country will need at least one type of humanitarian assistance – due to fragility resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to basic goods and services (UN estimates).
The crisis in Syria has been characterized by widespread use of explosive weapons and the risks communities face from explosive remnants of war, landmines, and improvised explosive devices (IEDs). Estimates indicate that 11.4 million children and adults live in areas contaminated by explosive remnants of war or IEDs (United Nations estimates 2023). In addition to the direct risks these hazards pose to children's physical safety, they also hinder access to schooling, the delivery of essential services, and humanitarian aid.
Awareness campaigns in war-affected neighborhoods of Deir ez-Zor aim to educate residents about the risks of landmines and unexploded ordnance. Through community outreach programs and informational sessions, locals learn how to identify and report hazardous areas, reducing the risk of accidents and injuries. These initiatives foster a safer environment for civilians to return to their homes and rebuild their lives amidst the aftermath of conflict.
Activities:
Conduct risk education for at-risk groups by following:
- Identification of At-Risk Groups: This involves determining who is most at risk from landmines and UXO in a given area. Common at-risk groups include children, farmers, refugees and internally displaced persons (IDPs) returning to their homes, and individuals involved in collecting scrap metal.
- Community Engagement and Involvement: This involves working with local leaders, schools, and community organizations to disseminate information effectively.
- Education and Awareness Campaigns: These campaigns use a variety of methods to educate people about the dangers of landmines and UXO. Techniques can include classroom education, community meetings, distribution of educational materials (such as flyers, posters, and brochures), Interactive methods like theater performances and storytelling can be particularly effective, especially for children.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Residents of Deir ez-Zor Governorate face significant challenges in accessing agricultural fields, private properties, local markets, schools, hospitals, public buildings, and other facilities due to the prevalence of mines in destroyed neighborhoods. These mines not only pose a threat to public buildings but also to the lives of returning residents in devastated areas, children who may encounter unknown objects while playing, agricultural workers on their lands, and construction workers. Children, in particular, are among the most at-risk groups, as their curiosity about unidentified objects found while playing can expose them to injury or death. Those returning to their homes in devastated areas encounter considerable obstacles in rebuilding their lives, hindered not only by reconstruction efforts but also by the lack of safe access to essential services like water and electricity. Agricultural workers, who rely on the land for their livelihood, face tough choices between risking their lives to cultivate the land and abandoning their only source of income. Reconstruction workers, crucial in rebuilding destroyed communities, also face the dangers of landmines, which can cause serious injuries or fatalities during their work. In light of these challenges, there is an urgent need for effective mine clearance efforts and the securing of affected areas to ensure the safety and security of Deir ez-Zor's residents.
