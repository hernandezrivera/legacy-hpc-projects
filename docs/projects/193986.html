Project Summary

The project target 100,000 in three state South Kordofan, Gadaref and North Darfur, will build the capacity of community governance structures as WASH committees, train and equip community committees to continue operating and maintain WASH infrastructure, conduct water chlorination, and monitor chlorine levels. As will conduct training to strengthen multi-sectoral approaches such as WASH in Health Facilities , WASH in schools, and WASH/GBV. NPO will preposition supplies for disasters like floods, epidemics, and other conflict-related displacements. Logistical costs of transporting supplies in collaboration with other sectors according to agreed point via WASH sector.
The project will intervene with health lifesaving interventions for outbreak control of cholera, typhoid, hepatitis E, COVID19, and other vector control in communities, this is due to more than 40% of the health facilities do not have sanitation facilities in place, so the project will provide technical support and carry out health interventions prioritizing common diseases treatment centers upon specific demands and investigations.
NPO has good experience in this field of WASH with technical staff will lead the implementation , NPO also has sub-office in all target locations to facilitate the movement, storage and management.
Project will provide WASH services to organize water existing sources management, rehabilitate some water points, constriction for new water yards, provide hand washing facilities as well the project will conduct hygiene promotion for communities focusing on women & children and will provide public and shared latrines.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Water collection in South Kordofan, North Darfur and Gadaref especially for returnees and host communities is women and girls responsibility, reducing time available for other activities. The situation is compounded by the fact that women often have little decision making power and influence in communities. It is critical that the project responds to the differing needs and rights of women and girls. We will do this by promoting their meaningful participation in planning, implementation and monitoring, and also in leadership roles. To ensure women’s involvement in these male dominated spaces, they will receive additional support, as negotiation skills, and public speaking, and men will be sensitized on the value of women’s involvement to act on their concerns. Where hygiene promotion is concerned, the activities will be made gender-specific: that found in general, women practice better hygiene than men (particularly where defecation is concerned), but also that women and adolescent girls have specific needs regarding menstrual hygiene, when looking after children, and protection from gender-based violence. The reality is that in some areas, promoting women’s involvement will be difficult, because of strict cultural norms on gender segregation, keeping women out of the public . We will use several strategies to boost women’s involvement as much as possible in the local contexts, such as work with male-dominated water committees to ensure they take the needs and rights of women into account; promoting community dialogue about women’s role. NPO will carry out a gender analysis for each targeted locality and will engage with communities on issues that emerge from the analysis. For the program as a whole, a gender equality inclusion strategy will be drawn up, which all partners will agree to, stipulating how the program will be implemented to promote gender equality. Sex and age disaggregated data will be collected throughout implementation to allow NPO to understand and adapt the program to ensure best possible impact for women and girls. An analysis of the effect of the project on reducing gender inequality will be included in the project reports.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Consider installing lights near the latrines, especially is these are communal or away from homes or alternatives such as providing torches for each household. - Do not place facilities near possible perpetrators. N.B. The police and armed forces are often seen as perpetrators of violations. Whether they provide a reassuring feeling or instill fear depends on the location. It is important to consult the community and potential beneficiaries about their preferences. - Consider ways to reduce/manage/cope with these threats, including location, accompaniment and arrange appropriate policing if required (as water monitors). - If an individual does not feel safe or dignified in using the latrine, (s)he may go elsewhere, potentially exposing themselves or others to harm. - Physically separate and label the latrines “male” & “female” - Ensure latrine design accounts for children (size of pits may present a safety risk for children) - It is preferable that latrines and showers can be locked from the inside to ensure privacy. - Consider that women and children are the most likely to be collecting water, and might be waiting in the sun for hours for a turn to fill their jerry can. Establish safe and shaded waiting areas where possible. - Schedule water collection times to limit overcrowding at the water points; collection in shifts. - Children should not carry jerrycans. However, assuming they will be forced to do so, make sure that jerrycans are not so large as to cause injury. - Ask individuals collecting water when they would prefer taps/pumps to be open. Recognize that different people have different work schedule, women and girls who are typically the water collectors have specific times when they are busy making meals. - Ensure that you have discussions on latrine/shower design with various groups and adapt the design.
