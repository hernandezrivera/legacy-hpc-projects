Project Summary

The need to sustain sexual and reproductive health “SRH” in Gaza, remains very high, considering the impact of the protracted crises, the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, and the latest May escalation, with associated redirection of resources (human, medical and logistics) from the provision of SRH to COVID-19 and casualties etc. As evidenced in the 2022 HNO, more than 150,000 women and girls were identified as high vulnerability of high-risk pregnancy, anemic mothers, female adolescents, and women with unmet need for family planning etc.
This project is aligned with HNO 2022 Health Cluster Objectives 1 & 2 linked to Strategic Objectives 2 & 3 respectively. The project contributes to preventing excessive maternal morbidity and mortality, which have markedly deteriorated this year. The project will increase access to SRH care and nutrition services and information amid COVID-19, including family planning, gynecological disorders, and ensure that safe delivery, and emergency obstetric and newborn care services are available with specific needs of people with disabilities “PwD” and protection/GBV needs are mainstreamed.
The project will complement previous and ongoing UNFPA programs/interventions; HPF 2021, third reserve allocation and other projects; by expanding the coverage of health system capacity to ensure continuation of essential SRH and maternal care services at primary and secondary health care levels and community.
The project will: -
- Ensure continuation of SRH services, enhance access to quality SRH care and strengthen SRH management and counselling, including the operationalization of relevant COVID-19 guidelines and protocols.
- Build the capacity of health providers in lifesaving SRH issues with focus on anemic pregnant and lactating women, high-risk pregnancy, detection and management of gynecological and pre-menopausal disorders and continuation of SRH services amid COVID-19, etc.
- Strengthen the preparedness and response of the health facilities, fill gaps and replenish lifesaving SRH equipment, drugs, commodities, and supplies including contraceptives and PPE.
- Actively engage communities, support coping mechanisms, sensitization, awareness raising and outreach including midwifery led home visits model.
- Support linking and informing communities on available services, including protection from GBV and psychosocial support, subsidies, and cash vouchers etc.
with special focus on marginalized communities and vulnerable groups including people with disabilities (PwD), young girls, the poor and elderly etc.
- Improve SRH services coordination among various stakeholders/providers, including mainstreaming disability, adolescents need, protection and GBV in SRH service provision and community outreach.
While this project is meant to address immediate response to urgent and critical needs of Gazan’s women, and young girls, especially those in marginalized areas, it will also support men and other vulnerable adolescents and strengthen the preparedness and resilience of both the community and the health system for absorbing shocks, saving lives and recovering from crises. UNFPA will work with MOH, Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS) and other NGOs as needed to implement this project.
UNFPA has been approached by Union of Health Works Committees (UHWC), who is applying for a project to support SRH services targeting women in their centers’ catchment areas in Gaza, to ensure coordination. UHWC has been encouraged to apply, and to continue coordination and sharing experience and information on localities and target groups upon project implementation to avoid duplication and ensure synergism with UNFPA and other projects. Similar coordination took place between UNFPA, CARE/PMRS and MAP-UK to ensure complementarity.

1 - Needs

Palestinian women, men, girls, and boys in Gaza face a range of serious protection and humanitarian threats including threats to life, liberty, and security. Gender-based violence (GBV) continues to be a key protection and health concern in Gaza, further impeding women’s and girls’ access to resources including lifesaving SRH services, counselling, and information. Following 14 years of tightened blockade on Gaza coupled with the internal political division, Gaza faces increased poverty and deterioration of living conditions (OCHA 2020), with a health system on the verge of collapse with limited access to essential sexual and reproductive health services (SRH). These persisting challenges, compounded by the latest military attacks in May, have led to a serious deterioration in the availability and quality of health services and are limiting access to basic SRH care, including maternal health services, for more than 80,000 pregnant and lactating women. 40% of pregnant and lactating women suffer from anemia and 14% of lactating mothers in the most deprived communities in Gaza are undernourished (HNO 2022). The health system is on the edge with limited resources to sustain basic, but critical, health services (i.e., maternal health, emergency obstetric services, family planning, preconception & antenatal care and management of gynecological disorders etc.). There is also an apparent lack of life sustaining SRH equipment, drugs, and disposables, as they are currently under-prioritized and inadequately funded., According to MOH September 2021 drugs report, 54% of essential MCH drugs are at zero stock level and 14 % will only cover a period of 3 months, which further increases the risk of death and disability as well as increase GBV for women and girls. A study by Women Affairs Center, supported by UNFPA on the impact of the May escalation on women, revealed that women, especially pregnant and lactating and those of chronic diseases including breast cancer survivors, have suffered from poor nutrition and inability to access health facilities to get their medications and needed health care services. During 2021, SRH continues to be of increasing concern in Palestine, and in Gaza with flaring increase in maternal deaths, including COVID-19 related, compared to 2020. According to the maternal mortality committee data, as of October 2021, 26 maternal deaths where registered, compared to only 13 cases all over 2020, with 70% of deaths is COVID-19 related. Caesarian section (CS) rate remains high in Gaza, 23.8%, compared to the WHO recommendation of 10-15%. Increased percentages of Anemia among pregnant women mounted to 40%. One of three pregnant women are registered as high-risk pregnancies in Gaza (MOH annual 2020 annual reports). High-risk pregnancy, ANC and CS are reflections of quality of care that needs to be improved as to avert maternal mortality and morbidity. Ensuring continuation and accessibility of essential SRH services in Gaza, given the new flare of COVID-19 cases and the instability of the situation along with the shortages of essential drugs, supplies and PPEs as well as human resources, becomes more needed than ever. Among other needs, family planning (FP) services are of a particular concern, due to stock outs, as reported by MOH, 3 items are out of stock as of October. There is a growing need to increase access to health care, by addressing its main barriers including the financial cost of medicine and services, which was reported among the major barriers to access health services in Gaza (MSNA/HNO 2022). Even during emergencies, SRH including maternal health and FP, GBV/protection services are essential and must be sustained, as they are central to health, rights and well-being of women and girls. However, this becomes a challenge in light of the recent shift in attention and critical resources away from this provision, as shown by research that unmet need for family planning is generally greater among more fragile countries (UNFPA/2020: Impact of Public Health Emergencies on SRH and Reproductive Rights in the Arab Region: The COVID - 19 Case). In general, pregnant women and young girls, especially under displacement, are more vulnerable to experiencing serious respiratory infections and must be treated with utmost priority. A recent systematic review/meta-analysis by the BMJ concluded that pregnant women with COVID-19 have a higher potential for admission to ICU and invasive ventilation compared to non-pregnant women. The analysis also showed that Preterm birth rates are high in pregnant women with covid-19 than in pregnant women without the disease (https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/370/bmj.m3320.full.pdf). In July 2021, UNFPA in Gaza have received a technical mission by one of its senior humanitarian experts from the humanitarian office based in New York, who met with senior MOH management and major SRH providers and delivered MISP TOT training to a group of health providers. One key recommendation out of that mission was to enhance SRH coordination among various players. Accordingly, UNFPA is finalizing the recruitment of a dedicated SRH humanitarian coordinator to support and strengthen coordinated SRH needs, response, preparedness, information & networking in Gaza.Through this project, UNFPA and its partners are aiming to strengthen the resilience and coping mechanisms and support the well-being of people, especially women and young and adolescent girls. These mechanisms will include set of planned interventions; implementing appropriate response, sustaining essential SRH services, replenishing shortages of supplies, building the capacity of the providers, enhancing referral and coordination among key players, engaging men and activating communities through outreach activities including awareness raising sessions, utilizing Media and home visits and supporting activities that address basic SRH and protection needs, protection from sexual exploitation, and GBV.

2 - Activities

This project will build on and complement previous and ongoing UNFPA interventions, both under humanitarian interventions i.e., CERF and pooled fund, as well as joint development project with WHO and UNCEF funded by AFD. UNFPA along with its partners will target major maternities and PHC facilities, through provision of essential SRH equipment, instruments and lifesaving pharmaceuticals and supplies including family planning commodities. Additionally, interventions will focus on expanding the method mix, and PPEs, training of health providers on quality SRH services, counselling and launching community outreach campaigns, reaching out to vulnerable groups, PLW, high risk pregnant women, and women with gynecological disorders, young girls and men with home visits lead by midwifery/nursing teams. The project will support vulnerable people’s access to SRH services (imaging, lab tests and other procedures) through health vouchers/subsidies. Outreach will include awareness raising, targeting tens of thousands of people, including men, and adolescents (boys and girls) in vulnerable areas of Gaza strip, through sessions, media, social media and other venues. Through this project, and building on lessons learned, and previous achievements, UNFPA will respond to urgent needs in SRH services, by filling gaps and preventing system collapse through sustaining and scaling lifesaving SRH services, providing critically needed supplies, community engagement including men and boys, and capacity building of staff and enhancing SRH coordination and reporting among various actors in Gaza. Practically, and in order to enrich the intervention and expand its coverage, UNFPA will work on the following domains.  Support SRH humanitarian platform of coordination and networking by active SRH actors and active linking to Health Cluster planning & reporting mechanisms.  Improve emergency preparedness and response of SRH needs in the health system and the community in the Gaza Strip. Strengthen access to lifesaving SRH services, through supporting major maternities and PHC facilities, with critically needed equipment/instruments, drugs and supplies including family planning commodities “introducing new methods i.e., hormonal IUD, implants etc.”, supplements, Iron and folic acid to support the response, and capacity building of staff including on adaptive service provision skills (tele-medicine etc.) Support preconception antenatal, premenopausal care and family planning services & information, mainstreaming disability, GBV and psychosocial support, prevention of sexual exploitation activities “PSEA” trainings of staff.  Enhance proper referral of SRH services among various providers and levels of care. As chair of GBV sub-cluster, ensure appropriate linkages and referral of GBV detection and management in the health facilities and the community, supporting access of vulnerable women to specialized health services through subsidies and linking them to protection services and women empowerment programmes i.e., cash voucher etc. Support networks of community activists and implement community mobilization campaigns including health education/awareness component, that will target the issues from a community and women empowerment perspective via educating families; men, women, boys and girls on basic gender and human rights issues, including women/girls with disability and their personal hygiene and RH needs. Moreover, risks “health and social” of early marriage and promotion of health outcomes and reproduction, supporting positive participation on relief and preparedness to emergencies will also be among the suggested themes. Activities/targets: 1. Provision of lifesaving needs; drugs & disposables, PPEs, equipment, instruments, and medical furniture to 10 (5 MOH and 5 NGOs) maternities and 45 PHC clinics (27 MOH and 18 NGOs). 2. Improve the capacity/skills of 300 health providers, practitioners on various SRH issues including: Preconception care, ANC, FP including the new methods, EmONC, infection prevention, detection, MISP, and management of gynecological and pre-menopausal disorders and other SRH issues. The training will be based on standard developed protocols by MOH including COVID-19 protocols. 3. Support access of vulnerable women to SRH services through health vouchers/subsidies. 750 women 4. Provide midwifery-led counselling services and psychosocial support to women, men and girls “in households” in 5 Gaza governorates, including distribution of supplements, linking/referrals to specialized heath, GBV, and women empowerment service. i.e., distribution of dignity kits, cash vouchers program etc. building on UNFPA’s and implementing partners’ experience and partnership with WFP cash voucher system through projects funded by Japan supplementary budget, CERF and HPF. 5,000 home visits/10,000 people reached.5. Support SRH coordination platform of major actors in Gaza to ensure complementarity, share experience, and sustain regular reporting and monitoring of gaps. increase access to SRH services and information in Gaza. 6. Conduct community-based interventions; supporting community networks, outreach/ awareness sessions on preparedness and available services, and media campaigns advocating for universal health coverage and right to health and access to services and increase awareness on PSEA among communities through media campaigns, and linking to protection, GBV, and MHPSS services/ 30,000 people

3 - Indicators

UNFPA shall integrate the project components/activities into its annual work plans with implementing partners (IPs) and will develop a detailed monitoring and evaluation plan, in line with UNFPA’s M&E system and framework, which includes appropriate tools and beneficiaries’ eligibility criteria, that will be used to track project performance indicators, beneficiaries’ feedback and achievements of results. UNFPA and its implementing partners have been working with beneficiaries on design and implementation of programming, as well as receiving feedback on interventions and adjusting interventions accordingly, including complains system PSEA training and the active hotline operated by SAWA. The monitoring framework shall track progress against achieving key deliverables and milestones both on partners and UNFPA directly implemented activities. UNFPA jointly with IPs will conduct focus groups discussions with beneficiaries and regular field monitoring visits to the implementation sites, to assess and track progress towards planned results, and address challenges that may affect implementation. UNFPA and IPs will be ensuring/advocating vulnerable beneficiaries i.e. marginalized people, GBV survivors and women & girls with disabilities; are reached and mechanisms to reach them and facilitate their access to services are considered by providers. Also, field monitoring visits may be planned and done jointly with the donors upon their request.
