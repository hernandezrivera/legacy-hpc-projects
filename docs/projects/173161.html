Project Summary

The aim of the project is to enhance disaster resilience among Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar through effective multi-hazard risk management. The Project will work in close collaboration with ISCG Sectors, Local Government and other relevant stakeholders to support ISCG's overall disaster risk management strategy through:
i) localised weather-forecasting and monsoon preparedness;
ii) DRR technical support to Site Management Site Development sector;
iii) technical assistance to develop DRM framework for Rohingya Response (JRP2021);
iv) strengthening Local Government and community capacity for DRR and preparedness in host communities and camps including synergy and coordination of DRR and response preparedness initiatives in the host communities and camps.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar is one of the most disaster-prone districts in Bangladesh and is highly exposed to natural hazards and extreme weather impacts including cyclones, landslides, flash floods, storm surges and extreme temperatures. The district is exposed to frequent cyclones in the recent years such as Cyclones Amphan (2020), Bulbul (2019), Mora (2017), Roanu (2016) and Komen (2015). Cox’s Bazar district also remains amongst the poorest districts in Bangladesh and has long struggled with food security, livelihood opportunities, insufficient water and sanitation facilities, weak infrastructure, poor health and education indicators. In addition, Cox’s Bazar is now home to around 1 million Rohingya refugees since 2017. The Rohingya crisis has increased the size of the population at risks, as well as added pressures on environment which undermine resilience. Due to congestion, site typography, and temporary shelter structures, Rohingya households are highly impacted by severe weather events - windstorms, heavy rains, slope failure, landslides, and flooding. Between May to July 2020, weather events such as windstorms, heavy rains, slope failure and flooding have impacted over 20,000 households in the camps. Even in the absence of a major disaster, this climatic context has considerable impact on the refugee response operation in Cox’s Bazar. The Local Government and first response agencies lack adequate capacity to effectively prepare for and respond to extreme weather events and disasters. According to the recent DRR KAP Survey (Sept 2020), only 28% of the host communities and 58% of the refugees reported to have enough information about what to do if a cyclone occurs. 67% respondents from both the host communities and refugees also reported that there are people who need extra help or are at greater risk before and during a cyclone. The project will aim to strengthen capacity of DMCs , first response agencies and community volunteers for disaster preparedness and response incorporating special needs of the extremely vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities, elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers. While the past SMSD efforts have undoubtably contributed to reducing risks in the camps; needs and priorities within host communities and the camps have evolved over time. Considering the protracted nature of the Rohingya crisis and disaster-prone population of Cox’s Bazar district, there is need to develop a comprehensive “Disaster Risk Management Framework”. Such a framework will serve as overall guidance for the Government, humanitarian and development actors working across the camps and in the host communities. The project will apply the guidance and practical tip-sheets developed by the Protection Sector to mainstream protection within the relevant activities such as access to hazard information, early warning and early actions to protect the most vulnerable groups. Protection lens will be used during the formulation of the Disaster Risk Management Framework. Women and girls make up more than half of the refugee population, as well as face higher risks of marginalization during access to information and resources. According to the recent DRR KAP Survey (Sept 2020), women and girls had consistently less access to information regarding severe weather early warning and preparedness. The project will work with relevant duty bearers such as the Local Government, DMCs and first response agencies ensuring early warning messaging, communications and resources are accessible to women, girls, boys and men. The camp level preparedness plans and union level contingency plans in host communities will seek to ensure participation of women and girls, boys and men as well as gender diverse groups.
