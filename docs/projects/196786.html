Project Summary

The purpose of NRC’s WASH intervention is to deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services to the most vulnerable people, and provide support to water, sanitation, and sewage systems to ensure continuous services for affected people in Syria. NRC will respond to the diverse WASH needs faced by vulnerable populations throughout Syria through the delivery of a multi-faceted WASH response. The different components of NRC’s WASH project for 2023 are intended to contribute to the HRP WASH Sector’s Objectives 1, 2, and 4.
NRC will deliver this project through a direct implementation modality: geographic areas will be targeted based on the severity and scale of needs, inter-agency coordination at hub-level, and access. NRC will provide assistance to vulnerable conflict and displacement-affected populations, including IDPs, refugees, members of host communities, and refugee/IDP returnees, and non-displaced households suffering the effects of the conflict. All interventions will seek to prioritize the most vulnerable households and communities. Where appropriate, multi-sectoral vulnerability scoring techniques will be used that integrate vulnerability indicators. The needs of the different genders, elderly, and persons with disabilities (PWDs) will be considered, during design, implementation and monitoring processes.
This project includes emergency response through the provision of one-off support as part of an integrated, multi-sector response in accordance with the Emergency Preparedness and Response Plans (EPRPs), and comprises of following Result Areas:
- Temporary WASH services (including water trucking), household assets (Hygiene NFIs), support to Education in Emergencies (WASH services for temporary learning spaces), hygiene promotion activities.
- The provision of medium-term WASH support at household- and settlement-level to populations living in and out-of-camps. Comprises of two result areas: provision of WASH infrastructure, and services and hygiene items in camps and communal shelters.
- The provision of support for long-term improvements in access to water, hygiene, and sanitation services at the settlement- and household-level. Comprises the following result areas: rehabilitation of community WASH infrastructure (boreholes, including solar energy systems, water and sewage networks, solid waste management); rehabilitation of WASH facilities in institutions (WASH facilities in schools as part of joint interventions with Education, WASH in medical centers, etc.); rehabilitation of household WASH facilities and provision of water storage units (see NRC’s Shelter/NFI project submission).
The project will also include consultation with communities through FGDs, HHQs, and KIIs, as well as with relevant authorities. Special attention will be given to ensuring that interventions are accessible by all and that there are functional complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFMs) in place.
NRC will invest in more sustainable, long-term, and cost-efficient WASH service provision in IDP camps, or rehabilitation of WASH infrastructure in in-camp settings, i.e., the rehabilitation and construction of water and sewer networks, reducing WASH needs and costs in 2023 in the targeted locations. NRC will also invest in more sustainable, long-term and cost-effective provision of WASH services, through the rehabilitation of infrastructure and WASH facilities in shelter and community infrastructure. This will minimize the need for short-term emergency solutions, and enable NRC to reduce the needs in covered locations, as well as to focus on other locations that remain in need.

3 - Needs

In 2022, damage suffered to WASH infrastructure during the conflict and a lack of maintenance were exacerbated by drought, a water crisis, as well as the lack of access to energy, causing further decreases in access to safe drinking water and sanitation. Over the past year, the number of people in need of WASH assistance increased by 2.6% from 2021, as estimated in the 2022 HNO. In Syria, only 48% of population relies on water networks as their primary source of water – other sources, such as household wells, surface water, and water trucking, often remain considered unsafe. This is compounded by the fact that 70% of sewage is discharged untreated, below average rainfall, and above average temperatures in 2021 and in 2022, leading to farmers using more surface water for irrigation (NRC Rapid WASH Needs Assessment, October 2022), which has severe negative impact on public health with increasing numbers of leishmania, malnutrition (raising trend in chronic malnutrition from 2020 to 20219), and an outbreak of cholera declared on 10 September 2022 by the Ministry of Health, with 30,219 suspected cases and 85 attributed deaths reported between 25 August and 29 October 2022.

4 - Implementation of activities

NRC will contribute to the Sector Strategic Objective SO1: rehabilitation of sewer networks and support to SWM and provision of communal- and household-level water storage units through rehabilitation of water extraction and distribution systems and/or equipping them with solar power systems. These activities will be done in coordination with the Water Authority and the WASH Working Group and relevant municipal bodies. NRC will ensure that communities are engaged from the design phase, to encourage ownership and households' contribution to connect their houses to the main sewer line and water networks, as well as appropriate solid waste disposal. Support for SWM will be done jointly with hygiene promotion sessions for communities, and the development of operational plans (part of SO2). In line with SO2, NRC will provide essential WASH NFIs (in-kind and through CVA) as part of an emergency response and of SWM. NRC will also improve access to water and sanitation in collective shelters and as part of other relevant WASH interventions. NRC will contribute to SO4 by rehabilitating WASH facilities and services in schools and medical centers, as well as providing HP trainings for school staff and cleaning kits. The proposed activities are intended to have a positive impact on public health, and decrease instances of diseases such as cholera, acute-water diarrhea, leishmania, and others.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Limited gender modified and disaggregated sanitation facilities within formal/informal camps, and lack of sufficient lighting might result in safety risks, particularly for women and girls. MM: (a) Consultation with diverse representatives of affected population. (b) Increased provision/budget for gender modified and separated facilities. (c) Increased provision/budget for communal lighting gender separated facilities, access routes and other areas. (d) CFM in place. WASH activities might cause unintended impact on local markets (e.g. cost inflation) or contribute to 'war economies'. MM: (a) Conduct Systematic market monitoring to understand its dynamics and impact. (b) Choose Suppliers based on an extensive procurement process, overseen by Compliance Unit. Distributions exclude vulnerable populations in need of assistance due to inability to reach them. MM: (a) Independent registration and outreach, reducing reliance on gate-keepers; (b) CFM in place; (c) multiple days for distributions; (d) include questions related to exclusions in PDMs; (e) conduct messaging to those eligible for distributions ahead of the distribution dates. The risk of COVID-19 transmission during such activities should be mitigated through proper enforcement of NRC relevant guidance.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Potential SEA risks are mainly identified during the beneficiary selection process, the distribution of assistance and while accessing services. NRC communicates clearly with communities on the expected assistance modalities as well as confidential complain and feedback channels available to them and makes sure gender balanced teams register and deliver assistance to communities whenever possible. During post-delivery monitoring (e.g. PDMs), NRC inquires if people were asked to exchange anything for aid or service. In order to reduce perceptions of power, NRC asks in PDMs who, according to the recipients of aid, provided the assistance they received, enabling identification of possible areas of concern. All NRC staff and non-staff are bound by NRC's Code of Conduct, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and child safeguarding (CSG) policies. Several sessions take place with staff to raise awareness on PSEA and CSG, in addition to sessions for contractors. All NRC teams have to complete mandatory online PSEA and safeguarding essentials e-courses as part of their induction. NRC's Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is open and sensitive to SEA and CSG reports, which are managed by a dedicated team in Head Office with support from regional and country office teams who regularly updates guidance to emerging needs and trends to tailor management and support to cases.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Centralized distributions can create security risks for beneficiaries and staff. In-kind NFI distributions might lack appropriate items for women, girls, and boys and/or the quality/type. MM: Prioritize household-level distributions where feasible; Risk assessment and alternative distribution methods; Market-based approaches in place; Complaints and Feedback mechanisms in place. Lack of adequate understanding of Housing Land, and Property causes risk of eviction and lack of security of tenure. MM: Due-diligence carried out to ensure understanding of rights and responsibilities and strengthen security of tenure; Complaints and feedback mechanisms in place; community awareness Barriers to identification of targeted areas/beneficiaries constitute a risk of selection bias, unintended marginalization of neediest communities, and/or fueling tensions between neighboring communities. MM: Needs assessment include FGDs with community members; verify beneficiary list; Train staff on Core Humanitarian Standard and accountability; ensure targeting different groups. Limited options for adequate distribution sites and bureaucratic and administrative impediments contribute to compromising accessible, organized and tension-free distributions. MM: Feedback and complaint mechanism; Safe and Inclusive Programming, PSEA training to staff; learn from outcome monitoring findings; consider Cash and Voucher modalities in areas with functioning markets to improve dignified outcomes.
