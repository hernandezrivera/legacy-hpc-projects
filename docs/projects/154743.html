Project Summary

In 2019, ACTED plans to continue to respond to the emergency shelter and SNFI needs of vulnerable households affected by conflict across Iraq. ACTED will target IDPs living in formal camps and informal sites, returnees, and host communities. Intention surveys indicate that the majority of IDPs do not intend to return to their area of origin. Almost 500,000 IDPs continue to live in camps. Critical SNFI service provision gaps have been identified in camps, including the need to upgrade AFAD tents in identified camps in Dohuk and Kirkuk. Under this project, ACTED thus plans to replace up to 6,600 AFAD tents in identified camps in Dohuk and in Kirkuk.
ACTED aims to provide highly vulnerable returnees with assistance packages at their place of return. As the recent conflict in Iraq has left numerous shelters damaged, ACTED furthermore plans to support at least 300 individual shelter rehabilitation for returnee households through cash and /or conditional voucher assistance Finally, as discussed with the SNFI Cluster, ACTED plans to provide up to 1,500 Sealing Off Kits and 1,500 NFIs to meet emergency needs in out of camp settings. ACTED’s geographical coverage will include the Kirkuk, Diyala, and Ninewa governorates.

Needs

In 2019, up to 500,000 IDPs are expected to remain in camps with 62.5% of these not intending to return to their areas of origin within the next 12 months for a variety of reasons. In the Dohuk governorate, ‘home has been damaged/destroyed’ was cited by 31% of respondents in camps not intending to return to their areas of origin (REACH, “Intentions Survey, IDPs in Formal Camps – Dohuk governorate”, August 2018). In the Mosul emergency camps, this number increases to 38% (REACH, “Intentions Survey, IDPs in Formal Camps – Mosul Response”, August 2018). 1,500,000 IDPs remain out of camp, with 550,000 targeted by the 2019 HRP, and 116,000 vulnerable IDPs living in informal sites across the country. In informal sites, 49% of respondents in the Dohuk governorate cite ‘home has been damaged/destroyed’ as a reason informing their decision to not intend to return to their area of origin in the next 12 months (REACH, “Intentions Survey, IDPs in Informal Sites – Dohuk governorate”, August 2018). In informal sites in Ninewa, this number is as high as 76% (REACH, “Intentions Survey, IDPs in Informal Sites – Ninewa governorate”, August 2018), Other key cited barriers to returns vary from governorate to governorate and by areas of origin but key reasons cited include: Lack of livelihood opportunities; Lack of security forces; Fear of discrimination; and Fear/trauma associated with returning (REACH, “Intentions Survey, IDPs in Informal Sites and Formal Camps, August 2018).The process of camp closure and camp consolidation strategy for 2018 did not materialise on a large scale in particular in the Ninewa and Dohuk governorates. As such, care and maintenance of shelter and basic infrastructures remain a critical need as living conditions in many camps are deteriorating. Many shelters have reached and even surpassed their life span, and as such are not suitable anymore to adequately protect IDPs in camps against adverse weather conditions. In out of camps, critical shelter and seasonal support are the highest priorities.Although 4 million individuals have returned, many returnees are unable to support themselves in their areas of origin, and this has led to a trend of returnee families returning to camps. Needs of IDP and returnees thus remain high. The most vulnerable continue to fall below minimum living conditions and depend on humanitarian assistance to survive. In heavily war-impacted areas, people’s highest needs include shelter repairs, transitional shelter solutions and seasonal support. While safe shelter is essential to ensure protection and security, the majority of IDPs and returnees have exhausted their financial resources to be able to cover for shelter repairs and upgrading as a result of prolonged displacement. ACTED therefore aims to make access to safe shelter conditions a continuous priority in its programming in 2019. Activities under the proposed intervention will cover the provision of SNFIs, SOKs, individual shelter rehabilitation through conditional vouchers, cash for shelter, and the replacement of tents in camps to respond to the need for rehabilitated shelter of vulnerable conflict affected population groups cross Iraq.

Activities

ACTED’s 2019 SNFI strategy will contribute to the following objectives of the SNFI cluster: Cluster objective 1: Maintain adequate living conditions of vulnerable displaced people in camps through adequate shelter and non-food items support Activity 1: Replacement of tents in targeted camps in Dohuk and Kirkuk governorates ACTED intends to replace a total of 6,600 tents (AFAD tents) in targeted camps in Dohuk and Kirkuk governorates to maintain safe and adequate emergency shelter conditions. Due to the prolonged nature of IDPs displacement, many of the initially established tents have reached and surpassed their life span, and as such are not suitable anymore to adequately protect vulnerable families in camps against adverse weather conditions. ACTED will procure the tents through its rigid procurement processes, and deploy teams to Bersive 1, Shariya and Yahyawa IDPs camps to replace the tents, in coordination with the camp managers. The procurement of the tents will likely be from outside Iraq. ACTED will conduct awareness campaigns in the camps to ensure beneficiaries are informed of the changes. The existing tents will be disposed of appropriately. Wherever possible, ACTED will employ CfW for the awareness activities, replacement of tents and disposal of tents. Minor maintenance work of the bases could also be implemented as required. Cluster objective 2: Assist highly vulnerable people out of camp transitioning to durable solutions through the provision of adequate shelter and non-food items in priority areas of displacement and return. ACTED aims at supporting the humanitarian needs of highlyvulnerable IDPs living out of camps through: - Sealing off kit (SOKs) and NFI distributions (basic NFI and/or seasonal top-up) in kind or through cash-based interventions;- Seasonal assistance through winter kits distribution;- Individual shelter rehabilitation activities through conditional vouchers and cash-for shelter (CfS) programming. HLP concerns will be taken into account into all individual shelter rehabilitation activities in line with the shelter cluster and HLP sub-cluster guidance. CWG assessment tools will be used whenever possible. Market assessments will be undertaken to ensure the viability of use of vouchers. Activities will target Hawija (Kirkuk); Mosul, Sinjar, Telafar and Diyala. Activity 1: Distribution of SOKs and NFIs; ACTED aims at supporting conflict-affected HH (IDPs, host communities or returnees) by addressing urgent shelter assistance needs through: • the provision of up to 1,500 emergency SOKs; • the provision of up to 1,500 NFIs. ACTED will distribute 1,500 SOKs to vulnerable HH living in unfinished/damaged shelters, collective sites, and informal sites to allow the targeted HH to upgrade their shelter. ACTED will also distribute 1,500 basic NFI kits to vulnerable conflict-affected HH. These kits are equipped with core basic items needed to ensure minimum living conditions and are aligned with guidance provided by the SNFI cluster, alongside seasonal assistance for IDPs out-of-camps will also be provided by ACTED (500 winter kits will be distributed). A total of 9,000 individuals will be targeted across mixed population groups (IDPs, host communities and returnees). PDM surveys will be conducted after the distributions to measure the impact of the intervention and the satisfaction and use of the items provided. ACTED will also provide leaflets that will accompany the kit to facilitate its use and avoid improper and unsafe use of the items. Activity 2: Selection and rehabilitation of 300 damaged shelter; ACTED will rehabilitate 300 shelters which have been damaged during the conflict to improve living conditions of vulnerable HH from mixed population groups, including returnees. ACTED will target Ninewa Governorate, with a focus on the Sinjar, Tal Afar and/or Mosul districts. ACTED will target shelters falling under category 2 or 3 of the War Damage Assessments, in line with the Shelter Cluster guidance. ACTED will use either the conditional vouchers (commodity or value vouchers) modality or the CfS modality.For the voucher modality, once the shelters are selected, ACTED will conduct detailed assessments for each shelter and draft personalized BoQs to drive the rehabilitation works. Technical assessments will be undertaken by engineers. In addition, photos and any notes will be collected. Included in the BOQ will be the cost for labor to complete the rehabilitation. ACTED will use the CommCare software when conducting the technical assessments of the shelters and to generate personalized BoQs. For the CfS modality, ACTED will provide the total amount of cash necessary to repair each structure to reach minimum standards. ACTED will negotiate with local suppliers to guarantee a maximum price basket. HH will be informed about a fair price. An additional fee for supplier installation will be posted for families that may be particularly vulnerable.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1. Activity of ISIL sleeper cells in project areas. Risk 2. Access to the project area restricted by KRG and/or GoI. Risk 3. Contamination with Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and/or Unexploded Ordnance (UXO). Risk 4. Target population hostile towards INGOs. Risk 5: Shipment of humanitarian assistance hindered by military forces or government authorities. Risk 6: IS resorts to use of chemical weapons. Risk 7: Inexpert construction may expose beneficiaries to unsafe structures or lead to the improper installation of elements like electrical materials and water boilers which could pose a hazard to beneficiary families.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation 1: ACTED will maintain close coordination and communication with relevant security forces and community leaders (Mukhtars) in the project areas and will continuously monitor security situation. Mitigation 2: ACTED already has access permissions for some of the project areas and maintain coordination with relevant governmental offices (BRHA, Asayish) ensuring that necessary permission are obtained. Shall the access permissions be withdrawn or refused, partners might consider distribution of assistance through relevant traditional community structures, taking into account feasibility. Mitigation 3: ACTED will ensure that all staff are trained on Mine Risk Education and will closely follow on reports of ERW/UXO incidents to get better understanding which areas are mostly contaminated. Same knowledge will be obtained from host communities who usually have good understanding which areas are safe and which are not. Mitigation 4: ACTED will ensure that their operations are culturally sensitiveand will involve traditional leaders to ensure that role of INGO is well understood and accepted by the community. ACTED will also work closely with the CCCM Cluster/Sub-Clusters and the coordination structures in the camps in order to collaborate with all the IDPs committees in camps. Mitigation 5: ACTED will pro-actively follow necessary procedures and processes and maintain close coordination with relevant stakeholders - Asayish, CCCM, SNFI, WASH and Protection Clusters, military actors - to ensure that necessary permissions are obtained on time. Mitigation 6: Staff will be trained on Chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear (CBRNE) defence, provision of protective equipment to field staff will be considered. Mitigation 7: ACTED employs skilled structural engineers to provide technical assessment and information to beneficiary HHs, and designs conditional and cash programs in a way that incentivize or require beneficiaries to use skilled local laborers, rather than doing their own work to save money. ACTED will cooperate with the WASH Cluster/Sub-Clusters in order to coordinate with all WASH infrastructures activities in and out-of-camps in the targeted location.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Ensuring accountability to affected populations is at the core of ACTED’s programming. ACTED’s approach is aligned with the IASC’s Five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations: (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. ACTED's firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For this reason, a strong Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) as well as continuous communications with stakeholders, including local leaders and government authorities as well as beneficiaries are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict. In order to uphold transparency and avoid increasing social tensions in communities where distributions will occur, the criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be communicated in advance, publicly, and ACTED will liaise with local authorities to minimize security concerns. Adopting widely accepted vulnerability criteria used by other international humanitarian actors will also reduce the potential for conflict arising from the use of different methodologies for beneficiaries’ selection. As part of its on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED has established a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) covering each project for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive a response in a timely manner. The CRM also contributes to identifying potential negative effects of activities in order to address them in a timely manner and improve ongoing and future projects. In addition to the referral of non-sensitive complaints (sensitivity levels 1 to 3) to the respective project management team, anonymised complaints reports are shared internally on a monthly basis to provide an aggregate view of the types of complaints received and inform future planning. A range of communication modalities will be made available (as relevant) to targeted communities to ensure the mechanism is easily accessible, taking into account the specificities of the local context and culture, as well as project resources and beneficiary vulnerabilities and preferences. While the most commonly used modalities are complaints desks, a dedicated phone line and direct engagement with ACTED staff in the field (including a dedicated Accountability Officer), other potential modalities include complaints boxes, a dedicated email address and a WhatsApp number. Multiple communication means ensure that vulnerable people – especially those with limited mobility such as the elderly and people with disabilities – are able to provide feedback, while guaranteeing total confidentiality of information shared. ACTED ensures that project design is informed by previous experiences, including previous evaluations, and through qualitative data collected from targeted communities, prior to the project.
