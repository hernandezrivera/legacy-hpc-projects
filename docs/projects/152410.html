Project Summary

This proposed project will address short to mid-term emergency shelter and non-food items needs of conflict affected vulnerable populations both displaced and in secondary displacement. The project will target 2000 HHs or 12,000 individuals both IDPs and host communities, disaggregated as follows (Men 2,403, Women 3,531, Boys 2,863 and Girls 3,203) for emergency shelter and NFIs activities response in Lakes (Yirol East & Yirol West) most vulnerable populations in need (after an assessment). The target population comprises of displaced persons in need of shelter and NFI for their sustenance. Yirol East and Yirol West have also been categorised under Catastrophe IPC 5, making the communities here more susceptible to famine. This is worse especially for newly displaced and vulnerable populations in secondary displacement. The project will aim to meet the needs of the vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community who become vulnerable as a result of conflict and continuous displacement as a result of conflict, food insecurity or due to economic deterioration.
Though there are 28574 vulnerable displaced persons (4618 HHs) in Yirol, CCOSS has previously through funding from RRF responded to 3000 HHs. There still exists a bigger gap of 1618 HHs that requires S/NFIs response (Men: 2047, Women: 2741, Boys: 2397, Girl: 2523 and total 9,708 individuals). In line with Do¬-No--Harm principle which is also supported by the cluster’s position, CCOSS will also include host community, (local populations) as beneficiaries, whenever applicable, as they are also sharing burden of displacement by giving their already meagre resources to their displaced relatives. The breakdown for host communities includes the following 383 HHs – 2,292 individuals disaggregated as follows (Men 356, Women 790, Boys 466 & Girls 680).
Prior to project implementation, CCOSS will conduct, focus group discussions (FGD) with women, men, boys and girls at targeted community settlements will be conducted to get feedback on design, implementation, ensuring safety and mitigation of Protection / GBV risks, especially for females. An assessment and verification exercise will be conducted to establish the exact emergency shelter and lifesavings non-food items needs requirements of the targeted population. This will be followed by registration of the targeted beneficiaries and subsequent distribution of S/NFIs. However it should be noted that the composition of the people in need may fluctuate according to the changing security situation. CCOSS will do its best to adjust actions in the most possible way, striving to adhere to SPHERE standards for the populations in needs for the duration of the project period. CCOSS will also ensure that there is proper coordination especially in locations where there is more than one implementing partner. County cluster meetings will be held weekly to ensure proper coordination, to avoid double counting and as well reduce duplication of efforts. CCOSS is a key member of other clusters working within the same locality. CCOSS will therefore ensure that there is synergy between S/NFIs Cluster, WASH and Food Security. This is particularly to ensure there is no duplication for WASH NFIs and S/NFIs being distributed, but also ensure the FSL compliments the work of the other clusters. For instance, during the first distribution, the IDPs sold some of the plastic sheets for food in market. CCOSS responded by requesting WFP to scale up its operations in the area.
General Operation & other Direct costs include costs associated with providing direct management & other support functions at the Main office in Juba. This includes management staff salaries, communication, stationary, rents etc. The Indirect project costs include bank charges, staff recruitment & servicing costs, auditing and consultancy costs.

Needs

According to the National Bureau of Statistics, Yirol East and Yirol West have been categorized as some of the locations in the country in Catastrophe (IPC 5). Lakes State is known for inter-communal fighting since for a very long time. In the last three years there has been communal conflict between Pakam and Ruup sub clan of Agaar. That conflict resulted into displacement of members of Ruup community into Cueibet and Wulu. The disarmament process initiated by the government led to forced displacement of the resident communities since their houses were burnt down and completely destroyed. Not everyone was able to salvage their property, while majority of those displaced left almost everything they owned at home while running for safety. The Inter Agency Assessment for Yirol East and Yirol West established that there were many displaced people from the Agar-Pakam community from Rumbek North to Yirol. Currently, there are 28574 individuals (4618 HHs) HHs of vulnerable people who were displaced to Yirol East and Yirol West. However, those verified by the assessment are as follows; • Total of 3,561 household heads were registered where 2,857 (80%) are female headed households and 704 (20%) are male headed. • A total of 28,574 individuals is recorded where 22,745 individuals are from female headed household and 5,829 from male headed household. The Inter-Agency Assessment made the following recommendations; • Shelter and protection intervention • Provision full package of NFI (cooking, kitchen, bedding, hygiene and fuel) in-kind recommended • Safe drinking water and sanitation intervention • Distribution of emergency shelter (tent and shelter package) for overcrowded families.CCOSS through RRF responded to just 3000 vulnerable households through WASH and S/NFIs response by distributing blankets, plastic sheets, mosquito nets and sleeping mats. However, the needs are more overwhelming arising from the fact that, about 90% of these displaced people lost all their household items through fire when their houses were burnt and lost their income sources and assets during the displacement. The gaps that exists include about 1618 HHs that did not benefit from any response, as well as the need for Kitchen sets (was raised in the PDM as a need), and need to provide local shelter materials such as bamboo and poles to all the IDPs. In line with Do-No-Harm principle which is also supported by the cluster’s position, CCOSS will also include host community, (local populations) as beneficiaries, for shelter upgrades. Most of the host communities’ shelters require upgrades as they are currently in a dilapidated state. A total of 383 HHs – 2,292 individuals disaggregated as follows (Men 356, Women 790, Boys 466 & Girls 680). Due to overwhelming need for emergency shelter and lifesaving non-food items, there is need to replenish the S/NFIs for majority of the IDPs within the camps. The needs are directly related to new and protracted displacement caused by conflict and the compounded effects of food insecurity and lack of livelihoods. CCOSS will focus its interventions on communities in crisis and emergency in Lakes (Yirol East and Yirol West). The beneficiaries will be populations in need and will be targeted using the cluster targeting criteria: • Households with children under 5; • Pregnant or lactating women (PLW); • Unaccompanied elderly (>60 years); • Persons living with disability (PLWD); • Female headed households; • Child headed households and/or unaccompanied minors; • Chronically ill people; • Persons with no effective community linkages. CCOSS will use participatory approaches to identify the needs of women, men, boys and girls. In this proposed project, barriers to participation of women and other vulnerable groups will be discussed and taken into account in both assessment and implementation stages. Gender and age considerations will be included in needs assessments prior to implementation.

Activities

1) Provide assistance with emergency shelter (in-kind) to 9,708 IDPs (Men: 2047, Women: 2741, Boys: 2397, Girl: 2523) who have not received any form of response. The emergency shelter will include bamboo, poles and plastic sheets. The needs will be identified after conducting a needs and verification assessment. This activity will protect displaced persons from harsh weather conditions and protection risks as well as enhancing access to other basic services 2) Provide assistance with live saving NFIs (in-kind) to 9,708 IDPs (Men: 2047, Women: 2741, Boys: 2397, Girl: 2523) who have not received any form of response. The lifesaving NFIs will include blankets, mosquito nets, sleeping mats and kitchen sets – as identified during the PDM as a need. The needs will be identified after conducting a needs and verification assessment. This activity will contribute to better health of the IDPs, enhance nutrition, protection and better hygiene 3) Shelter upgrades (in kind) to 2,292 host community members (men 356, women 790 boys 466 and girls 680) whose shelter requires upgrade. The shelter upgrade materials will include bamboos, poles and plastic sheets. A need and verification assessment will be conducted first to ensure proper targeting. This activity will help improve the living conditions, safety and dignity of the host communities. 4) Engage the community in a way that stimulates active participation. This will be through focus group discussions, formation of committees to help in distribution and in decision making as to when the project distribution will take place including the venues. The committees will comprise of 7 women and 5 men. This will enhance the community’s dignity while increasing their ability to respond to new shock and build on existing skills. 5) Monitoring of quality service provision – PDM will be conducted two weeks after the distribution is finalized. The format for the PDM will include conducting focus group discussions, random sampling for interviews. This activity will ensure fast, lifesaving response to the right people, enhances ability to use S/NFIs for supporting results bases health, protection, livelihood and hygiene needs and reducing overlap to reach the truly vulnerable. 6) Mainstream HLP and conflict sensitive approaches throughout all stages of S/NFI response through conducting conflict analysis, avoiding manipulation of distribution of S/NFIs by remaining neutral and practising “do-no-harm” principle. This activity will reduce harm to communities and build resilience by strengthening existing systems and community dynamics. 7) Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response – this will be done through ensuring that distributions are done in an open space, during the day, and close to the camps. This will ensure gender and protection risks are addressed directly, mitigating the chances of exacerbated vulnerabilities 8) Share HIV/AIDS prevention messaging – IDPs and Host community members will be introduced to issues of HIV/AIDS such as stigma, infections, prevention etc through public education and promotional materials. This will ensure gender, protection and health risks are addressed directly, mitigating chances of exacerbated vulnerabilities

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CCOSS will ensure that the messaging is context-specific by being sensitive to the needs of the displaced persons or those in secondary displacement. For instance, there will be different sessions with different groups such as women only forums held by our female staff to identify specific needs for women and girls.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CCOSS has a code of conduct that all staff members have subscribed to that details measures against sexual exploitation and abuse of the project beneficiaries. The code of conduct is therefore enforceable and is in place. Staff members are aware that they should not in any way be involved in any acts of sexual exploitation and abuse, which includes withholding assistance or giving preferential treatment, recruiting beneficiaries to work in a personal capacity, demands or requests for payment or benefit, intimidating, humiliating, degrading or aggressive behaviour towards beneficiaries, sexual relations with beneficiaries and sexual relations with children among others

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CCOSS has been working in this area for five years, and has been interacting and consulting with communities in many ways. CCOSS has established a working relationship with the local authorities in Yirol East and Yirol West, with whom project activities in the area must be reported to. CCOSS also works closely with the community leaders, and informs them of project plans throughout the implementation phase. For effective feedback, CCOSS works interacts and receives feedback from the community members directly through project planning and through feed-back mechanisms. Our staff regularly interacts and consults with the community members on a day to day basis. Prior to commencement of emergency shelter and lifesaving non-food items distributions, community participation in the planning and implementation of this intervention will be sought . CCOSS will ensure its programme delivery according to CHF and cluster guidelines. CCOSS always includes beneficiaries in project development through different surveys (with segregated data) and focused group discussions. CCOSS will have regular meetings with all stakeholders to share programme related information and also to respond to queries and complaints raised by beneficiaries.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Complaint /feedback mechanism will be agreed upon and established prior to commencement of project. Monitoring of the complaints and remedial action will be documented during the project implementation cycle. This will be done with the goal of promoting community engagement and accountability. The community participation in the planning implementation and monitoring of the post distribution will also be solicited. For instance, the community will be involved in the identification and selection of community volunteers to help in distribution among others After the completion of distribution, a monitoring team will be sent to visit selected samples of beneficiaries to confirm the actual quantity received and the quality of the relief commodities, as well as to collect further feedback from beneficiaries. Once complaints are received, CCOSS will look for the most appropriate way to respond to the complaints

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CCOSS operates in all areas based on need regardless of the controlling authority. In Yirol East and Yirol West, all areas are under the control of the government, and thus somewhat accessible. There are therefore no major security concerns that can create accessibility impediments. As a National NGO with static presence in Yirol East and Yirol West, CCOSS will continue to operate in the region even in times of conflict because of the goodwill the organization has established with the communities.
