Project Summary

This project is designed to link NPA’s life-saving emergency food assistance (General Food Distribution and Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program) with early recovery livelihoods activities and compliment in-kind food assistance to meet both the life-saving emergency assistance needs as well as early recovery and protection of livelihoods of conflict affected populations in Canal/Pigi & Fangak Counties.
As per August 2019 IPC report, Canal/Pigi and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) respectively while both locations persist IPC AMN 4 for Acute Malnutrition . Former Jonglei State in general has the highest number of people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase4) acute food insecurity with an estimated 1.25 million people, representing 64.8 percent of the state population.
NPA’s PDM report conducted in three locations of Canal/Pigi and Fangak Counties (Atar 3, New Fangak and Kurwai) revealed that respondents expressed their dissatisfaction over the quantity of the food items being not enough and untimely to satisfy the food need of the family due to sharing of food assistance with relatives, extended family sizes and returnees who have come from Sudan sharing their rations.
In this project, NPA is proposing to distribute combined vegetable, crop and fishing kits to 9,750 HHs (58,500 people) in Canal/Pigi County and 15,615 HHs (93,690 people) in Fangak County as per the HRP 2020 FSL targets. This will be followed by provision of training on improved agronomic practices as well as fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics to 5,000 farmers/fishermen (2,000 Canal & 3,000 Fangak). The trained farmers/fishermen will be expected to pass on their knowledge to the remaining farmers/fishermen. The knowledge gained from these trainings will be of importance in building the community resilience to future shocks. These activities are aimed at improving production of vegetables, crop and fish hence contributing towards reducing malnutrition rates. The vegetable, crop and fish products will go a long way in restoring the nutritional status of the affected population, through increased food consumption and better dietary diversity score.
Beneficiaries of this project are household members who are extremely food insecure individuals classified in IPC 4 and IPC 3. NPA will follow a community-based approach by setting up a “Beneficiary targeting committee”, whereby the community take up the role in the selection of eligible households, particularly internally displaced households and returnees, while also including vulnerable members of host communities. A vulnerability selection criteria will be used where communities also participate in discussing and endorsing the beneficiary selection criteria.
Community will be the prime movers of the implementation process through their continuous engagement in the project. In addition to being responsible for beneficiaries’ targeting, they will take part in periodic community meetings, Project Management Committees and Post Distribution Monitoring assessments, where NPA will ensure that the voices and concerns of all groups are captured. Further, to promote accountability, NPA will continue using the community based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide platforms for beneficiaries' active involvement in the project and avenues to voice any issues or concerns.
NPA has worked in South Sudan since 1986, and currently runs three programmes: Emergency Response Food Security Response, Rural Development and Partnership with Civil Society. NPA has extensive experience in majority of the Country in the area of Food Security and Livelihoods and is endowed with excellent reputation, robust relationship with local authorities & communities in South Sudan.

1 - Needs

The South Sudan IPC Key messages of August 2019 highlighted that the food security situation slightly improved during the 2019 lean season compared to the 2018 lean season . This improvement has been attributed to the reduced conflict, and improved access to livelihoods, markets and humanitarian assistance in most areas of South Sudan. It is however, important to note that despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement in South Sudan in 2018, the food and nutrition security situation remain dire in South Sudan’s Jonglei state where there is the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, pegged at 1.25 million people. Canal/Pigi and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) respectively while both locations persist IPC AMN 4 for Acute Malnutrition.Food insecurity in Canal/Pigi and Fangak Counties of Jonglei has sharply deteriorated since the beginning of the conflict as most people fled from their villages to areas in other parts of Jonglei where they felt were safer. This movement seriously affected their livelihood strategies of subsistence farming. This was further worsened by the structural deficiencies such as poverty and limited access to productive assets. South Sudan is ranked 189 out of 189 countries on the UN Gender Development Index (GDI) in 2018 and has one of the highest maternal mortality rates (2,054 deaths per 100,000 births) in the world. Inadequate food and poor nutrition make pregnant women more vulnerable to complications. Ongoing revenge killings, general insecurity and the humanitarian crisis in Canal/Pigi and Fangak Counties places women in a precarious situation. Canal and Fangak Counties are largely characterized by interferences from local authorities, harassment of humanitarian staff, divergence of assistance and increased bureaucratic impediments in the delivery of assistance. Beneficiaries of food assistance in Canal and Fangak have highlighted that food taxation by local authorities is very common, however, beneficiaries are always afraid of reporting this act to NPA and WFP for fear of victimization or arrest. NPA has been doing every effort to stop or minimize food taxation. According to a SMART Survey conducted in Canal/Pigi and Fangak in April 2019, the Counties have has a GAM (WHZ) prevalence of 24.3%, indicative of Critical (Phase 4 on the IPC for Acute Malnutrition scale), that require urgent and significant scale-up and intensification of treatment as well as humanitarian food assistance to address food consumption gaps. In September 2019, NPA conducted a post distribution monitoring (PDM) in three locations of Canal/Pigi and Fangak Counties for WFP in-kind food assistance. Among the major findings of the PDM, the majority of the respondents were satisfied with the food items and the quality but expressed their dissatisfaction over the quantity. Factors including sharing of food assistance with relatives, extended family sizes in the area and returnees who have come from Sudan sharing their rations have contributed to the level of dissatisfaction over the quantity of in-kind food assistance. As per NPA's PDM report from previous interventions supported by the Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, providing early recovery agricultural inputs enabled communities to get access to food in a shorter period thereby meeting their immediate food needs. The PDM has further confirmed that beneficiaries have managed to compliment food assistance they receive with fish and vegetables, thereby adding variety to their food.

2 - Activities

1. Project launching: NPA will conduct Grants opening meeting with project staff, local authorities and community representatives to discuss the project objectives, strategies implementation plan, distribution plan, community participation and source of funding.2. Community mobilization and Beneficiaries’ targeting: NPA will conduct regular meetings with local authorities, key stakeholders and communities in these locations for mobilization. NPA adopts participatory, vulnerability & rights based approaches in identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries through beneficiary targeting committees (BTC) comprised of men and women from various villages who will be elected by the wider community. The BTC committee will be tasked to identify and register the beneficiaries according to NPA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, child headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS, Survivors of GBV etc. This enables the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. NPA will furthermore strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria, and solicit feedback from community members.3. Distribution of complementary livelihoods inputs: NPA will deliver and distribute livelihood kits from the cluster pipeline in targeted locations. Accordingly, NPA will distribute vegetable kits & tools (Amaranth, Collard, Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda), Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum and Cowpea) and Fishing kits (hooks, monofilament and spools of twine) to 9,750 HHs in Canal/Pigi and 15,615 HHs in Fangak Counties. 55% of the target will be Women headed households and NPA will ensure gender and protection are mainstreamed throughout the project activities. These activities are aimed at improving production of vegetables, crop and fish hence contributing towards reducing malnutrition rates. The vegetable, crop and fish products will go a long way in restoring the nutritional status of the affected population, through increased food consumption and widened dietary diversity options.4. Formation of farmers/fisher folks groups and conducting trainings: NPA will encourage the formation of farmer groups and fisher forks group so as to encourage working together and encourage peer to per exchange. 5,000 farmers/fishermen (Canal 2,000, Fangak 3,000) will be trained on improved agronomic practices as well as fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics to. The trained farmers/fishermen will be expected to pass on their knowledge to the remaining beneficiaries. The knowledge gained from these trainings will be of importance in building the community resilience to future shocks.5. Formation and training of Project Management Committees (PMCs). NPA will facilitate and encourage the formation of project management committees which will help in the implementation of the project. Five PMCs (Canal 2, Fangak 3) each comprising of 20 members (10 women, 10 men) totaling 100 members PMCs will be trained and empowered to raise concerns of their communities to NPA and key actors in each area. 6. Monitoring and reporting. NPA will ensure that the required reports are submitted in time. NPA will also conduct post distribution monitoring, distribution process monitoring and end-line assessment.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, it is linked to community systems and will have beneficiary targeting committees, project management committees, promoter farmers and fishers. NPA will focus on strengthening local capacity to rehabilitate and protect livelihoods. Local communities have to play a prominent role in their own recovery process, therefore, conflict affected communities are at the center of this project’s local institution foundation. While NPA will continue to partner with affected communities to provide immediate humanitarian assistance there is also the recognition that rebuilding livelihoods is a critical component for conflict-affected families as they restart the recovery process. The project will also use promoter farmers and fishers to build a cadre of early adopters who will act as community owned resource persons and will be able to continue demonstrating improved agriculture and fishing practices to other farmers with or without support from outside.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with the national objectives such as the “Resilience Program Framework” of the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs & Disaster Management and the “Agriculture sector development policy and implementation framework” under the ”South Sudan Comprehensive Agriculture Master Plan”.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the environment is sufficiently safe to link the project to longer term durable solutions. NPA has plans to link the project with its Recovery, DRR, conflict transformation through Livelihoods, building resilience and other rural development projects funded the Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (NMFA) and the Norwegian Agency for Development Cooperation (NORAD). NPA will make every effort to build on existing community capacities to ensure that they do not completely become dependent on external assistance to meet their food needs. The decision to implement early recovery interventions to augment humanitarian response is a way of ensuring that local coping capacities are strengthened and not completely eroded.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, capacity building is part of the project activities to help beneficiaries to continue similar activities after the project completion. Beneficiaries will be able to reserve seeds from their harvest for the next season planting. The fish kits will serve the beneficiaries for long enough to sustain their food consumption. The project also forms and trains project management committees (PMC) who will be mobilizing and helping the community in similar engagements.This project has embedded a graduation strategy in its design. There will be synergies between the emergency department with the livelihood department for longer term planning on food security and livelihoods. Transitioning communities from free input distribution to seed and voucher fairs is also a transition strategy as it rebuilds the local seed system, leaving it to work without external assistance.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

NPA will do a risk analysis and conflict sensitivity analysis of the project. It will identify the different groups in the project and map out how assisting each group will have an impact on the other groups. The project will be designed in a way so as to avoid conflicts between the host communities and internally displace people. Every effort will be made to ensure that beneficiary selection is transparent and that errors of inclusion or exclusion are minimal.The project takes into account all the inequalities and all the protection contains. NPA is placing a Gender and Protection advisor beginning of 2020 who will be involved in supporting the project with regards to protection issues. NPA will have trainings on gender and protection with the staff and selected project management committees. The help desk during field will also be put in place to ensure that beneficiaries get the right information and are also able to register their complaints. NPA will also put suggestion boxes in the field offices as part of the complaints and feedback mechanisms.The monitoring will measure the access to beneficiaries particularly the distribution and registration center. NPA will look at the distance and ethnic differences so as to ensure everyone is included in the process. As part of accessibility, NPA will try to understand if there were any obstacles to receiving assistance including issues like bribery and sexual exploitation.The project will ensure and map out the distance travel by beneficiaries to the distribution and registration sites. They will be set in such a way that beneficiaries do not have to travel for long distances in order to receive assistance. Safety will be measured from the individual’s sense of security while receiving the humanitarian assistance. The beneficiary should not be afraid to receive assistance.The project ensures that all the people are treated with dignity and respect despite the circumstances that they are in.
