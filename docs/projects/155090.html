Objectives

"SO 1: Ensure effective, sufficient and equitable provision of life saving water and sanitation services for targeted men, women, boys and girls.SO 2: Ensure that all targeted women, men, girls and boys have the means and are encouraged to adopt individual and collective measures increasing health seeking behaviors to mitigate public health risks for the well-being of the affected population.SO 3: Ensure that all WASH assistance promotes protection, safety and dignity of targeted men, women, boys and girls with focus on sustainable resilience-building approaches."

Needs

Under the leadership and guidance of Department of Public Health Engineering (DPHE), the WASH Sector has conducted a needs analysis and developped a strategy. In light of the lessons learnt in past year, more durable and resilient facilities are the main focus for ensuring the protection and dignity of affected populations, particularly girls and women together with the lifesaving WASH assistance. Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD) is a key concern for the Sector as well as the high rate of malnutrition. Access to safe drinking water, living areas free of open defecation as well as handwashing with soap at critical times will reduce such risks. To ensure equitable access to safe water including quality, the Sector will focus on scaling up the context specific surface or ground water-based services, as 20% of the targeted men, women and children do not have access to a safe water. Despite the continuous efforts to maintain the water points, 73% hand pumps are functional on the ground and rapid maintenance remains challenging, which indicates to scale up the multiple water supply solution focusing on sustainable pipeline network-based solution. The critical water shortage in Teknaf Upazila during dry season requires a specific intervention to reserve surface water during a specific period of the year. The water quality test result shows that 50% of water samples at source and 89% of samples at household level were contaminated. An increase in regular water quality monitoring at the household level and promotion of household water treatment will require increased investment in community engagement, outreach and hygiene promotion. Access to sanitation and solid and liquid waste management: There is still an insufficient amount of appropriate and dignified sustainable sanitation facilities together with the lifesaving WASH assistance over thelve months into the response. A combined 53% of households continue to have access challenges including distance, overcrowding, location and overflowing. 49% of girls and 40% of women reported feeling unsafe using latrine facilities, 40% and 34% using bathing facilities. Women and girls are adopting coping mechanisms such as bathing in their shelters and not using latrines at night. About one third of the population dispose of solid waste in an indiscriminate manner and waste often ends up in the open drains, resulting in blockages/flooding. Hygiene and community engagement: The sector is in need of strong understanding of barriers and motivating factors for behaviors that contribute to public health risks for households and individuals. A review of existing data regarding knowledge, practices and perceptions is required, alongside capacity building for WASH actors in recognizing barriers to effective hygiene practices. The mid-term review highlighted that coverage of key hygiene items remains sub-optimal, however, the size and scale of the response is a limiting factor for many agencies to provide regular distributions on a massive scale. A review of preferences and remaining gaps of items required, and a market assessment of these items is required to support market-based approaches, including cash and vouchers, allowing for greater choice and accessibility to key hygiene items. Significant support is required for WASH sector actors to improve capacity in areas such as community engagement and participation, accountability to affected populations and gender mainstreaming.
