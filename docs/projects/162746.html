Project Summary

This project aims to provide humanitarian WASH assistance to 175,00 beneﬁciaries (57,750 women, 43,750 men, 36,750 girls, 36,750 boys - 12% of whom are estimated to be either elderly or disabled) to mitigate the suffering of populations affected by natural or manmade disasters and reﬂects all 3 outcomes of the 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan. Under Objective 1, IOM will provide life-saving assistance to people newly displaced by conﬂict or natural disasters in an effort to recreate pre-emergency conditions using early recovery approaches to improve the overall access to WASH services, connecting the need for short term emergency interventions with long term solutions and applying development principles by working directly with local communities to facilitate ownership and accountability. The objective is to not only focus on WASH emergency interventions but gradually contribute to socio economic inclusion of forcibly displaced populations and returnees by directly increasing access to basic services for the displaced and host communities and indirectly increasing employment opportunities. Under Objective 2, IOM seeks to improve and sustain access to safe water in target communities, including returnees, especially in Blue Nile, East Sudan, Abyei, North Darfur and South Darfur as these communities primarily rely on agriculture and livestock, therefore water is critical to their livelihoods. Under Outcome 3, improving and sustaining access to safe water and sanitation facilities and hygiene messages will address malnutrition related risks and increase community resilience to disease outbreaks. IOM will consider emergency water provision, access to proper sanitation facilities and hygiene campaigns; coupled with the establishment and rehabilitation of water points. This will include environmental impact mitigation such as the provision of solar technology to power water points and monitoring ground water to moderate depletion levels. Exit strategies will be developed to alleviate aid dependency in protracted emergencies by strengthening self-reliance and facilitating community solutions in a more holistic approach.

1 - Needs

The needs assessed for the proposed interventions are based on the gaps observed in 2019, especially tailored to female headed households as 80% of IDPs camps are made up of women and children. Women, children and the elderly remain the most vulnerable groups in need of assistance and protection. In locations where easy access to safe water is lacking vulnerable communities resolve to collect water from unimproved water sources, increasing the risks of water borne disease outbreaks and exacerbating malnutrition rates among children. IOM currently provides assistance in South Darfur and is planning to continue its interventions where there are a total of 204,765 people that have very limited access to safe water, sanitation facilities and in need of targeted hygiene and garbage collection campaigns. Voluntary returns from IDP camps to places of origin are also reported, a trend that is expected to increase in 2020 and WASH support will be required to support residents and returnees for their sustainable reintegration and the promotion of durable solutions. In North Darfur, IDP camps, such as Rwanda and Debenaira Camps, still have vast gaps in sanitation and hygiene, whilst water quality levels and availability need to be monitored regularly for the prevention of water borne diseases. Every year, IDP camps are also heavily affected by the floods, therefore, needs for critical WASH services will increase during the rainy season. IOM also proposes to provide life-saving assistance to vulnerable residents IDPs living among host communities in localities and villages around Tawilla in order to address the vast gaps in water, sanitation and hygiene. Low level of awareness on proper hygiene and sanitation, the lack of food and potable water, and the lack of access to basic services severely affects many IDPs and residents living in North Darfur. In East, West and Central Darfur, there is a need to continue addressing WASH gaps for IDPs, returnees and host communities who have limited to no access to WASH services. In South and West Kordofan there are at least ﬁve locations that require increased access to potable water ,sanitation facilities and hygiene campaigns for returnees, IDPs and host communities. In the two Kordofan States, high number of IDPs and refugees still reside with hosting communities, putting further pressure on the already limited resources. As the population increases additional water sources are needed to support the growing population, open defecation is widely practiced and there is a dire need for hygiene and garbage collection campaigns. The chronic underdevelopment of the overall eastern Sudan region has contributed to the scarcity of water resources and lack of infrastructure severely affecting communities’ livelihoods in the area. In Blue Nile, vulnerable returnees from Ethiopia along the main border areas continue to face challenges when returning to their areas of origin as they have very limited access to basic services, especially WASH services. . Access to safe water and other basic services remain among the lowest in the country; Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates are above the 15% international emergency threshold levels. The lack of access to basic services and the deterioration of two essential water treatment plants in the locations have contributed to the current Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) crisis.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

New water sources will only be constructed after a geophysical study is conducted and ground water levels are monitored. IOM will also work closely with the WASH Sector and the targeted communities to ensure that Integrated Water Resource Management Systems (IWRM) are established. Hygiene and garbage collection campaigns will also be designed in a way to encourage land preservation through proper / responsible garbage disposal methods and systems. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development and population growth have made water resources extremely important to Sudan’s economic development and social cohesion; IOM will focus on ﬁnding alternatives from fuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, able to scaled, environmentally sustainable and climate smart. Experience has shown that solar powered systems reduce running costs by two thirds unlike motorized pumps, which are also noisy, polluting (emitting greenhouse gases which contribute to climate change) and are highly susceptible to ﬂuctuating fuel prices and availability. Solar powered water sources will be prioritized where possible.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Under this project, consultative participatory approaches employed will allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identiﬁcation of priority needs based on gender and vulnerability, especially for the distribution of the Menstrual Hygiene Management kits. This inclusive participatory approach assured the inclusion of ideas from vulnerable women and girls, and people with disabilities, who are often excluded from community decisions or under represented during decision making processes, especially with regard to selecting the location for the construction/ re- construction of the latrines and rehabilitation of water points with the use of the spare parts to ensure that their needs are not only addressed but protection risks are addressed and mitigated.
