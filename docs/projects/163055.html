Project Summary

Zimbabwe has been experiencing a drought phenomenon due to climate change, which has negatively affected the provision of safe water and sanitation services thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases among the vulnerable communities. The proposed intervention seeks to ensure access to quality, clean and potable water for women, men , girls and boys among the affected populations. The rural and urban ZimVAC Reports (2019) show that more people are becoming increasingly vulnerable to these changes. Access to safe water and sanitation remains a major challenge, both in rural and urban areas of Zimbabwe. There has been a general decline in investment in the provision of water and sanitation services. The water and sanitation supply services situation in the country face an array of challenges ranging from capacity to provide, behaviours of users and lack of investment in the sector by the local authorities due the harsh economic crisis that the country is going through.
The Overall Objective of the project is to ensure that people affected by humanitarian situations have access to safe water , a means to practice good hygiene and improve health and hygiene practices.
The proposed intervention seeks to address some of the water and sanitation challenges through increased access to water for the affected populations of the 4 target districts of Manicaland Province, through rehabilitation of water supply points and drilling new boreholes. The project aims to reach out to 185,937 people (96,687 females; 89,250 males) with clean, safe and potable water, sanitation and health and hygiene promotion messages. The populations are further dis-aggregated by district Mutare rural 74,397, ; Chipinge Rural 84,464; Chipinge urban 8,724 and Chimanimani 18,352 people. According the urban ZimVac report (2019) Manicaland Province had the largest proportion of households using water from unimproved sources. The use of water from unimproved sources predisposes people to water borne infections. The activities would include rehabilitation of community piped water schemes and where possible incorporate renewable energy like solar. Buhera-Chipinge-Chimanimani which are part of our target districts had the largest proportion (20.1%) of households who relied on unimproved sources as their main water sources (ZimVAC, 2019). The project also seeks to support the provision of sanitation services for the affected population in the target areas through the construction of temporary latrines and promoting better access to sanitation services.
Hygiene promotion is one of the key components of this proposed project mainly targeting to work with community health and school health clubs as the major vehicles to reach out to the target populations with health and hygiene messages. School health clubs will be supported to reach out to children in schools with hygiene messages and community health clubs will be supported in the target communities. Menstrual Hygiene Management will also be a key component of hygiene promotion in schools targeting adolescent girls, boys and the community.
Specific Objectives:
1. Restore access to sufficient water of appropriate quality and quantity to fulfill basic needs.
2. Improve awareness of safe hygiene and sanitation practices, with a focus on participatory health and
hygiene education and water conservation.
3. Provide access to critical WASH related Hygiene kits, with a focus on the most vulnerable families in
the targeted areas including caregivers of MAM/SAM children.
Mercy Corps recognizes that women and girls face challenges in the society given their limited capacity to contribute better to household food security and income. The proposed project recognizes the roles that women, men, boys and girls play in families and society and activities have been tailored to accommodate these needs. Mercy Corps will build capacity of program staff as well as government and private sector partners
