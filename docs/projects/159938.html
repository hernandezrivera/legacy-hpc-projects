Project Summary

Qatar Red Crescent Society will improve the access and utilization of Emergency integrated Nutrition services for women and children living in the most affected areas in Balcad, Gardo, Baidoa, and Afgoi districts using a combination of static and mobile sites. The project objective contributes to reduction of mortality and morbidity among 29560 beneficiaries: 22360 Children U5 (10320 boys and 12040 girls) and 7200 PLWs through the treatment of uncomplicated SAM and integrating with basic nutrition service package (BNSP) services linked to WASH and health programs in the affected settlements these districts. The project activities will include: Screening and treatment of 7200 pregnant women and lactating women) and 22360 (0 to 59 months) children under 5 (10320 boys and 12040 girls) with severe acute malnutrition; training caretakers on the identification of malnutrition and ensure early action in treatment; Training of 42(16 male and 26 female) Nutrition staff on Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition; Training of 42(16 male and 26 female) Nutrition staff in Expanded Program on Immunization and Nutrition Health and Hygiene Promotion; Training 30(13 male and 17 female) Community Nutritional Workers in basic Infant Young Child Feeding (IYCF-E); Providing of multiple micro nutrients to 7200 pregnant women and lactating women; Provision of 22360 children (10320 boys and 12040 girls) 6-59 months with Multiple Micro Nutrient Supplementation; Provide Individual counselling and/or breastfeeding support for 4828 mothers of SAM children admitted to the program; Provision of immunization to 22360 children under five (10320 boys and 12040 girls) 6-59 months with penta 3 and measles vaccines; Provide Immunization services to 4828 pregnant women with tetanus vaccine; Providing 22360 children (10320 boys and 12040 girls) 6-59 months with deworming tablets at the nutrition sites; Provide maternal and child health and nutrition promotion messages using billboards and bronchures and other IEC materials.

Mitigation Measures

1. Ensure the location and access routes to nutrition facilities and services are safe. QRCS will Identify central locations and bring services closer to vulnerable populations in IDPs and host communities. For example, QRCS will use mobile outreach teams to provide services to populations living further away from static facilities to reduce their risk of exposure to protection issues. 2. Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women, and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their speciﬁc needs and preferences for nutrition center location, design, and services. 3. Ensure all nutrition staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneﬁciaries, including the conﬁdentiality of patients. 4. Ensure all staff, implementing partners and volunteers in direct contact with patients, health staff and beneﬁciaries have received training in the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). 5. Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self- esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. 6. QRCS will ensure that the distribution of humanitarian support is done in a safe space and location with adequate security that does not compromise the safety of beneﬁciaries as much as possible. On Disability inclusion: -QRCS will ensure people with disabilities are actively sought, mobilized, and engaged. -Especially, where available, disability advocacy groups, organizations, and networks owned, led, and run by people with disabilities will be engaged to recommend qualified candidates for suitable staff positions. -They will also be engaged in the registration of people with disabilities so that the exercise can be done in a dignified manner and without exposing them to risk.
