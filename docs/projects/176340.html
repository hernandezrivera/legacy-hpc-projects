Project Summary

The proposed project will be implemented in Uror, Twic East and Pochalla counties of Jonglei state as well as Aweil East, Centre and Aweil South county of Northern Bahr el Ghazal; and Yei, Morobo, Kajo Keji and Lainya Counties of Central Equatoria State. The needs across all three states continue to escalate throughout 2020, with poor harvests, prolonged floods and conﬂict resulting in depleted stocks. Uror, Twic East and Aweil East, Aweil South are in an Emergency level (IPC 4) in December - March 2021, while Pochalla, Kajo Keji, Morobo, are predicted to continue being in Emergencies level (IPC 4) in tApril 2021 to July 2021. Lainya, Yei and Aweil Centre are in Crisis level (IPC 3).
The project has been designed to address the deteriorating food security in the project locations. The proposed intervention will achieve the following objectives in line with the sector objectives:
Cluster Objective 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3)
Cluster Objective 2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 5, 4 and 3)
Cluster Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (in IPC 5, 4 and 3)
This will be done through the provision of lifesaving assistance to conflict affected communities by providing emergency food assistance, cash and voucher assistance, agricultural inputs and providing training to vulnerable households to increase food production through good agricultural and fisheries practices to improve food security. Emergency livelihood kits distribution, and increasing access to local markets through seed fairs will be organised for local farmers and the project will provide direct distribution of food rations to the most vulnerable communities. Tearfund will also look at ways of resilience-building that reduce recipient’s future risk and vulnerability to negative shocks.
The proposed project activities will enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, and fishing support in a way that prevents the spread and transmission of COVID 19 with adherence to and compulsory use of new Standard Operating procedures (SOP’s) and guidelines by Ministry of Health/WHO. The distribution of vegetable seeds will provide the households with a variety of vegetable food for their consumption in a span of 3-4 months, in addition, surplus harvest will be sold in the market for income or preserved for future consumption during the lean season. The Fishing kit’s will help the households get the much needed proteins almost immediately after they receive them and engage in fishing activities, the households will be encouraged to preserve and or sell surplus fish for future use or earn extra income for the household to address other household needs.
Project implementation will begin with community mobilisation across the the payams, mobilisation meetings will be conducted with the purpose to explain proposed distribution, define selection criteria and distribution sites. Once completed, project staff will begin the registration process whilst logistics staff will coordinate with logs clusters to transport kits by road or through air. Identified storage facilities will be utilised, Tearfund will cover the ‘last mile’ to the field before distribution.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has had a protracted crisis such as drought, floods and conflicts in Areas of Jonglei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. In 2020, the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19 hit communities hard. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021 consolidated by OCHA on behalf of the Humanitarian Country Team and partners some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. It also highlights insecurity, lack of basic services, and unresolved housing, land and property issues that prevent people from returning home in large numbers. As a result of these catastrophic events the overall food security in counties of Jonglei, Central Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal has worsened and some communities continue facing catastrophic needs. The needs across all three states continue to escalate throughout 2020, with poor harvests, prolonged floods and conﬂict resulting in depleted stocks. Uror, Twic East and Aweil East, Aweil South are in an Emergency level (IPC 4) in December - March 2021, while Pochalla, Kajo Keji, Morobo, are predicted to continue being in Emergencies level (IPC 4) in April 2021 to July 2021. Lainya, Yei and Aweil Centre are in Crisis level (IPC 3). The loss of livelihood assets during the protracted conflict and current poor macroeconomic conditions continue to be the primary drivers of high food insecurity, marked by deficits in cereal production, below-normal access to livestock products, and high food prices. Households are more heavily reliant on food assistance, fish and wild foods, and livelihoods coping strategies. Community members are reporting that lack of food is forcing households to resort to negative coping strategies, including skipping meals, borrowing food from neighbours and family members, and foraging for wild fruits and roots. Households, in particular women who carry the responsibility of providing food for the household, are using all their available energy, time and resources to meet their immediate food needs, and so are unable to plan further ahead, or engage in more long term, sustainable livelihoods activities. The COVID-19 situation is also expected to worsen the food insecurity as the pandemic and the resultant restrictions have disrupted the economic activities of many South Sudanese and reduced their purchasing power. More children are acutely malnourished. Women and girls continue to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial distress. People’s coping mechanisms has weakened because of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labour and child marriage. Besides the high levels of acute food insecurity there is declined crop production, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19, limited access to basic services,and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods.The current key livelihood opportunities include rearing of cattle, goats and sheep. They provide milk and meat for family meals and some are sold for income for other family needs like clothing, medical and foodstuff. Key crops grown in the area include sorghum, maize, kudra, cow peas and vegetables are grown for household consumption. Introduction of other varieties of vegetables by Tearfund through FAO supported programming will enable households to diversify their diets by growing tomatoes, kale, okra, eggplant, onions and watermelon. Based on the findings of a seed security assessment conducted by Tearfund in December 2020, it was observed that seed sources for most farmers are reserved from their harvest. To a large extent except in cases where one depletes their whole produce, the farmers always find comfort in using their own seeds reserved from the preceding season or they may purchase from markets or obtain through social networks.

2 - Project activities

The main activities will include the food and cash distribution, including distribution of complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, and fishing support in a way that prevents the spread and transmission of COVID 19 with adherence to and compulsory use of new Standard Operating procedures (SOP’s) and guidelines by the Ministry of Health/WHO. The distribution of vegetable seeds will provide the households with a variety of vegetable food for their consumption in a span of 3-4 months, in addition, surplus harvest will be sold in the market for income or preserved for future consumption during the lean season. The Fishing kit’s will help the households get the much needed proteins almost immediately after they receive them and engage in fishing activities, the households will be encouraged to preserve and or sell surplus fish for future use or earn extra income for the household to address other household needs. The distribution of kits will be conducted across the Bomas in agreed upon distribution sites with local community leaders and local government actors. Project staff, community volunteers and leaders will facilitate distribution and ensure crowd control. Post distribution monitoring meetings will also be implemented in all the selected Boma’s to identify key learnings, record statistical distribution data and confirm receipt on behalf of the communities. To ensure efficient utilisation of kits by individual recipients, appropriate training on vegetable production, agronomy, post-harvest and sustainable fishing and conservation methods will be delivered. Training sessions will be facilitated by Tearfund’s Food Security and Livelihoods Project officer in collaboration with Government extension workers. Lead farmers will also be targeted during vegetale kit and seeds training so as to disseminate key learnings to communities. The project will be implemented by a team of staff who are already established and currently implementing crop kit distribution food security programmes, supported by an Advisor with specialist technical knowledge. Tearfund staff in Juba, such as Grants Information Coordinator, will support report writing, monitoring visits and financial transactions throughout this project.The community and other stakeholders will be sensitised on the project and informed of the beneficiary selection criteria, which will be agreed by community members. During community sensitisation the importance of prioritising women and vulnerable groups will be emphasised in order to gain community acceptance of the selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs will be prioritised for assistance, including female, elderly and child headed HHs, those with malnourished children and people living with disabilities. Beneficiaries will be asked their preferences regarding seeds through FGDs, which will be held separately for men, women and specific vulnerable groups to ensure that vulnerable groups are able to fully participate in and contribute to the discussions. Community members, including representatives of vulnerable groups, will be involved in the selection and verification of beneficiaries.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Tearfund has been participating in Joint Market Monitoring Initiatives in the various target counties. The information gathered, plus the analyses from various market assessments showed that there is evidence of sufficient food and seed supplies in some markets hence the ability of the market to participate in the proposed project. Beneficiaries are able to get supplies from their usual suppliers when need be. They are also able to get commodities from other suppliers either within the market or other locations to meet increased demand. A majority of traders restock most of the commodities every one month. They are however able to restock weekly depending on demand, mostly from their alternative suppliers. The major disruptors of market functionality in the target locations include: floods (that make access a challenge), poor/impassable roads, especially during the rainy season, Insecurity, mostly along the Sudan - Uganda border, High taxes which mostly affects those that have to import commodities from neighbouring countries. The project will continue with the JMMI activities and market assessments to inform all the Cash and Voucher assistance interventions in the target locations.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Tearfund is committed to evidence-based delivery, continuous improvement, value for money, and providing appropriate, flexible aid to communities. So cash and voucher assistance is a key component of our programming as it not only dignifies the beneficiaries, but also enables them to prioritize their own basic needs, to make choices for themselves and do what is best for their household. Where the context allows, cash prompts a private sector response, helps local businesses survive a crisis and be part of a community’s recovery.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Tearfund will strengthen staff capacity on PSEA and will share with beneficiaries information about protection from sexual exploitation and abuse during all project activities. In targeted locations, Tearfund will work with GBV actors and community based protection actors to spread awareness about the GBV referral pathway including a range of services for survivors. Tearfund will collaborate with protection cluster to provide information that seeks to prevent and respond to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against women, men, boys and girls, and ensure that a comprehensive complaint mechanism is in place and sagefuarding reports and concerns are investigated. Tearfund will develop Information Education Materials (IEC) and disseminate information to the beneficiaries at the host communities/IDPs/Returnees on the available service providers, locations and services available. The IEC materials will also convey knowledge and skills to young men to adopt gender equitable behavior.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Carrying out need assessment will help us understand Information sharing and feedback mechanisms in order to adapt information to the literacy levels amongst specific vulnerable groups and to avoid excluding vulnerable groups. As part of the program design, risk assessment will be conducted to ensure Tearfund work does not cause harm to vulnerable people. The information tools will be contextualized and explained to generate understanding and be relevant. The project will disaggregate data to match the project affected population or sub-groups where practical actions will be embedded into planning and delivery to ensure inclusivity and needs of all gender groups. The engagement of the vulnerable groups will help us to understand expectations, involve them in the decision making process and enable us to receive timely feedback. The steps will help address the needs of the most vulnerable in reducing inequalities and help everyone benefit equally from the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To support better performance and Food Security and livelihoods outcomes for the community, Tearfund and partners will ground their Food assistance and livelihood response in the needs, priorities and expectations of the beneficiaries through a number of approaches for example, by engaging in explicit dialogue and joint analysis with beneficiaries on feedback and complaint mechanisms to empower the communities in ways to communicate their needs and concerns and provide the space to make decisions that affect them in order to enable beneficiaries to act on their own independently. At food distribution centres Tearfund will use notice boards with details about tearfund, contact persons, names and numbers of community representatives in committees, a summary of the project and a map of the project area. Communities will be included in the leadership of response activities in designing distribution processes and community participation in monitoring and evaluating response programs. Complaint help desks will help in providing basic assistance, psychosocial support and counselling to returnees/IDPs/ host communities through the use of community volunteers at the distribution points and community centres and sites so that the communities can provide regular feedback. Additionally, referral pathways will provide the communities with information on the existing service providers, their services and locations and how to access them.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Tearfund will gather data about communications channels in communicating with communities on food security assistance and consider how their rights and entitlements in order to identify feedback mechanisms to be adapted in emergencies. Tearfund will analyse information provided by the returnees/IDPs and host communities through the use of field staff and volunteers to triangulate the information analysis for service improvement. Tearfund will look at other types of information such as, life saving information, where people can access support, people’s rights and entitlements, sexual exploitation and abuse to the communities, security risks, corruption etc to provide adjustments if necessary throughout the project. Information on people’s rights will take into consideration international humanitarian standards (Sphere, HAP, IASC GBV guidelines, Red cross code of conduct) in the provision of impartial and timely access to services and right to protection. Tearfund will ensure beneficiaries participate in decision-making concerning activities and to lead or support the implementation of activities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To propagate the centrality of protection, Tearfund will train staff and stakeholders to integrate sexual gender based violence, child protection and general protection and social inclusion issues in the planning, implementation, monitoring and review of nutrition activities. To ensure women, girls, boys and men are protected from project related protection risks, Tearfund will train nutrition staff in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse including psychological first aid. Staff will receive training on safeguarding and child protection as part of enhancing our accountability to ensure that our programs and operations do no harm. Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse messaging will be integrated in community meetings including OTP and feeding centre activities. Tearfund will forge linkages with protection actors in project sites to promote referrals to survivor centered GBV and child safeguarding services. Tearfund will use the safety audit findings to determine the timing, venue and location of nutrition activities to minimize safety risks that women, girls, boys and men face when they participate in nutrition activities. All nutrition activities will be conducted in locations with safe, accessible, private, and gender-sensitive institutional latrines, handwashing facilities, and bathing facilities to promote greater safety of women and men, girls and boys.As part of Tearfund’s continued risk mitigation approach, a comprehensive analysis will be conducted and periodically updated in all the project sites. Tearfund considers seasonal risks such as access during the rainy season and the security risks associated with the dry season. In order to mitigate this, items will be prepositioned where possible, and procurement will be done in the dry season. Appropriate measures will be taken to secure warehouses. Conversely, environmental risks such as flooding, disease outbreak/crop interventions have also been considered. Tearfund will work closely with FAO and other NGOs to ensure timely detection and reporting to the appropriate focal points. Additional emphasis will also be placed on diversifying the inputs provided in order to mitigate the risk of crop failure. Tearfund’s integrated approach to programming also adds a layer of security, as Tearfund is present in the operational areas, carrying out WASH and Nutrition interventions, as well as working through local partners. This has greatly enhanced the level of community acceptance.
