Project Summary

The IRC will implement WASH activities at supported Health Facilities and host community level. The IRC will increase the availability, quality, accessibility, and reliability of potable water sources by strengthening the maintenance and operations of drinking water sources. The IRC will also promote the uptake of positive hygiene behaviors and practices of households, especially regarding safe water chain activities (from collection to consumption of potable water). The IRC will determine and administer project interventions in the targeted areas that have the highest level of unmet needs and in other areas that can be safely accessed by project staff. To further complement the impact of water supply infrastructure activities, the IRC will primarily utilize local methods and materials to promote post-project sustainability. Water User Committees (WUCs) will be formed by the IRC to support the project, and include members selected based on coordination with community leaders. Each WUC will consist of approximately 5 or 6 members, with selection criteria including: individuals from the targeted community; those over 18 years of age; if possible, persons with an ability to read and write in Arabic with preference given to a literate person(s) for record-keeping and written communications roles; an active member of community work participation, and those with technical skills or a trusted human resource such as masons, teachers, community health volunteers (CHVs), and religious/customary leaders. Women are specifically targeted to be trained and selected to take on leadership and management roles within the WUCs, and the IRC will advocate to ensure women make up at least 50% of the WUC members. Additionally, WASH volunteers will operate within the project’s three target locations and the IRC anticipates having at least two CHVs (segregated by gender) for every 1,000 individuals within the target communities. Women in particular will be empowered and trained to take on the role of CHVs to serve as agents for change within their respective communities.

1 - Needs

Due to a worsening economic crisis, non-functional/aging WASH infrastructure, decreased/insufficient revenue collections, poor budget allocation, and increased operation maintenance costs attributed to an increase in fuel prices in 2021, the WASH situation in Sudan has deteriorated. Internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and vulnerable residents are at significant risk of epidemics, food insecurity, malnutrition, COVID-19, floods, and new resource-related conflicts. 27% of the Sudan population (around 11 million people) do not have access to basic domestic water. The 2020 Multi-Sector Need Assessment (MSNA) showed that about 25% of the water sources around the country are not functioning. Per the assessment, 25% of the population reported that water quantity is not sufficient to meet their basic needs; and 50% reported that it takes more than 50 minutes to fetch water, increasing safety risks, especially for girls and women. In addition, 54% of the schools and half of the health facilities at the country level do not have basic water services. To meet these gaps, the IRC will increase access to drinking water from an improved source with a collection time of not more than 30 minutes roundtrip, including queuing. Furthermore, people targeted with basic water services will benefit from new installations and rehabilitation of existing handpumps, water yards, water treatment plants, protected wells, hafirs, and maintenance and operations in locations where the community cannot afford to repair these. Around 2 million people will also be targeted with water treatment at the source and household level for areas affected by floods and epidemics.In terms of sanitation, 70% of the population (around 28 million people) do not have access to basic sanitation. Out of this number, 33% show open defecation, 30% use unimproved sanitation facilities, and 7% have access to limited sanitation facilities. 49% of the existing schools also do not have improved sanitation facilities. The IRC’s WASH Interventions will therefore ensure the installation, rehabilitation, and use of ventilated improved pit latrine (VIP), pit latrine with slab, composting toilets, and flush/pour pit latrines where possible. The IRC with a local partner will construct the latrines with community support as much as possible. Hand in hand with sanitation activities, the IRC will also carry out hygiene promotion interventions to fill the major gap of only 14.26% of households across Sudan having access to a handwashing facility with soap and water, according to the MSNA 2021. The targeted population will receive hygiene promotion messages, focusing on handwashing with soap and water through household visits, campaigns, and media coverage. An effort will be made to enable households and communal setups to have mobile or fixed handwashing facilities with soap and water. These can be sinks, buckets with a tap, jugs/ibrik, tippy taps, and foot paddle facilities. Environmental health interventions, such as solid waste management and vector control will also be implemented in risk-prone areas. The IRC’s WASH programs will also be integrated into IRC’s health, nutrition and protection services to ensure maximum impact. At least half of the targeted population will be reached with items essential for practicing hygiene. Women and girls who are part of the newly displaced population will receive reusable sanitary pads or culturally appropriate menstrual hygiene supplies. Severely Acute Malnutrition (SAM) affected children and their households will be reached with the WASH kits along with proper hygiene promotion messages in the oral therapeutic programs (OTPs) and stabilization centers (SCs).

2 - Activities/Outputs

The IRC will employ an approach that builds the capacity of local communities, as well as improve community health and protect vulnerable individuals from WASH-related diseases. This will be through increased access to potable water and sanitation while promoting positive health, sanitation, and hygiene practices. The IRC achieves this through the following key outputs:1. Output 1: Strengthen water supplies both within supported health facilities and within local communities.Activities:• Improve water supply by expanding access to water points, including repair of hand pumps with locally procured supplies in water-stressed areas. • Strengthen the community’s capacities to operate and manage water supplies through the inclusion and training of selected community management structures, Women will specifically be targeted to be are trained and selected to take on leadership and management roles within the community structures. 2. Output 2: Facilitate hygiene promotion and environmental improvements through clean-up campaigns, improved waste management, and conducting hygiene promotion.Activities: • Conduct clean-up campaigns which are carried out in and around households to remove domestic waste, potential vector breeding sites, and feces. • Through a network of CHVs, conduct routine awareness meetings to promote waste management and hygiene behavior change messaging. Topics will include treating stored drinking water at the household level, household handwashing practices, identification and mitigation of WASH-related diarrhea, COVID-19 messaging, and hygienic health practices throughout the water supply chain. • Distribute basic hygiene kits and consumables including appropriate materials for menstrual hygiene to women and adolescent girls. All hygiene kits are provided following discussions with the local communities to ensure all needs are addressed. Kit contents typically include water storage containers, handwashing soap, cloth masks, powder soap, disposable sanitary pads, a laundry basins, plastic brick, and empty sacks for packaging. 3. Output 3: Offer sanitation facilities that meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups, such as women, girls, the elderly, and people with disabilities.Activities:• Design, construct and monitor sanitation facilities, including latrines and bath wares, in close collaboration with local communities. This will include discussions with women, girls, the elderly, and people with disabilities to ensure facilities meet the needs of these vulnerable groups. • Construct and/or rehabilitate water systems, including replacing damaged toilet and bath wares such as basins, drainage, showers, sinks, taps, and toilets.• Install handwashing stations and water taps within or near the latrine stalls, establish a Water Treatment and Quality Monitoring Mechanism, and as necessary, construct ramps to ensure the accessibility of latrines for persons with disabilities and the elderly.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will not impact negatively on the environment, though latrine construction activities are likely to have minimal on-site environmental impacts, most of the negative environmental impacts are likely to be seen off-site, where construction materials such as sand, fired bricks and timber will be sourced. Therefore, all tender/bidding documents for latrine construction activities will include clauses to ensure that contractors/suppliers obtain construction materials from authorized/certified sources. In addition, where appropriate IRC will promote the use of soil stabilized blocks (SSB) in lieu of fire bricks and contractors/suppliers will be required to adhere to the stipulated environmental mitigation measures below which will form part of the contract conditions

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The WASH activities will be inclusive of feedback and participation from target beneficiaries, particularly women, including through focus group discussions and by ensuring their inclusion within the WUCs and as hand pump mechanics. The IRC will increase the registration and identification of persons with disabilities and the elderly to ensure that unique needs are addressed, while also strengthening existing community support structures for these groups, such as surrogate water collectors. Hygiene promotion activities will also include information on services available for survivors of abuse and sexual violence. Beneficiaries will also be made aware of the IRC’s Code of Conduct and opportunities for reporting to prevent and reduce the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The IRC confirms that the proposed project will not be drilling any new boreholes but rather will be repairing existing water sources to reduce local tensions around new boreholes sites. The IRC will engage with the targeted communities and local authorities at the village and locality levels to explain its principles of impartiality and provide opportunities for complaint and redress through focus group discussions, community leader meetings, and suggestion boxes within supported health facilities. During project start-up, the IRC will conduct a needs assessment to further identify households that have people living with disabilities and elderly people to ensure the successful delivery of interventions, particularly regarding water collection and hygiene awareness. Although local communities and households have performed inclusive behaviors for vulnerable groups (such as collecting water for them), the IRC will identify and strengthen these existing cultural and behavioral practices through awareness sessions, as appropriate. Through house-to-house visits, CHVs will also identify people with disabilities or special needs within their respective communities, conduct follow-ups, and mutually agree on how to ensure that their unique needs are continuously being met and properly addressed

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The IRC will conduct regular feedback sessions with women and girls within the community to ensure activities and services are adequately responding to their needs. Women, girls and parents will be involved in the decision-making regarding the location of the latrines, contents of the Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) Kits and water points. Client satisfaction surveys will also be conducted with survivors of WASH on a voluntary basis. The IRC team will engage with women, girls, children, and caregivers from crisis-affected communities in planning and implementing strategies to prevent, mitigate and address the protection issues with WASH intervention. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its Meal and Evaluation (M&E) and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action. The IRC will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the IRC Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, child safeguarding policies, process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). The IRC will provide multiple channels of feedback to beneficiaries including focus group discussions (FGDs), KIIs, PDMs survey, KAP survey, feedback boxes, and a hotline. Moreover, the IRC has zero tolerance for bullying, harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC has the following policies in place: Child Safeguarding Policy; Beneficiary Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy; Harassment Free Workplace Policy. All allegations and investigation information received is maintained in a confidential centralized case management system. Cases are evaluated and prioritized when received considering, among other things, whether the alleged conduct is ongoing or historical. The IRC will ensure that all new staff hired for IRC Sudan are trained on and understand the seriousness with which the IRC takes such issues. The IRC also has a neutral department called the global Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, responsible for all major complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
