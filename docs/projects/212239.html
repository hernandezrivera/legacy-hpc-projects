Project Summary

Needs. Nearly half of Syria's population, around 12.4 million individuals, experience food insecurity, with 1.3 million suffering severe food scarcity. The population face severe impoverishment, with a staggering 90% living below the poverty line. In NES, 32% of individuals over the age of 12 years have disabilities. Women and men with disabilities have lower employment rates than their peers without disability and households living with disabilities are at higher risk of poverty (86% of the households with members with disabilities report insufficient monthly incomes to cover basic needs). There are also significant disability related barriers to inclusion within the livelihood sector. The exclusion of women and men with disabilities in working age from economic opportunities further limits the household’s livelihood prospects and constraints the development prospects of communities.
Intervention. The project aims to mitigate the impact of food insecurity on most vulnerable households, while enhancing their capacity to generate income. The primary target group of the project are most vulnerable food insecure households, who lack economic access to food (poor food consumption score and stress livelihood coping strategies) and are not receiving basic needs or livelihood assistance from the Food Security and Livelihood working group. Among the identified ones, the project will prioritize households with unemployed members in working age having disability. These will be targeted through two pillars:
► Pillar 1: target households will stabilize their access to food, thanks to temporary provision of food assistance; households will also mitigate severe food-related consequence, thanks to referral to other projects in the area, that support provision of MHPSS.
► Pillar 2: in target households, adults in working age will acquire skills and productive assets to adapt their households’ livelihood strategies to the economic context in order to increase self-reliance in accessing food. The project will specifically focus on women and men with disabilities, whose economic role will be strengthened, thanks to provision of inclusive case management to remove attitudinal barriers limiting their productive role and thanks to referral to physical and functional rehabilitation to improve functionality in relation to work.
Protection Risk Analysis. Although gender sensitive, the project will not change gender norms in target communities; these might cause tensions within target households, when supporting women increased productive role and access and control of resources; these tensions might lead to domestic violence. Mitigation measures: each target household's individual assessment will include the analysis of the gender roles and power relations; the participatory definition of the households‘ livelihood action plan will include facilitation of dialogue between women and men over gender roles and norms; project staff will be trained on safe identification of protection risks and referral to related services.
PSEA. The INGO is committed to combating sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. Robust policies and a Code of Conduct are in place, with mandatory and refresher training. Recruitment includes background checks, and PSEA policies apply to staff, partners, and accompanying individuals. Information sessions for beneficiaries, complaint mechanisms, and gender balance in staff address cultural sensitivity.
Risks. Risk of external pressure to divert aid to preferred households, due to economic scarcity, corruption and lack of unified political authorities. Prevention: target population and selection criteria are well defined. We will implement the FSL activities in collaboration with a local partner in order to leverage in its deeper knowledge of the different stakeholders and of the socio-economic dynamics and risks.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project has two pillars/outcome. Outcome 1: 'The capacity of most vulnerable food insecure households to stabilize their access to food and be referred to services that address mental distress is improved in target communities of NES.'A1.1. Socio-economic assessment of 200 identified vulnerable households and provision of temporary food assistance to food insecure ones (provision of CVA for 8 months; expected modality is cash) in coordination with the Food Security and Livelihood Working Group. 'A1.2. Internal referral of households for livelihood support (outcome 2) and assessment of their potential. 'A1.3. Referral of household members needing MHPSS to Raqqa National Hospital (PSS supported by another project).Outcome 2: 'Within target food insecure households, the capacity of vulnerable women and men in working age, including those with disabilities, to adapt their livelihood and secure access to food is improved.'A.2.1 'Participatory assessment of households' livelihood strategy, in relation to the findings of a labour market assessment and gender analysis, and definition of a gender-sensitive and inclusive pathway towards self-reliant access to food and related personalized support plan. 'A.2.2. 'As part of the adapted livelihood strategy, provide target households' members, including persons with disabilities, with gender-sensitive and inclusive technical skills training, business management training and coaching and provision of productive assets. 'A.2.3 'Provide inclusive case management to women and men with disability to support their engagement in their households' livelihood. As part of it provide gender-sensitive social work to increase their agency and remove attitudinal barriers within their households and close social networks. 'A.2.3 'As part of their inclusive case management plan, referral of women and men with disability to rehabilitation services at Raqqa national Hospital (rehabilitation services are supported by another HI project), to assistive devices and adapted productive assets. 'A.2.4 'Capacity building to women and men with disabilities and facilitation of collective work to engage with stakeholders in Raqqa to promote inclusive social dialogue over equitable access to livelihood resources.We will cooperate with local organizations in Raqqa to address food insecurity. Partners will provide food assistance and livelihood services, while we will mainstream disability in their work and directly empower women and men with disabilities through inclusive case management to ensure their full participation in services and inclusive livelihood.
