Project Summary

In the wake of the Covid-19 pandemic, many households are affected by food insecurity through loss of income. The government has adopted a range of measures to help these households, but cannot meet the huge demand on its own. In its ongoing AA project, Caritas has already identified 2,375 particularly vulnerable households and coordinated with local government and the food security sector to avoid duplication. In this follow-up project, 1,756 households are to be supported for 3 months (each month BDT 4500). Priority will be given to particularly vulnerable households living below the poverty line, elderly family members, people with disabilities and children under 5 years of age, as well as households of poor single women with dependents. Due to the pandemic and mobility restrictions, particularly affected occupational groups who are still unable to earn an income will be added. These include rickshaw drivers, day laborers in and outside agriculture, textile workers, tea sellers and other small traders. The measures and selection of households will be closely coordinated with local government and, at sectoral level, with other aid agencies active in the region. The proposed project aims to provide imm ediate and ongoing emergency relief to the target group, in line with the AA's strategy for humanitarian aid abroad. The activities are designed to alleviate the suffering of those most in need and enable them to live safe and dignified lives

1 - Identified Needs

"Even before the refugee crisis, the district of Cox's Bazaar was marked by malnutrition and food insecurity. In the last three years, the situation has worsened due to the significant population increase, which has already had a negative impact on food and nutrition security of the host community in some areas. The need to support the local population during the Corona pandemic is high in all regions of Bangladesh, but hits the host communities in Cox's Bazaar particularly hard as they live in close proximity to the camps and are already further burdened by the negative impact of the refugee crisis. Falling wages, rising food prices and a worsening sense of security have worsened living conditions over the past two and a half years. Labor migration was extremely restricted by the lockdown. The district administration estimates that more than 700,000 people who worked as day laborers in Cox's Bazaar are no longer able to earn an income due to government restrictions to contain the Corona virus. This loss of livelihoods, combined with reduced access to local markets, hampers the ongoing relief efforts in the camps and creates an additional need for support to the host community. The reduced household income has undermined the purchasing power of many households. According to an initial survey conducted by the National Assessment Working Group in mid-April, 52% of households surveyed said they had not received any support from the government since the lockdown at the end of March. 75% said they did not have enough food available and 91% cited lack of money as a cause. It is expected that negative coping strategies such as emergency sales, indebtedness and skipping meals will increase. While food availability on the markets remains stable, incomes remain below normal levels even after the initial restrictions were eased, resulting in a 42% drop in purchasing power in June compared to pre-crisis levels. At present, a household in Cox's Bazar can buy about 9 kg of rice on a daily income. Before the pandemic it was 15 kg. There are significant differences between income categories, with day laborers who had low incomes from the start losing up to 73% of their purchasing power. Prices for rice, lentils, chickpeas, oil and onions have stabilized or fallen. However, most markets are still operating at an atypically low level - subdued household demand is the main reason for this. These extreme economic impacts of Covid-19 further worsen the relationship between the refugee and host communities, who perceive the Rohingya as an additional burden.
