Project Summary

Fondation Caritas Luxembourg and its implementing partner SARD are committed to assist affected people most in need in North West Syria, despite the unpredictability and the risks of working in areas controlled by radical opposition armed groups.
The response plan with a budget of 3,476,932 USD will address the needs of more than 32,870 people over 12 months, addressing life-saving and life-sustaining assistance.
The action contributes to improve shelters and to provide life-sustaining assistance with non-food items (NFI) for selected most vulnerable persons including internally displaced people (IDPs) who arrived in 2018 and earlier (esp. those evacuated from besieged areas and left alone), returnees in their war-damaged houses and persons in need in host communities.
The NFI assistance will be provided according to the needs defined for each family based on the family needs assessment. It will include the necessary bedding units, household goods (kitchen set, cleaning items) and potentially clothes. The modality of the assistance will range from conditional cash to voucher or in-kind assistance, depending on the situation.
The shelter sector is organised around 3 main activities, all of them provide a special attention on House Land and Property (HLP) documents: they are checked using the HLP check-list. SARD will sign a memorandum of understanding with individual owners and landlords, depending on the situation.
• Collective shelter upgrade and rehabilitation with shared facilities
Collective shelter damaged by the conflict will be repaired through contractors or directly by the organisation, the modality will depend on the assessed situation.
• Individual shelter upgrade and rehabilitation
Individual houses damaged by the conflict will be repaired through conditional cash-grant and self-help modality, or through contractors, the modality will depend on the assessed situation.
• For community infrastructure rehabilitation:
The project will support communities in restoring their basic infrastructure through small scale projects to support public infrastructure repairs that can range from public building rehabilitation to road, electricity or other facilities’ basic repairs.
FCL and SARD will mainstream protection principles throughout the action, ensuring that assistance is provided safely in secured locations, responding to people's needs, and reinforcing their dignity.

Needs

The comprehensive needs assessment conducted by FCL and SARD in December 2018 which is in line with Humanitarian Needs Overview (OCHA 2019), as well as the analysis of the continuous assessments of needs done by field teams during the implementation of the 2018's projects.The needs assessment, conducted by the M&E department in December 2018, targeted 465 households in Idleb and Aleppo governorates (Daret Azza, Azaz, Atareb Teftnaz, Qourqeena, Ariha sub-districts). Respondents included internally displaced persons, returnees as well as host households. The assessment targeted both beneficiaries (e.g. most vulnerable households assisted in 2018) and non-beneficiary households. Findings show that food and NFI/hygiene items ranked as households’ main priorities for the next 3 to 6 months. 99% of households ranked food as their main priority. 98% of the households are unable or barely able to meet their current food and NFI needs, demonstrating that emergency relief remains a crucial and life-saving need. NFI needs tend to be diverse and include winterization kits, clothes, kitchen sets, bedding units and hygiene items. As per the HNO 2019, needs tend to be higher in Idleb governorate, where the number of people in major, severe and critical need of NFI assistance has tripled compared to 2018 (from 340,404 to 1,027,106 people in need (PiN) in major, severe and critical categories).HNO findings also show an increase by 42% and 50% of the number of people in major, severe and critical need of shelter assistance in Idleb (from 594,627 to 847,578 PiN in major, severe and critical categories) and Aleppo (from 698,998 to 1,046,025 PiN in major, severe and critical categories) respectively. This correlates with SARD’s needs assessment findings, in which about 3 in 4 households (71%) ranked shelter assistance as one of their main priorities for the following 3 to 6 months. 92% are unable or barely able to meet their current shelter needs, and most dwellings present multiple inadequacy issues. Top 3 challenges reported by households pertained to difficulties to protect against weather conditions, lack of privacy and lack of lighting. Presence of rodents and insects as well as lack of cooking and bathing facilities (mostly for returnees and IDPs) were also mentioned, albeit by a minority.In May 2019, hostilities increased in Northern Hama southern Idleb resulting in the displacement of 397,437 individuals moved to different community in NW of Syria between 1 May and 15 June 2019. To better understand the situation, FCL-SARD conducted a Rapid Need assessment (RNA) in May 2019 assessing the needs of the newly arrived IDPs. As a result, NFI is considered a critical priority for the newly IDPs for the next weeks. 68% of families are barely able to secure their needs NFIs, while 28% of families are completely unable secure NFIs and only 4% are able to secure access to the NFI (they are mostly people who travel with their asset and did not sell them as the time of the interview.In addition, findings indicate that approximately 24% of the newly displaced families are under trees in open fields, with no form of shelter. These families lack the minimum life-saving standards and are not able to secure their urgent shelter needs. More than 64% of the families are sheltered in “informal settlement “with a very basic form of shelter such as a tent, or simple sub-standard shelter. Some 23% of the IDPs are in Open field with either no form of shelter or make-shift shelter that do not provide any type of protection. The soft structure shelters installed by the IDPs themselves are not well insulated (no flooring, no thermal plastic sheets ... etc.).

Implementation of activities

The action provides multi-sector assistance with NFI and shelter for selected most vulnerable persons including IDPs and host communities. Identification is based on a set of eligibility criteria.The following activities will take place for NFI and shelter needs: • Assessment on the security, locations, tenders and markets’ surveys will enable to determine the modality of the response (cash, voucher or in-kind); • Family needs assessment confirm needs of each household. • Need base multi-purpose assistance includes conditional voucher for hygiene and NFI kits The Shelter sector is organised around 3 main activities:• Collective shelter upgrade and rehabilitation with shared facilitieso Infrastructure Site Assessment (ISA) for collective shelter and unfinished buildings, including bills of quantities o House Land and Property documents (HLP) is checked using the SNFI cluster's HLP due diligence guidelines and MoU is signed with the land-lord o Works are implemented through contractors (selected via tender) or directly by the SARD engineer teams, under tight technical supervisiono After action review and satisfaction survey is conducted by the M&E team to verify the implementation, to measure the impact of the action, and to draw lessons of each action.• Individual shelter upgrade and rehabilitationIndividual houses damaged by the conflict will be repaired through conditional cash-grant and self-help modality, or through contractors, the modality will depend on the assessed situation.o House Land and Property documents are checked using the HLP check-list. SARD will sign a MoU with individual owners and land lord, depending on the situation.o Damage and Vulnerability assessment (DVA) is conducted -only basic emergency repair works related to safety and security, weather protection, privacy and access to hygienic sanitary facilities are eligible. Structural damages (will not be covered by the project.o Based on the BoQ, the value of the grant will be agreed with the family or works will contractedo Destitution of cash grants is based on site follow up visit and SARD technician recommendation.o After action review and satisfaction survey is conducted by the M&E • For Community infrastructure rehabilitation: The project will support communities in restoring their basic infrastructure through small scale project to support public infrastructure repairs that can range from public building rehabilitation to road, electricity or other facilities basic repair.Activities to be undertaken are: o Identification of the location: prioritisation based on needs, damage level, concentration of MVF, size of the community, safety and security of the area; o Community consultation with a detailed description of the project and capacity assessment of the community. o The project has to be a priority for the common interest of the community, the feasibility must be within the technical capacity of the SARD engineer team o Initial shelter and infrastructure assessment (ISIA) at community level to determine the appropriate Bill of Quantity (BoQ) and Technical Specifications Notebook (TSN) .o Works are implemented by contractors through the tight supervision of both the SARD and the local community technical department.o The daily labors will also benefited from a salary during the duration of community projects implementation,o Work will be closely monitored by SARD technician

Protection Risk Analysis

All shelter activities are implemented with a protection lens and aim at restoring households’ dignity by improving their privacy and minimizing safety risks. Households, through FGDs with men, women, elderly/disabled, are actively involved in the design of the activities (i.e. identification of most pressing shelter needs, locations of facilities, etc.). As for all activities, shelter intervention is monitored by SARD M&E team on a regular basis throughout the activity’s lifetime. To mitigate any harmful effects, SARD has a strong complaint response mechanism (CRM) that is regularly advertised to local communities and offers multiple channels (CRM desk in SARD’s office, face-to-face field visits, and WhatsApp) to beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike. Regular field visits are also conducted by both SARD programme and M&E teams. Tensions between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and/or towards humanitarian workers, and crowd management issues: SARD –whenever feasible, offers door-to-door distribution with at least one female staff (especially when distributing assistance to female headed HHs). When this is not possible, distribution sites are chosen in coordination with local authorities, offer separate queuing lines and distribution of relief is staggered to facilitate crowd management. To avoid the risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members as well as the risk of inter-community tension, SARD’s staff adopt the SNFI Cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content, and ensure that distribution of NFI, Shelter kits are conducted based on needs and not based on status and the selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed as well as engaging the distribution partners with the communities to sensitize the population. All SARD’s staff are trained on targeting/vulnerability criteria and conduct community mobilization sessions. Criteria are clearly explained to local authorities through sensitisation sessions. Authorities are actively engaged in identifying and preparing beneficiaries list. To avoid Abuse of Authority by distribution actors, SARD’s has developed range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). All SARD’s staff are committed to sign the code of conduct. SARD conducting a strong and constant market monitoring before starting any local procurement in case of depletion of the market and inflation of the prices. As a consequence of the closure of the border or other internal security situations. SARD is maintaining close coordination with SNFI Cluster, submit the 4Ws and planned activities report, attend the Cluster meeting and seek Cluster support prior to the implementation of the activities in addition of liaising with other aid actors and local authorities to avoid the duplication of the assistance. Housing and Land Property (HLP) and lack of documentation:To avoid beneficiary’s eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated, SARDs staff are abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster. HLP documentation are required prior any shelter activity and SARD team regularly conducts HLP awareness sessions with local communities. Lack of identity documents (e.g. ID card) does not impede eligibility, as long as the household can provide another source proving its identity (e.g. marriage certificate).

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Insecurity conditions result in accessibility problems and exposure to risks to staff and beneficiaries: To prevent these risks, safety precautions will be taken such as ensuring with the families and stakeholders the safety of the selected sites/shelters.- UXO (unexploded ordnance): The action will take place only in location that has been cleared by the local civil defence. All staff will be trained on UXO awareness. In addition, and prior to any house rehabilitation, community members and beneficiaries will participate in UXO awareness sessions conducted by well-trained staff.- Lack of supplies and contractors: To prevent any shortage, FCL/SARD has its own mobile team who is capable to conduct shelter upgrades, in addition to an emergency stock of materials. In the past 3 years, supplies have never been disrupted. Market surveys conducted before establishing contractor’s contract, ensure that price range is acceptable and as per marker value.- FCL/SARD was part of the Due Diligence Guidelines that was adapted by IOM and the SNFI cluster to the operational context of the Turkey x-border Shelter/NFI Cluster. Key considerations will be integrated throughout the shelter program by SARD to ensure the Due Diligence approach throughout the different phases of preparedness, planning, during the implementation, as well as during the monitoring and evaluation phase. Should SARD be unable to check ownership documents (e.g. in case of public buildings, or in case families have lost the documents) village council and neighbours will be consulted to confirm ownership of the land/property. In such cases, FCL/SARD’s intervention (in both collective and individual shelters) will remain light by adding only basic elements (doors, windows, plumbing, etc.). FCL/SARD is committed to assist house and landowners in recovering their HLP documentation FCL/SARD will support communities to prepare addressing their HLP issues, ensuring that targeted communities are aware of HLP rights and responsibilities. Communities will be informed of the need for HLP documents or any corroborating evidence that could help them to support an HLP claim in the future. Thus, FCL/SARD intervention will only target house owners. - Risk of tension in the community due to selection criteria for rehabilitation of housing and essential infrastructures. Mitigate this risk with ensuring dialogue and explanation of criteria through community group discussion and involvement and consultation of local authority and all community throughout the whole process. In addition, a complain/feedback mechanism will also be implemented.- Risk of creating more desirable homes, which may cause an increase in rent price and overcrowding of certain areas; BoQ and houses rehabilitation site works to appropriately done according to local housing infrastructure practice and standards.
