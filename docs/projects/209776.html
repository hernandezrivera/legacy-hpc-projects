Project Summary

The project aims to increase the resilience and mitigate food insecurity of affected communities by recovering food production, generating income activities, and mitigating the impact of winterization through implementing sustainable FSL and ERL interventions in Bennsh subdistrict.
The food security situation in Syria has drastically continued to deteriorate since last year as a result of the protracted crisis. The 2023 HNO report highlights the ongoing struggles of Syrian families in North West Syria (NWS) due to a prolonged crisis. In Syria, 15.3 million people require humanitarian assistance, with 91%of the population in dire need, particularly in NWS, where 2.1 million IDPs reside in last-resort IDP sites, with an estimated 2.9 million people at risk of falling into food insecurity. Bennsh is an agrarian sub-district in Idleb Governorate with a population of app. 56,000 inhabitants, including 32,000 IDPs. According to HNAP (October 2021), most of Bennsh’s population is employed in the agricultural sector; local farmers cultivate irrigated wheat and potatoes. According to key informants of the Farmers’ Cooperative Association in Bennsh, an estimated 4,000 ha of arable land is currently irrigated. In total, 347 wells are established in that area. 47 of them are operated solely with fuel, 100 with a combination of fuel and electricity, and the remaining 200 wells are operated with a combination of fuel and other energy sources.
For FSL intervention, the project aims to support 400 small-scale vegetable farmers with agricultural inputs consisting of vegetable seeds, NPK fertilizer, pesticide, and fungicide, as well as the required assets of low plastic tunnel culture (LPTC). The LPTC is considered an approved and smart agricultural strategy to mitigate the effects of climate change by alleviating the effects of frost. and enhance crop production. In addition, 100 women from IDPs and LCs will be hired through CfW activity of vegetable harvesting. For ERL intervention, the project will enhance access to humanitarian aid and markets for IDPs and LC during the winter season by rehabilitation 10 km of access roads by implementing scraping, leveling, graveling, and spraying liquid asphalt MC0 processes, which more than 4000 of HCs and IDPs people will benefit from roads in terms of access to markets especially in winter to mitigate the negative effects it.
Risk management in MASRAT is based on a scientific approach using a risk matrix during project design, encompassing all possible risks and mitigation strategies. This matrix is shared with field staff. Potential risks include military operations, shelling and bombing, other security risks, involvement of military factions or civilian entities in humanitarian work, loss of crops, PSEA, and finanicial risks like USD rate exchange.
MASRRAT specialist team will give protection principals training for field staff, including PSEA, and prioritize the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. Also, MASRRAT is a member of the PSEA network. Banners containing hotline Tel. numbers and advertisements for the network will be shared to report any harassment or blackmail that will be referred to a special employee to address these issues.
PSEA Complaints directly will be recorded in the PSEA Network database. If any complaints are received directly by MASRRAT, they will be transmitted confidentially and within a database coded with the PSEA Network. The names of all new employees and vendors will be checked in the UN database before they are issued a contract. The response mechanisms for preventing exploitation and sexual assault will be sensitive to age, sex, diversity, and access to women, children, persons with disabilities, older people, minority groups, and others at risk.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The FSL intervention activities include: 1- Support 400 small-scale vegetable farmers with the required materials to establish LPTC (includes iron arched, black nylon and transparent nylon). 2- Support them with a vegetable kit sufficient for 2 dunums (vegetable seeds, N.P.K fertilizer (20:20:20), 50 USD fuel voucher, pesticide, and fungicide. 3- Provide agricultural training sessions in new agriculture practices, protected culture, smart agriculture and climate change, intercropping, and PSEA. 4- Secure a temporary income for 100 women from IDPs through CFW activity of vegetable harvesting for 60 days.5- Establish a broadcast channel that can provide agricultural extension messages to farmers.the ERL intervention will be conducted through rehabilitation process for four agricultural roads with a length of 10 km in Bennsh, through implementing scraping, leveling, graveling, and spraying liquid asphalt MC0 processes. More than 4000 of HCs and IDPs people will benefit from roads in terms of access to markets especially in winter to mitigate the negative effects it. The protection cluster recommendations will be taken into consideration, wherein all the population in the targeted locations will benefit from MASRRAT protection services in the event of any GBV referral cases.To ensure accountability towards the affected population, which is MASRRAT’s approach, the project team to actively engage women and girls in consultations during the design phase to ensure that their perspectives, priorities, and needs are adequately considered and addressed in the FSL interventions.To ensure the effectiveness and sustainability of the interventions, active consultation with women and girls will be integrated into the design, planning, and decision-making processes. This will involve identifying and addressing the specific challenges and opportunities faced by women and girls in the context of food security and livelihoods. Furthermore, measures will be taken to ensure inclusivity and equity, with a focus on enhancing the participation and empowerment of women and girls in project activities. By mainstreaming gender considerations and actively involving women and girls, the project aims to maximize its impact and contribute to more equitable and sustainable outcomes for all community members.During the design phase, the project team will actively engage with PWDs and relevant stakeholders to understand their specific needs, challenges, and priorities. This may involve conducting consultations, surveys, or focus group discussions to gather input and feedback from PWDs and their communities.So, MASRRAT will engage PWDs and disability organizations in the project planning and decision-making processes, provide training and capacity-building opportunities for project staff, community members, and service providers on disability inclusion, and implement reasonable accommodations to address individual needs and ensure equal participation of PWDs.
