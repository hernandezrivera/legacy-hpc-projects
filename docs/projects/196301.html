Project Summary

Through about 12 years of destruction and madness, the Syrian situation has become one of the most disastrous crises in the world, causing hundreds of thousands of victims and led to widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural infrastructure, including homes, schools, health facilities, water supply, and irrigation systems. Now, 13.4 million people in Syria need humanitarian assistance.
As a response to that the project aims to provide integrated emergency WASH response to host communities and IDPs in the communities and the in the camps and informal settlements north Syria in addition to responding to the costly and short-term water supply in northwest Syria and to reinforce the most urgent life-saving interventions, Watan Foundation aims to enhance the long-term WASH infrastructure alongside with the emergency response services for a certain period of time.
This improvement targets host communities and IDPs in 30 camps and 3 communities hosting approximately 120,000 individuals.
Therefore, in order to improve general and personal hygiene within the targeted camps in addition to functioning the Water and sanitation infrastructure as well, WATAN Foundation designed the following axes taking into consideration the priorities of WASH cluster:
1- Enhance access to drinkable water and Improve life conditions through rehabilitation and construction of infrastructure in the targeted location:
1.1- Construction and rehabilitation of water systems
1.2- Provision of water disinfectants
1.3- The operation and maintenance support to the water systems
1.4- Establishment of water safety plans
1.5- Support sanitation systems, including sewage networks, wastewater treatment plants.
1.6- Linking the constructed and rehabilitated water stations with suitable Solar PV system
1.7- The distribution of 1 cubic meter of metal water tanks.
2- improving personal hygienic behavior and practices, with ensuring the easy reach of humanitarian lifesaving emergency WASH facilities and services
2.1- The distribution of Full family Hygiene kits/ NFI kits
2.2- Providing regular hygiene promotion sessions, With Covid-19 prevention sessions as well
2.3- Distribution of chlorinated drinkable water through humanitarian lifesaving/emergency water facilities and services
2.4- Improving sanitation within camps through the desludging process of cesspits and septic tanks
2.5- Solid waste management and vector control facilities and services
2.6- The distribution of Garbage bins
2.7- Providing market-based assistance for WASH services
2.8- Distribution of AAP materials and brochures
3- Improve WASH facilities and services in institutions to minimize sub-standard WASH conditions of the most vulnerable people in targeted locations
3.1- The construction of Normal latrine blocks
3.2- The construction of PWDs latrine blocks
3.3- The rehabilitation and maintenance of existing latrine blocks
3.4- Employing daily workers to be responsible for keeping the facilities clean and functioning
3.5- Distribution of the needed cleaning tools, items, and equipment to the workers to do the job
Moreover, WATAN Foundation will participate in the monthly reporting to the WASH Cluster and any other stakeholders to highlight any gaps and participate in any innovative solutions that can enhance the living conditions of the affected groups in Northwest Syria.

3 - Needs

During the second half of 2022 WATAN has implemented more than 7 direct contacts with BNFs including open discussion and FGDs in addition to several need assessments in districts such as Harim, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Al Bab, and Jebel Samaan. Which provided a clearer image about the needs whether in camps and community. Where there was an open discussion about the contents of the regular HK and the need for sustainable sources for water by providing innovative solutions adopting clean energy approaches. On the other hand, regarding the Sanitation systems WATAN will knock on every donor’s possible door to achieve enough fund to make decent and safe sewage systems within the district we are planning to target and adopt a design for latrines to be fully supportive to PWDs. Moreover, WATAN is paying great attention to the needs related to women and teenage girls through various menstrual hygiene sessions and activities. A detailed needs assessment was implemented on 60 camps and informal settlements using the recommended WASH cluster assessment tools with additional questions by WATAN WASH team. The needs assessment covered 15,000 families living in informal settlements including women, children, and PwDs.The need assessment will be expanded to reach more areas during 2023 by using the same tools.

4 - Implementation of activities

1.1. Repair and rehabilitation of 11 water systems and construction of 2 in the targeted communities including introducing solar energy at the water stations and establishing tariff systems to ensure securing daily water per capita at a rate of 40 L/p/d at least as Cholera response. 1.2. Support water quality assurance, operation and maintenance of water supply systems. 1.3. Repair and rehabilitation of 14 sanitation systems and supporting solid waste management activities. 2.1. Distribution of essential WASH non-food items, hygiene promotion, and messaging on COVID-19 and Cholera as well. 2.2. Improve access to humanitarian lifesaving/emergency WASH facilities and services, including strengthening Infection Prevention and Control measures. This will include providing water trucking as a transition service (daily water per capita share will be 35 L/p/d at least), provision water distribution points designed specifically for PWD, Distribution of Water tanks 1/10 HH, including providing solar energy, solid waste management, desludging latrines septic tanks (max. three months), constructing new latrines for persons with disabilities and persons without disabilities, supporting the operation and maintenance of the existing and newly constructed latrines, and distributing garbage containers. 4.1. Support to WASH facilities and services in schools and child-friendly spaces in close coordination with Education and Protection sectors.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Hygiene Kits; Risk- Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. Mitigation-Promote Establishment of the adequate management plan; Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on the package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partner staff. Risk-Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards Mitigation - Ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities or local councils. Ensure and comply with agreed context /SPHERE standards and establish a water quantity tracking monitoring system involving beneficiaries. Risk-Water distribution not safe to access or not accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution Mitigation - Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design and implementation phases.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WATAN will publish PSEA material and inform BNFs and non-BNFs about their rights and have a complaint mechanism in place as well as building the local capacity in terms of protection issues through conducting appropriate training such as PSEA As for WATAN’s staff, all of its staff once hired WATAN to conduct a PSEA and safe gardening as well as code of conduct and humanitarian principles training. Also, WATAN is an active member in the protection cluster and always applies the referral mechanisms.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Loss of cash or assets (operational): the risk owner: the team leader in each site - This continues to be a threat to all areas of operation inside of Syria - mitigation measures: daily inventory, control the treasury better, involve many people in this process to extend the circle of suspicion. -Unforeseen Changes in the Conflict- Risk owners: Senior Management Committee - each office - Conflict is such that organization cannot safely access locations, staff gets besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. Mitigation measures: Unlikely Moderate Stable Local information shows an increase in conflict levels, as we as direct reports from staff on the ground. “Continue to gather information from a range of sources. Develop a contingency plan to work in an alternative location if the conflict continues. Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space. -" Kidnapping / Abduction - risk owners: Security Management/Program Management/Individual - risk mitigation measures: travelling in the daytime - transportation in convoys - high coordination on movement tracking - deterrence through local authorities. -Exposure of sensitive information - Risk owners: "Security Management/Program Management/Individual Responsibility/Executive reinforcement of policies" - Communications with anyone inside of Syria are very likely compromised and we must assume that leakage of electronic data will occur.
