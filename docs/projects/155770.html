Project Summary

IR aims to be part of the recovery and rehabilitation efforts in in tribal Districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) with other stakeholders to make a positive impact in the life of returnees, which suffered the most during the crises. Along with other key sectors, the purpose of inclusive and resilient WaSH interventions is to reach to the needy people in their areas of return in three tribal districts namely North Waziristan, Orakzai and Khyber. IR has valid NOCs in these three districts, which is valid till mid 2019 and can be further extended for the pipelines projects. In 2019, a total of 109 water supply schemes benefiting 379,394 individuals will be restored/rehabilitated to mitigate the difficulties of the returnee communities, particularly, women and girls. The returnee population, particularly, women, the elderly, children and people with disabilities face difficulties in getting easy access to accessing water. Traditional water sources and facilities are non functional because they remained unattended for long periods or are damaged by conflict. Thus, fetching water from distant locations by women and girls exposes them to unprotected environments and heavy workloads. This provides them with clean water and a protected environment not only by providing clean drinking water but access to protected environment nearby. Moreover, sanitation practices were poor before the conflict and during the conflict condition further deteriorated. School and BHUs will be equipped with inclusive WaSH facilities in areas of interventions. 42,717extremely vulnerable households will receive latrine kits and cash support to re/construct their latrines to promote healthy practices and to restore these facilities to bring life to normalcy. In addition, health and hygiene awareness is lacking among the tribal communities because of poor education and awareness. A total of 324 health and hygiene awareness sessions (including MHM) will be conducted with particular focus on women, girls, boys, elderly and persons with disabilities.

Needs

Through the act of parliament, Tribal districts, previously agencies of FATA, have been merged with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. This decision does not immediately addressed the deprivations, which this region has experienced over the period of decades, however, it has laid down the framework for socio-economic and political integration with the rest of province and country. At present an effort is being made to take administrative and judicial structures towards tribal districts but it will take years to be fully enforced and implemented. This change is positive but baggage of crises is still looming large on future of inhabitants of tribal districts.The return of TDPs has been more than two years old now but wider destruction left by the crises is requiring to be dealt with in comprehensive manner. In this backdrop, IR is already working for recovery and rehabilitation of returnees in Khyber tribal district. Flow of return is varying in terms of scale and timing in Khyber district but there are vast pockets of population in these areas where even if return is older but recovery and rehabilitating efforts are very nominal. On the other hand, funding estimated for FATA return strategy could not be materialized to the extent as required on the ground. This situation has demanded from humanitarian actors to put in their resources and efforts for addressing the needs of returnees emanating from protracted crises. Returnees have been stripped off their economic resources during the period of displacement to meet their immediate needs. On way back to their native places, they are encountering hardships like damaged and destroyed water facilities, shelter, health centers, schools and road networks. It is augmenting their problems and restricting their capacities to cope with existing situation and recover in the long term.Multi sectoral and sector focused assessment have widely covered the depth and magnitude of damages done to various sectors. IR further conducted need assessment at micro scale to validate overall facts and figures, so that immediate and unavoidable priorities could be determined and informed programming can be chalked out for the action.Overall WASH scenarios prevailing in Khyber tribal district is captured and summarized as; Damages to the water facilities is a major WASH issue, through the recent Household (HH) need assessment survey, FGDs and KIIs, it was found that 67% of households need their water sources repaired or rehabilitated in Khyber district. It has also been learnt that 52% returnees in the targeted areas of Khyber has inadequate water storage facilities. In terms of access to sanitation facilities, majority of population in Khyber returnees shared that they do open defecation and the main reason for practicing open defecation is non availability of latrines. Moreover, in Khyber 59% girl’s schools need latrines facilities. The existing facilities are either non-functional or damaged. Students, teachers particularly in girls' schools are facing difficulties to attend their needs in hygienic, dignified and protected manner. On the other hand patients and their attendants have to go through hardships because of poor sanitation facilities at Health Care Facilities. Moreover, 98% of respondents shared that latrine structures are not user friendly for persons with disability. Of hygiene practices, only 27% HH in Khyber, confirmed to wash their hands after defecation with soap and 22% women do not wash their hands before feeding the child. 67% people were taking bath on weekly basis due to unavailability of adequate water for their families.Water borne diseases such Diarrhea, scabies and malaria are common especially in summer. District Health Officer confirmed high prevalence of these disease in Khyber due to poor hygienic facilities and lack of awareness.
