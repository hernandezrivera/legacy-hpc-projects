Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Zimbabwe is has been further worsened by the current COVID19 pandemic that has so far affected 8303 people and 242 people have died as of 26 October 2020. The COVID 19 restrictions has negatively affected households’ access to incomes, food sources and basic services and commodities. The country is coming off the back of a devastating drought in 2018-2019, which was followed by rainfall below average poor rains in the 2019/20 season. In rural areas, only 30 per cent of the 55,593 water sources are functional and protected, increasing the risk of WASH-related disease. About 77% of households are accessing water from improved sources However, there are districts such as Mangwe (44.7%) and Kariba (32.5%) with high proportions of households accessing water from surface water sources. Matabeleland North has consistently had high proportions of households (over 50%) practicing open defecation. Over 80% of rural households did not have hygiene services available.
In response to this humanitarian situation, Save the Children proposes to timely water, hygiene and sanitation interventions in 5 Districts across the 3 provinces thus Binga, Beitbridge, Kariba, Matabo and Hurungwe districts.
The project aims to improve the water, hygiene and sanitation of the households within the priority districts through
• Improving awareness among children and women on safe hygiene, proper solid waste management and sanitation practices, with a focus on community engagement, participatory health and hygiene education (PHHE) and water conservation.
• Providing access to critical WASH-related hygiene kits, with a focus on the most vulnerable families in the targeted areas, including pre-positioning of emergency WASH supplies in critical provinces.
• Provision of institutional hygiene kits and awareness to hygiene practices.
• Strengthening water quality monitoring at the local level during the peak-period so that appropriate action can be taken early before an outbreak.
• Supporting the Health system to reduce the risk of hospital associated infections and enhance infection, prevention and control (IPC).
• Strengthening effective coordination and surveillance mechanisms on WASH at National and sub-national level, with links to other cluster coordination arrangements on critical inter-sectoral issues

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

SC engaged the communities to gather and share evidence to inform programme design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. SC supported monitoring by the community to contribute to better accountability and to engage them for improved ownership. A toll-free number is in place and will be promoted and posters that raise awareness have produced and distributed. Feedback meetings will also be held with children as well as adults in the targeted areas to strengthen accountability and engage the targeted communities. Complaints and Feedback Mechanism: SC will set up help desks staffed by men and women at key activities, use a toll free hotline and ensure all programme staff are trained on how to receive complaints and feedback using KoBo Toolbox – allowing staff to instantly document and submit a complaint/feedback to our online database through their phone. This builds on existing CFM approaches SC has been using in other responses and development programmes across Zimbabwe. Additional CFM channels, including child-friendly ones, will be identified through an accountability assessment, to understand affected children and communities’ preferences around information sharing and CFM channels, especially of people with specific vulnerabilities. Complaints and feedback will be analysed, key findings will be reported through a regular accountability report for decision-making, and the feedback loop will be closed on individual and/or community level. CFM data analysis will focus among others on understanding which vulnerable groups are able to access our channels, to ensure they are appropriate and accessible.
