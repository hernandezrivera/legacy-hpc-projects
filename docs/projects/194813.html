Project Summary

With this project CARE will respond to complex protection concerns, in particular related to GBV risks via integrated protection, GBV risk mitigation, prevention and response activities and fills critical service gaps. The aim is to ensure that women, girls, men and boys have timely access to quality, appropriate, and relevant information and services that help them protect themselves better and seek support, if affected by immediate protection concerns. The activities include :
1- Protection : via critical information dissemination to women, girls, men and boys on general protection and GBV via protection facilities, helpdesks, outreach services (home and camp visits) as well as referrals. Additional structured protection prevention PSS activities are planned for boys and girls via CCs, protection centre and in IDP camps.
2- GBV risk mitigation and prevention : by meaningfully engaging communities in the beginning and during implementation of activities, by conducting Gender Safety Audits in IDP camps and sharing GBV risks with stakeholders, information sessions and structured awareness raising activities on GBV prevention.
3- GBV response : through GBV Case Management, counselling for those at risk or survivors of GBV (in particular women and girls) and by provision of Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) including in kind assistance to GBV survivors and referrals to specialized GBV actors. CARE will utilise CVA modality only in GBV response and not under protection or child protection response.

3 - Needs

Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023 indicates 15,1 million Syrians (44% children) are in need of protection services in Syria . 2022 MSNA, 73 per cent of heads of households interviewed reported that at least one household member had exhibited signs of psychological distress over the previous 30 days , moreover GBV and child marriages are still prevalent with 33 per cent of households stated that women and girls do not feel safe in specific areas of their communities and 84% of children live in locations where child marriage is an issue for girls aged between 15 and 17 years(HNO 2023) CARE’s Rapid Gender Analysis (RGA) (August 2022) conducted in Idleb and Aleppo point to traditional gender norms still prevailing in the region. 88% of household survey respondents - 47% of them women and 41% men - confirmed shared cultural expectations that women should primarily occupy the private sphere as mothers and caregivers. The domestic roles and responsibilities assigned to women and adolescent girls continue to hinder women and girls’ equitable access to services and social development opportunities in the region. In NWS top safety and security concerns identified by women and girls were identified as domestic violence, lack of privacy at home, lack of safe spaces in the community, risks associated with travel outside the community. Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 28% of surveyed households with limited MHPSS and specialized services for children.

4 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activities address complex protection concerns, in particular related to GBV risks, and fills critical service gaps. CARE will provide comprehensive protection and GBV services including (1) information provision and structured awareness raising on GBV prevention and (2) safe referrals, (3) individual and group psychosocial support (PSS) for GBV survivors and those at high risk of GBV, (4) GBV case management (5) life skills and skills building activities for women and girls as GBV prevention measures, (8) structured activities against CEFM working with adolescent girls, boys, mothers, fathers and community leaders to complement prevention and risk mitigation measures. CCs, WGSSs, helpdesks in Aleppo Jarablus district as well as Idleb, Harim,Armanaz districts with outreach teams in IDP and 'so called' widows camps and a limited number of GBV response activities in health facilities are foreseen to continue GBV SRH integration. A small component is dedicated to structured PSS activities for children to flesh out protection and GBV risks and facilitate referrals to CP specialist agencies as well as to CARE's GBV CM teams. The activities are monitored via regular reporting, data analysis, monitoring, and evaluation by IPs as well as by CARE's protection technical consultants, independent monitoring consultants and programme team in Turkey .CARE and partners will design Indicator Tracking Tables to monitor reach with AGD in line with data protection standards.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Children including adolescent girls and boys constitute a significant share of the IDP and other vulnerable populations. Many adolescents are out-of-school and lack a daily structure and perspective for the future, resulting in high psychosocial vulnerabilities and needs. Adolescents, in particular those not in education, are also at risk of being affected by different child protection concerns such as child, early, and forced marriage, child labour, being unaccompanied or separated and/or responsible for other family members, domestic violence and mental/psychological abuse of children. Adolescent girls, in particular, are also at exposed to different types of GBV, including SGBV including physical violence, emotional violence, denial of resources, opportunities or services, forced marriage (of adult women) as well as early marriage. The current COVID-19 pandemic and cholera outbreak further exacerbates GBV risks as confinement and socio-economic and psychosocial stress contribute further to pre-existing risk factors. At the same time, in the context of conflict, displacement, and the pandemic, social networks and relations that may provide protective functions are weakened, and access to services, where they are available, is further reduced.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

CARE recognizes we have a responsibility to protect people we work with, and who work for us, and we will continuously strive to prevent sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse and child abuse from happening. We take seriously all reports of sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse. Our actions are informed by a survivor-centered approach which means that the needs and wishes of survivors guide our response, that survivors are treated with dignity and respect, and the rights of survivors to privacy and support are prioritized. CARE has a zero-tolerance approach toward sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse. We will carefully examine allegations and investigate, and take appropriate disciplinary action where this is needed, taking into consideration the rights and interests of the survivor, consistent with CARE’s survivor-centered approach. We make very clear that sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse in any form, perpetrated by our staff, partners or other related personnel, towards anyone, will not be tolerated. This policy applies to all CARE Employees and Related-Personnel. CARE mitigates risks by capacitating own protection teams as well as non- specialised actors on GBV risk mitigation, including roles and responsibilities as humanitarian staff via dedicated GBV Risk Management trainings and GBV safety audits in IDP camps to address GBV risks in camp settings.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

CARE maintains strong security protocols and closely monitors conflict developments. In spite of close monitoring and strong systems and protocols, it is impossible to fully mitigate this risk. CARE works on developing strong systems, identifies growing risks trending in the context, and continues to work with UN and donors to strengthen the community-wide response to this risk. CARE also continues to directly monitor project activities at several layers at the area and country office levels. The current UN Security Council Resolution (UNSCR) 2585 expires on 10th Jan 2023. Non-renewal would have a significant impact on activities and other essential services run by UN agencies. CARE has developed a draft non-renewal contingency plan which provides details on CARE’s planning efforts, scenario forecasting, and options to adapt and scale-up programming to address related gaps and needs. Displacement may continue during the program. Protection activities utilise outreach modality in all projects to have flexibility to respond to population movements. The spread of COVID-19 and cholera outbreak does not undermine CARE's ability to conduct field activities. CARE has developed contingency plans to continue to implement during a significant outbreak in NWS while also mitigating risks to field staff. Childsafeguarding and staff code of conduct are core standards operationalized in CARE's work and dedicated staff will ensure safe programming with adults and minors.
