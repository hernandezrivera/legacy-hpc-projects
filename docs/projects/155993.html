Project Summary

The situations in East Ghouta is rapidly unfolding while uncertainty over the precise numbers in the different towns, Returnees are hence not definite and expected to increase. The current estimates put the number as ranging between 56,500 -70,850 returnees’ majority is women and children (60%). Some IDPs and returnees are residing in damaged houses randomly despite lack of windows, doors and open walls, without any access to a proper living condition or privacy. This number is subject to be increased based on the movement of IDPs back to East Ghouta. UN OCHA most recent report state that 26 K are returning from other governorates, 25 K are returning to their homes from the same governorate. RESCATE shared results of assessment missions in East Ghouta (Arbin, Duma, and Misraba) from ST and UN reports. Beneficiaries, consisting of 5550 IDPs, 2450 returnees, and families who did not leave their homes, asked for household items to establish primary living standard for cooking, and sleeping, especially that their residences were damaged and looted, besides returnees were unable to bring these items with them nor have the resources to buy them. With limited support and no means of their own, these families are in urgent need of these basic NFIs.
RESCATE will coordinate with the sector and international partners through the sector working groups as well as in coordination with local NGOs for a needs-based approach and to avoid duplication of work. The project target is to provide mattresses, high thermal blankets, and kitchen utensils kits for cooking and eating to 1500 families in the three towns, namely Arbin, Duma, and Misraba.
To address the urgent humanitarian needs through the provision of NFIs, Rescate will maintain overall oversight for NFI delivery, and will work through an established network of NGO partners to ensure distribution as follows:
In consultation with partner agencies to ensure consistency in provision of relief items, ensure rapid procurement of NFI kits in accordance local requirements, availability and in recognition of SPHERE standards
• Determine the NFI distribution mechanism in recognition of the availability of goods, the urgent needs of the population, and the operational network of NGO partners;
• identify vulnerable individuals, and prioritizing assistance for most vulnerable populations, including women and children. The distributions of NFI will take place in the geographical areas of the selected beneficiaries which will be determined in cooperation with sector partners and partner NGOs, to the most affected and vulnerable population.
• Deliver NFI kits to the most vulnerable through NGOs network; home residents who did not leave, returnees, and IDPs

Needs

• Assessments based on field visits to multiple sites revealed that there is dire need for mattresses, high thermal blankets, and cooking utensils. Many of the inhabitants in East Ghouta left their homes with few belongings and have a high need for basic domestic items. Needs assessments were carried out in various affected zones in Syria including: several towns in Rural Damascus. • To address the urgent needs of 8000 most vulnerable returnees, IDPs and families who did not leave their homes during the events, including women, children, the elderly and people with disabilities, in Syria through the provision of NFIs in coordination with established networks of UN agencies and NGOs partners. • The total project targets assistance a minimum of 8000 affected vulnerable individuals who are affected by the Syrian crisis. • Rescate NFIs distribution will take into account specific needs and vulnerabilities of children, women, the elderly and persons with disabilities. • Targeted beneficiaries are: • Returnees living in collective accommodations such as unfinished buildings, and damaged houses. • Returnees living with host families, IDPs and families who did not leave their homes. • Households facing financial insecurity, often unable to leave the areas due to lack of resources • Families hosting returnees who are stretching their resources • Minimum of 8000 most vulnerable population improved their living conditions by received NFIs assistance.

Protection Risk Analysis

There is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. In order to ensure protection mainstreaming, Rescate applies the “Do No Harm” approach. Rescate will actively work with beneficiaries, targeting the most vulnerable from the returnees, host and families who stayed in their homes, and IDPs with focus on FHHs, elderly, and PWD. Rescate will present the selection criteria, its intended target population information, and the organizations core values, mandate and vision to all beneficiaries during the allocation of the living areas. Rescate will also ensure active involvement at all times of beneficiaries and the wider community, ensuring that the activities are accessible to everyone for examination and inspection (promoting acceptance of eventual extension) and will assist beneficiaries to actively involve throughout the projects’ life cycle. During the registration, Rescate staff will ensure to take clear information about the status of each family member so that special need, if required will be directed to those in need such as, elderly, persons with specific needs, GBV survivors, and FHHs who are considered among the most vulnerable groups. Rescate will ensure mitigation measures are taken in consultation with other partners from the sector so nothing will go wrong during the implementation of the project. Rescate will provide NFI items in consultation with stakeholders and we will coordinate with relevant partners to ensure do no harm. The intervention will cover IDPs especially in Duma (5000) Arbin (200) and Misraba (350), in addition to returnees to (Duma 1500, Arbin 500 and Misraba 450) these areas reaching to (8000 individuals) based on the local communities information that these areas are under-served by local and international actors.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Rescate is aware that during the provision of humanitarian assistance may sometimes have unintended adverse effects. In collaboration with affected communities and authorities, we aim to minimize any negative effects of humanitarian action on the local community or on the environment. With respect to armed conflict, we recognize that the way in which humanitarian assistance is provided may potentially render civilians more vulnerable to attack or may potentially render unintended advantage to one or more of the parties to the conflict. We are committed to minimizing any such adverse effects. Rescate will work closely with the local communities and the local NGO acting in the area to mitigate any adverse reactions from the population, the basis of selection will be transparent and shared.To ensure the above statement, Rescate will have a dedicated staff to be responsible to monitor and ensure this obligation. Rescate will ensure mitigation measures taken if any of the negative effects arise so nothing will go wrong during the implementation of the project since the criteria will be decided in consultation with community groups. In addition, Rescate will establish a special mechanism at the Project Management Unit and will announce the address and contact details at the Mukhtar office (where the distribution will take place) to receive any complaint from the inhabitants. A special staff will take care of addressing the complaints to the satisfaction of the individuals involved. Not only that, but Rescate staff at the PMU will be an arm to ensure “Do No Harm” principal is definitely in place during the project life cycle.
