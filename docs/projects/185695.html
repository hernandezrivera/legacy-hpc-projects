Project Summary

Green Hill (GH) has already established a health post in camp 1W (UID# 418) and is currently implementing Community Health activities in camp 1W and 4 providing continuum of care from community to facility. Through this health post comprehensive and specialized health services will be provided, including but not limited to maternal & child health/sexual & reproductive health (SRH) with a focus on availability of long-acting reversible contraception, communicable and non communicable diseases, emergency care, immunization, laboratory, pharmacy and GBV/MHPSS/disabilities services & referrals. All activities will be implemented with a focus on PSEA, accountability to affected populations, protection, age, gender and diversity, disaster risk management and environmental ecosystem with the concept of do no harm. The Health post and Community Health program jointly will scale up availability and equitable access to a range of preventative and curative services.
The semi-permanent HP will operate 6 days a week for 8 hours a day. The HP will serve approx. 12,000 beneficiaries where there is currently a gap of one Health Post and one Primary Health Center as per the recent gap analysis conducted by the Health Sector. The HP will be staffed with a Medical Team Lead, medical doctors, midwife, nurse, medical assistant, pharmacist, dispenser, laboratory technologist, data collector, and support staff. In camps 1W and 4, we have 65 trained Community Health Workers/Volunteers providing health education and awareness, referrals with emphasis on SRH services and facility based deliveries, maternal mortality surveillance/verbal autopsy surveillance, and routine EPI referrals. We will continue capacity building of the same CHVs in both camps in 2022. Green Hill is an active member of the Health Sector, Community Health Working Group, SRH Working Group and Epidemiological Working Group.
Patient information at HP will be managed through DHIS-2 and EWARS. Ongoing patient feedback will also be collected to measure quality and patient satisfaction with services. GH will coordinate with the Health Sector, Government Authorities (Civil Surgeon, UH&FPO, CIC, Site Management, RRRC, UNO and DC office), ISCG, relevant cross-cutting sectors, and working groups.

1 - Identified Needs

At least one health post is needed for a population of 10,000 beneficiaries, whereas in Camp 1W, block A and B comprises more than 11,000 beneficiaries without a Health post according to the recent gap analysis conducted by the Health Sector. Additional gap analysis identified the need for one health post in Camp 1W requiring the FDMN population to travel long distances through difficult terrain to access health care facilities in neighboring camps. The rate of facility-based deliveries in Camp 1W are relatively low and vaccination drop out is high compared with other camps. SRH Services: Access to essential comprehensive reproductive, maternal, and newborn health services is a major concern in Camp 1W & 4. Community feedback demonstrates that the husbands or in-laws often make decisions for pregnant women. GH will emphasize including the household decision makers in the door-to-door awareness sessions through CHVs. Family planning needs remain unaddressed, particularly for the most vulnerable populations which will be a focused area for GH community health programming and health post. NCD and specialized Services: Availability of NCD treatment and specialized services (eye-care, geriatric care, oral healthcare and services for people with disabilities and palliative needs) remain limited which will be addressed through the HP. Health promotion and health education: In the past, there were outbreaks of diphtheria, cholera, measles, and varicella. Early health promotion messaging is needed to prevent these outbreaks. Awareness messaging on vaccination, hygiene, nutrition, waste and water management, clean drinking water, etc. is essential to improve the overall health condition. COVID-19 Awareness: Rumors and misconceptions regarding COVID-19 continue to effect health seeking behavior of the FDMN community. Still there is high demand for continuation of risk communication and community engagement messaging, COVID-19 contact tracing, and referrals for cases and contacts.
