Project Summary

The proposed twelve months’ project will be implemented in Pibor County, Jonglei state, South Sudan. The project will directly target a total household number of 7,500 (2250HHs men of age 19-65, 3000HHs women of age 19-65, 1500HHs girls of age 12-15 and 750HHs boys of age 14-18). 3,750 households (1125HHs men of age 19-70,1500HHs women of age 19-70, 750HHs girls of age 12-15, and 375HHs Boys of age 14-18 will benefit in term of cash transfer (Conditional/unrestricted and unconditional/unrestricted). 2,250 households (675 men of age 19-70, 900 women of age 19-70, 450 girls of age 12-15 and 225 boys of age 14-18 will benefit from seeds and agricultural tools, and 1,500 Households (450 men of age 19-70, 600 women of age 19-70, 300 girls of age 12-15 and 150 boys of age 14-18) will benefit from fishing gears in Payams of Pibor Central, Lekuangole and Verteth, Pibor County, Jonglei State. Beneficiaries for seeds, tools and fishing gears are clustered into 750 groups of 30 members per group. Cash assistance will be given to the most vulnerable households in all proposed project locations and will include both Unconditional/Unrestricted and Conditional/Unrestricted cash transfers. The tool for selection of the beneficiaries will be developed and jointly applied by the local authorities and other stake holders on grounds of the most vulnerable groups drawn IDPs, returnees and host communities. Specific focus on single-headed Households, child headed households, PwDs, GBV survivors and persons with special needs who will be prioritized in selection. All these groups will be engaged into Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs) for households to save and borrow loans from within their savings to cater for immediate household needs and run their own businesses and at the same time will help to sustain the established groups. AADO intends to support the beneficiaries by providing agricultural inputs (Seeds, Tools and Fishing gears), provision of cash assistance (Conditional/Unrestricted and Unconditional/Unrestricted), training women, men, girls and Boys, youth groups on VSLAs, capacity building on improved farming and fishing practices, integrated pests management (IPM) including trainings on Fall Army Worm (FAW), community sensitizations on Climate Change and associated disasters, train traders/venders and Youth on business skills, record keeping, plans, provision of market linkages for farmers’/fisher folks’ produce and products, market strengthening through improving market structures like stores, market stalls, conduct Baseline surveys, post distribution monitoring (PDM). Gender Based Violence (GBV) and COVID-19 awareness will be mainstreamed into implementation. At the end of this project, the households are expected to be food secure in terms of accessibility, sufficiency (quantities and quality), balanced and nutritious enough to guarantee a healthy living and increased household income due to VSLA Schemes and/or income generating activities. Fewer cases of malnutrition and diseases associated with lack of dietary diversity is expected. Reduction in the level of poverty amongst the vulnerable households. The project envisages households with sustainable sources of incomes from both crops, livestock and non- farming activities. This should be able to support the families to erect their own improved shelters, access food, send their children to school, and meet basic health needs as well as clothing.

1 - Needs

Pibor County has remained in serious humanitarian crisis due to cumulative effects of years of armed conflicts, floods, ethnic confrontations, livestock diseases and plant pests and disease outbreaks which have destroyed people’s livelihoods and displaced thousands of people and livestock in these communities. Assessment conducted in Pibor County reported 39,621 people and 315,962 heads of livestock displaced (Inter-Cluster Assessment Report Pibor, Sept. 21-22, 2020). With all these, food security situation is expected to deteriorate in Pibor County during January to July 2021 projection period as household food stocks start to deplete, access to livestock products and wild foods reduce. An estimated 6.35 million, representing 52.6% of the regional population will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity and 2.102 million people face Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Emergency outcomes are expected to persist in Pibor County due to expectation of low availability of harvest, limited access to markets e.g. fish given expectation of continued insecurity and tensions amongst the communities. Overall, an estimated 11,000 people, representing 84.7 percent of the state’s population will be in Crisis and Emergency phases during the harvest period. The post-harvest period of January to April 2021, an estimated 380,000 people, 26.2% of the population is projected to be in Crisis or worse acute food insecurity as harvests deplete and food prices begin to rise in the markets. In Pibor county, incidents of insecurity are likely to persist which will disrupt livelihoods, affect trade and access to markets. Pibor is anticipated to be the most food insecure county (Greater Pibor Administrative Area), Jonglei State. Food insecurity remains the main concern in the project areas. Needs remain much higher than the overall humanitarian response can address. WFP food distributions were conducted in all targeted areas, but only target approximately 25% of people in need. 69% of households in GPAA have access to land for farming, but didn’t even manage to produce an average of 2.9 months of food for the household per year (IRNA 21-22 Sept, 2020 Pibor County). Acute food insecurity is driven by conflict, low crop production, cattle raiding, presence of IDPs and influx of returnees, high food prices, restricted market access, poor market functionality and poor road conditions. The project will address two key drivers of continued cyclical food insecurity; food availability and access to food. Reduced availability of food is largely due to a poor cereal harvest caused by severe floods all over GPAA in 2020. By this trend, food stocks will last less than 4 months in 2021. Availability of other sources of food, such as wild foods and fish has also been negatively affected by the dry spells (FSNMS- Round 28, August-December, 2020). There is urgent need to increase access to food for the most-in need population and ensure cash transfer, seeds, tools and fishing kits are provided. Evidence from past AADO’s project proves that cash transfer programming results in over 80% improvement in access to food (PDM Results, Feb 2021). Combination of fishing and vegetable kits provision and accompanying training support translates in to increased number of meals, amount of food consumed, and varieties of food at household disposal by 79% (PDM Results, Feb 2021). The project will provide Conditional and Unconditional cash transfers and livelihood inputs to vulnerable households. Highly vulnerable households will be provided with cash assistance to meet their immediate food needs while securing crop seeds for the main season of planting that begins in May/June.

2 - Project activities

CO1:Cash & voucher-based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives: This will target the most vulnerable households in all targeted locations to enable them cater for household basic needs like; food, shelter, medication, education and shelter. AADO has a vulnerability selection criterion which will be thoroughly explained to the established cash distribution committees (CDCs) members and the local authorities before beneficiary selection process starts. IRRM: food, fishing & quick maturing vegetable seeds: Fishing kits and fast-growing vegetable kits will be distributed to groups. Demo farms will be established for practical skills acquisition and fish kiln oven will be constructed for preservation of the fish harvested.Conditional cash transfers: This activity targets able bodied individuals from targeted households who are physical fit and willing to do any work given to him/her. Before the selection is done, the program team sits down with local authorities to have discussions on which public asset is to be rehabilitated.CO2:Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits. Distribution tokens will be distributed to registered beneficiaries one week before actual distribution. Train farmers and fisher folk groups on improved farming practices. This training will be done for each group at the community level and will be facilitated by AADO staff. It will be done in line with topics under improved farming practices that includes crop agronomy and farming as a business spread over times.Train farmers on crop protection and post-harvest handling and management. The training will focus on integrated pest management (IPM), Fall Army Worm (FAW) including post-harvest handling and management techniques to reduce on crop losses.Establishment of demonstration farms for practical learning. This will be used for demonstrating technologies which are then replicated or adapted by the farmers.CO3:Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc. The target farmers will be trained on farming as a business (analysis of farm accounts and records, enterprise selection including production and marketing plan-PMP). Selected farmers especially women will be enrolled in to vocational skills such as hair dressing, saloon, and bakery business. Diversification & restoration of livelihoods; small grants for petty trade; village saving & loan schemes & micro finance etc. Farmers and fisher folks’ groups will be guided, couched and trained on business skills and business plans development to help explain the general trend of their IGAs based on selected enterprises and this will form the basis of sub grants to the groups for operation and running of petty trade. Further, the groups will be engaged in VSLAs so that they can save and lend small money within their savings to meet their immediate household needs such as food, cloths, school fees, medical bills.Use of local biomass briquettes & fuel-efficient stoves. In order to address the issues of climate change and associated disasters such as floods, drought, selected beneficiaries will be given practical training on construction of locally made fuel efficient cook stoves. This will help to reduce cutting down of trees and emission of greenhouse gases which is one of the major causes of climate change. Trainings and interventions to promote market access. Producer groups will be guided and trained on collective marketing of their produce and products. Marketing committees will be formed to and trained on marketing skills such as business planning, market linkages, customer care etc. to help market easy market accessibility by the producer groups. Framer groups will also be guided towards common enterprises to enhance collective marketing.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Across Africa Development organization (AADO) normally conducts market assessments before projects are designed since cash interventions don't do well where markets are not functional. The package for the assessment includes traders survey with key informants (KII) and focus group discussion (FGDs) targeting both female and males traders per market. Depending on the findings, one can decide to either choose to include cash assistance or not during the project design phase, and after venturing there, bi-weekly price market monitoring and Joint market Mapping Initiative (JMMI) is taken into consideration to continue finding out how the markets operate in terms of commodity prices and other factors.

Before venturing into cash and voucher interventions, assessments are conducted find out whether the assistance will be accepted by the target community or not. With cash one can cater for all household basic needs like food, shelter, medication, education and clothings which in-kind assistance can't cover. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AADO acknowledges the importance of mainstreaming protection into all projects through awareness raising and advocacy for food security and livelihoods information to reach the beneficiaries/population. AADO coordinates with other food security and livelihood partners to establish and strengthen information sharing in all the three project locations of Pibor Center, Lekuangole and Verteth in Pibor County, Jonglei State which acts as service points. This will enable the vulnerable households to access Agricultural extension services which are brought closure to the community. Besides, through the cash distribution committees (CDCs), project steering Committees (PSCs) with help of AADO protection Committees, the FSL team on the ground will conduct Monthly awareness raising on protection/GBV prevention, protection, mitigation and response which will include services offered and service providers. AADO continues to use Age Gender Diversity Mainstreaming-AGDM approach which is disaggregated by sex and age for inclusiveness all categories of the beneficiaries. The project is design in a way that awareness raising and advocacy is the core mode of disseminating information to the beneficiaries on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). The Cash distribution committees which will formed and trained on the concepts of basic protection, GBV and PSEA will be at the front-line to create a relation between the beneficiaries and other stakeholders as food security and livelihood rode. The core role of the Cash distribution committees (CDCs) and the project steering committees (PSCs) will be formed from the grassroots under recommendation of the community leaders, religious leaders and other community structures to bring services closure to the communities. They have the geographical knowledge with their capacities built, will be an added advantage of beneficiaries’ identification and sense of understanding depending on which communities can easily relate to. AADO will conduct monthly community awareness raising and advocacy through open campaigns, road drives and FGDs which will enable beneficiaries to receive information throughout the project implementation. AADO will as well use IEC materials; T-shirts, caps, banners and talking compounds to disseminate PSEA information in English and all the local languages to enable understanding of the key messaging on PSEA. AADO has zero tolerance to PSEA AND adheres to Secretary-General’s Bulletin on special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and sexual abuse (ST/SGB/2003/13).

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

AADO has mechanisms through which feedback and complaints can be received in a safe and confidential manner and responded to promptly. The specific channels through which complaints are communicated vary between sites, and are decided through community consultations. So as to not marginalize vulnerable community members, more than one channel is available, focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. Examples include one-on-one meetings at offices, and discussing with a staff member during community events. Mechanisms are designed to minimize physical barriers due to age, disability, or distance, and ensure discretion and confidentiality. Focal points have been identified and trained at all sites to lead on Community Complaints/Accountability Response Mechanisms, including listening to and reporting feedback and complaints, ensuring timely response, and training communities on use and purpose of these mechanisms.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

AADO has experience of flexible and adaptive programming in the target areas to manage volatility, uncertainty and risks. In terms of specific security risks, AADO has dedicated Safety and Security focal persons who will jointly inform project implementation and support the programme leadership to continually assess and analyses the situation, revise the risk analysis and take any appropriate measures deemed necessary to protect staff and beneficiaries. In order to mitigate and control risk listed above where possible; AADO will draw upon its existing standard operating procedures (SOPs) which will be harmonized during inception. AADO risk register will be used as the program’s primary risk tool and will be reviewed and updated every quarter and will be based on information collected through field teams as well as from external (public and confidential) sources that will be collated to inform decision making based on mitigation measures. In the case of a risk escalating during a quarter, the Project Manager will coordinate with top management to advice on an appropriate response. The risk register will be used to inform the development of joint Scenario Based Contingency Planning for each implementation area during inception.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The recent revitalized peace agreement deal between the warring parties of national government herein referred to as “incumbent government” and government in opposition referred to as “IO” has resulted in some safety and stability within most counties in South Sudan. This has provided opportunities for people to free movement to return to their homes. Movement options for inhabitants have been widening to enable access to affected populations who are in most needs. For example, large numbers of IDPs and returnees in Pibor County. Engagements between opposition and government actors have paved way to allow unhindered access and movement of humanitarian workers and items. The work of local institutions of police and community leaders are gaining traction. Although there still exist prevalence of revenge killing by communities, the buy-in of different protection measures by armed groups even in volatile conditions are conducive for delivery of humanitarian assistance. The project has integrated gender and protection within its activities to ensure that no harm is done to the individuals and communities served considering gender sensitivity. As a feedback mechanism when accountability to affected population is put in place at all times, interventions are likely to heed the best interest of the people and ensuring that the people are served with dignity.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

High levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and other hygiene related diseases. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high food insecurity needs. Mothers and care takers of malnourished children lack basic hygiene knowledge and access to sufficient nutritious food which hampers the benefits gained in nutrition programs.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The high rates of malnutrition are attributed to several factors including high levels of food insecurity, poor infant and young child feeding practices as well as high morbidity due to a weak health system in the country. Poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum dietary diversity: 15%) and high prevalence of diseases (up to 40%). Elevated levels of food insecurity (IPC AFI Phase 3 and above) in counties also contribute to acute malnutrition. Furthermore, poor access to health and nutrition services during the rainy season due to poor road infrastructure constrain service delivery to areas already afflicted by high malnutrition levels. Therefore, by diversifying household’s dietary meals through distribution of plum nuts to children, other nutrition-dense foods such as fruits, dairy, vegetables, fish etc. that have sufficient food values such as proteins, carbohydrates, fats, calcium, and folates will also be given to such households with malnourished children.
