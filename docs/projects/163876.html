Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to reduce the burden of leishmaniasis in northern Syria via provision of diagnostic and treatment services in existing health facilities (HFs), operation of leishmaniasis mobile clinics(MCs) for areas with no HF with capacity to provide such services, strengthening of case reporting by HFs to ensure robust epidemiological data, capacity building of health care workers (HCWs) in leishmaniasis case management via training and implementation of Information, Education and Communication (IEC) and Behavior and Communication Change (BCC) activity to increase awareness of prevention measures for leishmaniasis and availability of treatment services.
MENTOR supports HFs with supplies, equipment and drugs to diagnose and treat cutaneous (CL) and visceral (VL) leishmaniasis. MENTOR is currently supporting 79 HFs across northern Syria. MENTOR additionally supplies and monitors use of reporting tools in each HF - designed in collaboration with WHO – to accurately record the trend of leishmaniasis in the region. MENTOR employs medical officers (MOs) who visit each supported HF at least once per week to monitor data collection, stock levels and consumption and to provide on-the-job training and mentoring for HCWs. HF staff are also encouraged to contact their assigned MENTOR MO to discuss and seek advice regarding any complex cases. Prior to supporting a HF for the first time, MENTOR ensures to train the staff how to diagnose, treat and record leishmaniasis data appropriately. MENTOR also organizes centralized intensive leishmaniasis 2-day training for HCWs from all supported HFs each year.
All data is shared by MENTOR with WHO, EWARN, health cluster, HWG and submitted to 4W and HeRAMS reporting. MENTOR also operates MCs dedicated to leishmaniasis, as well as supporting the MCs of partner organizations. MENTOR is currently operating 9 leishmaniasis MCs in northern Syria and supporting 2 MCs of partner organizations. MENTOR intends to expand its current medical and IEC activities during 2020 in areas of marked high incidence such as Deir ez Zor governorate.
MENTOR expects in 2020 to diagnose and treat 49,000 cases of CL and reach 100,000 beneficiaries via IEC and BCC activity.
VL is a fatal infection predominantly affecting children < 5 years, with malnourishment a known risk factor. Although NWS is the main area of VL transmission in Syria, MENTOR has recently diagnosed a case in a 2-year old child in Deir ez Zor in December 2019, highlighting the potential for an outbreak of this epidemic-prone infection in NES. Epidemic-prone diseases are highlighted in the 2020 HNO and HRP health sector chapter as areas of key importance. MENTOR’s project directly addresses control of leishmaniasis – one of the epidemic prone diseases of greatest current morbidity in Syria, as is specified in the most recent HNO health sector chapter.
The psychological and physical impact of CL is greater in children and women of reproductive age – priority groups highlighted in the 2020 HRP health sector draft. VL affects predominantly young children <5. MENTOR directs its limited resources to the areas in northern Syria with the highest incidence of leishmaniasis. MENTOR’s allocation of resources is data driven, based on robust incidence data collected by our medical and data staff in the field.
Control of leishmaniasis requires early case detection and treatment, good disease surveillance, and integrated vector management (IVM) prevention activities across the areas with high disease transmission, together with comprehensive information and education campaigns.
For 2020, to cover all proposed activities, MENTOR’s budget is $2,287,643. MENTOR expected funding for 2020 covers 84% of this budget.

4 - Needs

CL is highlighted in health chapter of HNO as 1 of top morbidities in Syria. Emphasis also placed on need for surveillance and response and joint effort with WASH actors to prevent and control disease outbreaks. MENTOR – in collaboration with WHO – strengthens data recording for CL ensuring early outbreak detection. MENTOR conducts extensive prevention activities - submitted separately under WASH sector, but implemented in coordination with medical activities. MENTOR recognizes that persons with disability disproportionately lack access to health services and works to address this gap through coordination with local authorities and provision of mobile clinic services (more info in PRA section). MENTOR has added a question related to disability status in our data collection tools for 2020 which will provide insight into how disability status intersects with both risk and severity of leishmaniasis. MENTOR also increases capacity of HFs and staff through training, supervision and supply of medicines and serves displaced and hard to reach communities via MCs, both priority needs outlined in HNO health chapter. MENTOR operates in all accessible areas of Idleb, Aleppo, Hassakeh, Raqqa and Deir ez Zor (DeZ) governorates. In 2019, MENTOR diagnosed and treated 39, 051 new cases of CL in northern Syria, and conducted 312,478 treatment consultations. 50.3 % of cases were female, and 66% were children. 21% were children <5 years. MENTOR’s data show high incidence of leishmaniasis across all governorates. The caseload is currently markedly high in NES. MENTOR diagnosed and treated 2,136 cases of CL in DeZ during December alone. Transmission of VL is now also occurring in NES (MENTOR data), as of December 2019, raising concerns of a potential outbreak in this area. Availability of leishmaniasis services for the recently displaced population in NWS – an area with continuous high incidence of CL and the epicenter of VL in Syria - will also be of vital importance in 2020.

5 - Implementation of activities

MENTOR implements its medical program via support to existing HFs & operation of CL treatment MCs. MENTOR provides IEC materials for HFs & beneficiaries, and conducts IEC & BCC sessions to raise awareness re leishmaniasis services & prevention measures. MENTOR medical officers (MOs) meet regularly with local health authorities. MENTOR coordinates with representatives of governorate directorates of health in NWS & Turkish authorities & has signed MoUs for working in Turkish controlled areas of Syria. MENTOR’s liaison staff in NES coordinate with local authorities to secure approvals for work in each area. Sustainability of service - MENTOR supports capacity building through training & supervision of nurses, doctors, lab staff & donation of lab materials like microscopes. MENTOR’s program is flexible to meet needs of IDPs via MCs as well as provision of services to insecure areas via remote management strategy of support. This strategy ensures safety of our staff & provision of services to those in most hard to reach areas. MENTOR receives weekly data from remotely supported HFs including case consultation and stock consumption information as well as hard copies once per month. MENTOR’s MOs communicate on at least weekly basis with focal point from each remotely supported HF. MENTOR’s MOs visit each directly supported HF at least once weekly to monitor data collection, stock consumption and provide on-the-job training and monitor treatment outcomes. MENTOR also employs M&E staff who conduct regular HF spot checks each week and yearly KAP surveys. M&E staff visit each HF at minimum each 3 months to check stock, availability of leish services, IEC materials, staff behavior and to interview staff and beneficiaries. MENTOR maintains continuous monitoring in relation to medical & IEC activities, assets, resources, & security & engages with 3rd party monitoring as required by donors. By doing so we obtain QA on our logistics, finance & operations in the field.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. PSEA: MENTOR’s code of conduct (CoC) describes standard behavior for staff and specifically forbids SEA. All new employees must read and sign this CoC. Beneficiaries are made aware of MENTOR staff responsibilities and their opportunity to report breaches of the CoC through our Accountability to Affected Populations framework which provides reporting mechanisms and focal points. 2. Gender Based Violence – Where possible (in some parts of NWS context does not allow), MENTOR employs a female staff member in each of our MCs, offering a safe environment for female beneficiaries to receive care. 6 out of 9 of MENTOR’s MCs are currently staffed with both male and female HCWs. 3. Provision of healthcare for Vulnerable Groups and equitable access to health servicesMENTOR ensures that all its supported HFs and its MCs provide service free to all beneficiaries, irrespective of gender, religion, ethnicity or cultural beliefs. MENTOR mitigates the risk of exclusion of marginalized and vulnerable groups such as Bedouins and persons with disabilities by operating MCs which are flexible and able to provide services to remote locations as well as IDP camps. MENTOR’s medical staff coordinate with the health authorities/civil councils so that they are aware of MENTOR’s services and can inform us if there are patients with disabilities or other impediments preventing them from traveling to a HF or a MC location. MENTOR is able to adapt its schedule to provide treatment to individual patients or groups on a case by case basis. 4. Severity and Vulnerability – MENTOR is intervening in all of the highest severity sub-districts across northern Syria including Hajin and Thiban sub-districts where we are the only international NGO currently operating. Our areas of operation are guided by incidence of leishmaniasis and therefore correlate with severity scale approach.5. MENTOR also has duty of care policy in place for our staff to cover unforeseen relocation, forced termination etc

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. MENTOR engages with the health cluster in NW, HWG in NES and WHO to ensure there is no duplication in programming. 2. MENTOR’s MOs engage with community leaders & conduct regular meetings with local health authorities (LHAs) to ensure to cover needs of beneficiaries. MENTOR places feedback and suggestion boxes in every MC location. MENTOR’s M&E team conduct surveys and interview beneficiaries to receive feedback. MENTOR has a Whatsapp number visible on all IEC materials. Our IEC staff engage regularly with beneficiaries through their activities and feedback information and suggestions. 3. Potential threats and mitigating measuresa. Security situation no longer allows direct access –MENTOR will switch where possible to remote management strategyb. HCWs unmotivated to collaborate with MENTOR and complete data tools - MENTOR will meet with manager of HF and LHA to request supportc. Continued population displacements in NWS and loss of access to previously supported HFs – MENTOR will adapt program to operate more MCs to cover new IDP camps and informal settlementsd. Loss of access to Amuda office and warehouse in NES – MENTOR will manage its supply chain from our Raqqa warehouse and office and then open new office and warehouse in Hassekah city.4. MENTOR follows Logistics Procedures to help prevent any cases of waste, fraud and abuse of assets, funds and supplies Thorough inventories are maintained of all stocks and assets in line with the Logistics Procedures with regular and spot checks conducted. As policy, MENTOR conducts background checks on all staff members, agencies or organizations with which MENTOR formally engages with (including all service agreement, MoUs etc.) and procurement orders (over USD500). MENTOR also conducts third party monitoring using external agencies to conduct QA on our logistics, finance and operations in the field. This includes inventory and asset checks.
