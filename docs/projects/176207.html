Project Summary

Through this project, Action Against Hunger is aiming to target 118988 (60727 males and 58261 females children, elderly and adults) of vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and Host community in the areas of Fangak, and Canal/Pigi of Jonglei State, Gogrial East, Gogrial West and Tonj East counties of Warrap state and Aweil East, Aweil Center and Aweil South counties of Northern Bahr el Ghazal State to improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations from 1st January to 31st December 2021.
We will use a modality of Primary health services, (PHCC, PHCU, and Mobile Clinic response packages) in collaboration with CHD and other health partners. We will support non functional health facilities under the CHD to achieve the following objectives:
Sectoral Objective 1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities
Sectoral Objective 2. Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems
Sectoral Objective 3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly.
Action Against Hunger response activities will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, protection and FSL services. We will take into consideration the protection area by providing the first aid care to the SGBV victims and MHPSS as well as identifying and strengthening the referral pathway for clinical management of rape and mental health cases. Our response will focuses on both IDPs, and host communities. With the high mortality rate of children under 5 (94.53/1000), maternal mortality rate-MMR (789/100,000) people with disabilities and mental disorders are included as vulnerable groups owing to their high risk during emergencies. The response plan will be prioritized based on epidemiological prole of disease outbreaks, cholera hot spot locations, measles outbreaks and malaria seasonality. Response modalities will be static and rapid response mechanism in an integrated approach. IDSR, EWARS, attacks on health care and disability tracking tools will be used to determine the feasibility of the response. Mobile response mechanisms will comprised up to 50 % of the response.
Our operational plans will always involve the community members, women girls youths and PLWDs (people living with disability) County Health department (CHD) in planning and organization of health services. Meetings will be conducted with the communities and the CHD are some of the indicators used to monitor the accountability for affected populations. Meetings will be facilitate with all stakeholders like partners, beneciaries, women groups and local communities will be involve in the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects in all stages of the program cycle. Due to increment on the levels of stability in the country, the refugees are coming back to the country resulting into an influx of 300,000 refugee returnees as per UNHCR South Sudan spontaneous household survey, April to June 2020. This will lead to continue increase humanitarian challenges in 2021 compared to the previous years. People in Need of humanitarian services are 8.3 million and 5.2 million are in need for health services. Action Against Hunger will mobilize and contribute funds to HRP for 2021 project for population of 118988 within areas of our intervention.

1 - Needs

As per South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot released by OCHA in January 2021, people in South Sudan continued to suffer with high humanitarian needs. Some 8.3 million—over two-thirds of the total population—are estimated to need humanitarian assistance in 2021 according to the findings reflected in the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview. This is an increase of 800,000 people from 7.5 million people in need in 2020. Some 5.82 million people, almost half of the population, are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worsening acute food insecurity in January, including 11,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity category in Pibor County. Sub-national violence and insecurity continued to displace people in Central and Western Equatoria, Upper Nile and Warrap. Ongoing sub-national violence in Tonj North County, Warrap, displaced thousands to Tonj South and Gogrial counties in Warrap. Almost 19,000 displaced people arrived in Gogrial East County. Clashes between an organized armed group and cattle keepers in Lainya County, Central Equatoria, displaced more than 9,400 people. In Western Equatoria, violence displaced some 9,000 people in Mundri East County, while an estimated one thousand fled their homes in Yambio County following tensions between an armed pastoralists group and the local community. An estimated 15,000 people were displaced by fighting in Maban, Upper Nile, while another 6,000 people reportedly crossed into Sudan and Ethiopia. Since April 2020, 3,929 people have tested positive for COVID-19.The increase in needs is largely driven by the rising food insecurity. When consulted, food insecurity or lack of food was identified as the primary challenge or one of the primary challenges faced by the majority of affected people across sex and age groups. Needs do not exist in a vacuum, however, as food insecurity weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. Similarly, lack of access to clean water increases the likelihood of waterborne diseases and malnutrition.As per 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview, around 5.2 million people are in need of life saving essential health care services.Action Against Hunger will ensure qualified health workers are mobilized to deliver essential healthcare services, and scale up response to vulnerable population, both in local community, and return refugees with the involvement of community stakeholders, chiefs, and women groups. The initial plan will be shared with the stakeholders to finalize and implement the project. COVID-19 preventive measures and guidance from the ministry of health and WHO will be integrated in all the project activities.

2 - Project activities

This project will focus on the following Activities: 1- OPD services that will provide consultation and treatment for common illnesses such simple malaria with ACT, Acute water diarrhoea, (AWD) with ORS and zinc sulfate tablets (for children U5), acute respiratory infections (ARI) Amoxicillin, active case finding for TB/HIV defaulters, Visceral Leishmaniosis (Kala-Azar) and refer such cases to the nearest TB-HIV or Kala-Azar treatment center on time. 2- Antenatal care and maternity services, providing the mothers with ferrous + folic acid, Albendazole for deworming, screening of pregnant mothers of Syphilis, HIV and other STIs, rapid test for malaria (RDTs) support of normal deliveries in health facilities and community using UNFPA commodities and referral of complicated pregnancies and deliveries to the nearest hospitals for emergency surgical intervention.3- Immunization services to respond to outbreak such as measles vaccination and Vitamin A supplement (targeting 6 months to 15 years and through engaging UNICEF to provide Artech, refrigerator for hard to reach areas and refer the rest of children under 5 years and women of child bearing ages to receive routine immunization in static and outreach EPI sessions.4- Detect, investigate, report and respond to outbreaks example of cholera, measles, COVID-19, EVD in the areas of target through strengthening EWARS/IDSR system in training and coordination with stakeholders (Health Cluster-WHO, MoH and CHD).5- Identication, provision of psychological support and first aid treatment to the SGBV victims as well as referral of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) through protection partners in the areas of our operation. Action Against Hunger's facility in Paguir Payam of Fangak county have a qualified medical doctor and Midwives with the advanced training on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR). We will use our existing project in Paguir to boost up the HRP project activities.6- Screening of MUAC in health facilities and outreach sessions for children under 5, pregnant women and lactating mothers-PLWs (PHCC, PHCU and Mobile outreaches) through family MUAC approach.7- Identification of PLWDs (People Living with Disabilities), mentally ill persons among the community and refer them to specialized centers with specialists after giving them basic health care at our health facilities. Action Against Hunger is capable to provide humanitarian response to emerging crisis that are anticipated in 2021. We will use our presence in 3 states to contribute to the strategic plan of health cluster, WHO and ministry of health in the year 2021. These activities will tailor to achieve the health cluster strategic objectives to:I. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities in the country.II. Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations Fangak county of Jonglei state, Gogrial East and West Counties Warrap of state and Aweil East and Aweil Center of Northern Bahr El Ghazal StateIII. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders.Reporting tools will be ERWARS weekly report, IDSR, monthly HMIS and 5Ws to Health Cluster, WHO, CHD and MoH. All these forms will be availed to the field staff in all locations and make sure all indicators are captured and any outbreak investigation will be recorded and reported during the implementation.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Action Against Hunger trains all of its staff and volunteers on the organization’s PSEA Policy. During our mobilization meetings, Action Against Hunger shares information about PSEA from the organization’s PSEA Policy. During project implementation, the organization regularly shares PSEA information with beneficiaries as reminder/refresher to emphasize the premium the organizations places Zero Tolerance for PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our integrated program (Health, Nutrition, WaSH and FSL programs). All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specifically asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements. In each of our project sites, we avail five FCM modalities as “minimum standards”: 1. Monthly staff meetings with first-line implementers, 2. Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries, 3. Individual interview form, 4. Comment Box at Field Office Gate and nutrition facilities, 5. Anonymous Hotline Number. The identities of all feedback and complaints mechanism (FCM) complainants will be kept strictly confidential by program management staff when provided. We have standard, context appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers), which are displayed at all project sites and on all project structures (e.g. equipment used by beneficiaries). IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals. During all community meetings, trainings, and sensitization sessions, a standard script will be used by staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities. Depending on the urgency, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementers during the monthly meetings. Specific responses will also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the national level. Urgent issues will be addressed immediately by the Head of Base and Program Managers through appropriate channels.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Action Against Hunger implements a comprehensive, gender-sensitive feedback mechanism. This feedback mechanism uses a range of modalities to solicit feedback from frontline program implementers (including volunteers and field staff), local communities in which programs are operating, and direct program beneficiaries. While the feedback mechanism is firmly institutionalized, the organization continues to examine how to best track and respond to the feedback received, and how to integrate this process into its M&E strategy.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Action Against Hunger considers the feedback from beneficiaries as an instrument to improve the organization’s acceptance, and improvement of our interventions in the community. Hence, all feedback from beneficiaries are analyzed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project as we place emphasis on the organization being a learning entity.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To prevent instigation of SEA and other forms of GBV by leading gender-targeted interventions, Action Against Hunger will aim to sensitize all gender and age groups on project activities before their implementation. All our project activities are designed to safely challenge harmful gender dynamics in a context-appropriate manner. Results of gender analyses will directly inform project design and implementation strategy. All staff implementing GBV referral pathway awareness, Men Group Discussion facilitation, Psychological First Aid, and any other sensitive discussion with target community members will be instructed on our mandatory policy for maintaining the identity of project participants strictly confidential. Data related to GBV and other sensitive topics will be anonymized in organizational databases to preserve the privacy of survivors. Moreover, survivors or reporters will not be solicited for further participation/discussion. Any GBV case concerning staff or beneficiaries which proceed to investigation will only involve the Country Program Ethics Committee, consisting of the Country Director, Head of Logistics and Head of Operations. All information will be confidential with access restricted to those personnel. We will train key stakeholders on the use of the feedback mechanism at each project location. Additionally, the organization will print complaint procedures in the local language and in simple English. The project team will regularly review the use of the feedback mechanism and information gathered from the review process will be used to make improvements. A diverse set of FCM modalities ensures that differing gender, age and ability groups have access.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Action Against Hunger provides integrated services to beneficiaries at community level. Key protection services are incorporated within our programs which include GBV focal persons identified at all facilities; training of staffs on GBV risk mitigation and available referral pathways and services; provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA) to GBV survivors approaching the project teams; hiring more female staffs in the project team and committees formed at community/village level; conducting safety audits at our project sites etc.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The production of more food, promotion of resilient activities will go a long way in supporting populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5. Enhancing the productivity of people in need of humanitarian aid will reduce the number of people in IPC 3, 4 and 5 as they become self-reliant and continue to fend for themselves. This will also include sensitization of the community on potential GBV threats and available referral pathways and services.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Action Against Hunger has a policy on fair provision of cross-sectoral/integrated services to all sections of the affected population including women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities and special needs. The program will follow this policy and all sections of the affected population will be served according to their needs. The provision of services will be based on the organization’s needs/vulnerability analysis.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The overall food security program is aimed at providing sustainable sources of foods which will positively impact a reduction in the level of malnutrition in our intervention areas. Our nutrition sensitive agriculture activities are specifically intended to reduce the GAM rate among the U5 children. This is complemented by our intervention in the nutrition programs being implemented by Action Against Hunger at static nutrition and outreach sites. This will also lead to reduction in exposure to potential GBV risks for the beneficiaries and their caretakers.
