1 - Needs

Stability and security in the three targeted areas under this remain precarious due to different contexts current Sudan is passing, such as peace agreement and expected return in North Darfur localities. However, Eastern Sudan is also passing in a different insecurity situation where food security and livelihood are concerns. As per the FSL sector in Sudan, 7,139,718 people identified as in need of food security and need action, for this specific project HDPO targeting South Darfur (Umdafug, Katila) and North Darfur (Kernui, Umbaru, , ELserief, Kabkabyia, and Saraf Omra) and Red Sea ( Sinkat, Haya and Dordieb), these localities have been identified as high priorities and in IPC phase 3 and HNO 2020/2021.The above-mentioned situation leading to urgent actions is required to save lives and livelihoods of populations by providing emergency agricultural and livestock support to farmers, with special focus on localities prone to natural disasters including floods and drought and affected by the new arrival returns. Lack of basic seeds (sorghum & groundnuts) of 5 localities in North & South Darfur and the main gabs to secure food are low inputs (through vouchers & fair), and ignorance of new basic agricultural techniques (farmers to be trained on agricultural extension, restocking and IGAs). Seed shortages, worsened by insecurity and constant increase in agricultural inputs prices, result in lower cultivation. This project aims to improve access to agricultural assets, inputs, and services to increase and diversify their food and seed production and hence restore and strengthen their capacity to sustain their food and nutrition security; improve access by female and male pastoral and agro-pastoral groups to livestock protection and production inputs and services to restore and sustain their food and nutrition security; especially women-headed households, malnourished and elders to diversify their income sources. The awareness, knowledge, and skills for better environmental protection and sustainable management of natural resources are improved in and around villages hosting IDPs, returnees, and drought-affected households in the affected areas. This includes vocational pieces of training for adult males & females in South Darfur and the Red Sea as there are already existing institutes and capacities, and CAHWs and equips them (revolving fund). Livestock looting and losses due to lack of veterinary services affected the complementary feeding for children. Scale-up diversified livelihoods program for improved self-reliance, resilience building, and social protection to all vulnerable communities and implement water harvesting programs in water- deficit areas and to improve the existing water sources for more water access for the livestock. Scale-up and improve access to basic services (health and WASH) throughout the year. Strengthen and support initiatives addressing cross-cutting issues, food security, and nutrition status of vulnerable groups. The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods and daily labour opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population therefore, packages of sessions on COVID19 prevention, distribution of hygienic kits include face masks, sensitizers to some vulnerable groups in the targeted areas as well as provision of agricultural & livestock packages to people with special needs (PWSNs) in different targeted areas.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1.1 Agricultural intervention to support the vulnerable returnees and local community in Um Dafug town and Mandowa village, Um Dafug locality, South Darfur.1.2 Establishment of two vegetable farms with a drip irrigation system for returnees and the local community in Um Dafug town.1.3 Support rain-fed agricultural practice for returnees and local communities in Mandowa village.1.4 Establishment of vegetable farms with a drip irrigation system for a total of 20 members (10 returnees and 10 local communities) in Um Dafug town.1.5 Design and establish two vegetable farms with a drip irrigation system and install a small pumping generator: One for the returnee group (10 members) and another for the local community (10 members).1.6 Tool distribution: Tools for the dripping system will be distributed, and this includes: hose, valves, bubblers, drum, 1-sqm stand, fittings, etc.1.7 General farming tools (4 pieces of hoe, ax, rake, and scooper per farm- so that a total of 8 pieces of each tool) will be distributed.1.8 Seeds distribution: watermelon, okra, tomato, cucumber, carrot, rocket.1.9 Design and conduct training: Conduct 5 days-training on installation and operating of drip irrigating system for 20 participants.1.10 Conduct 3 days-training on vegetable farming techniques and management for 20 participants.1.11 Establish community agricultural committee and Develop roles and responsibilities of committees: e.g., supporting agricultural-related income-generating activities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

• This project will work with both beneficiaries and the authorities. The food vendors across the intervention areas avoid the provision of unsuitable food packing material (against local regulations; not recyclable) and related improper disposal of food packaging waste. Work together to eliminate or minimize the activities related to environmental degradation and possible security and conflict risks through the increased need for firewood/water collection for cooking caused by food selection that may require long cooking time or large quantities of water.• Increase awareness that reduces rate Procurement goods that may lead to unsustainable cropping and livestock production systems, agrochemical use, land conversion (e.g., from forest to pasture) if not properly sourced, and the lack of treatment of waste in food processing.Technical support and awareness sessions at different levels to reduce environmentally unfriendly activities such as charcoal making, woodcutting, illegal hunting, distribution of assets, machinery, and cash in an area of insecurity: insecurity can lead to conflict and the displacement of people, the abandonment of business properties and assets, creating a problem of waste management. Eliminating the use of un-recyclable packaging and labels to boost commercialization creates waste if it does not absorb the product.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Food, cash, vouchers, or livestock responses may undermine people’s efforts to protect themselves and increase their vulnerabilities. Positive coping strategies contributing to food security and dignity are supported through the intervention and ensure none of the valid good norms is destroyed.The project also ensures Food, cash, vouchers, or livestock components not undermine people’s efforts to protect themselves and increase theirvulnerabilities. Positive coping strategies contributing to food security and dignity should, therefore, be supported. During all the project cycle phases, the outlined activities must not discriminate (or be perceived as doing so) against any group. Consider whether women and men may have different barriers to access cash compared with in-kind resources. Planned activities sound promoting and help protect the rights of people who have historically been marginalized or discriminated against, such as religious or ethnic minorities or women and girls.HDPO and when selecting FSL staff and relief committees will ensure that all groups within the community (e.g., gender, age, ethnicity, socio-economic group, disability) are represented. Similarly, ensure that food or livelihood committees are representative of all groups within the community. Include targeted measures to ensure the effective and meaningful participation of representatives of all groups in the community. All staff and committee members should receive protection mainstreaming training. In fact, they are on the frontline. They can play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and being proactive in ensuring marginalized groups' voice is represented.Ensure all FSL staff, implementing contractors, and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign, and adhere to a code of conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and beneficiaries' rights. Ensure measures in case of violation and fight impunity. Similarly, for PSE, ensure that mechanisms are in place for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and regularly updated and functioning effectively.
