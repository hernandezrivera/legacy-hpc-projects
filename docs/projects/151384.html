Project Summary

CPD is proposing to provide targeted emergency health and nutrition services to 288,200 people in need in Banadir, Lower Shebelle and Galgadud regions in south central Somalia. The project targets both host communities and internally displaced persons in the target districts.
The target areas have a high number of people affected by drought, conflicts, floods as well as poor health services. The project intends to improve their health and nutrition outcomes for these vulnerable communities.
The project will be integrated with services offered both at facility and community levels where the community members will be provided with health services to include maternal, reproductive and child services as well as nutrition screening, referral, treatment for SAM and MAM; promotion services to focus on infant and young child feeding/nutrition in emergency. Services will be provided through 10 health facilities and 6 mobile/outreach teams.

Needs

Despite the above-average performance of the April to June Gu rains and sustained humanitarian response which has led to a marked improvement in pasture, water availability and increasing herd size, an estimated 4.6 million people, including 2.5 million children, continue to require humanitarian assistance. Of these, 1.5 million are in crisis or emergency. Communities that lost most of their livestock during the 2016-2017 drought and those affected by conflict, as well as this year’s devastating floods and cyclone, will take longer to recover. The internally displaced are the most vulnerable and in dire need of immediate and long-term assistance. Humanitarian partners have continued to provide life-saving support and alongside livelihoods interventions to people in need. Food security partners reached 1.9 million people per month between February and July.According to the JMNCA report, less than 40% of the population seek health care in Lower Shebelle region districts with more likely to report increased difficulty accessing services (51%). The same report also states that the region has a high outbreak of AWD and measles (33%). All this points to the lack of health access due to distances, unavailability as well as inaccessibility. In Mogadishu, the high number of IDPs has led to an increased stress on the existing health facilities causing poor health outcomes.According to the results of 33 separate nutrition surveys between May to July, the overall nutrition situation is at sustained levels despite improved food security, reduced disease outbreaks and sustained humanitarian interventions. As a result, nearly 954,007 children are expected to be acutely malnourished, including 173,594 who are expected to be severely malnourished during the next one year period (Sep 2018-Sep 2019). The surveys indicate Critical levels of GAM (15-29.9%) in 12 out of 33 populations surveyed (7 IDP and 5 Rural) while Critical levels of SAM prevalence (≥4-5.6) were only seen among IDP

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Among the main risks facing the targeted population is their exclusion in terms of targeting and distribution when it comes to humanitarian assistance. The displaced families are often excluded in the targeting and distribution of humanitarian assistance despite them being the most bearers of the brunt of the humanitarian crisis. The other risk they face is exclusion from the decision making process on issues like their eviction and site planning. The project plans to incorporate the camp leadership into the local authorities' decision making organs so that the camp members are consulted whenever decisions that affect their lives are made. The project will target both host and internally displaced persons in the community. CPD will ensure that services are situated within or a walking distance from the IDP settlements in the target areas to ensure they are able to access services.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project will enhance gender equality as the services will be provided equally across the general population for men, women, boys and girls. However, some interventions are age specific such as maternal and reproductive services which target women of child bearing age, screening and treatment for malnutrition which targets children under five and PLWs among others.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

CPD will closely work with the authorities and communities in the target areas to prevent harm coming to the beneficiaries as well as the staffs. Daily security briefing will be conducted by the security focal point to ensure the area is safe before work starts and in the outreach areas, community leaders would be consulted before the team leaves for the site.The facilities and the outreach team sites will be set up in consultations with community leaders to ensure the sites are close to the beneficiaries and safe to prevent the distances they go to seek services and in the process reduce the risks of violence. CPD will ensure all its staff and volunteers in direct contact with patients, health staff and beneficiaries have received training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) to help understand this concept and prevent the occurrence of sexual exploitation and abuse.
