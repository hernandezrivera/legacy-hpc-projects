Project Summary

This project will provide lifesaving shelter and non-food items (in-kind and cash) assistance for total of 833 households (5000 individuals) in Mundri West County. The Shelter and NFI in-kind assistance will target 679 households in Bangolo, Ladingwa, and Medewu. The cash assistance will target newly displaced and returnee population within Mundri town through commodity voucher because there is a functioning market within Mundri town with capacity to provide SNFIs. The in-kind SNFI assistance will specifically target locations affected by the conflict from November 2017 and January 2018. The population experienced extensive burning of homes, displacement and looting of civilian property but have not received assistance since it was assessed by the interagency fact finding assessment team from 1-5 march 2018 (UNOCHA). According the assessment report a total of 138,954 people was affected by the conflict at different locations in Mundri from November 2017 to January 2018.
ASO has previously worked with Lacha Community and Economic Development (LCED) and Polish Humanitarian Action (PAH) in distribution of ES/NFI to some targeted beneficiaries in Mundri and have acquired experience in SNFIs intervention and is better positioned to access hard to reach areas with lifesaving NFIs and emergency shelter materials since it has established a long term relationship with local communities and authorities as a result of its well established static presence in Mundri and will continue to work in close coordination with LCED in similar manner to ensure quality and efficiency as new SNFI Cluster partner in Mundri.
ASO will depend on SNFI cluster pipeline supplies during the project. Register and verify the beneficiaries to establish the target number to be supported using an agreed selection criteria. This will be done through community engagements supported by the community leaders selected within the target area. A total of 579 household will be supported with in-kind NFI and 100 households of specifically vulnerable groups like elderly, widows and disabled persons will have additional support of shelter from Ladingwa, Bangolo, Bari and Medewo while 154 targeted beneficiaries from within Mundri town supported with cash assistance. Consult with the target beneficiaries and local leaders, safe and secure sites for distributions and schedule will be identified. ASO will closely work with local authorities for continuous security updates. ASO will hire community crowd controllers within the target area to support ASO during distributions. A complaint desk will be established at the distribution centre to address any grievances raised by the target beneficiaries and ensure continuous process to ensure ASO is implementing the project as per cluster standards in addition to ensuring the project meets its intended objectives. The beneficiaries will be part of the monitoring process right from the registration and will provide feedback that will form the basis for project reporting. A database containing names of the registered and supported beneficiaries will be designed by ASO M&E department and a post distribution monitoring will be conducted with support from Shelter Cluster to measure project performance across key focus areas of accountability to beneficiaries, effectiveness, efficiency and impact. Monitoring reports shall then be submitted to cluster periodically.

Needs

The population of Mundri Counties has experienced multiple displacements since the war broke out in the area in 2014. A few assessments have been done in the past and in some cases assistance provided; however, other populations which got displaced in later half of 2017 have not been reached. In November 2017, partners in Mundri conducted assessment in Mundri East, but could not assess in Mundri West or deliver assistance in both counties due to deterioration in security and safety as military confrontations between Government and opposition resumed in these areas. The related insecurity and restriction of movement from November 2017 to January 2018 delayed the resumption of assessment and response. The conflict affected population remains without access to essential services. According to Inter-agency Fact Finding Assessment Report for Mundri Counties: 1-5 (UNOCHA). The Emergency Shelter and NFI needs during the assessment categorized the needs according to locations which experienced extensive burning of homes, displacement and looting of property as "A" this locations are Karika, Kotobi, Bari, Medewu, Ladingwa, Lanyi, Lozoh and Gulu. The other locations categorized as "B" which include Mbara, Bangolo, Movo and Wiroh experienced lost of property due to looting and have been locked without access to market. Karika, Kotobi have been supported with NFIs in July 2018, however, those other locations like Bari, Medewu, Ladingwa, Lanyi, Lozoh and Gulu remained desperate in need of shelter materials, kithen sets, water containers, blankets and mosquito nets. Apt Succor Organization's priority is to save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys by improving access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to newly displaced or vulnerable populations in secondary displacement in locations as well as returnees which have not been reached.

ASO will closely work with local authorities for continuous security updates. ASO will hire community crowd controllers within the target area to support ASO during distributions. Monitoring reports shall then be submitted to cluster periodically.4. Transit storage and transportation of SNFI materials to the distributions sites from Mundri, ASO will also work with the cluster pipeline in Juba to verify the supplies for their projects during transportation in Mundri by the cluster.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

ASO will share distribution standards with the community and will set help desk at the distribution sites, which will be able to provide more background and contact information and will have discussions with beneficiaries during project monitoring and evaluation to make sure that their voices and feedback are heard. The project implementation will be based on assessment results generated by data and information collected during cluster meetings, making sure that the actual essential Shelter and NFI needs are identified and that the proposed activities are appropriate. After the completion of distribution, a monitoring team will be sent to visit selected samples of beneficiaries to confirm the actual quantity received and the quality of the SNFI commodities, as well as to collect further feedback from beneficiaries. Once complaints are received, ASO will look for the most appropriate way to respond to the grievances.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Posters will be produced and displayed showing distribution standards in form of pictures for easy understanding by the beneficiaries. Clearly displaying picture of items which will be distributed to the beneficiaries and how much each household will receive and briefly explaining the manner to which the distribution will be conducted. The distribution standard developed together with the community will be displayed and explained to the beneficiaries by the committee formed to address or forward grievances raised by the beneficiaries as they arise before, during and after the distribution of SNFIs.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

ASO will provide training and information for its staff on preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse and establish mechanism for submission and receipt of complaints regarding sexual exploitation by its staff by setting procedure for reporting, investigating and assisting victims of sexual exploitation through the complaint desk.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Community engagement during across the project period right from the registration and verification process will be made in a consultative manner with an established committee representing various sex and age groups selected by the beneficiaries to represent their views and their views will be used for designing the distribution standards to prevent exclusion. The same group will be involved in the monitoring process to ensure beneficiaries full participation in the processes.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

ASO will establish a committee made up of the beneficiaries to raises issues arising among the beneficiaries and report them to ASO for response where ASO will look for appropriate response for the complaints raised.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The community will be engaged right from registration and verification process by involving them in the identification of beneficiaries for this project and through out the monitoring process to ensure that feedback from them are considered as lesson learned and well as their grievances addressed.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In the current context women and girls are particularly at risk of sexual violence during distribution of SNFIs therefore ASO will establish close contact with local leaders through constant receipt security update to enable selection of appropriate time and place for distribution to protect women and girls from sexual violence. ASO will also ensure that women headed households benefiting from the cash transfer are secure when they are receiving the cash by selecting an appropriate time and locations for distributing the cash.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Road accessibility is the current barrier to service delivery in Mundri, ASO will ensure that it assess the road conditions before taking the SNFI Materials to avoid damages. ASO with its well established relationship with the local community will constantly consult them on the road conditions and chose appropriate time to reach this hard to reach areas.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ASO recognizes that NFI needs of men, women and children are different. The proposed intervention will pay close attention to gender dimensions and particular needs of marginalized groups by ensuring that women and other vulnerable groups are prioritized in the identification, targeting and distribution process to ensure their full participation as well as also ensure the participation of men, women, boys and girls while employing methods that protect women and girls from potential risks of violence arising against them. It will integrate gender and age during the registration and verification stage in order to ascertain their specific needs and the project will integrate needs of specific groups into the distribution process by selecting distribution sites carefully to ensure that women do not need to travel long distances to reduce the risk of exposure to violence. Women and other vulnerable groups are prioritized in the identification, targeting and distribution process to ensure their full participation.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project activities has taken into consideration the safety of both beneficiaries and project staff by putting in place sexual exploitation and abuse policy to protect beneficiaries through staff orientation on the policy issues to protect them from exploitation of the beneficiaries that will get them into problem with the beneficiaries themselves.
