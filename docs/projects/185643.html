Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) will work closely to engage 1,500 Rohingya refugees and 5.500 host communities in small-scale agriculture and plantation activities that will foster self-reliance, food security, dietary diversity, access to fresh foods and environmental sustainability. Agriculture activities focus mainly on homestead vegetable gardening and vertical gardening. The project will have a training component to ensure beneficiaries get the full benefit of the agricultural inputs. Training will be implemented following the COVID-19 preparedness measures, to minimize the risk of spread amongst participants. Additionally, behaviour change communication (BCC) and awareness activities will be incorporated with social and health messages. Empowering the parents and caregivers of the children through providing soft skills, vocational skills, job linkages support, entrepreneurship development, assets and cash transfer support, the project will strengthen the protective factors at all levels of the child’s environment in order to reinforce the child’s resilience and prevent and respond to threats to the wellbeing of children. The success of the project is only promised by mitigating risks, particularly the risk of natural and manmade disasters, such as, cyclones fire and monsoon season. To reduce the risks of natural and manmade disasters, the project aims to educate and create awareness about the disaster risk reduction strategies and components enabling the beneficiaries to be aware of these disasters, and be prepared and resilient.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya Refugees: While socio-economic impacts on Rohingya refugees are multi-dimensional, COVID-19 also threatens to exacerbate the already dire conditions for the communities. Limited self-reliance options lead the vulnerable groups to increased negative coping mechanisms (J-MSNA 2020). Supporting most vulnerable groups and settlements with forestry and medicinal tree plantation (through cash assistance) is needed to ensure that they can meet minimum essential and environmental needs. There needs to be a provision of adding value for better food in camp settings, and enhancing resilience. Through skills development training, establishing homestead vegetable gardens and incentive-based activities, the refugees can create economic opportunities. There is an increased need of portable skills development for youth (male and female who are not able to access learning activities) and women as well to generate informal income generation. Training or capacity building is appeared as one of the needs for the youth to mobilize a much needed skilled workforce. It will also enable the refugees to earn higher incentives, and to acquire durable skills. Host Communities: Over a million displaced Rohingya people from Myanmar have taken shelter in camps in Ukhia and Teknaf upazilas of Cox’s Bazar in 2017 which has created a high degree of pressure on local social, economic, and ecological systems. Establishing about 30 camps have denuded 6,000 acres of forest lands in Cox’s Bazar. Loss of forests impacted upon the forest-based local livelihoods. Additionally, the host communities living near the Rohingya refugees are also affected in various ways due to an overstressed local resource base. The poorer segments of the host communities suffer from decreased wage rates (30-50% low rates) due to the availability of Rohingya laborers in the market at low wage rates. Hence, the job market for the host communities has become extremely competitive. The income of host communities has decreased by over 10% in recent months while it further plummeted during the lockdown for COVID-19 as their income decreased by about 50% (Save the Children’s Feasibility Report, 2021). Most of the UN agencies, NGOs and INGOs are implementing largescale programmes in the refugee camps, while the host communities of Cox’s Bazar are deprived of receiving humanitarian and development services. These situations are creating tensions which have the potential to trigger conflicts between refugees and host communities in the future. Therefore, a comprehensive provision for peace building mechanisms through integrated social services and improved livelihoods for benefiting host communities is a major concern that may ease the tension, prevent crises, and build social cohesion.
