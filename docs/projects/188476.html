Project Summary

CARE’s proposed strategy is in line with the 2021 HRP and specifically aligns with SO3 (Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities). CARE’s proposed intervention will target Hasakah and Deir-ez-Zor (DEZ) governorate in NES, some of the most impoverished areas in Syria. The multi sectoral approach integrated with an innovative methodology grounding resilience in socio-economic wellbeing linking increased household economic capacities and opportunities with access to functioning basic infrastructure at community level and inclusive decision making at household and community levels, promoting more inclusive environment and improved ability of copying with stress. CARE has a strong programmatic focus around value chains since they link producers and buyers from different communities and purposefully integrate women, older persons, people with disabilities, IDPs and/or returnees. CARE’s proposed value chain approach in the short, medium and long term is expected to help Syrian families and communities affected by conflict to achieve a greater level of resilience, mitigate protection risks and participate in economic life. The Programme will focus on the restoration of livelihoods and markets related to wheat and sheep value chains as well as increasing community well-being and social cohesion i.e vocational training centers, SME grants for new businesses, establishment of Field Farmer School, cash for work activities for the rehabilitation of community infrastructures linked with agriculture and market places alongside capacity building for farmers/ stakeholders/local service providers, preparation of community Disaster Risk Reduction plans and development of relevant capacities at community level. CARE’s value chain and market-based approaches, are important aspects of establishing stability and creating social cohesion at community level (if designed and implemented in an inclusive and conflict sensitive way). Communities are consulted when identifying appropriate required interventions (i.e. community rehab or CFW activates). Women involved in income generation activities, including SMEs, should be supported through wider community sensitization on GAD approaches and mainstreaming, in addition to specific interventions around GBV and established referral mechanisms. CFW intervention supports vulnerable individuals in meeting immediate needs, whilst also benefiting the entire community over a longer period. CFW interventions are selected based on the relevance to specific value chains, payment amounts are aligned with CWG to avoid market distortion and activities targeted at women are culturally appropriate. Working hours and days are determined in consultation with the CWG and local stakeholders including local councils, HAO, camp management and community leaders in compliance with rights at work, obligations and religious customs. CFW projects can either set a specific timeline for ongoing tasks or projects that can clearly be completed within a set timeframe, or set a project deliverable where the project finishes once completed (i.e. a new road, etc.). The daily wage rate it’s regularly reviewed and agreed upon at the beginning of a project cycle based on the SMEB. CFW projects should refrain from competing with the demand for labor, especially in certain periods of the year and in certain locations, e.g. during sowing and harvesting to avoid competing with local employers and leaving them with limited workforce, and therefore creating labor shortages in the market.

5 - Implementation of activities

CARE coordinates programmes with and through respective WGs and partners to ensure that CARE applies best practice through the alignment with cluster guidelines. CARE consults with local stakeholders to ensure there is collaboration & connections established and maintained. CARE will establish community committees to increase community ownership and involvement in targeting. CAREs program team will conduct beneficiaries registration alongside vulnerability and selection criteria which is verified by MEAL. CARE has built on the learnings from previous or existing cash programming and selected these modalities through consultations with the target groups, whilst also considering their feasibility. The proposed CVA interventions will be coordinated through the CWG, HAO and local councils. CAREs approach to CVA is harmonized with the CWG) and reflected through the CVA specifications (i.e. amounts, # of hours etc) and selection criterion used. CARE participates in monthly market assessments with REACH and the CWG. When selecting appropriate markets, there are several factors of consideration i.e., markets must be permanent in nature, large and diverse enough to provide a sufficient variety of goods and commodities etc. The SMEs selected are based on market assessments to ensure that there is sufficient demand for produce. Linkages between the SME owners and local vendors/suppliers/local markets are also created to increase opportunities and sustainability beyond the project.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(i) Protection Risks: Including GBV/IPV when targeting woman for VCA. CARE will mitigate risks by ensuring interventions (i.e. types of CFW) are culturally appropriate and designed in consultation with the local community. Income generation interventions will be complimented with GAD sessions at community level and all field staff trained on Protection risks and referral mechanisms. (ii) Conflict sensitivity/social cohesion: CARE will create inclusive community committees who will be involved in design and beneficiary targeting. Needs and gaps will be identified through these committees. Selection criteria designed in consultation with committees who will be involved in participant selection. CARE will conduction the registration and verification. Approaches will be coordinated and harmonized with all relevant stakeholders. (iii) Security risks: To mitigate risks to staff, CARE will use a third part agents for the handling and distribution of cash during CFW and SME interventions.
