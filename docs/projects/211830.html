Project Summary

The latest HNO report available, from February 2023, highlighted the dire circumstances in NW Syria. Among the 1.9 million individuals residing across 1,437 IDP sites, two earthquakes led to an additional 108,906 displacements, intensifying the urgent need for WASH and Shelter support. It highlighted a critical water crisis, exacerbated by drought, damaged, unreliable and inequal access to water infrastructure.
Recent findings from CARE’s assessment in Nov, 2023, in 31 sub districts in Aleppo and Idleb Governorate, show that 48% of respondents in communities, and 47% in IDP camps reported lack of water to meet their needs. a vast majority (91%) of households in these regions are dependent on secondary water sources. In communities, 30% of respondents reported not having adequate water storage available. While 87% of respondents reporting having a water network in their community. In IDP camps, 57% of IDP households reported that their household water storage was shared with other families. The main water source for households living in IDP sites is water trucking (85%) which is primarily provided by humanitarian actors.
Through this project, CARE will focus on providing the following services:
Activity1: Water trucking services and distribution of water tanks
Activity2: Rehabilitation and operational support to water systems
Activity3: Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation system
Activity4: Installation of latrines and desludging services
Activity5: Wastewater Treatment Plant Rehabilitation
Activity6: SWM
Activity7: HP sessions and HKs distribution.
CARE is targeting a total of 350,000 beneficiaries through rehabilitation and operation of WASH infrastructure (SO 3) as well as 450,000 beneficiaries through the provision of emergency WASH services (SO1).
Protection Risk Analysis:
-Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards
-Not enough water distribution points available
-Water distribution not safe to access or not accessible
-Support to wastewater systems
Assumptions/risks and Contingency measures:
1-Conflict and deterioration of security conditions: Undertake a context and security analysis, with regular updates and information-sharing between the project partners; dedicated security personnel and harmonized operational standards to establish safe and secure routines with the teams and counterparts; establish risk mitigation plans for the project; establish a workflow for the reporting of incidents; training staff on security measures; developing protection/self-protection measures/protocols.
2-Negative impact of COVID-19 and cholera: CARE has developed COVID-19 SOPs for all programming areas as well as for its operations. Ensure application of COVID-19 protocols in planning and implementation; monitor health trends alongside duty-bearers and health/WASH agencies, taking pre-emptive measures where necessary to mitigate identified increases in case rates. Mitigation measures are regularly updated according to changes in context and coordination with relevant actors. CARE has developed Risk Communication and Community Engagement Plan on cholera to work with and inform women, girls, men and boys on cholera preparedness and response.
3-Coercion into ways of working that contravene humanitarian principles: centrality of protection embedded in operational SOPs, strong internal communication mechanisms that flag issues for decision-making, clear definition of red lines amongst SMT Supply chain is interrupted and procurement delayed– markets will be actively monitored so that potential alternatives can be identified for procurement.
4-Extreme weather patterns/natural disaster: regular monitoring of weather events and early warning indicators, use of environmental and social assessments, engage with DRR actors to assist in community action plans.
