Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) proposes to implement health activities in Akobo, Nyirol and Uror counties in Jonglei State, Aweil West and Abyei county in Abyei State Administration Area through static, mobile and outreach services. The 2023 Integrated Food Security phase classification reports high levels of acute food insecurity with Jonglei and Abyei states requiring priority attention in meeting the humanitarian needs of people in the states. Additionally, returnees and asylum seekers from the Sudan conflict settled within Abyei, Akobo and Wedweil Refugee camp in Aweil West. The proposed health project seeks to reduce morbidity and mortality particularly amongst under-five children, adolescent girls, pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerable populations by increasing access and utilization of quality basic primary health care services for communicable and non-communicable diseases including, reproductive health, emergency clinical management for survivors of SGBV, injury and first aid care, mental health, nutrition and community health activities, for women, girls, boys and men as well as surveillance and control of epidemic prone diseases and response to public health emergencies. Save the Children has presence in these locations that need to be strengthened to ensure life-saving healthcare services are provided to target populations and people in great need of health services. This will be achieved by supporting the State Ministry of Health (SMoH) and County Health Department (CHD) and in coordination with the health cluster through static and mobile health facilities and community outreach supporting the Boma Health Initiative. SC will coordinate the response with the RRC as well as with the health clusters and other actors in the areas to consider the need for initial assessments. The project will implement activities aimed at achieving the following strategic objectives; 1. Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs; 2. Vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action through AAP, protection mainstreaming and monitoring and; 3. Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand or cope with the impact of new and protracted crises is increased, and durable solutions to root causes of their vulnerability and shocks are enabled by humanitarian action.
Methodology and target: The project will target a total population in need (Residents, IDPs, and Returnees) of 120,016 (Abyei Administrative Area: 36,886, Awiel West: 3,943, Akobo: 27,155, Nyirol: 25,660 and Uror: 26,372). SC will support MOH in delivering quality and cost-effective healthcare services, health system strengthening, capacity building of staff at national level, state level including the CHD as well as coordination activities. Supported activities will include provision of curative consultations for adults and under-five children in targeted health facilities, mobile and outreach clinics, provision of routine immunization, ante-natal care for pregnant women, safe delivery through skilled birth attendants, post-natal care for newborns and lactating women, family planning counselling and service provision, nutrition screening for under-five children and PLWs, community based service delivery through BHWs as well as clinical management of rape survivors. In collaboration with the CHD, supportive supervision and on-job coaching of health providers will be done to build skills and competencies of health staff in managing health emergencies. Communities will be engaged through consultative meetings, focus group discussions with men, women, boys and girls as well as client exit interviews at facilities in order to receive feedback from communities aimed at improving the quality of care provided to patients and clients.

1. Needs

A total of 9.4 million people need humanitarian assistance (24% female, 54% children, 15% People with Disabilities (PWD)HNO 2023) in South Sudan. Large-scale displacement is also affecting the country (2.3 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs)). Furthermore, due to Sudan’s conflict (April 2023), 307.142 individuals crossed the border to South Sudan (49% female, 48% children); of those, 89,71% are South Sudanese returnees with reported measles outbreaks in Abyei and Jonglei states (OCHA sitrep Nov2023). OCHA estimates that 6.1 million people need quality health services (50.3% female; 53% children; 15% PWD) (with 86% of the population in extreme or catastrophic need). The country prolonged war (peace agreement in 2005 and independence in 2011) severely damaged the country's physical infrastructure and social systems, particularly the healthcare system.South Sudan’s health system relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance and is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide basic essential life-saving health services. Government contributions to health services was just 2.5% in 2022/2023 of the national budget allocations with the addition of an underperforming economy contributing to poor health outcomes. While all HF in country were to be equally assisted as per the National Health Policy (NHP), in practice this has been challenging since funding available could not cover all the facilities and components of the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNS), and limited access to some locations and weak Health Management and Information System (HMIS) further complicated the delivery of services. Beyond that, the lack of medicine and staff as well as equipment, transportation cost, further complicate accessing health services. Poor health facility infrastructure with limited health facility functionality and capacity, and poor coverage, have made it difficult to meet additional health needs posed by floods, food insecurity and frequent conflicts. The 2022 HNO reports only 11% of the 1,869 reported health facilities are providing the full package of BPHNS and 53% of the facilities are moderately functional, implying inadequate access and unavailability of some health services to some communities in many parts of the country. Community based service delivery is insufficient and about 58% of the Bomas in the country are still not having Boma health teams. Attack on health care workers/facilities continues to negatively impact provision of and access to health services in Abyei and Akobo counties. Access of communities to health services is further hindered by insecurity and harassment on the way to facilities. Women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to healthcare. Maternal and underfive mortalities in the country are amongst the highest in the world, with 1,223/ 100,000 live births and 115/1,000 livebirths(MMEIG,2022). Only 39% women are availing recommended Four Antenatal Care Services during pregnancy and skilled birth attendance are low at 14.7%. Access to BEmONC is very low with 39% of health facilities offering BEMoC services and contributes to pregnancy related and new-born morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable populations. Extremely low immunization coverage (49% Penta 3 coverage-HNO2023) has made children more vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases. Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 years, contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods is 6% with modern methods at 5%, with the unmet need for family planning estimated at 29.7% for 2020(FP 2020). Gender inequality and gender-based violence are widespread and perpetuated by several factors including socio-cultural norms and armed group presence in communities. Malaria remains the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 64% of all morbidities and 45% of all mortalities in 2021(NMCP)

2. Project activities

SC will continue to work with the State Ministry of Health (SMoH) and County Health Department (CHD), to support Primary Health Care Centers (PHCC) and mobile medical units. Community outreach will also be supported through Boma Health Initiative (BHI) volunteers to improve access to quality life-saving healthcare, with a focus on offering curative and preventative child and maternal health services. The support includes technical, managerial, material, and monetary support to enable facilities to provide quality life-saving interventions safely in the catchment areas and includes the provision of additional clinical staff for the PHCCs, setting up and running the planned mobile clinics, to support community-based disease surveillance. SC will continue coordination with the county health team, supporting coordination activities at the county level through our field offices in Akobo, Abyei, Nyirol and Awiel west. Project strategic objectives, Specific objectives, Health cluster objectives and indicators are all health cluster pre-approved. Strategic objectives (SO):SO1: Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs. SC will continue to support 13 PHCC, 16 PHCUs and 8 mobile clinics in Jonglei, Aweil and Abyei to provide curative consultations for under-five and adults, routine immunizations to children and women of childbearing age, ANC, PNC, SBA, CMR for SGBV survivors, nutrition screening, FP and SRH services to women, girls, men and boys. At community level, the trained BWs will be engaged to work with health care workers and vaccinators to support with defaulter tracing of under-five children and PLWs requiring immunization and ANC services from the communities and refer them to health facilities. Further, the BHWs will work closely with TBAs to make certain that women reporting to TBAs for delivery are referred and accompanied to health facilities for safe delivery through SBA. SC will focus on capacity building of health staff through CMEs, on-job coaching, supportive supervision in promoting uptake of skills, competencies and adherence to national guidelines and protocols when delivering health services. Postnatal care will be provided to newborns and lactating women with emphasis on counselling for family planning amongst women. SC will coordinate with WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA and MOH to secure drugs, medical supplies and equipment through direct purchase and through the humanitarian pipeline in making certain sufficient supplies are provided to support the static, mobile, outreach and BHWs providing health services to underserved populations.SO2: Vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action through AAP, protection mainstreaming and monitoring. Health care workers working in ICCM sites and PHCCs will be trained to recognize signs of common mental health conditions in children, adolescents, PLW and adults in humanitarian settings (utilizing content of mhGAP-HIG and the child and adolescent mental health module mhGAP 2.0), provide basic support and recognize when to issue referrals for additional MHPSS care. Client exit interviews and FDGs will support community feedback SO3: Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand or cope with the impact of new and protracted crises is increased, and durable solutions to root causes of their vulnerability and shocks are enabled by humanitarian action. SC health project will collaborate with the CHD to plan, assess and respond to acute public health emergencies and disease outbreaks, significant increases in morbidities, population displacements and other natural disasters. SC will work with community-based workers and facility staff in providing timely and correct weekly IDSR reports and monthly service delivery reports through the EWARNS and DHIS2 platforms.
