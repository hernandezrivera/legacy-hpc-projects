Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Mali continues to be drastic, characterized by massive displacements of the population fleeing insecurity, dysfunctional basic services that are already structurally failing. The gradual withdrawal of MINUSMA as of August 2023 and its consequences on the security situation have greatly contributed to worsening this situation, both in terms of needs and access. At the end of September 2023, the number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) reached 391,961 (VS 375,539 in April 2023). Between January and September 2023, 23,577 new IDPs were reported in Gourma Rharous and 25,871 in Bourem. IMC is proposing an integrated, multisectoral program to provide emergency assistance in health, including mental health and psychosocial support, nutrition, and protection focused on GBV to people affected by the conflict, including IDPs and people whose access to health services is severely limited or disrupted due to the crisis in Bourem and Gourma Rharous HD. Specifically, the action aims to (i) continue to ensure access to quality health services for the population affected by the crisis through the setting up of mobile clinics, referral and management of emergencies at the Gourma Rharous and Bourem CSRefs. (ii) Provision of integrated package of mental health services and psychosocial support, (iii) strengthen the detection and treatment of acute malnutrition, and contribute to the prevention of malnutrition through improving IYCF-(E) practices, (iv) provide a minimum protection package, including prevention and response to GBV, and (v) contribute to humanitarian monitoring, assessments and RRM responses in the areas covered by the project. IMC's access capacity, based on its knowledge of the intervention area and its acceptance within the communities, will enable it to implement its actions. The action targets 67,150 beneficiaries, (41,797 women, 25,353 men) among which 14,255 are IDPs.

1 - Contexte

Continuing and even increasing security incidents continue to generate population displacements in search of safer areas. At the end of September 2023, the number of IDPs was estimated at 391,961, up to 4% increase compared to IDPs’ statistics of April 2023 (CMP, Sept. 2023 report). The main regions hosting IDPs are Mopti (91,274), Timbuktu (61,827), Ménaka (45,121) and Gao (35,613). In Timbuktu and Gao, Gourma Rharous and Bourem cercles are among the most-affected areas. According to statistics from local development services, between January and November 2021, the Gourma Rharous cercle recorded 23,577 new IDPs, especially in the Gossi (18,971 PDIS), Bamabara Maoudé (1,328), Haribomo (1,558) and Inadiatafane (1,720) communes. Over the same period in Bourem, 25,871 new IDPs were registered, in the communes of Bamba (5,671 IDPs); Taboye (8,413), and TEMERA (2,573 IDPs). These displaced people, whether living with host families or in IDP sites, are exposed both to major health risks and to inadequate access to basic social services, including health. These IDPs face a combination of health problems, acute malnutrition and protection concerns. The health situation in the areas targeted by the project is marked by a lack of quality health services. This is the result of a combination of structural and conjunctural factors : low availability of qualified human resources, low capacity to supply essential medicines, and poor technical platform. The security crisis has exacerbated an already precarious basic situation. One of the causes of the shortage of qualified human resources is insecurity and difficult living conditions. The Bourem HDs has the lowest ratio of qualified staff per 10,000 inhabitants in the Gao region, with respectively 1.2 compares with 3 per 10,000 people in Gourma Rharous. The security situation is also having a major impact on the functionality of health facilities. In 2023, in Gourma Rharous HDs, only 17/30 health centers were operational, against 21/26 in Bourem HD. Access to health services and care remains a major challenge, particularly for many IDPs and for populations living in areas far from the nearest HF. Factors limiting access to health services are manifold : i) Geographical barriers : in the targeted areas, 39% of the population live at least 15 km far from a functional HF ii) financial barriers, iii) Insecurity leading to the displacement of populations, closure of HF and limitation of healthcare seeking due to security risks on the route. The use rate for health services remains low. In Gourma Rharous, despite an improvement of this rate in the HCs supported by IMC (88% in 2023), the rate at HDs level remains low (49%). In Bourem, the rate of use of health services in supported areas is 69% (compared to 52% at HDs level). Access to vital sexual and reproductive health services remains limited too. The rate of assisted deliveries (45% in the supported HF of Gourma Rharous and 50% in Bourem) is much lower in areas where security problems are significant, and modern contraceptive prevalence rate remains very low, at 1.49% and 11.48% respectively in Gourma Rharous and Bourem. Financial barriers, lack of information, poor availability of services, and insecurity limit access to quality care in HF, both for IDPs and populations living in remote or insecure areas. In this context, the provision of services through fixed health structures is insufficient to cover the needs of the population, particularly those living in remote and hard-to-reach areas and IDPs. In addition, the capacity of HF to organize mobile strategies to offer services is minimal, particularly in terms of logistics, financial and human resources. Vaccination coverage such as measles (82% in Gourma Rharous and 52% in Bourem) remains below the desired target values (>95%). This is mainly due to difficult access and insecurity, poor implementation of advanced strategies and population displacement. In both Gourma Rharous and Bourem, the only functional referral and evacuation systems for medical, surgical and obstetrical emergencies are those supported by IMC with funding from DG ECHO. Without this support, the referral system is virtually non-existent, increasing the risk of death for many of the patients that require emergency services. Thanks to ECHO funding, an average of more than 200 referrals for life-saving emergencies reasons per year are supported (including transportation by the ambulances deployed by IMC) in the Gourma Rharous and Bourem HDs. MHPSS : The deteriorating security situation, marked is a major source of stress, making the population more vulnerable to mental health problems. Discussions with communities during the assessment revealed that people were facing significant emotional problems. Fear and despair were mentioned by 82% and 63% of households respectively. Anxiety and sadness were also identified by communities in focus groups as mental health problems in the community. The high number of IDPs is another indication of the importance of mental health problems. Indeed, population displacement is known to be a major risk factor for the occurrence of mental disorders. Indeed, it has been shown that around 30% of populations displaced by conflict suffer from depression or post-traumatic stress. Despite the magnitude of needs, mental health services are sorely lacking. Indeed, the only HF offering mental health care are those supported by IMC in the target zone. Thus, our evaluation did not reveal any MHPSS actors outside IMC in the 2 targeted HDs. Nutrition : the nutritional situation in the Gao and Timbuktu regions remains alarming. According to the nutrition cluster's HNRP, by 2024, the number of people requiring emergency nutritional assistance to ensure their survival has risen from 2.5 million in 2023 to 3.3 million, representing an increase of 35%. The results of the SMART 2023 survey carried in August 2023 recorded a GAM rate of 12.5% in the Timbuktu region and 15.3% in the Gao region, with a SAM rate of 2.3% in Timbuktu and 1.8% in Gao. According to the IPC September 2023 analysis, the Bourem and Gourma Rharous health districts targeted for nutritional intervention in the proposed project are classified in phase 4 (critical situation) and phase 3 (serious situation) respectively. This situation is expected to continue until May 2024. GBV : the deteriorating security situation increases risk of GBV and human rights violations. According to the GBVIMS, 8,653 cases of GBV were reported between January and September 2023. Sexual violence remains predominant, accounting for 51% of incidents, with 39% involving rape. Physical and psychological assaults account for 18% and 10% respectively. This has been confirmed by the results of the analysis carried out by the International Medical Corps (IMC) in November 2023.
