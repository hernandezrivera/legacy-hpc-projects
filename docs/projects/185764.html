Project Summary

"The roll out of the Myanmar Curriculum Pilot (MCP) in the Rohingya refugee camps is undisputedly a significant milestone in the goal to ensure quality education for all. The pilot represents a timely opportunity for UNFPA to promote and provide accurate and age-appropriate knowledge, attitudes, and skills on sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR), prevention and response to gender-based violence (GBV), gender equality and diversity among Rohingya adolescents and youth. This will help Rohingya young people to take control and make informed desicions about their bodies, sexuality, relationships freely and responsibly. The comprehensive sexuality education (CSE) is also important as it will help Rohingya young people reflect on social and gender norms, cultural values and traditional beliefs, in order to better understand and manage their relationships with peers, parents, other adults and their communities and as well become the agent of change in their communities.
To this end, UNFPA proposes to deliver CSE programme for adolescent and youth through learning centres and community network schools to complement the MCP, given widespread prevalence of GBV, poor knowledge about SRHR, and challenge normalized notion of child marriage as well as social and gender norms that perpetuate these harmful practices. Building on the experiences and knowledge of implementing “Gender Equity Movement in Schools' in Bangladesh for a decade and UNFPA aims to contextualize the CSE curriculum for Ronhigya young people targeting girls and boys ages 10-19 years given their learning and development delays.
Through this programme, UNFPA will promote menstrual health and ensure availibility of menstrual products at the learning centers and community network schools to remove access barriers as well as challenge harmful social norms associated with menstruation. Rohingya young people will be able to access adequate and complete information in addition to counselling through Alapon Rohingya helpline that was established by UNFPA in December 2020. CSE programme will dismantle taboos around GBV and SRHR and will focus on respectful, equal, consensual, and healthy relationship building. This will also focus on changing existing harmful social and gender norms, values and behavior. Through effective SRHR and GBV information, awareness, skillset delivery programme backed up by the services offered at Women Friendly Spaces, Youth centers, Primary health centers, the programme will contribute to reduction of GBV, unwanted pregnancies, and the spread of HIV and other STIs, as well child marriage. The programme will be for, with, and by young people and hence young people themselves will be engaged at every stage of the implementation. Through peer education, campaign and empowerment activities, Rohingya adolescents and youth will lead creation of new norms for respectful, violence-free relationships, and future parenting. This program will be closely aligned with the education sector, child and adolescent-friendly disaster risk reduction approach that would be implemented alongside other modules as part of MCP. It would contribute to building risk resilience of learning centers and schools by supporting school disaster risk management and assessment and support building resilience. This will also support schools to have pre-positioned MHM kits to provide support adolescent girls when needed. UNFPA has been complying with the principles of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) since the designing stage of JRP 2022 and has clear plan to thoroughly follow through during the implementation of JRP 2022. For example, UNFPA used various tools such as FGD guide to review current GEMS manual and consulted with adolescent and youth on their needs when designing modules and activities that would be part of MCP curriculum. Futhermore, UNFPA intends to meaningfully engage, work and actively seek and put forward voices from the communities. "

1 - Identified Needs

The vulnerability assessment conducted by UNFPA in October 2021 revealed that despite many efforts made by the Government and the humanitarin community, education remains inaccessible to many children and adolescents. COVID 19 pandemic has exacerbated the learning and education loss even further as all education services stopped for over 18 months in the camps as well as in the host communities contributing to increased school drop out rates. Adolescent girls are not attending classes and are at much higher risk of child marriage, while boys typically start working. Limitations on the use of technology, radios, phones and tablets remain and is therefore widening the critical gaps in accss to education for refugee children and youth. The assessment also revealed that women and girls in the Rohingya refugee camps are disproportionately at risk of GBV. Some of the contributing factors include: inadequate and/or unsafe infrastructure, poverty, and social norms that condone violence against them. Many adolescents might face injuries resulting from violence or an accident, experience mental health or substance abuse conditions, go through early childbearing which includes increased risks for newborns and young mothers. Therefore it is critical to provide services related to Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights. While the government and humanitarian community put a lot of effort into addressing the basic needs of affected communities, a majority of respondents’ who participated in Vulnerability assessment reported having at least some unmet key needs. The most common needs identified include education services, sanitation facilities, household necessities, and health care services.
