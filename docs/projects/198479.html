Project Summary

This project’s objective is to contribute to the reduction of mortality and morbidity of the most vulnerable, IDPs and host community, including children under 5, pregnant and lactating women, adolescent girls and women of reproductive age, the elderly, and people with disabilities, in the hard-to-reach areas of Pulka and Monguno (respectively Gwoza and Monguno LGA), and in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC). To meet the most vulnerable populations’ urgent and complex needs, and ensure a long-lasting impact, integrated multi-sectoral activities in health, nutrition, protection, psychosocial support (PSS), food security, and livelihoods, will be implemented. The response will also be based on a robust community-based approach, with community activities planned for all sectors to ensure beneficiaries' and communities' ownership of the project. This comprehensive approach and the proposed activities align with all the sectors’ and HRP's priorities, including targeting hard-to-reach areas in Northeast Nigeria’s Borno State, the state most affected by the large-scale humanitarian crisis.
Premiere Urgence Internationale will continue to provide free quality life-saving healthcare services to IDPs and host community in the 9 facilities the organization already supports in Maiduguri MC, Monguno, and Pulka. These services include free outpatient consultations to all age groups and comprehensive sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRH) services. SRH activities comprise ante/postnatal care, 24 hours skilled birth delivery services, including Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmONC), and Gender-based Violence (GBV) treatment with CMR (Clinical Management of Rape).
PUI will continue to provide life-saving treatment to children under 5 suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) by supporting 4 OTPS across all three locations (one in Maiduguri’s Bollori II, two in Monguno, and one in Pulka), for treatment of SAM without medical complications, and three Stabilization Centres (SCs), one in every three locations, to provide treatment to SAM with medical complications. In case further emergencies arise, PUI plans to increase its SCs' bed capacities in Monguno and Pulka, and to establish three more OTPs. The nutrition response will include systemic identification and referrals of children suffering from SAM to PUI-supported OTPs and SCs, and awareness-raising sessions and sensitization implemented at community level.
PUI will continue to implement a range of activities to contribute to strengthening targeted protection services to address the needs and vulnerability of persons at heightened risks of violence and exploitation, with tailored responses for GBV survivors, through internal/external referrals whenever needed (i.e legal assistance). To prevent GBV and violence, awareness sessions for women and girls on their rights, as well as for community members, boys, men will be conducted. Community knowledge on protection will be strengthened, with key community stakeholders receiving training on protection issues, including GBV, mental health, PSS, to in turn support identification of cases and concerns, and ensure referrals. PUI will continue to ensure protection monitoring in the intervention areas, to ensure risk identification, referrals, concerted and tailored interventions, as well as advocacies with partners at all levels.
To promote the targeted beneficiaries' self-reliance, and reduce their dependency on humanitarian assistance, PUI will provide food assistance to 5,390 vulnerable individual beneficiaries on a monthly basis for 12 months, as food e-vouchers or e-cash. PUI will identify 700 individuals among beneficiaries of PUI Nutrition services and survivors of GBV or persons exposed to Protection risks due to their economic vulnerability. These persons will receive a training on the management of small business, and a start capital to launch an Income Generating Activity (IGA) of their choice.

1 - Needs

Borno State accounts for 76% of the total IDPs of the Northeast, with 1,865,175 Internally Displaced Persons, and for half of the 8.4 million people in need in the region, 80% of whom are women and children. The LGAs of Maiduguri M.C, Monguno, and Gwoza are respectively the second, third, and fourth LGA with the highest numbers of IDPs in the whole area: 240,580 in MMC, 160,239 in Monguno, and 159,584 IDPs in Gwoza, where Pulka is located, who are experiencing high level of vulnerability. With 60% of them living in host communities, access to already limited essential services and resources is further constrained.Difficulties to access an adequate food diet, in sufficient quantities -- 1.9 million are in need of food assistance in Borno State -- is a cause of high prevalence of malnutrition in Borno State in general, and in the hard-to-reach areas, such as Monguno and Pulka. These two LGAs are currently in IPC AMN Phase 4 or “critical” phase (IPC, November 2022). PUI’s SMART survey conducted in August-September 2022 indicated Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates of 24.4% and 12.9% in Monguno and Pulka town respectively, Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rates respectively were 10% and 2.9%. In Maiduguri, GAM rate was 14.4% and SAM rate 3.8%. These rates are above WHO thresholds and very concerning. The overall prevalence of acute malnutrition is generally highest among young children (6-23 months) compared to older age groups (24-59 months); in Borno’s hard-to-reach areas, GAM rates in September 2022 among young children ranged between 22.4% and 25.7%, whereas the range was 14.3% to 17% among older age groups, as highlighted in the Humanitarian Situation Monitoring (HSM) Bulletin of September 2022. Food consumption deficit and limited diversity diet are the main direct causes of this high prevalence of acute malnutrition. 45.4% of households in hard-to-reach areas have a poor or borderline food consumption score (HSM, September 2022). In Monguno, where several partners temporarily suspended their health activities in June – September 2022, access to basic health services has further shrunk. PUI is now the only international partner to provide 24 hours Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmONC). Pulka, which counts 135,000 inhabitants, half of whom IDPs, has only two Primary HealthCare centers and one Maternal and Child (MCH) one, but no secondary healthcare facility. In the two cities, access to Sexual Reproductive Health and Rights (SRH) is still low, as in Northeast Nigeria: the region has the country’s highest maternal mortality rate, with 1,549 women dying per 100,000 live births. In Maiduguri’s Bollori II, internal data from the Ngarannam facility PUI supports shows that more than 70% of the women who came for delivery services in September and October were from outside our catchment population area. The three locations remain prone to outbreaks. In September, a cholera outbreak was declared in 7 of Borno State LGAs, including MMC and Gwoza, which has since then recorded 252 cases, and which had already been affected by an outbreak in 2021. In September, in Monguno, devastating floods severely damaged cropland and shelters, resulting in secondary displacement and the deterioration of the sanitary environment. According to the Protection sector, IDPs face catastrophic levels of protection incidents. July-August 2022 GBV MIS report, 77% of all 249 GBV incidents recorded in the BAY states were recorded in Borno, and 34% of the total in Gwoza LGA alone. 95% of all incidents were perpetrated against women and girls, and in 56% of the cases by intimate partners. The GBV incidents concerned denial of resources (35%), forced or early marriages (32%), rape and sexual assault (4% each), and physical assault (14%). GBV however remains under-reported as survivors face stigma, discrimination. In January 2022 PUI Monitoring report 73% of women and girls mentioned the same reasons as barriers to access of health services.

2 - Activities

PUI will continue to provide, in the 3 locations, treatment of SAM with medical complications to children aged 0-59 months in SCs, and treatment of SAM without medical complications in OTPs, while increasing its bed capacity in the supported SCs and the number of supported OTPs in case of emergency. Community involvement in nutrition activities will remain key to ensure a long-term impact of the activities: Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNMs) will conduct active screening of malnutrition cases, nutrition awareness-raising sessions at community level, and train Mother Care Groups (MCGs), established to promote Maternal and Infant Young Child Nutrition practices (MIYCN). MIYCN will also be promoted in OTPs through an MYICN nurse. In the facilities, as part of PUI's sustainable and exit strategy, capacity building of Ministry of Health (MoH) staff on the management of SAM will be ensured. PUI will ensure the provision of supplies and essential medicines to OTPs.PUI will provide, in the 3 locations, outpatient consultation services, and comprehensive Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) services, including basic emergency obstetric care (BEmONC), and family planning services. Delivery rooms will be established to facilitate deliveries and referrals of complicated cases. PUI will support the MoH for the immunization of pregnant women and newborn babies. The health facilities will be capacitated to provide medical assistance to GBV through clinical management of rape (CMR) cases. Sexually transmitted diseases will be treated. PUI will provide medicines, medical supplies, and equipment for SRHR activities. Capacity building of MoH staff will be ensured.PUI will continue implementing its protection response through a community outreach methodology that has proven effective, with the establishment of local community outreach volunteer networks, and skilled protection monitors. Small group awareness sessions and campaigns on general protection issues, PSS, and GBV-related issues will be conducted, to enable and support in turn identification and referrals of cases and concerns at community level. Protection helpdesks will be established to serve as information centers, and to receive self-referrals from the community. PUI will continue i) to ensure protection monitoring in the intervention areas, with key informant interviews in the community and household level surveys, resulting in reports shared with the sector and partners to ensure risks are identified, concerted and tailored answers provided, and to inform protection advocacy; ii) to disseminate information on available services through mapping services. PUI will continue to implement Adolescent girls's safe space activities, meant to enable them develop positive coping mechanisms, and to provide them with life skills as well as information about SRHR; and its "Engaging Men through Accountability Practices" (EMAP) program, to help prevent and respond to GBV cases with the active involvement of men. GBV survivors will provided case management services, Individual Protection Assistance (IPA), dignity kits and PSS, and all services needed through internal referrals and external ones when required (legal assistance).5390 vulnerable individual beneficiaries will receive food assistance on a monthly basis for 12 months, as food e-vouchers or e-cash redeemable at partner vendors operating within the targeted area. The disbursement will cover 70% of the basic food needs of the beneficiaries and the cooking fuel costs. 700 individuals among beneficiaries of PUI Nutrition services and survivors of GBV or persons exposed to Protection risks due to their economic vulnerability. These persons will be trained on the management of small business and will be provided a start capital to launch an Income Generating Activity of their choice. This support is expected to promote self-reliance of the targeted beneficiaries and reduce their dependency on humanitarian assistance.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Premiere Urgence Internationale works with a holistic approach, guaranteeing the centrality of protection and ensuring cross-cutting protection and gender issues are integrated across all sectors at all stages of implementation and monitoring. PUI also strives for the safety and dignity of people and to minimize unintended adverse effects of its interventions, with all of its programme having the minimum requirement to apply the “do-no-harm” approach. All interventions are based on the needs and gaps identified through participatory assessments conducted with different groups of the targeted populations, be it Focus Groups Discussions, Key Informant interviews, or community-based and beneficiary-based dialogues. The targeted population is consulted in all phases of the project cycle (design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation) to ensure the response is adapted to its needs and capacities. In this process, different groups from diverse backgrounds are engaged to ensure meaningful access to services, participation, and empowerment of communities. PUI hence ensures accountability to beneficiaries, guarantees equal access, and consider gender needs. Premiere Urgence Internationale ensures this way that women and girls, but also people with disabilities are part of the decision-making process, and give an enlightened advice on the modalities of the project. PUI will moreover ensure regular analysis of the local protection environment to ensure that the implementation of its different activities does not put beneficiaries, especially women and girls, and people with disabilities, at greater risks of violence, abduction, or abuse. For instance, latrines, but also showers, will be gender-sensitive, separated for women and boys, and equipped as needed or required for people with disabilities or reduced mobility to be able to use them, as will be health facilities supported. In order to facilitate meaningful access, PUI adopt multiple approaches, including sharing information about the available services with the populations of concern and ensuring the disability-friendliness of the facilities and activities. With regards to disability inclusion, PUI also diversifies the communication and information-sharing channels to improve communication with people suffering from different types of disability (e.g. pictorial aid, mega phones, etc.). PUI trains its staff in protection mainstreaming and raise their awareness of the protection principles, Code of Conduct, PSEA, etc. to ensure compliance with safeguarding principles. PUI pays special attention to specific vulnerabilities in order to reduce barriers to healthcare access by implementing anactive and efficient triage of patients to ensure the most vulnerable are given priority. In all interventions, gender dynamics/norms will be thoroughly analyzed and mainstreamed in the activities (e.g. separated waiting areas (male/female) when feasible; gender-balanced teams to cater for the different needs of both males and females, etc.). PUI attaches high importance to the safety and confidentiality of the beneficiaries and ensures that all the staff are aware of these principles, specificallydata protection protocols. In addition to GBVIMS (Gender-Based Violence Information Management Sysytem) information sharing protocol, PUI has developed its internal data protection protocol, signed and adhered to by all relevant staff.Premiere Urgence Internationale MEAL department has accessible and well-understood mechanisms for suggestions and complaints (see below) in the project it implements, in coordination with the protectioncoordinator and Human resources department to ensure that all complaints mechanisms are in line with PUI policies and Protection cluster recommendations.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Premiere Urgence Internationale's commitment is to make sure that communities, beneficiaries, and among them the most vulnerable members, can provide feedback, make suggestions or express, file complaints if required. PUI has developed a community-based feedback system that consists of multiple entry points such as the helpline, which is free of charge for the beneficiaries and functional at any time during the working hours/days, the mobile helpdesk, community volunteers' networks, and monthly Community Committee Meetings. These are bilateral meetings with beneficiary representatives. PUI also regularly conducts key informant interviews (KII) and FGDs to understand the evolving context of its intervention areas as part of the monitoring strategy. Besides, PUI works alongside community leaders and representatives to gather inputs that influence the program design and implementation, the targeting methodology, the gaps, and unaddressed needs analysis. These channels are reviewed yearly following beneficiary feedback to ensure the mechanism is inclusive, context-specific, and accessible safely and confidentially by women, girls, boys, and men. Accountability teams are composed of both male and female members who speak the most popular language of the area of intervention to allow beneficiaries to easily express their concerns. To ensure a consistent feedback management process across the intervention areas, SOPs emanating from the organizational accountability field guide are in place to track and respond to complaints and feedback. The SOPs describe steps for managing both non-sensitive and sensitive cases (e.g, fraud, corruption, PSEA, child protection). A well-trained accountability team is responsible for collecting, documenting, and categorizing the complaints and feedback from the different channels in a dedicated secured database.
