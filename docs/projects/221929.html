Project Summary

This project is for twelve-month and will be implemented in Pibor, Renk, Malakal, Maban, Mayendit, Rubkona, Leer and Yirol East counties designed to provide inclusive quality education to girls and boys and those living with disabilities, affected by floods, food insecurity and conflict. The interventions will target 1,1304 as direct beneficiaries (197F, 197M and 5455G, 5455B). The men and women targeted by this project will be within the age of 18-60 years while the school age going learners are those between 3-17 years and out of this target, at least 15% of adults, youth and children will be People Living with Disabilities (based on the cluster recommendation). The deliverables mainly focus on Early Childhood Education, Primary and Alternative Education Program and secondary school levels of learning in all targeted counties. This project is designed to create access of learners to inclusive learning institutions while addressing barriers such as poor school accessibility, lack of scholastic materials, school retention for girls and WASH facilities for children's education. In addition, it will also improve the quality of education by building the capacity of teachers through continuous professional development tailored to increase their teaching skills. The program will also enhance capacity of local education authorities such as County and Payam Education Supervisors and Inspectors through strengthening their skills of school supervision, report writing and how to develop strategic plans alongside equipping their offices. This will improve the already ravaged system of education in the selected counties and ensure delivery of quality education services to schools is attained. Also the project shall distribute teaching and age-appropriate learning materials to both teachers and learners targeted since a lack of school supplies has been identified as one of the factors causing poor attendance and learning outcomes in schools. Moreover, the project intends to create better learning environments in schools through rehabilitation of temporary inclusive learning spaces to cater for all learners including CWDs. Additionally, the program plans to renovate those schools that have been destroyed by conflict and floods to enable learners access education in targeted locations while ensuring that gender segregated latrines and WASH facilities are established in schools lacking such facilities to mitigate girl’s hygiene challenges while attending classes. Also distribution of sanitary materials to adolescent girls alongside training them on MHM and SRHR issues will be implemented by this action such that girl’s attendance and retention in schools is improved. The project will further establish Roll Models in the communities who shall engage community members on importance of education of children especially the girl child and CWDs. This will be done in close coordination with the PTAs/SMC as well as teachers who will be trained on school development as well as community outreach activities to engage parents and guardians of school children and youth on education related issues

1. Needs

The HNRP 2024 estimates 2 M school children lack access to quality education services and only 0.75 M were targeted for education interventions. Children and youth aged 3-17 yrs are the most affected yet out of this number, 53% are girls pausing educational barrier to education of the girl child. The enrolment trends of girls in schools continue to deteriorate as parents prefer educating boys since girls are a source of wealth through payment of bride price from husbands who marry them at early age. In addition, schools have limited learning kits, learning spaces as well as qualified teachers pausing additional challenges in accessing quality education. Moreover, the South Sudan Government press release of March 2024 by the Ministry of Health temporarily closed schools a measure to mitigate the extreme heatwave. This worsened the situation as millions of learners lost significant time without accessing learning institutions adding more challenges to the already week education system. UNHCR report of Oct 2024 reveal that 810,000 returnees & refugees from Sudan conflict live in Upper Nile. The victims are in Malakal, Renk and Maban and many are integrated in the communities while others are in camps. Unfortunately, most of them are school going age children and girls are the majority. This has stressed the already limited education services and facilities in Upper Nile which has resulted to increased demand for heavy investment in education services. Yet the Nov 2024 report of UN OCHA identified presence of cholera disease in Renk which is likely to spread to neighboring counties. As such, there is huge need for preparedness and response actions including WASH interventions in all learning institutions. The Oct 2024 by OCHA report stressed the intensive flooding in Unity state, warning that there is need to fund basic services including education as a response mechanism to the flood affected population. The flooding affected most counties including Rubkona, Leer & Mayendict and some schools are already submerged in water causing excessive damages while disrupting children access to learning facilities. Therefore, schools will require heavy rehabilitations and refurnish them with the necessary supplies and equipment. The situation worsened due to the Cholera outbreak in Unity state as report by OCHA in Nov this year. This response plan will definitely increase the education services in order to make schools Cholera free zones through funding the necessary preventive measure required by WHO. Furthermore, the 2023 Response Plan for Pibor by OCHA reveals enormous education challenges in GPAA due to cultural norms that prohibit education opportunities for children specifically girls. The communities in GPAA do not value education and they prefer their girls to be married out at the age of 12 year in exchange for cattle from the husbands of the girls. This has been emphasized by OXFAM report of July 2024 noting that GPAA hunger crisis has deepened making most residents eat wild vegetables collected by school age children particularly girls. Moreover, the EMIS data states that over 140,000 school-aged children are living in Pibor and nearly half of them are not accessing education services. This project will implement strong education interventions that will re-enroll children to schools while tackling the negative norms against education. In addition, communities in Lakes state are cattle keepers who prefer their children to look after animals compared to schooling. Therefore, school age children in most counties including Yirol East are looking after cattle. Also, the economic crisis has affected the communities hindering parents to support education needs of children and as such, a significant number of children do not access schools. Yet there is limited government support to finance education interventions (GPE Transforming Education 2024). This project will provide education services to enhance access of children to schools

2. Project activities

The project activities and objectives are aligned to the needs on the ground but also based on the HRP 2024 as well as those of education cluster. In addition, this project will be implemented and monitored using United Nations Girls Education Initiative (UNGEI) gender-based toolkit and INEE minimum standards to address barriers to children’s education particularly girls. Overall Objective: To increase access to inclusive quality education for IDPs, returnees and host community children and youth affected by, conflict, hunger and floods in Renk, Malakal, Maban, Mayendit, Rubkona, Leer, Yirol East and Pibor counties of Jonglei, Lakes, Unity and Upper Nile states as targeted by the education cluster. Objective 1: To strengthen community engagement and support in fostering children access to quality education through enhanced community participation and management of learning institutions. Objective 2: To increase enrolment of learners especially girls and CWDs in all targeted schools. Objective 1 Activities 1. Establish and strengthen 40 (20F,20M) Community Education Committees or role models 2. Facilitate quarterly Education dialogues with the communities facilitated by the role models 3. Facilitate Bi-annual PTA and SMC exchange visits in 50 targeted schools in selected counties 4. Establish and strengthen the role of adolescent girls clubs in 50 primary schools. 5. Facilitate annual Parents Day in 50 schools to talk about cultural barriers to education. 6. Support capacity development of 80 (40F, 40M) PTA/SMC members through trainings to improve management and development of schools. 7. Train 120 (60F, 60M) primary school and ALP volunteer teachers on continuous professional development to improve their teaching skills 8. Train 90 (45F, 45M) ECE facilitators on better methods of teaching in 50 ECE centers. 9. Provide incentives to 210 (105F,105M) primary and ECD school volunteer teachers to motivate them to provide quality services in supported schools. 10. Procure and distribute teaching materials to 120 (60F, 60M) primary & ALP teachers in supported schools. 11. Provide teaching materials to 90 (45F, 45M) ECE facilitators in 50 supported ECE centers. 12. Support quarterly Joint monitoring of schools with local education authorities in 50 schools. 13. Train 40 (20F, 20M) project staff and volunteers on PHSEA and Safeguarding of beneficiaries. Objective 2: Activities 1. Conduct back to learning campaigns to increase access of children and youth in 50 supported primary schools, ALP centers and ECE facilities 2. Rehabilitate of 8 climate resilient classroom blocks to increase enrolment of learners including girls in targeted primary schools and ECE centers. 3. Construct 8 inclusive gender segregated latrines in supported primary schools and ECE centers. 4. Establish 8 Champion of Change clubs to increase girl’s rights to education in targeted locations 5. Distribute sanitary kits to 2000 adolescent girls in all supported schools in the targeted locations 6. Distribute learning materials to 7908 (3954B, 3954G) learners in supported primary and ALP schools. 7. Procure and distribute ECE kits and recreational materials to 3002 (1501B, 1501G) learners in 50 supported ECE centers. 8. Provide hygiene kits to 30 primary schools. 9. Train 2000 adolescent girls in 50 primary schools on MHM and making of reusable sanitary pads 10. Train 24 (12F, 12M) Local Education Authorities on supervision and monitoring of schools. 11. Conduct disability assessment to 1636 (818G, 818B) Children Living with Disability and provide basic assistive devices as well as referring to specialized partners 12. Conduct awareness raising on SRHR targeting 3000 adolescents (2000G,1000B) in all targeted schools.
