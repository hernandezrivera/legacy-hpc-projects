Project Summary

We will continue to deliver comprehensive, service-based protection programs prioritizing the needs of the most at-risk and vulnerable individuals including IDPs and host community children, adolescent girls, Boys, caregivers, and persons with disabilities in line with specific objective 2.1 of the HNO (Specific protection needs are mitigated through the provision of quality and integrated protection services). We will expand our case management program to support children and their caregivers through provide comprehensive case management plan, PSS and parenting support, CFP and referrals to specialized services to ensure at-risk individuals are provided with holistic inter-sectoral support. For complex protection cases, requiring specialist protection services such GBV services will be referred to other agencies within NES in line with interagency guidelines on referral pathways. Likewise, other humanitarian actors in NES who is not providing the case management services will refer cases to case management team. At the same time case management team will receive cases through internal referrals from other sectoral programs within the organization. We will ensure meaningful access through our outreach Program and mobile case management team. We will expand support to isolated communities in the western countryside of Deir-ez-Zor, and remote areas in Tabqa, Menbij and Kobane. which are currently underserved by humanitarian actors while also expanding our existing outreach and case management capacity in Manbij, Kobani, Raqqa, Dier ez Zour and Tabqa where we are one of the few NGOs providing these services. In 2022 the case management activities will include children who is victims to the be involve with armed conflict and armed forces through providing case management services and enable theses to benefit from our services to have access to education and livelihood opportunities. This collaboration between the organization and the CAAFAG TF will remain within the organisational capacity to provide assistance in areas such as Kobane and Menbij and Tabqa where there is no other protection NGO with protection capacity.
In 2022, with the protection team initiative they start to organize for coordination monthly meeting in 5 location with local and international INGOs who is delivering child protection activities and activities related to children which they can provide support to the children through referrals. The meeting aims to update service mapping, case discussions, highlighting gaps and planning response to child protection topics.

We will continue to support some of the most isolated IDP settlements in NES by operating a minimum of 12 child-friendly spaces (CFS) will target children between 5-11, and adolescent friendly spaces GFS and BFS the target age 12-17 with an increased focus on Deir-ez-Zor. In addition, hard to reach areas. In order to achieve reaching to isolated areas we start operating GFS mobile team in Menbij and Kobane. These spaces provide structured and systemic PSS, life skills and awareness activities to children and adolescents and educate parents on parenting skills and protection-related topics to build resilience through learning positive coping mechanisms. These spaces also help us develop community acceptance in newly accessible areas, in turn strengthening the outreach program by providing us with sustained and extensive access throughout the community.

4 - Needs

Continued conflict and crises, war, forced movement, lack of services and access associated with lack of water and bad economical situations have led to a severe protection crisis in Syria with children and women in particular exposed to serious protection risks. Inadequate living conditions in makeshift settlements or overcrowded sites, particularly in NES, as well as the loss of livelihoods, property and asset compound protection risks, including risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection concerns. The assessment shard by HNO numbers indicating that the people in need for protection services in Aleppo 523000 in need for protection services while the number reached was 188000 and Der-ezor almost have the people in need was reach. at the same time it shows that boys and girls between 12-17 years where only targeted by 13% of the total reach, Moreover, the COVID-19 pandemic and deteriorating economy have had serious consequences on the safety and wellbeing of vulnerable groups. In NES protection survey analysis for 2021 the papulation adapting negative coping mechanism such as pegging and engaging with illegal activities, at the same time women and children they remain at the top of the venerable target in NES. The protection TWG shard information highlighting the protection need was identified which put child marriage, child labor and lack of documentation on top of the risks.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will support 5,840 Children through a minimum of 12 Child Friendly Spaces across NES targeting children 5- 11 years old and adolescent’s spaces targeting 12-17 year old. The NGO will provide structured group based PSS activities and life skills activities and unstructured recreational and awareness activities at each site based on a curriculum. Through the program activities, the NGO will be prioritizing underserved areas, including informal settlements where there is no access to formal education or community-based PSS support. The organization will monitor CFS activities through field visits reports, at the same time we will evaluate the progress of children through pre-post test for each cycle to masseur sense of safety and wellbeing and Child and Youth Resilience. We will also provide dignity kits to 500 women and girls with GBV risks. 1,920 caregivers will benefit from awareness-raising sessions; will focus on protection topics such; children’s rights, positive parenting and communication with the child, child labor and the importance of education. This activity will be delivered twice a month. Through case management, the program will provide comprehensive support to 700 from children and their caregivers, during the duration of the project we will support case management beneficiaries with PSS and parenting session, CFP will be giving to beneficiaries to reduce or mitigate protection risk referral to other and specialized services, prioritizing children.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project priorities the importance of PSEA by explicitly addressing the issue in its Programme Participant Protection Policy and staff Code of Conduct, which together define our expectations of staff and partners, their responsibilities as well as a mandatory duty to report in cases of potential abuse. These policies are backed up by a Community Response Mechanism open to and accessibly to staff, beneficiaries and the general public to provide multiple means of reporting. In the specific case Syria. The project is committed to supporting and engaging with the current Inter-Agency Standing Committee PSEA Expansion Plan for the Whole of Syria - to date three staff have been trained as internal focal points for PSEA and are involved on rolling out PSEA training and awareness sessions for all staff.
