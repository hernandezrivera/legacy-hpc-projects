Project Summary

Mercy USA will assemble and train nutrition support teams, and referral network across Ma'arrat An Nu'man subdistrict.
This project aims to support the affected population in the targeted area
39032 (children under five and pregnant and lactating women )
13176 PLWs 25856 Children under five years old
The nutrition support team consist of:
· 36 community healthcare workers (Trained in IYCF, and CMAM according to WHO guidelines.)
· One monitoring and evaluation officer.
· One Information manager (trained in case management software management).
· One project manager)
· Two full-time pediatricians
This team will provide community and facility-based nutrition support in 18 communities across Ma'arrat An Nu'man Subdistrict, Idleb, Syria (including camps, informal settlements, and collective shelters also
in schools, community centers, places of worship, health facilities and in partnership with local councils and other stakeholders)
This project will support the establishment of one nutrition support center in Al Mara that will serve as the focal point for all nutrition activities in the region.
Two strategically located health facilities will be equipped with the following:
• Two full-time pediatricians dedicated to nutrition-related (Trained in IYCF, and CMAM according to WHO guidelines.)
• Nutrition equipment & supplies
Activities will include
- Support facility and community based 1-1 counseling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding practices
- Provide Micronutrient supplementation to women and children for prevention of micronutrient deficiencies
- Provide Vitamin A supplementation for children 6-59 through health facilities (routine) and during a campaign
- Prevention of acute malnutrition through the provision of specialized nutritious food to children 6-59 months through health facilities, communities, and alongside
- Facility and community-based screening for acute malnutrition
- Treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW

Needs

In July 2017 Nutrition SMART survey was conducted in Idleb during (10/7/2017 to 14/7/2017). • The prevalence of acute malnutrition (WHZ<-2 and/or edema) was highest among younger age group (6 – 17 month) • The prevalence of stunting, defined as Height-for-age Z scores (HAZ) <‐2 in children 6-59 months was 14.2 % (10.2 – 19.5 95% C.I.), this result indicates that there is a high level of micronutrient deficiency so focusing on prevention of stunting should be strengthened • Data also indicates that anemia rates of 35.29% among children. that required immediate humanitarian assistance potentially reflecting a longer-term inadequate dietary intake, including micronutrients, repeated infections such as diarrhea in younger children, as well as poor feeding practices as reflected by low uptake of exclusive breastfeeding in the first 6 months of life & complementary food in term of quality and frequency. In September 2017 Barrier Analysis of Infant & Young Child Feeding and Maternal Nutrition Behaviors among IDPs in Northern and Southern Syria • This analysis indicates that the lack of knowledge between the PLWs and care caver is not always the main barrier, but access to IYCF services, as well as focus attention on the need for increased integration of IYCF programming activities into other sectors particularly Food Security and others sectors. Mercy-USA, since the Syrian crisis started, has been working on integrating the food security program / Health program with Nutrition program by referring mothers/child’s for nutrition assessment, micronutrient supplementation, and food security / Health intervention as needed and ensure referral/treatment of anemic and malnutrition in mothers and children under five, this integration will provide a comprehensive approach to accomplishing behavior change in regards to complex behaviors in an emergency context.• In March 2017 nutrition cluster with technical support from the global nutrition cluster rapid response team conducted Infant and young child feeding, Knowledge, attitude and practice (KAP assessment) in Aleppo, Idleb and Hama in North Syria governorates, areas accessible from Turkey cross border, the results revealed that: Exclusive breastfeeding rate is 30.9% (25.3% - 36.8% C.I), early initiation 37.8% (34.6% - 41.0% CI), Minimum acceptable diet 57.3% (53.4%- 61.1% CI), which is lower than pre-conflict data of all key indicators with 46% of children in the country initiating breastfeeding within the first hour of life and even less (43%) able to exclusively breastfeed for the first six months of life. However, there is an improvement of the exclusive breast feeding rates compared to post crises in Hama and Idleb 21.2% & 21.1% as per the SMART survey (2014-2015), this may be due to the scale up on infant and young child feeding programme by partners and large scale advocacy and awareness raising on the SOPs for control of random distribution of breast milk substitute (BMS). This was also confirmed by the joint community-based food security and nutrition assessment that was conducted in 8088 communities in 7 governorates in Syria for key IYCF indicators.

Implementation of activities

Mercy USA will assemble and train a nutrition support team and referral network consisting of: · 36 community healthcare workers (Trained in IYCF, and CMAM according to WHO guidelines.)· One monitoring and evaluation officer. · One Information manager (trained in case management software management).· One project manager)· Two full-time pediatricians (in two different facilities in the targeted areas) dedicated to nutrition-related (Trained in IYCF, and CMAM according to WHO guidelines.)This team will provide community and facility-based nutrition support in 19 communities across Ma'arrat AL nu'man Subdistrict, Idleb, Syria (including camps, informal settlements, and collective shelters). This project will support the establishment of one nutrition support center in Al Mara that will serve as the focal point for all nutrition-related referrals and Activities in the region. This will be achieved by Equipping Two strategically located health facilities to conduct nutrition services working alongside the community-based nutrition services Activities : 1- Nutrition equipment & supplies Strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in an emergency, micronutrient interventions and optimal maternal nutrition through the following activities:- Counseling (1-1) and awareness raising on WHO recommended infant and young child feeding IYCF E and optimal maternal nutrition in health facilities, hospitals, pharmacies, and in partnership with mobile clinics, and established first aid, schools, community centers, places of worship, and in partnership with local councils and other stakeholders. - One to one counseling of Pregnant Lactating Women at the household level. - Micronutrient supplementation to women and children for the prevention of micronutrient deficiencies.- Vitamin A supplementation for lactating women through health facilities (routine) and during campaigns.- Distribution of LNS according to nutrition cluster guidelines2- Improve equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services through systematic identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished cases for boys and girls under five and PLWs. This includes the following activities: - Community-based screening and treatment for acute malnutrition (children 6-59 months – PLWs) in schools, community centers, places of worship, and in partnership with local councils and other stakeholders.- Facility-based screening and treatment (children 6-59 months – PLWs for acute malnutrition in health facilities. - Appropriate referral of complicated acutely malnourished children.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Cross border access from Turkey is available.- Easy and smooth Logistical access.- Cooperation from local councils- Facilitation and acceptance from major regional stakeholders/actors (Turkish Government, OAG groups, etc.)- Timely fund dispersal - Targeting of most vulnerable beneficiaries- Recruitment of female staff within teams will be considered.Risks: - General daily security threats including robbery and looting - Macro-level conflict-related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors, shifting alliances, border closure, and chemical attack. - Delays in fund dispersal-Fire hazard and/or conflict-related activities happen during the ongoing implementation. Risk Mitigation: - Following Mercy-USA safety and security guidelines and established safety protocols. - In the event of major macro-level conflict-related threats, Mercy-USA will take appropriate action that is commensurate with the scale of the threat. This may include: - Suspending operations temporarily-Shifting resources and operations to more secure locations - Clear steps will be taken to mitigate risks between staff and beneficiaries including gender-based violence. - Project staff may be dismissed at any time in the event of endangering beneficiaries or other staff, and this will be clearly outlined in each employment contract. - Contingency stock will be in place for shipments to avoid any impact if Bab Alhawa borders become closed (for items purchased from Turkey) - Gender balance and equal opportunities will be taken into consideration when hiring staff for a different position.-All workers will be given the proper needed instructions ahead of any deliveries.-All necessary precautions will be taken to prevent a fire incident moreover, unarmed guard(s) will be available in the facilities at all times, in order to avoid any conflicting activity might happen.
