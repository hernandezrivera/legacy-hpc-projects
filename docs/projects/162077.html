Project Summary

UNICEF aims to reach 210,000 Rohingya children (3-14) with early childhood development and learning opportunities by maintaining the operation of 2,500 learning centres and establishing 500 new LCs under the Government of Bangladesh's "Reaching Out of School Children" project in collaboration with the World Bank. UNICEF will upgrade 230 existing LCs to 115 double-storey, medium-hazard structures, aimed at reducing the cost of repairs as well as contributing to increasing access to safe shelters for the refugee population in case of disaster. Additionally, 31,500 Rohingya adolescents will be provided with integrated life and vocational skills programme in multipurpose centres and secondarly level education using Myanmar National Curriculum as a pilot. The pilot will target 10,000 (out of 21,500) adolescents eligible to enroll at any of the four grades of middle school (Grades 6 to 9) starting from the second quarter of 2020. The curriculum will be delivered as prescribed by the national curriculum framework with adaptations where appropriate for the context of the camp and the needs of Rohingya children. This piloting will provide the formal educational opportunities to the Rohingya adolescents and this is expected to attract more adolescents and youths to prevent them from being vulnerable to trafficking, abuse, violence and anti social behaviours.
In the host communities in Teknaf and Ukhyia, UNICEF will support access to improved quality of education 24,424 children (4-14) in pre-primary and primary schools. The support to host community will include system strengthening component aimed at enhancing the capacity of the district primary and secondary education office to accelerate access to equitable quality education.

Needs

Cox’s Bazar District is currently hosting over 913,000 Rohingya refugees from Myanmar, including 730,000 refugees who arrived since the August 2017 influx. Even though the Government of Bangladesh expects and prioritizes speedy repatriation of the refugees back to Myanmar, so far two attempts of voluntary repatriation have been unsuccessful and there are currently no indications the refugees will voluntarily return soon. In the mean time, with support of the Government of Bangladesh, donors and humanitarian partners, access to basics services, including education has progressively improved as services delivery systems are generally well stablished across the camps. As of September 2019 (the most recent available data) over 294,000 refugee children aged 3-14 years (over 95 per cent of the estimated population) are accessing education in 5,475 learning facilities established by humanitarian actors across the camps. This includes over 2,480 learning centres (LCs) supported by UNICEF in collaboration with 10 implementing partners, enrolling 206,000 Rohingya children aged 3 to 14 years. A multi sector assessment conducted by the ISCG in October also shows that over 86 per cent of children 6-11 years (86 per cent for by and 89 per cent for girls) have access to education in LCs which is in line with the enrolment data reported by education sector partners. However, sustainability of the services in the camps is entirely dependent on external support without which the current gains could quickly be lost. While considerable progress has been made in expanding access to education particularly for children 3-14 years, substantial challenges remain, especially with regards to access to education and skills development for adolescents 15-18 years. The multisector needs assessment conducted by ISCG shows that access to education services diminishes for older adolescents (15-18 years) and youth (19-24 years) especially female adolescents. Only 34 per cent of adolescent girls 12-14 years, compared with (54 per cent for boys) have access to learning in LCs and just 2 per cent of adolescent girls 15-18 years (13 per cent for boys) have access to learning opportunities. In the new JRP, the education sector response needs adopt strategies that are primarily aimed at enhancing access to education and skills development opportunities for older adolescents. Cox's Bazar District is also home to historically one of Bangladesh’s poorest and least disaster-resilient community. National statistics show that 32 per cent of the population in the district falls under the poverty line and child survival and development indicators are amongst the worst in the country. Education outcomes in Cox’s Bazar are also among the lowest in the country, with a 79 per cent net primary enrolment rate compared with the national average of 98 per cent. Similarly, 39.6 per cent of boys and 22.8 per cent of girls in the district drop out of school before completing grade 5, compared to national dropout rates of 22.3 and 16.1 per cent respectively. The influx of Rohingya refugees has therefore exacerbated the pressure on an already overstretched educational infrastructure in deeply deprived communities. The refugee crisis therefore presents an opportunity to address some of the historical deprivations of rights in Cox’s Bazar District and to create a win-win situation for the refugees and their hosts. Addressing the development challenges in the host community will mitigate the impact of hosting the Rohingya refugees, contribute to reducing tensions and increase the prospects of a peaceful relationship between the refugees and the host community.
