Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Somalia is among the most complex and protracted emergencies worldwide. Growing conflict across the country and simultaneous environmental hazards, such as recurrent droughts, hamper the livelihoods of Somalia’s 12 million people.
The assessments by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) and Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWSNET) (Post Gu, 2019) also indicate that, without humanitarian aid, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia (including Somaliland) will face severe hunger through December 2019. FEWSNET and FSNAU findings further anticipate the livelihood zones of Eastern Somaliland to be at high risk and to slide from phase 2 (stressed) to phase 3 (crises) or phase 4 (emergency), within the next months. Widespread malnutrition persists across Somalia and one million children are expected to be acutely malnourished over the next year, including 180’000 children who are likely to become severely malnourished in case the humanitarian community fails to scale up its support. Finally, the analysis projects more than 2.6 million people to be internally displaced across the country, in either rural or urban settings.
Also, an assessment (March 2019) by Candlelight with one of its partners revealed that poor food security levels in Togdheer and Sool was partly due to poor food access; the dietary diversity for households is limited to only 3 food items or less, composed of mainly carbohydrates. Also, based on the analysis, most of the children between the age of 6-59 months suffering from acute malnutrition were mostly from families with limited income or in which the parents were not engaged in any income generating activity at all. The study concludes that providing lifesaving food assistance by means of Conditional or Unconditional Cash Transfer will improve access to food for the most affected families in the proposed target villages. The analysis also stresses the importance of women as main caregivers and their potential to act as key players in humanitarian aid.
The proposed project’s intervention logic will put emphasis on relieving the distress suffered by affected families, preventing their situation from getting worse and strengthening collective resilience mechanisms.
Against this background and in line with the Food Security Cluster guidelines, the project aims at providing lifesaving and resilience building assistance to the most vulnerable and affected families. The overall objective of the suggested project is that the food and livelihood security situation of vulnerable IDP and host community families in Eastern Somaliland is stabilized and communities are more resilient towards adverse weather conditions and conflict. In order to achieve the objective, the project offers: a) unconditional and conditional cash transfers for improved access to food and b) agriculture and livelihood support activities (Trainings, quality seeds support and animal restocking)
Cash has been prioritized based on Candlelight’s previous experience and best practices in cash transfer programming. Cash especially unconditional is an ideal intervention because of the freedom and dignity it gives to the beneficiaries, its limited administrative requirements and reduced risk. Conditional cash transfers support to achieve a range of objectives and will result in increased access to food, short term employment opportunities and re-building of communal infrastructure. Assessments by Candlelight and other agencies reveal that in most of the villages, markets and small shops can easily cope with the demand for food created by the cash transfers; thus boosting the local markets.
The project will target 11,060 beneficiaries (Men, Women, boys and girls) and will have a duration of 12 months (January 2020 – December 2020) to allow families to recover from adverse weather conditions.

Project Activities

Component 1: Emergency Conditional and Unconditional Cash for atleast 600 householdsThe proposed project will support atleast 600 food insecure pastoral and agro-pastoral households with unconditional cash transfers. This will be delivered on a monthly basis for atleast three months especially during the growing/ lean seasons until the harvest becomes available.Conditional cash transfers through Cash for Work (CfW), will be in the light of recurrent droughts, where it has become apparent that the collective resilience of drought-affected communities should be fostered. In close consultations with affected communities and village leaders communal assets such as rangelands will be selected for rehabilitation through cash for work in preparation for the rains. In addition, the CfW component is aimed at providing short term employment opportunities to the people who are able to work. The cash values to be transferred on a monthly basis are provided by the Cash Working Group (CWG) Somalia based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB). The MEB takes into consideration a minimum culturally adjusted mix of basic food items and non-food items (NFI) a Somali family requires per month. The latest cash transfer values for families in Togdheer are USD 75 and USD 70 for Sool and Sanag. Cash has been prioritized based on Candlelight’s previous experience and best practices in cash transfer programming. Cash especially unconditional is an ideal intervention because of the freedom and dignity it gives to the beneficiaries to choose what type of food they can buy, when and where to buy, its limited administrative requirements and reduced risks. Conditional cash transfers support to achieve a range of objectives and will result in increased access to food, short term employment opportunities and re-building of communal infrastructure. Assessments by Candlelight and other agencies reveal that in most of the villages in the target regions, markets and small shops can easily cope with the demand for food created by the cash transfers; thus boosting the local markets. In terms of coordination (transfer amounts, frequency and targeting criteria), Candlelight is an active member of the Cash working group and the Food security sub cluster where they will share all project related information on a monthly basis in order to avoid confusion and duplication of resources. Component 2: Agricultural Livelihood Support In order to discourage displacement and keep households productive on their land, agro pastoral households will be supported with farming inputs at the opportune times based on the farming season. Such inputs will include quality seeds to plant, trainings in agriculture best practices. Atleast 400 pastoral households will be supported with atleast 10 shoats each to replenish their stocks. In total, the project will directly support approximately 1,580 vulnerable households, 11,060 (Men, Women, Boys and Girls) within pastoral and agro-pastoral villages classified to be in IPC 3 and 4 in Eastern Somaliland. This includes approximately 500 households who will benefit from the rehabilitated communal assets. Component 3: Beneficiary accountability mechanismsAccountability is part of Candlelight responsibility to receive feedback, demonstrate that activities have been implemented in accordance with standards and also to report on performance i.e. plans vis-à-vis achievements. At the midterm phase of the project especially after the distribution of animals and the first round of Cash transfers, a post-distribution monitoring survey will be conducted to evaluate the satisfaction levels of the beneficiaries. A toll free line will be communicated to all the beneficiaries to be used to provide feedback on project interventions.

Mitigation Measures

To overcome the above risks, Candlelight will undertake the following measures; 1. Ensure that beneficiary registration targets the most invisible groups in the community, considering the power relations, and the existing specific needs of any group according to age/gender, in order to avoid targeting and registration exacerbating vulnerability.2. Adopt and implement protocols to ensure that vulnerable populations, such as women, children, members of minority clans as well as persons with disabilities access humanitarian assistance.3. Ensure that the situation of all displaced populations, including those who are not in camps or broader settlements, as well as those dispersed in smaller groups and living with host families is reflected in humanitarian assessments and targeting.4. Avoid turning cash transfers into a pull factor if there are other alternatives.5. Avoid registering only the male head-of-household / head of family and never exclude the registration of other adult family members or unaccompanied minors. 6. Consider the frequency and rates of transfers and other distributions to avoid any eventual protection risks – Cash rates will be based on the rates as set by the Cash working group of Somalia. 7. Disaggregate beneficiaries by gender and age. Personal data and lists of recipients will be firmly restricted to concerned actors and safe from unintended use, due to the sensitivity of the information.8. Provide information on all activities of the project through different methods such as community meetings, group discussions to ensure communication reaches all beneficiaries.9. Make Cash transfers through mobile money (ZAAD) which eases access, and promotes dignity of the benefiting households. 10. Promote its Toll free number which is part of a complaints mechanism to address individual concerns and ensure accountability. Complaints/ feedback will be analyzed regularly and action taken. 11. Ensure that all staff involved in the project have received training in prevention and response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and other protection issues.
