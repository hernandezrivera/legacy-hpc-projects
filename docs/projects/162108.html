Project Summary

UNHCR will implement comprehensive nutrition programmes in 18 integrated nutrition centres across 14 camps and three stabilization centres in Cox Bazaar refugee operation in partnerships with nutrition partners. These will include community management of acute malnutrition among children under fives years , pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerable groups in the population, blanket supplementary feeding programmes and infant and young child feeding programmes , implement nutrition programmes for the adolescent girls and support the annual nutrition surveys to monitor the health and nutrition situation among the Rohingya refugee as per the Nutrition sector strategy and UNHCR public health strategy. Age and gender mainstreaming will be observed throughout the programme cycle.

Objectives

Needs

Needs Assessments: High level of undernutrition continues to be observed among the refugees, linked to pre-existing vulnerabilities such as inappropriate hygiene and sanitation conditions, food insecurity ,limited dietary diversity, limited access to safe water as well as poor living conditions contribute to recurring communicable disease outbreaks resulting in increased risk of negative nutrition outcomes among the most vulnerable young boys, girls and adolescent girls in all camps. The preliminary findings of the most recent nutrition survey conducted in October 2019 indicates a serious nutrition status among the Rohingya refugees living in the registered camps and makeshift settlements. The overall prevalence of global acute malnutrition was found to be 11.0% and remains within the serious category (WHO classification 10-14%) and still above the UNHCR standard of <10% GAM prevalence in refugee camps. There is no significant difference in the prevalence of in GAM found between girls and boys. Anaemia and stunting remain a major concern as both prevalence are above the 40% and 30% public health thresholds. Children 6-23 months are the most affected by anaemia with a prevalence of > 50% reported across all the camps. Uptake of appropriate infant and young child feeding practices is still poor with only 64% of children 0-5 months being exclusively breastfed, 26% have access to minimum acceptable diets. Furthermore, high vulnerability among the refugees has been reported in the refugee emergency vulnerability assessment (REVA) where refugees have negative coping mechanisms such selling of assets sale of food ration and other humanitarian relief items to obtain cash. These practices pose negative impact the household food security contributing to inadequate food at the household level. Majority of the refugee still rely on the general food assistance which is limited in the high biological proteins and micronutrients often offering very dietary diversity. In 2020 UNHCR will implement comprehensive nutrition programmes through 18 integrated nutrition centers and 3 stabilization centers. UNHCR and its nutrition partners will therefore strengthen community outreach activities to ensure early detection of children suffering from malnutrition and referrals for treatment of acute malnutrition in the CMAM programme. More efforts will be focused on the prevention activities such as growth monitoring and promotion (GMP), blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP), infant and young child feeding (IYCF) programmes at the facility level and the community level through the establishment and scaling up mother to mother support groups. Community Management of at-risk Mothers and Infants under six months of age (C-MAMI) approach will be integrated in the existing IYCF programme to ensure more focused attention to beneficiaries with breastfeeding difficulties in order provide appropriate support. The nutritional needs of older adults and other vulnerable categories such as children above five years, medical cases and other specific needs will be considered and supported as per the sector guidelines. Continued capacity building of the nutrition staff will be strengthened to ensure implementation of the nutrition services as per the protocols to ensure good quality of care and overall outcomes and the overall nutrition wellbeing of the beneficiaries. . Integration of Nutrition services with the other sectors such as health, food security, WASH, protection and CBI and livelihoods will be prioritized to ensure collective response in addressing the high levels of malnutrition in the refugee camps. The total number targeted number of benefiaries by UNHCR funded Nutrition programmes are 111,726 across 14 refugee camps. An estimated number of children under five years are 57,330 adolescent girls 24,696 and 34,695 pregnant and lactating mothers in the different nutrition programmes as per the sectors needs analysis.

Activities or outputs

Project Activities: The project activities will be implemented by UNHCR in partnership with nutrition partners. The project will seek to meet the needs identified under the nutrition sector needs assessment and is aligned to the nutrition sector strategic objectives and UNHCR public health strategy. Specifically, project activities include;Objective 1 Treatment of acute malnutrition a). Community outreach activities; Hiring and training of community nutrition volunteers, Screening, detection and referral of acutely malnourished children aged 6-59 months to the nutrition center, community nutrition education sessions and home visits to follow up non-responders and defaulters.b). Treatment of acute malnutrition: Implementation of 3 stabilization centers for inpatient management of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications and outpatient therapeutic feeding program (OTP) and targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) for the treatment of severe and moderate acutely malnourished children under five years in 18 nutrition centers. c). Construction of two nutrition centers and maintenance of nutrition centers of 20 nutrition facilities. d). Human resource; Hiring of approximately 600 staff, to support to provide nutrition services. e). Procurement of nutrition equipment, medicines and therapeutic nutrition product including plumpy nut/RUTF, therapeutic milk (F100 and F75) and resomal. 2: Objective 2a). Infant and young child feeding programmes Provision of IYCF-e services in the IYCF-e corners and CMAMI and in the community through mother to mother support group sessions targeting pregnant and lactating women and care givers of children under 2 years. Cooking demonstration using locally available food to enhance and support complementary feeding among children 6-23 months.b). Implementation of adolescent nutrition programmes IFA supplementation and counselling, c). Blanket supplementary feeding programmes for children 6-59 months and Pregnant and lactating women. d). Behaviour change communication through community- and facility-based nutrition education, provision of culturally appropriated IEC materials e). Micronutrient supplementation (MNP) for children aged 6-59 months f). Nutrition support to the new arrivals: Nutrition screening and provision of meals to the newly arrived refugees in the transit center. 3: Objective 3Information management and Assessments: Support the implementation of the annual SMART/SENS survey to be conducted in October 2020 and ensure analysis and reporting of the nutrition data to inform about the nutrition situation and develop strategies and interventions to improve the nutrition wellbeing.
