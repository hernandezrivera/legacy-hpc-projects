Project Summary

Education is a fundamental right of every person, one that all children including those caught in natural and human made emergencies, should be able to access. Because of this, the project shall improve provision of equitable access to safe and inclusive education services for emergency affected returnees IDPs, and host community children, with special consideration of children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys), to most vulnerable communities affected by conflict and floods in Upper Nile State. This will be done through implementation of project objectives such as;
Improve equitable access to safe and inclusive education services for 630,000 emergency-affected IDPs, returnees, refugees, and host community children inclusive of children with disabilities, and youth (girls and boys) by 2023 in the priority counties.
Improve the quality of education services at all levels and protect the wellbeing of all children, and teachers' capacity to deliver quality services and prevent the negative impact of crisis on learning environments in the priority counties.
Strengthen the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities in priority counties.
In this project UNKEA will establish 25 learning spaces, 12 rehabilitate classrooms, establish 8 gender segregated latrines, rehabilitate 5 latrines, distribute 18 recreational kits, provide training to 161 teachers (28 females) on pedagogy and 50 teachers (9 females) on MHPSS, 29 PTA/SMC and members of county education department.
Provide support to Children with diverse disabilities and integrate gender-based violence activities into these interventions. This project targets learners 14448 (8036 females) from formal and non-formal education, of which 9192 (5075 females) from host communities, 2089 IDPs (1149 females) and 3167 returnees (1741 females) in targeted counties within the mentioned counties. 27,232 (10,520 females) indirect beneficiaries will benefit from these interventions in this state.
UNKEA’s education programme is strongly commitment to Early Childhood Education, primary and alternative education systems ensuring provision of quality, inclusive, and equitable education for all children in the areas affected by natural and man-made disasters.
UNKEA will use integrated approaches in activities implementation, continuous partnership with other sectoral partners to integrate nutrition, GBV, health and protection in schools to be supported.
UNKEA also will work in collaboration with beneficiaries of all groups, community leaders, county education department, state and national clusters and line ministries for better service delivery and sustainability of this project. The project shall require $ 971,904.00. Communities that shall benefit from the project shall be mobilized to contribute in-kind 20% of resources that will be needed for the response.

1. Needs

South Sudan’s context is very challenging due to effects of climate change like floods and draught, and violences that lead to mass displacements, destruction of livelihoods, and loss of property. These have impaired Children’s access to education services, child protection and safeguarding concerns. Due to flooding, some 877 schools were reported damaged, 20% increase from the number of schools damaged by flooding in 2021, interrupting the education of an estimated 381,495 children in Upper Nile State. Sub-national violence and insecurity let to 6,326 children missing out of school. Insecurity incidents in and around the education facilities, destruction, occupation of schools was reported as factors limiting access to school, plus displacement of teachers, children, and the risk of revenge killings of boys in schools. Other barriers include distances and limited financial resources within communities to enrol their children in school. Girls face additional challenges with parents discouraging girls from attending school due to cultural perceptions. Delayed or unpaid salaries and incentives for teachers result in teacher apathy, high turnover rate and poor-quality teaching. Some 21,510 identified children with disabilities face challenges accessing quality education, for instance training for teachers to deliver inclusive education that addresses the needs of CwD and social stigma make them feel unwelcomed and unsupported within the local schools. Unequal access to services has exacerbated tensions between the communities and is a source of violence, including attacks on humanitarian assets and personnel, and the destruction of vital public infrastructure (HNO 2023). Nasir County experienced several episodes of flooding in three years and since then the situation has never improved. In 2022 the flooding caused massive internal displacement of populations with their Livestock in parts of the county posing humanitarian concerns. School infrastructures and learning materials were all submerged and destroyed. Cycle of violence exposes women and girls to rapes and robbery by men when they are moving alone. The lack of WASH facilities such as latrines in schools leading to open defecation in forests, which is hazardous to girls as they are exposed to GBV. Psychological distress as results of lost lives, properties and livelihoods in the past two years. Child labour and abuse were identified, as girls and boys are doing heavy works which are protection concerns. The boys are engaged in looking after cattle, fishing in the river, selling items in the markets, being used as porters. Harmful traditional practices like early/forced Marriage with 80% of the girls are being married off because of shortage of food or lack of income in the family level. Physical assault by spouse, this has increased because of the Flood (IRNA September 2022). Since 2021, Panyikang County is flooded causing severe displacement of the population. The populations were squatting on higher grounds as others moved to refugee camp in Sudan. The results were Loss of lives, properties, houses, destruction of facilities such as schools, water points, health facilities and the livelihoods. All schools in Panyikang have not been functional due to displacements caused by floods and violence. Fashoda County received 7,519 returnees from Sudan crisis that began in April 2023, the influx of returnees continues to increase daily with dire need of humanitarian aid. Some integrated themselves into the host communities and IDP settlements. According to RRC, IDP settlements already host over 30,175 individuals who were displaced by armed conflicts when fighting broke out between the SPLA IO factions. 22,494 vulnerable populations need humanitarian aid. The rapid population increase caused pressure on the already weak social and education services in the County (Fashoda IRNA, June 2023).

2. Project activities

The project activities shall include the alignment of sectoral objectives such as: (i) Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 866,000 emergencies affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys). The project shall implement. Objective 1 i) Establishment of temporary learning spaces in line with INEE’s minimum standards, ii) Rehabilitation of learning spaces in line with INEE’s minimum standards, iii) Establish change room for girls in school, iv) Establish gender sensitive latrines in line with INEE’s minimum standards, v) Rehabilitation of gender sensitive latrines in line with INEE’s minimum standards, vi) Mobilization/campaigns to enrol children in ALP, functional literacy, life skills, pre-primary, primary, secondary school, and TVET, vii) Distribute dignity kits to adolescent girls, viii) Conduct awareness raising on key issues, ix) Distribute hygiene kits to schoolboys and girls, x) Distribute assistive devices to children with disabilities, xi) establishing reporting(toll free lines, suggestion box, designated focal points etc) mechanisms, xii) Conduct training of teachers and staff on child safeguarding and GBV risk mitigation, referral mechanisms, xiii) Provide school meal to children, xiv) Establish school gardens at school, xv) Training of teachers on PSS. Provide PSS service to learners. Objective 2. i) Distribution of ECD kits to school children aged 3 -6 years old, ii) Distribution of recreational kits to school kits, iii) Distribution of teacher kits, iv) Train teachers on EiE, subject matter and pedagogy skills, v) Support volunteer teachers with financial incentives. Objective 3: i) Conduct training for SMC/PTA members on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), ii) Conduct training education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).Indicators: Objective 1.
