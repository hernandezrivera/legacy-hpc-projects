Project Summary

The effects of prolonged violent conflict and limited investment in the country’s infrastructure have taken a heavy toll on availability of, and access to, basic services. The COVID-19 pandemic and climatic conditions have exacerbated poverty and general living standards for many South Sudanese. The country’s health system and infrastructure are among the poorest in the world. Health facilities are poorly equipped and staffed. There is a severe shortage of trained health professionals in all categories. Thus, people rely on inadequately trained or low skilled health workers to provide their healthcare. A quarter of internally displaced and returnees reside in settlements further than 5km from a functional health facility (South Sudan HNO 2022). Approximately more than half of the country’s 2,300 health facilities are non-functional. Nearly 60 per cent of the functioning health facilities are supported by humanitarian and development organizations but many of them are close to displacement sites and returnee settlements and may not be easily accessible by established communities. Health facilities in some locations were reported to be closed due to violence and flooding, for example in Tonj North. The response design focuses on providing lifesaving essential health services in line with national and international humanitarian standards, and is based on the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, disease burden seasonality, and trauma-related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, response readiness, response, and mitigation activities will be undertaken with special attention given to providing vulnerable groups including women, children and persons with disabilities. IRW in collaboration with the Ministry of Health and partners will support different intervention modalities suited to the needs of affected people, including COVID-19 control and prevention interventions.
Islamic Relief will continue prevention, preparedness, and response readiness activities addressing COVID 19 IRWSS will further support static and mobile health facilities, and community-based healthcare systems to serve communities who do not have access to health care facilities and are affected by emergencies. Additionally, IRWSS plans to further strengthen health cluster technical, operational and field coordination support to partners and authorities. IRWSS will continue to collaborate with development partners who are providing regular health services through static health facilities in crisis-affected areas by identifying communities that are still not reachable and providing services through mobile outreach as well as strengthening support to static health facilities. Improved access to essential health services will contribute to the overall reduction of the high maternal mortality rates and under-five mortality rates and mitigate the transmission of vaccine- preventable diseases and other infectious diseases. IRWSS will involve affected communities and county health system (implementing national health promotion, prevention, and control programs) in planning and organizing emergency health services. The cluster coordination mechanism at the national and state level will be utilized to coordinate, collaborate, liaise with partners and authorities to implement, monitor, and review the response activities. Referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support, GBV survivors, and for maternal and child health emergencies will be streamlined and strengthened. Referral pathways will link the community and facility-based health care system to provide continuity of care. To protect both the patient and health staff, IRWSS will encourage and support infection prevention and control (IPC) and WASH measures including COVID-19 appropriate prevention and management activities. The Inter-cluster coordination mechanism at all levels will work towards comprehensive integration and collaboration with other clusters, including FSL, WASH etc.

1 - Needs

In 2023, 417,357 people are in dire need of essential health care services in Tonj North, Wau, Kapoeta Eats and North counties with health cluster targeting to reach 227,186 people. Out of the health cluster target of 227,186, IRSS will target 90,000 people (male=24300, female=31500, girls= 22,500, boys=11,700, PWDs=13500) in the four counties of Wau, Tonj North and Kapoeta East and North. The IRSS will focus on reaching following need in particular:1) Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations.2) Reduce excess in morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases 3) Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms Islamic Relief aims to improve people’s access to and utilization of essential health care services to reduce the rate of morbidity and mortality. Disease outbreaks will be detected proactively, responded to in a timely manner and adequate surveillance systems will be in place through the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS). In coordination with the heath Cluster and its partners, Islamic Relief South Sudan will provide health services that are adapted to the current COVID-19 context and ensure COVID-19 prevention while continuing to provide essential lifesaving health care. Community and County Health System reliance capacity will be promoted through bringing synergies and optimizing use of limited resources among humanitarian, development and peace actors. Community consultation, feedback and partnership will be promoted.

2 - Project activities

Islamic Relief South Sudan will implement the following activities in the target locations in the following facilities ((Bagari PHCU, Biringi PHCU, Taban PHCU, Gaitan PHCU, Gedi PHCU, Athiengpuol PHCU, Lopua PHCU)1. Curative consultations for common medical ailments per age, sex, disability (65,800 (Women 23030; Men: 17,766; Girls: 16,450; Boys: 8,554; PWDs: 9,870))2. Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases for children less than one year with different vaccine antigens including immunization of women of reproductive age with tetanus toxoid vaccine; (17,100 (Girls: 11,250; Boys: 5, 850)3. Referral of medical conditions from the community to the health facilities by community health workers;4. Management of non-communicable diseases at the static health facilities and referral to the next level of care;5. Conducting joint supportive supervision visits to the supported health facilities to ensure that health workers are given the necessary on-job training (4 Visits);6. Provision of reproductive health services such as contraceptives;7. Provision of maternal healthcare services to pregnant women such as clean deliveries by trained health workers, antenatal and postnatal services (7,100 pregnant women);8. Formation and training of health facility management committees in all the static health facilities (7 HFMCs formed and trained)

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will reach the persons with disabilities, illiterate women and girls through:(i) Using pictorial media and communication materials for the illiterate individuals;(ii) Developing inclusive communication materials using local languages(iii) Consultation with the beneficiaries;(iv) Female health workers will be in place for collecting feedback of the women and girls particularly on maternal and reproductive health issues;(v) Following the principles of privacy and confidentiality during disclosure;

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Islamic Relief South Sudan has own Complaint Response Mechanism in place. The project will collect the feedback of the beneficiaries through consultation with them and they will be informed through the following mechanism:(i) Consultation with the beneficiaries before developing project and consultation meeting after approval of the project;(ii) Islamic Relief South Sudan will collect beneficiaries feedback through consultation meeting, one to one discussion, complaint/suggestion box, hot line number and e0mail ID;(iii) The Monitoring Evaluation and Learning (MEAL) system will also be in place and the MEAL will include collection of feedbacks of the stakeholders and capturing lessons learned systematically.On quarterly basis, the project will measure access of services by beneficiaries through regular monitoring and evaluation of equitable access to services in this projects through consultations and conducting Focus group discussion with communities particularly women and girls, disabled and elderly regarding their satisfaction, access and perceptions of safety in relation to, nutrition interventions, IRW- will also use other platforms like community health committee, Mother to Mother support groups to establish quality of services, challenges and lessons learnt to come up with solutions for challenges faced in implementation. The groups comprise of different communities/tribes/groups living the catchment of our target area; Male/Female, PLW and people with disability, data collected will be sex and age dis-aggregated and can as well be through the support of an established Community Help Desk Assistance.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Islamic Relief South Sudan has its own policy Gender Based Violence, Protection, Safeguarding and PESA policy. The policy included international standards and in line with the CHS and UN Cluster strategies. The project will follow the Gender markers, Age markers, Inclusion markers etc.Under Health/Nutrition, Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR), malnutrition and mother-to-mother groups are giving preference to the specific needs of women and girls, and in Protection, SGBV prevention, response and awareness is prioritized. Before implementation of any activity, group awareness sessions and consultation will be made with all target groups to remove any barrier to equal participation, through community representatives and as well displaying posters on PSEA for recommended and non-recommended practices at the Nutrition Facilities and the communities in support of the CNVs as they conduct community mobilization processes (such as issues of location, time, schedule), and to bring forth gender related needs, capacities and aspirations of women, men, girls and boys separately.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will collect beneficiaries feedback through:(i) Consultation with the beneficiaries;(ii) Post distribution/service feedback mechanism;(iii) Installation of suggestion boxes at the delivery of health service Centres;(iv) Introducing citizen led community based monitoring and evaluation mechanism;
