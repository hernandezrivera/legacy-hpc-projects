Project Summary

The project will target 30 communities in Kereniek and Geneina localities to reduce barriers and improve access to basic education for girls and women. ADRA Sudan’s preliminary assessment reveals key gaps in these communities of 10 school latrines, two school water systems and 20 classrooms in need of rehabilitation as well as critical needs in hygiene supplies and education. Not all of these gaps need to be targeted in this proposal.
The project will support the targeted population with facilities to increase access to inclusive quality education for children to basic education in WD especially girls. The project will contribute positively to enhance human rights, governance, environment and gender among women and girls students. The project will support rehabilitating school environment in the targeted areas including classrooms, latrines and water sources, thereby increasing enrolment, particularly for girls. The project will support teacher trainings in key subject such as math, Arabic, English and also in management, monitoring and other areas chosen by the schools principals and teachers in coordination with SMOE training departments in WD, resulting in strong curricula, higher performing teachers and strong school systems including record keeping.
The project will advocate for free, good quality inclusive education without discrimination of any kind. Specific addition will be given to peace building and GBV prevention among families and students of different identities or tribes. This will be done through awareness, events and public engagement that targets schools, families, communities and government. Key topics include peace and peace building, conflict resolutions and mediation, SRHR, safe school environment and prevention of SGBV. The project will support non-formal education through REFLECT to help empower women to better support their households and particularly thee education of their girls, thus supporting girls enrolment and reducing the rate of dropout and absenteeism.

Needs

• Lack of access to education for girls and marginalized, tribes, pastoralists and returnees in Kereinik and Geneina localities of West Darfur. This is due to the following • Lack of awareness and sensitization on education importance mainly for girls.• High percentage of out of school children, particularly girls.• Shortage of trained teachers, with critical lack of training on CFS and inclusive education. The result is poor quality of education, which contributes to the high percentage of children out of school as parents are reluctant to invest in poor quality education. • Weak PTAs and lack of community support for girls education, particularly from community leaders. • Lack of gender sensitive latrines and water sources in schools• Lack of fixed class rooms• Conflicts between tribes and particularly between pastoralists and agriculturalists, with IDPs/returnees being caught in the middle. • Poverty among population that affects directly the numbers of children enroll in schools due to high education fees• High illiteracy rate• Poor learning environment

Impact on environment/natural resources

In regards to this project and environment effect; there are three main concerns: First, due to desertification there is overcutting and overgrazing in WD. Thus schools must be carefully fenced and fences must be maintained. Otherwise camels, goats and other ruminants are likely to enter schools and eat everything. Schools constructed from local materials or schools conducted under trees are particularly vulnerable to disruption by herds of animals. Second and also exacerbated by desertification and climate change, there is seasonal flooding which can damage schools, particularly schools built with local materials and schools that are not wisely sited outside of flood prone areas. Third and also related to desertification and climate change, the average temperatures are raising, making it difficult to travel to school during the hot season. Furthermore, there are severe dust storms that can render education impossible. Rehabilitation of schools and school WASH will involve the use of concrete and permanent materials. Travel to and between targeted areas will be conducted in 4X4 vehicles that burn gas or diesel. Implementation must be largely done outside of the rainy season as there is no real road infrastructure in the target area and movement is frequently impossible during the rainy season. Dust storms may also limit implementation but the delays are usually merely a matter of hours or days. Proper design and siting mitigate most environmental concerns in terms of impact on activities. Rehabilitation of schools and school WASH will involve the use of concrete and permanent materials. Travel to and between targeted areas will be conducted in 4X4 vehicles that burn gas or diesel. So materials will be locally sourced were possible. Travel will be minimized while possible. Trainings will include mainstreamed environmental projection. Women are generally better environmental stewards than men. Empowering women as peace builders and community leaders will have a positive impact on the environment. Conflict and displacement are harmful to the environment. The project will strengthen peace building, reducing conflict and thereby having a positive environmental impact. The temporary materials are commonly used to rehabilitate schools include trees and bushes. Cutting the trees and bushes has a negative impact on the environment. Permanent rehabilitation will mitigate the harm that would be caused if the communities fixed up their schools without project support. The project will increase community cohesiveness. The community will be more likely to few the natural environments as a shared asset to be preserved rather than individual asset to be exploited.
