Project Summary

This intervention is inserted in the framework of the strategic objectives 1, 2 and 3 of the HRP 2019 and is designed to complement the efforts of the Protection sector in Syria. This project aims to contribute to the reinforcement and improvement of the psychological wellbeing of women, men, girls and boys (WMGB) through structured and comprehensive PSS, capacity building, awareness campaign and robust Risk Education. The project targets mainly IDPs and returnees in need of protection in Homs, Hama, Tartous, Lattakia, and Quneitra governorates. This intervention will be implemented in partnership with SARC. The project plans to provide structured PSS to those in need, Case management will also be conducted through individual assessment and Each SARC team is accompanied by a CM, who would be providing on-site induction/training for SARC volunteers who are acting as activity facilitators, on identification of PoCs with special/ specific needs. So PoCs with special needs are identified and referred to CM by SARC volunteers, also CM through direct observation may identify cases him/herself and may also receive referrals from other partners. CM will then do individual assessment through a session with the PoC, and using TDH standard forms ( based on IASC standards) and decide the action plan for each case. Even though it is a mobile team, we do usually have a temporary facility provided by SARC ( SARC sub-branch, or another facility, including a rented facility as a choice) where privacy for CM sessions could be guaranteed. CM to decide the needs of the case ( provisioning of material, in-kind assistance) and providing what is available through project resources/ funds, and if needs are beyond that, refer case to respective service provider/ parner based on referral mechanism composed on field data of present partners or provided by sector if available. GBV/SGBV cases are also referred to GBV actors. Cases are handled by CM in consultation with TDH PSS expert and technical supervisor in field, and SARC specialists. TdH-IT will conduct Capacity building at the beginning of the intervention (through TdH Expert) on various topics including (PSS, PFA, CP, Resiliency, Life Skills, Communication, and MRE. As per past experience SARC will nominate double the number of volunteers needed, where TdH will build their capacity and select top volunteers for the intervention.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in the priority locations has been assessed as follows: South-West Syria: Following the suspension of hostilities across South-West Syria, hundreds of displaced families returned to their areas of origin in Dar’a and Quneitra governorates. As of mid-August, 57,000 individuals were estimated to remain displaced, representing a nearly 70 per cent reduction from 184,000 IDPs in the beginning of August 2018, noting that the UN are currently working to obtain new and reliable IDP and returnee numbers. Health, repair works for the water, electricity and sewage networks, and education have been listed as priority needs.In eastern Homs/Tadmor people are returning back to their city, where the city is still contaminated with war remnants and these returnees are coming back from displacement in austere situations and camps. Numbers are not yet defined but our partners SARC believe it’s of high importance to meet needs of returnees.In Homs/Mikharam 17000 PiN including 1200 IDPs are in need for CP/PSS/MRE support.In Hama/Hamra city is also similar situation, with 196 IDPs, 4055 returnees, and total PiN 4567.In Tartous, total people in need are 100,263 including 42000 IDPs.In Lattakia 415305 IDPs need support in terms of PSS and protection support especially as they come from Idlib, and previously ISIS controlled areas.In Haffah/Lattakia, 3814 PiN are still in need for MRE/PSS/CP. Source is : 2018/2019 HNO, and SARC statistics.

Implementation of activities

5 SARC mobile teams will be deployed in locations (Lattakia, Tartous, Hama, Homs, Quneitra) of implementation. Each SARC mobile team consists of 10 volunteers, 1 TDH Technical supervisor, case manager who will conduct PFA, Structured PSS activities and MRE activities for WMBG. As the implementation will be through mobile teams, TdH/SARC modality will be to search for safe areas to implement, by coordinating with the branches and institutions working in areas of implementation (such as schools), where TdH/SARC team will implement structured PSS sessions for 3 months (400 beneficiaries max) for each round. These activities will be supervised by tdh-it on job technical supervisor that is directly reporting to TdH-IT expert for planning the intervention, conducting the activity. TdH-IT technical supervisor in each area will be working directly with SARC team (CM, and Volunteers) implementing the activities based on our organization’s standards.Specific activities would be tailored to target adolescents and youth to benefit them especially since their needs may be different from other components of the population.Also capacity building and awareness raising training will be held for SARC volunteers, and others. Brief Description: - Before the implementation of the activities directly TdH will conduct capacity building to SARC volunteers so they receive adequate training before intervention, based on TdH-IT/SARC standards. Top 11 volunteers in each area will be selected to conduct activities planned. - Selection of beneficiaries will be done during the gathering phase of 10 to 30 days while conducting preliminary PFA activities and coordinating with actors in the field. Priority will be given to target Returnees, IDPs, and most vulnerable PoCs (based on the number of HHs, breadwinner of HHs, Social status (based on SARC data) etc.). After considering PoCs to participate in the Structured PSS course for 3 months where they will be trained on 3 components: Communication skills, social interaction skills, and Resilience. The above will be conducted through interactive, group activities. MRE will be geographic area specific (Mainly Hama and Quneitra), and will target all age groups in contaminated areas, through awareness raising activities, printed material will be based on UNICEF/ MOE approved material. Distribution of material will (if needed) be decided by the field and under activities budget lines, no pre-determined materials, especially since some parts (Tadmor, Homs) is/will encounter continuous returnees that will raise its own needs, accordingly PoCs feedback is best source for feedback on needs to meet. Case management is a decisive component, since each team will be accompanied by a CM to do individual assessment of cases referred by SARC volunteers acting as activity facilitators. CM will then meet needs using project resources( material provisioning), psychosocial counseling, and if required referral to specialized services. CM also keeps/creates referral mapping of partners present in the field and refer PoCs to them, and accepts their referrals accordingly. Each PSS course will be supported by monitoring tools (Child protection monitoring tool, Structured PSS tool, and on spot evaluations) to measure improvement for each of participating beneficiaries ( child monitoring form, adult monitoring form), exit strategy is planned near the end of each 3 month course ( open day). Case management is also documented as per IASC guidelines.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Capacity building activities, Likelihood: High, Impact: High. 1. Quality of intervention is scarce due to inadequate service provision and inadequate capacity, high turnover and loss of capacity; lack of specialized and well-trained staff/service provider in particular areas of expertise. Mitigation: Conduct training needs assessments, Consult with targeted authorities, capitalizing on existing resources, Follow-up, mentoring and coaching initiatives. MRE: Risk: Tension between IDPs themselves, and with host community 2. Vulnerable groups may not have the same possibility to access protection services provided as part of this intervention 3. Delay in implementing. Familiarization may cause the civilian populace to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards. Mitigation: intense training of staff as facilitators inside Syria will ensure awareness of hazardous materials/items through higher quality awareness sessions and activities, this will mitigate the risk of familiarity and overconfidence among population, especially as hazardous materials are tricky and could be hidden for long time before exploding and causing casualties. Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Mitigation: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. 2. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In the assumptions that TdH-IT along with SARC have already the access and approvals to work in selected areas as already discussed with SARC, in addition to SARC having the minimum capacity required in each locations with regards to number of volunteers, CMs, and Team leaders required, in addition to availability of SARC branches for each location, TdH-IT considers the following risks: Risk: 1. Content of awareness sessions and activities may not be fully adapted to the social and cultural environment and May create misunderstanding or even tensions and has repercussions on the organization implementing the campaign 2.Methods of delivery of awareness sessions and awareness materials may not be the most effective in the specific environment/ community Mitigation: 1. Consult with population groups and communities asking their inputs on most effective way of delivering messages and their contents; consult with line ministries and departments on the organization of mass campaign. 2. Consult existing assessments on communication to communities (MSNA); resort to knowledge of local counterparts and social workers in the communities to better plan and assess the effectiveness of intervention within population of concern.
