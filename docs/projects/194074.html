Project Summary

The project will endeavor to improve access to quality inclusive basic education for 6,525 children (4,015 girls and 2,510 boys) in 17 schools in South Kordofan state. These children are affected by conflict and natural disasters such as floods in Al-Goz, Alreef Alshargi and Kadugli localities. The project will improve the learning and teaching environment by revitalizing the damaged schools and the hygienic conditions of adolescent girls will be improved. The learning process will be supported by strengthening the capacity of teachers through “Package for Teachers in Crisis Contexts” training modules and providing them with teaching kits and teachers` incentives for volunteers and trainees, while schools children will be provided with learning supplies and materials. The project will further carry out awareness raising activities on the rights of all children to education and identifying and addressing oppressive norms that hold girls and boys back.

1 - Needs

The Education Cluster's 2023 PiN is 3.7 million. The population groups most in need of humanitarian assistance to continue or resume their education include IDPs, refugees and returnees, host community members facing severe food insecurity, nomadic populations, adolescents and youth, children with disabilities, and, in some communities, girls. Many of the IDP children have endured protracted conflict, multiple displacements and continue to be exposed to ongoing clashes. Conflict-affected girls and boys need psycho-social support, a safe, protective, and stable learning environment, and the normality of a school routine. However, due to large influxes of IDPs and frequent displacements in some localities, many of the learning spaces are overcrowded and are not adequately functioning. Children in these areas also have reduced access to learning supplies, seating, and latrines, and 100 pupils per teacher. As a result, the majority of students progress through the school system without the requisite basic skills to comprehend increasingly more complex subject matter and effectively participate in learning. While lack of adequate school infrastructure is a long-standing issue in Sudan, the situation is exacerbated due to conflict in some areas, compounded by the impact of climatic shocks and annual floods. These regular shocks result in increased damage to school infrastructure, rendering more children out of school and most often without access to any alternative learning modalities (98 per cent of schools surveyed in the JENA were not able to offer meaningful distance learning options). In 2022, nearly 400 schools across Sudan were confirmed as having sustained significant damage due to the floods, affecting access to education for more than 138,000 schoolchildren and delaying the start of the new academic year.Adolescents and youth remain a mostly invisible target group across Sudan, and require specialized interventions to provide protection, improve their well-being, and ensure they are included in formal education system. Without adequate adolescent and youth education initiatives, which offer critical goods and services, young people are exposed to a myriad of risks, including recruitment into armed forces or groups, gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, abduction, and child labour, with girls facing the additional risks of early marriage and pregnancy. Structural gender inequality and insubstantial social protection measures have exacerbated disparities between young men and young women/young mothers, making adolescent girls particularly vulnerable if not provided with safe environments such as learning spaces. Additionally, when out of school, adolescents and youth in emergencies are often faced with forced maturity, disrupting their gradual transitioning from childhood to adulthood, which is critical for healthy development and overall well-being. Although there is limited data available on children with disabilities in Sudan, it is estimated that 15 per cent of children have at least one disability, which translates to approximately 2.9 million school-aged children (ages 6-18) living with disability. Children with disabilities are often out of school and require specialized support to access, and then remain, in education. For these children, school environments can offer an additional layer of protection and help them access specialized community support services. To ensure children with disabilities receive the required support and are included in all aspects of the education programme design, the Sudan Education Cluster has activated a disability inclusion working group.Educational needs are projected to remain high throughout all of 2023, with a slight peak in September-October due to the annual floods which directly impact schools and schoolchildren at the beginning of the new academic year.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

FPDO has ensured the inclusion of protection mainstreaming and GBV from this planning phase to the delivery of service to vulnerable communities. The project takes into account preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of its intervention which could increase people's vulnerability and diminish the purpose of the intervention. The project considers ways to minimize tension and conflict between beneficiaries, by ensuring that accurate information reaches all relevant community groups, including children, adolescents, older persons, and persons with disabilities, using different means of communication and dissemination of appropriate information. The project will insure that the target communities have equitable access to the services provided. In addition, gender-responsive complaints mechanism will be established for beneficiaries to provide feedback on concerns. The project will have specific gender focus, adolescent girls are supported with dignity kits and other hygiene materials to makes sure they attend classes and are not left while gender segregated. Protection issues will be raised through out the project implementation to enable the target communities to understand protection issues that can affect the different people by gender, age and displacement status and reporting mechanisms. Basic do no harm principle and accountability to affected populations will be upheld to ensure all aspects of the project take into consideration the needs of the elderly, the sick, people with disabilities, women, youth, men and children. The project will seek to increase girl’s enrolment in primary and secondary education by deliberately targeting an increased number of out of school girls and by training and providing incentives to female volunteer refugee teachers.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

FPDO will be committed to provide the proposed education services to the affected people in target localities which has high numbers of IDPs and vulnerable host communities. FPDO is always assures to apply the participatory approach in its projects. The community will be consulted and been oriented on the project through community dialogues meetings to increase access, acceptance and to ensure that the project is well understood since the starting of the project. This approach and process of engagement and participation will encourage ownership and self-confidence in own ability to participate in management of interventions. FPDO also will make efforts to share findings and lessons learned from projects with beneficiaries, as well as with partners when appropriate. Complains and recommendations mechanism will be established at the community level to insure transparency and to provide a feedback from the community on the project implementation, and the community feedback will be discussed at project staff meetings. FPDO will provide training for project staff to be able to engage with communities in identifying protection issues for the different gender and age groups. Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) training is mandatory for all FPDO staff. FPDO will informs all beneficiaries of the PSEA policy, reporting procedures, and awareness raising on their rights. Any PSEA-related report would be immediately considered by FPDO and send to sector managers. FPDO will commit to deliver services to affected people timely without any delays as possible and will be participating in cluster meeting and cooperate with other acting implementing agencies to provide good coverage of services for affected people in target localities.
