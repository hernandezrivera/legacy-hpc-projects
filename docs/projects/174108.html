Project Summary

While building on the achievements and lessons learnt from the education in emergencies (EIE) intervention implemented in 2020 under ECW FER, CERF education project and ECW Cameroon COVID FER projects, UNESCO proposes to continue targeting out-of-school children to access learning and minimise drop-out for those enrolled in formal and non formal settings, in particular girls and children with disability while strengthening the quality and delivery of inclusive distance learning in the NWSW crisis and improve pupils learning outcomes to promote transition. The EiE 2020 projects enabled over 25,000 out-of-school IDP and Host community children access learning in non-formal community learning spaces and some have successfully transitioned into formal schools and also facilitated access to formal education for over 7,000 vulnerable out-of-school children through disbursement of capitation grants to selected schools (contingent upon deduction of school fees). Additionally, the UNESCO E-learning portal has registered 77,100 users, which is number far beyond what UNESCO had anticipated to reach during this period. UNESCO will therefore use these achievements and lessons to improve the quality of learning and inform further expansion in NWSW, as well as Littoral and West Regions, to provide access to learning for the IDP families that have settled there from NWSW. Through this action, UNESCO seeks to reach in 2021 at least 52,000 learners in school age 4-14 years old (46,045 girls and 15,955 boys) by: i) strengthening the quality and delivery of distance learning (online and off-line) to improve pupils learning outcomes and promote transition to formal learning. This includes building the capacity of learning facilitators and supervisors on learning content, pedagogical skills, learner assessment, and SEL. Further UNESCO will strengthen the E-learning Resource Centre recently established at the University of Buea (UB) to manage distance learning and provide continuous support to teachers and learning facilitators in the Regions. Thirdly, UNESCO proposes to improve the distance-learning infrastructure through provision of IT Equipment and learning materials (mobile learning units, Tablets, USB sticks and E-learning portal, books) including the production and dissemination of additional pre-recorded lessons and internet-based online/offline learning modules, to effectively reach the additional learners. UNESCO will also train the teachers of selected formal schools and community learning spaces on PSS, CMDRR, Sexual and reproductive health, GBV and Gender rights to strengthen protection especially of girls and boys, and equip school principals in managing EIE. Through this action UNESCO seeks to mitigate deterioration of livelihoods due to the ongoing conflict and COVID to improve the living conditions of at least 460 vulnerable and displaced youth and women, through the provision of tailored life skills (including psychosocial support-PSS, Social and Emotional Learning-SEL)Lastly, UNESCO recognizes the important role of youth in peace process and therefore will support selected 250 youth (15-17 year old) to join and complete TVET cycle of education and engage in meaningful livelihood system and act as peace ambassadors. Protection and Safe COVID-19 messaging will be cross cutting themes. UNESCO will further contribute to monitor attacks to schools and setting up early warning systems.

1 - Besoins / Needs

According to the HNO Report (June 2020), an estimated 6.2 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance in Cameroon, with 2.3 million from the NWSW Regions. The socio-political crisis in North West/ South West (NWSW) Region, now entering its fifth year, has led to massive population displacements, with spill over effects in the West and Littoral regions and increased vulnerabilities of people who have often left their villages to live in the bush or as IDP’s in urban hubs in Buea SW and Bamenda NW. It is reported that 680,000 people have been displaced in NWSW (HNO 2020), with the largest number of IDPs registered in the divisions of Meme (SO), Mezam (NO), Ngo-Ketunjia (NO) and Fako (SO) and 52% of the displaced being women (HNO Report 2020). Further, Multi-Sectorial-Needs-Assessment (MSNA) of August 2019, indicates 44.5% of IDPs are children (96,472 girls and 95,031 boys) of school going age. Additionally, It is also estimated that 200,000 have fled to the neighboring regions of Littoral and the West, where of the 200,189 persons identified in Host communities, 76,880 in the Littoral and 123,309 in the West, are internally displaced (MIRA Report October 2019). The NWSW conflict has put a strain on social services in the Region, with the education sector being severely impacted by the crisis. Over the past three years, insecurity, displacement of teachers, threats and attacks on infrastructure and education personnel left 1.2 million children in need of education assistance before the COVID-19 outbreak, and 1.7 million post COVID-19 (HNO 2020). The total closure of all 6,400 schools and 4,200 community-learning centres in the two regions led to an estimated of 1,033,000 school aged children being forced to stay at home increasing the number of children in need of education (HNO Report 2020).Despite the official opening of schools in August 2020, the conflict and attacks on education have continued brazenly targeting children, teachers and school infrastructure. This situation worsened with October 2020 experiencing the deadliest and most violent attacks on education since the beginning of the crisis in 2017, whereby at least eight school children killed, 12 injured in an attack in a school in Kumba on 24 October 2020 (Cameroon Education Cluster/OCHA). Two teachers were also reportedly kidnapped and killed in NW, as 669 Gender Based Violence (GBV) incidents were reported in the two regions during the same period Additional reports indicate that only 730 primary schools out of 3,127 (23%) and 142 secondary schools out of 558 (25%) in the NW region are operational, while in the SW region, 837 out of 2,195 (38%) primary schools and 89 out of 352 (25%) secondary schools are operationall (OCHA- October SitRep).In addition to the above reports, UNESCO, continued to monitor attacks on education through its nine (9) Local implementing partners in NWSW, who reported attacks on selected community learning spaces in NW Region in Ngo Kentunjia division that led to temporary closure of learning for children and teacher’s safety. Additionally, a Rapid Risk and Acceptability Assessment (2019) in selected Communities in NWSW undertaken by PAIDWA on behalf of UNESCO reported that 69% of IDP children did not attend school and the likelihood of them not attending school was high due to poverty, with girls being the first to drop out of school, hence the need to prioritize support for girls and children with disability. GBV continues to be a critical concern, as girls education, especially after disruption of services due to COVID-19 mitigation measures. Children with disability risk to be left out of education due to lack of school’s capacity to support them. The need therefore for increased and blended approaches to ensure continued learning and support to education through distance learning is critical.
