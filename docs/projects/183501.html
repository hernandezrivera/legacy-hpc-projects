Project Summary

This project aims at reinforcing the quality of available GBV multi-sectoral services in six safe spaces in Gaza Strip and West Bank, and three women programs centres in Gaza Strip's camps to fulfil the women and girls’ needs and enhance the community awareness on GBV issues through three main outcomes:
Improved accessibility, availability, and quality of GBV services for 4000 women, girls, men, and boys, with focus on women and Girls with Disabilities, Elderly women and GBV Survivors in the affected communities in Gaza Strip and West Bank.
Enhance and upgrade MHPSS services physically and remotely for 5000 women, girls, men, boys, and women and girls with disabilities.
Enhanced capacity of 220 GBV governmental and non-governmental specialized and non- specialized service providers and awareness of the community on GBV, GBV available services, cyber- crimes and
violence and PESA.
The project is built based on the affected community needs and feedbacks, considering the vulnerable groups and women and girls at risk, in addition to UNFPA partners' experiences and updates from the field, safe spaces, and home visits. Moreover, this project shows by evidence the importance of integration CVA as part of the GBV multi-sectoral services as one package of protection for women and girls GBV survivors and at risk, and enhance the role of integration, consistency, and coordination between the service providers in the referral pathways.
The project will reflect UNFPA experience and focal points' capacities in PSEA to spread the knowledge and reporting mechanisms, and the awareness within all target groups.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, humanitarian needs in Palestine continue to be driven by a protracted protection crisis that leaves many Palestinians struggling to live a life with dignity. More than 53 years of Israeli military occupation, characterized by; lack of respect for international humanitarian and human rights law, internal Palestinian political divisions, and recurrent escalations of hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups. The latest escalation in Gaza in May 2021 has led to further deterioration in the overall situation.The Israeli bombardment and the high intensity of fire led to the displacement of 107,000 women and their families, forcing them to leave their homes and seek protection and refuge in the homes of relatives, and in UNRWA schools. Women and girls in the oPt continue to experience various forms of violence in private and public spheres due to entrenched discrimination and prolonged Israeli occupation. According to UNFPA study on PwDs in 2019, women and girls with disabilities encounter persisting challenges due to discrimination, marginalization, social exclusion, stigmatization and routine failure to ensure their social inclusion and effective participation in public life. Women with disabilities live under more stringent social hardships, compared to other men with disabilities. Based on UNFPA MHPSS assessment published in August 2021, and the protection priorities 2022 analysis, the impact of the conflict in Gaza has worsened the existing mental health crisis. Prior to the escalation, but subsequent to the COVID-19 pandemic, amid increased pressure on access to basic services, households’ violence and negative coping mechanisms, there was a reported quadrupling of suicide rates especially amongst adolescent girls, and according to some surveys 82% of adolescents in Gaza reported overall poor-very poor mental well- being. The fear for life, loss of loved ones, physical injuries and wide-scale damage and destruction during the escalation have only exacerbated the risk of psychological distress and mental disorders, with rapid needs assessments conducted following the escalation highlighting distress symptoms among children. The capacity to respond to these needs was already adversely affected by major shortages in specialized personnel and drugs, and the May 2021 conflict directly affected many frontline workers, leaving some unable to resume work or at risk of overload. In Gaza, 63% of persons with disability lack accessibility to mental health and psychosocial support services. Inter-sectoral MHPSS services for children, families and service providers in Gaza, are needed to respond to distress and prevent development or relapse of mental health disorders. Pursuant to the Rapid Needs Assessment conducted in May 2021 by the GBV SC, women and girls were subjected to different forms of violence during escalation. The escalation and its consequences caused anger, fear, anxiety, over crowdedness, lack of security and privacy, and the inability to provide basic needs, which founded a fertile ground for continued and increased violence against women and girls. The most prominent types of violence that the majority of girls have been subjected to is psychological violence as a result of marginalization and lack of attention, ignoring their special needs, disrespecting their privacy, and restricting their freedom of movement and expression, especially displaced women. A number of women with cancer and disabilities were bullied. Moreover, it was found that all women and girls, especially widows, bereaved and wounded women, survivors of GBV, women with disabilities, internally displaced, cancer patients and women with chronic diseases, have an immediate and critical need for MHPSS services. Such services will help improve the psychological situation and self-care of groups of women and girls and their families and mitigate the effects of the recent escalation. The immediate and priority need to provide CVA for protection give women the freedom and flexibility to spend it according to their needs and priorities. The results from 2020 UNWOMEN gender alert stated that 31 %of female-headed households are vulnerable to food security challenges. Female-headed households face increased vulnerability due to COVID-19. The impact of the COVID-19 related economic recession can be seen in the Gaza Strip as 84.4 % of female-headed households recently described an urgent need for food.Female-headed households are also at particular risk as economic insecurity may leave them vulnerable to forced displacement or eviction. UNFPA through this project will sustain its GBV high quality multi-sectoral services through 6 of its 11 safe spaces, supported shelters, and the Women Programs Centres in vulnerable areas. The proposed interventions are designed based on the needs of women and girls in the affected communities, feedback from service providers, findings from round table meetings and discussions, and other previously used monitoring tools.UNFPA has PSEA focal point, and assessed all its partners and sub-grant organizations on PSEA, and had capacity-building interventions for them to gain the highest score 8. During the assessment phase, UNFPA partners stated that some women raised their concerns but were reluctant to provide complaints regarding sexual exploitation and abuse from some CBOs service providers. The case manager didn’t know how to refer these cases, so she dealt with the complaints as sexual GBV. Also, in the first orientation sessions, some case managers stated that they knew cases of SEA in a village school, but the perpetrator was moved to another school with no punishment nor action to the victims. Therefore, UNFPA conducted training session for all its partners and the GBV SC members in Gaza and West Bank on PSEA, and reporting mechanisms, and included the PSEA leaflets that include SAWA hotline 121 in all humanitarian services distribution as DKs and CVA.

2 - Activities

Outcome 1: Improved accessibility, availability and quality of GBV services (Health, SRH, psychological, legal, vocational, economic empowerment, Dignity kits) for 4000 women, girls, men and boys, with special focus on women and Girls with Disabilities, Elderly women and GBV Survivors in the affected communities in Gaza Strip and West Bank. Sub-activities: 1- providing health and reproductive health services for 400 GBV survivors in Gaza and West Bank with focus on women and girls, and GBV survivors with chronic illness and cancer survivors. 2- Providing GBV case management services for 500 GBV survivors in Gaza and West Bank, through four specialized case management organizations working according to the updated guidelines and the GBV standard Operating Procedures. 3- Providing protection services, reintegration support, safe exit plan to 100 high risk women victims and survivors of GBV in two shelters in Gaza and West Bank, shelter in Gaza which is Hayat Centre, and West Bank Shelter is Nablus shelter. This activity is mainly to expand UNFPA support to shelters to be able to provide continuous high quality psychosocial services, vocational training activities in addition to protection tools such as DK, food items, and awareness activities. 4- Providing CVA for protection for 1000 women and GBV survivors in Gaza and West Bank, to enable women to fulfil their protection and basic needs and enhance their well-being, maintain their human dignity, reduce their exposure to violence, and avoid further conflicts with their family members. 5- Providing dignity kits (according to UNFPA standard dignity kits) for 1000 women, girls and GBV survivors through the four implementing partners and women program centres in most vulnerable areas in Gaza Al Shate’ camp, Nusairat, and Rafah. 6- Provide legal consultations, representations, and awareness for 500 women and girls GBV survivors. 7- Conduct innovative and traditional vocational training sessions for 500 women and girls based on their needs and desire. Outcome 2: Enhance and upgrade well-structured MHPSS services physically and remotely for 5000 women, girls, men, boys, and women and girls with disabilities. Sub- activities: 1- Provide Mental Health and Psychosocial support services (MHPSS) for 4000 women, girls, women and girls with disabilities and GBV survivors in Gaza and West bank including: psychological first aid, individual and group psychological counselling, family counselling services, hotlines, social media tools. These interventions aim at improving the psychological status, self-care, wellbeing of the targeted groups and their families, and to mitigate the negative impact and effect of humanitarian crisis and violence against them. 2- Provision of care of service for 300 service providers in UNFPA safe spaces and health providers in primary health care centres and RH departments. 3- Referral for advanced and specialized MHPSS services for 400 GBV survivors through the authorized and specialized service providers in Gaza and West Bank. 4- Upgrade the infrastructure of remote services for 10 organizations 5 in Gaza and 5 in west Bank through procurement of the needed equipment for remote services. 5- Equipping three safe spaces (1 in Gaza and 2 in West Bank), and three women’s program centres in Gaza with 60 recreational and psychological kits to increase the quality of PSS services. 6- Conducting 20 open days (12 in Gaza, 8 WB) for 1000 women, men, girls and boys (7 for 350 men and boys, 13 for 650 women and girls).Outcome 3: Enhanced capacity of 220 GBV governmental and Non-governmental specialized and non-specialized service providers and awareness of the community towards GBV, GBV available services, cyber- crimes and PESA. Sub-activities: 1- Strengthen the capacities of service providers in virtual service delivery, including running help line, to support and refer GBV survivors. This activity will be achieved by conducting three-day refresher training for 60 GBV sub cluster members (30 in Gaza and 30 in west Bank) targeting health, psychosocial, legal service providers. 2- Strengthen the capacity of local GBV organizations on PSEA by conducting three-day PSEA training for 60 GBV Service providers (30 in Gaza and 30 in West Bank) to be included in their complaint mechanisms, reporting and monitoring capacities. 3- Conduct two days training for 40 specialized GBV service providers (20 Gaza, 20 West Bank) on GBV detection and referral, mainly front line workers who deal directly with GBV survivors. 4- Conduct two days training for 60 Non GBV specialized actors on GBV detection and referral to enhance the referral for specialized GBV service providers and avoid any harm in dealing with GBV cases in their field. 5- Strengthen the capacities of 60 GBV service providers (30 Gaza, 30 West Bank) on certain issues that exacerbated along with the continuous humanitarian crises (the last escalation on Gaza strip, COVID 19, increased poverty, and high rates of unemployment) such as Cyber-crimes, blackmailing, and sexual violence and improve the service provider capacities on dealing with such cases. 6- Conduct 100 awareness and advocacy campaigns for the community through social media and protection networks in Gaza Strip and West Bank about GBV issues, COVID-19 effects on vulnerable people, cybercrimes and PESA.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No harm” is one of the main GBV guiding principles and approaches according to UNFPA minimum standards and all partners of GBV service providers know how to apply these principles in their work with GBV survivors. The service providers received and continuously have trainings and capacity building to protect the identity and safety of GBV survivors by not disclosing any personal information about the survivor or the incident to anyone without the survivor’s explicit permission and consent. Also, GBV service providers have robust experience in ensuring GBV survivors are protected from harm and in providing psychological and emotional safety to survivors who may be at high risk from the perpetrators or from their family members due to notions of family honour. This also is ensured through UNFPA capacity building to GBV SC members to know and have experience on PSEA, and establish safe and confidential pathways such as SAWA hotline that covers Gaza and West Bank, where we inform women and girls and all target groups to use if any incident of SEA occurred or was suspected. All UNFPA partners are required to follow the GBV Survivor Centred Approach to provide meaningful access to services, ensuring accountability mechanisms are in place as well as participation and empowerment of GBV survivors. UNFPA will continue to provide capacity building to partners on these principles including on GBV Minimum Standards and safe GBV remote service delivery. For the CVA risk mitigation, CVA for protection supports access to GBV and SRH services and contributes to the safety, dignity, and resilience of women and girls in humanitarian contexts. CVA offers discretion and flexibility compared to in-kind assistance. Based on UNFPA experience in CVA, and the feedbacks from the benefited women in previous projects, they stated that CVA has the potential to strengthen protection outcomes for women and girls, such as renting safe housing or meeting basic needs, which reduces the likelihood of risky coping strategies such as early marriage, forced marriage, selling their assets and jewelry, and beggary from the community. Additionally, integrating the CVA into the GBV multi-sectoral services ensures providing the full package and complete cycle of services for women, which make them safe, empowered, and referred when needed to other types of required services such as cash or small grants.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is designed to provide safe and equal access to adequate multi-sectoral services for women and girls and for the women and girls with disabilities through six UNFPA safe spaces' service providers, who are specialized in providing comprehensive services for GBV survivors.These six safe spaces in Gaza and West Bank are physically adapted for PwDs including the construction of ramps, installation of handrails, signs, toilets, the width of doors and corridors, and non-slippery floors, under high-quality technical supervision from specialized organizations. Also, Safe spaces in Gaza adapted the service directory and printed it in Braille language. Additionally, the staff and service providers in UNFPA safe spaces received advanced training on how to interact with PwDs conducted by Stars of hope as a disability organization (DPO) through the partnership agreement with UNFPA. Moreover, most of the staff received basic sign language training, to facilitate interaction with persons with hearing and speech impairments, and UNFPA and its partners will make sure to get the media productions like videos with sign language interpretations.On the one hand, our safe spaces provide referrals for specialized rehabilitation services as occupational therapy as well as support services such as assistive devices for women and girls with disabilities.On the other hand, UNFPA coordinates with Humanity and Inclusion (HI) which is a member of GBV SC, in relevant activities or referrals for PwDs, in addition to other PDOs such as Stars of Hope, GUDP, ATFALUNA for Deaf children, and others. Also, the project focuses on reaching the vulnerable beneficiaries from different areas of the Gaza Strip and West Bank, Additionally, the project will target women who are cancer and kidney-failure patients to provide them with responsive services in addition to some special needs and treatment. Moreover, based on UNFPA study about violence on the elderly, the project will target elderly people mainly women to ensure their equitable access to health and other services. On the other hand, the awareness, outreach initiatives, advocacy campaigns, and social media materials will cover wide areas to ensure all targeted communities have a better knowledge about GBV, link of COVID-19 and GBV, Cyber Crimes, rights of PWDs, PSEA, and other important topics.Moreover, a significant number of DPOs is targeted in PSEA capacity building by UNFPA, and the SAWA hotline operators also received training on dealing with people with disabilities remotely.It is worth mentioning that UNFPA regularly coordinates with UNRWA regarding the refugees' coverage. UNRWA is a member of the GBV SC, and there is good coordination between UNFPA and UNRWA to ensure proper, equal, and fair coverage and outreach to all target groups of refugees and host communities.Also, UNRWA adopts the referral pathways and service directory that UNFPA and its partners use in their work.
