Project Summary

Children in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, face severe and chronic protection challenges getting to and from school every day. They cross Israeli military check points and go through closed military zones where they can experience harassment and even violence. On their daily commute, they pass by Israeli settlements which can also be a source of abuse, harassment and violence.
The project will contribute to the 2019 HRP education objective 2 of improving safe access to quality learning environment in vulnerable areas by providing protection in accessing education to primary and secondary students (ages 6-17), 6700 (2700girls and 4000 boys) and 200 teaches (100 female and 100 male) in H2, South Hebron Hills, Bethlehem, South Nablus, East Jerusalem and other selected areas in need in the North of West Bank. Children and teachers facing obstacles to schools or children exposed to risks of harassment on their way to schools will be accompanied by international volunteers.
This intervention has been developed in consultation with the affected communities who express increased demand for it and will also be coordinated with the Protection Cluster. Several assessments, including recently completed Hebron H2 household survey conducted by OCHA in August 2018 have shown that parents are likely to send their children to school when they are accompanied on their way to and from school.
The lack of safe access to education has significant and negative consequences for children’s schooling and results in a decrease of school hours, an increased drop out rate of children, discouraging teaching staff and the displacement or separation of families as they seek solutions in getting their children in schools elsewhere. In the most severe cases, these negative factors effect children’s overall well being. In coordination with and building on the ongoing evaluation of the protective presence by the Protection Cluster UNICEF will conduct an evidence-based study to evaluate the current protective presence approach and its effectiveness as it relates to the education access and retention. The study will suggest alternative modalities that would mitigate the risks of relying on the international NGO partner in case of access constraints. Communities, parents, students and teachers will be included in the study through focus groups and surveys. UNICEF team is also responsible for monitoring of the implementation to ensure quality and course-correct if needed and achievement of all planned objectives.

Needs

In the West Bank, children continue to face violence and challenges in accessing schools., In the first half of 2017, there were 144 incidents of educated-related violations, a 13 per cent increase from the 127 recorded in the same period in 2016. The overwhelming majority have been in Area C, the Hebron H2 area and East Jerusalem, affecting 18,073 children. In the West Bank, tear gas and rubber bullets are frequently fired at students, and Israeli forces make regular incursions onto school grounds. Frequently, children must pass through one of 59 checkpoints, where they are subjected to bag and/or body searches, and at times harassment and detention. Students also risk confrontation, intimidation and violence while walking past settlements or buildings occupied by settlers. These children can be exposed to conflict-related violence and delays, which can, coupled with other push factors such as weak teacher preparation, lack of remedial education support or poor learning environment in school, lead to higher school drop-out rates. Girls in particular face a high risk of school drop-out due to the fear of parents for their safety during the school commute. Several assessments clearly demonstrate the need for protection and accompaniment interventions. Hebron H2 household survey found that 88 per cent of children in H2 cross checkpoints to attend their schools, and most parents reported that their major education related concern is soldier’s harassment and settlers’ violence against their children, and 60 per cent of families reported that their children are facing harassment by soldiers at a higher frequency compared to the period before October 2016. In addition, needs assessments done by EAPPI in 2018, one of the key providers of protection presence, and provided to the EIE WG identified the following as the most vulnerable and in need areas: 1) H2 Checkpoints 55, 56, 209 (covering Qurtoba and Ibrahimi schools and the Sumud KG) – 294 (102 girls) children and 16 teachers; 2) South Hebron Hills villages of Birin and in At Tuwani, as well as As Sefer community where children walk pass the settlements; 3) Bethlehem: Tuqu’ schools (Al Jarmaq, Al Khansa’ and the Secondary boys schools) where there is continuous military presence near and at the entrances of those schools; 4) Al Khader schools compound located near road 60, 5) East Jerusalem: Old City schools and Damascus gate, Harrods gate, and along Al Wad street leading to Al Awqaf schools in the Old City, where older children 15+ are more likely to be stopped and have their school bags searched and Issawiya: schools where there is regular military presence around schools; 6) Nablus: As Sawiya - Al Lubban Boys' Secondary School, Al Lubban Girls Secondary School are located on Road 60, which is heavily used by settlers and Israeli military forces. About 600 students (370 boys) are at risk of harassment from soldiers or settlers; Burin secondary Boys’ School and Urif Boys Secondary School is often exposed to tear gas and sound bombs; there are occasional military incursions on the school grounds and occasional roadblocks in the morning on the road leading to the school, thus making students late for classes or preventing them from coming at all.

Activities

Train accompaniers and protection service providers (cultural and country-specific background, including gender sensitivity and equality issues, monitoring, reporting, intervening, awareness raising).1. Accompany 6,700 children (boys and girls ages 6-17) and 200 teachers (50% female) on their way to / from schools in Area C. Send accompaniers/observers to additional checkpoints and schools on an ad-hoc basis, as a form of rapid response, in cases of a reported increase in harassment, violence, and intimidation towards boys and girls2. Conduct a study of the current Protective Presence approach effectiveness and a research on alternative modalities.
