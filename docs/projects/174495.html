Project Summary

Since 2017, the Non-state Armed Groups (NSAG) have demanded the closure of schools. They threatened or burned down establishments that have remained opened. As a consequence, as of December 2019, 83% (5,307 out of 6,379) schools are closed, putting 81% (841,330 out of estimated 1,033,000) children out of schools and 73% (21,128 out of 28,866) teachers not reporting to work owing to the life-taking threat posted on them and children as well as attacks on education facilities.
Schools have been shut down in most of the difficult and hard to reach villages in the Anglophone Regions for the past four years. Parents who had means displaced their children from these villages to attend schools in urban areas in the English speaking Regions where schools were ongoing while others moved to French speaking Regions. The very vulnerable parents and children who lacked the means to seek education out of the very difficult and hard to reach crisis zones have been out of schools for four academic years, this has led to child recruitment into arm groups and early marriages.

An estimated 1,033,000 school aged children have been affected by the crisis. They were denied the opportunity to learn and develop themselves. Tellingly, failing in achieving Sustainable Development Goal (SDG), specially, the goal 4: ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all. The current system is leaving more than 955,700 children behind
Separatist issued conflicting statements about the 5th October 2020 reopening of schools. Whereas some groups stated that there has been no change in their position about schools in restive Regions, statements issued by other groups endorsed the reopening of schools with certain preconditions.
Schools officially resumed for the 2020/2021 academic year on the 5th October 2020, however, the doors of public schools in some hard to reach areas in the NWR and SWR of Cameroon are still closed. As a result parents and communities have organised learning centres for their children from nursery, primary and secondary levels to enable them enjoy their fundamental right to education.
Due to the extremely high risk operational environment in both Regions, for which education is used as political instrument in the conflict, some communities have developed strategies to grant children access to education through learning centers. The creation of learning centers in hard to reach villages in crisis affected Divisions in the NW (Bui, Donga Mantung, Mezam,) and SW(Fako, Lebialem, Ndian) has offered opportunity to many vulnerable children to have access to education and support affected population to meet their basic need of education
The main objective of this project is to support and Promote the functioning of 13 learning centers within 6 Divisions for 1490 boys and 1300 girls (3-18years), in the NW(Bui, Donga Mantung and Mezam) and South West (Fako, Lebialem and Ndian) Regions of Cameroon for 12 months
The specific objectives are;
• Provide school learning kids to 90 teachers and 2790 children (3-18 years) in 13 learning centers within 6 Divisions in the NW(Bui, Donga Mantung and Mezam) and SW(Fako, Lebialem and Ndian) region of Cameroon
• Sensitized 2790 children (3-18 years) on effective COVID 19 safe protocol in 13 learning centers within 3 Divisions as in the NW, 3SW and provision of COVID 19 safe materials for 12 months
• Assist 2790 children (3-18 years) with school feeding programs in 13 learning centers within 6 Divisions in the NW(Bui, Donga Mantung and Mezam) and SW(Fako, Lebialem and Ndian) for 12 months.
• Train 90 Teachers and education Personnel in 13 learning centers within 6 Divisions in the NWR (Bui, Donga Mantung and Mezam) and SWR(Fako, Lebialem and Ndian) on psychosocial training for 12 months.
• Actively engage Community participation through creation of feedback committees, hotlines, complaint and feedback mechanism for 12 months.
