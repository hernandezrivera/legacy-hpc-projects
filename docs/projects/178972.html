Project Summary

SAMS through this Project will sustain its integration of protection services (GP, GBV, and MA) into health facilities utilizing SAMS experience and success stories in the field of intervention in addition to the lessons learned collected throughout similar projects.
The planned PSS and GBV services will be integrated into 16 health facilities in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, where a safe space (protection space) in each facility will be dedicated for those services. Each protection space will have 2 PSS workers + 1 GBV case Manager + 1 Case Worker, they will provide in center and outreach (GBV and GP) services including Identification of cases; GBV case management; GBV prevention and awareness-raising; non-GBV and non-CP case management; PSS; monthly Protection monitoring surveys and Referrals to specialized services (CMR, HLP, Health, mental health, livelihoods, etc.,); Key messaging and awareness-raising targeting beneficiaries in direct contact with humanitarian workers on the PSEA and community awareness for all protection concerns. As for the MA component, it will be provided in two health facilities. The VA team in each facility will consist of: 4 Physiotherapists + 4 PT assistants + 2 PSS workers, they will provide specialized services (PT and rehabilitation in addition to MHPSS services) for the survivors of explosive hazard accidents (both in center and outreach mobile services), Moreover, SAMS will refer the survivor to livelihoods service providers.
SAMS will follow all the precautionary measures as per SAMS COVID 19 PRP and in alignment with the COVID-19 Protection Cluster Recommendations and Observations on the Criticality of Protection Activities in NW Syria (March 2020) and Best experiences and practices in implementing GBV programs in the time of Covid-19. Furthermore, awareness against COVID 19 will be included in all community messages and awareness activities.
Through this project, SAMS will work on achieving the following objectives:
Objective1: Improve protection of population affected by the crisis through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and through advocacy with duty bearers
Objective2: Improve access to quality and life-saving GBV response services for GBV survivors and women and girls at risk and put measures in place to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV
Objective 3: Minimize the impact of explosive ordnance for communities most at risk
SAMS will conduct quarterly monitoring visits to the supported facilities. SAMS will use the M&E tool kit to monitor the GBV activities, SAMS will monitor the quality of the project also, through KAP surveys and semi-annual FGDs. The VA services will use Functional Independence Measure, and WHO DAS.

4 - Needs

Over 4M affected persons in NWS; an estimated 2.7 M IDPs are in need of immediate humanitarian assistance (NWS Situation Report, 26 Mar, 21). 2020 witnessed the further deterioration of security, mass displacement, and economic challenges in addition to the onset of the COV19 pandemic. Violence, protracted displacement, ongoing violations of IHL and human rights, extensive destruction of infrastructure, and inadequate access and services are likely to continue impacting the protection landscape (HNO 2021). Civilians across Syria continue to be killed or injured by attacks from IED incidents in populated areas and ERW incidents leading to increased security concerns and further displacement; approx. 1 in 3 survivors suffer at least 1 limb amputation. Among the survivors benefiting from rehabilitation services in Syria, 2/3 will sustain lifelong impairment. Last Sep, SAMS conducted a KAP survey for SGBV and PSS needs in NWS, interviewing 154 women and 15 adolescent girls from 217 HHs, the emotional-psychological abuse was the most reported type of girls abuse 81%, followed by physical abuse 62%, economic violence 44% and sexual abuse 38%, while 7% stated that they don’t know the main types of abuse girls face in the community, 56% stated they heard about domestic violence against women and girls in their communities. 43% stated they know that there are GBV services in their community and they feel comfortable in reporting the incidents of violence; 43% don’t know of the availability of GBV services in their communities and 31% stated that there are no GBV services in their communities. 52% knew to whom they should report the GBV incidents, while 48% don’t know. The KAP survey results is aligned with Voices 20 & 21.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activities will maintain the already established 16 WGSS integrated into 16 HFs in NWS (16 different communities, located in sub districts ranked between 3 - 5 On the severity scale). SAMS will continue providing VA services (Physiotherapy and MHPSS, individual and group sessions), outreach services, distribution of the assistive products and referrals; GP including awareness raising; psychosocial Support; general Protection Case management: Including identification of persons under high protection risk (newly displaced, elderly, PWD) and documenting these cases and referral to the needed specialized services; GBV services for women and men including GBV case management (Identification + Documentation + Referral + awareness) in addition to the preventive interventions.SAMS will work also on building the capacity of the service providers, SAMS currently conducting a TNA for all facilities and a training plan will be developed accordingly. In each WGSS (GP and GBV) there will be 1 Case Manager + 1 Caseworker + 2 PSSW + 2 outreach team members. Led by 4 protection team leaders and a field human rights IM officer (based in SAMS Syria main offices). The VA team in each facility consists of 4 Physiotherapists + 4 PT assistants + 2 PSSWs. MEAL will conduct monitoring activities by using MEAL toolkits (GBV and protection);GBV/protection: Ensuring the quality of services and impact by conducting KAP surveys and FGD with beneficiaries.MA: Conduct beneficiary satisfaction surveys, random location spot-checks, and direct interviews with the related staff. besides reporting the Mine action cases to cluster.Convene regular review sessions to discuss MEAL findings and identify adaptation/changes in response to population feedback.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: 1. Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reappraisal and further trauma; 2. Breach of confidentiality putting at risk SGBV survivors due to lack of capacity on information management of service providers. Possibility: high, Impact: high; Mitigation: 1. only trained personnel should conduct the case management and they should include only those that can provide survivor-centered services in the service mapping, 2. Ensure that confidentiality principles are in line with global standards, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. Risk: Beneﬁciaries subject to abuse and abuse by center staff due to poor eﬃciency and lack of knowledge in providing services. Possibility: Low Impact: High Mitigation: The availability of a clear feedback mechanism within the HFs (feedback boxes, brochures, hotlines, emails, etc.), in addition to the availability of periodic training for service providers and ensure supervision Effective technical service. Risk: Persons with disabilities, older persons and areas away from centers. Possibility: High Impact: High Mitigation: Outreach teams will be allocated to reach beneﬁciaries who cannot access the centers to participate in activities for people with severe disabilities and elderly people with mobility diﬃculties in remote areas. Beneﬁciaries access will be facilitated by providing transportation as needed to ensure their effective participation. Risk: Local population becomes reliant on VA provided by SAMS. Possibility: Low, Impact: High, Mitigation: Work with and coordinate with local medical authorities to ensure the enhancement and building of local medical capacities.
