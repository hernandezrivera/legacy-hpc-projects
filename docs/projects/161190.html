1 - Needs

Over 4 million people were forced to flee their homes in South Sudan due to insecurity, including 2.27 million refugees still hosted in neighboring countries. As per the most recent OCHA report, the number of IPDs ranks at 1,5 millions in the country. The alarming humanitarian situation, combined with climate change, usual seasonal droughts, flooding and resource-based conflicts, have also impacted these displacements. Moreover, the health situation is only likely to deteriorate : 4,5 millions of South Sudanese people do not have access to sufficient healthcare services in 2019, while 3,296 823 millions remain in need of humanitarian assistance. Public health services are non-existent and health indicators in the country are amongst the worst in the world. According to a study released by “The Lancet”, South Sudan presents one of the lowest health-related Sustainable Development Goal in the World insofar as the country ranks 186th out of the 188 countries studied . Moreover, in South Sudan, the mortality rate of under 5 children is 90.7/1000 while the maternal mortality rate ranks 789/100,000. Finally, the protection cluster underlined the fact that Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) cases reported increased by 72% compared to the same period in 2017. In South Sudan, health implementing partners are funded through the mechanism of the Health Pooled Fund (HPF). Through this mechanism, health actors support a designated number of health facilities (Primary Health Care Units, Primary Health Care Centers and State Hospitals), as well as contribute to strengthening the capacity of MoH staff. However, not all health structures are covered by the mechanism, leaving parts of the population without access to basic primary health care. In Aweil, former Northern Bahr El Ghazal State which has now split into 5 states, the projected population for 2019 is 1,214,576 individuals. In Aweil East and West Counties, the population ranks 770,778 individuals (63% of the total population of the 5 States), including 485,590 children under 15 years old. ALIMA intends to scale up its intervention in both locations where the total of people in needs ranks 520,500 individuals. In Aweil East County, 133,900 individuals are suffering from a lack of access to healthcare, while 85,700 are in need of protection. In Aweil West, 145,900 individuals are in need of humanitarian assistance (especially health and nutrition). In Aweil East and West, a total of 106 health facilities are registered across the State. Health services in these areas are mainly supported through the HPF which covers 48 health facilities, while 59 remain supported by the State Ministry of Health (SMoH). These health facilities only rely on the SMoH regarding the provision of essential drugs, equipment and human resources. Moreover, healthcare professionals remain underqualified and frequent rupture of essential drugs have serious consequences on quality health care. As per poor road conditions and distances to be covered, the population remain isolated without access to basic primary health care, especially during the rainy season. ALIMA will work in close collaboration with the MoH staff within the health structures selected. Despite HPF support to health facilities, needs are not covered as 280,000 individuals are still in need of healthcare in Aweil East and West. Finally, ALIMA is actually present in Aweil and delivers primary health care services to the most affected population. Since January 2019, the teams in the field provided 60,000 outpatient consultations. Also, the organisation has actually scaled up its response in order to provide increased services to the most affected population during the malaria peak.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

ALIMA will promote equal, equitable and impartial access to basic primary health care while implementing its project. ALIMA will promote access to primary health care services to all vulnerable population, without distinction of race, religion, sex, gender or political appurtenance. ALIMA is fully aware that the right to health is an inclusive right, endorsed by the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, adopted by the UN General Assembly in December 1966.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

ALIMA designed its project for a specific number of targeted vulnerable individuals. However, according to the evolution of the humanitarian crisis, ALIMA will advocate to scale up its response in order to provide basic primary health care to the most vulnerable population. Strong coordination and clear communication with other organization in the field will be maintained to ensure that the needs of the people in need are covered. This includes displacement-affected communities. Indeed, if ALIMA notes that the workload increases for instance due to a massive movement of population, ALIMA will make sure that all the most vulnerable affected population within the areas of intervention will have an equal access to primary health care. Finally, ALIMA will manage all patients requiring primary health care support, without any distinction regarding the are of origin of the patient. Moreover, the provision of primary health care is free of charge.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project intends to strongly involved MoH staff working within the health structures. Moreover, Traditional Birth Attendant and Community Health Worker's network will be created and/or revitalized to ensure a good collaboration between health workers within the community. This will allow the management of patients at an early stage at the PHCU/C, to avoid patients coming late to the health structures with potential health-related complications. For instance, working with a strong network of TBA aims to provide informations related to complications during pregnancy and highlight the necessity for a pregnant woman to be closely followed.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project intends to address the needs of the most vulnerable population living in the areas of intervention, according to the 3 national objectives of the health sector : 1) Reduce the excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases 2) Improve the access and scale up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations 3) Increase the access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders. All planned activities are in accordance with those listed by the health cluster.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

In Aweil East and West, the protection environment is sufficiently safe/stable to linked the project to durable solutions. Indeed, along the project, ALIMA will continuously work with the State Ministry of Health and its workforce present within the primary health care unit and centers. ALIMA will make sure that their capacities are ensured along the project through trainings on specific topics, as well as with on-the-job trainings. Improving and strengthening their capacities will be made a priority along the project.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Along the project implementation, ALIMA will ensure that the SMoH staff capacities are improved and strengthen through different trainings: on the job trainings, trainings on specific topics according to the needs raised by the team etc. Improving and strengthening SMoH staff capacities aims to that healthcare workers acquire more self-confidence when performing medical procedures. Finally, this will ensure a good sustainability of the project at the time of the project handover to the State Ministry of Health and communities.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

During consultations and especially the SRH consultations, ALIMA will ensure that women and girls are provided with a specific interviews by the medical staff, underlining their needs. Privacy will be ensured by the medical team, and women and girls will have the opportunity to express themselves freely without fear, stigma and judgement by the medical team. All the complaint they will express will be raised in their names and ALIMA will ensure that their voice is listened at the time of decision making and processes. If necessary, ALIMA will adapt the design of its project. ALIMA will ensure that youth have the possibility to raise their voice and express their needs freely without fear and stigma among the community. The organization will make sure that at consultations and decision making processes, youth are fully involved in the mechanisms. For example, all will have the possibility to express their needs and complaints through a complain box available in each health facility. While supporting the health facilities, ALIMA will work in close collaboration with the MoH, as well as TBA and CHW. This will allow women who experience fear to go to the health facility to seek SGBV care to have a first relay within the community. Working with TBA and CHW will create confidence for SGBV victims, who will be orientated to the health facility depending on their will. Their consent will be free and informed. Also, SGBV’s consultation room won’t be clearly signaled within the health facility to ensure a total privacy for the victims. Finally, all patient seeking care will be informed on the availability of services in the health facility and ALIMA’s team will make sure that all patients are well informed on the services provided to SGBV victims. Despite the fact that ALIMA will provide primary health care within fixed structures, the organization will also made available a mobile clinic to ensure that the most vulnerable people have access to basic primary healthcare.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

All along the project, ALIMA with ensure good communication with communities and religious leaders as well as with elders to ensure good information about the location and the needs of the most vulnerable population. While delivering health care, ALIMA will adapt the information delivered to the patient. This allow the patient to undertake informed decisions and choices. The most vulnerable population (women, children, elderlies and disabled) will received context specific messages and the organization will make sure that the patient understands. ALIMA’s team will provide information in the local dialect. ALIMA will ensure that consultation are conduct without putting aside any group of vulnerable people. While implementing the project, ALIMA will ensure that the affected population and especially people with disabilities, women and youths will have an active role in decision making and have influence. ALIMA will pay a particular attention to conduct consultation while the voice of young girl, boy, disables, elders and religious groups is listened. In each of the health facility where ALIMA will provide primary health care support, the organization will ensure that the affected population has the possibility to share their feedback. A complaint mechanism will be set up in all health facilities. The complaint mechanism will include different steps, such as communication, receipt, process, response and action taken according to the complaint. In all health facility, ALIMA will make sure that a box for anonymous complaints mechanism is available. While implementing the project, ALIMA will ensure regular meeting with community representative, religious leaders, but also key players in the community such as traditional birth attendant, community health workers etc. This will allow the organization to re-orientate the project if needed, according to the needs of the supported population.
