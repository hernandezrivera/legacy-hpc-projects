Project Summary

The proposed WASH project will be implemented in Leer & Pibor, as well as other emergency and IPC 4 and 5 locations. According to the WASH Cluster's county prioritization for 2023, Leer and Pibor are Priority 1 counties. The project is designed based on Core Humanitarian and Sphere Minimum Standards to ensure that affected populations receive timely, coordinated, adequate, resilient, and impartial basic water, sanitation, and hygiene services. Medair engages the affected populations in all stages of the project cycle and ensures that the 'Do no harm' principle is followed by not exposing affected beneficiaries to any risk as a result of project implementation. Furthermore, the proposed WASH project will adhere to the WASH Cluster technical guidelines and WHO drinking water quality standards. Medair will continue to integrate WASH, nutrition and health internally, as well as collaborate with other nutrition, health and protection partners, to maximize the impact of the proposed WASH project. WASH activities will also be integrated into health activities in the context of disease prevention and response. It aims at breaking the transmission cycle of water related diseases including diarrhoeal, cholera and vector borne diseases. This is intended to be achieved through multiple integrated approaches. Overall, the project is targeted to reach 11,111 people through improved access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services by December 2024.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan has continued to deteriorate for the last few years due to multiple shocks including economic downturn, the ongoing impacts of consecutive years of severe flooding and recurring inter-communal violence. The WASH Cluster estimated that the living standard and well-being of 6.1 million people will be impacted in 2023 as a result of either inadequate WASH services or a lack of access to WASH services (HNO 2023). The 2024 HRP is targeting 5.5 million people, including host communities, IDPs, and returnees, in an effort to address the pressing needs. WASH conditions were found to be most severe in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity and Northern Bahr el Gazhal (HNO 2023). All Medair’s static project locations in Pibor and Leer counties are located in two of the above-mentioned states; Jonglei and Unity State respectively. The WASH programs are integrated with both health and nutrition programs at facility and community level. Medair also has an ERT team which responds to emergencies all over the country based on triggers. The ERT targets hard-to-reach areas, including those affected by the Sudan Crisis due to an influx of refugees and returnees, where partners on the ground have limited or no capacity to respond. The humanitarian funding trend has been observed to be declining in recent years, which has had a negative impact on WASH partners' capacity to respond to the growing chronic humanitarian need. As a result, the WASH Cluster is recommending partners to target people who are in extreme or catastrophic WASH vulnerability. This also prompted the Cluster to classify counties into different priority levels based on the FSNMS data collected for the HPC 2023/2024. According to the WASH Cluster classification, Leer and Pibor Counties, where Medair has static projects, are classified as Priority 1. A baseline survey conducted by Medair in Pibor in 2023 revealed that only 13.2% of the population has access to an improved water source which corroborated its classification. Additionally, the HNO 2023 report states that only 42% of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide. Poor access to WASH services and goods, combined with high levels of food insecurity, has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people, as evidenced by the high prevalence of malnutrition in the country. Lack of access to basic water, sanitation, and hygiene services, combined with insufficient healthcare, has put communities, particularly the most vulnerable groups such as the elderly, women, and children, at greater risk of water-related diseases.

2. Project activities

The activity goal is to increase access to quality essential services and reduce morbidity and mortality through integrated health, nutrition, protection, and water, sanitation & hygiene (WASH) services for emergency affected IDP, returnees and host populations in targeted areas in South Sudan. This contributes to sectoral objectives SO1 to SO4 of the WASH Cluster objectives. The proposed WASH project will carry out various activities. The first and most important activities are rapid needs assessments, focus group discussions, KII, baseline surveys as needed, and desk reviews to understand the context and environmental and climate factors that may influence the project during implementation. This will include an analysis of factors that contribute to gender inequality in WASH, including an understanding of the gendered norms, roles and barriers that impact on access to, use of and influence on WASH. Supporting nutrition programmes and improving access to basic WASH services are integral parts of WASH activities in the WASH Cluster First Priority Counties Pibor and Leer and other WSC and IPC 4/5 locations across the country. The activities include construction and rehabilitation of resilient water points such as boreholes, water yards and surface water treatment systems (SWATs). Medair will scale up raising handpump platforms in flood affected areas. In areas where there is no safe water source, Medair will consider water trucking and distribution of household water treatment systems such as chlorine products and water filters with water collection and storage containers to targeted households. The proposed project aims to improve access to safe and gender-segregated sanitation with handwashing facilities in health and nutrition sites. However, due to limited available resources, Medair will only consider emergency latrine construction in active displacement and disease outbreaks and if their absence has a significant public health risk. The proposed WASH activities also include improving infectious waste management practices in health and nutrition facilities and promote personal and household hygiene through the Care Group network and traditional hygiene approaches. WASH will also support the health team with WASH aspects of Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) activities at facilities. In all project locations, Medair ensures the ‘Do No Harm’ principle is implemented by training the WASH staff on PSEA and creating community wide awareness about PSEA. Furthermore, Medair will continue with protection mainstreaming by conducting safety audits and disability inclusion FGDs and collaborating with protection partners to address GBV concerns.
