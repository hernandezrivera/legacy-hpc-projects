Project Summary

IOM's 2020 HRP nutrition project will contribute to the reduction of emergency levels of acute malnutrition through an integrated approach for management for Severely Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in children under 5 years. Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) services for SAM treatment will be integrated into all of IOM’s static and mobile primary healthcare facilities across Somalia. In addition to reduction of SAM cases, the project will improve family and community capacity to promote appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices, as well as provide micro-nutrient supplementation and link to immunization services particularly measles vaccination. The project will ensure effective referral mechanisms to and from other nutritional services. The project will target 86,000 children (43,860 girls and 42,140 boys) aged 6-59 months living in IDP settlements and their host communities. A further 12,500 pregnant and lactating mothers will benefit from learning and adopting proper IYCF practices, with continued counseling and follow-up. The project is aligned with protection mainstreaming to ensure safety and dignity for affected populations without causing harm; there will be meaningful access, accountability and participation of beneficiaries in the delivery of nutrition services. The project will be a part of an IOM integrated emergency response program consisting of nutrition, health, WASH, CCCM, shelter, NFI, and protection activities.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1: Integrate 25 OTP centers into static facilities currently supported by IOM, and into 7 mobile clinics in Jubaland, Southwest State, Somaliland, and Puntland. Activity 1.2: Identify and treat 86,000 children aged 6-59 months with severe acute malnutrition through access to equitable, high-quality lifesaving OTP services using RUTF and micro-nutrient supplementation. Activity 1.3: Provide 12,500 pregnant and lactating women with infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counseling and training, combined with micronutrient supplementation and linkage to immunization services especially measles vaccination. Activity 2.1: Preposition and distribute ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) and medicines to 25 static OTPs integrated into primary health facilities and 7 mobile OTP sites. Activity 2.2: Establish referral mechanisms from community level to OTP sites, and from OTP sites to/from other nutrition programs— to/from therapeutic supplementary feeding centers (TFSC) for moderate acute malnutrition, and to/from stabilization centers (SC) for SAM with complications requiring inpatient care. Activity 3.1: Provide training and on-the-job mentorship for 200 clinical health and nutrition staff (100 women, 100 men) across all integrated OTP and primary healthcare centers on IMAM and IYCF guidelines. Activity 3.2: Train 300 community outreach nutrition volunteers (150 women, 150 Men) via on-job training who will provide MUAC screening, referral to OTPs, and counseling in catchment communities of the OTPs.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measures 2a - Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance i. IOM will gather data (age, sex, diversities) of communities and beneficiaries for effective and informative curative and preventive nutrition programing. This will help identify vulnerable and marginalized persons for planning, inclusion purposes and engagement ii. A systematic outreach process will be done to reach marginalized/minority vulnerable groups with nutrition community screening, mobilization, and outreach to avoid exclusion iii. Efforts will be made to ensure that information regarding provision of nutrition services will be disseminated to all members of communities (including women, girls, boys, men) through appropriate channel of communication such as radio iv. An inclusive feedback mechanism that will include all beneficiaries will be put in place which help evaluate selection criteria and overall services gaps. v. In all nutrition service delivery points, a mechanism will be established that will give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women, so that they wait for less time. 2b- Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes i. Women, girls and boys will be consulted during programme design to gather accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for location and design of nutrition facility- and community-level services. Issues of privacy and access to persons with disability will be discussed. ii. Community nutrition committees will be comprised of minimum 55% females, including young women. iii. Efforts will be made to ensure that women are included as hygiene, nutrition, and health promoters so that they actively contribute to the overall program implementation at community level. iv. The project will hire female staff members at project locations to ensure that women and girls will feel more comfortable talking to them and this will also provide a feedback mechanism v. Girls and boys will actively be engaged in children nutrition, health and hygiene promotion groups/campaigns 3b- Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. i. Nutrition service delivery locations (static health facilities and mobile units that will have the OTPs integrated in them) will be put in central, visible places and not more 500 meters away from settlements, and ideally within settlement areas. This will help protect women and girls from risks of sexual violence/exploitation when accessing services ii. Separate toilet facilities with internal locks and secondary enclosures will be in place for women and men in OTPs (the health facilities they are integrated into) with clear labels such as pictorial/signs to ensure that they are used by intended gender iii. In setups where threats are anticipated or previously experienced, location, timing and modality of service delivery will be considered. iv. Coordination will be done with Shelter/NFI partners to put solar light installations at OTPs (nutrition service delivery points) to deter any risk of violence against women and girls v. Nutrition services at community-level (screening, nutrition promotive services, OTP patient follow-up) will be delivered during daylight hours to allow beneficiaries who are often women and girls to reach home early.

Please provide further explanation

This nutrition project is linked to government and community systems, as it is implemented in partnership with the Ministry of Health, utilizes Ministry of Health workforce, is based on national development and humanitarian response frameworks, and includes community engagement throughout the project process including accountability to affected populations. This project has inbuilt sustainability. OTP services integrated within primary healthcare services are implemented through Ministry of Health staff, building their long-term capacity and in the long-run enabling full government ownership/operation of the OTPs and linked primary healthcare clinics, and ultimately reduction of donor dependency. The entitlement of beneficiaries to nutrition, health and related services in this project is generally legally recognized in government-controlled areas, but not necessarily for all groups of population (e.g. migrants, refugees).
