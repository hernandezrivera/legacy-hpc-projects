Project Summary

Armed conflict affects the entire populace but it makes a special impact on children as the most vulnerable group. They are made to pay the price for a war that is not of their making, hence losing out on the beauty of childhood as they to grow up among families and communities torn apart by armed conflicts, or in some cases partake in the conflicts as child soldiers. They are exposed to human rights violations as the conflict affects the provision of services, including food, health, education and infrastructure, and also affects adults who are significant to the children, such as fathers, mothers and siblings. Often children are separated from families and loved ones, left to face the harsh effects of conflict as internally displaced persons, and suffer from hunger and disease as a result of the tactics of the combatants to disrupt the supply of food. Children in Borno state have experienced their fair share of the insurgency ravaging the north-eastern part of the country. Some children have been killed, abducted, forcefully recruited, and internally displaced, while others suffer family separation.
The intended intervention is aimed at addressing the objectives of the Child Protection for enhancing capacity to ensure mechanism for identifying, monitoring, reporting and responding to protection risks and enhancing the provision of quality front line child protection and coordinated services to meet priority needs for children at risk of or affected by protection risks through provision of case management response for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) and other vulnerable children at risk of abuses by Identification and documentation, Placement in alternative care, Follow-up of cases, Tracing and Reunification with their primary caregivers and Referral and support to access basic services, while boys and girls particularly those under the age of 13 years affected by psychosocial and mental distress will be reached through the provision of psycho social support in terms of Structured recreational/creative/social activities, individual and group counselling and life skill education for adolescent boys and girls.
Children identified with protection concerns will be supported with direct case management while unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) will be supported through reunification with their primary/customary caregiver, alternative care arrangement, psychosocial support and provision of life skill education for adolescent boys and girls under the case management component of this project
This project seeks to strengthen capacity of community base child protection mechanism to identify, prevent and respond to incidence of child rights violation - this will be achieved through engaging community-based staff to implement field activities.

1 - Needs

In Borno state, particularly Monguno LGA, children’s rights are being violated in diverse ways and they continue to suffer the most part of the ongoing insurgency in the state due to activities of Armed Opposition Groups (AOG) also called Boko Haram and Military offensive against the AOG in the surrounding villages resulting in massive civilian displacement. Subsequent attempted attacks on Monguno on 28 December, 2018 have triggered the movement of new arrivals mainly into Maiduguri, Monguno, Konduga and Jere local government areas (LGAs). In Monguno, new arrivals are seeking refuge in camps that are already congested. Over 10,600 approximate number of new arrivals since 1 November 2018. While the number of new arrivals continue to increase since 2018, More than 80 per cent are women and children who are in dire need of humanitarian assistance, especially food, shelter, WASH, and protection-related services. Impact of these conflict related experiences have both long-term and immediate effect on children psychosocial well-being, their families and communities. Many children continue to be exposed to one or more violence, including; loss of friends, family members and neighbors, accumulated stress, deterioration in living conditions, inability to provide for one’s self and family, and lack of access to services. It can also hamper their ability to access other services, such as education, further damaging their life chances. High influx of IDPs are leading to camp overcrowding and congestion in the hosting communities. As a result, significant numbers of new arrivals are sleeping outside in the open, without shelter. Women, girls, and children arrive in the visibly poor physical condition that require the provision of crucial supplies and support services. A joint rapid protection assessment has revealed safety concerns, especially for women and girls, who are exposed to survival sex in search of water, food and other critical needs. Incidents of rape and other forms of GBV have been reported, perpetrated by adult men targeting adolescent girls. Reception/screening processes are compromising the safety and dignity of women, girls and the elderly.

2 - Activities

The project will support Child Protection objectives through providing 1. children that require CP case management services by providing: - Identification and documentation of unaccompanied, separated and children affected by other protection risks-Placement of unaccompanied, separated and other vulnerable children in alternative care-Follow-up of cases of unaccompanied, separated and other vulnerable children-Reunification of unaccompanied and separated children with their primary caregivers-Referral and support to access basic services 2. Children that require Pychososcial support using Community Based Therapy (CBT) approach under the following activities; -Structured recreational/creative/social activities for girls and boys particularly for those under 13 years of age-Life skills education for adolescent girls and boys -Individual and group counselling and mental health support for girls and boys affected by psychosocial and mental distress

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Despite the good intentions of ROHI, we recognized that we are not exempt from processes needing improvement, fraud, or a breach of the code of conduct by members of our staff. A complaints and feedback mechanism are an essential part of quality management as it provides a mechanism to amend serious issues and systematically gather feedback on the quality of our projects. We are committed to giving children and their communities the chance to be part the decisions that affect their lives and to raise concerns or complaints, and to respond to them appropriately.ROHI has an effective, accessible, safe and confidential complaints and feedback mechanism that considers the context and preferences of the children and communities of implementation which comprises a set of clear, transparent procedures that encourages and provide children and communities with access to a safe confidential means of voicing complaints on issues within the control of ROHIA feedback mechanism is created where inputs are received using either a written complaint log, Feedback and complaint desk or Feedback and complaint hotline, then record the message in database with the date it was received, name and contact details, then identify who should be responsible for the action point required to respond.The project will ensure feedback is collected by raising awareness on the importance of community inputs in the project and sensitizing the vulnerable members of the community on hoe to send and receive feedback using the existing mechanism
