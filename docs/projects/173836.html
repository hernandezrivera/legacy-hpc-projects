Project Summary

This emergency food security support project is a 12 Months
response to assist 80, 665 vulnerable IDPs and host community households in Adamawa and Borno state North East Nigeria. Over these period, VSO will employ 3 National Professional Volunteers, 20
National Graduate Volunteers and 2 international volunteers who will live and work with
50 community volunteers to deliver an early recovery project designed to build back
better living conditions in 20 wards within 7 Local Government areas in Borno Adamawa
The intervention aims to ensure that lives are saved and human suffering is reduced through provision of food security production inputs, to targeted beneficiaries,
in order to ensure immediate access to inputs and subsequently to food for the most vulnerable populations.
The chief targets in this action are females heading households, children
heading households, returnees, unemployed youth, vulnerable host community members and children with disabilities. VSO plans to use the relational volunteering model that creates social cohesion, that builds long lasting relationship, and which promotes long term and sustainable change. VSO volunteer rational model ensures that vulnerable and excluded beneficiaries are empowered to realize rights and full potential by building up and on their assets, skills, resilience and agency. Our Relational Volunteering Model empowers project beneficiaries,,making them leaders of their part of the development bargain.
The model promotes inclusion laying great emphasis on disability, age,
gender identities whilst addressing discrimination and harmful social norms and practices. The proposed project seeks to revive and strengthen protection mechanisms for a total of 44, 000 persons mainly girls,, women, boys faced with grave protection concerns. VSO will
conduct awareness raising sessions, link up survivors with the appropriate agencies for
trauma counselling, healing as well as psycho social support. VSO working through local community volunteers will revive and build on local community mechanisms for
supporting survivors. We will work to make these very functional and responsive to the
demands of the project beneficiaries
The prioritized objectives to achieve the overall goal of this project are;:
i. To mitigate immediate food security outcomes by providing access to food security inputs to the targeted beneficiaries.
ii. To support the vulnerable persons to rehabilitate their basic livelihood activities that have been disrupted and/or destroyed by the conflict crises
The main activities to be carried out to realize these objectives and achieve the overall goal will include: 1)
Distribution of agricultural (vegetables seeds and tools) inputs to improve access to food production by the targeted vulnerable IDPs and host community
individuals both men and women.
2) The project contributes to providing young people with equal opportunities and experience which enable them play a full part in all aspects of society and, in particular:empowering young people to promote, in their daily lives, cultural diversity as well as intercultural dialogue and co-operation
A number of factors have predisposed these targeted IDP and host community households to their current of food shortages and lack of income. These factors
include the abandonment of farms due to conflict, destruction of crops by floods in the fields, and the loss of or abandonment of food stocks and livelihood assets
(e.g. livestock, farm yield, market increase). Proxy indicators show that coping mechanisms have been stretched to the last limits with evidence of decreased daily food consumption of most
vulnerable households, (FWENET Assessment Report, 2019) i.e. restricting consumption by adults in order for small children to eat .Thus the need to support the increasingly vulnerable beneficiaries with access to food production inputs (to get food through
farming) and basic training is key.

1 - Needs

NeedsInsecurity in the North East has resulted in loss of employment, destruction of property,forced migration, displacement and disruptions in education calendars. The Boko Haraminsurgency has led to the deaths of 600 teachers, displaced 19000 teachers, anddestroyed 1200 schools settings. (RBPA 2016) Schools have been forcibly occupied andused as IDP camps and where camps have been relocated from schools, parents andchildren have found it difficult to go back to the same schools. The reintegration ofchildren especially the girl child is a challenge as they face marginalization, discriminationand rejection by family and community members due to social and cultural norms (VSOSGEA 2019). It is estimated that of the 8.7 million children out of school, 60% are girls.Drop-out rate is high in general, with more girl drop-outs in the north, forced into earlymarriage. In Borno, 18% of secondary school age girls attend formal school but at theprimary level, meaning they are behind for their age and at greater risk of dropping out(MICS, 2011). The prolonged conflict adversely impacted on the lives of communities,destroying livelihoods, denying children especially the girl child of her right to a decenteducation, further denying women, boys and girls their rights to protection, worseningthe conditions that favor the abuse of children, girls and women. a situation that persistseven today. The resultant outcome is that even at this phase of early recovery 56% of theimpacted populations is still without a secured source of livelihood or an income, millionsof IDPs have returned to what was once home, but they make a return to where a landthat no longer fully recognizes them, they have to start life anew. Host communities feelthe stress as IDPS continue to benefit of the limited social services that exist in thesecommunities. Large population of unemployed youths , unaccompanied children, teenagemothers, single mothers, females headed households and girls are at risk of Sexualexploitation because they are unemployed and cannot find employment. The insurgencyfractured an already fragile educational system, bringing the infrastructure to its kneesand worsening vulnerabilities of the girl child and other marginalized persons such asthose living with disabilities. It destroyed livelihoods and deepened the protection crisis,Women and girls have been subject of rape, were denied their rights to shelter, a home, alivelihood an education and the right to a safe and healthy motherhood. The crisis hascreated many households headed by children, a growing population of single mothers,large numbers of teenage mothers, an unusually high number of Female headedhouseholds. There are also concerns about the significant numbers of unaccompaniedchildren. Returning IDPs find they have lost their asset base, their social standing and arestruggling to re integrate into a society that they once called home. Host communitiesare struggling to provide for their families and returnees or IDPS. There is a stress on thelimited social services and infrastructure, leading to tensions between IDPs, returnees andhost communities. Data indicate that there are very high numbers of households (above50% across the BAY states ) that do not have a regular source of income. TheInternational Community has invested hugely in curtailing the humanitarian crisis in theNorth East, with many INGOs providing lifesaving services, At this phase there are hugegaps of recovery that need to be met. The gaps are largest in protection, livelihoods andeducation. A recent VSO assessments of the needs in the NE indicate that VSO uniquerelational volunteer approach to working with community volunteers, supported bynational volunteers will help lay the foundation for a health early recovery programme.This project is built around community volunteers as key actors for building back bettertheir own devastated communities.

2 - Activities

All the interventions proposed were highlighted by beneficiaries and partners after a series of consultation meetings done in October 2020 which include the seasonal livelihood programming and community participatory planning workshop.Output 1.1. Vulnerable households equipped with tools and inputs and tools to grow nutritious crops . The project intends to assist 4 groups of beneficiaries comprised each of 25 members with2 focused cash crops. The seeds will be procured The fortified crops will play a crucial role in the malnutrition prevention for their rich nutrients. In addition, other 4 groups of the same size each will be assisted with vegetable seeds and associated materials for them to start establishment of vegetable gardens. Produce for these types of crops will be used partly for home consumption whereas another portion will be sold to get income to procure others needed items for the households.Output 1.2. Capacities of targeted households increased on improved farming methodsThis section is concerned with beneficiary capacity building as well as learning and reflection. Beneficiaries will be provided with extension services through the Farmer’s Field School Model. Besides that, there will be beneficiary trainings specifically on kitchen garden and compost pits establishment and management; crop production; vegetable seed production, grading and storage; rain water harvesting and conservation techniques, post-harvest t handling techniques. After this capacity building, a group of selected beneficiaries will be taken for look and learn tours in the surrounding whereby they will widen their knowledge related to improved farming techniquesOutput 2.1. 3000 targeted vulnerable households have access to community financial servicesBeneficiaries will be encouraged to form VSLAs to promote a saving culture among them. They will be sensitized to manage efficiently household resources through social conditionalities. The project team will facilitate capacity building on income & investment management basics as well as training on entrepreneurship principles to equip them with enough skills to manage the little resources they have and help them to create themselves small jobs that can help them to generate little income.Continue providing coordination among partners of early recovery. •Training Establish and build capacity of women, youths and girls on vocational skills suchas: knitting, tailoring, shoe repair making. Finally, 2 associations will be supported to make soap locally, starting kit will be availed by the project and the members will be trained. Supporting youth to setupand manage bookkeeping businesses.
