Project Summary

For 2022 and 2023, War Child Holland (WCH) and its consortium of six Syrian implementing partners will provide an education response to 22,000 (11,500 boys and 10,500 girls) highly vulnerable children in North West and North East Syria across a 24-month action. 100% of the sub-districts are severity 3, 4 and 5. A two-year response will support more flexible and sustainable planning.
In Idleb, WCH and its partners will operate in Dana, Harim, Maaret Tamsrin, Qorqeena, Idleb, Ariha & Badama sub-districts. In Aleppo, operations will be in, but not limited to, Al Bab, Afrin, Atareb & Daret Azza sub-districts. In NES operations are planned, and not limited to, sub-districts of Raqqa, Kisreh, Hajin & Susa.
Recognising the interconnection between protection and education, the action will increase access to non-formal education and strengthen the protection of vulnerable girls and boys who are out of school or at risk of dropping out, to build their resilience and provide a pathway back into a safe and protective environment in school. The action will include non-formal education interventions including Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) for out of school children of the ages 5-17 who have missed more than two years of school. The action will also include guided self-learning for informal settlements with no access to learning spaces to keep children connected to learning, and for youth mainly boys who joined the working force to support their households and girls who are not sent to school and run the risk of early marriage. For schooled children with learning gaps, remedial and homework support will be offered to limit the risk of dropping out.
An effective teacher professional development through a coaching system based on INEE’s TICC and WCH’s Core program to ensure teachers’ and children’s wellbeing, will have coaches build the capacities of 400 teachers receiving incentives under the action. The coaches will be trained and supported by WCH education officer based on INEE’s TICC trainings, to help teachers obtain skills and methodologies for the creation of a safe and quality learning environment for children. For guided self-learning modality, teachers will be trained to support caregivers with guidance and tips to support their children’s learning and provide them with a safe learning environment at home. The action will adopt an integrated approach through developing the capacity of teachers on child protection and enhancing the accessibility and safety of the school environment. Teachers will be trained on safe identification and referral for CP concerns. This prevention and response approach will address immediate and urgent identified risks to access to education while contributing to long-term change.
WCH will pilot social emotional learning as an integrated approach that complements WCH’s evidence-based Deals PSS programs. Teachers will be trained to identify, and work with children to develop their life skill and learning competencies in the safe learning environment of the classroom.
To support in the removal of barriers to education, WCH through its partners will provide transportation to 250 CWD and other vulnerable children such as girls at risk of early marriage, provide cash for education to children who are out of school for economic difficulties based on vulnerability selection criteria, establish temporary learning spaces where none exists to provide a safe travel distance for children. To ensure a safe and quality learning environment, this action will include school rehabilitation with WASH facilities, light repairs, and school kits and stationery.
WCH will be conducting a needs assessment in NWS and NES in 2022 to ensure its response is appropriate to the needs of communities. An inclusion checklist will be applied to assess accessibility, meaningful participation, inclusive development, engagement of marginalised groups, representation in monitoring and evaluation activities.

4 - Needs

The action will address needs that will support the elimination of barriers to access to education. Those barriers include: poverty not allowing families to afford education costs, displacement, security due to distance to safe learning spaces/schools, child labour, unavailability of number of learning spaces, and disabilities hindering access to school and at school if buildings are not equipped to facilitate access. The number of children who are out of school are estimated to be at: Raqqa 35%; Deir ez-Zor 25%; Idleb 28%; Aleppo 26%. Enrolment in post primary grade levels falls to 23% in Idleb & Deir ez-Zor and 12% in Raqqa for preparatory year grades 6 to 9, and is estimated at 8% in Deir ez-Zor, 5% in Idleb, 1% in Raqqa for secondary grades. The average ratio of functioning classrooms to the school aged population is 1:54, but varies greatly among the six governorates: Idleb (1:78, with 53% of operating schools at two shifts while 8% up to four shifts) & Aleppo (1:62) have the highest ratios; Deir ez-Zor (1:52) (WoS Education sector findings 2022). Idleb is critical with nearly half the schools non-operational, low attendance rate, highest rate of school attacks, 45+mins traveling to school (WoS HNO Draft 2022). Approximately 84% of all school-aged children are living in areas with severe, extreme or catastrophic education conditions. These have spiraled by an additional 5% this year for Aleppo (97%), Idleb (99%) & Deir ez-Zor (84%) (WoS Education sector findings 2022).

5 - Implementation of activities

WCH and partners will provide 22,000 children with activities to ease access to education by rehabilitating classes, establishing temporary learning spaces, WASH facilities and school kits, and with appropriate learning programs as per children’s assessed needs. ALP will ensure out of school children (OOSC) receive the cycles necessary to support a sustainable reintegration using curriculum B. Remedial education programs, based on the formal curriculum in NWS and NES, will ensure retention as children recover gaps in their learning. Guided self-learning programs using the SLP curriculum will reconnect children to learning to ensure that no child is left behind and limit the OOSC numbers. Teachers visit children weekly to provide support, follow-up and assign learning materials. Cash for education can also be provided where schools are available but the economic conditions do not allow families to send their children to school. Children will take a placement test to assess their academic level and provide them with the program, grade level and support appropriate to build on and further their learning experience. Academic results will be measured at the end of every cycle, to assess the children’s progress. Within the teacher coaching program, coaches provide teachers with trainings, coaching support and space for shared learning for a sustainable professional development. Teachers will be trained on SEL. Class observation on a regular basis will support M&E and adaptation.

Protection risk: Harm or conflict through cash distributionLikelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation measures: Clear vulnerability and selection criteria, open communication to the community in advance of the action, strong and active feedback loops.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection risk: Sexual abuse, or attempt of sexual abuse of a childLikelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation measures: (1) Train partners to review or set anew a child safeguarding (CSG) policy for their respective organisations(2) WCH program team monitors partners and general media postings on activities and alerts child protection/safety focal point at WCH whenever there are concerns (directly discussed with partners and action plan agreed upon)(3) Staff of partners on the ground have one CSG focal point who reports to WCH any breach(4) Child safety concerns identified through internal monitoring and evaluation are discussed and followed up with partners For all field staff (including facilitators and teachers) and before activities begin, WCH will provide trainings on CSG/PSEA. Trainings will be provided on WCH Safeguarding policy, to ensure awareness, response and compliance across all partners. WCH has developed child-friendly PSEA materials that can be displayed in classrooms and CFS, as well as child-friendly identification and reporting mechanisms, to ensure the safety of children. These are constantly monitored through the project lifecycle by the Child Protection team at WCH, and regular training and updates are conducted with partner field staff and management teams. To avoid inefficient referrals that do not result in service provision, referral pathways will be mapped for all project locations and regularly updated throughout the lifecycle of the project.
