Project Summary

The HNO, estimated an expected 7.9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020 of whom the humanitarian partners will target 5.9 million of the most vulnerable people. Currently it has become evident that the COVID-19 pandemic will impact on these projections and lead to significant increase in numbers. Meanwhile, the capacity of the health system to address the possible impact of the pandemic, remains limited and or overstretched.
The COVID-19 outbreak has rapidly evolved in the WHO African Region, as reflected by the significant upsurge in the number of cases and the rapid geographical expansion of the disease. In Nigeria, the Federal Ministry of Health confirmed the first coronavirus disease (COVID-19) case in Lagos State on 27th February 2020 while the first case in Borno state was 19th April 2020. Nigeria has witnessed an exponential rise in the number of cases of COVID-19 since the detection of the first case in February in Lagos state. As on 28th April 2020, the number of confirmed cases was 1532 in Nigeria and 53 in Borno State.

The lack of adequate potable water and sanitation infrastructure coupled with extreme flooding has seen cholera and acute watery diarrheal disease cases rise every rainy season while the protracted conflict has kept IDPs in overcrowded camps and overstretched the little resources and infrastructure in host community. Based on the programme Criticality (PC) assessment, the tasks to be maintained during the COVID 19 response are linked to the Humanitarian Response Strategy 2019-2021 and with a specific focus on two strategic objectives 1 and 2: Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and protection interventions to the most vulnerable and enhanced timely, unhindered, and equitable access to multi-sector assistance and protection interventions through principled humanitarian action. SCI is implementing nutrition activities in Maiduguri, Mafa, Magumeri, Konduga, Kaga and Jere. For business continuity, SC has aligned all interventions to the Ministry of Health guidelines, Northeast Nigeria Nutrition Sector Programming Adaptations in the Context of COVID-19, which are based on the Global Nutrition Cluster guidelines and WHO guidelines.
SCI will complement and leverage already existing structures and systems established to provide services that are aligned to the current needs caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. SCI will continue to coordinate and collaborate with the UN agencies and the State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) to ensure operations are line with state response plan and strategy. These include washing of hands and basic hygiene practices, social distancing, restriction of big gatherings, meetings with limited number of participants, provision of PPE kits and trainings to front line workers, IPC measures in all implementing locations etc.

1 - Needs

North-Eastern Nigeria has seen chronic underdevelopment compounded by poverty and years of conflict and displacement, malnutrition, diarrheal and other diseases are endemic (malaria, measles, Lasa fever, Meningitis, Hep E). The lack of adequate potable water and sanitation infrastructure coupled with extreme flooding has seen cholera and acute watery diarrheal disease cases rise every rainy season while the protracted conflict has kept IDPs in overcrowded camps and overstretched the little resources and infrastructure in host community. Jere, MMC and Konduga have been flagged as very high risk LGAs due to overcrowded and congested IDP camps with specific needs such as; infection prevention and control in health facilities and communities (including IDP camps), risk communication, social mobilization and community engagement, psychosocial care . All these coupled with chronic food insecurity and displacements have contributed to the poor nutrition status in NE Nigeria. Though SC has been implementing nutrition projects in LGAs above, these were designed before the onset of COVID-19 and thus mitigation factors were not included. As it is, there is need to intensify the dissemination of information on COVID-19 infection protection and control in these communities, build the capacity of the health workers so that they can roll out the IPC measures at the facility level and in the communities, provide the PPEs to ensure that they are not exposed in line of duty and ensure that adaptations are in place to continue providing lifesaving services.

2 - Activities

1.Screening, Identification, and Referral of Children with Acute Malnutrition.•To adhere to social distancing directive, SC will suspend the mass MUAC screening and will instead will Intensify efforts to strengthen the capacity of mothers and caregivers through the ‘Mother MUAC’ approach to detect and monitor their children’s nutritional status using low-literacy/numeracy tools including Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) tapes. This will be conducted both at the OTP and the community. Training will be carried out in groups of fives while ensuring that social distancing is maintained. Each caregiver will practice with their child while receiving guidance from the community nutrition volunteers or the health workers. At the community, the CNMs will verify any case before they are referred to OTP for therapeutic treatment. To ensure that the MUAC tapes are not shared in the community, each caregiver will be given a MUAC tape and this follow up will be made to replenish based on need. Contacts of the caregivers will be taken to follow up on the progress and provide counselling whenever its needed.2.Management of acute malnutrition at the Outpatient Therapeutic centres and the stabilization centres•Continued provision of inpatient and outpatient treatment of acute malnutrition in both the stabilization centre and the outpatient Therapeutic program centres. All IPC strategies will be adhered to ensure that health workers, caregivers and beneficiaries are not exposed to any risks of COVID-19 transmission. PPEs will be provided to all health workers. SC has adapted a 2 weeks follow up •Strengthening remote monitoring of nutrition activities through community structures with existing partner EYN3. Identification, referral and treatment of children 0-59 months with acute malnutrition and suspected with COVID infection. •SC will Intensify screening of all persons visiting nutrition services sites, and refer suspected COVID cases to nearest testing center for confirmation. For the stabilization center, SC will restrict visitors to reduce the risk of transmission and will maintain the contacts of any visitors for easy contact tracing in the event that there is a suspected case in the stabilization center4. Infection, prevention and control (IPC) in nutrition service provision sites•Continued active sensitization to health and nutrition workers and community volunteers on self-protection, prevention, control and referrals in the context of COVID-19•Facilitate adherence to the recommended Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures in health/OTP centers and communities by providing PPE; disinfection of health facilities and surrounding households with chlorine solution by trained staff; and sanitizing the OTPs. Trained volunteers will be engaged in the community to disseminate key messages on IPC measures. To ensure that social distance is maintained megaphones will be used to amplify the sounds and increase the coverage. •Increase the hand washing stations in the OTPs and SC, and provide WASH kits to admissions, supported by WASH education for caregivers. 5. Risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) to inform decision making related to personal risk, mitigate rumors, share information and advice, and to effectively involve communities in the response to control the outbreak.•Nutrition essential supplies prepositioningSCI will preposition the necessary nutrition supplies to ensure that there is no stock out.
