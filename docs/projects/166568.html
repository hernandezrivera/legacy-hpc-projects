Project Summary

With the outbreak of COVID-19, migrants, mobile population, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and host communities bear a high risk of infection due to several factors and barriers, such as: lack or inadequate access to proper information or prevention, limitation or exclusion from accessing diagnosis and treatment services, crowded living conditions, and stigma and discrimination. This vulnerable groups of the population must be taken into consideration when planning for readiness and response operations for the COVID-19 outbreak. High density sites and communities are more vulnerable due to overcrowded living conditions and the risk of transmission in these communities and sites is high. Overall, there is still 43,352 individuals displaced due to cyclone Idai located in 1,571 locations, with the majority living in host communities within the two provinces (Manicaland and Masvingo). The residual effected of the cyclone Idai, has left 198 households still staying in temporary camps and yet to be resettled, the hardship facing many households in these areas means displacement remains a constant feature of the crisis. Displaced people are still living in tents, in host communities or have returned to homes of habitual residence but at staying in makeshift structures which are not suitable for human habitation. Majority often live in over-crowded conditions, in close proximity to others, with inadequate access to water and sanitation services. These conditions are ripe for the transmission of the virus. Informal settlements on the fringes of urban areas are of particular concern. Internally displaced people are also especially reliant on either humanitarian assistance or casual labour and any breaks to humanitarian supply pipelines or employment opportunities will hit these vulnerable groups hard. In addition, DTM assessments have shown these IDPs are in poor health, making them more vulnerable in the context of COVID-19. The main objectives of this intervention is to limit human-to-human transmission of the virus by providing assistance to the affected population through specific mitigation measures, mitigate the risk in overcrowded shelters, collective centres, tents, or any other shelters at risk, reduce secondary infections among close contacts, ensuring protection remains central to the response.

1 - Activities

- Support government to ensure continuation of services in existing camps and camps like settings, strengthening communication and advocating for inclusion and prevention of stigma on displaced population.-Conduct a rapid analysis in all high-density places and isolation centers to determine gaps and possible Shelter/NFI and CCCM support required and assess condition and the impact of a possible spread.- Provision of technical guidance and tools to ensure risk communication messages are tailored to the displaced population. - Promotion of risk communication and community engagement activities.- Building the capacity of health care workers and camp coordinators and other relevant actors on psychological first aid adapted for pandemics.- Mainstreaming good hygiene practices through the development and dissemination of information and education (IEC) materials tailored to the needs of IDPs and communities.- Strengthening community event-based surveillance, training community leaders to assess health condition in the camps and report any suspected symptoms, to local authorities.- Enhanced data collection on needs, gaps, risk and health conditions among the IDPs.-Support the adequate provision of WASH services in displacement settings and their alignment with context relevant IPC measures. - Support on the development of protocols for hand washing and waste disposal that are fit for purpose of the needs of IDPs and communities. - Promote hygiene in high density areas with non-food items for hand washing, developing behavioural change communication towards community members for improved hygiene practices.- Support the government in the refurbish of infrastructure and isolation facilities, close to camps and procurement of critical medicines, medical supplies, and Personal protective equipment to provide assistance to IDPs.- Support to supply chain management with shelter and non-food items (NFIs).- Ensure assessments of the barriers and the measures that are in place to guarantee safe and meaningful access to health services and information.- Support the delivery service of MHPSS specifically tailored for IDPs and populations affected or in quarantine, as well as deployment of psychosocial mobile teams linguistically and culturally able to serve those populations.
