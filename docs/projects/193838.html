Project Summary

In the West Bank (WB) and East Jerusalem, Palestinians face ongoing coercion and discrimination due to Israeli policies and practices, including restricted access to basic services and escalating levels of violence. In the WB, 1,021,561 persons are in need of multisectoral services (Protection Cluster Analysis Update August 2022), while the Multi Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2022 identified that 5% of households are using emergency coping strategies, 23% are using crisis coping strategies, and 11% of households are under stress. This situation has deleterious effects on psychosocial well-being, with 12% of households reporting at least one member with psychosocial distress. Girls and women also face GBV risks: 14% of households report risks of verbal harassment affecting girls and 9% of households affecting women, and 6% report risks of sexual harassment or violence for girls and 4% of households for women (MSNA2022). Overall, the oPt PAU identifies 367,000 persons in need of GBV services in the WB. Persons with disabilities (PWD) suffer a range of mental health problems, stigma and lack of accessible infrastructure and services. In WB, 10% of households have at least one member with a disability, of which 4% are children (MSNA 2022). Children with disabilities are vulnerable to protection concerns, with 44% of households reporting safety concerns. The Protection Cluster identifies MHPSS as a key need. The proposed project aligns with Objective 2 of the HRP: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources” and is based on findings of a Needs Assessment conducted by Terre des hommes (Tdh) in September/October 2022. The assessment revealed 13 localities with the highest MHPSS needs as reported by key informants' interviews, and indicated that women and girls were among the most affected, especially those with disabilities. The project aims to ensure Child Protection (CP) and GBV prevention and response mechanisms are accessible to communities in East Jerusalem (Sheikh Jarrah, Old City, Shufat camp), Hebron (H2 area and Dir Samet/Doura), Ramallah (Deir abu Mushal, and KufrNeimeh), Nablus (Burqa and Beita), Tubas (Tamoun and Ein Albeida) and Jenin (Faqoua and Silet Harthieyeh).The action will strengthen community-based protection mechanisms and build the capacities of CBOs to provide quality, inclusive CP, MHPSS and GBV services which are gender and age appropriate. It will equip communities to both establish and sustain community protection mechanisms, which is vital, especially when formal protection mechanisms are lacking. The project demonstrates a holistic approach through a consortium of partners and reflects good complementarity between partners’ areas of strength; Stars of Hope in disability mainstreaming, YWCA in vocational training and WCLAC in legal support alongside Tdh’s proven expertise in MHPSS, GBV and case management, and all organisations will build the skills and professional confidence of partners’ psychologists and CBO Social Workers on MHPSS structured support, the safe identification, case management and referral of CP and GBV cases, disability mainstreaming, safeguarding and PSEA. GBV survivors will be provided with income generating opportunities, vocational skills and legal counselling and representation as needed. Overall, 7622 persons will directly benefit from the project (1560 girls, 1560 boys, 3906 women, and 596 men). Partners and Tdh will work directly with beneficiaries for activity design and monitoring and they will coordinate with the relevant authorities, child protection actors, the Protection Cluster, GBV-sub-cluster, and CP-AoR

1 - Needs

In the West Bank (WB), including East Jerusalem (EJ), Palestinians continue to face a coercive environment due to Israeli policies and practices. Restrictions and discriminations applied limit the population’s access to basic services and to meet their basic needs. In August 2022, the Protection Cluster reported a total of 1,021,561 persons in need (PIN) in the West Bank, of which, 153,234 are persons with disabilities (Protection Analysis Update Summary, August 2022, Protection Cluster). Tdh has conducted a recent needs assessment (Sep-Oct 2022) in eighteen localities in West Bank and Jerusalem, with the purpose of understanding the MHPSS and GBV needs and available services for the vulnerable people, particularly children, women, and People with Disabilities (PWDs) affected by violence. The assessment covered localities in Jenin, Tubas, Nablus, Ramallah, East Jerusalem, and Hebron. These governorates have the highest incidents of violence, taking into consideration the recent situation especially in East Jerusalem, Shuffat Camp, Nablus and Jenin. Of the 18 localities, 16 were identified as being severely in need of assistance. The main concerns investigated were the availability of services, awareness levels related to services, and the issue of violence against women and children, within the family. The findings revealed that 18% (28.6% of females and 8.5% of males) of community members across the assessed areas believe that the prevalence of violence against women in the family is between very high and high. The majority of community members interviewed, 71.9% (73.8% of females and 70.2% males) believe that there is no prevalence of violence against men in the family. In contrast, 9% (11.9% of females and 6.4% of males) believe that there is a medium prevalence of this violence occurring. As violence against women is demonstrably more common than violence against men, the planned intervention will target more women GBV survivors than men. The needs assessment also highlighted the continuing existence of violence against children within both schools and the community in the areas assessed. 12.8% of respondents (18.2% of children and 9.4% of adults) believe that the prevalence of violence against children in neighborhood streets is very high. Moreover, 6.9% (11.8% of children and 3.8% of adults) believe that the prevalence of violence against children in schools is very high. This indicates that the awareness and perception of community members regarding violence against children needs to be addressed since they do not view such violence as an issue, while the children confirmed that they are exposed to high levels of violence. This finding is supported by findings from the OCHA MSNA 2022, which noted that 19.5% of households reported children feeling unsafe or very unsafe when travelling to or from school. Children with disabilities are also subject to violence in neighborhood streets and in schools. Based on the results of Tdh’s needs assessment, 33.3% of community members with disabilities believe that violence against children with disabilities in neighborhood streets is very high, but only 1.2% of community members (without disability) believe similarly; this shows a significant disparity between the realities of the situation for disabled children, and the perception of this reality by able bodied persons. 11.1% of community members with disabilities believe that violence against children with disabilities in schools is also very high. These numbers reflect the lack of awareness of community members without disability about the needs and risks faced by people with disability. However, community awareness - in terms of accessibility for disabled individuals – is higher, with 88.9% of households perceiving that children with disabilities (both mental and physical) face additional challenges in accessing education services (MSNA 2022). During the needs assessment, the respondents, including children, shared the major risks in each area and the services needed according to their perceptions. These risks are primarily conflict related violence, especially due to proximity of the separation wall, in addition to school dropout, drug use, smoking, lack of safe places for children to play, and violence against people in society. Based on this, respondents believe that MHPSS awareness raising, adaptation of places for Persons with Disabilities (PWDs), provision of safe spaces for children to play, awareness sessions for parents on child protection issues, and structured sessions for children and adults are the main services needed in the area. The full assessment report will be disseminated by end of November, with the detailed information per locality. The project has also been designed based on learning from the humanitarian fund project currently being implemented by Tdh and their partners, which responds to the needs of children and families through providing capacity building to enhance community-based protection mechanisms as well as providing MHPSS, Case management, and Awareness raising services. This project has a strong focus on providing support for persons with disability, with MHPSS project modules and CBOs’ locations being assessed and revised or adapted for the needs of persons with disability; the learning from this has been used particularly to inform the disability mainstreaming components of the proposed project.

Tdh ensures high quality and confidential case management. 9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

a - Generic email,b - Project hotline,c - Complaint box

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

During project implementation, Tdh will ensure that project beneficiaries’ feedback, suggestions and recommendations are encouraged and collected, to ensure activities remain relevant and beneficial. At the beginning of project implementation, a complaint and feedback mechanism will be explained to beneficiaries. Different way of making complaints, feedback and suggestions will be available for beneficiaries to utilise, such as complaint/feedback boxes in the locations where activities take place, a phone number, a Whatsapp number and an email address will be disseminated to the target population. This will ensure that all persons, regardless of ability/disability, have equal access to feedback mechanisms. Based on the beneficiaries’ feedback in the project design phase, adaptations will be made ensure it is child friendly, gender sensitive and inclusive. Complaints will be tracked and logged by a dedicated and trained focal point in a confidential manner, with each complaint being resolved before an update is provided to the complainee. All complaints will be handled efficiently and confidentially by Tdh accountability team, and lessons learned are generated regularly based on complaints received. Complaints and feedback channels are assessed on a periodic basis to remain relevant and accessible to the affected population. Beneficiaries, including children, will also be engaged in the action's regular monitoring through feedback sessions and project activities, as well as satisfaction surveys. Tdh has a comprehensive Child Safeguarding policy (CSP), PSEA policy, Code of Conduct and confidential mechanisms in place for reporting and response. Tdh staff, partner and volunteers will be trained on the CSP, PSEA and the Code of Conduct and will sign these policies. Periodical consultation meetings with beneficiaries will be organized to create a safe environment for them to express their feedback.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

As the project targets specifically children and adults at risk, directly affected by conflict related violence and/or affected by GBV, the safety and dignity of beneficiaries is a key priority. The project has been designed according to the Do No Harm framework, tailoring services according to identified needs of beneficiaries, and taking into account the context. The project will mitigation measures to ensure beneficiaries' safety. Specific methodologies within the service provision delivery have been integrated to avoid re-victimization of children and survivors, including capacity building, support activities and awareness to families, to community members and to child protection workers. All staff involved in the proposed project, including partner organization staff, will be trained on protection and gender mainstreaming as well as Tdh’s Child Safeguarding Package (CSP, PSEA, DSMW and GCC) to promote best practices and to ensure that no harm is resulting from Tdh’s activities and staff. Additionally, preventive and responsive protection mechanisms will be also applied following a multidimensional holistic approach. It will also include awareness-raising and community-based integration activities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In respect of equity, under this project, boys, girls, women and men will equally participate and benefit from the proposed activities according to their assessed needs. Tdh will also provide equal access to services to all identified children and survivors, including persons with disabilities but also taking into account the gender differences. The project will also target equally all people of concern without discrimination. The project will also use an AGD lens, to collect and analyze disaggregated data, and ensure that Tdh G&D Marker is implemented and is taken into account in the MEAL plans in compliance with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines. Gender balanced participation will be specifically monitored through M&E diversity, gender and age disaggregated tools and indicators in order to quickly identify possible participation and protection concerns related to activities and take consequent measures to promote safe and dignified access. Dedicated spaces and moments will allow free expression, participation and the possibility to provide feedbacks. The partner align with Tdh Gender and Disability policy that informs the program in all its stages together with the IASC guidelines on inclusion of persons with disabilities in Humanitarian Action. In accordance with Tdh Gender and disability policy, the proposed action sits at level three of the G&D scale, namely Gender and Diversity responsive. Specifically, the implemented approaches aims at proactively overcome existing biases, discriminations, barriers, taking into account different needs, opportunities, interests for the targeted population in the program design. The partners will ensure that systematic inclusive needs assessment and needs observation is done, taking into consideration the voice and needs of people with disabilities and people with specific needs. The capacity building package on child protection, safeguarding PSEA mainstreams inclusion to ensure appropriate capacities are developed within the targeted communities and among community-based workers. Feedback and complaints mechanisms are also designed to be inclusive across gender, age and diversity. Priority will be given to children, caregivers, survivors marginalized and discriminated, disabled in accessing services and participate meaningfully to the proposed activities. Activity locations will be assessed against inclusivity and accessibility standards and remedial actions will be taken to enhance compliance with Tdh policies and IASC guidelines.
