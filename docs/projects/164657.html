Project Summary

Although there are significant gaps in the emergency response, particularly in hard to reach areas, other areas have been free of conflict for some time. In many of these areas, households lost their productive assets and are currently reliant on emergency food assistance, despite having the capacity, experience, willingness and skills to develop their own businesses and increase their self-reliance. Providing livelihood assistance in the form of the restoration of productive assets lost due to the crisis offers a cost-efficient way to improve resiliency and self-reliance among vulnerable households beyond the life of the project. The INFUSE project targets a total of 950 men and women for small business training, followed by the provision of a livelihood restoration in-kind grant for 662 households. A total of 3,598 individuals are expected to benefit from the project.

4 - Needs

As the crisis in Syria enters its ninth year, the depth of its impact is increasingly evident in the lives of the 11.7 million individuals in need of humanitarian assistance including 5 million children, 0.5 million elderly , 6.2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), and approximately 1.1 million of those who are in hard to reach locations. Moreover, there are millions of Syrians who have fled to neighbouring countries in search of a safe haven. All those who have been displaced were forced to leave behind their homes, belongings, jobs, enterprises and productive assets, which severely curbed their ability to provide for themselves and their families. Besides livelihoods, all their other basic needs were affected by their displacement, including food security, education, health, WASH and shelter. These massive losses have also put displaced individuals in a vicious cycle of debt, which coupled with limited economic/employment opportunities, lead to a surge in extreme poverty (currently estimated at 83 percent). Rough living conditions as vulnerable populations exhausted their coping mechanisms, especially children, youth and adolescents. The increase in household indebtedness and poverty has forced many children and youth to drop out of school and work instead, leading to a surge in child labour and early marriages. Additionally, many women have had to bear the brunt of providing for their households, having lost their husbands, brothers and fathers to the war, which raised their livelihood needs. Similarly, many men suffered from the loss of their livelihood through loss of productive assets and jobs, including those who work in certain trades and crafts, such as bakers, barbers, carpenters, construction/electrical workers/mechanics, farmers etc. Targeted areas currently include Homs Rural Damascus.

5 - Implementation of activities

Those with previous experience in small business management but lost their productive assets due to the crisis will be identified and given the opportunity to participate in a small business management training course, ADRA will target 950 beneficiaries. Sessions of between 25-30 individuals using curriculum already developed by ADRA Syria that has been previously used in ADRA’s projects will be conducted that will ensure the inclusion of both men and women.ADRA staff will provide individual assistance in the development of a business plan and then participants will produce a final business plan for their proposed ideas. A committee will be formed and 662 proposals with are most likely to succeed based. Priority will be given to female headed households, households with a disabled / elderly family member, households with infant and young children and large households of IDPs, returnees and host communities. ADRA will ensure that both men and women have the opportunity to participate in businesses of their own choosing. Targeted beneficiaries will receive productive assets (equipment, tools and seeds; etc..). The grant size will be on average the equivalent of $1,150. ADRA Syria will has already consulted with beneficiaries for the development of the training materials, and the best ways to undertake the distribution, ensuring accessibility, dignity and choice. Monitoring will continue through the life of the project through regular visits, as well as receiving feedback from the complaints mechanism. ADRA staff and stakeholders will implement and monitor the project, taking into consideration the needs of the targeted community including men and women and modifying the project as necessary based on the feedback provided by the community. Feedback could be received by ADRA staff and volunteers which then will be passed to management, or through the complaint hotline that will be provided to participants and the community.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

ADRA Syria has embedded a conflict-sensitive ‘Do No Harm’ approach into its project design for beneficiaries and staff. The target groups (female-headed households, people with disabilities, the elderly, HHs with critically ill members or children under 5, PLW and IDP, returnee and host community households with limited or no household income) are highly vulnerable to protection risks, mobility challenges and GBV. ADRA acknowledges that some risks withing the community may arise as a result of this project, such as beneficiaries facing hostility, discrimination and jealousy from other members who are not part of this project. Considering the length of the crisis household debt has increased and purchasing power has significantly diminished, which may result in low returns for the newly established businesses resulting in a tardy takeoff for these businesses. Supporting women in the community means that there is a risk of them facing GBV by members of their household.ADRA will provide a hotline so that community members can provide feedback either anonymously or by identifying themselves. Beneficiaries can also directly give feedback to field staff located in the targeted communities. ADRA remains open to consultation with local communities throughout the life of the project.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

* Ability to reach targeted individuals with the most vulnerability, especially in highly displaced communities where individuals frequently move from one place to the other. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: working closely and partnering with local governorates in local partners and communities to ensure that the needs assessments are conclude the most recent information and data on the communities of concern regarding vulnerable populations, including IDPs, returnees and host community members.* Ability to locate appropriate venues to conduct the business trainings. Likelihood: Low; Impact: Medium Mitigation measures: ensuring to partner with relevant stakeholders and local partners who can facilitate the trainings by either offering the site or helping procure one. ADRA Syria will work in close collaboration with partners, local authorities and community leaders, to overcome any obstacles that might arise and make sure that the target venues are in safe locations for men and women, and accessible to people with disabilities and elderly people. * Heightened tensions can escalate between community members when distributing the productive assets (tool kits and materials etc.) Likelihood: Low; Impact: High Mitigation measures: community leaders will be oriented regarding eligibility for the project and the agreed selection criteria pre-distribution. Project activities will be implemented using the Do No Harm humanitarian principle to mitigate any unintended consequences.
