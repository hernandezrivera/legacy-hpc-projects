Project Summary

Objective 1: Strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency, micronutrient interventions and optimal maternal nutrition.

Needs

Malnutrition (undernutrition) remains the major problem in Somalia with high levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) well above emergency threshold of 15%. During 2016 and 2017, thehumanitarian situation in Somalia continued to deteriorate because of the poor rains in Gu and Deyrseasons of 2016 and 2017, causing widespread food insecurity, deepening health, nutritional and waterrelated issues further exacerbated by large-scale movement of people. In 2018, the overall food security situation in Somalia has improved. Most recent Post Gu 2018 assessment of the FSNAU 2018 indicated that 4.6 million people are acutely food insecure (IPC phase 2 or worse). This improvement is due to the above average performance of the April to June Gu rains. The results indicate that most pastoralist areas have seen an improvement in pasture, water availability and improved livestokc productivity. However, despite the positive outlook and gains made, humanitarian assistance needs to be sustained to support the most of vulnerable Somalis. Most importantly these gains have not translated into improvement in the nutrition situation and categorically the nutrition situation remains of serious concern. Recent nutrition surveys across the country indicate a slight improvement in few areas and poor nutrition situation in most parts of the country. The trends in GAM prevalence (WHZ) across Somalia remain at serious levels when compared to last six months back. Furthermore, this is evidenced by the high admissions in nutrition sites/centers over the last six months as compared to the same period in the previous years.

Activities

• Train community nutrition workers and health facility staff on providing IYCF and NHHP counseling and messaging to beneficiaries• Counsel pregnant and lactating women on appropriate IYCF techniques• Provide boys and girls aged 6-59 months with MNPs and Vitamin A supplements• Provide pregnant and lactating women micronutrients including iron folate• Screen PLW for acute malnutrition• Screen children aged 6-59 months for acute malnutrition• Treat children 6-59 months with severe acute malnutrition• Integrate nutrition interventions into schools for a comprehensive nutrition package
