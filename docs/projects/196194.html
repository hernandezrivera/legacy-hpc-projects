Project Summary

This Shelter and NFI project will target the most vulnerable households from IDPS, returnees and host communities in the targeted project locations. The project will be implemented and integrated where possible with the relevant clusters such as WASH, CCM and protection cluster in order to achieve maximum benefit to people affected by conflict and natural disasters. Utilizing approved standard tools and policies, all activities will be conducted to ensure there is no harm to affected communities. Most project activities will be implemented in remote and hard to reach areas and CH will utilize the available resources to efficiently deliver the project activities.
In this response, Coalition would like to integrate in kind support for new shelters and repairs/rehabilitation of destroyed shelters for returnees, hosts and people living in camp like setting. It will also pilot on cash-based modality where there are basic functional markets. Coalition for Humanity will conduct Rapid market assessments and monthly price monitoring to determine appropriate and specific CVA modality. CH will undertake due diligence and make referrals to mitigate cases, and support either as standalone or integrated training on HLP access rights for returnees, IDPs and host community mostly women in collaboration with relevant authorities. Beneficiaries will be supported on mechanisms around dispute resolution, legal assistance, and land right among other issues. With involvement of other cooperating partners such as IOM, NRC, UNHCR and relevant government authorities at state and national level, beneficiaries will be provided with general information about land rights, ownership as well as clarity on tenure arrangements.
A total case load of 72,402 people will be reached through this intervention. The figures are broken down to 18,100 women, 15,102men, girls 20,100 and 19,100 boys across the targeted locations.
The project will provide IDPS, host communities and returnees with shelters and NFI both for new displacement as well as old to refurbish existing structures to meet basic dignified shelter needs.
The project will enhance market functionality by using mixed intervention of cash and in-kind support to people most in need where there will be basic market structures and functionality.
This shelter and NFI project is designed in line with the HRP 2023, humanitarian need overview (HNO) that shows serious gaps. CVA modality will cover 20% while in-kind response will be 80% based on market functionality in the project location

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to face perennial challenges as a result of violent conflicts and natural disasters such as floods. In 2022, approximately 2.4 million people in South Sudan continue to be impacted by the destruction of housing and loss of essential goods caused by ongoing flooding and conflict. This is expected to continue to 2023. In addition, nearly 329,000 refugees and asylum seekers also require shelter and non-food item (NFI) support. At the end of 2021, nearly 1.9 million men, women, boys, girls, and older people live in inadequate shelter, while a total of 2.3 million required NFIs according to OCHA. The number of people in need increased in 2022 due to the increased displacement of approximately 300,000 people in 2021 and the returns of nearly 270,000 from neighboring countries and the situation is expected to worsen in 2023. The top three shelter/NFI-related needs are access to emergency shelter, NFIs and housing, and land and property (HLP) rights to ensure security of tenure. Findings from the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021 indicated that among host communities, IDPs and returnees access to shelters was impaired due to destruction of shelters by conflict and flooding. Affected people are in need of emergency shelter and NFI support in 2023 and this include displaced people, returnees and five per cent (5%) of host communities. Three years of catastrophic flooding has damaged shelters and destroyed household goods even for non-displaced communities who also face challenges in hosting their displaced neighbours. In 32 counties, more than half of the affected people requires emergency shelter support, particularly in Jonglei, Lakes, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity and Upper Nile states, areas with the highest concentration of displaced people. Affected people urgently need access to emergency shelter and NFI materials to prevent further deterioration of their living conditions and of resilience against recurring hazards. The needs of people vary depending on their location, for example, displaced people in IDP sites rely on humanitarian materials while those residing out of managed sites are assumed to have more opportunities to collect and procure materials locally. However, the continued displacement and increased movement of people, combined with sustained flooding, have also further depleted natural resources such as timber and grasses. Increased competition among people in need for local shelter materials will also generate conflict and localized violence as well as increased protection risks as women and girls venture further afield to collect increasingly scarce materials in 2023. Furthermore, continued challenges related to transportation of materials and limited support for local markets have left even areas with functional markets lacking essential shelter materials and NFIs. This presents particular challenges to displaced people who have faced multiple displacements as their resources likely have been depleted and they face competition for items with local communities. In addition to the destruction of shelter and NFIs, three consecutive years of flooding have led to an increase in longer-term displacement and relocation of communities from areas that have been completely inundated and are prone to flooding in the rainy season. This will exacerbate existing issues related to security of tenure among communities and may drive non-displaced people or those who do not rely on humanitarian shelter and NFI support to seek managed sites to access resources. This decreases the resilience and coping mechanisms of affected people and residents of formal sites continue to remain extremely dependent on humanitarian support to meet all basic needs. Coalition for Humanity will support shelter reconstruction and rehabilitation as well as replacement of essential NFI through CVA modality which will cover 20% of needs of the target population and in-kind response which will cover 80% of the population

2 - Project activities

The project key activities are in line with strategic and sectoral objectives of HRP 2023. Activities include both in-kind SNFI support and CVA modality where feasible. To improve cash-based delivery modality, Coalition for Humanity will conduct Rapid market assessments and monthly price monitoring to determine appropriate and specific CVA modalities. CH will undertake due diligence and make referrals to mitigate cases, and support either as standalone or integrated training on HLP, access rights for returnees, IDPs and host community mostly women in collaboration with relevant authorities. Beneficiaries will be supported with mechanisms around dispute resolution, legal assistance and land rights among other issues. With involvement of other cooperating partner such as IOM,NRC,UNHCR and relevant government authorities at state and national level, beneficiaries will be provided with general information about land rights, ownership and provide clarity on tenure arrangements. The activities will be aligned to strategic and sectoral objectives aiming to respond to the needs of newly displaced persons and populations with new vulnerabilities with emergency shelter and non-food items. The project activities will help improve the living conditions of protracted IDPs and Host Communities living in formal and informal settlements. The project will support the most vulnerable returnees and host communities with durable solutions that rebuild lives through shelter and NFI solutions. It will also support recovery, resilience, building and access to land, housing and property of returning populations to achieve durable solutions as well as participation of displaced persons to enhance the community’s ability to respond to new shocks, build resilience and foster co-existence.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Coalition for Humanity through its static presence in the project locations has frequently been monitoring market functionality. CH has also gathered information from other partners including information from the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) spear headed by REACH to help analyze feasibility of the CVA to the target beneficiaries. Availability and prices of SNFI among other items have been monitored in the markets across the project catchment area. The project will recommend in-kind response where the markets are not functional

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and Voucher assistance (CVA) will be essential as part of this project. Given the current perennial flooding in the entire South Sudan and specifically in the target project locations, cash-based programs are likely to have lower transport and logistics costs. The transfer is also likely to positively impact and/or boost markets and local economies especially after the long exposure of the country to conflicts. Finally, the cash provides the flexibility to move beyond a standard kit/ ration that meets the average need of the average household.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Local staff are leading the process of collecting the feedback from the beneficiaries especially individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are illiterate, women and girls. Most of the meetings and consultations are delivered in local languages hence helping members to effectively contribute. We also encourage beneficiaries who have skills in different languages(Nuer, Dinka, Arabic) to provide it as they know it and if need be get our translators to interpret for us the content they shared. This helps in ensuring nobody is left behind in our project intervention.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the beginning before project role out, a number of inception meetings will be carried out. These consultative and engagement meetings with beneficiaries will help further explain the nature of support to the beneficiaries and obtain their feedback on how such support will be delivered to them. The inceptions and other consultative meetings will build on earlier engagements (pre project design engagements) done with members of the community while this proposal was being prepared. Setting up complaint mechanisms and referral channels during and after the project are some of the important tasks to be carried out. Effective community mobilization meetings and Focus group discussions, safety audits with different groups mostly women and girls will be done to seek their views on safety of distribution points.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Coalition for Humanity has Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy in place. All staff members including consultants and volunteers sign it before engagement. The organization protection focal points in the field locations organize standalone sessions on PSEA. PSEA discussions are also incorporated in meetings and trainings that are conducted from time to time. PSEA is therefore an ongoing process and is one of the important aspects of our project implementation

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback gathered from the beneficiaries during inceptions meetings, monthly and complaints feedback boxes placed in strategic locations are collected, analyzed and the feedback used to improve and adapt the project implementation strategy. An action on feedback from the beneficiaries is incorporated into the project implementation matrix
