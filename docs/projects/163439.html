Project Summary

This project is to scale up UN Women ongoing humanitarian intervention in the SW-NW and aims to improve the protection of women and girls affected by the conflict through the enhanced capacity of protection actors, the establishment of protection services and a wider educational program targeting communities in the South west and North west regions of Cameroon. The main objective will be achieved through three outputs:
1. Enhanced national actors' capacities for the protection of women and girls affected by the crisis and gender mainstreaming in Humanitarian response; The capacity development for national actors mainly the police and the gendarmerie in two regions is essential to put adequate protection measures in place. This output will focus on the improvement on the knowledge and skills of the protection actors on the rights of women and girls affected by the conflict and gender mainstreaming.
2. Women and girls affected by the conflict access protection services through gender desks, safe spaces at the MINPROFF, gender desk at police and gendarmerie in SW-NW. Based on the concluding previous experience under the CERF Grant, and the Italian government support, the country office will work with the national police and gendarmerie to scale up the activities of four (4) gender desks already established. A referral pathway will be reinforced with other protection actors like the medical staff and the judiciary.
3. Increased economic support to GBV survivors, public awareness and social mobilization against Sexual gender-based violence. Community based activities and awareness are foundational to accompany the provision of SGBV services so that communities (including religious and traditional leaders) see the value of necessary services being provided and support access for those who need them. This also contributes to prevention of new cases.

Besoins / Needs

Cameroon has been experiencing a socio-political crisis, affecting the North West and South West Regions. The conflict has been ongoing since 2016, causing many deaths of civilian and armed forces, large internally and externally displaced population and a huge slowdown of the economic activities. OCHA reported More than 530,000 people have been internally displaced in the two regions, as well as in the Littoral and West Regions, as of the end of April 2019. While there was some political momentum in May to resolve the conflict, high levels of insecurity persisted, and the humanitarian response continues to be in the emergency phase. Protection remains the key humanitarian concern. Clashes continue, and civilians are at risk of being caught in crossfire. Armed actors have continued to burn houses in several localities, especially in the NW, leaving many displaced and homeless. Human rights violations persist. There are indications that sexual violence, with a disproportionate impact on women and girls, is underreported. The ongoing UN Women intervention focusing on protection of women and girls affected by the crisis shows that gap major gaps are: Access to remote areas: Humanitarian access has been a significant challenge limiting the ability of organisations to deliver a scaled-up emergency response that is comprehensive and effective. Protection needs of women and girls: are huge but difficult to be documented as people are afraid to talk. People are very concerned about their own security and often traumatized by violence they have experienced. Random killings and harassment are serious threats for IDPs in the bush. Many therefore stay inside the village, rather than going out.  Referral pathways (GBV service providers): the referral and counter referral are very weak and not sufficiently functional. There is a need to reinforce the referral pathways including documentation of GBV cases, GBV case management. Capacity building of local actors: local civil society organizations have been at the forefront of the response, providing emergency relief to families forced to flee their homes (towns and villages). These actors are trusted within the communities and are the key link in building trust, negotiating access and delivering the response. This project will be achieved through three outputs:Output 1: Enhanced national actors' capacity for the protection of women and girls affected by the crisis and gender mainstreamingIndicators: a) Number of police forces with enhanced capacities in the promotion and protection of women and girls’ rights in conflict situation, B:250 T: 500b) Number of Humanitarian actors’ capacity building on gender mainstreaming, B:30 T:50 Activities  Produce and disseminate communication tools for 1500 security forces  Provide technical expertise for the project coordination (Field coordinator, GBV expert, Women Economic empowerment Expert ) Output 2: Women and girls affected by the conflict access protection services through gender desks at the police and gendarmerie and safe spaces in SW-NW regions Indicator: a) Number of women and girls’ GBV survivors reported by the community and supported by the gender desks/safe space and referred to other specialized services (health, legal) B: 600 T:1200 b) Number of Gender Desk supported and functional B: 3 T:6 Output 3: Increased economic support to GBV survivors, public awareness and social mobilization against Sexual gender based violenceIndicator: a) Number of IDPS Host community exposed to GBV prevention activities B:46.000 T:60.000 b) Number of cases reported by the community to protection actors including the documentation of those cases: B:150 T:500 c) Number of quick impact projects set up to economically support GBV survivors and group of women; B:30 Target: 50
