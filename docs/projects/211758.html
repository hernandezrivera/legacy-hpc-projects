Project Summary

The school meals programme (SMP) aims to restore and stabilize access to education and enhance the health and nutrition status of children by addressing short-term hunger and improving intake of essential macro and micronutrients. Simultaneously, SMP contributes to protection of children from reverting to negative coping mechanism ( child labour, early marriage), as a consequence of financial constraints. The programme meets the sector’s 1st objective “ Saves lives: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable .“
SMP targets boys and girls including those living with disabilities:
- In kind daily distribution of fresh meals or fortified datebars to children in formal education (pre&primary) and to children enrolled in non-formal education (NFE) supported by UNICEF.
- Provision of electronic vouchers to out of school students enrolled in curriculum B or NFE.
SMP is implemented in coordination with MOE,NGOS, INGOS and UN. Considering the elevated needs and limited resources, the project will focus on areas with low food security and education indicators(Severity >=3).
SMP supported 839,362 children over 2023, and is planning to reach 780,000 children monthly in 2024.
Needs
The education system in Syria faces compounded challenges due to prolonged conflict, economic instability, and the recent earthquake, impacting infrastructure and disrupting learning. WFP's monitoring data from 2023, indicates a consistent level of enrolment and retention rates compared with 2022, while a noticeable decrease in attendance from 97% in 2022 to 87% in 2023 was noted. Concurrently, anecdotal data gathered from beneficiary interviews indicated a rise in incidences of child labour, with 21% of households withdrawing their children from school in Q1 2023, marking a 5% increase from Q4 2022. A 2022 national assessment, led by MoH, of the nutritional status of school-aged children revealed elevated prevalence of malnutrition. The findings indicated that 40% of children were skipping breakfast and 80% lacked dietary diversity. Additionally, one in five children were identified to suffer from iron deficiency anemia, with certain regions facing even higher rates. As addressing these challenges remain vital, noting the long-term repercussions of malnutrition and inadequate education, school meals play an instrumental role in providing essential nutrients key for learning and development, while also alleviating economic burdens on families.
Implementation process:
1- Distribution of Fortified Date Bars :
Targets children in schools and NFE in prioritized sub-districts in the 3 WOS hubs.
•WFP dispatches monthly shipments of fortified date bars to target schools/learning centers.
•Daily distribution of 1 pack (80 gr) of fortified date bars per child .
2- Distribution of Fresh Meals :
Targets primary schools children in selected sub-districts in Aleppo, Rural Damascus, and Deir Ez Zour. Targeted locations are identified based on education and food insecurity vulnerability and feasibility.
•Sandwiches are prepared in kitchens run by WFP partners, with fresh material sourced out from local markets. The project aids in creating employment and enhancing livelihoods of specifically vulnerable women within the community.
•Fresh meals are prepared and distributed daily to children in targeted schools.
3-Electronic Vouchers (Cash Transfer):
targets OoSC, encouraging them to re-enter the educational system either through CB or NFE, providing incentive to children, and families, to pursue their education and to safeguard children from dropping out again.
•the transfers (e-vouchers) are conditional to achieving 80% monthly attendance rate, and are redeemed at designated retailers, allowing beneficiaries to purchase essential food items.
Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) are conducted by WFP FM utilizing a comprehensive array of data collection tools, post-distribution surveys, and engaging focus group discussions.
