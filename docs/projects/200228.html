Project Summary

Solidarites International is responding since 10 years to the relevant needs of the population in Lebanon with close collaboration with the water sector, the leading ministries, the local authorities, water establishment and off course the affected population.
The recent development in the county context, following the economic crisis and devaluation of the local currency pushed SI to review its country strategy and adapt to respond to the emerging needs related to access to basic services including water, sanitation, and health.
SI, and through generous funding from OCHA, was able to tackle the drastic decrease in water supply in the most vulnerable areas in Lebanon (i.e Baalbek and Akkar) by sustaining the water source through emergency repairs and solarization, and given its active presence in the field in Akkar, in partnership with UNICEF, was able to actively respond to the Cholera Crisis that outbroke in October 2022 and channel additional funding form OCHA and the French Government to enhance the preparedness of the water and health functions in the high risk areas (Hospitals, PHCs, Water Stations, etc).
SI, and in close coordination with the sector, will tackle the emerging needs in sustaining water supply to all affected populations within its area of coverage, by coverage the already assessed needs through the sector vulnerability mapping and any additional emerging needs in the upcoming months, aiming to meet at least the minimum standards in terms of water quantity (i.e. 30lpcd) with cautious attention to the quality.
SI, will implement health-based approach in responding to cases of water borne diseases (Cholera and Diarrheal Diseases, Hepatitis) or vector borne diseases, through the implementation of wide awareness campaigns and distribution of Hygiene and Disinfection Kits to the communities in concern, and in collaboration with active Health organizations.
SI, through its expertise in the field of sanitation, will work to reduce relying on wastewater trucking, through the installation of decentralized wastewater units in locations that lack access to sewage network and treatment. SI will base this intervention on the agreed design and implementation modality as the ongoing Sanitation Action Plan of the Sector and its contribution to the WWT component of EU-HawKaMaa consortium.
Si, will ensure that do-no-harm approach is applied throughout the project, and the integration of protection, gender and disabilities consideration in all its field activities. SI, will ensure that all staff and contractors are abiding the code of conduct and the relevant protection policies. SI will give special attention to the gender inclusion throughout the project cycle management and will grant all beneficiaries a safe and easy access to feedback and complaint mechanisms.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Stand-alone protection is not a mandate of SI in Lebanon. Yet, significant efforts have been conducted to mainstream protection into all programming. All key principles of protection mainstreaming are embedded into the objectives and modality of the intervention. Indeed, the project will have a particular focus on 1) Ensuring safety and dignity in access and delivery of services, through enabling discrete channels for safe reporting of complaints, GBV incidents and SEA allegations; 2) Access to services, not only by providing the targeted population with emergency assistance but also through availability of information about humanitarian actors and assistance, eligibility criteria and modality of service delivery; 3) Participation and empowerment, by giving communities channels to voice their needs and priorities; and 4) Accountability and FCRM through effective and timely two-ways communication channels. 3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

SI is committed to providing assistance which is appropriate and relevant to the needs of the communities, and understands AAP as ensuring meaningful engagement and working with communities, to actively involve them in driving SI’s strategy and objectives. Establishing channels for beneficiaries to receive information and sharing feedback and complaints is one of the key commitments of accountability in humanitarian response.Key principles of AAP will be strongly embedded into the project implementation. In particular: Do no harm and context analysis: Several stakeholders and community members will be consulted to inform about the project, to assess needs of different groups and ensure coordination of humanitarian efforts and avoid duplication of activities. FCRM and PSEA: Community members will be informed about all FCRM channels during the vulnerability assessment, to make sure that all groups are aware of how to submit feedback or complaints in a safe and confidential manner.
