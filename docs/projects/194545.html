Project Summary

The goal of the project is to contribute to the reduction in health-related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high quality Lifesaving emergency health interventions for the most vulnerable populations in Duk County.
The overall objective is to maintain and sustain the existing basic essential emergency health services with improved emergency referral services for communities in Duk County with a focus on women, children & IDPs/returnees including people with disabilities. This project will provide support to host communities, Internally Displaced persons and returnee communities in underserved areas affected by the long period of flooding that has destroyed all the basic infrastructures in Duk County.
From the Health facility data, there is low coverage of antenatal care (ANC) first and fourth visits as well as deliveries by skilled birth attendants (SBA) are extremely lowest with ANC 1 at 30% and ANC 4 at 28% while skilled delivery is less than 26%, since lacks permanent staffed with skilled birth attendants. The unmet need for family planning remains high with alarming Child Health indicators associated with low immunization coverage which is extremely very poor with Penta3 coverage of only 32% (Facility register 2021). There is high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (60%), diarrhea (20%) and pneumonia (5%) in U 5 years which have a direct negative impact on women and young children. These statistics requires urgent scaling up of the emergency health intervention.
This project targets 15,000 (M= 7,200, F= 7,800) vulnerable population in Duk County (PIN 2022) of which 500(M=200, F=300 are persons who will receive MHPSS services, 500 (M=200, F=300) SGBV survivors will receive CMR services and 750 (M= 300, F=450) persons with disability will receive rehabilitation services. In addition, ,3000(M= 1,400, F=1,600) boys and girls between 6 months – 15 years will receive measles vaccination, 600 (M= 250, F=350) under 1 will receive PENTA3 and 600 deliveries will be assisted by a skilled birth attendant
These targets are focusing on the vulnerable people among Internally Displaced Persons, returnees and host communities with limited access to health care services.
This program is relevant in addressing problems identified by strengthening provision of essential health services with particular focus on maternal and child health care both preventive and curative services to the most vulnerable group of the rural communities supported by prepositioning of essential drugs, medical equipment & supplies to all the targeted health facilities as well as strengthening health facilities and capacity of health staffs to respond to quality health services in Duk County.
The package of services to be supported includes Child health services, Maternal and neonatal health services, Basic emergency obstetric and newborn care, Comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care, Mental health services, gender-based violence services, referral Distribution of essential medicines and supplies, Emergency preparedness and response, Disease surveillance and outbreak response, Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) /WASH services and supervision.
These frontline interventions will be supported in specific areas with the roll-out of community-based health services to bolster community resilience and basic services provision. This combined with emergency preparedness and response will ensure service continuity.
This project will allow JDF to complement on the health services gaps not supported by WB/UNICEF. JDF partner with Mary Help association from Western Bahr el Ghazal for assorted equipment supplies as part of in kind donation
The project will support 3 activities that include PHCC PHCUs and mobile units supported with all the clinical packages.

1 - Needs

The South Sudan has continued to suffer some of the worst health indicators globally with <5 mortality rate of 96 deaths per 1,000 live births and with 75% of child deaths due to the preventable diseases of diarrhea, malaria, and pneumonia. In addition, the maternal mortality rate is 789 per 100,000 live births with less than 8% of deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants. It will take many years to build a primary care health system that reaches the majority of children. The survey further showed that only 44% of the population is within walking distance of a functional health facility (HSDP 2012-2016). The county is rated IPC 4 Emergency and it is among the ten priority counties that were severely affected by flooding, intercommunal conflict, the economic crisis and COVID-19 that interrupted provision of basic life-saving primary health care services due to destruction of basic social services. The county is inhabited by an extremely vulnerable and traumatized community majority being women, girls, boys, and elderly people and people with disabilities who are physically and psychologically exposed to a multitude of stressors. Access to basic services in the area is in many cases non-existent with population who are subjected to protracted conflict, poor economic conditions and disruption of livelihoods. In addition, demand for basic health services is high and 40% of population does not have access to health care with only a fraction of the population having access to health care. In spites of the implementation of World Bank Project by different partners, the County health situation is fragile with extremely low health care coverage, high infant mortality and under five mortality and morbidity rates. The high poverty and long history of conflict has left an already very basic health care system extremely weakened, with severe shortages of health workers and vandalized health facilities leaving vulnerable communities with limited access to health services in addition to the huge influx of Internally Displace persons and returnees. The county primary health care system has many challenges, such as insufficient qualified staff, poor infrastructure, inadequate health facilities, lack of drugs and medical supplies despite of significant efforts made by the NGOs. From the Health facility data, there is low coverage of antenatal care (ANC) first and fourth visits as well as deliveries by skilled birth attendants (SBA) are extremely lowest with ANC 1 at 30% and ANC 4 at 28% while skilled delivery is less than 26%, since lacks permanent staffed with skilled birth attendants. 2 - Project activities

1. Conducting Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities. 2. Provision of IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities 3. Strengthening community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives and ICCM 4. Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. 5. Providing technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population6. Conducting community consultative meetings within the community regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In order to keep track of the project outputs and make sure that the project ismeeting its intended goals, JDF has in place monitoring systems using aparticipatory monitoring and evaluation approach between JDF and thebeneficiary communities, households and Government to foster a greater senseof ownership. Monitoring tools will be used to ensure that documentation andreporting is timely and representative throughout the life of the project. Once theproject is approved, the Quality Assurance team, the Project Manager and thesector advisor will jointly complete the Monitoring and evaluation framework,and will identify tools to be used for collecting data for each of the respectiveindicators. The data sources, roles and responsibility and frequency of datacollection will also be established. John Dau foundation will put in place the accountability to the affected population , this will be done through continuous meeting the beneficiaries, CHD ,community leaders and the local authority to make sure services intended for them are recognized and implemented.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

JDF has ensured that the PSEA Focal Person is known to all staff and beneficiaries and that contacts are made widely shared. Additionally, JDF provides awareness-raising sessions on sexual exploitation and abuse for all staff and beneficiaries and other personnel in JDF on a regular basis. The Human Resource department makes appropriate recommendations to management on enhancing PSEA prevention efforts thus thus confidentiality for whistleblower is kept however, the whistleblower can be transfer somewhere else if he/she feels threatened. The HR department will follow the procedures based on our HR. manual either through investigation with a formed committee or if the case need court intervention then the plaintiff will be allowed to follow a legal procedures in a rightful way with protection in place.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project has developed feedback mechanism by providing informationthrough preferred means on entitlements of project beneficiaries, treatmentguidelines, and partners and how to give feedback and/or complaints.Complaints and feedback mechanisms will be established in the community aswell as at health facilities. The project will keep feedback and complaintregisters which will be reviewed on a fortnightly basis and the issues raised areaddressed
