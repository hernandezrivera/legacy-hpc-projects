Project Summary

This project aims to significantly improve water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) conditions in Rohingya camps and host community locations, particularly addressing the challenge of safe drinking water access. This project is a co-funding initiative with UNICEF's regular fund where CARE is UNICEF's partner for LTA WASH project implementation in camps 15 and 16. CARE plans to establish a water distribution network in these communities to enhance water accessibility. The organization also emphasizes sanitation, providing technical support for decentralized desludging in densely populated camps and implementing mechanized sludge extraction to protect public health and the environment. Additionally, CARE will construct and maintain high-quality, safe, and secure latrines and bathing cubicles.
The project is dedicated to ensuring safe and accessible sanitation facilities for all community members, incorporating gender-sensitive designs to reduce gender-based violence risks. This includes creating women-friendly spaces and conducting educational sessions to increase community awareness. Measures such as gender markers and enhanced menstrual hygiene management facilities, featuring private washing areas and disposal units, are integral to this initiative.
Another crucial element is strengthening WASH committees by enhancing their skills in maintenance, water safety, and facility management, and training community leaders to disseminate effective messages and promote behavior change. In response to COVID-19, particularly in camps 15 and 16, CARE is leveraging its network for rapid action, focusing on vulnerable groups and adhering to Ministry of Health guidelines. This holistic approach aims to build community resilience, curb COVID-19 spread, and prevent diseases like acute watery diarrhea. CARE emphasizes community engagement, organizing local-level activities to heighten health and hygiene awareness and encourage participatory efforts.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The primary challenge in many households is accessing adequate sanitation, with a heavy dependence on communal facilities. Limited land availability for latrine construction in camps and the poor condition of existing latrines, which were built during the initial influx and now require significant repair, are major concerns. A WASH sector analysis indicates that, although overall latrine coverage conforms to the standard ratio of 1:20, achieving this ratio is difficult in at least 13 of 33 camps, including camp 15, due to high population densities. Many latrines, constructed in early 2018, are in dire need of major rehabilitation or reconstruction.The J-MSNA report from November 2023 reveals key statistics about water and sanitation in the camps. In terms of water supply, 59% of households in Camp 15 and 77% in Camp 16 primarily rely on tap stands for drinking water. Additionally, a high proportion of households in both camps, 88% in Camp 15 and 92% in Camp 16 reported having sufficient water for their needs. The average daily water requirement per person is 21 liters in Camp 15 and 20 liters in Camp 16.Regarding safety and sanitation, 59% of women in camp 15 and 71% in camp 16 feel secure using communal latrines at night. However, only a small percentage of households, 1% in camp 15 and 4% in camp 16, have access to private latrines. Around 81% of households in Camp 15 and 85% in Camp 16 practice waste segregation at home. The distribution of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits shows a disparity between the two camps. In the past six months, 67% of households in Camp 15 and 45% in Camp 16 received these MHM kits.Additionally, while the gap in bathing spaces has been partially addressed with an increase in private household bathing spaces, many of these facilities are poorly maintained and misused, leading to public health risks. Women and girls are particularly affected, facing overcrowding, lack of privacy, and the threat of harassment and violence in public bathing facilities. As a result, many prefer makeshift private bathing areas within their shelters.These conditions underline the urgent need for the construction of new latrines and the maintenance of existing facilities, including fecal sludge management units. Another significant concern is solid waste management; only a little over half of the refugee households use designated disposal areas, with the remainder disposing of waste in open spaces, leading to unsanitary conditions.Promoting hygiene is vital for preventing diseases and maintaining overall health, especially in densely populated environments. Despite widespread awareness and access to soap, changing hygiene behaviors is a persistent challenge. Moreover, many women face obstacles in accessing MHM resources, hindered by social stigma and the absence of tailored MHM kits. The combination of inadequate sanitation infrastructure, poor hygiene practices, and these barriers significantly increases the vulnerabilities of the affected population, emphasizing the need for holistic WASH interventions.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

In camps 15 and 16, CARE's dual role as the exclusive WASH agency and the Site Management Support (SMS) agency in camp 16 enables a well-coordinated approach to camp management and service delivery. This integrated functioning is in line with the two key ISCG rationalization principles that CARE considers crucial in its project design and implementation.The first principle focuses on enhancing inter-sectoral camp-based coordination. By aligning the WASH and SMS roles, CARE fosters improved collaboration across different sectors within the camps. This approach streamlines processes, ensuring that the various needs of the camp populations are met more efficiently and cohesively.The second principle is about ensuring predictability and continuity in refugee operations. As both the WASH and SMS agency in these camps, CARE provides a stable and consistent presence, crucial for maintaining the overall stability of the camps. This stability is key to building trust and fostering positive relationships with the refugees, the Government, and other camp partners.Adhering to these principles, CARE effectively addresses the urgent needs in these camps, such as the limited access to safely managed water and the necessity for enhanced sanitation facilities. The integrated management of WASH and SMS under CARE's oversight allows for a comprehensive understanding of the camps' requirements, leading to more effective resource management and targeted interventions. This approach not only improves essential services like water and sanitation but also contributes significantly to the overall health, safety, and well-being of the camp inhabitants.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project incorporates protection mainstreaming as a key component, focusing on the welfare and safety of all community members, particularly those most vulnerable. Emphasizing the importance of gender-sensitive approaches, the project utilizes CARE's "Women Lead in Emergencies" methodology. This approach empowers women’s groups to take active roles in decision-making and leadership within WASH-related activities. These groups will identify, create, and implement action plans to address specific protection needs within their communities.Joint safety audits by the WASH and protection teams are critical to this strategy. These audits assess potential risks and barriers in accessing WASH facilities and are essential for adapting WASH safety tools and procedures to effectively mitigate protection risks within the community.In addition, the project focuses on the coordination and distribution of menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits and materials. Working closely with shelter/NFI teams and protection actors, the project ensures these resources are readily available and accessible, prioritizing privacy and convenience. This effort includes careful planning to avoid duplication between different types of aid kits, such as dignity kits and emergency kits.

3.3 - Age & Disability

This project prioritizes the needs of individuals with disabilities and those of different age groups, ensuring inclusive and accessible WASH facilities. Training for the WASH team will include a focus on addressing the unique challenges faced by these groups, enhancing their ability to provide safe and effective services.Joint safety audits, involving the WASH team, will identify and assess barriers to accessing WASH facilities for people with disabilities and those of various age groups. These audits will inform the adaptation of WASH safety audit tools to ensure they are inclusive and cater to the diverse needs of the community.In collaboration with shelter/NFI teams and protection actors, the project will ensure the accessibility and appropriateness of menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits and materials, with a special focus on the requirements of women and girls with disabilities. The distribution of these resources will be managed to respect privacy and ease of access, especially for those with mobility issues.To avoid duplication of resources and ensure efficient service delivery, the project will maintain close collaboration with relevant actors and focus points, ensuring that the specific needs of people with disabilities and different age groups are effectively met.Finally, the project commits to adhering to best practices in the design and operation of WASH facilities, prioritizing accessibility, and inclusivity. This includes setting aside specific hours at water points for the exclusive use of people with disabilities, ensuring that these facilities are accessible and safe for all community members, regardless of age or disability.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

CARE Bangladesh has established a comprehensive and efficient Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) across all humanitarian projects in Cox’s Bazar to maintain accountability. This system encompasses a wide range of channels, both solicited and unsolicited, for receiving information, recommendations, feedback, opinions, questions, complaints, or requests from various groups including beneficiaries, stakeholders, staff, volunteers, government agencies, and other organizations. Key channels include feedback and information centers, info hubs, hotlines, mobile CFRM teams, community meetings, complaint boxes, household visits, and focus group discussions. CARE ensures that all members of the affected community have equal access to this safe and responsive CFRM, irrespective of their sex, age, disability, race, caste, or color. The system is managed and overseen by a dedicated focal person, supported by a specialized CFRM team in each project. This team, comprising CARE staff and volunteers, is responsible for issue collection and field-level coordination. The CFRM team is committed to addressing all received issues promptly, depending on their nature and urgency. On average, CARE handles about 3000 to 5000 issues monthly, successfully resolving 2000 to 3000 of these cases. Accountability focal points regularly share CFRM reports with various donors and projects, providing updates on a weekly, monthly, or quarterly basis, as required.To efficiently collect and manage CFRM data, CARE employs a variety of platforms and tools. These include KOBO, ODK, Activity Info, CFP, and MODA, among others. Staff and volunteers are well-trained in these platforms, ensuring the effectiveness and reliability of the CFRM system.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

This project is committed to integrating Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) into its WASH interventions by addressing various gender-related issues, vulnerabilities, and opportunities. At the onset or during the program's implementation, a rapid gender analysis will be conducted or existing data will be analyzed, with a specific focus on gender concerns. The project will regularly collaborate with the Gender and Protection sector to provide gender-related technical support for the implementation strategy. This collaboration will include capacity training for project staff and volunteers on identifying gender-related challenges in project interventions, guided by the technical expertise of the Gender and Protection sector.The project aims to efficiently address and overcome gender barriers, enhancing the reach and effectiveness of its activities. Crucial to this effort is ensuring women's meaningful participation in community-level groups and adopting CARE’s Global Gender in Emergencies framework as a gender mainstreaming approach.Accessibility of WASH facilities for all genders, persons with disabilities, and other diverse groups (including LGBTI individuals) is a key goal. This will be achieved by establishing gender-segregated latrines, disability-friendly latrines and bathing facilities, and accessible water points. The project has already made significant strides in PSEA training, having facilitated basic PSEA training for 430 volunteers, laborers, and 64 vendors, in addition to refresher training. Furthermore, a group of 12 Master trainers specializing in PSEA has been prepared, who have conducted these training sessions for the community.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project emphasizes DRR/DRM and climate change mitigation measures. Cox’s Bazar, prone to extreme weather events such as heavy rainfall, landslides, and flash floods, has become even more susceptible due to environmental strain from the influx of Rohingya refugees. The consequent reduction in natural vegetation has negatively impacted soil and water quality, further diminishing the region's resilience to natural disasters.
