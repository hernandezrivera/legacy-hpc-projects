Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to contribute to the Health Cluster objective #1, aligned to the HNO Strategic Objective # 2. More precisely, the project will emphasize the following specific objectives:
-Integrate MHPSS (with special attention to GBV & the needs of PWD, LGBTQ groups) at ER departments in general hospitals for better detection, response, and referral for youth risky behaviors.
-Enhance the specialized MHPSS provision of care to vulnerable youth (especially girls, GBV survivors & PWD) at Community Mental Health Centers level
-Promote psycho-education and public health awareness in conﬂict-affected communities about youth vulnerability (especially girls, LGBTQ, & PWD) with regards to self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide, involving CBOs, religious leaders, and schools.
-Support the responding capacity of SAWA’s free helpline- in detecting, supporting, and referring youth presenting self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide vulnerability.
-Strengthen access to COVID-19 medical services, GBV, and MHPSS services through SAWA Mobile Clinics (MCs) and reaching vulnerable groups including PWD in Area C & EJ, especially for women, & PWD.
The project will mainly target Palestinian youth aged from 15-29 years old presenting MHPSS issues and/or suffering from Years Lived with Disability (YLD) due to a suicide attempt, chronic exposure to violence and/or due to COVID-19 pandemic, infection, social stigmatization in vulnerable communities. The recent Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2021 has shown that 25-38% of households have at least one member reporting self-diagnosed mental distress. In addition, PCBS (2019) reports 29% of Women in oPt had experienced violence (psychological, sexual , economical) in oPt there are 2.51 million female, 9.1% of these women would need specialized MHPSS services beside protection services. According to WHO fact sheet on adolescent 10-20% of children and adolescents experience mental disorders. Half of all mental illnesses begin by the age of 14 and three-quarters by mid-20s. If untreated, these conditions severely influence children’s development, their educational attainments and their potential to live fulfilling and productive lives. Children with mental disorders face major challenges with stigma, isolation and discrimination, as well as lack of access to health care and education facilities 43% of the Palestinian population are children and adolescents and there is a need to strengthen mental health services provided to them. A 2020 WHO survey on the impact COVID-19 demonstrate that people with pre-existing mental health issues become more susceptible to virus infections, are more often severe and can lead to death. A 2019 mixed study conducted by MdM-CH on the risk factors of attempted suicide patients in the West Bank found that 52% of the total attempted suicide patients were youth, ages 16-25 years old. COVID-19 enlarges this group to include infected patients, as the virus causes various mental and neurological complications. The project proposes activities, aiming to:
1. Decrease stigma related to Self-Harm and Suicide, as well as disability caused by suicide attempt and self-harm, in Palestine and increase detection and referral of vulnerable cases to specialized MHPSS care, through speciﬁc actions at institutional (MoH hospitals) and community level (Mobile Clinics, CMHCs, CBOs, schools, and religious leaders).
2. Enhance the SAWA Emergency hotline in detecting, supporting and referring youth presenting self-harm and risky behaviors, and increase the targeting of PWD to benefit from these services.
3. Support SAWA Mobile Clinics in the screening and detection of youth at risk of self-harm and/or
suicide, with improved access of PWD to SAWA MC services.
4. Integrate MHPSS at hospitals to better detect, respond, and refer risky behaviors of youth and PWD by emphasizing on mhGap intervention.
5. Enhance specialized MHPSS provision of care for youth and PWD at CMHC's.

1 - Needs

Mental Health and Psychosocial issues represent one of the most signiﬁcant public health challenges across the oPt. Although the precise extent of mental disorders in Palestine is unknown, the ﬁndings of a study published in 2017 (The Burden of Mental Disorders in the Eastern Mediterranean Region, 1990-2013) indicate that Palestine has the largest burden of mental disorders among the Eastern Mediterranean Region. 2019 WHO estimates of mental disorders in conﬂict settings show that more than one in ﬁve people (22.1%) in post-conﬂict settings has depression, anxiety disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, bipolar disorder, or schizophrenia. Adapted to the Palestinian Population, and considering the escalation of violence in hot zones, we can consider that in Palestine, at least 250,026 individuals are in-need of MHPSS interventions. A study published in 2017 (Suicidal ideation and planning among Palestinian middle school students living in Gaza Strip, West Bank, and United Nations Relief and Works Agency (UNRWA) camps. International journal of pediatrics and adolescent medicine, 4(2), 54-60), explored suicidal ideation among Palestinian middle school students in grades 7, 8, and 9 and analyzed data from a Global School-based Student Health Survey. The study found that the overall prevalence of suicidal ideation and/or planning was %25.6. Males were more likely than females to report suicidal thinking (Itani et al. (2017)).The 2021 UN Women Gender Alert highlights how conflict disproportionately impacts the mental health of women in general and women with disabilities in specific, especially in vulnerable communities. SAWA, the only national helpline providing MHPSS, GBV, medical, and legal tele-services to suicide attempt and self-harm patients, reports a total of 154 counseling sessions for suicide attempters in 2020, the vast majority of whom were females (97 patients). In 2021, the number increases to a total of 200 counseling sessions to patients of attempted suicide in the first 9 months of the year (112 of those sessions were delivered to female patients). With the recent escalation of violence in the West Bank last April/ May, the provision of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) has become more urgent. SAWA indicates a spike in calls related to suicide ideation in the two months following the escalations. WHO estimates highlight that years living with disabilities (YLDs) for depression and posttraumatic stress disorder in conflict contexts are 5 times higher than the existing global mean burden of disease estimates. In parallel, ﬁeld workers in the West Bank reported a sharp increase of attempted and/or completed suicide in recent years, whilst consolidated national data is still lacking on the speciﬁc causes of death due to the associated stigma that leads families and healthcare professionals to under-report these cases. Worldwide, suicide accounts for 1.4% of all deaths and is the 18th leading cause of death. Suicide is the second leading cause of death among youth 15-29 years of age around the world. Police and government records in the Arab World report annual completed suicide rates of 1.1/100,000 to 6.2/100,000 (Karam et al., 2008). WHO indicate that 79% of suicide occurs in low- and middle-income countries. In Palestine, police public statistics demonstrate an annual increase in suicide rates since 2016. The COVID-19 pandemic has led to a surge in mental health issues across the globe (WHO 2020). Social isolation has caused reduced physical activity, and reduced cognitive stimulation, which in turn may cause an increase in the risks of cognitive decline and dementia (ibid). A 2021 WHO & Juzoor study emphasizes the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the mental health of youth and adolescents in Palestine. It shows that this group has suffered increased levels of psychological distress caused by the pandemic and the continuous lockdowns. Over 60% of the 1,800 surveyed adolescents exhibited poor to very-poor mental wellbeing. It revealed significantly elevated rates of psychological distress, among this group, compared to before the pandemic and lockdown, and negative impacts on family’s financial situations, family relationships, social connectedness, motivation, and learning. Furthermore, the pandemic has disrupted all forms of health provision in Palestine (Health Cluster HNO). More specifically, the disruption has impacted mental, neurological and substance abuse victims, which WHO 2020 attests as life-threatening; particularly when impacting service provision for attempted/ suicide patients. The pandemic has caused a widespread disruption to critical mental health services for vulnerable people (72% disruption to services of children and adolescents, and 61% disruptions to women requiring antenatal or postnatal services). Finally, literature within the field of psychology emphasizes that understanding the risk factors for suicide and suicide methods is very signiﬁcant towards the development of effective preventive strategies. On this basis, MdM-CH conducted an assessment study in 4 Palestinian Public Hospitals in the West Bank to identify speciﬁc risk factors and suicide prevention strategies. The results highlight a crucial gap in the detection of people at risk, due to severe psychological distress, risky behaviors and self-harm, as well as to the paucity of cross-sectoral responses. It recommends consolidated, crosscutting, efforts to build a sustainable system of detection, alert and referral of people in need of urgent lifesaving MHPSS interventions, and advocates for further prevention national strategies. Taking into account the study ﬁndings, and WHO’s recommendation to include mental health as an integral component in counties’ COVID-19 response plans, this joint project between WHO and MdM-CH proposes evidence-based activities tailored to the unique and volatile Palestinian context.

2 - Activities

Based on the speciﬁc objectives described above (see summary) the project proposes the following activities listed below: Training for MoH► Practical on-site training at ER departments in previously targeted hospitals: Train a new group of staff (50 ER staff) at 5 WB hospitals in the Southern governorates of Hebron, Bethlehem, and Jericho (Jericho,Mohtasib, Yatta, Alia, Beit Jala hospitals) where phase one of this project was implemented on soft skills and stress management related to suicide prevention and managements, emphasizing youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors, suicide attempts, and the need to provide inclusive healthcare and safe access to services for PWD, LGBTQ, and GBV survivor groups. During MdM-CH pilot training 53 ER staff were trained at these hospitals. ► Practical on-site training at ER departments in newly targeted hospitals: Train a group of ER staff (50 staff) at 5 new hospitals (Rafidia hospital, Medical Center, Thabet Thabet, Nazal Darwish, and Yaser Arafat hospitals) in 5 different governorates in the West Bank (Nablus, Ramallah, and Tulkarem, Qalqilya and Salfit respectively) on soft skills and stress management related to suicide prevention and management, emphasizing youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors, suicide attempts, and the need to provideinclusive healthcare and safe access to services for PWD, LGBTQ, and GBV survivor groups. ► Practical on-site training for psychiatrists: Train MoH psychiatrists (13 psychiatrists and 2 resident psychiatrists) on the medical and psychotherapeutic treatment for Suicidal and At-risk behaviorsfollowing a scientifically based and culturally adapted approach. A special section will bedesignated to the topic on suicide amongst children & adolescents (with a focus onvulnerable groups: PWD, LGBTQ, and GBV survivors, chronic mental health patients andsubstance use).► Train Mental Health professionals at Community Mental Health Centers (CMHCs) on youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors, suicide attempts, and the need to provide inclusive care and safe access to services for PWD, LGBTQ, and GBV survivor groups. ►Supervision sessions to trained MoH staff (ER and Psychiatrists). SAWA ► Continued operation of the SAWA helpline (15 operators to be available 24 hours/7 days a week).► Specialized training for all SAWA helpline operators (seniors and juniors) about youth and PWDvulnerability to self- harm, risky behaviors, suicide ideation, YLDs, and plans. ► Ad hoc & systematic clinical supervision for 12 months of SAWA helpline operators and 2 casemanagers for cases of self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide.► Provide MHPSS, medical, GBV, legal, and COVID-19 awareness services to youth, PWD, and P&L women in most vulnerable communities through SAWA Mobile Clinics.► 10-day training on mhGAP intervention guideline 2.0 and 2-day training on detection of suicidal risk behavior for SAWA's Mobile Clinic team, emphasizing youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors,suicide attempts with special attention to PWD, GBV survivors. This will include the provision of one clinical supervisor for each Mobile Clinic team. Sensitization and Awareness Raising. ► Mapping activity of all service providers (Health, MHPSS, and Protection-PWD & GBV) to create a revised comprehensive referral booklet and referral pathways that can be used by trained staff at public hospitals and military medical service centers. ► Raise awareness and sensitize CBO staff and community volunteers in marginalized areas on suicide prevention and management, especially amongst vulnerable youth and adolescents- GBV survivors, LGBTQ and PWD; including information on groups at risks of suicide to become actors of support for these specific populations including suicide prevention response and GBV within their operational portfolios.►Raise awareness and sensitize religious leaders on suicide prevention and management;including information on vulnerable youth and other groups (GBV survivors, LGBTQ, &PWD) at risk of suicide to become actors of support for these specific populations and toprevent social stigma against these groups as a result of religious misconceptions onsuicide.► Workshop for Protection (GBV, CP-MHPSS) cluster members about suicide prevention and recommendations of best practices.► Quarterly coordination with the International Association for Suicide Prevention (IASP), the Ministry of Health, and the WHO.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

At all stages of the Health Project Cycle management: Diagnosis, Implementation andM&E, protection mainstreaming is a cross-cutting theme for WHO and MdM-CHprograms and projects. WHO and MDM-CH understand and fully adhere to the coreprinciple of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian imperative, a cornerstone in all programsfollowed by the pyramid of IASC minimum standards in regards to MHPSS & PSEA.We follow strong PSEA policies to protect beneficiaries and staff. Both partners remainvigilant throughout project implementation to identify and prevent unintended negativeconsequences of their actions, especially on PWD. In this regard a number of steps and measures areadopted to ensure that its interventions maximize positive impacts for communitystability, mainly by strengthening PIN capacities and resilience, while minimizing harm.Moreover, in order to further enhance protection mainstreaming the project will ensurethat health staff will be capacitated on the following 4 topics: - MHPSS IASC minimumstandards: Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in EmergencySettings (WHO guidelines) - GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response toGender-based Violence in Emergencies (following UNFPA standards) - Child ProtectionMinimum standards in Humanitarian action (following Unicef and Sphere guidelines) -Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (following Spherestandards and MdM guidelines) - Disability mainstreaming and health: CBR(Community based rehabilitation guidelines) health component of WHO.- PSEA IASCguide our feedback & complaint mechanisms.Further, SAWA has developed internalguidelines and policies that are regularly updated and improved to reflect the realitieson the ground. In this respect it is important to mention that SAWA has direct contactwith hundreds of beneficiaries each day. Two thirds of the consultations are follow upcalls which means that the callers trust the counselor and express the wish forcontinued counseling. Since the counseling is based on the method of active listening,the counselors do not impose their own solutions on the caller and they do not try tofind solutions for the caller's situation. It is the beneficiary that is at the center(survivor-centered approach) throughout the consultation and it is the caller whoindependently takes the decision on the next steps to take. Also in cases of referrals toother partners or authorities, the counselor triggers the referrals on the beneficiaries'own wish and DO NO HARM policy is strictly followed by counselor. In case when thecounseling is done with children, additional policies apply (Child Safeguarding Policy),so that the consultation follows all standards on how to work with minors.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Both WHO and MdM-CH are basing their interventions on ethical and humanitarianprinciples. This project aligns with the principal approach that is widely accepted in thehumanitarian community through such vehicles as the SPHERE Humanitarian Charter(and SPHERE MHPSS). Ethical principles in healthcare include respect for persons toadvance human dignity and for individuals’ autonomous choices; beneﬁcence, thepromotion of others’ well-being, commonly known as “do no harm,” as describedabove and justice, encompassing both the fair distribution of resources and fairprocesses for decision-making. Humanitarian principles is the second source of valuesand norms that animate both applicants’ actions. HP include humanity, requiring thatsuffering must be addressed wherever and for whomever it is found; neutrality, theduty of humanitarian actors not to take sides in a conﬂict; impartiality, which stipulatesthat humanitarian actors must not discriminate or give preference to any nationality,physical ability, race, religious belief, class, political opinion, or similar status; andindependence, which demands that humanitarian actors retain their autonomy andremain independent of political or military objectives of other actors. As for SAWA, it isa free and anonymous helpline where any caller can receive the services offered bySAWA without distinction based on gender, age, religion, sexual identity or socioeconomicbackground. SAWA helpline is a simple 3-digit phone number that is easy toremember, easy to compile and it is above all free to call from the West Bank and GazaStrip. For Jerusalem, the number is another but it is also free of charge.
