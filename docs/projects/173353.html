Project Summary

Sirri Care Foundation will be targeting to improve inclusive, quality and protective education to vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities’members in Jere and Maiduguri metropolis LGAs in Borno state. This will be achieved through an education intervention with the integration of school and community based mechanisms to provide psychosocial support services for children and teachers by strengthening the capacity of volunteer teachers, ensuring save, accessible school enrollment for out of school children especially girls including persons with disability, provision of quality learning materials and equipment for pupils and construction/rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces (for non-formal education) to respond to the education needs.

1 - Needs

2 According to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview, 1.03 million children (98% children, 15% people with disability) are faced with barriers to accessing education need access to basic quality education. Of this total, 33% of are internally displaced persons, 20% are returnees and 47% are from host communities with about 60% anticipated to be school-aged children. Also, an estimation of 1.4M out-of-school girls and 1.3M boys facing uncertain future without education (HNO, 2020). The COVID-19 outbreak also exacerbated the situation as schools were closed to contain the spread of the virus. This contributed to more children dropping out of school with girls mostly affected increased rates of early/forced marriage for girls, not continuing their education. The majority of girls in the north of the country do not attend primary school, with only 47 per cent of girls in Northeast and Northwest Nigeria enrolled. School dropout occurs at higher rates with older learners (especially girls) in Northeast Nigeria The overall drop-out rate for children at age 12 is 1.2% compared with 2.5% at age 14, 6.5% at age 16, and 14.1% at age 18. Schools needs provide specific protection to adolescent girls in particular find schools not adapted to their needs. Menstruating girls sometimes miss or skip classes due to the challenges they face with menstruation. School closure has exacerbated the already existing attitudes of non- enrollment of boys and girls in formal schools increasing inequalities, spurring children to carry out alternative tasks for their families and disproportionally exposing girls to the risk of exploitation and sexual harassment. There have been reports of an increase in violent crime and drug abuse, all these serving as coping strategies for survival with increase in out of school and children without opportunity to attend school. Teachers needs to be supported with psychosocial services in order to cope with the crisis’ impact and deliver quality, learner-centred education. There is need therefore to address these conditions to promote quality, safe inclusive education for boys and girls in IDP Camps and members of host communities in Maiduguri and Jere LGAs of Borno State.

2 - Activities

Act1. Construction/Rehabilitation of 4 gender and disability-friendly TLS in existing public schools and communities. Act2. Procurement and distribution of learning supplies including ECD kits to 2000 boys and girls Act3. Provision of Sanitary and Hygiene kits for adolescent girls attending non formal classes to ensure their retention and reduction of the number of drop-out of adolescent girls during menstruation period. Act4. Training of 40 Volunteer Teachers (20F, 20M) on PSS to cope with the effects of insurgency and improve the outcomes of the returnees, GBV and referral path ways to able to identify and link boys and girls to access required, appropriate services. Act5. Enrollment of 2.000 out of school boys and girls into TLS. Act6. Training of 4 SBMC (60 members; 15 in each community) on schools’ management, GBV and resource mobilisation to ensure safe access and inclusiveness. Act7. Training of 40 teachers (20 females 20 males) on Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) to strengthen their capacity to provide quality, learner-centered education. Act8. Carry out Community awareness and sensitize on the need to enroll children into school with more emphasis on focus on girls’ access to education.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The proposed project is designed such that gender is mainstreamed at all stages of the project (design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation). It will enhance access to services for women, men, boys and girls including persons living with disabilities thus, promotes gender equality. Also, project will ensure compliance with COVID-19 principles and also carry out sensitization on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, ensure the strict implementation of Do No Harm Principle and child safeguarding. Sirri Care shall provide hotline numbers where protection concerns shall be reported

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Sirri Care will work with community members including women, men, boys, girls and people with disabilities to establish gender-sensitive and age appropriate compliant mechanisms in project target communities. The established complaint channels will provide for community members and people with disabilities that are directly affected by the project a means to raise complaints about the quality of program including staff behavior. Also, there will be complaint and feedback desks and boxes at strategic areas in the project communities to ensure that community members are able to provide feedback and complaints about the project. Field consultations shall also be carried our to evaluate the level of project delivery based on Sirri Care program policy
