Project Summary

The overall situation in northeastern Nigeria, can still be characterized as dire with IDPs, returnees and host community members all facing acute life-threatening risks to safety and dignity. Following nearly ten years of attacks by Armed Opposition Groups (AOGs), families have been displaced numerous times following either failed attempts at return to areas of origin or the spreading of violence into new areas. , Populations as a result of conflict and displacement face grave protection risks and violations such as exploitation and abuse, arbitrary and extended detentions, family separation, restrictions on freedom of movement and insecurity. These are then further exacerbated by insufficient access to basic services and psychosocial distress. The ongoing conflict and continued displacements in the northeast particularly affect the most vulnerable populations, including the elderly and chronically sick, persons with physical and mental disabilities, female and child-headed households, unaccompanied or separated children, adolescent boys, pregnant and/or lactating women and people returning from captivity. Violations and sexual abuse against women and children are widespread. The IRC will provide holistic Protection services to meet the large number of households with high protection needs. Throughout its intervention the IRC will ensure protection mainstreaming, inclusive program design for persons with specific needs, and attention to gender sensitivity.

1 - Needs

the project will addresses the huge protection needs/gaps such as risk of further exposure to protection issues due to lack of resources, negative coping mechanisms and risks of tensions between IDPs and communities. other assessed needs such as limited access to information on rights, availability of referral pathways. vulnerable individual who face heightened protection risk. people who dont have access to means of identity which has led to them being harassed at military check points, unlawfully arrested, tortured and molested while most of them are financially extorted. There is also need to engage with community/camp structures to establish and coordinate location-specific coordination and referral mechanisms. The majority of the conflict-affected population have experienced brutal violence, loss of family members, friends and property, accumulated and protracted stress, deterioration in living conditions, increased military presence, social disruption in communities and lack of access to social services, such as schools and healthcare.

2 - Activities

1.protection monitoring: the IRC will conduct protection monitoring to identify trends in human rights violations and promote access to services. Findings will be disseminated to improve humanitarian action and to IDPs, returnees and host community members through outreach and information centers.2. community-based protection (CBP) groups: IDPs, returnees and host community members will be engaged through Protection Action Groups (PAGs). Members of each CBP group will benefit from specialized capacity building activities to prevent violence, provide psychological first aid and refer for specialized services. 3. protection mainstreaming activities and protection mainstreaming beneficiary assessment: shall be conducted across all humanitarian sectors to ensure promotion of meaningful access, safety, dignity, participation and empowerment of all IDPs and returnees. Community feedback mechanisms and regular sex and age segregated consultations will increase humanitarian actors’ accountability to beneficiaries, including improving safeguarding from humanitarian perpetration of exploitation of abuse. Community groups and staff will be trained on safeguarding and protection mainstreaming action plans which will be developed, monitored and adjusted throughout the project to accommodate and address the realities on ground.4. Relevant stakeholders follow rules and laws to protect civilians: the IRC will strengthen the knowledge, skills, capacity and accountability of duty bearers in both government institutions (e.g. SEMA, NEMA) and at the community level (e.g. religious and traditional leaders). This will be achieved through technical and material support; monitoring duty bearers’ actions and through pursuing an evidence based advocacy strategy targeting duty bearers at all levels, including where appropriate, at the national and state level.Knowledge and skills will be enhanced through trainings to ensure duty bearers access information and know about protective rules and laws pertinent to the prevailing context in the North East. This will focus on protection principles, protection mainstreaming and legislative frameworks governing IDPs such as guiding principles on internal displacement and dignified returns, the Kampala Convention on IDPs, humanitarian law, human rights, the Nigerian constitution and state laws of interest. Community leaders will be trained in dispute resolution. 5. Case management services will expand to encompass individuals who face heightened protection risk but are not eligible for either specialized case management services for women or children.6. Targetted Individual assistance (TIA): through TIA which is a one off life saving assistance to beneficiaries after failed referrals, the IRC will strengthen quality, targeted service provision for vulnerable IDPs and host community members. Person with Specific Needs (PSN) including persons with disabilities, the elderly, and chronically ill, will be assisted with legal services. Information, counselling and support will support beneficiaries to gain legal identity and civil documentation through mobile registration.7. Protection help desk: The IRC currently has protection help desks across the BAY states set up to disseminate information on available services, where clients can also make enquires, give feedback/suggestions and make complaints as well as supporting newly arrived displaced identified with protection needs with in kind and cash services through its referrals pathways.8. Community members have information about laws, their rights and availability of services: through information dissemination and sensitization sessions, and as an off shot of the outcome from protection monitoring visits, protection risk analysis, the prevalent issues arising in the communities will be revealed which will inform the topics to be treated during the sessions so as to reduce the threats, vulnerability while increasing the capacities of the community members.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The IRC will integrate robust feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, older people and people with disabilities. These will include regular FGDs and listening sessions, including sessions specifically with women and girls; suggestion boxes at project sites, including within protection information centres; a toll-free hotline accessible to beneficiaries who can neither read nor write, with notices in local languages with the hotline number posted prominently at project sites. household interviews, which will reach beneficiaries served by mobile teams in hard-to-reach locations. Feedback is received through hotline handlers, who refer requests for assistance or feedback on programme quality to the relevant programme teams for action. Each point of feedback is documented and compiled in monthly reports allowing management to track the status of response and follow-up.
