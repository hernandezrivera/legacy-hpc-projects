Project Summary

The project consists of two components in two geographical areas. In Benghazi, UNMAS will carry out Non-Technical Surveys (NTS) in combination with surface clearance of explosive hazards. In Tawergha, UNMAS will coordinate and support the deployment of a combination of survey and clearance teams, and provide Risk Awareness to prepare Internally Displaced People (IDPs) for their return.

Needs

Benghazi has been at the centre of violence and destruction since the beginning of 2011. As a result, the contamination level in the city and its surroundings is arguably one of the highest in Libya. Despite this, support from the international community has so far been limited due to the ongoing conflict and difficulties in secure access. Some of the areas to be targeted under this project, for example Sabri in the city centre, were virtually rendered uninhabitable by explosive hazard contamination. In others, such as Ganfuda, Souq Al Hout and Al Gawarsha, also located in central parts of Benghazi, although partially inhabited, ERW continue to pose a serious threat to the local community, inhibit save access to vital services, and prevent other humanitarian actors from entering the areas.Following the revolution, Tawergha's entire population of 40,000 was forcefully evicted by armed groups from Misrata. As punitive measure, the groups proceeded to inflict large scale destruction to residential and commercial areas, and destroyed vital infrastructure and services so as to prevent the return of the Tawerghan population. Since then any reconstruction efforts have been prevented, and the city remains heavily contaminated by explosive hazards. As IDPs return to both Benghazi and Tawergha they will be faced by explosive hazard contamination. Many IDPs will be forced to take up residence in affected areas, despite the risk the contamination poses to their life and physical well being.The Joint field visit assessments that were conducted by OCHA and UNMAS in both Benghazi and Tawergha in 2018 and the several liaison and coordination meetings that were held with relevant stakeholders including local and national authorities, the Libyan Mine Action Centre (LibMAC), local communities, civil society organizations, and national and international Mine Action operators, confirmed the necessity to conduct Non-Technical Surveys in order to assess whether areas are contaminated by explosive hazards and categorize them as suspected or confirmed hazardous area (SHA/CHA) to support priority setting processes and contribute to efficient and effective planning of subsequent technical interventions. The MSNA 2018 also highlighted the low number of civilians made aware of the risk of explosive hazards. An estimated 5% of IDPs and returnees have reported presence of explosive hazards at neighborhood level and did not receive awareness training.

Activities

Benghazi and Tawergha:- Non-technical survey teams training and accreditation- Coordination of tasking orders and team deployment- Community liaison to identify the areas that are suspected to be contaminated - Mine action survey to confirm the presence or absence of explosive hazards- Safe areas and dangerous areas recorded in Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) and mapped - Contamination information and maps shared with stakeholders (local authorities and humanitarian actors)- Quality management visits ensure that all activities are compliant with Libyan National Mine Action Standards - Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) teamS training and accreditation- Destruction/removal of explosive hazards- Meetings with relevant local authorities to coordinate destruction/removal activities- Coordination of tasking orders and team deployment- Update of clearance activities shared with stakeholders (local authorities and humanitarian actors) Additional activities for Tawerghan IDPs:- Recruitment and training of 10 volunteers in Mine Risk Awareness ToT- Review of Risk Awareness ToT training material - Monitoring of training sessions. Coordination:As the representative of the country’s National Mine Action Authority, the Libyan Mine Action Center (LibMAC) has a key role in facilitating and coordinating humanitarian mine action activities in Libya. For its part, UNMAS continues to support the enhancement of the Center’s capacity to accredit humanitarian mine action implementing partners, and for prioritising and providing tasking orders for survey, clearance, and risk awareness activities. Surveys, clearance and mine action risk awareness activities will be conducted in coordination with the LibMAC and the Mine Action partners. As the Mine Action AoR Coordinator, UNMAS is coordinating Mine Action activities in Libya through regular contacts and monthly meetings with implementing partners, chaired by the Libyan Mine Action Center (LibMAC), to discuss activities, challenges and set priorities. Mine Action interventions are requested by the LibMAC through task orders and are reported into the Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA), which ensures mine action interventions are not being duplicated and respond to the Libyan needs. Mine Risk Education ToT and delivery of MRE activities will be conducted in consultation with other sectors such as the Protection Sector and the Child Protection Working Group to ensure messages are adequately developed and disseminated according to International and Libyan Mine Action Standards. Note: The number of individuals targeted under the Mine Action response project in Benghazi, supported by CERF, was originally calculated through a catchment area estimate as 120.000. In addition, the Mine Action response project in Tawergha, not supported by CERF, targets 40.000 beneficiaries (the total number of IDPs). This exceeds the total number of People in Need (PiN), as calculated by the HNO/HRP. The number of people targeted under this project as part of the HRP is therefore set as equal to the total PiN of protection in both the Benghazi and Tawergha areas (83,661).

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

Accountability to affected populations will be ensured during project design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation. Survey activities by definition require close personal interaction with the affected population. Information and preferences communicated by local residents, both men and women, will be taken into account when prioritizing and targeting areas for clearance.Surveys and clearance will be determined and carried out in close cooperation with local authorities, local communities and the Libyan Mine Action Centre (LibMAC). The hotline number will be communicated to local populations during the Mine Risk Education sessions so any complaints or explosive items can be reported to local organizations and the LibMAC. Survey reports will be received from the implementing partners and will be shared with the Protection Sector and the wider humanitarian community. Qualified NTS and EOD teams are accredited by the LibMAC with the technical support and assistance provided by UNMAS. Quality Management visits will be carried out by UNMAS Libya to ensure that the interventions comply with International Mine Action Standards (IMAS) and the Gender guidelines for Mine Action Programmes and are in line with PSEA policies and the United Nations Code of Conduct. Mine action activities should not adversely affect local communities. The presence of mine/ERW survey or clearance teams in impoverished mine-affected communities could potentially be a catalyst for sexual exploitation or abuse of women and children at risk in the local population. UNMAS will make sure contractors or implementing partners adhere to the relevant Codes of Conduct and will apply a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse. This project specifically contributes to increasing access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries by assessing whether areas are contaminated, by disposing of explosive remnants of war, and by raising awareness of local population, men, women, boys and girls about the risks of explosive hazards. The comprehensive Mine Action response in Benghazi and Tawergha will allow to provide safe access of beneficiaries, including IDPs and returnees, to basic services and infrastructures.
