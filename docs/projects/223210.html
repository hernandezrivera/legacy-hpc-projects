Project Summary

The goal of the program is to improve the living conditions of and restore dignity to vulnerable conflict-affected displaced households in Chiúre district, Cabo Delgado province.
CRS and Caritas will conduct: Distribution of Shelter Kits and Training in Build Back Better approaches and related concepts to ensure shelters are safe, dignified and aligned with Sphere standards.
To support displaced households in completing critical and essential repairs to their shelters, tool kits will be distributed in-kind to groups of approximately 4-5 households. CRS and Caritas Pemba will work with camp and community leaders on identifying appropriate and feasible groups to share shelter kits. CRS and Caritas Pemba will work with community and camp leaders to form tool management committees, comprising of male and female members to oversee sharing of tool kits and to ensure safe storage of toolkits. In order to reduce risks of loss, theft, or misuse of tools or repair techniques, CRS and Caritas Pemba will engage camp and community leaders in identifying appropriate sequencing of households to receive assistance in the resettlement site; vulnerability criteria defined with the community will be used to determine the order families receive assistance and begin repairs to their shelters.
CRS and Caritas Pemba will conduct workshops with at least one representative per IDP household on the Build Back Better approach, including IEC materials. Technical staff on the ground will also provide technical support during the implementation of repairs; the project will provide hands-on training using some of the beneficiaries’ houses as demonstration and training. Training topics will include Disaster Risk Reduction principles, hazard and risk awareness, safe building techniques, and recommended building materials. Security and personal safety aspects, including safe placement of a shelter’s entrance given the direction of the flow of rainwater, adequate spacing between temporary shelters to reduce the risk of fire, the safe use of tools to limit the risk of accident.
Targeting
CRS and Caritas Pemba will conduct an assessment to reconfirm and validate that all households meet vulnerability criteria determined in consultation with government authorities and community members. Vulnerability criteria is likely to include:
• Single/separated/widowed female head of households;
• Households with pregnant or lactating women;
• Household with children under the age of 5;
• Minor-age head of household;
• Elderly and persons with disabilities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Regarding project design, CRS will ensure that the Shelter support is tailor-made to mitigate protection risks and takes into consideration of the needs of vulnerable groups. Greater protection concerns will be mainstreamed into the multisectoral teams (in addition to having protection representation within the CRS structure), who conduct referrals and services specifically targeted for at the most vulnerable persons. CRS is an active member of the Protection cluster. CRS will conduct joint shelter-protection assessment visits including household surveys that focus on vulnerability criteria and other protection aspects to ensure the integration of these aspects into the design of the program activities. Assessment teams and participants will be notified of the CRS Code of Conduct. Where needed CRS will also establish referral, feedback and complaint mechanisms to complement activities under this project. The project will be implemented based on the Shelter Cluster Protection Mainstreaming Checklist approach and the principles of ensuring access to impartial assistance and services, especially for the most vulnerable and at-risk groups; accountability to beneficiaries; supporting the development of self-protection capacities; and ensuring "do not harm" by adapting activities to mitigate identified protection risks. Finally, during a monitoring visit, beneficiaries will be consulted on the level of satisfaction and will be informed to Shelter cluster, donors and local government.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The intervention is designed with a recognition of the complex relationships among men, women, girls and boys and the impact of the crisis on their respective roles and responsibilities. While women constitute 51% of the population in Mozambique, across all regions of the country, men possess more authority and power than women in communities and within the household. Women and girls in Cabo Delgado were vulnerable even before the conflict began. IDP families will be engaged in on-the-job training exercises with the intention to give guidance on the use of shelter materials and improved construction techniques. A community-based participatory approach is essential to create meaningfully involvement. The community’s leadership, but also other representatives of men, women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities will be included in the design of programming and assistance activities. They will be informed about the project deadline, the quantity of items including shelter topology of assistance also the quality of items to be distributed will be presented to the communities for approval and a sample will be left in the community for people to compare. Finally, during a monitoring visit, the beneficiaries will be asked about the level of satisfaction.CRS will also set up community complaint desks to ensure anonymous feedback can be received from the affected populations. CRS teams will conduct weekly visits to obtain the complaints and feedback and take action where needed. CRS also closely works with the Linha Verde 1458 complaints hotline to provide regular information and feedback.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People with disabilities will be prioritized during distributions. Distribution points will ensure access for community members, especially people with disabilities and women. If there are any people with disabilities and if they have some leadership character and ability to do some work, such as registering, organizing beneficiaries, writing reports, they can be included in the field team. For those who cannot walk, some transportation will be provided to transport their shelter kits.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The focus of the project is the repair of shelters, which aims to deliver tarpaulins and shelter fixing tools. The community will be trained and explained on how to keep the environment clean, avoiding littering in inappropriate places, as well as promoting the use of latrines to avoid infection of the environment. Avoiding digging big holes, throwing garbage anywhere and promoting debates to ensure the well-being of society as a whole.
