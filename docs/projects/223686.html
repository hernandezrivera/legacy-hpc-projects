Project Summary

The health component of the intervention seeks to deliver quality preventive and curative services, through improved access to basic lifesaving primary healthcare, mobile medical services, and referral services for vulnerable populations in the IDPs and host communities in the two LGAs.
This will be achieved through the provision of effective quality health care for the inpatients and outpatients, treatment of minor ailments, strengthening early warning for disease outbreaks, reproductive and maternal health services, counselling and psychosocial support, health education, community dialogues and sensitization, and the provision of essential medical drugs and supplies in the two LGAs. In addition, the project will conduct active case searches for Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and Cholera, while prepositioning essential Test Kits, Drugs and supplies to respond rapidly to outbreaks. Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials will be employed to raise awareness of gender-based violence (GBV) and prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), with referral systems established to connect affected women and girls to specialized GBV services.
The Nutrition interventions will focus on early detection of nutritional issues, skilled counseling on Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN), and the treatment of acute malnutrition. Key activities include expanding community-level screening, improving referrals to Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTPs) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programs (TSFPs), and providing treatment for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) in children under five. The initiative also seeks to advance MIYCN efforts to improve overall nutrition.
The project seeks to increase the availability of essential lifesaving health services to meet the current health needs of 32,500 vulnerable populations, especially Children Under-five, pregnant and lactating women. The project will enhance effective quality health care for the inpatients and outpatients, strengthen early warning for disease outbreaks, RMNCAH services, SAM/MAM treatment, Counselling, health education, and the provision of essential medical drugs and supplies in the two LGAs

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states in North-East Nigeria has reached a critical point, with the lives of over 230,000 children under the age of 5 hanging in the balance due to acute malnutrition during the lean season, which typically spans from May to September. The alarming condition is further exacerbated by the volatile economy, rising inflation, and soaring prices of basic food items, making it increasingly challenging for vulnerable populations to access essential nutrients. At least 16 local government areas in the BAY states are currently grappling with a critical nutrition situation, characterized by high rates of global acute malnutrition. The affected areas are witnessing a significant surge in cases of diarrhea, pneumonia, measles, and other diseases that not only increase nutrient losses but also alter metabolic demand and disrupt physiological functions in already malnourished patients. This perfect storm of malnutrition and disease is expected to lead to increased morbidity and mortality rates, particularly among children under 5. As the lean season reaches its peak, a staggering 1.5 million children are anticipated to be afflicted with acute malnutrition. Moreover, the number of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases is projected to increase by a daunting 54% from 2023 levels, further straining the already overwhelmed healthcare systems in the region. Bama is one of the local government areas where the situation is particularly dire. According to sentinel surveillance data, Bama is likely experiencing a critical nutrition situation, classified as IPC Phase 4, with global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate of 15.0-29.9%. The trend analysis of admissions from nutrition facilities across Bama reveals a disturbing increase in the number of severely and moderately malnourished children admitted into treatment facilities, with a 27% and 59% rise, respectively, compared to 2023. This uptick in malnutrition cases is particularly pronounced in inaccessible wards, where the lack of access to healthcare and nutrition services is exacerbating the crisis. The situation in BAY states demands urgent attention and collective action from humanitarian organizations, governments, and stakeholders to prevent a catastrophic outcome. It is essential to scale up nutrition interventions, provide emergency food assistance, and support healthcare systems to mitigate the devastating effects of acute malnutrition and related diseases. The international community must come together to address the root causes of this crisis, including the protracted conflict, displacement, and economic instability, to ensure the survival and well-being of the most vulnerable populations in North-East Nigeria.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The organisation maintains stringent adherence to established Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and Codes of Conduct, particularly in relation to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). All team members will undergo PSEA training and sign the DO NO HARM pledge and the organization's PSEA Policy. We will implement confidential reporting channels that allow individuals to report SEA-related complaints securely and without fear of reprisal and enhance follow-up. These channels will ensure that survivors and witnesses feel comfortable coming forward with their complaints.Also, there will be dedicated PSEA Focal Points that will trained on confidentiality protocols to ensure that survivors receive appropriate support and assistanceAccess to the reporting mechanisms will be facilitated through various channels, taking into consideration the diverse needs and barriers faced by affected populations. Information will be disseminated in multiple formats such as meetings, posters, etc
