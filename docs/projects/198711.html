Project Summary

This project focuses on food security and humanitarian protection for IDPs, host communities, and returnees in northeastern Nigeria, northern Cameroon, and western Chad. In all three countries, violence against local populations, conflict-related movement restrictions, and market disruptions, combined with climate shocks and negative macroeconomic developments, have led to food insecurity. In addition, sexual and gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection are two major issues for the majority of women, boys, and girls in project areas. Negative coping strategies such as child marriage, survival sex, and begging as a source of income are widespread and threaten the future prospects of the affected people.
The project "Multisectoral Resilience Strengthening of Vulnerable Households in Conflict-affected Regions of Northeast Nigeria and Northern Cameroon" aims to improve the availability, access, and use of food, the (re)development of agricultural, psychosocial, and other services, and water infrastructure. A particularly important component is psychological support for people burdened by displacement experiences or violence. Other measures reduce flood and erosion risks. In addition, the project is characterized by an explicit focus on strengthening female roles as well as inclusion and participation of different population groups with the aim of holistically strengthening social coexistence.
The target group of the project is particularly vulnerable mainly subsistence farming households in rural areas. The communities are composed of different status groups (displaced persons, returnees, host population), which are included in the measures in case of individual or household vulnerability. A special focus is put on the support of women and youth. The project follows a package approach in which households and communities benefit from several project activities simultaneously. In total, about 6,000 households with about 36,000 individuals in 35 rural villages are expected to benefit from the different components by the end of the project.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity usually affects the whole family, and parents often reduce their calorie intake in favor of their children, which weakens their ability to work. Malnutrition and health risks particularly affect pregnant and lactating women and young children. Because of the conflict situation and inadequately funded and sometimes dysfunctional government extension services for small farmers, the level of agricultural production is very low. In the event of minor shocks such as crop failures, there is a risk of negative coping strategies and malnutrition. The project aims to break this cycle through targeted increases in crop yields, improved nutrition and by expanding the marketing of agricultural products.In rural areas, access to water for many people, especially vulnerable populations, remains below the minimum standards defined by SPHERE (15l per capita per day). This has implications for drinking water consumption, personal hygiene, and the use of water for agriculture. Access to water is to be improved and agricultural productivity increased through the development of new sources or the rehabilitation of existing ones, as well as environmental protection measures that improve water management.As a result of the long-standing conflict, traumas suffered sometimes remain untreated for years. In addition, young women in particular, but also men, are at constant risk of becoming victims of gender-based violence. Therefore, the project focuses on psychosocial help for those affected. By dealing with the psychological stress factors, the affected people are to be personally strengthened and reintegrated into society.Through community structures, conflict drivers such as inter-religious and inter-generational tensions and escalating conflicts between farmers and pastoralists are to be reduced. Due to years of conflict around the communities, traditional problem-solving mechanisms are often no longer functional, but can be reactivated through targeted support.Resilience building focuses on the household level (increased production, environmental protection, improved distribution, improved access to land) and on the level of local organizational structures (strengthening of diverse community groups, community-run granaries, infrastructure maintenance committees). In addition, the psychosocial counseling and vocational training components provide targeted resilience support to particularly vulnerable individuals (women, youth, elderly, persons with disabilities). A combined resilience, gender and conflict analysis was carried out by the partner organizations with the support of national research institutes and the technical support of Prof. Dennis Dijkzeul (Ruhr University Bochum) (see Annex 5). Already in early 2021, DCV commissioned a conflict study in three regions of Cameroon, including the project region, from the research institute Observatoire PHAROS. In order to ensure a conflict-sensitive selection of beneficiaries for the respective packages of measures, a vulnerability survey of households will be conducted in the 35 target communities at the beginning of the project. The subsequent selection of beneficiary households will be based on context-specific criteria in consultation with representatives of the target group, but will focus on particularly vulnerable groups (returnees without capital, single women and mothers, unaccompanied youth or orphans, people with disabilities, ethnic and religious minorities).

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The focus of the project is on promoting long-term development potential in the area of agricultural production. In line with the HDP nexus, these are supplemented with elements of short-term intervention in acute crisis situations (water supply, psychosocial assistance) and peacebuilding measures (Outcome 4). A transition from short-term relief to development approaches is ensured primarily by the multisectorality of the project, which covers multiple needs and thus makes communities more resilient to crises and shocks. Increased resilience to periodic food crises results in permanently reduced humanitarian needs in the area of food security. By building up food reserves in community grain stores and within households, communities are enabled to draw on local resources in crisis situations, particularly reducing dependence on external support in crisis situations. Improved water supply will address other hu-manitarian needs. The provision of psychosocial services improves the mental resilience of individuals, enhancing their prospects of emerging independently from personal crises. Peacebuilding is a central component of the project. Appropriate local mechanisms and organizational structures at the community level are specifically promoted (see Outcome 4 and related measures and results), with respect for functioning mechanisms from the pre-crisis period. The central position of community structures in the selection of beneficiaries, the solution of community problems and involvement in various measures ensures a locally rooted promotion of social cohesion. Targeted inclusion of marginalized groups (women, youth, people with disabilities) reduces the risk of exclusion and recruitment by armed groups.
