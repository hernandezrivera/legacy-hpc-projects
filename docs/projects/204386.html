Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work in restoring food & nutrition security services and Cash Transfer to 29,000 affected vulnerable and pastoralist households in Bendasi, Wadi Salih & Zalingie in CD, Jebel Awlia & Karrari in KH, Ed-alfursan & Alsalam in SD, Kosti & Aljabalain & Tendalti in WN and Ag geneina & Kereneik in WD states that will contribute to the FSL sector objectives.
The main activities will be delivered by HOPE in this project are:
• Cash/Vouchers Based Transfer (Half Ration) in Kh, WN and WD
• Agricultural Support includes Distribution of assorted/improved agricultural seeds; Agricultural Mentorship/ Extension training using FFS approach; and Provision of vegetable seeds & irrigation pumps.
• Livestock Support includes Mobilization and support vaccination campaigns against prioritized diseases; and Provision of goats as restocking of dairy goats.
HOPE will focus on the targeting localities based on high levels of food insecurity identified through the IPC acute food insecurity analysis.
HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through fully community participation, strengthen the current community mechanism/ committee as well as do follow up for all benefit targeted separately to assess project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

Clashes between SAF and RSF have been ongoing since 15 April, particularly in and around residential areas in Khartoum, Darfur stats except East Darfur as well as a north part of West Kordofan state and capital city of North Kordofan. As of 13 May, the Federal Ministry of Health reports that at least 700 people have been killed and over 5,200 injured since the onset of the fighting. The number of casualties is likely to be higher, as many individuals were reportedly unable to reach hospitals, services facilities and markets due to movement constraints. Several cities have faced heavy bombardments and clashes, and associated unexploded ordnance, causing hundreds of civilian deaths and casualties, as well as damage to critical water, health care and power infrastructure. Looting and destruction of health facilities, markets and government institutions was the main feature in the Khartoum and Darfur states. Ongoing violence has caused extensive damage to infrastructure, leading to water shortages, blackouts, communication disruptions and incidents of looting. Large sections of the capital, including factories, banks, shops and markets, have been looted, damaged, or targeted by rocket attacks.Sudan especially the targeted states have been affected by local and regional conflicts, unemployment, displacement, poverty and under development. Many of the communities in these states are in conflict or on the verge of being drawn into conflicts. The huge pool of unemployment among the youth and women are found to be susceptible to these conflicts. In addition, these areas have been witnessing an influx of refugees besides IDPs and youth with conflict carrying capacity. There are very limited economic opportunities in the area, which further aggravates the situation. In addition, these conflicts affected nomadic routes thus putting additional pressure on natural resources, which have a propensity to further influence local conflicts. Acute food insecurity in Sudan is escalating rapidly as a result of sharp rises in the cost of food and other commodities, low harvest, and ongoing conflict crises. 3 out of 4 targeted states is highest anticipated percentage of residents in need of immediate assistance, West Darfur (42%), North Darfur (36%) and Central Darfur (35%) (FAO record, Oct 2022).Based on PiN – May 2023 by FSL sector, the FSL severity for 8 localities it is 4 or 3, the total People In Need (PIN) for 12 localities are 1,903,764, FSL overall target are 1,649,285. The PIN/ target disaggregated to 23% of men, 24% of women, 26% of boys, and 27% of girls.According to a rapid assessment conducted in Sep-Oct 2022 and updated on May 2023, the FSL needs are: (1) Cash transfer for people who are under fighting and no access to bank and work such as KH, SD, WN, CD and WD states, (2) Improved agricultural seeds to increase production of one Mukhamas and reducing the risk of diseases due to the use of local seeds repeatedly; (3) Increase the agriculture good practices by following the agricultural technologies; (4) Expand the cultivation of vegetables to reduce acute malnutrition; (5) Support vaccination campaigns against prioritized diseases to maintain the livestock assets; (6) Restocking of dairy goats to affected/most vulnerable households to reduce acute malnutrition; (7) improve revolving loans capacity of small business groups as financial good practices. Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for rearing food & nutrition security services and cash transfer.

2 - Activities/Outputs

• Cash/Vouchers Based Transfer (Half Ration):1. Cash Based transfer half ration to 15,000 people for 6 months in Kh, WN and WD states.• Agricultural Support:2. Distribution of 40 MT assorted/improved agricultural seeds as various cereal/oil to 4,000 HHs.3. Agricultural Mentorship/ Extension training for 16 FFS for small scale producers all of them women (complete agricultural cycle) according to FFS approach. 4. Provision of 800 kg of vegetable seeds & 4 irrigation pumps to 400 women.• Livestock Support:5. Mobilization and support vaccination campaigns against prioritized diseases to cover 200,000 different species of animals belonging to 8,000 HHs of affected IDPs and pastoralists (25 animals per each HH).6. Provision of 1,600 goats as restocking of dairy goats to 400 affected/most vulnerable households who have lost their livestock assets together with emergency animal feeding.• Livelihood Cash/Voucher Support:7. Unconditional cash transfer to 800 vulnerable HHs to enable them meet their immediate needs including food. 3 USD per day per HH for 30 days.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In recruitment process HOPE will continue to recruited project staff from targeted communities, the staff involved and fully dedicated to implement and follow up on. Project staff will be oriented on Code of conduct to avoid potential risks of violence, exploitation and abused of the beneficiaries of the project. The project will adopt community based approach that promotes the active engagement and participation of all community segments in design and implementation phases to ensure the community by-in and sense of ownership. HOPE team confirmed that the Community-based committee compositions considers balanced representation of gender, age, social status and minority to improve the decision making and allow the voices for these groups. Following the participatory and community based approaches, targeted communities will be involved in discussion, consultations and decision regarding planned activities. Through this, the project intends to ensure full participation and strengthen the ownership of communities. This will contribute to provide the target communities a concrete plate forum to identify their concerns, needs, challenges and needed actions to improve the protective environment. APP is core activity, additionally applying Do No Harm approach and principle to clearly done in this project through involvement and inclusion of the communities with equal access to direct assistance based on vulnerability criteria, as well as HOPE will include DO NO Harm in all training delivering to community-based committee.During design, HOPE assessed the safety, security and risk for target areas as well as types of GBV that facing women. During implementation, HOPE will support the capacities of committee members and assist to know their rights, concepts of GBV, Child Rights, Child Protection, DO NO Harm and PSEA. HOPE has put in place for better understanding among the beneficiaries for self-protection on various programmatic matters. HOPE also will develop a system to collect feedback from beneficiaries to measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

HOPE will consider the dignity and confidentiality of beneficiaries by using and follow Safety, Access Desegregated Date (SADD), in monitoring and data collection and will strongly prioritize the best interest of the beneficiary by following protocols and standards. As part of the PSEA capacity building, HOPE will provide training to all project team members on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and code of conduct. This will help to mitigate the risk of harassment, abuse and other violation to be committed by staff members against the beneficiaries. In addition, the project team will conduct regular consultation with community leaders/ members and local authorities and receive their feedback about the project delivered service through complaint mechanism. This will help to identify any negative consequences that might be caused by the intervention as well as the necessary corrective/mitigation action.Additionally, there are 4 actions to ensure accountability to affected people and conduct feedback mechanisms to beneficiaries: 1. Establish/ refreshment a community-based mechanism of Community Management Committee to involve community members in project activities with TOR of mechanisms.2. Explain the role of HOPE and community mechanisms related to each activity, regular meetings, reporting, feedback, etc.. these actions to consider accountability to affected people and ensure the sustainability of the project as well as promote community participation and sense of ownership. 3. For Community-based mechanism, HOPE will conduct regular feedback/ reports, recommendations, monitoring feedback, lessons learned, etc.. the aims to these actions to improve the quality of project results and ensure accountability.4. Establish Complaints Mechanism for Community Management Committee members and community beneficiaries.
