Project Summary

Based on ACF’s ability to deliver and the Government of Nigeria’s call for support for the provision of critical lifesaving support for affected populations, the project will continue the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) approach launched under the previous 2018 SIDA project. The project will focus on the provision of emergency rapid WASH and NFI assistance to the most affected population in Yobe and Borno state, in close coordination with humanitarian partners assisting to ensure activities are launched where there are existing gaps. The RRM will be activated for the provision of emergency WASH support in super camps in a scenario where existing partners are unable to provide critical lifesaving emergency support when there is a surge of new arrivals reported in the IDPs camps. In close coordination with the Government and the WASH sector-working group, the RRM will be deployed, integrating protection concerns and adopting a gender-sensitive approach to adequately determine the needs of the male, female, boys and girls.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in Northeast Nigeria continues to cause mass displacement and loss of human lives. There are an estimated 1,615,163 internally displaced people (IDPs) and 833,955 returnees in Borno and Yobe states of whom, 79% are women or children and 26% are children under six. An estimated 7.1 million out of a population of 13.1 million people require humanitarian assistance (UNOCHA, HRP, 2019). The displaced population resides in makeshift structures within overcrowded settlements, which have inadequate drainage systems and latrines making them prone to outbreak of water-borne diseases, especially during the rains and seasonal floods. According to the August 2019 Interagency Flood Assessment report, 10,490 shelters and 550 WASH facilities were damaged affecting 21,056 households in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states which include loss of food, agriculture, and livestock. IDPs will require long-term support to establish permanent latrines and sanitation structures. Under the humanitarian response plan, some 9.9 million people need WASH services. In Borno State, the withdrawal of security forces to consolidate and form super camps at key positions has left areas that were once accessible exposed to attacks by Non-state actors and groups (NSAG). The new security development is likely to cause an influx of IDPs from areas that are no longer under the protection of security forces. The number of IDPs in Borno has increased by 15,658 people and Maiduguri, that hosts the most IDPs, experienced a 7% increase of IDPs since the May 2017 IOM displacement tracking matrix (DTM ). In Borno state, the IOM Emergency DTM recorded the largest movement of 986 individuals on 10th September, which included departures from Magumeri, Monguno, Gubio, Nganzai, Diffa, Kondguga, Gubio, Bauchi, Magumeri, Geidam LGAs. Moreover, protection and gender concerns are likely to heighten as settlements get more crowded. According to the gender assessment conducted by CARE International, the SGBV against women and girls, has increased by 7.7% since the conflict began. It further reveals that sexual violence occurs at the toilet or latrines (21.8%), while travelling outside of the camp/community for wood collection (21%), at the water point (11.9%) and on way to/from schools (15%). These risks are predicted to heighten if super camps are established, increasing the number of people and competition of needs within settlements and camps.

2 - Activities

Activity 01:70,000 affected male, female, boys and girls will receive standard 10,000 NFI kits. • Registration of beneficiaries for standard NFI kits • Distribution of standard gender NFI kits distributed for man, women, boys and girls • Post distribution monitoring Activity 2: 70,000 affected man, women, boys and girls will receive standard 10,000 emergency shelter kits • Registration of beneficiaries for emergency shelter kits • Distribution of standard emergency shelter kits for man, women, boys and girls • Post distribution monitoring Description: A rapid assessment will be conducted during the first phase of emergency to identify the specific needs of the NFI (Kitchen items) and shelter of the affected population. Needs of most affected individuals such as women, children and separated girls will be identified and to reduce the risk of gender-based violence, women and girls with high needs will be prioritized for the distribution. Special consideration will be taken to support the separated girls and female-headed households with WASH hygiene kits to ensure that they received the basic items related to menstrual hygiene management. The hygiene kits will be distributed under the WASH sector.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure accountability to affected populations, Action Against hunger set up mechanisms to create awareness to beneficiaries. When the communities/camps were identified for support, the accountability team held community discussions to ensure communities/beneficiaries understand intended purpose of the project, rights beneficiaries as well as complaints/feedback mechanism put in place by the project. The team places banners showing access to the feedback mechanism in strategic locations in those camps or communities. The Accountability team also hold bi-monthly feedbacks days in camps/communities where the projects been implemented. Such feedback days are used as avenue to continuously sensitize the targeted project beneficiaries on their right and availability of the feedback mechanism. Toll free lines are clearly written on beneficiaries’ smart cards used for disbursement so that they can easily have access to the line when they have complaints or feedbacks regarding the projects. At all intervention sites, banners of complaints/feedback mechanism are compulsorily displayed as well as prices of supported food items. Complaints/Feedback Channels is mechanisms or channels for receiving complaints/feedbacks in the projects include the toll free line, complaint boots, help desk, community meetings and focus group discussions as well as staff field visit and post distribution Monitoring exercise. Complaint booth and help desk will developed to receive the beneficiaries face to face in the office and communities. PDM will be conducted to understand the satisfaction of beneficiaries on shelter and NFI kits.
