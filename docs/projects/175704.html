Project Summary

Although the formation of transitional government of national unity in February 2020 resulted in improved security however the subsequent shocks including COVID-19, flooding, inter-communal violence and political conflict in part of country continue to exacerbate the already fragile humanitarian context in South Sudan. The livelihood and food insecurity was prompted by years of conflict, climate shocks, wrecked economy and extreme poverty. According to the IPC situation analysis released on 18, December 2020, an estimated 6.35 million people (52.6% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worst acute food insecurity, over two million faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 24,000 people were likely in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). The projection for the lean session estimate that over 60% of population will likely to be in IPC Phase 3 or worse.
The proposed FSL interventions aim to improve food production, strengthen the household’s ability to enhance their productive assets, create livelihood opportunities, build resilience and contribute toward overall improved nutrition status of the affected communities. Concern will support vulnerable IDP, returnee and host community households with emergency access to agricultural and fishing inputs to improve food production and access to nutritious foods. In Aweil West and Aweil North, NBeG, Concern will continue to provide access to seeds, agriculture tools and crop production through block farming as well as technical support and training on seed multiplication and seed saving. Community Agricultural Extension Workers and lead farmers will be supported to train vulnerable farmers on Climate Smart Agriculture, bio-pesticide production and post-harvest handling. Concern will support dry season vegetable production to support income generation opportunities and access to nutritious foods. In Juba IDP camp and Bentiu PoC, Concern will build on its approach of nutrition gardens to support improving dietary diversity through training lead mothers on sack gardening where space is limited in IDP/PoC sites and they will train Mother-to-Mother Support Groups. Focus will be on households with members in OTP and TSFP. In Rubkona and Guit, Concern having supported vegetable gardens to improve access to nutritious foods will expand its FSL interventions considering the gaps in FSL support and most of the population being dependent on food assistance. Vegetable production and fishing will be supported to provide income generation opportunities and increase dietary diversity. Considering asset depletion, crop losses due to flooding, inflation and limited access to livelihood, hunger is expected to deepen due mainly to depleted food stocks and high food prices therefore Concern in partnership with WFP in NBeG continue to provide emergency food assistance through General Food Distribution, School Feeding Program and Cash/Food for Asset program.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in South Sudan has contributed to an extended humanitarian crisis compounded by high level of flooding and the COVID-19. The formation of transitional government of national unity (TGoNU) in February 2020 provided some hope in term of political stability and reduced conflict related incidents however, the overall progress on implementation of peace agreement remained slow with no progress on the critical area of security sector reform. The inter-communal violence has continued in many pockets of the country resulted in new displacement often many times and impeding humanitarian access. According to the Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO)-2020, 8.3 million people in South Sudan estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. The HNO estimate that about 7.7 million are in need of food security and livelihood assistance in 2021. According to the IPC situation analysis released on 18, December 2020, an estimated 6.35 million people (52.6% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worst acute food insecurity, over two million faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 24,000 people were likely in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). The IPC projection for the lean session (April to July 2021) estimate that over 60% of population will likely to be in IPC Phase 3 or worse. The most food insecure states are Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile, Lakes, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal where more than 50% of their respective populations are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Nonetheless, food prices remains high as insecurity, COVID-19 and inflation continues to affect the population. In proposed target areas of Concern interventions; IPC projections for April-July 2021 indicate emergency level of food insecurity in Rubkona County (Bentiu PoC) while Guit County is projected for crisis level. Central Equatoria is projected to face emergency level and Juba IDP camp in crisis level. Similarly both Aweil West and Aweil North in NBeG projected to face emergency level of food insecurity. The flooding in Aweil North and Aweil West in NBeG and across Unity impacted the crop production. Concern’s Rapid Crop Assessment survey conducted in November 2020 revealed that the hunger gap this year is going to be slightly more than three months. This is because of prolonged dry spell and erratic rainfall combined with the devastating impact of the flood crisis. Pests and disease infestation was also reported by the households interviewed as one of the causes of poor crop performance. The high levels of malnutrition are attributed to chronic food insecurity and poor diets. The HNO (2020) estimate that 1.9 million people (74% children and 26% PLW) are anticipated to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021. Out of 78 counties, 47 counties have a proxy prevalence of GAM above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent. The agriculture being the key source of livelihood of major portion of the population as much as 85% of the working population are engaged in non-wage work, mainly subsistence agriculture and livestock rearing. Therefore considering the high malnutrition, food insecurity, impact of year of conflict and flooding, the food security and livelihood intervention are crucial to support the affected population.

2 - Project activities

CO1: Provide food assistance In partnership with WFP, Concern will improve food security through increased food access and improve livelihoods through temporary employment and building of community assets. During the lean period, Concern will ensure food access through conditional cash transfers and food distributions to vulnerable populations. Temporary employment and asset creation will be provided through Cash for Assets (CFA) where vulnerable households will receive cash for construction on community-prioritized assets. Similarly, Concern in partnership with WFP in NBeG continue to provide emergency food assistance through General Food Distribution (including more hybrid rations in kind plus cash component as a pilot activity) and School Feeding ProgramCO2: Enhance emergency food productionEmergency access to agricultural inputs will be provided to vulnerable households who rely on agriculture as their main source of food and livelihoods to address the severe food insecurity. In Aweil West and Aweil North, NBeG, and Guit in Unity, targeted emergency seed distributions for both vegetable crops and main season cereals and legumes will be conducted among households whose means of livelihoods is agriculture but whose production levels are chronically low.Concern will continue to provide support on vegetable growing and kitchen gardening activities in Aweil West and Aweil North, Bentiu PoC, and Juba IDP camp. Vegetables provided will be high in nutrition including okra, eggplant, pumpkin, onion, tomatoes, and amaranths, which will be provided depending on beneficiaries’ seed preferences. Targeted beneficiaries will be encouraged to practice kitchen gardening at their homesteads as it provides available low-cost vegetables to meet dietary needs of households and improve livelihood by selling surplus produce. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to identify and allocate suitable land for vegetable production. For those living along rivers, especially the Rivers Kiir and Lol, fishing has the potential to be a significant form of livelihoods. Concern will target vulnerable fishing households in NBeG and in Guit with fishing nets, hooks and fishing line. Furthermore, Concern staff will train targeted households on practical post-harvest management approaches.CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs The intervention shall continue working with Community-Based Agricultural Extension Workers (CBAEW) to extend services aimed at promoting Climate Smart Agriculture practices such as Conservation Agriculture, Good Agronomic Practices in vegetable production, and increased crop diversification for both improved household incomes and nutrition as well as strengthen household’s ability to absorb shocks particularly related to changing weather pattern. The lead farmers, drawn from well-functioning established cereal banking and VSLAs shall be identified and engaged in the seed multiplication and seed saving training exercise. Concern will identify lead farmers in the community to support other farmers in their locality. Concern will train beneficiaries on the farmer group approach, establishment of demonstration plots, farmer-to-farmer learning, and on mass agricultural campaigns. Concern will conduct refresher trainings to County Agriculture Department Officers and CBAEWs. The training is aimed at reinforcing skills and knowledge so that government extension staff and extension workers can reach out to farmers with properly structured and quality agriculture extension messaging over a sustained period of time. This will support the program’s sustainability strategy.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitor are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, Concern informs them about their right to assistance, entitlements, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. In PoC sites Concern refers complaint to the camp manager and protection concerns to the Protection partner. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns which are referred to Protection partners. All recruitments of staff and trainings ensure that there is equal access to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Monitoring will collect data on any information on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understand different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at distribution points/beneficiaries registration points will be used to avoid exclusion of access as for as possible considering the operational challenges in target locations. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through their local representative and relatives. Concern will maintain a help and feedback desk at distribution points. In addition, during monitoring and support visits FGDs will be conducted separately with women and men to seek feedback and provide opportunity to discuss any concern/issues or complaint.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Prior to the initiating project activities and delivery of assistance, Concern will conduct an inception meeting with the target beneficiaries and stakeholders to inform them about the project scope, target criteria and their entitlements. Similarly, during the inception meetings and during regular consultations with the community, Concern will inform the beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. Depending on type of assistance, context and target population profile, Concern ensure that the banners with information on complaint channel and feedback mechanism are displayed broadly in the target areas and at the distribution points.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Concern engage with the target community and relevant state authorities during the needs assessment, project design and monitoring to seek their feedback and adapt project assistance/delivery accordingly. The broader consultations with the community during the assessment will inform about the project design, priority activities and modality of assistance. The project/villages committees including most vulnerable such as women, elderly, disable will be formed at inception stage to engage in project delivery and monitoring. The project/villages committees from target beneficiaries share feedback to adapt the assistance accordingly. The regular monitoring and quarterly reviews will collate the feedback, lessons and inform about necessary adaptation to project assistance. Similarly, the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly. Concern conduct project end-line survey to review the progress on project outcome and seek feedback from future improvements. The lesson will be document and shared with cluster and other relevant stakeholders

8.11. Mitigation Measures

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. The interventions will prioritize gender and protection mainstreaming, and ensure inclusion of older people and PWDs based on our experience and learnings from previous programs. Concern will use the Gender-Age Marker toolkit as well as protection guidelines during the assessments, ongoing monitoring, and evaluations of projects. The project targeting criteria and entitlements will be shared with the community and Concern will ensure that the program priorities are always identified by the affected community, beneficiary selection criteria are always inclusive, and the community is always involved in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Prior to distributions, Concern will consult with communities to identify distribution sites to ensure they are in safe locations such as churches, schools and health facilities and close to beneficiaries to minimise travel times. Concern will conduct sensitisations on risk reduction measures before distributions such as travelling to sites in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Dates of distributions and fairs will be on a “need to know” basis to minimise information circulating. Help desks will be used to identify and refer protection cases to Protection partners and to adapt the programme to minimize further risks. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in program to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.
