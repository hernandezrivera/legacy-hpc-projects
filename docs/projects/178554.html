Project Summary

This project aims to enhance the living conditions of vulnerable groups thorough providing a comprehensive Shelter and NFI services and through upgrading of IDPs sites infrastructure reaching for 69,970 individuals in vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities through the following:
1- Installing of water-rain drains in 5 IDPs sites affected by floods in A’zaz, Aghtarin, Suran, Ghandourah, Jarablus and Albab sub-dsitricts. During the first quarter of 2021, Shafak completed the installation of 2 water-rain drains.
2- Upgrading infrastructure in 20 IDP sites affected by floods through levelling and gravelling of main, sub and access roads (alleys between tents) of the sites and through providing tents in needs with ground insulation supporting the IDPs to mitigate the effects of floods. Targeted beneficiaries number is 26,620 individual IDPs in 20 sites are located in Dana, Maret Tamsrin, Armanaz, Quorqeena, Idleb, Janudiyeh, Badama sub-districts in Idleb governorate and A’zaz, Aghtarin, Suran, Ghandourah, Jarablus and Albab sub-districts in Aleppo governorate. During the first quarter of 2021, Shafak completed the upgrading activities in 9 IDPs sites, provided 800 tents with ground insulation and will launch the work for 6 other sites starting from July 2021.
3- Replacement and installation of 400 tents torn out by floods and storms and provide them with internal, external and ground isolation in the targeted 20 sites in Dana, Maret Tamsrin, Armanaz, Quorqina, Idleb, Janudiyeh, Badama sub-districts in Idleb governorate and A’zaz, Aghtarin, Suran, Ghandourah, Jarablus and Albab sub-districts in Aleppo governorate.
4- Rehabilitation of emergency shelter spaces (5 collective centers with 100 HHs in Suran, Aghtarin and Maret Tamsrin sub-districts; 150 unfinished buildings in Dana and Maret Tamsrin sub districts, and 250 damaged houses with medium and light rehabilitation in Bennesh, ldleb and Aghtarin sub districts. Shafak will work on upgrading and rehabilitation of 150 unfinished houses and 3 collective centers during second half of 2021.
5- Distribution of 300 shelter kits for 1500 IDP individuals and conduct rapid training on NFI and shelter skills for 350 individuals of the same kits beneficiaries. The kits will be distributed in camps and off-camps locations for the IDPs to support them to take part in the rehabilitation process and upgrading of their shelters.
6- Construction\Installation of 100 RHUs for IDPs in A’zaz an Albab districts.
7- Distribution of 5,000 new arrival kits/NFIs for 5,000 IDPs households (25,000 individuals disaggregated by gender: 5405 men, 6345 women, 7022 girls, and 6228 boys ) in Afrin, Jandaris, Raju, Albab, A'zaz, Jarablus, Ghandoura, Suran, Aghtarin, Armanaz - Idlib - Sarmin - Bennesh - Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts. During first and second quarters of 2021, Shafak completed the distribution of 4000 kits.
8- Distribution of 3000 winterization kits/vouchers to 3000 HHs (60% IDPs, 20% returnees and 20% host)/ 15,000 individuals disaggregated by gender (3243 men, 3807 women, 4213 girls and 3737 boys in Afrin, Jandaris, Raju, Albab, A'zaz, Jarablus, Ghandoura, Suran, Aghtarin, Armanaz - Idlib - Sarmin - Bennesh - Maaret Tamsirin sub-districts. During first quarter of 2021, Shafak completed the distribution of 3000 vouchers and might be able to restart another rounds of distribution of 1000 vouchers.
9- Provide 500 IDP HHs with rental assistance for the site in the case of private lands
site. The assistance will be cash vouchers ($ 100 USD per voucher)
10- Train 350 IDPs ( youth, women and men) on tents and roads maintenance and shelter rehabilitation and link them with Cash for work activities.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, over seven million people are highly dependent on humanitarian WASH assistance, including 1.9 million people living in 1,116 IDP last resort sites that require comprehensive and continued WASH assistance to survive in Idleb and Aleppo Governorate in Aleppo the number of displaced people increased significantly, with many IDPs forced to seek shelter in planned camps, informal camps or settlements, transit/reception centres, and collective centres. The vast majority of these IDPs resides in informal settlements where shelter and WASH conditions as well as access to basic services, including health and protection, are inadequate or absent. Shafak conducted a rapid need assessment (multi-sectorial assessment) where 1031 households in 18 sub-districts participated and were interviewed. Major problems of shelter are ranked as the following: Shelters overcrowded (22%), shelters are not protective against weather conditions (19%), shelters do not have enough space between each other’s, and shelters are not enough available (16%). Incidents continue to impact IDP sites, exacerbating existing challenges and vulnerabilities, such as lack of proper infrastructure or site planning. In 2020, the CCCM Cluster in North-west Syria reported 101 incidents in 152 sites, that happened mainly from fires (79 incidents) and floods (12 incidents). 2,278 shelters were destroyed, and 2,761 shelters were partially damaged. According to Shafak above mentioned assessment, the specific shelter interventions are most urgently required are; 93% for cash assistance, 66% for shelter rehabilitation, 43% for electricity/lighting, 22% for temporary shelters/tents and 20% for shelter replacement.

5 - Implementation of activities

Shafak will implement the proposed activities according to its SOPs and guidelines based on the SNFI guidelines and best practices. Shafak will do -Needs assessment to determine the targeted sites and communities for the planned activities,–Community mobilization sessions for the IDPs and host communities explaining the criteria for selection of camps and communities. Site analysis and mapping overview (when needed for the IDP sites). Technical assessment and preparing BoQs and site analysis. – Market assessment for rehabilitation and infrastructure items- Contracting with service providers to implement the maintenance activities -Conduct upgrading activities in camps locations: 1) maintenance and rehabilitation of rain-drain systems. 2)gravelling of main and sub-roads (gravelling 120,000 m2) in the site to improve IDPs access to health and WASH services and provide the tents (estimated # is 2500 tents) at the targeted camps with ground insulation 3)tent replacement and installation of 400 tents. In off-camps locations, the rehabilitation activities will include: 1)Rehabilitation of 5 collective centers (100 HHs), 2)Upgrading of 150 unfinished houses 3)Light and medium Rehabilitation of 250 damaged houses, 4)Installation of 100 RHUs. Shared activities will take place between Off-camps and camps like distribution of shelter kits (300 kits) and training of 350 individuals on rehabilitation, core NFI activities and focus would be on integration of skills from both camps and off-camps. Distribution of NFIs and winterization would be major activities through mobilization of the communities, rapid needs assessment, situation monitoring, door-to-door registration, redemption & monitoring of of vouchers and kits distribution.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk (1): Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), including harassment, against women and girls by cash for workers, male beneficiaries while registering for the services or attending the training. Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - recruit male and female volunteers who are respected in the community and independent of armed groups. No children to be employed (under 18 years old) - make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA - set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children Risk (2): Marginalization of most vulnerable groups like disable and women during project design and implementation. Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: Set a fixed quota for these groups to be involved in the camps committees, ensure that field teams have female staff- ensure that mobilization sessions for the project include all categories. Risk (3): Difficult access for women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities (PWD) Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - Register all adult women, unaccompanied Register all adult women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities as main recipients of services- During post-distribution follow-up, understand how these groups feedback on the process and materials Risk (4): Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures. Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: Involve the key leaders in the community to endorse and motivate women engagement in project activities

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1st Risk Eviction from shelters units, camps, transit sites, spontaneous settlement due to lack of approval from property owner/conflict in property ownership. Likelihood: medium. Mitigation: Ensure that no launch of site improvement activities takes place without obtaining the approval from the land owner or the stakeholders, Include HLP documentation into the MoUs or agreements signed, Work closely with population at risk of eviction together with other sectors and local authorities to find solutions and clearly communicate the options available.2nd Risk: Fluctuation of infrastructure materials prices, Likelihood: high Mitigation: Sign a fixed framework agreement with the contractors, conduct market assessment of regular basis for monitoring the prices.3rd Risk: Increased risk of inter or intra-community tension due to varied assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to a feeling of discrimination. Likelihood: High. Mitigation: Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Conduct mobilization sessions about the project scope with clear unformed criteria of selection, Highly coordinate with community key representatives and leader.4th Risk: Misuse of distributed cash for rent by the IDP HHs due to other priority needs like food. Likelihood: High Mitigation: Ensure that selection of IDPs is based on verification of their needs and vulnerability, coordinate with other humanitarian actors to provide needed support for the targeted IDPs.
