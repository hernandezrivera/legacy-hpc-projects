Project Summary

NP's overall objective is: To effectively respond, prevent and reduce protection concerns of conflict affected communities in South Sudan.
This objective will contribute to HRP SO1: Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys, SO2: Protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys through provision of specialized and integrated services and SO3: Support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks.
The activities are designed to increase the safety and security of civilians and saving lives of civilians affected by violent conflict through risk mitigation, deterrence, protection through presence, strengthening local protection mechanisms, community based psychosocial support and protection mainstreaming. Project activities are implemented in both static locations, as well as through mobile response missions that allow protection officers to respond to the most urgent needs throughout the country. Specific and unique needs of women and men are incorporated into all interventions.

Needs

Since July 2016 conflict between opposition and government forces in Juba led to renewed conflict and violence across many parts of the country resulting in continued instability, restricted humanitarian space and access and population displacement. This has contributed to the prolonged suffering of many South Sudanese. As of April 2018, there were a reported 1,76 million IDPs and further 2,45 million refugees in neighboring countries. Long-term political instability and a lack of accountability has led to the failure of state-mandated institutions to protect civilians making the situation a protection crisis. The deliberate targeting of civilians, widespread human rights abuses, forced recruitment and the destruction of civilian property and infrastructure are urgent protection issues. Acts of violence perpetrated by armed actors against civilians, including GBV, have reached a critical and devastating level. To address the urgent protection needs facing many civilians across South Sudan, this project will adopt a participatory approach which seeks to 1. Ensure the immediate safety and security of local populations, 2. Support response efforts for those affected by violence, and to 3. Strengthen the protective and adaptive capacity of communities through community engagement, trainings and mentorship. This approach will help address immediate protection concerns of each beneficiary population while also providing support to lessen the impacts resulting from violence and abuse. Capacity building and community engagement will seek to build up and/or empower the protection mechanisms which have been significantly weakened by the conflict. NP interventions in 2019 will include the following states and counties: Jonglei (Nyirol); Western Equatoria (Yambio, Mundri East & West); NBeG (Aweil East reaching Aweil North and Aweil South); Unity (Rubkona, Mayom, Guit, Koch, Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar); WBeG (Raja, Wau); Upper Nile (Ulang; Fashoda); Eastern Equatoria (Torit, Kapoeta South); Central Equatoria (Juba); and Lakes (Rumbek East & Centre, Cueibet). Mobile response missions will be conducted in Southern Unity (Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Paniyjiar) in order to meet urgent protection needs. In addition, dedicated mobile response teams are deployed to areas, which have been identified by the protection cluster as hotspots with urgent needs.Initial project assessments and earlier projects inform our estimation of the expected number of beneficiaries. Direct beneficiaries are the direct participants in NP activities: GBV workshops, awareness raising events, protective accompaniments, and similar protection activities. Indirect beneficiaries are counted as those who are living in zone of influence of the project and who benefit as a result of improvements made to the direct beneficiaries.Areas of disrupted infrastructure with repeated waves of displacement, such as Upper Nile, Jonglei and Southern Unity, require increased coordination mechanisms and relationship building. In addition to regular interaction with other protection partners, NP will build bilateral relationships with national and international partners. Physical access will always be a challenge for remote communities, and NP will endeavour to utilize static and semi-static bases to cover a broad area. To avoid duplication, NP will continue doing service mapping and work with deep field coordinators. In addition, NP will ensure and increase coordination with national and international actors and stakeholders on the state and national level.

Activities

1. Direct Protection, Prevention and Risk Mitigation for Civilians at Imminent Risk (Links to HRP Obj 1, 2 and 3) o Direct protection for civilians vulnerable to violence, including protective presence, accompaniment and patrolling in insecure areas to deter direct attacks on civilians. There will be a particular focus on women/young women who are accessing aid or other resources to address the basic needs of their families/communities: accompanying women and girls when they travel to markets or to collect firewood.o Identify civilians at risk of recruitment and implement protection strategies to prevent recruitment by armed groups.o Carry out regular assessments to identify high-risk areas and factors exposing civilians to conflict and violence. Assessment reports will be shared with the cluster and sub-cluster coordinators.o Conduct conflict mitigation and dialogue facilitation activities to reduce tensions and prevent violence in communitieso Engage in timely and effective rumor control to prevent unnecessary displacement and reduce communal tensions 2. Improve Protection Response Services and Community-based Psychosocial Support (Links to HRP Obj 1, 2 and 3)o Strengthen/Support the establishment and utilization of referral pathways to medical, psychosocial and aid services.o Provide safe spaces for psychosocial support and community dialogueo Conduct protection workshops for law enforcement, judiciary, traditional and local leadershipo Facilitate referrals and support safe access to services for individuals requiring individual protection assistance 3. Improve the Protective Capacities of Communities (Links to HRP Obj 2 and 3)o Conduct early warning and early response assessments and trainings in high-risk communitieso Facilitate monthly community security meetings between beneficiaries, security actors and humanitarian actorso Conduct capacity building workshops for community members to strengthen local capacities for violence prevention, protection and responseo Conduct advocacy and awareness-raising campaigns on pertinent protection issues with all community memberso Develop, train and mentor community-based protection mechanisms and/or Women's Peacekeeping Teams in all sites to facilitate community participation in peace and security. Coach teams to carry out protection identification and response activities 4. Enhance partner protection mainstreaming practices (Links to HRP Obj 1 and 2)o Provide on-the-ground protection support prior to, during and immediately following aid distributions. This includes working with agencies prior to the distribution to ensure that the layout of the distribution is set up in such a way as to minimize the risk of violence as much as possible; intervening to de- escalate tensions, prevent violence and identify vulnerable individualso Train agencies on practical protection mainstreaming tools so that they can independently implement the measures described in the previous activity. All activities are focused on general protection, but contain elements of child safeguarding and GBV prevention & response. NP will ensure to coordinate with the CP & GBV sub-cluster focal persons in all areas of operation and provide monthly 5Ws.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

In addition to local authorities and community leaders, NP frequently consults with communities through different means, such as community meetings, as part of our trainings and workshops, and engagements during our direct protective measures. Furthermore, we coordinate with other aid agencies and the protection cluster to identify needs and gaps. All this information ultimately informs our project design.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Informal and formal feedback mechanisms exist. At the end of every capacity building event, we ask and encourage beneficiaries to provide us with feedback on our services. In addition, we are currently preparing best practices with Women Protection Teams, which will include a community feedback mechanism between communities, WPTs and NP staff. Once finalized, the best practices will be implemented at all field locations.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, several of our activities directly address these threats, such as:- Direct protection measures, such patrolling- Protective accompaniment- Conflict mediation and dialogue facilitation- Provide on-the-ground protection support prior to, during and immediately following aid distributions. This includes working with agencies prior to the distribution of material aid, such as food and livelihoods to ensure that the layout of the distribution is set up in such a way as to minimize the risk of violence as much as possible; intervening to de-escalate tensions, prevent violence and identify vulnerable individuals . In addition to protection mainstreaming activities, NP continues to build bilateral relationships with partners across our various field sites and sectors. All humanitarian agencies have a commitment to the centrality of protection and NP will continue sharing context information, concerns of beneficiary populations and analysis of protection concerns related to the delivery of materials and services.
