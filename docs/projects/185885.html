Project Summary

The crisis in the South-West and North-West Regions of Cameroon has compounded pre-existing vulnerabilities. Since 2016, political and social instability, exacerbated by sporadic violence, has had a negative impact on the civilian population of Cameroon’s South-West and NorthWest Regions, hosting four million inhabitants (16% of the total population). In November 2017, the sociopolitical crisis progressively translated into insecurity and armed violence. Since then, the escalation of tension and upsurge in hostilities between non-state armed groups and defense and security forces have triggered humanitarian needs across the two regions, linked to significant internal displacement. The destructio of food crops, water supplies, health services, and subsequent large displacement of families and communities takes a heavy toll on children and women especially. Even greater numbers have died from malnutrition and disease during this period in the SW and NW Regions of Cameroon.
To assist conflict-affected populations in western Cameroon, FFP enables WFP to distribute in-kind food rations to IDPs and host community members in Northwest and Southwest with the use emergency food vouchers and essential relief commodities to vulnerable people in affected Regions.
However, it is worth noting that Lebialem Divisions is one of the areas that have been hardest hit by the crisis. Since the coming of the Humanitarian Response Plan in 2019, Lebialem has hardly received assistance due to the high level of insecurity and enclave nature of the area. Even so various cluster reports show that there are no interventions in this area because development agents are unable to penetrate the area due to insecurity.
The situation is further complicated by the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic. More cases of COVID-19 are being recorded as community transmission grows in the regions. The propagation of the virus in communities has forced humanitarian actors to restrict movements and activities pending the adoption of cluster implementation strategies and SOPs to mitigate risks from COVID-19
This project aims at supporting 1000 IDPs and Returnees to rehabilitate their potatoes and backyard livestock farms for income generation and improve living conditions of inhabitants and encourage more returnees.
Project Objective
General objective: To support resilience and economic recovery for vulnerable IDPs and Returnees in Alou and Wabane subdivisions, Lebialem Division through modern Irish potato and pig farming.
Specific Objectives
- Increased revenue from Irish potato and pig farming in the project target area for at least 70% of IPDs/Returnees reached.
- Increased knowledge and practice of the COVID-19 prevention measures
Project Output
- Two sub divisions in Lebialem mobilized and 1000IDPs/Returnees selected for the project.
- 18 x2 day capacity building on potato farming organized for 50 participants each from target communities.
- 6x2day capacity building on modern pig farming organized for at least 15 participants each.
- 900 IDPs/Returnees trained on modern potatoes farming in Alou and Wabane subdivisions.
- 100 IDPs/Returnees trained on modern pig farming in the project target communities.
- 1100 COVID-19 prevention kits distributed to project clients, community relays and NADEV staff.
- 900 clients receive potatoes farm input (fertilizer, herbicides, sprayers, seeds, hoes and cutlasses)
- 100 pig farmers receive farm input for pig farming.
Project Outcomes
-
Indicator: At least 98% of clients reached are engaged and making income from potatoes and pig production activities in Alou and Wabane subdivisions by the end of 2023

1 - Besoins / Needs

The crisis in the South-West and North-West Regions of Cameroon has compounded pre-existing vulnerabilities. Since 2016, political and social instability, exacerbated by sporadic violence, has had a negative impact on the civilian population of Cameroon’s South-West and North West Regions, hosting four million inhabitants (16% of the total population). In November 2017, the sociopolitical crisis progressively translated into insecurity and armed violence. Since then, the escalation of tension and upsurge in hostilities between non-state armed groups and defense and security forces have triggered humanitarian needs across the two regions, linked to significant internal displacement.Thousands of people have been killed, three times as many have been seriously injured or permanently disabled and countless others have witnessed or taken part in violent acts. The destruction of food crops, water supplies, health services, and subsequent large displacement of families and communities takes a heavy toll on children and women especially. Even greater numbers have died from malnutrition and disease during this period in the SW and NW Regions of Cameroon. Overall, 3.9 million people in the country require emergency assistance to meet their basic needs, according to the UN. In 2019The rapid Emergency Food Security and nutrition Assessment (EFSA) and Mobile Vulnerability Assessment and Mapping (MVAM) conducted by WFP revealed that 1.5 million people in SW and NW are food insecure. To assist conflict-affected populations in western Cameroon, FFP enables WFP to distribute in-kind food rations to IDPs and host community members in Northwest and Southwest. FFP also jointly supports an NGO with USAID’s Office of U.S. Foreign Disaster Assistance to provide emergency food vouchers and essential relief commodities to vulnerable people in Northwest and South West. However, it is worth noting that Lebialem Divisions is one of the areas that have been hardest hit by the crisis. Since the coming of the Humanitarian Response Plan in 2019, Lebialem has hardly received assistance due to the high level of insecurity and enclave nature of the area. Clashes between non-state armed groups and defense and security forces have displaced the civilian population of Lebialem into the surrounding forests and villages – 80% of the displaced populations have found refuge in the forest. Even so various cluster reports show that there are no interventions in this area because development agents are unable to penetrate the area due to insecurity. The situation is further complicated by the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic. More cases of COVID-19 are being recorded as community transmission grows in the regions.
