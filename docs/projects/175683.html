Project Summary

Since the end of 2019, the food security situation has generally deteriorated across the greater Upper Nile region hitting particularly Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity States. According to the last IPC report performed in November 2020, an estimated of 6.35 million people (representing 52% of the population) face crises or worse acute food insecurity. These numbers are only expected to increase throughout the first semester of 2021, especially during the lean season where between April and July 7,24 million people are expected to be in IPC phases 3,4 or 5, with at least 31,000 people likely to be in catastrophe acute food insecurity.
In 2020, INTERSOS has been presented in several of the most in need counties including Akobo, Ayod, Nyriol (Jonglei State) and has reopened its operation in Pibor at the beginning of 2021, through multi-sectoral interventions targeting the needs of the most vulnerable individuals, particularly women, People with disabilities and elderly, through protection projects (protection monitoring, gender-based-violence and Child protection) integrating components of food security, education and NFI/shelter.
This project aims to build on the already strong relationship INTERSOS has with the communities in said locations, all of them considered in IPC 5 or 4 by the last IPC report, and increase their access to immediate food security through the distribution of food both in in-kind and in cash, as well as the provision livelihood support in the form of vegetable, crops and fishing kit distributions as well as trainings aimed at enhancing food production.

1 - Needs

The multiple shocks the country faced in 2020, including unprecedented floods, increased inter-community violence and the economic impact of COVID19, will leave alarming numbers by the peak of the lean season in 2021 with 3,47 million people facing crise of worse acute food insecurity in Greater Upper Nile. The situation has even reached the point where Pibor had by November 2020 already 11,000 in catastrophe stage, a number expected to increase throughout 2021 with famine pockets emerging in other counties like Akobo during the lean season. This is mainly caused by the intensification of conflict that resulted in massive displacement and the exceptional floods that disrupted markets and the delivery of humanitarian assistance. According to the need’s assessment performed by INTERSOS in January 2021 in Akobo, it is estimated that 90% of households depend on general food distributions, and in the last months, 50% of households in Denjock Payam and up to 95% in Nyandit Payam have had to reduce the number of meals they eat in a day. The situation is similar in Ayod, where until January 2021 flood displaced people coming from Gorwait, and other neighboring payams were still arriving to Ayod town due to the floods and the disruption of livelihood activities. This has increased the demand of Ayod town market, already in severe distress to meet the demands of the population. In Mayendit county unity state the disruption of markets relates mainly to the inaccessibility of the area, since even until January 2021 an important part of the payams remain under water with canoe as only mean of access. Market functionality remains a challenge with for example the market in Canal town, in Canal/Pigi county still partially under water at the beginning of 2021. During the assessment conducted, it was estimated that 50% of cattle has died since the beginning of the floods and that 25% of households eat wild fruits on a regular basis to meet their dietary needs. The situation is particularly difficult for returnees coming back from neighboring Malakal since they are not registered yet for the GFD and lack sustainable livelihood activities. In Nyriol county the floods also disrupted the markets and livelihoods of a significant number of the population, the last IPC reporting that over 25% of households meet over 50% of caloric needs from humanitarian assistance. Additionally, the needs assessment of January 2021 showed 53% of households were estimated to have a food stock that would last less than a week, 40% of households would go entire days without eating. In overall the food security situation by the end of 2020 remains fragile with the likelihood of degrading by the lean season of 2021. To reduce this insecurity, immediate food assistance and strong support through the lean season should be complemented with emergency livelihood activities that will enhance food production in the area. The last needs assessment has showed that the most common livelihoods are agriculture, livestock and cattle rearing, and fishing. Additionally, for most of the counties access to land is not a major issue. The needs assessment also showed the food insecurity impacts disproportionally women. This is caused by the fact in over 90% of households assessed, the woman is the last one to eat in the house after the children and the men of the family. The food security gender analysis also showed the main livelihood activities mentioned above are ensured traditionally by men, leaving child headed and women headed households in an even more vulnerable situations and forced to live from other livelihood activities such as selling firewood and charcoal in the market, activities particularly dangerous that forced to leave the villages to collect the items to sell and exposes them to GBV risks, while still having a reduced income. This income inequality strengthens gender disparity, shown for example by the fact men are the only ones deciding how the money is spent.

2 - Project activities

The main objective of the intervention is to reduce the degradation of the food security situation in the target counties by ensuring most vulnerable households have access to food throughout the lean seasons while at the same time strengthening and protecting their livelihood activities until the harvest period. The project will therefore have two components: (1) the first of immediate food assistance the (2) second of emergency livelihood support. The main activities will include:1.1 Monthly market analysis aimed at determining functionality (stock, prices, and quality of food items). This activity will allow for a close monitoring of the local food availability while also informing the decision on whether to provide food assistance in in-kind or cash. 1.2 Identification of most vulnerable household including child /women headed households, PWD, elderly and other households not registered to the blank distribution including newly arrived IDPs and returnee households. This activity will aim to ensure most vulnerable households are registered and have access to food assistance.1.3 General food assistance throughout the lean season (April-July) through in-kind distribution for counties with dysfunctional markets and unrestricted unconditional cash distribution for counties with functional markets. Distributions will be carried out on a monthly basis and will amount up to 156 USD dollars (figures to be updated through the above-mentioned market analysis and the price of the food minimum expenditure basket in each location). The set-up of distribution sites will include the establishment of COVID19 social distancing measures and the mainstreaming of protection principles to ensure significant and safe access to the services. 1.4 Emergency rapid response kit (fish kit/cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed) Each kit will follow cluster standard regulations and will be delivered based on pre-established vulnerability criteria. Particularly vulnerable households, including households with pregnant women and children in the first 1,000 days of life will be prioritized along with PWD, elderly and child headed households. 1.5 Post Distribution monitoring aimed at evaluating the positive impact of food distributions on food security status of the beneficiaries and gathering inputs on how the project rationale and methodology can be improved and made more effective. 2.1 Distribution of emergency livelihood kits (livestock, fishing, vegetable & crops). This activity includes the selection of beneficiary households based on the above-mentioned criteria as well as household capacity to perform the livelihood activity in a sustainable way based on individual assessment and FGD performed at community level. 2.2 Livelihood training. The main topics will be the following ones: how to set-up and / or strengthen the agricultural / livestock / other income generating activities; how to plan and manage activities; how to have access to the market and how to make trade more profitable technically and financially. Detailed plans will be developed at the beginning of the project, according to decisions made through activity 2.1 and by taking average competences of already identified beneficiaries into account. 2.3 Training of farmers on crop and livestock disease control through establishment of community surveillance and early warning mechanisms. 2.4 Livestock vaccination and treatment.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project will have full dedicated activity to market analysis in all locations to determine market functionality before determining if cash can be used. The functionality of the market will be assessed against established criteria that will include among other: physical accessibility -specially during rainy season-, stock of food items and prices.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash will be prioritized whenever possible to support local economy and support tenders in their livelihood activities. Additionally, internal evaluations performed in Jonglei state have showed vulnerable households prefer cash instead of income since it allows them to better decide the priorities of the household and promotes dietary diversity. Additionally, cash distribution has been considered safer for households with vulnerabilities by being a more discrete way of assistance and hence reducing the risk of being robbed. Finally, cash assistance also allowed households to ensure minimum access to food for longer periods of time with cash rather than in-kind by allowing them to procure food at different intervals.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

For complaints related to PSEA and CP policy issues, two members of each CRM committee (one men and one women) will be selected by the community as PSEA / CP focal points. They will receive specific sensitivity training and direct communication line (phone and airtime) with the PSEA focal point in mission in Juba. The focal point will then follow INTERSOS PSEA protocols and liaise directly with the internal audit.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Each CRM committee will have one representative of the most vulnerable groups of the community including among others PWD, elderly, youth, and women. The CRM committee mechanisms was put in place to ensure most vulnerable members had access to said mechanisms after other feedback mechanisms including complaint boxes showed their inadaptation to the context precisely by the important number of illiterate individuals. It is expected that through this inclusive mechanism feedback of the most vulnerable will be collected on a regular basis.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will use several channels to ensure proper communication with beneficiaries. First, the project will include a strong awareness and mobilization component where community mobilizers will be in charge of reaching all the community using door to door methodologies to comply with COVID19 regulations and FGD in small groups to present the project, the selection criteria and the activities in general. Additionally, the project will work closely with local authorities to ensure they also promote the project within the community. To ensure beneficiary perception of the project is gathered on a regular basis, satisfaction quality survey questions will be included in the PDM exercises done after every distribution and training. The project will also include a final satisfaction survey at the end of the project, aimed at determining the impact of the project in the overall food security situation of beneficiaries. INTERSOS will ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place throughout the implementation period, aiming at safeguarding proper accountability and program quality, For complaint and feedback on the implementation of activities (including for example delays of implementation, complaints about selection of beneficiaries, etc.), a CRM committee in each location will be established by ensuring all groups of the society - and particularly women, children and people with disabilities - have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained about standard procedure for treating complaints and feedbacks. Additionally, complaint box will be established at every distribution point and training venue, and the CRM will also be in charge of collecting feedback provided by this mean.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

On a monthly basis, program staff on the ground will meet the CRM committee and add to a pre-established matrix all feedback received by the beneficiaries will the measures to be taken to address said feedback. This matrix will be shared with INTERSOS coordination team in Juba that will do a strict follow up of the measures taken during field visits. For the PSEA and CP complaints, a separated matrix will be kept by the focal point where all feedback receive will be stored as well as the measures taken. This matrix will be shared on a regular basis with the internal audit.
