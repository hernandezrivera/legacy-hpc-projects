Project Summary

Older women and men are among the most vulnerable groups affected by the armed conflict in Eastern Ukraine. According to UNOCHA Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019, of the 3.5 million in need, 30% are above the age of 60 and of the 1.5 million registered IDPs, over 50% of them are over 60. Displacement, family separation and isolation, and lack of access to essential goods/markets affect older women and men disproportionately.
Many of the most vulnerable conflict-affected older women and men are at risk of being excluded from conventional forms of aid distributions. They may face mobility limitations, have disabilities or chronic illnesses which prevent them from seeking assistance during physical distributions in town or village centres. Older men and women are often in need of personalised assistance and require a different approach, such as home-based care/assessments or, where secure and possible, community-based events that help reduce isolation.
This project builds on previous protection based humanitarian interventions implemented by HelpAge International in Ukraine and funded by OFDA and ECHO, to provide direct Psychosocial Support (PSS) to older women and men affected by the conflict through Home-Based Care (HBC) for the most vulnerable households, Community Safe Spaces (CSS) and provision of assistive devices, hygiene kits, diapers and blankets.
These actions will mainly be implemented by HelpAge trained community based volunteers that will provide “outreach” home visits to immobile and vulnerable older people and persons with disabilities located in the most isolated and hard to reach areas near to the line of contact. The home-based care provides support to promote greater levels of Independent living capacity and reduced isolation, facilitated through basic exercise sessions and provision of assistive devices.
The support for 3 Community Safe Spaces will focus on providing PSS activities to the more mobile conflict affected older women and men located in the 5km zone near to the line of contact. Each CSS Centre will provide a safe venue for older women and men to meet and share their experiences, find support from other peers, access Psychosocial First Aid through discussion groups and taking part in group activities.
The proposal will also set up a robust referral system to signpost the most vulnerable to other life-saving essential services provided by a range of humanitarian organisations, both local and international as well as from local health centres and the department of social welfare. Agreements have been reached with NRC/DRC to support legal assistance referrals and with PIN to provide referrals for 100 shelter repairs (moderate level) and 50 households with disability friendly improvements in the home (hand-rails, lowering kitchen taps etc..).
Through these activities and by using a multi sectoral approach addressing protection, health and nutrition and WASH concerns, older people and persons with disabilities will be identified and their critical needs supported through appropriate interventions, allowing for improved access to humanitarian and social services, improved psychosocial wellbeing and resilience and referral to available and appropriate service providers based on the actual needs of the most vulnerable individuals.

1 - Needs

HelpAge International has been operating in Ukraine since November 2014 and has just completed a protection-based humanitarian response, funded by OFDA and ECHO, that supported the most vulnerable conflict affected older people. With this project, HelpAge significantly improved access to humanitarian assistance and protection for older women and men, including those with disabilities who were affected by the conflict in Eastern Ukraine.A needs assessment exercise was conducted in the Government Controlled Areas of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts in July 2018 by HelpAge volunteers. Structured beneficiary interviews were conducted using a multidimensional Vulnerability Assessment Form (VAF)The survey covered 7,199 older people of which, 4,595 were pre-selected based on specific vulnerabilities), measuring their needs across income, protection, health, etc. The report found that 75.4% of total older people were women, 11.4% of the total number of older women and men interviewed had a registered disability. 17.2% of the people who participated in the survey were immobile (of these people only 17.1% had an official document confirming their disability) and 11% were internally displaced persons. 96.4% of older people are experiencing various conflict-related psychosocial issues. 41.2% of older people report that they are living alone (86.4% women), 20.5% of older people (75.8% women) reported experiencing at least one type of violence and abuse. From this VAF assessment undertaken in July 2018, HAI has maintained a vulnerability progress database up to July 2019. This database allows for the identification of potential beneficiaries who need ongoing support and assistance for this response. Of the 53 prioritized locations, HAI will support 19 settlements, of which 13 of these have information included in the HAI VAF 2018/19 database, so providing information on potential beneficiaries and key needs in these settlements.Even though government, NGO and international assistance has been provided since the start of the conflict, we can clearly see from these findings that the needs of older people are still critically high. Overall 40.3% of older people (75.6% were women) indicated that they have received humanitarian aid in the last year. The most common type of assistance was for food (97.5%). Of those who had received aid in the last year, only 1% received NFI and hygiene support. This points to a critical gap in meeting older people’s needs for NFI support including assistive devices for older people and persons with disabilities as well as the follow up support required to operate them, and other items required for persons with specific needs such as diapers and hygiene kits. A majority of older people are isolated and at the same time reliant on the assistance of others to be able to take part in daily community activities. A high percentage (41.2%) of older people live alone with women representing the overwhelming majority (86.4%). 69.6% of all older people indicate a sense of loneliness and note that they do not always feel that they are full members of the society. In addition to living alone, 48.9% of older people report they are feeling isolated. 52.6% of older people depend on assistance from family, friends or neighbors in their daily activities. Isolation and a sense of loneliness seem to have negatively affected the psychosocial wellbeing of older women and men since the beginning of the conflict. These findings highlight the critical need to provide direct Psychosocial Support to conflict affected older people through Home-Based Care for the most vulnerable households that this intervention intends to deliver. HelpAge is therefore submitting this proposal with the intention of contributing to the unmet WASH, Health and Nutrition & Protection needs of older people and persons with disabilities.
