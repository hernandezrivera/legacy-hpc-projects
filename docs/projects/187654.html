Project Summary

In 2021 ZOA implemented projects in multiple sectors including NFI, reaching a total of 118,130 beneficiaries. In 2021 ZOA was able to secure $4,732,378 in funding, of which $1,200,000 was SHF funding. ZOA was able to allocate $1,350,000 for activities under the NFI sector. In 2022 ZOA has to date secured $2,122,655 with around half million USD secured for NFI interventions. The cost per beneficiary for the 2 years planning is estimated to be $58.
Currently, ZOA is distributing around 1700 kits in in-kind and vouchers modalities in Quneitra Governorate, Khan Arnaba sub-district. For the winterization activities the kits prices are as follows: Winterization kits - $125 (5 jackets, 5 boots and 5 pairs of socks), multifuel heaters with a price of $47 and 4 rounds of olive pomace as alternative fuel for $67 in total. Also, ZOA is implementing a cash for winterization project in Dar’a Governorate, As-Sanamayn sub-district for 2,720 families. During the months of January and February 2022 each family will receive an equivalent of $196 in total. ZOA has taken into consideration the sector as well as the Cash Working Group recommendations.
For the HRP 2022-23 ZOA is proposing shelter & winterization activities and provision of solar energy to reach populations living in substandard, damaged, and/or inadequate shelter and households susceptible to harsh winter conditions and no access to energy, in underserved, conflict affected communities in Aleppo, Dar’a, Hama, Idleb, Quneitra and Rural Damascus. Based on the sector guidance and community based needs assessments, ZOA will target the most vulnerable households with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity of needs in the target locations. Proposed activities are aimed at minimizing vulnerable households’ exposure to harsh weather conditions, negative coping mechanisms as well as health concerns especially in light of the COVID-19 outbreak. Interventions will ensure that households susceptible to the harsh winter conditions are well equipped with the necessary resources to survive the winter period. Proposed activities based on needs, market assessments, sector guidance and beneficiary preference include the distribution of heaters, vouchers for heating fuel (olive pomace), provision of commodity based vouchers, winterization kits and/or MPC. The modality will be decided once the locations are finalized and based on the rapid market assessment. ZOA has wide experience in CVA activities & uses mixed modality according to location. Also, ZOA will supply and install HH level solar energy systems. The assistance will be prioritized for newly displaced vulnerable families (including people displaced multiple times), people living in sub-standard shelters and spontaneous returnees. Assistance is targeted at about 16,900 households over 2 years 8380 HHs in 2022 & 8,520 HHs in 2023. It is important to note that some beneficiaries will benefit from more than 1 service, based on the needs.
ZOA will also rehabilitate shelters and distribute shelter kits in communities which are damaged by the prolonged conflict in Syria. Proper shelter is also important for enhancing the protection of vulnerable people.
The proposed activities are aligned with Sectoral Objective 1 and 2 Most of the targeted sub-districts have a minimum severity score 3 according the HNO 2022 sector severity and/or winter severity index shared by the sector in 2021. Apart, during the past months ZOA has implemented different projects and done assessments in some of the sub-districts, therefore we are aware of the needs in the targeted areas. The number of the targeted beneficiaries are also identified based on these assessments, sector needs, as well as feedbacks collected from different ZOA projects. The final numbers of population in the targeted areas will be cross checked and verified with actors working in the areas as well as local authorities. ZOA will target 1,325 IDP HHs, 6,355 Host HHs, 700 returnees HHs.

4 - Needs

NFI support during winter is vital for coping with harsh conditions. ZOA’s past experience & assessments show the varying NFI needs by gender & age. The estimated number of PiN has increased from 4.69 million in 2021 to 4.91 million in 2022. Overall, this is a 5% increase in PiN from 2021. The HNO 2022 draft also confirms the importance of energy & winterization considering similar interventions as emergency (HNO 2022 sectors chapters draft p. 39). It is expected that during 2022-23 and especially under the economic downfall and COVID-19 impact, access to shelter & NFI will be more difficult for the people in need (HNO 2022 sectors chapters draft p. 41). Number of shelter PiN is also expected to increase in 2022. 38% of total population in Syria are currently living in damaged or inadequate shelters. Adequate shelter conditions are vital to maintain physical health, mental well-being, and to promote and sustain learning and educational opportunity (HNO 2022 sectors chapters draft p. 40). During 2022 ZOA will target 8,380 HHs. Targeted areas under this project have severity score 2-4 according to the PiN severity score sheet shared by the sector. Only locations in Idleb have low severity score, yet their winterization score is high and ZOA is intended to do winterization interventions. Also, recently all INGO representatives met with the Governor of Idleb and he confirmed that the needs are enormous.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed winter intervention will use the most appropriate modality – in-kind distributions, commodity vouchers, or cash through Financial Service Providers, depending on context. ZOA has wide experience for all CVA activities in Syria. A total of 16,900 HH are targeted over 2 years. Seasonal voucher assistance is for vulnerable families to access core relief items to meet their additional needs during winter months. Vouchers are exchanged for fixed quantity & quality of specified goods from vendors at local markets. Vouchers will be distributed in the form of paper, a readily-available delivery mechanism; a voucher for personal insulation and for a heater (in-kind) which will be distributed at the start of the winter. Additionally, 4 fuel vouchers (olive pomace) per HH will be distributed from Nov.-Feb. ZOA will provide & install solar panels for basic energy to the most vulnerable HHs. For the shelter activities qualified contractors will be contracted according ZOA procurement procedures. Monitoring is the responsibility of the country based Manager of Program Quality, supervising MEAL officers in-country. The collected data will be sex & age disaggregated. ZOA selects beneficiaries according to set criteria, distribution is through distribution points and if needed door to door. After each distribution ZOA does PDM and wherever possible organizes focus group discussions.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation measures: 1. Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities; 2. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonized as possible through protection service providers; 3 Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to ensure that selected beneficiaries to which ZOA/partners just provided assistance can be assisted in different sectors; 4 Consistent M&E & feedback mechanisms that contribute to project development/changes. R2: Increase in CP & GBV cases because of intervention. Likelihood: low. Impact: low. Mitigating measures: 1 All contractors sign ZOA's code of conducts. 2 ZOA field staff and volunteers monitor contractors to avoid any child labor. 3 ZOA clearly communicates with beneficiaries on the purpose of distributions . R3: Physical safety of beneficiaries & risk of exploitation/abuse Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigating measures: 1 Liaison with communities & local stakeholders to develop detailed implementation plans; 2 Project coordination, sharing & seeking lessons learned; 3 Amending project plans based on identified protection vulnerabilities, such as disabilities, age & gender; 4 Regular staff training; 5 Raising awareness for all beneficiaries on complaint mechanisms.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ZOA’s Integrity Framework has policies that encompass fraud, corruption, PSEA, complaints and child protection. As a matter of fact ZOA has zero tolerance to PSEA. ZOA HQ is signed up to the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme of IASC and does yearly report on behalf of all the country offices. PSEA Risk: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended, 2. Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation to the service providers ensuring confidentiality of the reporting, 3. Consult periodically with the population receiving the services/assistance, through regular and targeted sessions, including separate sessions with different groups that may be more at risk, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 4. Implement specific complaint mechanism and assign adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 5. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA, 6. Perform post-distribution monitoring.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Government entities require agencies to adhere to new regulations or additional approval levels are required to be able to implement activities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measure: 1. Coordinate within the sector, to be aware of how other actors deal with the challenges; 2. Engage closely with authorities; 3. Build capacities of local actors & continue to implement through them. R2: Market & exchange rate instability. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Continuous monitoring of the markets; 2. Transfer funds into Syrian bank according to the immediate needs. 3. Hold maximum possible amounts in Syrian banks in hard currency. R3: Movement restrictions due to COVID-19. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Carry out activities as soon as possible through existing infrastructure before more restrictions & lockdowns are enforced; 2. Explore remote alternatives for field monitoring. R4: Fraud & corruption. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Clear communication with target populations on their entitlements using multiple channels; 2. ZOA has a clear conflict of interest policy in its code of conduct; 3. Procurement, Logistics, Fraud & Corruption Policies in place for the mitigation of these risks; 4. All staff are trained on policies that address fraud, corruption & abuse; 5. All financial transactions are documented and filed; 6. Regular finance spot-checks & unannounced audits are carried out.
