Project Summary

The project goal is to improve the well-being and self-reliance of refugees and host communities in five states of Sudan. This will be achieved through the provision of integrated, multi-sectorial services including health care, nutrition, protection, WASH, Shelter and NFIs.
The overall objective for health is to increase meaningful access to promotive, preventive, curative, rehabilitative and palliative care within a primary health care approach through the implementation of basic package of primary healthcare services including management of communicable & non communicable diseases, maternal and child health care, nutrition, mental health and psychosocial services, with access to referral to secondary and tertiary health care in reference to referral SoP in place. Alight will collaborate with other stakeholders to ensure epidemiological surveillance and will actively participate in prevention and response for any new or existing disease outbreak. All these will be achieved through maintaining skilled health care providers and reinforcing their capacities based on identified gaps. In WASH Alight will implement water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion with NFI distribution, environmental health activities including solid waste management and safe disposal.
In protection (General, child and GBV) Alight will strive to ensure equitable access to essential services for prevention, mitigation and response (PMR) to protection needs for PoCs. The project will strengthen community engagements for PMR to protection risks against PoC while ensuring change in their attitudes and contribution towards reducing the risks, promoting protective environment and provision of appropriate and quality multi-sectoral services. All PoC, including people with special need and attention, will meaningfully participate /play a central role in community activities and decision-makings on an equal basis with others and while co-existing peacefully with neighboring host communities. Strengthening of community protection structures, promotion of Communication with Communities, strengthening the Community- Based Complaints Mechanisms (CBCMs), ensuring transparency and Accountability to Affected Persons (AAP), reducing the risk of GBV, as well as Engaging Men in Accountable practices (EMAP), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) are core activities. Protection minimum standards, SoPs and guidelines will be adhered to as well as clear mechanisms for accountability. Child safeguarding policies, PSEAH, Code of Conduct, Anti-Fraud and Anti- Corruption standards will be adhered to while implementing protection interventions. Age, Gender, Diversity sensitive programming will be enhanced to ensure equal and meaningful access to services by diverse groups, disability inclusion is key.
For S/NFI, Alight will monitor situations; preparing hotspot areas and identifying vulnerable communities, and raise awareness of the communities on hazards. We regularly coordinate with COR, HAC, UNHCR and other key partners & exchange information on existing humanitarian situations. Whenever emergency occurs, Alight will trigger joint need assessment (with state level stakeholders), conduct joint verification exercises and respond to the S/NFI needs. Prioritization and targeting will be done through cluster conventional processes. Activities that are in line with cluster objective and relevant to the locality culture and traditions are carefully selected and implemented to deliver effective responses. Wherever and whenever possible Cash Base intervention (CBI) (up to 20% of total budget) will be applied to help households fulfill their S/NFI needs.
Monitoring and evaluation will continue to be reinforced at all levels while people of concern will be engaged at different levels to ensure they remain informed on planned action and any related change and their voices are heard and reflected in implementation and project design.

1 - Needs

Since April 15, 2023, a violent conflict between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has been ongoing, mainly in and around Khartoum and the Darfur and Kordofan regions. The Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH) has reported at least 705 people have been killed and 5,287 injured as of May 16, over one million people have been displaced both within and outside of the country . The conflict resulted in significant disruptions to the economy and access to basic services such as food, water, and healthcare. The states of Khartoum and South Darfur have been particularly affected, with Aljazeera state hosting IDPs and serving as a transition point for IDPs and refugees traveling to other states in Sudan or Ethiopia. East and South Darfur states have also been indirectly impacted by the conflict and pre-existing complex emergency conditions.In light of these challenges, Alight Sudan has proposed a humanitarian response program to address the health, nutrition, WASH, S/NFI of affected. The humanitarian situation in Sudan is dire, with 24.7 million people in need of assistance, including 18.11 million who face life-threatening health and mental issues . The main sectors of need are health, nutrition, WASH, protection, and food security and livelihoods. The needs are driven by multiple factors, such as conflict, insecurity, social tensions, climate shocks, poor governance, and disease outbreaks. The recent conflict has worsened the situation for all families, but especially for the vulnerable groups, such as new and protracted IDPs, refugees, and returnees. These groups live in overcrowded conditions and face repeated emergencies and displacement. Conflict and violence are anticipated to continue, leading to widespread human rights abuses, protection issues and GBV, as well as damage to infrastructure and housing. The effect of displacement, the complex and unpredictable political and economic situation in Sudan has greatly impacted on the humanitarian space and ability to comprehensively address the arising needs for persons of concern. The situation has also exacerbated the vulnerability of refugees and risks of GBV due to other negative coping mechanisms including, survival sex and abuses and there is a high likelihood of the persons of concern being exposed to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Another concern is chronic harmful traditions; on average 47.2% of girls are married before the age of 18, which is contributing to 47,4% of children <5 years old to have severe or moderate malnutrition. The current Health care, WASH, S/NFI, food, education, and livelihood services are inadequate to provide service to the existing number of refugees that meet the UNHCR minimum standard. As the condition in neighboring countries has not improved, new refugee arrivals are expected in 2023. The new PoCs, whilst presenting unique needs and challenges, a strain on social amenities and service delivery is expected on basic services and protection, legal and justice. However, even when all such services are made available, a surge in GBV incidences, mental health and psychological distress, child abuse, and crime rate are expected. The conflict and displacement have exposed the children to serious protection risks such as separation from parents or other family members, exposure to different forms of violence (including gender-based, physical or emotional violence), abuse, neglect and exploitation manifested in harmful traditional practices such as child marriage and child labor taking place within families and communities. There continues to be challenges in implementing Protection policies in the refugee camps, transit centers due to absence of a defined protection system, constrained resources and capacities that limit access to services among the most at-risk population.

i) Provision of age and gender sensitive individual case management to children and adults as per their specific needs and in their best interests.ii) Protection by presence and monitoring jointly with Government and partners and ensure the protective environment for the persons of concern is strengthened.iii) Identification, assessment and response to PSNs prioritized needs. iv) Strengthen and support community leadership and community- self management structures.vi) Implement peaceful co-existence projects with communities such as sports, commemorative events, cultural events celebrations. vii) Implement child protection activities such as Child Friendly spaces, placing and supporting Unaccompanied and Separated children in Alternative careviii) Provision of MHPSS coupled with expressive arts, therapies and PFA.ix) Women Economic Empowerment embedded with Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs). x) Provision of Multi-Purpose Cash Grants for Most at Risk Persons with Specific Needs (MARPSNs).xi) Strengthen and expand protection desks and women and girls friendly spacesxii) Engagement of Men in Accountable Practices activities, working constructively with men and boys to examine the gendered impact of conflict and the social norms re-construction. For health, Alight intends to refugees and asylum seekers have easy access to primary health care through outpatient consultations for acute conditions, communicable & most common non communicable diseases. Alight expect to receive most of essential drugs as in kind donations and will procure essential drugs and laboratory reagents to complement supply chain. Alight will provide the following services;-Health promotion through community outreaches and engagement-Child health through EPI and IMCNI services. -Maternal and reproductive health (antenatal, safe delivery, post-natal and family planning services). -MHPSS including raising awareness for increase demand of services. -Access to secondary health care services including referrals (on approval of UNHCR referrals to tertiary health care). -Nutritional services (including IYCN practices promotion, MUAC screening children & PLWs). Prioritization and targeting will be done through cluster conventional processes and SOPs, and support will be provided considering relevance to the PoC culture and traditions. Wherever and whenever possible Cash Base intervention (CBI) (up to 20% of total budget) will be applied to help households fulfill their needs.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Through this project, Alight will produce medical waste including infectious , non infectious, sharp waste(e.g expired drugs, laboratory reagents, biological waste, injections) and plastic waste from packaging of drugs and nutrition supplies. To ensure proper medical waste management, Alight will segregate, collect waste with proper handling and disposal according to national guidelines and where necessary Alight will engage respective SMOH to ensure no harm to environment, facility users and people surrounding the facility. For nutrition supplies , care givers will be encouraged to return sachets of nutrition supplies (e.g RUTF, RUSF...)after consumption for proper disposal. To achieve this, health care providers, cleaners and volunteers will be trained or oriented on medical waste management and awareness raising on environment protection will be done regularly. Through supervision and monitoring, Alight will assess the compliance and will engage stakeholders where necessary.Alight will minimize the quantity of expired drugs by coordinating with other facilities internally and externally for possible exchange or donation within shelf -life.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ALIGHT uses the AGD approach throughout the project design and implementation, especially during beneficiary selection and data monitoring. ALIGHT follows the IASC Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action, the IASC Guidelines for Inclusion of Persons with Disability in Humanitarian Action, the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action and the Sphere Handbook. ALIGHT commits to ensure meaningful access across the age categories and vulnerability scenarios, with particular regard for pregnant women/new mothers and infants, older and severely ill and people with disability. In addition, while selecting suitable location for the services, the voices of the vulnerable groups will be prioritized to address barriers related to discrimination and access due to distance. The project will strengthen community-based protection structures and ensure synergies with all the sectors involved, to prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm. In particular, WASH sector will create safe and friendly structures; easily accessible and accommodating to individuals with special needs and appropriate for GBV prevention. Alight will ensure representation of both women, girls, men and girls in community structures including community health, nutrition and WASH volunteers. ALIGHT also participates in the Sudan Protection Cluster, GBV and CP sub-clusters and local working group to ensure coordination, networking, sharing and learning of best practices, advocacy and protection monitoring. Finally, ALIGHT ensures accountability through the presence of an inclusive FCRM.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ALIGHT follows the AAP approach, supporting community participation, feedback and ensuring transparency about the interventions. This is to ensure the project decisions and actions are responsive to the expressed priorities, needs, capacities and views of all persons of concern irrespective of Age, Gender, Diversity and Disability backgrounds. ALIGHT will ensure a balanced boys, girls, women and men in all project activities with relative 60% female and 40% male. Data for beneficiaries will be disaggregated by gender, age groups and location, as the project activities are tailored for different groups in line with developmental stages, needs and vulnerabilities and basing on relevant parameters appropriate in reverence to heightened protection risks of the persons of concern. Activities like awareness raising and dialogues will be conducted in women and child/youth safe spaces and in the communities.ALIGHT participates in the PSEA Network in Sudan, attending meetings and supporting assessments and coordination with other UN agencies and other relevant international INGOs. ALIGHT has a PSEAH Policy, Guidelines for Sudan, Child Protection Policy and Code of Conduct and it is undergoing a revision of the PSEAH Implementation structure, ensuring accessibility to Feedback and Complaint Response Mechanism to all the beneficiaries and capacity building for staff, partners and suppliers or contractors about SEAH standards. The PSEAH Implementation structure in ALIGHT follows the Six IASC Core Principles and the principles of anti-discrimination, accountability, safety security and protection and survivor-centered approach, ensuring the respect of survivor’s will and confidentiality in every step of the reporting and responding process. Different communication channels are available for the beneficiaries (hot line, front desk, suggestion boxes, email...) and confidential data of SEAH incidents will be shared to other agencies or donors based on pre-existing agreements. Under the project, ALIGHT will conduct Safeguarding risk assessments to mitigate any potential or identified risks of SEA. ALIGHT will ensure Zero Tolerance for SEAH. Jointly with the PSEA task force, ALIGHT will develop relevant IEC materials and distribute it in communities, raise awareness on SEAH and ensure appropriate measures are undertaken for investigations.As part of AAP, Alight will ensure to inform community representatives for planned action at the beginning of project and will remain informed on any change during project implementation, this include awareness raising on referral mechanism and limitations in referrals as per referral SoP. Through MEAL , Alight will collect community feedback on satisfaction and suggested areas of improvements, where necessary Alight will investigate the complaints and actions will be taken to address the gaps while the collected information will be used for future project design.
