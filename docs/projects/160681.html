Project Summary

The project aims to meet the basic life-saving ES/NFI needs of affected populations due to conflict or violence within South Sudan. It is part of an emergency response so can take place in any location at short notice. The target is to reach 40,000 individuals who are deemed the most vulnerable using both the cluster’s and Medair’s vulnerability criteria. Medair will target newly displaced persons, protracted displaced persons, returnees and vulnerable host communities. Within these groups the most vulnerable will be defined as sharing limited resources, over-burdened households, people with disability, female headed households, pregnant and lactating females etc. Medair puts AAP and protection mainstreaming at the core of all programming to ensure meaningful access to services and to protect the safety and the dignity of beneficiaries.
With ongoing violence throughout South Sudan Medair continues to see many people still displaced and most having left their homes with no items and in some cases homes have been looted and burned down. IDPs are then sharing resources with already vulnerable host households often meaning multiple families will share 1 saucepan, 1 mosquito net, 1 blanket etc. Medair aims to address this by responding to sudden onset displacement due to violence. Medair will complete an effective analysis of the populations needs using the clusters analysis tools. An analysis (using tools such as the stakeholder analysis, a protection risk analysis, research, observation and Medair field teams’ findings) takes place to then discuss if an intervention is appropriate and what interventions are possible. The communities are then involved in discussions to help determine the intervention needed to best suit the needs alongside Medair teams.
Once an intervention is confirmed Medair then works closely with the ES/NFI cluster and IOM to receive items needed for the distribution in a timely manner for beneficiaries. Medair set up a community distribution committee made up of representative community members to discuss the appropriate and safe way to distribute to allow meaningful access. Guidelines are given on the selection criteria and this is disseminated to communities and the teams are available to answer questions on this. A safety audit is completed to make sure the distribution site is safe and accessible.
Throughout the distribution the Medair team makes sure representative casual labourers are used and then there is a clear and well organized complaints feedback mechanism so any issues are resolved swiftly on the day. Following the distribution, Medair teams will complete exit interviews so they remain accountable to the populations, they can deal with any urgent issues whilst in the field, they can identify any furthers gaps or needs and for internal lessons learnt. Anytime between four weeks and 3 months a PDM will be completed to fully assess the impact of the intervention and if the community feel that their most basic needs have been met and if this was done in a safe and dignified manner.
The Medair team then report to the cluster, donors and sometimes communities on findings from the intervention and will also share with other partners and cluster if further needs have been identified that Medair are unable to respond to. This means Medair programmes remain accountable and transparent to all involved.

1 - Needs

Despite improved security, there is still significant risk that populations will continue to be displaced by armed groups, cattle-raiders and inter-communal fighting. It is likely that the humanitarian situations will deteriorate over the next year as a result of the repercussions of the formation of the new government, a result of six years of conflict and a continued reduction in access to basic services (ECHO’s Humanitarian Implementation Plan, September 2019). Emergency Shelter and NFI assistance is still urgently needed to serve those who have fled their homes and left their belongings behind. An estimated 1.47 million people are currently displaced (UN OCHA, August 2019) and most are in desperate need of some form of ES/NFI assistance. The Cluster estimates 1,989,228 individuals are in need of S/NFI assistance within South Sudan currently (Draft HRP cluster narrative, October 2019). Medair teams continue to find multiple families sharing very limited and very basic resources with vulnerable hosts, IDPs and returnees who continue to flee violence with nothing. Looting and the burning of houses have caused the destruction of belongings. In particular, IDPs outside camps require basic household items, especially those who have relocated several times. In general, those who are newly displaced will have priority over protracted displaced populations. Women and children will remain particularly vulnerable to protection risks due to lack of shelter, and in some instances host community members will also need support due to pressure on their limited resources to help IDPs. Men will also remain at risk of violence in some areas where they can be targets and child abductions are an on-going concern. Since the signing of the R-ARCSS peace agreement in September 2018, there has being an increase in refugee and IDP returnees throughout South Sudan. According to round six of the IOM DTM, from the signing of the agreement to the end of June 2019, 149,073 returnees were recorded. Should the establishment of the new government take place successfully in early 2020ore returnees can be expected. Many IDP and refugees returnees are in need of emergency shelter and NFI support. Medair aims to address the needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees particularly pregnant and lactating women, female-headed households, the elderly, and the disabled. In this specific context, Medair responds to recently displaced populations fleeing conflict. If warranted, Medair will also support some of the most vulnerable host community members in order to build their resilience and ease community tensions. This is in-line with the cluster response for 2020 where targeting includes prioritising people unable to support themselves with life-saving shelter and non-food items (Draft HRP cluster narrative, October 2019) The goal is to support those in need by giving them life-saving ES/NFI items to support people without eroding their existing coping mechanisms. Medair will also be prioritising more multi-sector responses for more of a holistic approach to emergency response so multiple life-saving needs can be met within interventions in coordination with partners and the clusters hubs, which meets the current cluster HRP plan for 2020.

2 - Activities

• Carry out ES/NFI needs analysis upon receiving cluster alerts• Respond with an appropriate modality (survival kit, quick intervention, planned intervention, integrated response) providing ES/NFI items and training to meet the life-saving needs of communities. • Conduct post-distribution monitoring assessments (PDMs), exit interviews and record registration of positive and negative feedback to ensure accountability to the affected population. Before, during and after distribution, implement feedback mechanisms to take into account affected population’s input. • Coordinate with other partners through attending national- and state-level cluster meetings, networking with other agencies, and interacting with partners on the ground from other agencies.• As an emergency response team Medair will coordinate with the Humanitarian Hubs in an emergency to allow for a more efficient and timely response and reduce the likelihood of duplication of activities between other partners. It will be an effective way to make use of prepositioned supplies and contribute toward general coordination.• Medair recognizses that with the increase in returnees and ongoing high numbers of IDPs there is more of a need for an area based approach covering multiple sectors to allow for meeting all the basic needs of a population. To fully understand the complex needs in a changing context it is recognised that more time needs to be spent within a community and more collaboration with partners and specialists is needed. Medair will spend as much time as possible within communities to ensure a full assessment of the needs has been completed but also to ensure effective AAP is being implemented and the mechanisms are being used. With returnees and IDPs in an area facing multiple needs Medair will coordinate with other partners for a more holistic approach allowing multiple needs to be addressed within the area. Medair will do this by engaging specialists in HLP and protection where necessary. Medair will coordinate with other actors in the area to ensure no duplication of activities. Medair will also coordinate with the different sectors within Medair ERT to deliver a multi-sectoral response where needed and possible. • Be prepared to be involved in the Strategic Advisory Group (SAG) if selected to participate.• Protection mainstreaming in programming: include “do no harm” analysis in intervention plans, ensure most vulnerable people have priority access to distribution sites, and the population is served in line with Medair values and with Medair’s Code of Ethics, incorporating accepted AAP good practice.• Consider gender inclusion throughout project implementation through gender analysis in needs assessments, gender sensitive activities (including women in distributions and seeking out female community leaders as key informants) and gender sensitive outcomes.• As State Focal Point for Central Equatoria, proactively visit field sites across Central Equatoria to understand the needs in those locations; network to build up key contacts and relationships across the state; organise (at least) monthly NFI/ES state coordination meetings; build the capacity of partners on ES/NFI emergency responses; engage and build relationships with key RRC and ROSS officials.• Medair will continue to monitor IDP and returnee movement as part of the initial needs analysis. This is to fully understand where IDPs are planning on settling to help determine the kind of response needed. Medair also monitors returnee, both refugee and IDP, movement to see what is driving people to return, whether they plan to stay and to help identify the number of people returning or planning to return to an area. This is then shared with the cluster to help monitor returnee movement.

The Medair team work hard to triangulate information received through multiple sources and get field teams to verify in person before intervening. This is to reduce either missing of the most vulnerable households or the favouring of one community/tribe over another which can exacerbate tensions and lead to conflict. Medair also runs trainings in humanitarian principles where necessary and have clear selection criteria which are communicated to stakeholders.8.14 A risk assessment, stake holder analysis and protection analysis is carried out before every intervention. 9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

the teams will consult with the community to identify the most vulnerable in collaboration with protection specialist partners. Special assistance will be given to those who need it. Exit interviews will be conducted with the most vulnerable and their carers 9.1-9.4, 9.10 The analysis, interventions and PDMs include input from local authorities, community leaders and elders, women and youth groups (teens, not children), church leaders, people with disabilities, and other NGOs. IDPs, Returnees and hosts are all consulted so all different perspectives of the context are explored and understood by the field team as are the views of different local tribes. The Medair team also tries to find and consult with any at risk individuals who are not connected or outside the local communities. Medair usually tries to work with Protection partners that can also help identify any particularly vulnerable households that may get otherwise overlooked. 9.6-9.8 Prior to the distribution the Medair team make themselves available and accessible for any feedback the community wishes to give. . As part of routine practice Medair first determine how the community wishes to give and receive feedback during the initial analysis and act on this at distribution.There is a feedback desk at every distribution to address and document complaints and any other feedback. These are recorded in a feedback mechanism document. The team aims at resolving complaints immediately. Where this is not possible, the feedback received informs and shapes future interventions. Post Distribution Monitoring is conducted within two weeks to four months after the distribution wherever possible, to inform future distributions.9.9 All Medair staff receives regular training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Medair staffs remain vigilant in the field to staff and beneficiaries at risk of exploitation and are all made aware of the feedback mechanism for reporting any incidences.
