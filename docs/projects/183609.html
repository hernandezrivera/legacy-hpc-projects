Project Summary

The project is a 12-Month intervention aims at working closely with MoH and Aisha, (Association for Women and Child Protection) contributing to the quality of healthcare services including structural MH services to most affected people through support MoH responsiveness to conflict situations including provision of logistics, disposables, training, and equipment as well as non-structural support of Aisha to enhance early detection and referral to moderate to severe MH cases to proper MH attention for and people in need for MHPSS support who were affected by the protracted crisis, eruption of hostiles and C-19 circumstances.
The structural track, (through MoH) of intervention will utilize support to MoH efforts to best response to conflict situations including A) provision of logistics, medication (Including psychotropics), disposables, capacity building and resources to become more responsive to emergency reaching out approx. 1,000 patients including measures the amount of the medicine carbamazepine in your blood, lithium carbonite, phenobarbital tests and associated disposables for the central lab and MHPSS facilities , B) Improved capacity of 8 PHCs located in the North Area with devices to compute support including index, nursing and receptive patient information, C). Training of at least 100 health care workers on the mhGAP located in the north area (8 PHCs staff) through structural training on basic ICT skills as well as application of mhGAP.
The non-structural track (through Aisha) of intervention will include A) support community workers ability to conduct structural MH service referrals for detected moderate and severe MH cases (new) from community in Gaza City focusing on linking women and early adults (girls) by applying training of mhGAP through Aisha, B). Support availability of psychotropic medications for 200 patients including women and PWDs with deteriorated MH situation, and in need for direct support through community-based service providers in Gaza City, and C). facilitate access to hotline services both at the ministry level (103) and Aisha hotline to facilitate hearing back from community on service support through available hotline-free services.
Worth to mention that MDM-Suisse will oversee all procurement of services, drugs, and other support needed throughout the project according to apprising needs through MoH and Aisha including the delivery of mhGAP training.
The designed project represents a small contribution to the health cluster identified total of 1.5 million Palestinians two thirds of whom in Gaza as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2022. Almost 2 years of responding to the imminent health needs among most vulnerable populations, the Ministry of Health is still exhausted to properly respond to all intervals including the C-19 situation deterioration, along with other existing needs for Palestinians to access an acceptable, quality essential health care services. The proposed intervention is aligned with OCHA strategic objective#2 and Cluster Objective#6, MDM Suisse will implement a focused intervention to support access to acceptable and quality essential health care services to approx. 13, 014 patients, (per-Month) including 1,000 patients to MHPSS services to vulnerable communities located in Gaza and North Area through structured and non-structured, (HERAMS 08/2021report) In parallel, a complementary to the project implementation will be facilitated by MDM-Suisse through continued support to MoH efforts for C-19 prevention measures, testing and control as well as management of vaccine to most prone and affected communities in Gaza Strip through a Swiss Solidarity Fund.

1 - Needs

• The 12-month intervention is designed to meet the needs of the existing structures in the North Area of the Gaza Strip stating that there is an existing lack of proper information system functionality, worth mentioning that upon assessment of the eight PHCs located in the north area, each PHC indicated the availability of maximum of one computing device that is partially functioning to support fluidity of information including MH information as well as referral for the whole area, worth to mention that approx. 13,014 people access the eight PHCs per month, (HeRAMS August 2021). In addition to lack of proper computing systems at PHC level to support record management, referral, as well as possible effects on the reporting for both internal within the ministry level as well as externally to WHO and cluster level, over 100+ PHC staff are still lacking skills related to ICT, (basic patient information management) as well as mhGAP as agreed guidance within MoH and healthcluster, (Desk interview with D. Majdi Abed- Head of PHC at MoH). • Inline to support people in need of specialized MH services, the GZ-HNO Matrix indicate that an expected additional 10,000 people will need MHPSS services, a total of 25,000 PIN the number is drafted according to GCMHP and MoH active files. Adding to that the impacts of the recent escalation of violence in May 2021 have resulted in the death of 242 Palestinians, including 66 children, 38 women and 138 men in Gaza. Around 1948 Palestinians have been injured, including 610 children, 398 women and 940 men. Essential infrastructure was heavily damaged, hospitals and healthcare centres. A severe mental health impact on children and families is already reported by local organizations, particularly on children who are showing serious physical and psychological symptoms following intense Israeli shelling. Without rapid intervention, it is estimated that many may develop severe mental health suffering (OCHA, 2021).• The impacts manifested on the overall mental health situation of the population, it is assessed that 60.8% of women were subject to psychological violence during home quarantine for once at least, as 74,4% suffered cursing, 54,5% suffered threats of beating or expelling, 45% suffered severe blame because a family member infected by the pandemic, 97,2% sustained extra chores and responsibilities during the quarantine, 68,8% suffered from husband and sons irritability, 69,3% endured carelessness by the breadwinner, 47,5% suffered from fear as the breadwinner crash house furniture or other stuff, and 57,4% face inferior treatment. On the other hand, 33,9% of the surveyed women suffered from psychological violence during their stay in isolation centres, as 37,1% suffered cursing, and 30,7% faced threat, (UN women factsheet-quarantine impact on GBV in the Gaza strip- Jan 2021). • According to the health factors for vulnerability identified by the Health Cluster, the situation surrounding the structured and non-Structured MHPSS services is the constant instability of available psychotropic medications, where for 30% - 50% of the time the patients take the prescriptions and search for the substantial available medication in the private sector- pharmacies. On the other hand, only 180 staff members, including only 19 clinical psychiatrists are available to serve the total 25,000 patients with advanced MHPSS issues in need to support, as well as insufficient integration of MHPSS services at PHC levels along with insufficient rehabilitation services for MHPSS services at the community level, (verified through a facilitated FGDs with women with household member in need for medication at Aisha and factors identified by the cluster September 2021), the needs are also validated in The impact of COVID-19 on the Psychosocial and Mental Well-being of Palestinian Adolescents, (WHO and Juzoor 2021) • The non-structural support of the project is aligned with moderate MHPSS cases linked to community support through Aisha, through the integration of mhGAP to community workers, and improved access to accountability, consulting the available hotlines of MoH and Aisha to learn back from communities on their needs and MHPSS needs in response to deteriorating impacts of the surrounding situations including the outbreak of C-19. Worth mentioning that Aisha receives approx. monthly 200-350 phone calls requesting MHPSS support, family counselling as well as over 700 people benefit from structural family support through MHPSS consulted when comprising the project intervention, September 2021.

2 - Activities

MDM-Suisse’s intervention is to facilitate supported access to acceptable and quality essential health care services to vulnerable communities in Gaza and North Area, (including 55% men, 20% boys, 25% women, 20% girls, 16% PwDs, and 18% elderly). The project is aligned with OCHA objective 2 and cluster objectives 2 & 6, Outcome (1): structural support is provided to mental health services to the most affected people through support MoH responsiveness to conflict situations including the provision of logistics, disposables, training, and equipment. The provision of logistics, medication, Mh GAP medications, psychotropics, disposables, capacity building and resources to become more responsive to emergency reaching out approx. 1,000 patients, in addition to support MoH laboratory with Serum Levels of Psychiatric Drugs, (anti-epileptic drugs) for patients including, phenytoin level, valproic acid level, lithium carbonate level, carbamazepine Level and phenobarbital level. This will improve effectiveness and safety of patients who are in need of regular and ongoing psychotropic doses. The tests will assist in the treatment plans to patients by understanding Serum levels, (tolerable levels), understanding individual patient drug absorption issues and Gauge correct serum levels for monthly intake, the anticipated direct target group is 660 males, (420 men, 240 boys), 540 females, (300 women, 240 girls), PWDs 216, (61 PWDs Male, and 59 females), 216 elderlies, (108 females and 108 males). An improved capacity of 8 PHCs located in the North Area with devices to compute support including index, nursing, and information management: the indirect group is all patients at targeted PHCs, up to the volume of 26,000 consultations, (HeRAMs- Sept. 21), (incl. 55% men, 20% boys, 25% women, 20% girls, 16% PwDs, and 18% elderly). Training of 100 health care workers on the mhGAP located in the north area (8 PHCs staff) through structural training on basic ICT skills as well as application of mhGAP, the focal point of the training will be facilitated by PHC directorate trainers liaising with WHO protocol and engagement of D. Foad Issawi on behalf of MDM-Suisse, (Target: 50 Females and 50 males, in line with PHCs) Outcome 2: Strengthened referral pathway of service by standardizing tools, MH patient practices protocol and guidelines among all providers through non-structured support through Aisha”, (aligned with cluster priority#6):Support community workers ability to conduct structural MH service referrals for detected moderate and severe MH cases (new) from the community in Gaza City focusing on linking women and early adults (girls) by applying training of mhGAP through Aisha, the final target of this activity is approx. 1,500 women, including teenage girls linked to protection and mental health services. The project will support the availability of psychotropic medications for 200 patients mainly 80%women, 10% teenagers, and 10% PWDs exposed to GBV and acute MH conditions and in need of direct support through community-based service. Facilitated access to hotline services both at the ministry level and Aisha hotline to facilitate hearing back from the community on service support through available hotline-free services including printing of communication materials the target is a monthly volume of 400 phone calls for counselling, including at least 15% of monthly phone-calls cases verified and dealt with, (mild and moderate MH interventions), (all females). Implementation: MDM-Suisse will revalidate, (validated in planning), the needs of the 8 PHCs in the north area by obtaining the result needs of the PHCs ICT gaps and key staff using the information system on ICT skills aside with MH GAP training by PHC Directorate loop of trainers. MDM-Suisse Senior medical referent will liaise with mh GAP steering committee at PHC level during training. (Expected 100 participants, 40% females and 60% male). In coordination/consultation with the CDS, of all needed psychotropic medications, mh GAP medications to provide full service for the targeted population. On the other hand, the project will support the capacity of PHCs in the North Area to compute services incl. index, nursing, management total of 32 devices, the devices will meet the MoH technical standards. A specific assessment of ICT skills gap to selected 100 PHC staff, the trained staff will be able to apply mhGAP protocol in computing detection of MH cases. The delivery of essential psychotropic medications for 1000 patients (5 rounds), will also be joined with laboratory disposables to measure Serum Levels of Psychiatric Drugs, (anti-epileptic drugs) including, phenytoin level, valproic acid level, lithium carbonate level, carbamazepine Level and phenobarbital level to improve the effectiveness of psychiatric medications, including the level of toxicity at the patient blood and to link more safety inpatient treatment plan. 20 case managers and psychotherapists, (10 females and 10 males) will be trained on the mhGAP at Aisha. Throughout the project, trained Aisha staff will be followed up by the MDM-Suisse team to ensure training is applied for 10 months at Aisha mh clinic. By applying the mhGAP, the total number of cases referred will be listed and reported. (approx. 300 patients, 80% women and 20% men, 18% PWDs). 200 patients including 80% women and 18% PWDs with deteriorated MH status including acute depression receive their monthly dose of psychotropic medications (free-of-charge), for at least five months. Case files will be managed, disaggregated by females and males, and disability, among other important patient’s information. MDM-Suisse will continue communication efforts to normalize contacting hotline services both at the MoH and Aisha toll-free numbers, where the purpose of supporting Aisha is to enhance the connection among females to shout out for help in a process that entitles help, provision of service, documentation, and referrals.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

This project responds to the urgent health needs of patients and the needs of primary health care facilities in Gaza and north area taking into consideration the inter-linkages between health and mental health. The project follows the do-no harm principle and does not create new risks for the vulnerable communities particularly to families with young children. MDM-Suisse will ensure a human-rights based approach and the equity focus on the targeting and delivery of this response with specific consideration to the gender and disability balance since the interventions proposed in this project are also targeting children and their household with moderate and severe MH issues caused by protracted situation. On the other hand, the project will focus on supporting women who are exposed to additional harm by society, through opening a new non-structural channel for them to call for support, receive medication and get linked to protection as well as proper/accessible MH treatment through Aisha. During implementation, MDM-Suisse will ensure participation of the affected communities in all phases of the project by reviewing existing patient files and tailoring support/medications according to the needs of affected community basically women and existing medication needs available at MoH CDS. 20% of project activities will be conducted at the PHC level which is accessible for patients located in the areas and linked for constant health support, the bulk of 60% in medications will be supplied for the CDS at the MoH whereas patients will properly access their medications through the MH-PHC at MoH, the remaining 20 % of project activities focus on enhancing mechanisms of listening to community through an available and trusted community channel for support, where they are detected for deeper interventions and pathway referral throughout the intervention, where Aisha is a protection based organization with existing network with police, MoSD, protection house, mental health actors and GCMHP among other partners which will be in favor for project participants to interlink according to their needs with their priorities. The key associated assumption is that A) Essential drugs and medical supplies can enter Gaza; B) Healthcare providers can continue to operate at the community, facility, and home level; C) Continued commitment of health providers and other key actors.The associated risks remain the fear of (A) escalation of conflict due to unstable political situation (B) Additional pressure on health workers (C) Further constraints on the entrance of medical supplies into Gaza.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project responds to good integration of people with disabilities needs and demands into the planned activities, where it is expected that at least 18% of the target group are people with disabilities. At the inception phase, the project will organize a focus group with the affected population that will include people with disabilities and other vulnerable women with a focus on GBV victims to ensure the planned activities are well-tailored to their needs, where their voice is being heard and well-considered. Moreover, the prime target group under Aisha operational partner will be affected women and girls at different groups level such as: households, divorces, separated, with disabilities, and others who are suffering and being stigmatized. In addition, the project will work at the community level to remove attitudinal barriers, stigma, and double discrimination. However, the project will contribute to enhanced mental health care and related service provision through building partner capacity, access to hotline services and providing the necessary equipment, drugs, and other supplies to ensure completion of the service provision, treatment, and case management cycle. MdM CH technical team in coordination with the Aisha team will elaborate/develop the service package admission criteria at different dimensions: vulnerability criteria and eligibility criteria, both combined will ensure targeting the most abandoned, neglected gender affected population through enhanced access to specialized and mainstreamed services.
