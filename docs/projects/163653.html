Project Summary

UNICEF in partnership and coordination with health partners including Ministry of Health, National Center for Diseases Control and UN agencies and NGOs will focus on institutional and human resource capacity building enabling them to provide responsive lifesaving and essential health and nutrition services. The activities focus on meeting the needs of the vulnerable children and their families in undeserved and remote areas of the country and in the detention centers. The target population include children under five and Pregnant and Lactating women. The protracted emergency and weak health care delivery exposes most of the population to preventable morbidity and mortality. The health institutions in most of the districts is unable to provide health services to the people in need. The SARA 2017 report remain relevant where average general service readiness based on amenities, equipment, and medicine is 69 percent in hospitals and 37 percent in PHC centers. About 31 percent of the health facilities have no capacity at all to provide any health services. The immunization supply chain system has drastically suffered from the conflict and severe shortage of vaccines have been reported at all levels exposing children to vaccine preventable diseases. During 2019, small scale diseases outbreaks were reported especially in south (Morzuq), Misurata and Benghazi. The national centre for disease control monthly surveillance report shows 590 suspected and 180 confirmed measles and 50 Rubella cases (from Jan to Nv 2019) and also EWARN shows trend of meningitis suspected cases, mainly in Benghazi. Multi Sector Needs Assessment 2019 also indicates urgent need for vaccination and nutrition intervention. In addition, Libya is at risk of polio importation from neighbouring countries (outbreak declared Niger and Chad). In the given situation there is urgent need for strengthening health system for providing preventive and curative lifesaving and essential services to most vulnerable population in the country. This project is expected to contribute significantly in prevention of maternal, neonatal and child morbidity and mortality including vaccine preventable diseases.

1 - Needs

Due to ongoing conflict and political fragility the health service delivery deteriorating at all levels in the country. Resulting in huge gaps in Maternal, newborn and child health and nutrition services. In the given situation the critical needs include; a) Urgent provision of essential and lifesaving medicines and equipment to health facilities especially in West, south (most affected) and east b) Ensure availability of Essential Newborn Care to prevent neonatal mortality c) Establish and implement Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses strategy for prevention and treatment of major childhood killer d) Establish a system for timely screening and appropriate treatment of children with all kinds of malnutrition including Severe Acute malnutrition and micro nutrient deficiencies e) regular updating of health and nutrition of the children and women for need based support f) Preparation and response to vaccine preventable diseases outbreaks g) regular monitoring of the situation;

2 - Activities

1. Provision of essential medicines and equipments 2. Strengthening health system for prevention of vaccine preventable diseases and timely response to outbreaks. 3. 6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

The accountability to affected population will be ensured through regular and active involvement of the local communities and the target beneficiaries. There will be more focus on involvement of communities as active participants in the project instead of being passive recipients of the services. The project addresses the community needs and priorities with interaction. A feedback mechanism will be established and functional to listen and review comments from the communities while providing services. The project will facilitate participation of local communities by establishing Health facility supervising committees comprising of local representation. The exit interview will be conducted to assess parents/client satisfaction and feedback on the services. The basic objective is to ensure that communities actively participate and demand for quality and continued services. A monitoring framework will be in place to monitor and evaluate the program implementation, quality and effectiveness. Third Party Monitoring is also envisaged in the monitoring plan so include views of the affected population.
