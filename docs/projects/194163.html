Project Summary

This project aims to provide humanitarian assistance to refugees and hosting communities under the Strategic Objectives of the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan. IOM will provide multi-sectoral interventions, including Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), Emergency Shelters and Non-Food Items (ES/NFIs), health, and nutrition-based activities to respond to the urgent needs of refugees and hosting communities. The military conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that commenced on 15 April 2023 has caused increased vulnerabilities of the 1.1 million refugees and asylum seekers across Sudan and triggered urban to rural, intra/interstate and cross border population movements. For example in Khartoum it has caused secondary movements of refugees and asylum seekers. There are further concerns that premature returns to countries of origin will place refugees at risk of forced and unsafe returns. The conflict and subsequent displacement in Sudan is causing refugee crises in all the neighbouring countries. IOM will focus on providing the multi-sectoral assistance in Darfur, Kordofan, Khartoum, Blue Nile, Kassala and Gedaref. Numerous instances of internal displacement and increases in the number of refugee and migrant movements have been reported in 2022 and are expected to increase significantly in 2023 in these states due to the crisis. The proposed WASH activities will increase access to safe drinking water, adequate quality sanitation facilities, and improve hygiene practices. The proposed ES/NFI interventions will be used to improve living conditions for vulnerable refugees and host communities that receive high rates of displacement and offer protection to vulnerable people from the harsh elements. The proposed health activities will focus on delivering a basic package of primary health services (BPPHS) and nutrition services integrated to BPPHS, to improve access to services and reduce risks associated with morbidity and mortality and support more favorable protection outcomes. IOM's project will focus on targeting both refugees and hosting communities in an effort to mitigate the risks of tensions over scarce resources and increase the capacity of hosting communities to absorb population influxes. However, lifesaving assistance will be prioritized for new arrivals and vulnerable groups including women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities or other specific needs to ensure that the most urgent humanitarian and protection needs are addressed.

1 - Needs

Sudan is host to 1.15 million refugees (Humanitarian Needs Overview - HNO, 2023) and asylum seekers from countries such as South Sudan, Eritrea, the Central African Republic, Ethiopia, Chad, and Syria. The fighting in Sudan has increased vulnerabilities of refugees and asylum seekers across the country and the destabilization of Sudan since April 2023 means increased vulnerabilities, secondary displacement (intra/interstate and cross border movements or forced returns) of Sudanese and other refugees, asylum seekers, third country nationals. In Khartoum the ongoing conflict has caused secondary displacement of refugees and asylum seekers, including South Sudanese, Eritreans, Ethiopians, Syrians and other nationalities from Khartoum to White Nile, Kassala, Port Sudan, Aj Jazirah, Gedaref and other states. There are further concerns that secondary displacement will increase the vulnerabilities of the refugees and premature returns to countries of origin, will place refugees at risk of unsafe returns. According to the IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix, the current conflict in Sudan has caused the mixed cross-border movements of 425,482 individuals into neighbouring countries – 64 per cent of arrivals tracked in those countries were Sudanese nationals and 36 per cent were foreign nationals and returnees. The majority of arrivals were reported in Egypt (41.3%), Chad (27%), and South Sudan (20%). Multi-sector needs of the refugee population are high both in and out of camps. Many refugees are living among host communities that already have very limited access to basic services; and in many receiving states the new influxes of the displaced will put further pressure on access to services and increase tensions over already stretched resources. A lack of safe water sources, proper water management, sanitation facilities, and poor hygiene practices have also been observed among these communities including limited waste management and widely practiced open defecation. The gaps in services, negative coping mechanisms and practices will also increase due to the destructive impact of the current crisis. Therefore, there is a great need to support these communities with construction, rehabilitation, and maintenance of water structures and safe sanitation facilities; as well as facilitating hygiene awareness campaigns and waste management campaigns. The current conflict has also further deteriorated the health systems impacting both host communities and refugees. The health and nutritional status of refugees entering Sudan was dire before they arrived in the country but the ongoing conflict has and will continue to increase the health needs of the crisis-affected refugees and host communities. Due to the current crisis there is a great need to strengthen community health preparedness and response capacity since many health facilities are not functional especially in crisis-affected areas and resources such as medicines and medical supplies are limited. The potential for outbreaks of diseases has also increased with diseases such as acute watery diarrhea, cholera, and dengue (already identified as high risk of outbreak before the crisis); current conditions including mass displacement, an overburdened and collapsing health system will only exacerbate the multiple health crises. Vulnerable population groups are also increasingly exposed to protection risks with immediate as well as longer-term harm to their physical and mental wellbeing. This includes an increased number of people unable to access critical services such as shelter, food, and non-food items. Women, girls and at-risk groups are also exposed to risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) as they try to avoid the conflict and access services/assistance. The needs are vast and urgent interventions are needed across all sectors, especially WASH, health, shelter, and protection.

2 - Activities/Outputs

WASH: 1. Monitor water depletion levels, rehabilitate water points to improve access to safe water and increase quantity to 7.5-15 l/p/d (Sphere standards). 2. Assess area needs and provide gender-segregated and sensitive sanitation facilities - latrines, shower blocks, and bathing facilities, constructed in well-lit areas with ramps and railings to provide access for people with disabilities, and locks to ensure privacy and reduce personal security risks. 3. Rehabilitate water yards for refugees and vulnerable and underserved host communities. 4. Ensure the Operation and Management (O&M) of WASH facilities including the provision of tools and training of community committees and mechanics on water quality treatment and monitoring for water points and desludging for sanitation facilities. Support Ministry of Health and Water Resources Management authority staff in monitoring operations at waste stabilization ponds (WSPs) and provide support in purchasing of required consumables to conduct effluent standard tests in government laboratories to ensure standards are met before finally releasing to the environment. 5. Distribute context appropriate Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits, jerrycans and soap to vulnerable households (HH) requiring essential NFIs. 6. Conduct hygiene and cleaning campaigns including the mainstreaming of COVID-19 mitigation measures to encourage the community to develop waste management protocols that prevent environmental pollution and promote the use of better hygiene practices. ES/NFI: 1. Based on initial Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) assessments, conduct ES/NFI specific vulnerability assessments to identify and select vulnerable refugee and host community HHs in need of immediate shelter and NFI assistance. 2. Identify suppliers and procure materials for emergency shelters - plastic sheets, poles, ropes, grass mats, etc. Cluster standards NFI kits: 1) two 4 by 6 meters plastic sheets, 2) two jerrycans, 3) one kitchen set with 21 pieces such as pots and utensils; 4) three blankets, 5) three sleeping mats, 6) two mosquito nets, and 7) one solar light. 3. Purchase and distribute NFIs and environmentally friendly materials for emergency shelters from the local market based on the needs/ shelter designs. 4. Provide training to beneficiary HHs on proper usage of the NFIs and construction techniques including fire and flood mitigation measures, and assist in the assembly/construction of emergency shelters, to ensure durability and sustainability. 5. Carry out post distribution monitoring (PDM) for the selected vulnerable HHs receiving assistance. Health: 1. Conduct needs assessment in coordination with MoH and the Cluster leads to ensure that IOM and health and nutrition partners do not duplicate services and focus on emergency response and recovery plans for refugees and host communities in target areas. 2. Increase access to health services by rehabilitating health facilities and providing medical equipment and furniture. 3. Provide BPPHS and integrated nutrition service, including outpatient consultation and treatment for communicable diseases and non-communicable diseases including MHPSS, supporting maternal health including antenatal/postnatal care, family planning and deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants, infant and young child feeding counselling, nutrition screening and referral. 4. Refer specialized cases for further treatment. 5. Strengthen the capacity of healthcare providers and the community to support essential health services through training of health staff, community health workers, and community midwives. 6. Provide community health and nutrition programmes to enhance education on these topics, community-based surveillance, and referral pathways.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Water sources will only be rehabilitated after a geophysical study is conducted and ground water levels are monitored. Ground water levels will be constantly monitored and analyzed during rehabilitation; for example, through installation of data loggers. Volumes of water extracted will be recorded giving an overview of the extent of extraction from the aquifer versus recharge levels. IOM will also work closely with the WASH Cluster, targeted Communities, Ministry of Irrigation and Water Resources, Higher Council for Environment and Natural Resources, Range Land Authority, and Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry to ensure that Integrated Water Resource Management Systems (IWRM) are established. Calculations to deduce water balance will be done as per inputs from these entities to ensure there is no over-extraction of water from the targeted catchment areas. Hygiene and garbage collection campaigns will also be designed in a way to encourage land preservation through proper/ responsible garbage disposal methods and systems. Health facilities will also be encouraged to employ proper IPC and waste management protocols in line with MOH and WHO guidelines and standards. IOM will also provide health programs that raise awareness among refugees and host community about environmental health risks and causes and mitigation measures for diseases like malaria, cholera, and COVID-19. The awareness campaigns will focus on issues like preventing the spread of infectious disease through vector control, proper waste disposal and improving personal hygiene practices. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development and population growth have made water resources extremely important to Sudan’s economic development and social cohesion; IOM will focus on finding alternatives from fuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, scalable, environmentally sustainable and climate smart. Studies will also be conducted to research on use of biogas from solid waste in an effort to save trees as an alternative to firewood and charcoal. For shelter/NFI activities, beneficiaries will be involved in awareness raising sessions on hazard mitigation such as safe construction of emergency shelters, use of environmentally friendly materials, and the environmental impact related to flood and fire hazards, in addition to protecting their shelters and best handling of NFIs for longer term use.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project activities will be designed in a way to support women, children, and people with disabilities. Special attention will be paid to women, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), most vulnerable to illness and severely lacking in access to basic services and support needed. For WASH activities, consultative participatory approaches will be employed to allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identification of priority needs based on gender and vulnerability, especially for the distribution of the Menstrual Hygiene Management kits. IOM will undertake participatory assessments/exercise to identify gender and age specific needs and context specific risks of different groups, especially women and girls, related to access and safety around service provision. This inclusive participatory approach assures the inclusion of ideas from vulnerable women and girls, and people with disabilities, who are often excluded from community decisions or underrepresented during decision making processes, especially with regard to selecting the location for the rehabilitation of facilities and shelter sites. The facilities themselves will be built with gender sensitivity, accessibility, privacy, and safety in mind considering protection risks are addressed and mitigated and chosen locations in nearby, well-lit areas with ramps and railings, and locks on the doors of the latrines and bathing facilities and for water points to decrease walking distances to reduce associated risks of gender-based violence (GBV) especially for women and children. Locations of shelter structures will also be chosen through community consultations and built in areas where there is access to other basic services. Based on needs assessments, IOM will ensure that most vulnerable HHs with people with specific needs including women, children, persons with disabilities, will be prioritized to receive emergency shelters. During the construction of the shelters the most vulnerable will receive additional support to assemble emergency shelters. Privacy and safety standards will be a top priority for the shelter construction activities especially emphasizing construction techniques and training of community on disaster risk reduction measures that address fire and flood related risk to the shelters. Health services will provide support to the most vulnerable beneficiaries but also wider host community which will reduce community tensions over resources. Pregnant women will be prioritized for assistance including antenatal care, skilled deliveries, and postnatal care.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Under this project, consultative participatory approaches employed will allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identification of priority needs based on gender, age, disability, and vulnerability. The participatory approach assures the inclusion of beneficiaries and the available avenues of feedback and complaint mechanisms, which IOM and its partners can use to address activity design flaws or other issues. For instance, individualized interviews with vulnerable beneficiaries especially women and girls during certain activities like PDM missions opens communication channels for beneficiaries to discuss topics they may not want to discuss in a group setting. IOM is part of the inter-agency coordination groups and will ensure to follow the relevant guidelines on accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms as agreed by the inter-agency groups. As for the prevention of SEA, IOM has zero Tolerance policy towards SEA and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA as well as guidelines from IOM's Institutional GBV in crises framework on how to manage GBV related cases during crises. IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to refer GBV cases through the referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to any implementing partners to ensure compliance.
