Project Summary

Oxfam is proposing a 12 months action aiming to reach the total of 75,000 most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in worst affected locations of Deir EzZor (Al Mayadin, Deir-ez-Zor, Khasham,Tabni), Rural Damascus (Kafr Batna) and Aleppo (Hadher, Tall Ed-daman, As-Safira, Menbij) governorates.
The action aims to: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining NFI and shelter support through a) Provision of core and essential NFIs b) Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter.
The Action is informed by rapid assessments, observation visits, Oxfam's ongoing work in the three governorates, and reliable secondary data, including the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2019. The action is designed to meet specific NFIs needs of affected populations suitable to access situation and security challenges; this includes implementation through reliable and experienced public health promotion volunteers and focal points and Oxfam local partners.
Oxfam is proposing to deliver 7,500 family winter Jacket Kits (1 kit per family consisting of 1 man, 1 woman and 3 children waterproof jackets) along with 37,500 blankets for 7500 Family (5 blankets per family).
In January 2018, in Deir EzZor , after the area was freed from ISIS, Oxfam was amongst the first few humanitarian actors on the ground providing humanitarian assistance. Its interventions in the area included the distribution of family winterization kits (25,700 household HHs) and distribution of mini hygiene kits to (20,000 HHs).
In October and November 2018, in Tal Rifaat in Aleppo and Arbin and Harasta in Rural Damascus, Oxfam respectively supported 10,750 IDPs children from East Ghouta and 6,400 children displaced from Afrin with basic clothing.
Oxfam is currently preparing for the distribution of 5,000 family winter jackets and 11,500 blankets in Sheikh Maqsoud area in Aleppo city.
Building on previous experiences and based on Oxfam’s findings and communications with beneficiaries for their urgent needs, Oxfam is proposing to replicate this activity in Deir EzZor, Rural Damascus and Aleppo, specifically providing for all the members in the family including different groups based on gender and age.by procuring kits in advance of winter. Distribution would take place for vulnerable families wherever the need is highest at the time of intervention.
The activity has additional impact, as Oxfam is keen to ensure that the procured NFIs would be locally manufactured focusing on women’s employment (supplier selection criteria would support production of materials by women). Through the proposed project, Oxfam will be supporting the IDPs and vulnerable affected families as well as returnees through NFIs distribution in Deir EzZor, Rural Damascus and Aleppo in specific areas which experience hard conditions during winter and continue to need seasonal support.

Needs

In 2019, Syria has been witnessing rapidly changing dynamics of the eight years of long conflict since additional areas have been retaken by the Syrian Government. Vast areas, especially in the North of the country are still out of the control of the Government of Syria. The needs in Syria in the current year have only increased, with the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2019 reporting an estimated 11.7 million persons in need and 5 million persons in acute need, out of which an estimated 6.2 million are internally displaced, 611 thousand people are newly displaced and 1 million people are returnees. 4.4 million persons are in need for NFI assistance only. Oxfam has seen some ease in access granted by the GoS to different degrees for needs assessment especially in Deir EzZor, Rural Damascus and Aleppo.Between 17-23 September 2018, Oxfam conducted rapid needs assessment in Deir EzZor and between 29-31 December 2018, another needs assessment was conducted in Aleppo. Lack of winterization items especially clothing and blankets were communicated through key informant interviews, and focus group discussions with affected communities. The communities in the three governorates have experienced conflict and multiple displacements, while moving, they carried few belongings with them. Majority of assessed communities reported not having adequate clothes and blankets to resist the hard winter conditions and more specific severe needs were expressed by communities for children's clothing. Severe humanitarian challenges included shortages in non-food commodities and the inability to compensate.Access to NFIs has generally improved and become more stable. However, needs remain substantial, particularly among the newly displaced IDPs and vulnerable affected communities requiring seasonal assistance due to unavailability and unaffordability.Within this context, readiness to support displaced, vulnerable affected people and returnees, particularly during the hard winter months in Syria is prioritised. As evidenced by previous Oxfam responses, populations in the target locations are economically pressured and eventually are the most people in need.Oxfam will use this funding to prioritise support in the locations under most acute emergency need for delivery of non-food items, for women, men, children and elderly on the move and vulnerable affected communities. The project target selected criteria are in line with “protection, vulnerability and prioritizing the most in need”. Implementation of activities

Oxfam will implement the distribution of winter jackets kits and blankets with consideration to female and male specific needs according to their sex and age. This project is designed in consideration to Oxfam gender minimum standards to support vulnerable women and men, girls and boys. Prior to implementation of project and distribution of materials; 1- Supplier selection would consider quality and quantity of products. The supplier selection would consider his agenda of supporting women employment without violating their rights in (decent job, timing and salary). Community advocacy to denounce sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and sensitization around SGBV; 2- Monitoring women’s and girls’ safety throughout the project and providing a strong and safe referral and feedback mechanism to access SGBV prevention and response services as well as measure beneficiary’s satisfaction.Distribution location, timing and handover person would be identified based on consultation process with local communities (women and men). • Activity 1: Provision of blankets• Activity 2: Provision of winter clothing Oxfam will directly implement and monitor this operation. During and after the distribution of the kits and blankets, Oxfam will administer DDM (during distribution monitoring) and PDM (post distribution monitoring) questionnaires in all target locations.

Protection Risk Analysis

Oxfam will involve women and men beneficiaries to ensure their concerns and recommendations are considered during site selection to minimise protection risks.Physical and psychological harm arising from harassment, violence and coercion. Oxfam would address this risk by providing feedback and accountability mechanisms by establishing a help desk to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The distributions may not be started due to access constraints to the target locations. In case of any access concerns, Oxfam will coordinate with its partners in the target governorates to support in the implementation of distributions. Rapid increase in Beneficiaries numbers in target locations. Oxfam will work with other actors to meet expanding needs. This will minimize duplication and appropriate and efficient use of available resources. While implementing the distributions, further communication with Oxfam for other urgent beneficiaries’ NFIs needs in targeted locations. Oxfam will work with other actors to meet other life-saving needs.
