Project Summary

Internal conflict has severely affected the livelihood of HHs in Tigray, Amhara, Afar, Oromia, SNNP and Somalia regions. A number of peoples are displaced and farmers couldn’t produce crop in these areas. Large amount of resources is lost including livestock, household utensils, veterinary clinics etc. Farmers/pastoralist are not able to access inputs as before for livestock and crop input supply systems are affected. Other off farm livelihood activities are disrupted due to the conflict.
The great East African drought has affected livelihood of peoples in Somalia, Eastern & Southern Oromia and southern parts of SNNP regions. As a result, food security of the population has been affected expected to deteriorated in the years to come, unless appropriate recovery measures have been taken. To cope up the disaster and feed their family, affected HHs sell productive assets and HHs equipment. Coupled with market inflation and macro-economic crisis, conflict and drought has affected the market supply severely. Therefore, to reduce the impact of these disasters and restore livelihood of the affected community appropriate and timely emergency response is required. Accordingly, this project aims to restore the livelihood of farming community in 14 woredas from drought affected and 26 woredas from conflict affected woredas.
Project Goal: Contribute to improved livelihood situation and food security of conflict and drought affected HHs of the targeted woredas and rehabilitation of their livelihoods affected by the disasters.
Purpose:
•To improve food and nutrition security of affected communities through provision of agricultural input and improve production
•To restore productive assets of the affected communities and build their resilience to further shock.
•To restore off farm livelihoods and ensure functionality of systems and structures that contributes to livelihood of the community
The project will target 300,000 HHs (Male headed HHs 210,000, Female headed HHs 90,000). The project will prioritize most affected Woredas and most affected vulnerable groups such as female headed HHs, child headed HHs, people living with disability, HIV, Elderly, poorest of the poor and HHs who lost their assets. Other community groups will be benefitted through the assistance of community-based services like veterinary support.
Major project activities: 1: Provision of key inputs to facilitate engagement in rain fed and irrigation-based crop (eg. vegetable & fruit) production. Crops seeds, cereal crop seeds (tef, maize, sorghum, wheat and barley) and pulse crops (Faba bean, field pea, Haricot bean and chick pea) will be procured and provided based on crop suitability of the specific area and supplied to farmers. Vegetable seeds and fruit seedlings will be procured and provided to farmers, particularly women farmers enable to access nutritious food. The project also will support farm tools.
Activity 2: Restocking support like oxen, heifer, sheep, goat, poultry, camel and donkey based on the specific needs of the community.
Activity 3 Multi-purpose cash transfer: cash will be transfer for poor and severely affected households. Moreover, Cash will be provided together with crop seed or animal feed to maximize the benefit.
Activity 4: Technical support and training will be provided.
Other sectors like access to improved WASH, nutrition, food, health etc will be integrated during the project implementation and linked to ensure food and nutrition security of the targeted HHs. The project will mainstream cross cutting themes; environment, protection, gender, AAP, peace building & conflict resolution. Fruits that provide multiple benefit or have agroforestry purpose will be planted and farmers will be trained to practice organic farming rather than using inorganic chemicals, fertilizer and pesticides-avoid GHG emission.

1. - Projects activities

Activity package 1: Provision of key inputs to facilitate engagement in rain fed and irrigation-based crop (including vegetable and fruit) production a) Crops seeds provision such as cereals/staple crops and protein dense pulses as per the agro ecology of specific target woredas b) Vegetable seeds provision: to encourage farmers to re-engage in irrigation cropping of vegetable seed c) Fruit seedling provision: with focus on fruits that need short period to mature such as papaya, apple and also grafted mango fruit seedlings will be supplied to farmers and cover both in irrigation area and homestead aread) Provision farm tools: with the timing of the project focus will be given to farm tools that are required for harvesting season mainly sickles, and water can for micro-garden beneficiaries; e) Multipurpose cash transfer for labor poor women headed households and severely war affected households: as most households have no cash at hand for required necessary investment to effectively re-engage in crop production, the project will provide multi- purpose cash transfer for investment in areas of traction, fertility inputs, labor inputs… to be disbursed as per the decision of the household. Farmers (mainly labor poor female headed households and HHs that are severely affected by the conflict) with each of the beneficiary receiving ETB 4,000.00 Activity Package 2: Restocking support with focus on livestock that need short period to generate return, households who lost their livestock by the conflict and drought.

The World Vision Ethiopia has experience of working in these regions for more than three decades in areas of both emergency/humanitarian response and community-based development. WVE is currently engaged in providing wide range of humanitarian response assistance, including: (1) provision of food distribution with plan to reach to 932,716 people – including IDPs, PSNP beneficiaries and conflict affected communitiesA number of coordination structures and mechanisms have been put in place in the regions, mirroring the existing cluster system that exists at national level for humanitarian response. The regions Coordination Center (ECC) is attended by Regional Government line bureaus sectors and Cluster Lead Agencies and meets weekly. The ECC is complemented by a recently formed Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) in the regions. UNOCHA host a weekly meeting as do the HINGO group. Additionally, all Humanitarian cluster groups meet on a weekly basis for information sharing and coordination of efforts (nutrition, WASH, protection, health, food/logistics, education, agriculture, emergency shelter (ES) and non-food items (NFIs). World vision is active in all humanitarian cluster groups at a national level and have been attending key sectoral meetings. World Vision is already deployed project staffs who will be able to attend meetings, represent world vision interests, ensure coordination and provide valuable informational updates. Importantly, world vision as a key implementing partner also actively engages in these meetings and can assist the proposed project in ensuring a coordinated response and efficient use of resources. During project implementation world vision and the project donor will ensure coordination with any additional actors with response in the targeted woredas. This livelihood recovery project in particular will work with bureau of Agriculture, DRM and Administration offices at all level. The project will work closely with related institutions like cooperatives, seed enterprises, poultry farms and etc

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Feedback and Complaint Mechanisms: World Vision Ethiopia will conduct initial consultations with community representatives to establish feedback and complaints committees at kebele and Tabia level and agree on mechanisms and processes for submission of complaints. The name of individuals submitting complaints will be kept confidential throughout feedback and complaint handling process. World vision Ethiopia will assign one accountability focal person per woreda who will receive feedback and complaints and communicate the issue to management (inclusive of donor). Information about complaints mechanisms will then be shared with communities in pre agreed locations (likely to include screening and distributions sites,). World vision in partnership with donor will regularly review the available capacity and accountability systems and take corrective actions to improve.
