Project Summary

The situation in northern Ethiopia continues to be tense and volatile. The hostilities continue to affect civilian lives, their safety and restricting their access to livelihoods, increasing the humanitarian needs notably with a continuing ad growing number of displaced people and hindering humanitarian access and delivery of aid to affected areas.
According to different needs assessments conducted in the regions have indicated lack of access to basic services and, needs and gaps in service provision which exacerbates protection risks of women and girls in the IDP sites. IDPs in the project sites are residing in sub-standard shelters, sharing sleeping space and in an unlockable shelter exposing them to various protection and Sexual Gender Based Violence (SGVB) risks. Settlements are unsafe for women and girls because of mixed sex sleeping arrangements and overcrowding. The shelters are further lacking windows, doors and fencing. These factors contribute to the increasing rate of SGBV in the IDP sites. Provision of shelter and ensuring a safe living environment is the prioritized need of IDPs, especially of women and girls.
According to DPO rapid needs assessments show the following acute needs. In the targeted woreda of Jamma conflict occurred over the last three months and highly affected the livelihood of surviving households. Out of the total kebeles of the woreda, 14 rural kebeles and the two urban kebeles of Dogolo town were affected. The degree of hit differs from kebeles to kebeles and a total of 543 houses were totally destroyed by the war and a total of 230 houses were partially damaged. Families who lost their houses were displaced and camped at a different place at the beginning and now returned to the woreda and hosted by extended families until their home is reconstructed or maintained. In Raya Kobo woreda, according to the gathered information from Raya Kobo Woreda, there are more than 28,000 IDPs who are living in 2 centers which are Dingay Kulili and Millennium schools. Almost all urban and rural kebeles are affected by the conflict and the problem is continuing since the conflict has occurred in Raya Kobo, Raya Alamata, and Waja Timuga kebeles. The majority of the IDPs are residing in the centers, Host community & at two sites /Millennium School & Dengay Kulil schools. The participants of the discussion clearly state that the living condition of the IDPs in the centers (schools) is worsening. There is no room for privacy, lack of electricity, shortage of water, poor waste management & other problems. Almost all the IDPs mentioned that shelter and NFI are the top priority needs of them. During our physical observation at one of the IDP sites in the town kobo, where the majority of the IDPs are displaced from Raya Alamata woreda, we noticed that there is an overcrowded living condition with an average of more than 35 individuals in the classroom. Hence, DPO has a presence in this woredas, this project is proposed in close consultation with the sub National cluster and based on the assessment recommendation DPO plan to distribute NFI kits for the target woredas.
This project will provide emergency lifesaving activities for 5,248 Households which means HHs will have access to NFI, and 15% disability kits included (3,081 HH at Kobo town, and 667 HH at Sekota woredas) and 1500 HH at Habru woreda jara idp sites will have emergency shelter at Amhara Region.

1. - Projects activities

DPO has already in place an agreement with EDRMC; briefing on ESNFI guidelines for distribution in COVID-19 context; training and briefing on PSEA based on the Ethiopia Network Training Package; protection mainstreaming approach. -Sites ‘selection in the target woredas, prioritizing those with higher number of IDPs, access to basic services; Shelter and NFI needs/gaps and protection threats. -Procurement of the materials/items according to the type of response and the technical specifications and quantities as designed by the Cluster -Beneficiaries‘ identification, selection, verification and registration considering the following criteria: displaced households who have not received any Shelter/NFI support; female-headed and child-headed HHs, HHs with 6 children or more, with member(s) part of the vulnerability groups (infant, children, elders etc.) or households with specific needs (a member with disability, pregnant women, persons with critical medical conditions, among others). The criteria stated above will be revised and agreed according to the feedback of the main stakeholders in line with humanitarian principles with the Shelter&NFI cluster at Federal, regional and sub-regional level. -Set up of a feedback and complaints mechanisms aimed at sharing information on the project and entitlements, implementing organizations and donor; reporting mechanisms, installation of suggestion boxes and notice boards at site level, the identification of formal and informal leaders/community committees in charge of delivering feedbacks/complaints to DPO. -Set-up of selection committees made of elders; religious leaders; local authorities, IDPs/Returnees and hosting community representatives and DPO Staff. The project will ensure equal participation of women and men. DPO will ensure that data will be disaggregated by gender and age and, when it applies, the type of disability. DPO will ensure that its data protection policy is in place. This project will provide emergency lifesaving activities for 5,248 Households which means HHs will have access to NFI, and 15% disability kits included (3,081 HH at Kobo town, and 667 HH at Sekota woredas) and 1500 HH at Habru woreda jara idp sites will have emergency shelter at Amhara Region.Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM). It will assess the quality of the process and the degree of satisfaction of beneficiaries regarding the following dimensions: Quality control of process (Assessment, verification, distribution)/ Effectiveness of the response; Accountability to affected population (Information provision/Sharing, participation, feedback and complaint mechanism); distribution process; timeliness of the response; Protection; quality control of the items, appropriateness, usage and satisfaction.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

DPO will ensure that information sharing and coordination for the project activities implementation is maintained at all levels. At national level, DPO will continue attending and sharing information in the Shelter and NFIs cluster's meetings as well as submitting timely 5Ws/updating Report Hub for the cluster's coordinator. DPO will ensure proper engagement and participation in the Shelter&NFI clusters at subnational and zonal levels, in order to avoid duplication of efforts, maximizing use of resources and supporting an integrated emergency response. DPO will establish clear, easily accessible mechanisms that allow partner staff and community members to provide feedback on the project and how it is implemented. These mechanisms will be included in the MEAL plan and accountability framework developed for the project and include locally preferred and most effective means, such as surveys, direct feedback, face-to-face meetings, focal persons and suggestion boxes. DPO will also deploy Safeguarding Monitors to distribution/activity locations to observe distributions and converse with participants, providing another direct avenue for sharing feedback and any safeguarding concerns. Timely responses will be provided for programmatic issues reported by staff and community members.DPO will actively involve target communities and local government from the initial introductory meeting in order to gain the full support of the local community and concerned local authorities. The project will also promote ownership through the joint participation of the hosting local community and IDP representatives in implementation and monitoring of the response activities. Before project commencement, DPO will organize meeting with beneficiaries to discuss on the planned response including the planned activities, type and number of NFIs kits and the duration of the support.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Affected populations will actively participate in all levels of the project cycle, including in the rapid needs assessments in the design and planning, implementation as well as in monitoring and evaluation. DPO will establish information sharing mechanisms to enhance participation and ensure accountability to beneficiaries.DPO will maintain a commitment to engaging with affected communities through focus group discussions with women, men, and youth on issues regarding their concerns and feedback about the project. Regular meetings with community leaders in the areas of operation will generate feedback for the calibration of activities.Using the different complaints and feedback mechanisms, beneficiaries can report compliances in person, to DPO staff or anonymously by using the reporting tool in the area.DPO will pursue a people-centered and rights-based approach that will promote empowerment and protection of sexual and gender minorities, women, and girls. Additionally, regular oversight will be provided, and monitoring visits conducted to observe service delivery. Feedback collection mechanisms will be instituted, and data will be collected and segregated by gender and age.The projects will work with all segments of society equally. It will ensure participation of men, women, boys, girls, people with disability, people with chronic illness and the like starting from the inception of the project. The project intends to actively engage and provide opportunities to communities in targeted areas to participate in all aspects of the project. The project will ensure that protection is mainstreamed in all activities of the project by using a protection mainstreaming checklist. In beneficiary identification, priority will be given for vulnerable groups such as FHH, PWDs, pregnant and lactating mothers. Distribution of in-kind NFIs will be made in a time and places that is safe for women and girls and PWDs to avoid any safety risks. Trainings will also be provided in a time and place that is convenient for women and people with special needs to increase their participation. Training on gender-based violence and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse will be provided for selected beneficiaries, and representatives of government and other partner organizations to raise their awareness and to allow beneficiaries to report if cases of sexual exploitation and abuse occurs. At time of the training COVID-19 prevention and mitigation measures will be adapted.
