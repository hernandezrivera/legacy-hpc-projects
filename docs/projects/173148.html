Project Summary

The project intends to provide lifesaving/humanitarian assistance to refugees in camps, livelihood recovery, and resilience-building support for the host community population, targeting the most vulnerable population such as poor households relying on wage labor, households with no or very limited productive assets, households affected by disasters (Covid 19, cyclone, heavy monsoon, influx, etc.). Households with a high dependency ratio, headed by women, children, chronically sick persons and households having an individual with special needs like PWD, PLW, elderly persons will get priority while targeting beneficiaries. The ultimate goal of the project is to contribute to ensuring food and nutrition security through emergency assistance, livelihood recovery support to promote income for both refugees and the host community population. To achieve the goal, the project intends to undertake food security and livelihoods interventions such as (1) Food assistance (cooked meals); the main target for cooked meals from the community kitchen is Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs), Children under 5 years, adolescents, elderly person, persons with a disability could be entitled to this support (2) Livelihoods assistance; support for homestead based food production, nutrition education, food safety, and hygiene sessions, Business plan development, Livelihood/IGAs grant support, and Livelihoods restoration activity are planned to reach this activity (3) Self-reliance; technical training and inputs support for kitchen gardening, food processing, and small business are planned to reach this activity (4) Skill development (support for skill-based technical training and inputs) (5) Disaster Risks Management through small scale risk mitigation measures. CFW will be linked with small-scale risk mitigation measures to reduce disaster risk as well as to provide temporary employment for the affected population.
The project will ensure a safe environment by upholding gender equality as per IASC Gender and Age Marker. Protection mainstreaming principles are some of the measures taken to ensure gender-responsive programming. The project will give priority to special groups; female-headed households, PLW, under 5 children, PWDs, and the Elderly during the implementation of project interventions according to their needs. Sex and Age Disaggregated data will be collected, analyzed, and used to understand the needs of men and women. Those needs will be taken into account during service delivery to the project participants. Creating an enabling environment for the women participants is another key consideration and it will be taken into account in all service facilities including capacity building training in training centers.
The project will ensure the active participation of the community in different activities. The project will have a contingency plan to respond to man-made and natural disasters (monsoon flood, landslide, and cyclone) with close collaboration with all stakeholders.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar District is one of the poorest districts in Bangladesh, highly susceptible to environmental and economic shocks such as natural disasters, manmade humanitarian crisis, and COVID-19/health pandemics. Almost 50% of the population in this district belongs to lower-income groups who basically rely on unreliable income sources mainly wage labor. These population groups are the main victim of these disasters and making them more vulnerable both socially and economically. The proposed projects will be implemented in the areas included in the Joint Response Plan for Cox’s Bazar district coordinated by ISCG in where women and girls constitute about half of the population (51%) and almost 31.6% cannot decide about their own concerns related to their lives and livelihoods. Only 16.6% can decide whether to have a baby or not, 73.4% are totally immersed and responsible for unpaid household chores like childcare, cooking and serving foods, and taking care of others. 61.6% of women and girls have unmet needs for food, shelter, and sanitation. SMART survey (October 2019 for refugees), shows a stagnant rate of malnutrition with a slow recovery rate (GAM 10.9% - CI 8.0-14.7%). The coverage survey (SQUEAC Nov 2019) indicates a significant increase in access to and coverage of nutrition services [SAM: 76.9% (95% CI: 72.1-81.7%), MAM 61% (95% CI: 57.5-64.6%)], it is still below Sphere standards (>90%). Anaemia among children 6-23 months (>50%) and stunting among children 0-59 months (>40%) remains a major concern. While for the Bangladeshi host community the SMART survey 2018 conducted in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila showed a GAM rate of 11.4% (8.9 - 14.5 95% C.I) with SAM rate of 1.5% (0.8 – 3.1 95% C.I.). According to ACF October 2020 food security survey conducted in our intervention area in camps, HDDS is 35%. The Fill the Nutrient Gap Analysis (WFP in 2019) revealed that almost none of the Rohingya households can afford a nutritious diet and remain 100% reliant on food assistance from humanitarian aid. WFP Household economic assessment indicates nearly 70% population as poor in Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas. According to REVA 2019, levels of vulnerability remain high in refugees. 94 % of all Rohingya refugees are highly and moderately vulnerable and in continued need of humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs. A higher share of households headed by women is vulnerable (52 %) than those led by men (38 %). Over 31,000 of the camps’ population are still living in areas considered to be at high risk of deadly flooding and landslides, with drainage congestion and waterlogging. The situation has further aggravated by the COVID-19 crisis with a severe impact on the income and earning opportunities and automatically on food security. According to the J-MSNA report, more people are likely to fall down below the poverty line due to loss of income and employment. 93% of the households in the host community reported diminished income as an impact of the COVID-19 outbreak. According to the Action Against Hunger’s Rapid Assessment, about 88% of the refugee households reported no income due to COVID-19. 66% of refugees and 78% of the host community households reported having reduced expenditure on food, while 66% of the household in host community reported less access to food as an impact of COVID-19 [J-MSNA]. The pandemic and its subsequent containment measures impacted food security and livelihood execrating existing poverty in the host community (ISCG/WFP situation report).
