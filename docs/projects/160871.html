Project Summary

This project will improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced or populations with new vulnerabilities and improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees and host communities/affected non-displaced who are unable to meet their SNFI needs in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo counties. This project targets 10000 persons (1667 Households) 50% in Mundri East, 30% in Mundri West and 20% in Mvolo counties respectively.
According to 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview, an estimated 2,442,716 individuals require Shelter and Non-Food Items (S/NFI) assistance in 2020 and IDPs outside of displacement sites may have better access to shelter materials but due to the absence of functional and accessible markets they lack essential household items, in particular, IDPs that have been subject to multiple displacement. This figure includes the internally displaced person (IDPs), returnees and the Host Communities (HC). ASO's well contextual knowledge of the area will enable easy access to the hard to reach areas with experience acquired through joint SNFIs intervention with LCED in Mundri. ASO is better positioned to access hard to reach areas with lifesaving NFIs and emergency shelter materials since it has established a long term relationship with local communities and authorities as a result of its well established static presence in Mundri as well as Mvolo and will continue to work in close coordination with LCED and other shelter and nonfood items cluster partners in similar manner to ensure quality and efficiency as a static SNFI Cluster partner in Mundri.
ASO will depend on SNFI cluster pipeline supplies during the project. Register and verify the beneficiaries to establish the target number to be supported using an agreed selection criteria. This will be done through community engagements supported by the community leaders selected within the target area. A total of 1667 household will be supported with in-kind NFI and 334 households of specifically vulnerable groups like elderly, widows and disabled persons will have additional support of shelter from Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo counties. ASO's response will be in-kind and ASO will continue to work with other cluster partners doing cash program and learn from them for future response. There will be Consultation with the target beneficiaries and local leaders, safe and secure sites for distributions and schedule will be identified. ASO will closely work with local authorities for continuous security updates. ASO will hire community crowd controllers within the target area to support ASO during distributions. A complaint desk will be established at the distribution centre to address any grievances raised by the target beneficiaries and ensure continuous process to ensure ASO is implementing the project as per cluster standards in addition to ensuring the project meets its intended objectives. The beneficiaries will be part of the monitoring process right from the registration and will provide feedback that will form the basis for project reporting. A database containing names of the registered and supported beneficiaries will be designed by ASO M&E department and a post distribution monitoring will be conducted with support from Shelter Cluster to measure project performance across key focus areas of accountability to beneficiaries, effectiveness, efficiency and impact. Monitoring reports shall then be submitted to cluster periodically.
ASO will work with the other Housing, land and property partners in Mundri to ensure that housing, land and property issues arising during the intervention is resolved.

1 - Needs

The 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview indicates that an estimated 2,442,716 individuals require Shelter and Non-Food Items (S/NFI) assistance in 2020. This figure includes the internally displaced person (IDPs), returnees and the Host Communities (HC). Based on the REACH Situation overview of April-June 2019 Western Equatoria State situation overview. The Episodes of conflict, displacement and associated food insecurity have had a negative impact on the humanitarian situation in Western Equatoria State. Information remains critical, however logistic and security constraints have limited humanitarian access to many areas across the region. In the second quarter of 2019, Western Equatoria experienced a continuing mixture of internal returns, refugee returns, and protracted displacement from past incidents of insecurity and hunger. The report indicated that Access to durable shelter varied by population. The proportion of assessed settlements reporting host community use of permanent structures for shelter remained high, but 28% of assessed settlements with IDPs reported the main shelter type for IDPs to be temporary forms of shelter. In particular participants noted the needs for saucepans/cooking pots, tents, and jerry cans (cooking pots were also reported as the most-needed NFIs for IDPs in Western Equatoria as a whole). According to REACH Assessment of hard to reach areas as of July 2019 report; 47% of the population in Mundri East county is reporting blankets as their primary NFI need, while 36% population in Mvolo reporting blankets as their primary NFI need. 81% population in Mundri West reporting cooking pots as their primary NFI need and 45% population in Mvolo reporting cooking pots as their primary NFI need. Proportion of assessed settlements reporting presence of IDPs in Mvolo, Mundri West and Mundri East counties is between 81-100%. According LCED distribution report of 17 June 2019, all the returnees residing in Bishop Gunyi buildings seemed to be vulnerable, due to the limited assistance, the project had to prioritise guided by the selection criteria. This meant that some returnees could not benefit as well as a few host communities who continuously share the meagre resources they have with the returnees. According to IOM DTM Flowing Monitoring Registry Publication of 10 October 2019, the share of self-reported refugees is higher among short-term or uncertain returnees (54.3%). Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo counties are like to receive more returnees during the coming months. The fact-finding mission for return of Kedi’ba IDP in Kedi’ba and Wandi payams, Mundri East of 21 June 2019 reported that the security situation in Kedi’ba and Wandi Payam had improved to such an extent that Kedi’ba 4,878 (942HHs) IDPs in Mundri have expressed willingness to return to their place of origin in order to resume normal livelihood activities. This project will respond to the needs of such Returnees/IDPs as wells as conflict affected non displaced people who are unable to support themselves more so with the expectation of the people after the return of peace due to formation of the new government, if the formation is peaceful and on time, many returnees/IDPs are expected to return to their place of origin, this project respond to the needs of the returnees and IDPs in their areas of return, however this project will not rule out the possibility of worse case scenario such as if the parties to the government fail to form the government and resort to war there will be high new displacement in the area and the project will respond to their needs. This project will response to the shelter and NFI needs of 10000 individuals and 1667 households, Mundri East 5000 (833HH), Mundri West 3000 ( 500HH) and Mvolo 2000 (333 HH).

2 - Activities

The main activities of the project will be the following. 1. Verification and registration of beneficiaries to establish the target number to be supported using an agreed selection criteria. This will be done through community engagements supported by the community leaders selected within the target area. A total of 1667 households will receive in-kind NFI assistance in Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo while 334 households of targeted beneficiaries within Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo will be supported with Shelter materials. ASO will continue to join LCED during cash program implementation to equip itself with cash based intervention methodologies for future response 2 Distribution of S/NFIs and cash assistance; In consultation with the target beneficiaries and local leaders, safe and secure sites for distributions and schedule will be identified. ASO will closely work with local authorities for continuous security updates. ASO will hire community crowd controllers within the target area to support ASO during distributions. A complaint desk will be established at the distribution centre to address any grievances raised by the target beneficiaries. ASO will sign a pipeline agreement with the shelter and NFI pipeline to ensure it access the SNFI supplies 3 Monitoring; this will be a continuous process to ensure ASO is implementing the project as per cluster standards in addition to ensuring the project meets its intended objectives. The beneficiaries will be part of the monitoring process right from the registration and will provide feedback that will form the basis for project reporting. A database containing names of the registered and supported beneficiaries will be designed by ASO M&E department and a post distribution monitoring will be conducted with support from Shelter Cluster to measure project performance across key focus areas of accountability to beneficiaries, effectiveness, efficiency and impact. Monitoring reports shall then be submitted to cluster periodically. 4. Transit storage and transportation of SNFI materials to the distributions sites from Mundri, ASO will also work with the cluster pipeline in Juba to verify the supplies for their projects during transportation in Mundri by the cluster.
