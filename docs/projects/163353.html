Project Summary

The Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) responds to emergency needs when there is rapid, medium to large-scale population displacement. The RRM is an interagency multisectoral response mechanism implemented jointly by 04 UN agencies: IOM, UNFPA, UNICEF and WFP. The RRM relies on DTM (Displacement Tracking Matrix) for rapid assessment and data verification.The RRM is implemented jointly with a number of national and local NGOs.
The mechanism aims to deliver immediate, life-saving supplies to families and individuals on the move as they flee conflict including those located close to military frontlines, delayed at checkpoints, or stranded in between. In Libya, the RRM was first used to respond to the humanitarian needs of IDPs in Tripoli. The first distribution took place on 11 April 2019 in Ain Zara (the university dorms and Al Asmaa school). Since then, the RRM has provided one-off assistance packages composed of food parcels, immediate response rations, blankets, mattresses, hygiene kits, dignity kits, baby kits as well as PSS services to over 7,233 families (over 35,000 people). RRM responded to the population displaced following conflict in the western area and Murzuq as well as people affected by the floods in Ghat. The RRM has reached out to affected people in 25 municipalities, 12 mantikas in 2019.
In 2020, RRM aims at providing assistance to 12,000 families (60,000 affected people) across the 04 humanitarian sectors.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Libya is largely defined by: 1) Pockets of acute needs prompted by outbreaks of violence, shifts in territorial control of the local power of armed groups, 2) Situation of migrants, refugees and asylum-seekers suffering from grave abuses in official and non-official detention and exposed to extreme risks to their lives while in Libya; and 3) An environment of deepening vulnerability for the population at large, with a rapidly deteriorating economy and public sector. These three dimensions are all rooted in a protection, basic services, economic and governance crisis that has engulfed the country since 2011. In Tripoli, the effects of the conflict continue to impact the civilian population. More than 200 civilians have been killed and more than 150,000 people have fled their homes since the conflict began on 4 April. More than 135,000 civilians remain in frontline areas, and an additional 270,000 people live in areas directly affected by conflict. The humanitarian community estimate that 897,465 people, of which 362,755 are children and 251,290 are women in reproductive age, need humanitarian assistance and protection in 2020. Sudden displacements have been characterized by increased vulnerability of the affected populations in a context of a protracted crisis in Libya. Women and children are among the most vulnerable. The most critical immediate needs of the displaced persons are usually food and basic personal effects for hygiene and dignity as families are uprooted suddenly from their homes with no time to pack anything. While addressing these needs it's important for the humanitarian actors to be aware of the scoial norms and gender power balance. Humanitarian assistance should be provided to all household members and all people in an indiscriminate way. However core relief items should include gender and age sensitive goods. Rapid response needs to target most vulnerable people in a coordinated manner working jointly with NGOs, local authorities, national auhtorities, sectors and humanitarian aid agencies.

2 - Activities

The Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) responds to emergency needs when there is rapid, medium to large-scale population displacement. The mechanism aims to deliver immediate, life-saving supplies to families and individuals on the move as they flee conflict including those located close to military frontlines, delayed at checkpoints, or stranded in between. The mechanism forms the initial emergency first line response, which will then be quickly followed-up by sector-specific or governmental responses that are coordinated through the Inter-Sector Coordination Group led by OCHA or the Emergency Cell.The RRM response is provided one time to newly displaced people within 72 hours from the receiving and verifying information of new displacements by the DTM teams. It doesn’t respond to multiple displacement or chronically affected population (people affected by the crisis for over 3 months). Therefore RRM is designed for rapid on-set emergencies, facilitating response to outbreak of communicable diseases, displaced migrants and refugees out of detention centers.RRM allows for provision of rapid and essential assistance and doesn't only respond to the immediate basic neds for food and non-food items, it's also an early entry point for specialized protection and health services.Through RRM, the four agencies provide the following assistance package:IOM: blankets, mattresses and hygiene kits.UNFPA: Provide dignity kits (including kits for adolescent girls), baby kits, clean delivery kits and psycho-social first aid.UNICEF: Provide hygiene kits, baby kits, blankets, recreational items, mine risk education materialsWFP: Provide unconditional food assistance (in-kind transfer) to crisis-affected vulnerable populations RRM commodities are designed in a cultural, age and gender- sensitive way after consultations with local community and in agreement with sectoral standard kit content. Agencies are prioritizing local procurmenet for faster response and better acceptability
