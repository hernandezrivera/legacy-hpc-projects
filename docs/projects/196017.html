Project Summary

"The Site Management and Site Development (SMSD) project aims to continue the current efforts of IOM to strengthen equitable provision of humanitarian assistance through coordination of service providers, enabling community participation and feedback to humanitarian programming, reducing risks from natural hazards, and increasing the degree of access to key services in refugee camps and host community of Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas. In collaboration with implementing partners (IPs) and key stakeholders, IOM intends to target Rohingya refugees and host communities that will benefit from coordination of service provision, site planning support and interventions for disaster risk reduction (DRR) which contribute to ensuring equitable and safe access to assistance, protection, and services for improved and dignified living conditions. The project follows an integrated approach to improve coordination between (i) the Site Management Support (SMS) and administration functions supporting the Camp in Charges (CiCs) (ii) SMS and the sectoral partners (iii) Site Development (SD) and DRR partners including engineering project vis-a-via Site Maintenance and Engineering Program (SMEP) to ensure services for refugee population has geographic coverage between the blocks and camps under IOM Area of Responsibilities (AoR). The Capacity Sharing Initiative (CSI) will continue to work closely with the Office of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) to enhance the cooperation between humanitarian sectors and actors and the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) in the management of camps.
The SMS agencies will ensure that service providers consider the specific needs, safety and dignity of people of different age, gender and diversity. The components of the project include SMS coordination function, information management, advocacy to maintain minimum standards, the Common Feedback Platform (CFP) and referral mechanism, representation, and engagement of Refugee population with their significant role as an entry point for accountability. The project will focus on increased information-sharing through Communication with Communities (CwC) programs to ensure that camp residents understand their entitlements in terms of minimum services or relief provided. Community participation and empowerment will be enhanced through camp committees and groups. Their capacity enhancement will be through training, resources, and establishment of platforms for meaningful dialogue with camp stakeholders and reinforce community capacity and ownership to maintain camp infrastructures.To mitigate multi-hazard risks, IOM with SMEP and Site Planning support will continuously implement cash-based SD and DRR interventions which include the construction, repair and maintenance of slope stabilization, drainage, access (including pathways, stairs, bridges, vehicular roads, etc.), plantation and environmental restoration and solar streetlights. The scope of work will include assessment, prioritization, and design the solutions to address immediate needs linking with existing planning frameworks and mainstreaming environment to achieve nature-based solutions where possible. The project will continue to strengthen community-based disaster response volunteer capacity, camp level contingency plans and Disaster Management Committee, and effective life-saving rapid emergency coordination and assistance delivery systems.
Need and Population Movement (NPM) will continue to support for informed humanitarian and development decision-making through identification of needs, conditions and service provision for the refugee and host communities, enabling strong evidence-based response planning by administering thematic assessments, joint multi-sectoral needs assessment, assisting the inter sectoral coordination group (ISCG) on the post-disaster and preparedness through daily incident reporting, shelter addressing system, facility mapping and joint needs assessment framework. "

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Since the beginning of the Rohingya influx in 2017, the emergency phases have allowed to respond to the immediate needs to save lives. In its sixth year, the Rohingya crises is still without durable solutions. With decline of funding as experienced in 2022, the space for increasing the degree and quality of services is steadily shrinking. Over the years, the camps have increased risks of protection and severe concerns over safety and security with growing tension over the resources with the host communities. Refugees have left the camps and risked their lives to Malaysia or Indonesia. Increasing number of new arrivals from India and Myanmar and returnees from Bhasan Char puts more pressure to provide basic human necessities in a dignified manner. Involuntary relocation of vulnerable households to Bhasan Char need continuous monitoring for higher level advocacy. Constant changes to services with fluctuating camp actors, administrative rules, create a complicated humanitarian setting that is difficult to navigate without referral systems and information provision. High turnover and rotations of CiCs and their continual disregard of humanitarian and do-no-harm principles, there is continual need for capacity sharing through CSI activities including day-to-day support by experienced SMSD teams to ensure equitable access to and advocacy for humanitarian assistance. Community engagement and participation still remain an area to be strengthened in the absence of a strong community representation system. According to the report “Our Thoughts” (2021, IOM & ACAPs), half the participant reported negative experiences when trying to report problems and issues in the camps. Only 33% of male and 19% of female participants were able to recall a positive experience.72% of female and 67% of male focus group discussion participants did not feel engaged in decision-making processes. They expressed that Mahjis, NGO volunteers, literate people, religious leaders, and older men are more likely to be included in decision making consultations, while illiterate people, younger people, and women and girls, elderly and people with disabilities are not.51% accounts women and girls in the camps with 16% of households as female headed1. The baseline survey conducted by IOM in 2021 indicates that Rohingya women face multifaced barriers and discrimination with limited participation in societal leadership and decision making and restriction to livelihood opportunities. 14% of the Refugee population in the 17 camps are adolescent boys and girls of 12 to 17 years old, who have suffered from lack of higher education and humanitarian assistance tailored to develop their physical and mental well-being. 12% of people with disabilities2 face issues with access, lack of understanding on the issues of disability and absence of support network to raise their specific concerns. Rohingyas continue to live in congested areas exposed to natural hazards. In May 2021, a major fire affected over 9,000 households with 11 officially recognized deaths in Camp 9. On 9 January 2022, another major fire incident in Camp 16 affected 380 households including 7 host families. As per NPM, a total of 22 dead and 61 injuries have been recorded in all camps due to weather related and fire incidents from January to October 2022. It is estimated that currently there are 7,000 families threatened by landslides (around 30,000 people) and 10,000 people are living in flood risk in IOM’s AoR. Access to services and mitigation of risks from natural hazards to ensure safety continue to drive the site development cash based interventions over the coming year. While the fluidity of population movements within the camps has stabilized, facilities/services frequently change which requires NPM to update and analysis to understand key gaps. The challenges presented by the monsoon and cyclones require an information management framework to ensure the impact on populations is monitored effectively.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"Project will follow rationalization principles for efficiency & effectiveness in Rohingya response with presence of 1 agency for SMS & SD agency per camp either directly through IOM or IPs in IOM AoR.SMS will continue to use service monitoring tool with on-site observations to service delivery points will improve quality of response,& help partners rationalize activities.CiCs will benefit from sharing information products from service monitoring,facility mapping,Common Feedback Platforms to make informed decisions re approval of partner’s activities.SMS will advocate & seek support of ISCG for proposals that reduce duplication of services & through regular coordination & dialogues with sector & camp level sector’s FPs will ensure common strategy,protocol,& tools are used across camps.NPM will continue collaborating with ISCG,sectors,& various agencies by designing & conducting various assessments & providing technical mapping capacity through collecting drone imageries & producing maps & footage,visualize site conditions to support operational & strategic needs of sectors,ISCG & others.All partners implementing DRR plantation or energy projects coordinate through SD camp focal point to avoid duplication. Site Planning guidance provided will be followed by all partners to ensure adequacy of work.Unlike SD works,SMEP will focus on infrastructure & development works cutting across 2 AoRs & equally benefitting HC.SMSD will ensure effective prioritization,ensuring that most pressing needs are addressed first & ensuring coordination between stakeholders,manage community expectations & rationalize resources.In camps where IOM has stable programs of Protection, Shelter/NFI, Health/MHPSS, Livelihood will have IOM as SMSD FPs & same would be encouraged to IPs to ensure predictability & continuity of operation building the trust of stakeholders & reducing staff & overhead cost.IOM will commit to maintain operations in 17 camps under IOM AoR with joint fundraising efforts.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

SMS will continuously monitor & report safety risk and risk of violence through service monitoring,safety audit,community feedback & referral mechanism.SMS will advocate with local authority and landowner in coordination with HLP actors to obtain agreement to use land & reduce risk of eviction to mitigate conflict & foster social cohesion.NPM in close coordination with Protection & PSEA unit continues to conduct regular Rohingya Hobor Trends survey & PSEA assessments,aiming to understand Rohingya population's knowledge & perception,information will be used to improve complaint & feedback mechanism,and boost reporting of sensitive issues. Project will include installation & maintenance of SSLs for lighting on pathway to facilitate access to services (particularly WASH facility) & reduce protection risk.SD work will be tailored to specific need of extremely vulnerable persons.Project emphasizes capacity building of staff & volunteers of IOM & IPs to monitor & report protection concerns

3.2 - Gender

Project will be guided by IOM’s Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in crises.Activity spaces & initiatives/interventions will be accessible to all (FHH, boys/men, LGBTQI, etc.).Sensitive cases will be confidentially reported & immediately referred.WPP will ensure women/girls are represented through leadership roles;& community projects will use skills-development training to empower self-reliance.Fair access to opportunities will be ensured (CfW rotations to constitute 20% women).Work type/location are transparently communicated through female staff briefings.Community recommendations on safe female working environments will be integrated.Female SMS & DMU volunteers & WPP committee members will be information sources(40% DMUs are female).Female-led meetings & D2D visits will be conducted.NPM will manage age & gender disaggregated data & analysis. NPM will gather & analyse information from women & girls to ensure inclusion of voices & capture specific protection concerns & risks

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

SMS will work closely with actors within ADWG & Child-Protection sub-sector to agree on common standards & tools to ensure quality response,address specific needs & adequate strategies.Global & country level partnership between IOM & CBM will support SMSD intervention inclusive to people with older age & disability.With BSF,project will support broader mobilization of resourceful youth leaders to solve community issues. Through the Community Led Project,SMS team will ensure participation of different age groups. Service monitoring tools will capture gaps through observation of child spaces. Results are shared with facility owners for improvement. Safety audit will identify & mitigate physical risks for children in camps.Similarly,an accessibility audit will be conducted in key service points to identify barriers with recommendation.Project will also offer right kind of livelihood opportunity to people with disability and elderly via CfW schemes.

3.4 - Diversity

Gender-balanced teams to promote participation & inclusivity by creating & strengthening AGD groups(community-led projects,two-way communication).SMS will engage dedicated age groups (youth & elderly)to promote gender equality(Women Participation Project)& culturally dignified practices through specialized committees(Imams,graveyard,dead body management,etc.).CfW opportunities remain open to LGBTQI refugee & host communities.Gender-diverse populations (e.g. Hijra) will be included.SMSD programs will address socio-cultural & economic barriers,strong referral systems protecting minority groups.Project will continue successful existing services through interactive & innovative tools,spaces,methodologies(youth sports).Recreational activities will be equally accessible across genders.Youth will be provided with trainings & resources to identify & resolve community needs to foster ownership & sustainability.NPM to increase representation assessments through no:of female enumerators

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will adhere to IOM’s AAP Framework and continue to strengthen AAP through the inter-agency and multi-sector Common Feedback Platform, ensuring accessibility and confidentiality. The mechanism collects, manages, refers, and responds to complaints through dedicated spaces and teams. The CFP safeguards beneficiary personal details via data protection principles. In addition to seasonal preparedness campaigns, routine community engagement is delivered by Rohingya to increase awareness on key issues. IOM will contribute to updating AAP guidances, in coordination with CwC WG. NPM will regularly produce CFP IM reporting outputs and implement thematic needs assessments. IOM has a zero-tolerance policy against SEA and ensures designated PSEA champions in all camps. Annual refresher trainings will be conducted with all staff, including Rohingya volunteers, who also understand, sign, and adhere to a CoC Standard.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Project includes strong DRR component to reduce risks related to seasonal hazards through construction,repair & maintenance of drainage network & slope stabilization including access routes with plantation to ensure a longer lasting stability & reducing erosion & landslides aligning broader frameworks & integrated Meso-level plan.Physical measures are complemented by DMU volunteers managed by SMS as first responders in case of emergencies using equipment provided including network of mobile firefighting units & fire points.IOM is currently working on identifying areas for holistic watershed management where community led interventions for environmental restoration will be implemented with much efforts on plantation for reforestation of camps.NPM continues to support provision of timely & adequate information via daily & weekly incident reports & datasets to capture & convey key information on damages due to weather-related incidents & trends analysis monitoring impact of natural events
