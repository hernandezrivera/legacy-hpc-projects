Project Summary

For this HRP cycle, our NGO aims to address urgent and lifesaving nutrition needs for pregnant and lactating women, adolescents, and youth (10-24 years) are included. and children under 5 years, the most vulnerable group, in Northwest Syria. Nutrition activities throughout 2022 and 2023 will continue scaling up lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition interventions to the mothers and children living in Azaz sub-district (Al Rayyan Camp) and Jarablus sub-district (Zoghra Camp) in Aleppo governorate, and in Dana sub-district (Atmeh Camp Cluster), Qah sub-district (Al Karameh Camp Cluster), Maaret Tamasrin sub-district, and Idleb sub-district (Idleb City) in Idleb governorate. Nutrition activities will continue prioritizing the following responses:
-Strengthen adherence to operational guidance for response adaptations in the context of COVID-19 to mitigate risk of infection including roll out of the family MUAC approach to increase early acute malnutrition case detection and referral for treatment.
-Quality improvement in the treatment of acute malnutrition
-Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies using different modalities: supplementation through distribution of micronutrient powder and tablets targeting children aged 6 to 59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in collaboration with the health sector, diet diversification awareness, and fortification.
-Protect, promote, and support recommended infant and young child feeding practices in emergencies (IYCF-E) including breastfeeding/ complementary feeding, via counseling or education. In tandem, Mother to Mother Support Groups will be established with new mothers (PLWs) and caregivers with the aim of providing a space for them to be supported in caring for their children's nutrition needs. They will be trained in nutrition topics and be able to share this knowledge with other mothers in their communities.
-Prevention of chronic malnutrition and maternal malnutrition through adequate focus on the first 1,000 days of the child’s life including the pregnancy period.
-Capacity building of partners on key nutrition interventions (community-based management of acute malnutrition, promotion of appropriate infant and young child feeding and caring practices and age-appropriate micronutrient supplementation).
-Strengthening collaboration between Nutrition, Health, and Protection activities on implementing an integrated response that addresses the immediate and underlying causes of acute and chronic malnutrition.
- Conducting Family MUAC approach among the mother to include them more in preventive nutrition activities and early detection of malnutrition cases.

4 - Needs

The prevalence of malnutrition is a significant public health challenge in Syria. The main groups affected by malnutrition are infants, young children under five, and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The different forms of malnutrition include wasting, severe acute malnutrition (SAM), moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) as well as chronic malnutrition or stunting (low height for age), and micronutrient deficiency (especially iron-deficiency anemia which occurs in young children, PLWs, and girls of reproductive age. Based on Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) conducted by Physicians Across Continents, in NWS (including our targets areas) (June 2021), the overall GAM rate by WHZ was 2.5 %, SAM was 0.4 %, GAM rate by MUAC was 4.7%, Stunting rate was 24.5%, and the PLW MAM rate was 12.4%. The rate of exclusive breastfeeding in infants 0-5 months was already low but further declined from 43% to 29% (2014-2020). In 2021, 25% of children in 85 sub-districts across Syria suffered from stunting (Severity Classification 3). 46% of children (aged 6 - 59 months) in forty-four sub-districts had iron-deficiency anemia (Severity Classification 4); this was also coupled with poor infant and young child (IYCF) practices including low minimum acceptable diet (MAD) in children aged 6-23 months. Furthermore, 54% of PLW (Severity Classification 5) experienced lack of iron within their diet (2022 Syria HNO Nutrition Sector).

5 - Implementation of activities

Over the course of the project, our organization's nutrition team will support facility and community-based infant and young child (IYCF) activities through providing consultations on IYCF practices in two supported IYCF facilities, seven primary health care facilities, and one maternity and child hospital provided by trained IYCF facilitators specialized in working with Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and other caregivers. Additionally, doctors and nurses will provide micronutrient supplementation for the prevention of micronutrient deficiencies for women and children with Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) through four supported CMAM clinics. Our organization will also support facility and community-based screening for acute malnutrition through Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening for PLW and retain Z-Score Charts for children aged 5 years and under with detection of the edema intensity. CMAM clinics will provide treatment for moderate acute malnutrition cases among children and PLW through Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) and Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) for severe acute malnutrition cases among children. Depending on the activities, our organization will target adolescent mothers through individual counseling and group sessions.
