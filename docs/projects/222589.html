Project Summary

Provision of life-sustaining assistance to returnee populations in Mueda and Moçimboa da Praia, rebuilding livelihoods activities (agriculture and fishery) which have been severely affected during the occupation of the target areas by non-governamental army groups of; activities entail the provision of assets, tools, commodities to reestablish pre-conflict productivity levels; the approach will entail both in-kind and vouchers.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project targets 6.000 households for a total of 30.000 individuals (1.600/8.000 in Mueda, 4.400/22.000 in MdP), returnees, and non-displaced vulnerable HH; the set of targeting criteria privileges women (i.e. fisherwomen), women head of households, cooperatives with at least 50% women membership. The project targets a diverse demographic, primarily returnees and individuals residing in accessible areas identified with specific needs and vulnerabilities. This includes vulnerable small farmers facing food insecurity, income instability, and social vulnerability, as well as members of fishery and agriculture cooperatives. The project, in coordination mainly with SDAE and SPAE and other actors, aims at making available quality agricultural tools, seeds, seedlings, fishing kits, post-harvest kits, storage facilities to recover previous levels of production and income, prevent losses, maximise production, food consumption or sale for income generation. The project will focus on dry season and rainy season support, aiming at diversifying the production of staple food, adapting to context, capacities, culture, and soild characteristics, and the dietary intake of households with vegetables. SI will support both fishermen and fisherwomen, individually or as members of cooperatives, with catching and fishing tools, processing and storage tools, trainings, marketing support. The modality will be in-kind, although SI is assessing the feasibility of CVA, and if appropriate some of the assistance will be delivered in vouchers and/or cash (i.e. the provision of agricultural kits in vouchers and protection food in cash, or the procurement of fishing tools).

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

- Dry season support to 1550 households in MdP and 500 households in MdP, seeds, tools, training, post-harvest, pest management, for vegetable gardens- Rainy season support to 1500 households in MdP and 900 in Mueda, seeds, tools, training, post-harvest, pest management for staple crops (maize and pulses)- Implementation of pilot plots of new crops (cassava, sweet potato, and rice), 300 in MdP and 100 in Mueda, aiming at production, recovery of cultivars, and multiplication of seeds and stalks. - Distribution of seeds and tools, post harvest kits for rainy season- Food rations to protect seeds (and to avoid resell), covering 85% of the recommended food intake, in rainy season (2400 rations) - Support to 35 cooperatives (associated in 5 associations) for 350 HH in MdP (18 fishing cooperatives and 17 farming cooperatives) with fishing kits, training, market support- Food assistance to 300 vulnerable households in MdP and 100 in Mueda, covering three months food needs during lean season, for those households/individuals not capable to engage with productive activities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

SI has policies in place to train its staff on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and Child Safeguarding. In this way, the organization ensures that all staffs are aware of the policies when hired and trained to identify any case of violence or exploitation throughout the project. Likewise, in the frame of this project, SI will mainstream protection throughout all its different activities, thanks to its trained staff, dedicated tools, and community engagement. Interventions through staff trained on Protection mainstreaming, as well as community consultation prior to setting up activities, will ensure that all beneficiaries, from all social and gender categories, have safe access to the services provided. Standards of mainstreaming protection in the different sectors of interventions will be taken into consideration in the approach carried out by SI teams. For instance, latrine blocks will be segregated while ensuring that the protection component is taken into account in terms of where they will be built. The roll out of FCRM and SI whistleblowing policy complement this approach and make sure that protection is mainstreamed within the project.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

SI uses internal policies, procedures, and frameworks to ensure the effective implementation of actions that take gender, age, and other vulnerabilities into account in its programs (SI’s Gender Position Paper is framing SI’s programs in Mozambique in this regard). All staff members have been sensitized to having a gender-balanced approach. For the productive activities, both farming and fishing, SI’s staff will continue the effort to understand the role of women and men in each of the activities and ensure that each of them’s contribution is well addressed and supported. However, SI pose the accent, and increases the efforts, on how to support women, and women’s groups, to be better equipped to access productive means, livelihood opportunities, and markets, meet own and their families’ food and other needs. The same approach will be adopted with regards with different age groups, addressing the different needs and capabilities, and vulnerabilities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Inclusion is a challenging topic in Mozambique context because of the invisibility of people with disabilities in the population. Well aware of this situation SI, as a regular actor at household level within the sites and as CCCM coordinator at Mueda district will make sure that inclusion is an core to its activities. SI will also advocate at district level for greater inclusion by providing and communicating the relevant data needed for inclusive programming. The project will identify people with disabilities through a community-based approach, engaging directly with community leaders, families, and conducting regular surveys which include the Washington Group questions (vulnerability surveys, KAP, baseline assessments, etc.). During initial assessments, project staff will collaborate closely with these groups to identify individuals with physical challenges and understand their specific needs and barriers. Community workshops and door-to-door visits will help ensure that those who are isolated or less visible are also reached, providing a comprehensive understanding of the challenges they face in accessing FSL activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Environmental considerations have been core to SI project design, in all its aspects: - Definition of the agricultural practices to adopt and support (seeds, tools, pest management), topics to address in the training to farmers; - Adoption of agroforestry techniques in agriculture;- Adoption of local varieties and adapted crops; this work entails further research and major effort in the diversification of the crops re. the characteristics of the soils, culture, rain patterns, etc. and will be further deepened in the future. - Definition of fishing patterns: protection of fish nurseries and fragile areas (mangroves and coral reefs); rotation of fishing areas and spots, vetted areas (also seasonal rotations), prohibit trawling, define sizes for each speciesAll these aspects represent the minimum standards for the activities proposed in the project, and will be presented to SDAE and SPAE to align with legislation or to advocate for the adoption of these standards.
