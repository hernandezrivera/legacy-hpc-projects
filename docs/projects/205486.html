Project Summary

The outbreak of fighting in Sudan on 15 April 2023 has caused an influx of people fleeing the country to the crisis affected South Sudan. More than 259,451 individuals have fled from Sudan into South Sudan. Nine out of ten of those arriving are South Sudanese; of those fleeing, 50 % of the new arrivals are women and girls, nearly half of the Women and girls fleeing are below the age of 18 and 4% of the Women affected by the Sudan crisis are women over 60 years (UNHCR 2023). The primary cause of movement of people, particularly women and girls, from Sudan to South Sudan is to find Safety/protection. However, the Women and girls fleeing conflict in Sudan continue to face multiple risks of GBV including rape, sexual exploitation and abuse, intimate partner violence and physical violence when they arrive in South Sudan. Adolescent girls are even more vulnerable with child marriage increasing in many locations across South Sudan, more so in Unity State. Incidents of conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) have been documented in the Sudan crisis and the impacts of this are compounded by gaps in lifesaving GBV services in South Sudan including clinical management of rape (CMR) and GBV case management and psychosocial support (PSS). Overall, the capacity and coverage of multi-sectoral GBV responses including health, GBV case management and Psycho Social Surpport, higher-level mental health (MHPSS) and other services remain scarce in areas experiencing elevated levels of returnees in Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei States (UNICEF 2023). This proposal seeks to scale up GBV in emergencies; prevention, response and risk mitigation which is in line with the South Sudan’s HNO 2023 overview to provide safety, inclusion and dignity to Women and girls in all the Humanitarian response programs.
Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO) is committed to scaling up Gender Based Violence in Emergencies (GBViE); response, prevention, and risk mitigation programming to address the new and emerging needs arising in Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei States from the Sudan crisis. HACO proposes to intervene to ensure that women and girls are protected and GBV survivors have improved access to lifesaving GBV services and through referral – GBV survivors get health and Mental Health and Psycho social surpport (MHPSS) and overall Women and girls face reduced GBV risks in their communities. The response will be premised on services for survivors of GBV at women and girl friendly spaces (WGFS) and through mobile teams with referrals for health and other services, risk mitigation across other programmatic sectors, and prevention activities targeting gender inequality and other harmful social norms that underpin GBV. This will encompass the provision of GBViE response interventions to improve help-seeking, strengthen referral pathways and ensure quality case management for GBV survivors, particularly displaced Persons on the move from Sudan at the transit sites and return locations in Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei States. Additionally, HACO will carry out Safety audits in camp, community setting or focused on a specific facility, advising other sectors on how to most effectively reach marginalized sub-groups within the community and carrying out Consultations with women and girls focused on their safety and access concerns related to programming in other sectors. HACO will also Provide those working in other sectors with up-to-date information about where GBV survivors can seek support and Conducting trainings for other sectors on GBV risk mitigation and how to safely and appropriately respond to survivor disclosures. HACO Program staff will through community dialogues and engagement challenge existing social norms that are the root cause of violence against women and girls, as well as a barrier to service seeking behaviours aimed at preventing GBV in emergencies.
