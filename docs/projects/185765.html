Project Summary

In 2022, Tdh will scale up it’s WASH response through gender-responsive, rights-based, adolescent and youth-friendly inclusive services in camp 26, camp 27 and the surrounding host communities. WASH activities will offer knowledge and awareness on how to maintain personal hygiene and WASH facilities in appropriate ways. Tdh will also address adequate and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene, which Tdh continuously advocates for. All activities will ensure the do-not harm principle by prioritizing the safety and dignity of women and girls. Tdh will continue its sensitization regarding safety measures before and during natural disasters, including disaster risk reduction, AWD outbreak, and mainstreaming of COVID-19 messages. WASH committee and key stakeholders were included throughout the project development and community feedback mechanism and response will be maintained during the implementation period.
In 2022 Tdh's WASH activities will focus on Rohingya refugees residing in camps as well as the surrounding host communities, according to the gaps and needs at different community levels, being inclusive to age, gender and diversity. Tdh will continue to operate and maintain the Feacal Sludge Management plant (desludging, treatment and disposal) that is already built (including an incinerator) in camp 27 as well as waste management activities. Additionally, mechanized desludging system will be installed with the connection to UNHCR integrated faecal sludge transfer network (IFSTN). Tdh-operated FSM and waste management services will continue to receive, sludge, treat and dispose (dry sludge and waste) from the camps 26 and 27, the two Primary Health Care Centers and the host communities, as well as the Multi Purpose Community Centres. The two PHCs run by Tdh have an incinerator, but given the PHC’s current production of large quantities of contaminated waste which cannot be treated at the PHC, this waste will be managed at waste management area within the existing FSM plant, where an incinerator is already built in accordance with proper standards. Tdh will continue to operate and maintain the WASH facilities in the PHCs, Camps and surrounding host community as well as construct/rehabilitate/decommission WASH facilities (latrine, bathing cubicle and garbage bin) in the Camps based upon their condition and needs. A plastic recycling plant will be installed in camp 27 (to making toys and alphabet cube). Due to the scarcity of water in Teknaf, a surface water treatment plant and water distribution network will also be installed in camp 27, to ensure access to safe water and sanitation. Tdh will conduct continued management and improvements of these facilities to ensure adequate Infection Prevention and Control (IPC)/disinfection at two PHCs and the MPCC.
Awareness raising activities will be delivered to both refugees and host communities through group sessions, campaigns, day celebrations and the development and distribution of IEC/BCC materials. These activities will cover the topics of waste management, sanitation, handwashing, COVID-19, and Menstrual Health Management (MHM) among others. This will be complimented by the distribution of NFI/hygiene kits (during emergency we will distribute NFI items as contigency plan, as like; handwashing device, hygiene kit, top-up kit, MHM kits and plastic bin for the individual HH’s) to ensure beneficiaries’ have both the knowledge and access to adequate WASH facilities.
As per the need and lessons learnt from COVID-19, Tdh will continue developing implementation modalities that mitigate against interruption of WASH activities and will continue to build the capacity of community WASH volunteers involved in hygiene promotion, waste management and desludging.

1 - Identified Needs

As per the May'2021 J-MSNA, ongoing WASH needs in camp 26, 27 and surrounding host communities include, the lack of access to functional sanitation (now overall functional 23%), waste disposal services, faecal sludge management (FSM) operational systems, and the potential for water source contamination and health and hygiene issues. According to the J-MSNA 2020, access to safe and functional latrines for males is 20%, female is 33% whilst overall access is reported to be 23%. Faecal sludge treatment facilities need to be upgraded to improve efficiency and coverage and minimize maintenance costs. WASH agencies need to strengthen solid waste management (SWM) systems, including through the establishment of safely managed disposal sites, promotion of waste segregation, safe disposal and recycling, and composting of waste, where possible. There is a need of harmonizing SWM systems and to engage WASH agencies in sustainable, gender-responsive strategies by increasing female participation and engagement.Uptake of appropriate hygiene practices for specific risk behaviours remains low. For example, only 32% of households reported washing their hands after handling children’s feces (REACH, 2019). Safe water handling practices at household level are also not widely observed, with the reported levels of contamination of stored water exceeding 90% (WHO/UNICEF -2019). In respect to Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), 24% of women and girls reported not receiving MHM materials within the last 3 months (REACH, July 2019).After four years of implementation of WASH activities in Cox’s Bazaar, WASH agencies now need to focus on carrying out continuous improvements to existing latrines and bathing cubicles to increase durability, quality, ensure safety, privacy and address the specific needs concerning gender, disability, age and other vulnerabilities. The construction of new latrines following the unified designs, will address coverage gaps. At the same time, there is a need to give emphasis on quality monitoring and the upgrading of faecal sludge treatment facilities to improve efficiency through full chain treatment processes that address disinfection, separation of liquids and solids, biological treatment processes and the drying of sludge. Another priority area is to strengthen the SWM systems, including the establishment of safely managed disposal sites, in conjunction with assessing the factors that motivate people to segregate waste at household and disposal sites. There is a need to follow the hygiene promotion strategy and focus on strengthening participatory approaches and capacity building for Rohingya refugees in order to change high-risk behaviours. Gender-focused community mobilization will be reinforced to ensure participation - especially of women and girls - in decision-making processes related to the design, feedback mechanisms, implementation and monitoring of WASH services. As Teknaf is a water scarce area in Bangladesh, there is a need to focus more on the development of systems for water resource management, including the modelling of groundwater extraction, in order to mitigate adverse impacts. Introducing solar power for water pumping, appropriate sewage treatment and disposal (in line with national standards), and safe and appropriate SWM (including reuse, recycling and disposal) will contribute to preservation of the environment.
