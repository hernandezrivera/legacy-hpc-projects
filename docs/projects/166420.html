Project Summary

The interventions planned under this project will ensure timely access to essential lifesaving and critical sexual and reproductive health services to Rohingya refugees and the host community in Ukhiya and Teknaf Sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar during the COVID 19 situation. The project aims at addressing the priority sexual and reproductive health including ensuring the minimum initial service package (MISP) for reproductive health in crisis for the affected population. Of the total number of people targeted for the COVID response plan, this project will focus on those refugees and host communities which include women of reproductive age that are facing critical life-threatening situations in the Teknaf and Ukhiya sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar. These women of reproductive age are at constant and immediate-risk of pregnancy-related complications and will need urgent and immediate care.
The project also seeks to improve and maintain the quality of SRH facilities, ensures adolescent-friendly services, improve the infection prevention & control (IPC) measures at the health facilities, strengthen the referral pathway for safe access to 24-hour rapid transfer of all obstetric life-threatening emergencies related to COVID condition, procure personal protective equipment for the health service providers, midwifery-led support across the isolation corners, awareness sessions on COVID messages through community health workers, capacity building of the health service providers on IPC and more importantly a dedicated Field hospital in camp 4 to support Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care services to COVID suspected/infected pregnant women including Women and girls.

Needs

The need to adapt Sexual Reproductive health services including GBV informed by consultations with women, men, boys and girls will remain critical during this phase to minimize the gendered impact of COVID-19 to the extent possible for those in lockdown, isolation or quarantine facilities.Limited information is available on COVID-19 in pregnancy, but the studies published to date do not show an increased risk of severe disease in late pregnancy or substantial risk to the newborn. Maternity services should continue to be prioritized as an essential core health service, and other sexual and reproductive health care such as family planning, emergency contraception, treatment of sexually transmitted diseases, post-abortion care also need to remain available as core health services. The gender and age disaggregated data from the SRHR health facilities has shown a drop in the different SRHR services (ANC, Family Planning, Facility Based Deliveries etc.) so there is need to focus on strengthening community based SRHR services and behavioral change approaches by ensuring that quality SRHR information and services are available near the communities. Maternity care providers (including midwives and all other health care workers providing maternal and newborn care) are essential health care workers and must be protected and prioritized to continue providing care to childbearing women and their babies. Maternity care providers have the right to full access for all personal protective equipment (PPE), sanitation and a safe and respectful working environment. Maintaining a healthy workforce will ensure ongoing quality care for women and their newborns; without healthy midwives and other maternity care providers there will be limited care for women and newborns. There is also an urgent need to ensure the infection prevention & control measures are in place at the health facilities along with the capacity building for the health service providers on different IPC measures. More support is required to strengthen the referral facilities to provide 24/7 management of comprehensive emergency obstetric care and gynecological complications in this COVID situation at the Isolation center. Care provision for pregnant mothers with other complications, which may increase the risk of obstetric emergencies, need to be strengthened and planned accordingly so that these services are available at the isolation corners in each 24/7 HFs. Continued support to the SRH Working Group coordination will be required to provide high quality technical assistance to ensure comprehensive SRHR information and services are of international standards and on par with nationals.

Activities or outputs

SO 2: • Triage and screening for COVID-19 exposure and symptoms needs to be undertaken for all women presenting to the health facility • Set up hand-washing services at entrance of health facilities • Employ/Identify volunteers to ensure hand-washing before individuals enter the facility as well as support in triage. • Train all healthcare workers on proper utilization of PPE and safeguarding measures from potential infection • Provide adequate supplies of recommended cleaning materials to all health facilities • Train community health workers to raise awareness about handwashing, identifying symptoms of COVID-19 among Rohingya and Host Community • Procurement of materials for Infection Prevention and Control set up • Provide PPE for healthcare providers of the SRH-Working Group partners • Capacity building and training on IPC for whole staffs of the health facilities. SO 3: • UNFPA as the lead of SRH WG, will provide leadership to sexual and reproductive healthcare provision in Cox’s Bazar, during the COVID-19 situation • Working group will prioritize bringing together relevant actors to address gaps in quality services and will be accomplished through assessment, interagency monitoring of current SRHR provision, strengthening health systems through effective coordination. The group currently is focusing on ensuring implementation of SRH minimum initial service package addressing the critical maternity needs of women and girls in the COVID-19 crisis. SRH-WG is supporting interventions aiming to raise community awareness of available SRHR services so that women and girls are encouraged to visit health facilities. SRH-WG aim to strengthen coordinated referral system that facilitates transport and communication from the community to the health center and between health center and referral hospital/designated Isolation and Maternity Treatment Center so that COVID cases could be effectively taken to the health facilities for providing appropriate care • SRH-WG will assure coordination with other sectors, working groups, agencies, MoH, NGOs, affected populations and other relevant stakeholders for collaborative response in COVID-19 crisis with provision of technical guidance, protocols/essential, critical SRHR services during COVID19 pandemic and COVID Response EmONC referral Pathways etc. SO 4: • Referral pathway and mechanisms to provide emergency transport from facility to facility/isolation corner is needed for potential transfer of EmONC complications, ambulances for suspected/probable patient referral to designated isolation units are required at the health facilities • Establishment of new Referral Hubs to support emergency referrals from community to facility and health facility to higher-level facilities. With setup of a triage and screening at referral hub potential COVID cases from community could be linked to Dispatch Referral Unit (DRU) so these cases can be transported immediately at isolation center/SARI treatment center. Other emergency obstetric cases could be prioritized for referral by respective ambulance of referral hubs • Support in development of a 50 bedded hospital adjoining to existing Hope Field Hospital in Camp 4 treating COVID cases of pregnant women and girls to ensure 24/7 lifesaving services including Comprehensive EmONC services. This will include procurement of all equipment and supplies: beds, essential medicines, pulse oximeter, oxygen cylinder/concentrator, nebulizers, etc. required for establishment of isolation and comprehensive Maternity Treatment center and supporting adequate human resources • Support set up of isolation corner in all other UNFPA supported health facilities along with maintaining minimum standard of triage for suspected cases, to provide safe delivery in case of second stage of labour or initial stabilization for life-threatening complication before referral through DRU • Midwives support isolation center/corner for treatment of COVID cases with focusing maternity needs.
