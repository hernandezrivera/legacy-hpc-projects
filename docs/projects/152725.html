Project Summary

Action Against Hunger has been operational in what is now South Sudan since 1985, responding to both chronic and acute humanitarian needs. The strategy of Action Against Hunger addresses the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition in children U5 by linking nutrition with WASH and FSL interventions to foster a meaningful impact upon the communities’ resilience as well as prevent childhood morbidity and mortality. Currently Action Against Hunger is implementing its program activities in NBeG, Warrap and Jonglei state with established Field Ocer in Malualkon, Alek, Ajogo, Old Fangak and Paguir Payams. WASH projects will be implemented to ensure that the “host communities and returnees has safe access to water and sanitation facilities with improved hygiene behaviour”. Proposed activities will improve the nutritional status of children U5 and pregnant and lactating women through an integrated WASH and Nutrition response to break the diarrhoea – malnutrition vicious cycle. The proposed objective will ensure the effectiveness of the treatment for malnutrition in the proposed program target area. In order to achieve the overall objective of the project following activities will enable us to reach/provide assistance for approximately 13,670 HH (app. 96,000 individuals). Following activities will be implemented to achieve the overall targets:- • Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected women, men, girls and boys • Enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services • Provide WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community • Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. Action Against Hunger will implement proposed project in the catchment areas of nutrition sites in NBeG, Warrap and Jonglei states. Baseline KAP survey will be conducted in the target areas to identify the knowledge, attitude and practices of the communities. Further a detail need assessment will be conducted to assess the access of the communities to safe water sources and sanitation facilities. Implementation strategy follows the need assessment and KAP surveys at project start. Additionally, the program will contribute to the ACF multi-sector emergency response throughout the country, by supporting its WASH component. Proposed program strategy takes into account specific gender needs during surveys to ensure the equitable participation of all gender and age groups.

Needs

South Sudan remains in a protracted humanitarian crisis that peaked in middle 2016. Since the beginning of the conflict in 2013, an estimated 3 million people have fled their homes (1.9 million people internally displaced and 1.2 million who have fled to neighbouring countries as refugees). According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) 2018 report and projections for May-July 2018, the situation in South Sudan continues to deteriorate with 6.5 million (about 58% of the population) estimated to be severely food insecure (IPC 3,4 & 5) from May to July projection. As a result, a food gap leading to extensive malnutrition is expected to rise towards peak of lean season up until September 2018. In Fangak County, the latest SMART survey conducted by Action Against Hunger in May 2018 recorded a GAM rate of 16.1% which is critically higher than the WHO threshold for emergency (15%) and has increased as compared to the last SMART survey report conducted in July 2017 (15.8%). The lack of access to safe water and sanitation facilities in the area mean that risks of Cholera outbreak and increase in diarrhoea cases can be expected. According to UNOCHA IPC Classification for Acute Malnutrition, Fangak County has been placed in critical area for May to July projections and is one of the 11 high priority counties identified by the cluster. Last SMART survey report conducted in Gogrial West (Warrap State) a GAM rate of 26.6% was recorded which is critically higher than the WHO threshold for emergency (15%) and has increased as compared to the last SMART survey report conducted in June 2017 (22.8%). According to the KAP survey (March 2018) conducted in Gogrial West, 76% of the communities are practicing open defecation and only 84% of the communities are able to access protected boreholes in rainy season; queue timings for accessibility to safe water sources is more than 1 hr as an average which is further creating burden on females to complete their household chores. Initial need assessment in Paguir Payam (Jonglei state) in Oct 2018 shows only 30% of the community is able to access the water from protected borehole which is not fit for human consumption due to heavy fecal coliform i.e. 35 /100 mg. Access to sanitation facilities is very critical as 99% of the community members are practicing open defecation practices. In NBeG state the KAP survey conducted in June 2018 results share that 21% of the communities has to cover distance of more than 500 meter to access the protected safe water source while 44% have to spend 30 min to 1 hr to complete single trip to fetch water. 50% of the communities are practicing open defecation due to limited or no access to sanitation facilities.The deteriorated WASH situation throughout the country and notably in the target areas has resulted in continuous increase in SAM and GAM rates of the target areas. Action Against Hunger has established its nutrition centres in the target areas for treatment of Malnutrition issues while the support from WASH program will ensure the cut off from diarrhoea-malnutrition vicious cycle and will ensure that communities have safe and equitable access to water and sanitation facilities while they can practice good hygiene behaviour. This is equally important in emergency response in hard to reach areas of South Sudan, so as to cut the vicious cycle of water borne disease and affliction, including malnutrition.

Activities

The project aims to provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected women, men, girls and boys.To ensure that caregiver families of SAM and MAM children visiting nutrition sites have access to safe drinking water and to conduct the appetite test at the static and emergency nutrition sites. We will undertake the detail assessment of the existing functional and non-functional water points and requirement for new drilling at OTP/TSFP sites of target areas. Technical assessment will be conducted to assess the practices of the catchment communities for safe water sources and to measure Sphere standards for water access. In partnership with respective state water ministries. Action Against Hunger will conduct detail mapping of the functional and non-functional boreholes in the area to collect the record and update in the data base. Data collected will later be used to strategize the rehabilitation of existing non-functional boreholes/water sources in the target area. In partnership with local state ministry and State Cluster PMA (Pump Mechanics Association) will be identified and trained on the rehabilitation of boreholes to ensure the sustainability of the program activities. The project aims at enabling vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services So far 25 villages are declared as open defecation free through CLTS approach in Aweil East. As part of sanitation strategy Action Against Hunger will launch ODF campaign to all target areas of Aweil, Gogrial West and Paguir. Organizaiton ensure to declare 50 ODF villages in 2019. CLTS is also considered as the primary component of South Sudan Environment and Sanitation strategy while considering SDG 6. Self-construction of latrines at household level will ensure the equitable access of women which will further help in addressing GBV and protection issues at the community level. Further, at Nutrition sites gender segregated latrines will be constructed to ensure caregiver families at nutrition sites can access sanitation facilities. The project will also provide WASH minimum package on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificity of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community Access to hygiene items and its usage is considered as key tool to break the diarrhoea-malnutrition vicious cycle. Action Against Hunger will distribute Hygiene kits to caregiver families of SAM and MAM children. Female sanitary items will be included in the kit for pregnant and lactating women to cover the gender aspect. The care giver families visiting nutrition sites will be educated on hygiene behaviour through regular hygiene promotion sessions to understand the needs of hygienic behaviour and to know how to use the kit items.Moreover, the project will Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. Through PHAST and CHAST approaches at community and school level ACF will train and build the capacity of local communities and children for improved hygiene behaviour. Student Hygiene Clubs will be formed at the school level where children will be trained to practice and promote the hygiene behaviour at school and community level. Children will work as agent of change while community elders will be included in the hygiene campaign through PHAST approach. PRA tools will be used to motivate communities for proper hand-washing at key time, safe water handling and drinking, environmental hygiene and household hygiene practices.Our countrywide multi-sector emergency team (MET) provides response to recurring acute malnutrition crises. It establishes OTP sites, improves WASH conditions through rehabilitation of boreholes, minimum WASH package, hygiene promotion in deployment areas, capacity building of local partners.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Action Against Hunger has a participatory approach for its program. At the planning phase, Action Against Hunger holds consultations in the communities with gender and age diverse groups. The beneficiaries can obtain information through these consultations as well as through interaction with project staff. For distribution of hygiene kit items PDM survey are conducted to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of distribution process and quality of items distributed. Further the program strategy includes consultation as key pillar. Communities are involved from program planning until implementation stages as well as sharing their experiences for the program during FGDs conducted at community level before and after implementation of program. Water User committees are formed and trained at each community to identify the water sources for rehabilitation and to identify the appropriate areas for drilling of new areas. Women and girls are specifically consulted during selection of the drilling sites as they are culturally considered as responsible for household water needs. This approach is done for static as well as countrywide emergency response program.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Considering the different context of the target areas and the diversity in implementation areas the hygiene messages are developed on the basis of pre KAP survey findings. IEC material is tailored for the young groups while for female’s menstrual hygiene messages are developed and are being shared. Most messages use images, given the low literacy rate of the target population. Messages are directed to specific groups (practicing family hygiene, menstrual hygiene, hand-washing, sanitation, etc.). This approach is done for static as well as countrywide emergency response program.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project integrates the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse through safety audit, gender analysis and interaction with beneficiaries. In addition, Action Against Hunger implements it PSEA policy through a robust internal system and ensures reporting on any identify cases. Action Against Hunger has ongoing GBV program which shares its feedback to the program implementation to ensure the inclusion of GBV issues during implementation. Further, the tools such as KAP, PDM developed for data collection has been verified by GBV specialist considering the context of the area. Action Against Hunger field staff is trained on GBV issues and have effective referral system to report the GBV issues at office where GBV specialist took appropriate measure.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

FGD are conducted at each target areas as well as at state ministry level to identify the needs and to suggest the appropriate measure. Communities and local government authorities are involved in the consultation practices to ensure the proper implementation of program activities and enhance community ownership. This approach is done for static as well as countrywide emergency response program.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Action Against Hunger is committed to ensuring that its interventions are timely and appropriate to meet the needs of its beneficiaries throughout its implementing sectors in South Sudan. To ensure that Action Against Hunger is Accountable to its beneficiaries and the community it serves it has developed a feedback mechanism that can be integrated into current program activities to solicit feedback from beneficiaries and first-line implementers.The three groups that Action Against Hunger is interested in soliciting feedback from are:1. First-line program implementers (including volunteers and field staff)2. The local communities where our programs operate3. The direct beneficiaries of our programsSince first line implementers are the ones working directly with beneficiaries on a day to day basis they can often provide the most useful readily available information regarding the challenges beneficiaries face as well as how programs are functioning, what is difficult, and what needs to be changed.A total of nine (9) Feedback Mechanism tools have been developed for program staff to use at the field level. Out of the nine mechanisms five have been identified as “minimum standards,” which should be implemented at the program site level. 1. Monthly staff meetings between first-line implementers & field office staff, 2. Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries at the site level, 3. Individual interview form, 4. Comment Box at Field Office Gate, 5. Fraud Hotline Number.Depending on the urgency of the issue, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementers on a monthly basis during the monthly meetings. Specific responses can also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the PM or national/ program level. Urgent issues should be addressed immediately by the Field Coordination.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community engagement is key for ensuring the effective implementation of program activities. Water user committees are formed and trained on operation and maintenance of water sources as well identification of appropriate drilling places for new boreholes. Community volunteers are selected from each village to conduct the hygiene promotion. FGD are conducted at quarterly basis in community to engage them regarding the progress of the program and share the achievements during CLTS campaign.PDM surveys and post KAP surveys are being used to collect the feedback from the communities. PDM survey helps to measure the effectiveness of the distribution process and includes community’s suggestion regarding the quality and quantity of hygiene items.This approach is done for static as well as countrywide emergency response program.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Facilities developed or provided during the program are properly analysed for any risk to different gender and age groups. At nutrition sites gender segregated latrines are constructed while boreholes are drilled at safe places considering the risk associated for female and children during water collection. Action Against Hunger in South Sudan conducts Safety Audits and Gender Analyses in order to assess protection risks associated with its WASH program. We will assess and pilot different tools and methods in order to mitigate any protections risks related to water and sanitation.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Water user committees and sanitation committees are formed in each target community with equal representation of different age groups and gender. The selection of water user committee is conducted with defined criteria and to ensure that female and young adult’s suggestion and consultation can be taken into account.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Depending on the area of implementation, the protection issues are identified and addressed with the consultation from the communities. Further the facilities are handed over to the communities so that no single person or group of person can influence and creates barrier or exploit the available resources for any benefit. To reduce protection threats, which are more significant for women and girls, the project involves more women and girls in the water committees and consultations. Through this it finds out specific protection threats and puts in place measures that increase access, minimize burden and through sensitization of men and boys in the community attempt to create a more favorable environment for women’s access to services.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Communities are trained and on the monitoring of the project activities. Activities details are shared with the communities with the targets to ensure that they are aware with the criteria and process for activity implementation. Further communities are consulted during FGD which are organized at community level on quarterly basis to measure the access of beneficiaries in a participatory way. KAP surveys also reveal potential challenges in access, which are addressed based on the specific recommendations.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

GBV indicators are included in the WASH program proposal to ensure that GBV threats and hazards can be taken into account. Further gender-segregated latrines are constructed at nutrition sites level while during CLTS campaign communities are motivated to construct household latrines to ensure the safety of the children and females from sexual exploitation. Water sources are developed at appropriate places so that each group based on gender and age can easily and safely access the facility. Planned safety audits will capture in more detail any outstanding safety issues.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

During FGD (Focus Group Discussion) the feedback of the community is recorded to ensure that they have not been exploited during the activity implementation. Community Volunteers involved in the monitoring process with the communities, shares the first hand information with project staff to share the progress and quality of activity. Further WASH program manager regularly visit the field area to receive feedback from the communities and to ensure that dignity of beneficiaries is respected through appropriate involvement and respect for individuals and group diversity. PDM reports also consider the aspect of beneficiary’s dignity, such as staff behavior and work processes that avoid putting additional burden on the people in need. This approach is done for static as well as countrywide emergency response program.
