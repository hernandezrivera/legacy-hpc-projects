The project aims to maintain and improve equitable access to formal and non-formal
education for the most vulnerable groups in Maaret Tamsrin and Afrin sub-districts and
the surrounding camps. The project is targeting 3300 for formal and non-formal
education activities, children aged between (5-17) years old, including 1750 boys and 1550
girls with a focus on providing accessibility to children and people with disabilities. The
the project also provides job opportunities for more than 90 educational actors from both
genders. IHR aiming to meet the objective of this project through six main interviews:
1-IHR will conduct a BTL campaign in 4 communities at the community level and in the
targeted camp. Activities will target children, community leaders, and caregivers with a total of beneficiaries.
2-Maintain and improve access to equitable formal Education for 2320 children in 5 formal centers at Maaret Tamsrin and Afrin sub-districts. Focusing on safe drinking water accessibility in schools
and education centers. The academic calendar will include providing psychosocial support sessions provided by specialists, and the teachers will have service maps and train on referral mechanisms to
the nearest protection and GBV centers. Taking into consideration gender-sensitive WASH
facilities for both students and teachers.
3- Enhancing access to equitable non-formal Education for 980 out-of-school children in
both communities and Camps (550 boys, 430 girls) in 7 education centers in Maaret
Tamsin and Afrin. Through this component, remedial, literacy, and numeracy classes are
provided to school-aged children to help them reengage informal Education and teachers
are supported with incentive.
4-IHR will hire a specialist to deal with CwD and give training to the teachers on how to treat CwDs, and make sure they engage with the normal children in the classrooms—noting that they will have special toolkits (chairs, notebook…) in the classrooms if needed.
5-IHR will conduct hygiene raising awareness campaigns to meet ASO1 in coordination
with WASH cluster.
IHR will repair the schools in the mentioned locations. Repairing is limited to light repair in
classrooms, paint, doors, windows, common spaces and WASH facilities. The WASH
component will be further discussed with the WASH cluster.
Educational actors are provided with capacity building courses to ensure providing protective and effective learning through multiple training including INEE standards-classroom management and planning-PSS–alternatives of corporal punishment–Public safety principles/Education patterns, active learning- dealing and integration the Disabled, inclusive, gender-sensitive, protective pedagogy and psychosocial support. The project activities will be followed up by the MEAL team. The team will conduct continuous visits to the schools and the educational centers, to collect data about the project indicators through direct observation, reports, and other activities through various tools that fit the nature of each activity. PSS activities will be monitored through the MEAL field visits and evaluated by FGDs sessions to figure out the students' satisfaction about the PSS activities. For the students with a disability, the MEAL team will conduct interviews with them during field visits in order to figure out if they face any challenges and difficulties.
Plan B:
The target areas are one of the safest areas in Syria, but in case of inaccessibility or the COVID-19 continues, IHR depending on a contingency plan, will provide online platforms for E-learning if it became difficult to give the physical training.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, 2.45 million children in Syria are out of school, 1.6 million are atrisk of dropping out. More than one in three schools are damaged or destroyed, whileothers are used for purposes not related to education.IHR team conducted the assessment study in two districts: Afrin andMaaret Tamsrin in Feb 2021. IHR team coordinated with ED and the LC to conduct this assessment.Students 6–11 years old formed the largest age group of students in the assessedcommunities accounting for 58% of the total enrolled students. Female students aged 6–11 constituted 30% of the total number of enrolled students. Students aged 12-15 yearsconstituted 28% of the total number of enrolled students and students aged 16-18 yearsconstituted 14 % of both genders. The percentage of male students in the targetedcommunities represents 51 % of total students, while female students represent 49%.Respondents noted the importance of providing support for schools' rehabilitation andsecuring transportation for students. As for teachers, it was found that 30% have nosource of income and 20% are not specialists. Thus, the students' dropout may reach 20%of the total children. The respondents suggested solutions to reduce this phenomenon,which was summarized as the following Providing the teachers' training, theestablishment of no formal educational centres, schools rehabilitation, raising the parents'awareness to send their children back to schools. About 27% of students have specialneeds, including visual impairment (28%), mobility issues (24%), cognitive impairment(17%), hearing problems (14%), communication di¨culty (11%) and self-care di¨culties(6%).

5 - Implementation of activities

This project is going to help 3300 children (1750 boys and 1550 girls) school agedchildren host and IDPs to have access to quality formal education opportunities 2320 children who are late in their educationand children dropout of schools to access non-formal education(980 children in catch-up& remedial) with preventive measures of Covid-19 during 2021 in 5 formal schools and 7 nonformal with rehabilitation the WASH facilities taking into consideration the needs of CwD and gender sensitive issues. Besides, conducting back to Learningcampaigns for about 1650 beneficiaries(650 children and 1000 caregivers and parents)to reach out to the children dropped out of schools and re-enroll them and increase the awareness of parents and caregivers towards the importance ofeducation and CP basic rights and child safe guarding. The total cost for each beneficiary is calculated after dividing the final budget on the number of beneficiaries which makes it about 175$ per beneficiary.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk (1): Tension between host community and IDPs (who were evacuated) RiskManagement Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - clearly communicate selection criteria to thecommunity, community leaders and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of theactivity. - harmonize the assistance delivered as per Cluster instructions and guidelines. -coordinate with local council in the targeted area as well as being active in ¦eldcoordination Risk (2): Difficult access for women, unaccompanied children, elderly, peoplewith disabilities (PWD) Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - Register all adultwomen, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities as main recipients ofKits distributed - During post -distribution follow-up, understand how women, children, andother groups accessed the distributed goods and their feedback on the process andmaterials Risk (3): Child safety incidents in school due to bullying and overburdenedteachers Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - Training of partners and localteams on Child safety, development of schools’ specific child safety policies;establishment of complaint mechanisms in school;- Security/safety training fororganization, students and school staff. Risk (4): Protection risks of mixing different agegroups in the same center/class Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - Createseparate times of attendance for each age group; Risk (5): Resistance to gendermainstreaming from male leadership structures. Risk Management Strategy/MitigatingMeasure: - Programme activities which engage women will be endorsed by communityand religious leaders to gain support from male community members to reduce thelikelihood of resistance to these activities.

No children to be employed (under 18 years old) - make sure staffare already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and childsafeguarding, and sign and adhere to a code of conduct that respects and uphold therights of beneficiaries and PSEA - set up communication and complaintsmechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries,including women and children.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risks: Fighting between military groups in the region, military operation andshelling escalation are possible security risks. Mitigation: The security situation inMaaret Tamsrin is considered to be stable compared to other areas of conflict in Syria. Nodirect airstrikes or shelling were recorded during the previous months. IHR maintainsdetailed security protocol for its field staff. The security team inside Syria is linked withother NGO teams and community stakeholders for information sharing. The decision ofsuspension will be taken on a daily basis with up-to-date information. Schools, , and education centers are all in contact with through radio contact maintained among the teams and security team to ensure information flow. Also, schools and education centers are provided with safety equipment used in emergencies like firefighting equipment and first aid kits. Additionally, all staff will get safety and security training to deal with emergencies (Evacuation -first aid). It’sworth mentioning that IHR developed an attached contingency plan for all the target are andin case of high risk situation, IHR immediately will provide cars to evacuate the team, CWD andsecure a new workplace to continue the activities in (Dana-Sarmada-Afrin) It’s worth mention that IHR has an ongoing project in the mentioned area and we have protocols with Turkish authorities to implement Education and protection project in olive branch areas. In addition you will find in the summary the plan B which includes E-learning.
