Project Summary

The public health system in Syria has been degraded by over a decade of conflict and remains extremely vulnerable as a result of the displacement and migration of qualified professionals and the widespread destruction or damage to health facilities. UNRWA’s health services in Syria are facing growingincreasing pressures due to socio-economic crisis, with an increasing number of Palestine refugees now relying heavily on the Agency for health care. It is expected that at least 790,000 patient visits will be made to UNRWA health centres in 2023.
The Agency's 25 health facilities in Syria (23 health centres and health points located in Palestine refugee camps and two mobile clinics) provide a range of primary health care services, including out-patient consultations, immunization services, infant and child growth monitoring, dispensing of medications, gynaecology consultations, laboratory services, non-communicable diseases (NCD) care, MCH care, dental care, and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services. Mobile clinics are deployed to areas that have been the most affected by the conflict and where Agency health facilities have been damaged or destroyed, including Yarmouk and Ein el Tal camps.
UNRWA will continue to subsidise access to secondary and tertiary health care for around 10,000 refugees through a network of partner hospitals and private laboratories. This intervention has become increasingly critical as families’ ability to afford hospitalisation services continues to decrease. Support provided by the Agency will cover between 75 and 95 per cent of the costs, depending on the level of vulnerability and availability of funds.
Dedicated support will be provided to persons with disabilities through the provision of prosthetic devices, including hearing aids for older persons , while baby kits (including diapers) will be provided to 800 pregnant women for their newborn infants. Persons with long-term disabilities and in need of physiotherapy will be referred to external partners in community-based organizations (CBOs) across Syria. It is estimated that 16,000 physiotherapy sessions will be provided.
In 2023, UNRWA will also conduct minor repairs to 15 of its health facilities that require urgent maintenance (this does not include facilities covered under the Emergency Repair, Rehabilitation and Maintenance of UNRWA Installations section of the present appeal). To promote environmental sustainability, UNRWA will install solar systems at UNRWA health facilities to support an uninterrupted cold chain storage system and internet connectivity for eHealth real-time reporting as well as for lighting. UNRWA aims to establish green spaces around Agency health centres and will phase out the use of plastic bags when dispensing medicines to patients and replace them with paper bags or reusable bags, funding permitting. A review on waste disposal practices in Agency health facilities will be carried out to provide recommendations on how to manage waste to reduce pollution and reduce the risk of infectious diseases spreading. Partnerships will be sought with other organizations thatwho are supporting environmental conservation measures.
As part of its commitment to AAP, the Agency will familiarise health centre staff on AAP related principles and actions. This will include supporting patients to provide feedback, and act on same. A standardized CFRM will be strengthened for all SFO programmes, including health.
Under this appeal, UNRWA will continue to respond to COVID-19 by subsidizing COVID-19 testing for 500 Palestine refugees. This assistance is extended to all UNRWA staff to contain the spread of the pandemic and ensure the safety of Agency staff and those they serve. Since 2021, UNRWA has been responsible for administering COVID-19 vaccinations for all UN staff and partner NGOs in Syria as part of the UN vaccination campaign, which has contributed significantly to the inter-agency pandemic response. 3 - Needs

The public health system in Syria has been degraded by a decade of conflict and remains extremely vulnerable as a result of the displacement and migration of qualified professionals and the widespread destruction and damage to health facilities. The COVID-19 pandemic has further strained existing capacity. In 2022, UNRWA’s health services in Syria faced increasing pressures due to the socio-economic crisis in the country and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic as well as the Cholera epidemic that started during the year. Therefore, the 438,000 Palestine refugees living in Syria are in acute need. In the context of the socio-economic crisis and pressure on the public health system, Palestine refugees increasingly rely on humanitarian assistance to respond to their basic health needs. This includes 16,000 persons with disabilities, who are especially vulnerable. More than 4,000 Palestine refugee families returned to Yarmouk and Ein El Tal refugee camps and are particularly in need for support. Due to the heavy damages of UNRWA health facilities during the conflict, Palestine refugees in these camps currently depend entirely on mobile health services.

Mobile clinics will be deployed to areas that have been the most affected by the conflict and where Agency health facilities have been damaged or destroyed.UNRWA will subsidize access to secondary and tertiary health care for around 10,000 refugees through a network of partner hospitals and private laboratories. In addition, dedicated support will be provided to persons with disabilities through the provision of prosthetic devices, including hearing aids for older persons, while baby kits will be provided to 800 pregnant women for their newborn infants. In 2023, UNRWA will conduct minor repairs to 15 of its health facilities that require urgent maintenance. To promote environmental sustainability, UNRWA will install solar systems at UNRWA health facilities.UNRWA will continue to respond to COVID-19 by subsidizing COVID-19 testing for 500 Palestine refugees. In addition, UNRWA will continue spearheading the cholera response through government and Humanitarian Agencies coordinated response. UNRWA will also conduct health staff and patient awareness sessions on COVID-19 & Cholera prevention.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

(1) Exposure to Explosive remnants of war as Palestine refugees access UNRWA Health centres. Mitigating measures: 1.Training and awareness-sessions to UNRWA staff and community members is conducted; large-scale distribution of ERW awareness raising fliers is organized to enhance awareness among the community, particularly in areas with high levels of destruction. 2. Mobile health clinics and health points have been established, in close proximity to the PR communities, minimizing refugees’ movement to access the health services; immunization campaigns limit unnecessary movement. 3. Telehealth services are provided for patients which helps limit their exposure. 4. UNRWA delivers regular medication of NCD patients to their homes and can also provide them by delegation through relatives or friends. (2) Safety, dignity and privacy are eroded for GBV survivors and vulnerable groups when attending health clinics due to overcrowding. Mitigation measures: 1. UNRWA will use the appointment system to manage overcrowding. 2. NCD patients are receiving long term supplies of their medication to reduce the number of visits made to the health clinics. 3.Ensure numbering system and prioritization of most vulnerable; 4.Ensure private spaces are made available where possible 5.Train staff on GBV.(3) The cholera epidemic worsens in Syria. Mitigation measures: 1. UNRWA will continue strengthening its Cholera response through staff training, fundraising and enhanced reporting.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of SEA in the context of provision of health services Likelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigation Measures:- 1. UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time.- 2. UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion. - 3. UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a biennial basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles.- 4. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts.- 5. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA.- 6. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure confidentiality and set up accessible and efficient complaint mechanisms. - 7. UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is responded to.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of Palestine Refugees in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic cases will not start rising again. 5. The Cholera epidemic will not worsen. Risk UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. Risk UNRWA staff and refugees are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security threats and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic start to increase again and cholera epidemic worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will continue delivering preventive, treatment and immunization services 2. Cholera response will be integrated in the response.
