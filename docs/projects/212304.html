Project Summary

The ongoing Syrian crisis has had devastating effects on the population's health.According to the 2023 HNO,14.9 million people need health services, however,the health system faces many challenges, e.g.lack of qualified healthworkers,drugs,medical equipment,and financial support for PHCC staff.35% of hospitals and PHCCs(44%) are either non or partially operational.MDM-ES'S 2023 survey revealed that >80% of assessed PHCCs lacked MHPSS services despite the high prevalence of mental health cases.Limited information was also available on SRMNCAH services.The combination of ongoing insecurity,the COVID pandemic,and earthquakes has further strained the fragile system,reducing capacity for case management and routine service delivery.
In October 2023,MdM conducted an assessment identifying multiple protection needs and risks among IDPs,returnees and host communities in Hasakeh, Aleppo and Raqqa governorates.Lack of access to health services was a huge concern,along with WASH,livelihood support,education,and protection for women and children.Other findings included limited capacity (lack of GBV training and trained psychologists)of women houses' staff to respond to GBV cases and offer PSS and low school attendance rates especially for girls.
The project thus aims to address the most urgent health needs and reduce morbidity and mortality rates in Aleppo, Raqqa and Al-Hasakeh by improving access to inclusive, comprehensive & quality PHC services(including SRH, MHPSS and epidemic response)to affected populations.4 components will be addressed:1) Provision of PHC consultations while facilitating referrals to secondary healthcare;2)regular and continuous training and supervision of medical and non-medical staff and CHWs;3)Community health for psychosocial well-being,health promotion and epidemic prevention(in HFs and communities);4)Emergency preparedness/response plans and stock for responding to epidemic-prone diseases, including facility staff incentives, basic running costs,drugs,consumables and minor rehabilitations.
Risk and mitigation measures include:
Health:Insecurity in the surrounding areas or direct attacks on facilities endangers beneficiaries seeking services and staff working in the PHCCs.Long distances to facilities and lack of transportation also hinder access.SEAH can also occur,due to power imbalances that exist between healthworkers and beneficiaries.
•Likelihood:Medium; Impact:Medium
•Mitigation measures:Enhancing security measures around health facilities,e.g establishing communication channels with local security forces and working groups to stay updated on incidents; continued advocacy on the respect of international humanitarian law and the importance of protecting health services;community-based sessions to reach those with limited access to PHCCs;Regular PSEAH training for health staff and establishing confidential and accessible reporting mechanisms for reporting all incidents.
•GBV risk (WASH):Common toilets and water collection points lack privacy,compromising the dignity and safety of individuals,especially women, girls and PWD.Lack of separate facilities for men and women can also increase GBV risk,including sexual harassment and assault.
•Likelihood:High; Impact:High
•Mitigation measures:Provide separate facilities for men and women to ensure privacy and reduce the risk.Rehabilitation of supported facilities to make them more inclusive and accessible to PWDs;conducting community awareness campaigns to promote a culture of respect and zero tolerance for GBV.
MdM has a zero-tolerance policy towards SEAH.All staff must sign and adhere to the 6 IASC Basic SEA Principles.SEA is considered a serious offence,warranting employment termination.Dedicated staff at HQ level are responsible for ensuring compliance with the ethical framework while focal points are assigned at mission level to oversee PSEAH activities,including training staff and developing/implementing culturally appropriate, context-based mechanisms.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The PRA highlighted beneficiaries’ difficulties accessing essential health services as a significant concern due to long distances,high costs,and lack of information, especially for vulnerable cases,e.g.GBV survivors.In addition to the lack of capacity of women's houses to respond to GBV cases,they were not inclusive and accessible to PWDs.Limited access to safe drinking water due to drastic reductions in electricity and fuel supply caused by TAF strikes on critical infrastructure especially in Hasakeh could increase vulnerability to waterborne diseases.The project is thus designed in line with OCHA HNO2023/2024 and 2023 HRP to ensure quality PHC service provisionto populations in Kalasa,Serrine,Tel Ghazal,Khanik,Khatoniya,Hama, Kobash and Salhabia PHCCs through:•Provision of financial support for key running costs,minor rehabilitations,supplies and support services(HR,drugs,pharmaceutical supplies,nutrition supplies).•OPD consultations,including diagnosis and treatment of common illnesses,CDs and NCDs.MdM will also support trauma cases by providing first aid, management of mild cases/stabilisation and referral to specialised care.•Supply of essential drugs, consumables, PPE, and medical equipment to health facilities(integrating environmentally sustainable management) in all targeted governorates. •SRH service provision:deployment of female staff,including midwives to all PHCCs,BemONC service provision;capacity building to strengthen obstetric services;tailoring/adaption of services to meet beneficiaries' needs, e.g,through home visits.•Establishment of an OTP centre in Salhabia PHCC in Raqqa to treat MAM and SAM cases and conducting IYCF health education (in HFs and communities).•MHPSS:individual and group counselling, mhGAP training for all doctors to assess and manage severe mental health disorders, establishing play corners in each PHCC to foster child growth and development, and community-based PSS to reduce stigma,create stronger linkages,ensure access to MH services,and reach those with limited access to PHCCs.•Community outreach activities to expand prevention, health promotion and healthcare to vulnerable populations.Mass awareness campaigns, radio shows,social media & distribution of IEC materials will be used to enhance community engagement. •Capacity building of health staff to ensure continuum of care.Special consideration is given to strengthening local service providers,installing capacities,contributing to the effectiveness of coordination mechanisms & harmonization and mutualization of tools through clusters and technical working groups) •Establishment of referral linkages from the community to PHCCs and from PHCCs to secondary and specialised services. •PHHC surveillance, preparedness, and response activities for epidemic-prone diseases: active surveillance and response,prepositioning of drugs and medical supplies,staff capacity building,ensuring IPC, case management,PSS support to affected/infected populations.
