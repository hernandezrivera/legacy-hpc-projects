Project Summary

The project objective is Improve households’ immediate access to food through provision of conditional and unconditional assistance depending on the severity of food insecurity phases, vulnerability and seasonality of the livelihoods in Bay, Banadir, Hiraan and Lower Juba.
The project will support urgent life-saving access to adequate food and nutrition and support livelihood recovery for 200,000 vulnerable people (70,000 women. 60,000 men, 40,000 girls and 30,000 boys) who include IDPs, host communities, pastoralists and farming communities in Somalia through conditional and unconditional transfers as well as in kind provision of assistance.
Through the provision of livelihood support, the project will protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings.
The project will involve food assistance, cash combined with livelihood support (“Cash+”) and conditional transfers to help improved immediate access to food of beneficiaries and simultaneously improve access to food, increase livelihoods assets and promote the resilience of the affected population
The project will also complement urgently required investments in resilience and recovery, humanitarian partners and support work towards enabling more sustainable solutions to the cyclical crises in Somalia and help ensure that the current and future droughts do not turn into a famine.
Although famine has been averted in Somalia in 2017 and 2018 response, the project will support further investments in building resilience and enabling recovery to effectively address underlying causes of crises and reduce risk and vulnerabilities. Needs

While large-scale famine has been averted due to timely and scaled-up response in 2017/18, the number of people in need of humanitarian food assistance remains high with 4.6 million people facing acute food insecurity across Somalia. High levels of acute malnutrition persist in several population groups due to underlying/structural causes and urgent treatment and nutrition support is needed for approximately 294,200 acutely malnourished children, including 55,200 who severely malnourished between now and December 2018. Integrated support interventions should be sustained to maintain recent improvements as well as prevent further deterioration in the nutrition situation. Over 1.5 million people facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse need urgent humanitarian assistance and livelihood support between now and December 2018. Livelihood support is also required for 3.1 million people who are Stressed (IPC Phase 2). Priority locations include Bay, Banadir, Hiraan, Gedo and lower Juba. With the elevated likelihood of an El Niño event occurring in late 2018, driving above-average rainfall during the October to December Deyr season, investments in livelihood and resilience support will be required to boost recovery in most areas. Mitigation measures are also needed to prevent flooding and the likely cause crop losses in riverine and lowland areas.While the Somalia Gu rains have been favorable in most places of Somalia, with improved availability of pasture and water for livestock, this has not translated into improved food security for all. Most pastoralists households are yet to recover from the adverse impacts of the 2016-2017 severe drought, especially pastoralists who lost entire or most of their animals and who fell into destitution and displacement. Continued support in livestock production, restocking, livelihood diversification and resilience strengthening is required through 2019 to re-establish their livestock into viable livelihood units.

Activities

Activity 1: Support livelihood rebuilding through conducting emergency livestock treatment, including vaccination of animals against major livestock diseases (SGP, PPR, CCPP, CBPP) Activity 2: Livelihood rehabilitation and diversification support through the provision of fishing gear, assets to fisher folks, provision back-yard poultry input to beneficiaries and provision start-up capital / input to beneficiaries to promote IGAs, honey bee production and vocational skills. Activity 3: Support livestock production for vulnerable household through provision of water to livestock through vaccinations, provision Fodder / feed for “reproductive” animals, and fodder seed for fodder production. Activity 4: Livestock rebuilding - Provision of small ruminants to target beneficiaries based on local contextActivity 5: Training of farmers and agro-pastoralists on good agricultural practices, post-harvest, integrated pest management (IPM), trans-boundary pest and disease.Activity 6: Distribution of in-kind food assistance (GFD) to targeted beneficiaries. Activity 7: Provision of conditional cash to beneficiaries participating in cash for work or assets activities (CFW/ CFA) to support the construction / rehabilitation of productive assets such as water resource structures ( water catchment, irrigation canals and soil water conservation structures, repair of river breakage, rehabilitation of rangeland. Activity 9: Provision of in kind food assistance for MCH delivery. Activity 10: Provision of wet feeding to the targeted beneficiariesActivity 11: Provision of school feeding support to children in school. Activity 12: Provision of certified seed (voucher) and toolsActivity 13: Training of beneficiaries on livelihoods diversification activities (IGAS, honey bee production, vocational training, back-yard poultry farming).

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Building of resilience of food insecure people will be a key component for the project implementation to tackle the key risks and address the underlying causes of food insecurity and vulnerability for IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in terms adequate and sustainable food security and livelihoods. SYPD will work with vulnerable people including forcibly displaced people, both refugees and IDPs and host communities to support resilience building through capacity building in agropastoral practices, income generation and asset creation. Even in the context of protracted displacements, the environment in Hiraan, Lower Shabelle, Bay region, middle Shabelle still presents opportunities for durable solutions and resilience building. SYPD will work with the community leaders, relevant ministry at the local and national level, and local government authorities to find durable solutions including secure land tenure, capacity building and income generation activities, community assets, irrigation systems and hand over mechanisms for community based projects. During the project implementation, SYPD will mainstream capacity building initiatives for community water infrastructure management systems in order to create a strong management structure and eventual hand over mechanisms.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Internally displaced persons in Somalia face the greatest risk of exclusion due to absence basic services, lack of protection mechanisms, legal frameworks such as housing and property rights and discrimination. Minority clans, women and girls are particularly excluded due to cultural norms and community structures mainly dominated by men.SYPD will incorporate inclusion considerations into project design by prioritizing internally displaced persons, returnees and host communities in food security interventions including establishing and strengthening community committees that are balance in terms of age, gender and clan diversity. The project implementation will carry out through needs assessment to identify the differentiated needs of displaced people and other vulnerable groups. The beneficiary registration will ensure that needs analysis brings to the fore all dimensions of exclusion. SYPD will establish inclusive community communication mechanism by forming beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms to ensure that all vulnerable groups have a voice in the project implementation. Feedback will be processed through trained staff and resolved through established community mechanisms. The project implementation will mainstream capacity building on all dimensions of exclusion with proper hand over mechanism to ensure that the mainstreaming is maintained throughout the project implementation and handover. SYPD will carry out community consultations in order to identify obstacles which may hamper the full participation of IDPs, including from marginalized communities in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects. SYPD will include both IDPs, host communities and refugee returnees to prevent any aggravation of tensions between groups of IDPs or between host communities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

SYPD will carry out needs analysis during the beneficiary registration to ensure that all vulnerable people have access to assistance without discrimination. Beneficiary registration will involve community leaders and existing community structures and beneficiary representatives. SYPD will ensure that risks regarding exclusion based on gender and age are identified and mitigated in a timely manner. This will be done through constant community consultations to identify barriers to the participation of women, girls and youth. The project implementation will also include the formation of feedback mechanism that involves women, men, and youth to gather information from beneficiaries access to assistance and provide timely feedback on the project achievement. SYPD will carry out careful and deliberate inclusion of women, girls, youth and other vulnerable groups in the project design, implementation and monitoring of the project implementation. Project committees will be all inclusive of gender and age. SYPD will train gender balanced committees to strengthen the capacity of vulnerable groups in participating in project implementation.To ensure that women and girls have access to assistance without exposing them to risks such as violence, SYPD will manage assistance provision proximity to the dwelling places and in day light to allow safe travel back home. Distributions will be managed through effective crowd control mechanisms and separate lines for men, women and people with special needs.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

SYPD will ensure that beneficiaries are not exposed to armed conflicts and violence by nature of assistance provide by undertaking community consultations during the project mobilization, communication and implementation to understand local dynamics and take due care in project design.SYPD will also under pre-implementation evaluation to identify any risks and community dynamics that may lead to violence or armed conflict. In particular stakeholders consultations and participation will be mainstreamed from the onset of the project to final hand over. In particular, SYPD will ensure the participation of all on affected communities, inclusion of all stakeholders in food security projects and coordination with government authorities.
