Project Summary

IOM will continue to provide and further expand its provision of integrated protection services to conflict-affected populations across NW Syria in community centers and through mobile protection teams. Activities include:
• Specialised child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) case management;
• Structured psycho-social support (PSS) and Mental-Health and Psycho-social support (MHPSS);
• Individual protection assistance (IPA);
• Protection monitoring (on-site and mobile teams);
• Legal advisory services (civil status documentation and HLP) and HLP Due Diligence;
• Community mobilization and outreach;
• Protection capacity building for camp management actors;
• Provision of dignity kits for GBV/emergency response;
• Advocacy with relevant duty-bearers on IASC standards and IHL.
Overall, IOM will directly support 109,890 beneficiaries (32,967 men, 38462 women, 16,483 boys and 21, 978 girls) across NW Syria through community-based and individually-targeted protection interventions, with the objective to mitigate and reduce protection risks and vulnerabilities for crisis-affected populations. Specifically, activities comprise of:
-Provision of full range of integrated protection services, including specialized GBV, CP, MHPSS and legal advisory services through static facilities and mobile outreach teams with a focus on reaching underserved, hard to reach areas across NWS. This includes case management and IPA, explosive ordnances risk education, and protection monitoring.
-Protection service provision at IOM’s 3 Reception Centres and in 10 IOM Planned Camps, including specialised GBV and Child Protection (CP) prevention and response services, safety audits, legal support, IPA, risk education and structured PSS through permanent on-site as well as mobile teams.
-Legal clinics/mobile outreach teams to provide legal awareness and individual legal counselling on civil status documentation (CSD), as well as HLP to conflict-affected populations in Idlib and Aleppo Governorate and HLP and CSD trainings for humanitarian workers in NWS and Turkey.
Protection support in 20 informal IDP sites in Idlib Governorate to mitigate protection risk and build protection capacity of camp management committees (CMCs), including site-level assessments to monitor protection risks.
-Advocacy and awareness raising with duty bearers on IHL, IASC standards and protection principles, including consultations on duty bearers’ policies and the development of SOPs and structured site-based assessments to enhance protective environment in IDP sites.
-Procurement of 10,000 dignity kits for distribution in GBV/emergency response among women and girls of reproductive age in line with GBV SC selection criteria. These include pregnant women, nursing mothers, and women-headed households.
In addition, HLP Due Diligence support across XB Clusters will be continued throughout 2021, including through the formulation of sector-specific guidance on how to minimize the risks of HLP grievances, and appropriate trainings of humanitarian workers. This work will be closely aligned with the Gaziantep HLP Technical Working Group.
IOM will provide capacity support and daily follow-up for its IPs in all aspects of programming and implementation. IOM and its partners prioritize community participation and outreach throughout the project cycle, including through regular consultations and support to community structures. All staff are trained in WGQs for identifying, involving and assisting persons with disabilities (PWDs) which constitute 15 % of all project beneficiaries. All centres will be equipped with ramps/handles to reduce access barriers for PwDs.
Third-party monitoring will be utilized to monitor and evaluate all project activities, including outcome monitoring for GBV/CP case management services in line with GBV SC M&E toolkit. Disaggregated beneficiary numbers will be verified through 4W reporting to the Cluster.

4 - Needs

Across NWS, 42% of assessed communities respondents reported the presence of at least one protection issue as very commonly occurring (HNO 2021). IOM’s protection services are provided in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates, which are between severity level 4 and 5 and include the most underserved areas of NWS.Gender-based violence continues to affect the lives of women and girls who bear the brunt of the crisis. Across NWS, 73% of key informants (KIs) reported that early marriage occurs within the community. Child labor, including child recruitment, is a growing concern with 93.3% of KIs reporting that child labor prevents school attendance (CP MSNA 2020). In 2020 alone, the UN verified 813 incidents of recruitment and use of children in combat roles (HNO 2021). MSNA 2020 data shows that 77% of HHs surveyed across NWS report at least one household member showing signs of psychological distress, in particular children, while PSS/MHPSS services are widely lacking. HLP and CSD issues remain key challenges, with 75% of KIs across NWS reporting lack of civil documentation and 32 % reported HLP concerns such as secondary occupation. Widespread explosive ordnance contamination presents major protection risks across NWS; however, 70 % of key informants had not attended explosive ordnance risk education sessions in 2020 (MSNA 2020). 28% of the population of NWS has a disability (HNAP 2021).C19-associated restrictions on movement have led to an increase in incidents of domestic violence, while persons with disabilities (PwD) are specifically impacted., There remain significant gaps in dedicated protection services, in particular in hard to reach areas close to frontlines and informal IDP sites where protection risks are most acute.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks-Creates an expectation of response; risk of discrimination if a service is provided to specific individuals at the exclusion of others-Physical security risk due to conflict; accessibility barriers for PWDs-Limited judicial mechanisms for redress; risk that by providing information about documentation is perceived as endorsement of said legal system-Lack of quality services and referral expose GBV survivors to harm and risk of stigmatization and further trauma-Resistance from sections of the community on GBV messaging-Risk that existing community structures conflict with CP principles-Familiarization may cause the civilian populace to become complacent to the threat of explosive hazards-Exploitation/abuse by staff Mitigations-Develop clear communication to manage expectations about what services are available and who services are for; receive feedback on the information provided-Collaborate with key members on identifying access barriers and engage in accessibility mainstreaming for PWDs; ensure staff are trained on WGQs, PFA and first aid-Regularly map the existing forms of violations redress available for program design-Training on GBV core concepts and dissemination of SOPs for all staff involved in referral use of GBV SC ethical closure tool for all GBV programming; service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centred services-Culturally sensitive parenting awareness sessions and community outreach activities-Ensure proper environment for carrying out the activities to preserve comfort and confidentiality-Emphasis on "Stop, Warn, and Report" messaging in risk education-Reinforce that humanitarian services are free, provide regular training staff for code of conduct and PSEA

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

As per HNAP 2020 assessment, 48% of HH nationwide (79% in NWS) which reportedly received humanitarian assistance, 14% of those HH were asked for a physical or emotional relationship. Female-headed households are five times more likely to be asked for physical or emotional relationships. The primary obstacles that significantly reduce the number of reported SEA incidents include the fear of losing aid, feelings of shame, a lack of trust and concerns about confidentiality and retaliation. Surveys show that the key SEA risk posed to affected populations are by non-traditional humanitarian actors which are outside of the existing accountability lines of the UN. To reduce the risk of SEA IOM will provide regular staff training on conduct and PSEA through trainings based on the IOM and IASC training packages. IOM and partners are also taking measures to mitigate SEA risks, including the broadcasting of PSEA video and messages on posters, and the interagency SEA hotline on display. All of IOM’s IPs are members of the WoS PSEA Network which provides in-depth training on PSEA and the existing prevention and mitigation tools. IOM and its partners will also conduct regular community consultations and events that enhance awareness on PSEA inside Syria as well as the SEA hotline. To address the risk of SEA posed by non-traditional humanitarian actors, IOM will specifically consult, sensitize and train local authorities, camp management and community leaders on PSEA principles.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:1. Local authorities deny/restrict access to implementation locations or attempt to influence programming. 2. Security-related or physical constraints hinder access to target areas.3. Service providers do not fulfil their obligations and remain committed to coordination and information sharing, so beneficiaries will not enjoy meaningful access to the referred service providers. Mitigating measures: 1. IOM will continue to strictly adhere to neutrality and impartiality and, based on sound risk analysis, adjust interventions accordingly if the context demands it. Ongoing coordination and sensitization between IPs and local authorities. Develop and disseminate targeted key messages on protection to concerned stakeholders. IOM will continue applying its security procedures and policies for program implementation. Strengthen relationship with Turkish authorities, incl. AFAD, TRC, Turkish NGOs, PDMM etc. and observing changes & updates closely. 2. Ongoing situational analysis and adjustment of project activities, including relocation. Continuous monitoring of the situation and provision of services where accessible. 3. Conduct monthly service mapping to ensure the availability of services and make sure beneficiaries are aware of such. Coordinating with the Protection Cluster to ensure awareness of referral pathways.
