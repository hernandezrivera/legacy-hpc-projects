Project Summary

HNO has concluded that despite the beneficial effects of continued agricultural and livestock engagement, many households have had to abandon farming or herding or scale back production due to the unavailability and costliness of inputs; and the HNO document has also captured the significant decrease in livestock in Syria: The livestock sector in Syria has suffered substantially since 2011, with considerable decreases in flock numbers (30-40 per cent for cattle, sheep and goats, 50 per cent for poultry). In 2016, the sector recorded a further decrease in cows (18 per cent), sheep (23 per cent) and goats (20 per cent),112 and further illegal export of productive females. Syria’s veterinary services are running out of vaccines and routine drugs for treatment of Transboundary Animal Diseases (TAD), resulting in a lack of requisite drugs available on the market. Livestock owners are resorting to low-quality drugs with unknown efficacy and side effects on consumer health.
Breeding milk cows is an important livelihood for a considerable number of the agro-pastoralist area of Idleb, which is the target area for the proposed project. Cow milk and it products are important sources of food and nutrition and hence play a key role in food security. Cow milk and it products play more important role than sheep and goat dairy products because the latter are more expensive and contribute less in meeting the needs of the community. The 2018 Syria response plan indicates that as per 2018 estimates, 6.5 million people in Syria are food insecure, while a further 4 million people are at risk of becoming acutely food insecure.
An estimated 590,000 men, women, boys and girls are facing emergency levels of food insecurity in Idleb Governorate. Protracted conflict, inflation, high food and commodity prices; and poor purchasing power among the population including livestock keepers signifies the dire situation and vulnerability of people. The situation is more so for children and pregnant women. The protracted conflict and frequent inter OAG fighting, higher prices of feed, prevalent livestock diseases have further constrained food security, veterinary service delivery. Majority of vulnerable host communities and IDPs, among them men, women, boys, girls, the elderly and the disabled that are highly dependent on animal source foods (meat and milk) for their nutrition are at risk of hunger and malnutrition, while livestock assets are at risk of diseases
Because of the needs and rationale above, Nasaem is requesting funding to conduct the following:
- Provision of technical guidance for breeding and feeding milk cows.
- Provision of fodder and other dietary supplements to male and female cow breeders to protect their assets.
- Provision of trainings on establishing community-based cooperatives for male and female cow breeders.
Nasaem's team will discuss and agree on selection criteria with local councils and communities. Following that, Nasaem will identify and register eligible vulnerable households who have one or two cows to be supported. Each household out of 1000 eligible households will receive 14 fodder bags, each bag contains 50 kg of concentrated pellet fodder, in addition to that the cow breeders (men and women) will receive 10 kg of minerals supplements and vitamins (premix) with 30 kg of mineral salt block. The total amounts that will be provided from these nutrients adequate for one cow for about 150 days. The fodder and other dietary nutrients will be distributed to cow breeders in two batches; the first batch will be distributed in November, the second in December 2019. The quantity provided of fodder will cover at least 50% of the fodder needs for one cow during the winter season. This quantity has been determined to first ensure coverage of the maximum number of vulnerable households and at the same time avoiding creating dependency. Nasaem will conduct solid monitoring and evaluation for the project to measure its impact.

Needs

According to the HNO 2018, Syria is now the world’s most complex humanitarian crisis. The 2019 Syria HNO estimates 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies.The HNO 2019 indicates that 200,000 breeders’ households are suffering from the loss of productive assets (livestock) and damaged infrastructure (land, irrigation infrastructure, facilities, etc.) which is affecting both farmers and breeders. Sector assessments indicate that several population groups are particularly affected by food insecurity, including host and resident population particularly headed by vulnerable members are more food insecure. The estimated ratio between urban and rural People in Need in the sector is 55:45.With the winter coming, it is expected that populations will need money to cover heating costs and livestock breeders might not be able to afford feed for their livestock. Likewise, during winter, there are few vegetables grown and people will rely more on milk and dairy products to enrich their diet. The threats of livestock diseases require a robust and sustained response to enhance protection of livestock assets, access to livestock derived food (milk and meat) and rehabilitation and early recovery of livestock based livelihoods. Urgent provision of feed and veterinary interventions among these livestock will protect the livelihoods of these vulnerable men, women, girls and boys. Nasaem Team was conducted key informant interviews with livestock specialists and members of the FAO-led Livestock Technical Working Group in Gaziantep. Interviewed specialists have advised that provision of winter fodder for vulnerable cows breeders (most of these are women as it is mostly women who look after cows within the household) for the following reasons: - Cows produce abundant quantities of milk (20 liters per day or more), but they need large amounts of concentrated mixture fodder, which is relatively expensive. One cow consumes about 10 kg of this on average. In addition, cows rely less on pastures compared to sheep and goats.- The cows available in the area are come from improved, imported species and they are more prone to be affected by the harsh condition, which poses risks on the cows, their vulnerable owners and the essential milk product market in the area, which is very important for the food security and a source of indispensable animal protein.- Cows are the most underserved livestock as many NGOs focus on sheep or agriculture despite the fact that cows provide important sources of milk and dairy products.- The number of cows has decreased significantly. One key informant said that in a village, in Ehsem sub-district, currently there are only 18 cows but before the crisis there were 80 cows.- Female and elderly cow breeders are facing more challenges while trying to protect their productive assets and are more likely to sell their assets in the coming winter. For the above reasons and others, specialists advised that supporting cow breeders has been a gap that needs to be bridged urgently to ensure milk and dairy availability in the target area.

Implementation of activities

- Meeting local councils and cow breeders to inform them about the aim, the main activities of the project and selection criteria for beneficiaries.- Nasaem will distribute the concentrated pellet fodder, minerals supplements and vitamins (premix) with mineral salt block to 1000 of the most vulnerable breeders. According to HRP 2019 logframe, the indicator for this activity is # of Quantity of animal feed distributed.- Identifying beneficiaries and qualified trainers who will conduct technical guidance trainings.- Cow breeding trainers will tailor the trainings through conducting focus group discussions with the beneficiaries to better understand their concerns and problems.- Conducting the trainings in each location. The indicator is # of targeted herders assisted.- Nasaem will announce for tendering, contracting and procurement of fodder and other dietary nutrients.- Inspection of fodder quality by Nasaem's technical staff.- Agreeing with beneficiaries on times and locations of distributions ensuring that these are as safe as possible.- Selecting active cow breeders who show interest in collaborative work and who have the minimum necessary qualification.- Identifying trainings and agreeing on the training materials in collaboration with FAO and Gaziantep Livestock Working Group. - Conducting the trainings.

Protection Risk Analysis

To avoid creating tensions among cow breeders as not all of them will receive assistance, Nasaem will discuss the project with the local councils and will also explain the project’s goal and vulnerability criteria to all cow breeders in the target area. To avoid doing harm, Nasaem will ensure that the quality and validity of distributed items. Moreover, Nasaem will train its staff on its code of conduct, child protection, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies. Nasaem’s female field staff will receive a training on referral of vulnerable cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to service providers.
