Project Summary

More than 11 years of crisis have inflicted immense suffering on the civilian population, who have been subject to massive and systematic violations of international humanitarian and human rights law. More recently, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have increasingly become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. The overall estimated Sector PIN for 2022 – of food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity – is estimated at 13.9 million, with 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The acute food insecure figure includes 2 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure. FSA sector foresees an increase in the number of food-insecure households in 2023 (HNO 2023).
The food security situation of Syrian families continues deteriorating which will lead to a possible increase in the number of food-insecure households in 2023. According to WFP RBC’s remote food security monitoring, in 2022, (especially female-headed) reported facing food shortages and engaging in some sort of food coping mechanism to maximize the use of little available resources.
Therefore, in order to improve the food security of the resident and displaced population, mitigate the negative coping mechanism, and restore income-generation opportunities to increase the state of food security, their livelihoods, and the population’s participation in economic life, WATAN Foundation designed the following axes taking into consideration the priorities of Food Security & Livelihoods cluster:

3 - Needs

During the second half of 2021 hostilities have re-intensified along front lines in northern and southern Syria, triggering new displacements and destruction as well as continued violations of IHL and IHRL. At the same time, the long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9 million IDPs remain staggering, particularly for the over two million people in 1,760 informal settlements and planned camps, often hosted in inadequate shelters and limited access to basic services. Throughout 2021, the macroeconomic context has continued to deteriorate sharply in Syria. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal spending, widespread job loss, and unilateral coercive measures have plunged additional segments of the population into humanitarian need. As a result, families have taken on more debt and they increasingly resort to harmful coping mechanisms. These include child labor, child marriage, and the sale of productive household assets. Food insecurity has grown; Syria ranked among the 10 most food-insecure countries globally by mid-2021, with an estimated 12.8 million people considered food insecure (OCHA 2022). The suggested Interventions by WATAN will support FSL sectors in the local communities. WATAN will target both IDPs and host communities equally, ensuring the equal participation of males and females. To maximize its impact and avoid duplication, the intervention addresses the gaps identified by the FSL cluster.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

WATAN will apply the standard commitments to gender and diversity in emergency programming including mainly, and not limited to, Commitment to Dignity, Commitment to Access, Commitment to Participation, Commitment to Safety, and Gender-based Violence (GBV) prevention and response and Child protection. As specific actions are taken to reduce the risk of GBV, projects inputs distribution will be conducted by a distribution team consisting of male and female members. Distributions will be carried out during daylight hours and in locations reported to be safe by women and girls. GBV consultation services will be in place to ensure safe, confidential, and appropriate systems of care for survivors who report experienced GBV violence.
