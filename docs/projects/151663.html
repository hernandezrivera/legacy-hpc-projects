Project Summary

This 12 months project will seek to support and improve protection standard of 2871 (Boys 1157 and Girls 1714) Vulnerable children including UASC, Child at risk, CAAFAG and 1260 (men 481 and Female 779) among which 500 care givers in the host communities, IDPs camp and returnees with direct intervention. In Additional Build the Capacities of 25 staff relevant line Ministries to support child protection sustainability and humanitarian development nexus in two LGAs ( Nganzai and Magumeri) of Borno State.

Needs

In North –east Nigeria, to be precise in Borno State, Nganzai and Magumeri Local Government has suffer a lot as the result of the armed conflict due to emergence of Boko Haram since 2009 has resulted in forced displacement, with an ever-growing number of people fleeing in search of protection both within Nigeria and the neighboring countries. The conflict has remained protracted without end in sight. It is a serious protection crisis. According to NEMA report, as at the end of September 2018 in Borno State, more than 100,000 people had been internally displaced across neighboring LGA of Guzamala and taking shelter in Magumeri and Nganzai LGAs including in excess of 65,000 children. Nganzai LGA alone received over 80,000 IDPs. According to the same DTM report, about 56 per cent of the displaced population is persons below the age of 18 years. Those children are at a high risk of violence, abuse and exploitation, including some of them being victims of grave violations inside and around camps or other areas where they sought refuge. It is noted herein that, in contexts such as in the one at the Local Government Areas (LGAs), Boko Haram took advantage of the vulnerability of displaced populations to recruit children and commit other crimes, including sexual violence and abduction of children.The Humanitarian response plan 2019 to target 115, 069 Children and 16,652 caregivers in the two LGAs. Among which children in area some is said to have been recruited, abducted or held by non-state armed groups; approximately 15% of these children are survivors of sexual and gender-based violence and require socio-economic reintegration assistance. Sustained follow-up and community-based approaches to reintegration for children formerly associated with armed groups will be required to mitigate risks such as stigmatization and rejection within communities and many has been recruited or has been victims of violent attacks in the ongoing crisis. Such vulnerable girls and boys and their caregivers require socio-economic and psychosocial assistance to recover from their experiences and to reintegrate back into society. A threat to the physical security of children has been cited as a barrier to children’s access to learning. A multi-sectoral approach will be required to address the protection risks affecting children’s access to basic services. Children and their families need critical knowledge on how to mitigate the risk of injury by such devices.Though it is the role of the relevant State Ministries to provide protection services to displaced children through identification, documentation and integrated case management, health care, high-quality education and psychosocial support, significant capacity gaps have been highlighted and assistance has been requested in these two LGAs. It is therefore imperative that the humanitarian community scale-up child protection work for this highly vulnerable group and other groups at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect, both in terms of immediate direct support for CAAFAG, integrated case management of UASC and capacity building of community structures and the Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development (MWASD) (also referred to as “Ministry” hereafter) to be able to fulfill their mandate for sustainability

Activities

Through this project and building on any existing structures, GSF with the support from UNICEF and other stakeholders proposes to implement a broad-based programme the following strategies. Output 1: LGA-based actors are trained and mentored on integrated Case Management, MRM and CAAFAG Reintegration Approaches1.1 Train about 12 key project staff of GSF on the concepts of integrated case management, community-based socio-economic reintegration programme for CAAFAG and on MRM information gathering, documentation and reporting;1.2 Train at least 10 case workers and 10 Community Base Volunteers in child protection in emergency with focus on MRM, reintegration of CAAFAG and integrated case management;1.3 Train 10 MRM Interviewers on MRM and on how to interview children;1.4 Identify and train 2 CPCs and 2 MSGs (total of 60 participants) on community mobilization, child rights monitoring and prevention of grave violations against children;Output 2: The system for identification, interviewing and verification of incidents of grave violations is strengthened at community levels2.1 Conduct MRM interviews for at least 10 potential cases of grave violations per week. This target might not be met or could be exceeded based on external factors on the ground including security situation and access to information/affected people;2.2 Transcribe MRM interviews using standard incident report form, on a daily basis, and send to UNICEF on a weekly basis some verified or reported incidents for entry into the central MRM database managed by UNICEF in Abuja;2.3 Carry out child rights monitoring activities through weekly family visits, and organize community meetings with new arrivals to understand impact of conflict on children; identify and prepare potential families/individuals affected by grave violations to be interviewed;2.4 Participate in the Borno State level Sub-Working Group on CAAC and contribute inputs into the quarterly Global Horizontal Notes and Annual Report of the Secretary General on Children and Armed Conflict for 2016.Output 3: Recruitment and use of children by armed groups, particularly by the CJTF, is prevented in 5 focused LGAs;3.1: Conduct project familiarization meetings at the focused LGAs with community leaders, members of the CPCs and MSGs on the main project components, emphasizing their anticipated roles and responsibilities in the project implementation. This is one way to ensure community buy-in, participation, ownership and sustainability of the child rights and protection awareness campaigns;3.2 Establish new Child Protection Committees (CPCs) or reactivate existing ones and form Mother Support Groups (MSGs) in all focused LGAs. It is proposed that each LGA will have 2 CPCs and 2 MSGs, making a total of 4 community-based committees for the child rights awareness aimed at prevention of grave violations against children. These committees will help facilitate awareness sessions along with GSF staff and other volunteers;3.3 Carry out monthly community awareness campaigns on child rights and child protection. The target audiences are children, parents, community leaders and members of security forces, CJTF and vigilante groups. Every LGA will conduct at least 1 session and 1 community meetings per month. 3.4: Develop locally acceptable and culturally sensitive awareness materials (posters, radio messages and flyers) on child rights violations, with focus on prevention of recruitment and use of children by armed groups. Distribute and disseminate the messages to all project beneficiaries.
