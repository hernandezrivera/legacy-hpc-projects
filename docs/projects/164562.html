Project Summary

the projects aims to:
•Increase access to physical rehabilitation centers, and provide equitable health service for those most vulnerable (people with Disabilities PWDs) through physiotherapy treatment and rehabilitation centers in (Dara’a, Quneitra, Rural Damascus and Aleppo ) and to improve their strength and mobility. Also teaching beneficiaries how to manage their condition more effectively, such as improving their lifestyle by eating more healthily and exercising regularly through conducting awareness raising sessions besides reinforcement and improvement of the psychological well-being of WMGB through structured MHPSS. In addition, providing awareness-raising sessions based on dealing with persons with disabilities and disability inclusion in everyday activities. As well, provision of assistive devices for PWDs.
all projects services will be provided through physiotherapy center in each location, that is including a team of volunteers , physiotherapist, and medical team, in order to provide physiotherapy sessions to PWDs after conducting case study and case assessment, in addition, monthly based assessment will be conducted to project beneficiaries to provide them with assistive devices. and each center will be run in two shifts (morning and evening from 9 AM to 7 PM / six days a week).
•Multi-purpose cash provision to most vulnerable people to support people with disabilities to meet their basic immediate needs, to ensure their ability to obtain their consumables as they prefer such as diapers and hygiene items, to prevent people with disabilities from using negative coping mechanism practices among severely vulnerable people and uphold their dignity and to complement to the governmental efforts during any humanitarian response and maximize the impacts for emergency responses.
TdH together with the local partner will rehabilitate PHC center in Al Tal area which will provide primary and secondary health services and ensure refferal to tertiary health care services.

4 - Needs

According to HNO Syria 2020, there are almost 12.01 M PiN of health services. Currently TdH running 2 Physiotherapy centers in Aleppo& Rural Damascus, it was found that more than 5,000 PiN health services.Based on HNO Syria 2020, field observations and the number of people who received physiotherapy services in TdH Centers the results show 28% of adult men and 50% of adult women reported trouble walking/climbing steps, Amputation, Hemiplegia, arthritis and Spinal deformities. Related to orthopedic health problems, roughly 12% of children boys and 10% children girls suffer from congenital orthopedic abnormalities, Pied BOTS, Scoliosis or congenital dislocation of the hips,Cerebral palsy and Amputation cases.Due to the observations and dealing with beneficiaries, It was found that there is a big need for MHPSS services especially for children and adults who suffer from Cerebral palsy. In order to assess the prevalence and gender division of mental distress among the assessment locations, a range of questions was asked on the frequency of experiencing certain feelings such as sadness, nervousness, restlessness, fidgety, hopelessness, worthlessness(using Kessler Psychological Distress Scale).The results show 12% of adult men and 27% of adult women,26% children boys and 35% children girls score moderate to high on mental distress.The main findings confirm the needs to provide health intervention programs for the different age groups in the assessed communities to help them for coping with their difficult situationsBeneficiaries will be selected through criteria mainly (Severity of Disability, number of family household, number of disabled persons in family, women headed HHs, PwDs headed HHs, in addition to other social criteria).

5 - Implementation of activities

Leveraging their existing expertise & access in a way that is compliant with GoS protocols, ensuring that the most vulnerable people have access to suitable care & aligned with the health sector 2020 response strategy .TdH through SCFPA will establish (5) physical rehabilitation centers in strategically located areas accessible to a wide population. 1 will be in Dar’a City, 1 in Quneitra City &1 in Nashabiyyeh,1 in Jaramana & 1 in Aleppo with mobile teams in each locations to serve 6600 beneficiaries.The project complies with the health sector objectives 1&3.Activities are as following:Provision physiotherapy services for PwDs in addition to provision of assistive devices, home visits&follow up for Peers of PwDs. Provision of MHPSS services for PwDs Provision of multipurpose cash for PwDs based on pre-determined criteria for 240HHs in each location. TDH also in coordination with local partner to rehabilitate Al Tal PHC center which expected to provide up to 75,000 services per year for both host community & IDPs including PwDs. The project complies with health sector objectives 1,2 & 3. TdH will take into consideratio GBV standards through trained staff Male & female, suitable working times for women & girls, Separate rooms for medical examination & GBV reporting incidents,Separated bathroom for staff- beneficiaries, ensure women’s access to basic health services,Engage men & boys in all activities based on existing resources, effective referral pathway & advocacy .Beneficiaries involved in the evaluation process & project design through Participation in evaluating the services provided & their suggestions to develop the work ,there are also boxes for complaint &suggestions in the centers, which are opened in coordination with the local partner. they're also involved in proposing the topics as well as ways of discussing either awareness campaigns, FGDs or other proposed methods and topics,Participation in the questionnaires and taking their views and observations.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

TDH-IT will ensure the confidentiality of service providers & beneficiaries to protect information gathered about women, children and PwDs according to data protection policiesStaff should not reveal beneficiary's names or any identifying information to anyone not directly involved in the programBy ensuring protection considerations of all humanitarian interventions during all stages of project cycle, prioritizing safety, dignity & avoid causing harm by Prevent & minimize any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people’s vulnerability to physical & psycho social risks.In order to grant access to most vulnerable elderlies & People with disabilities the project will ensure the access to the services through the mobile team and centers will be prepared with special accessibilityRecruitment of staff is very critical & essential, as it will ensured to recruit project staff from the area of intervention as they have better understanding for area’s culture, needs & community acceptance Communication between project staff & beneficiaries should respect the culture believes, so communication will be conducted from women/staff to women/beneficiaries & vice versa & will apply the same for men to men communication in area of interventionAs project activities are targeting the most vulnerable PwDs we have to Address all logistical & environmental issues that are preventing their access as for distance, proximity and safe spaces.TdH has set up an internal reporting procedure to report allegations of violations of code of conduct including cases of sexual abuse and/or exploitation procedure is regularly updated to reflect best practice in international communityA reporting mechanism is created & placed in easily accessible locations to TdH staff & beneficiaries via Posters includes reporting hotline to PSEA focal point & complaint Box locationTDH will not share data linked to group or individual including GBV survivors, women and children.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Local partners lose access to the project locations and are unable to implement activities, M1: SCFPA has displayed an outstanding ability to implement humanitarian projects despite constraints and limitations put onto them. Currently they are already accessing the mentioned locations with funding from various entities for several months. However, if they lost the access to project locations, Should they, TdH would either identify other suitable partners or, in coordination with them and with MoH, identify other locations with same high needs, where the programming could continue. R2: TdH are unable to timely contract technically skilled employee, M2:TdH has solid recruitment/human resources processes and regulations to ensure the best qualified candidates are hired in a timely manner. Early launching of the process is key. Vacancies will be publicly announced soon after the approval is received .R3: bureaucracy and delay in getting needed approvals from local authorities, M3: TdH already obtained approvals to work in Northern and southern area of Syria, in addition, TdH will advocate through the local partner to have the needed approvals based on the signed MOU, R4: delay in receiving the fund due to the sanctions on the banking system in Syria, M4: TdH has different banking accounts in Syria with different pipelines to receive the fund from different sources. R5: Security risks : TdH and Local partner’s staff are exposed to threats, including kidnapping, detention, intimidation, and serious injury, M5: the security situation has been considered relatively calm but unpredictable and partners are able to conduct their work in relative safety, TdH Security person and Security Focal points in the project locations are regularly analyze the operating environment and have established safeguards to prevent and respond to risks of insecurity which may arise, addressed through location-specific safety and security management plans.
