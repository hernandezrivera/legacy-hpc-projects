Project Summary

This project aims at providing quality essential lifesaving and life-sustaining health and nutrition services in Bay and Bakool area, with focus on women, children, and other vulnerable groups and strengthens the local capacity to deliver evidence-based and cost-effective services based on the EPHS and Primary Health Care Approach. In addition, this will involve measles case management, immunization campaign, emergency preparedness, and response capacities, awareness and mobilization campaigns.

Project Activities

READO will provide daily life saving essential medical services including daily treatment of patients and proper case management of AWD/cholera and measles affected patients in Baidoa, Hudur, Dinsoor and Wajid district urban/rural areas as well as referral of complicated cases to the nearest hospital. Cholera case management. UNICEF and MOHSWS will support for the health supplies Measles case management - Measles case management it depends on the severity of the patient, severe complicated measles, refer urgently to Hospital, Measles with EYE or Mouth Complications, give vitamin A, if pus draining from the eye, treat eye infection with TEO, If mouth ulcer treat, follow up in 2-days and Measles Give Vitamin A, Advise mother to return in one week for the third dose of Vitamin A. READO will support 9 Health facilities. 5 fixed PHC and 2 Hospitals in Bay region Huddur general Hospital and Diinsoor (3 in Baidoa Rural 1 village Awdiinle 1Lowile 1 Barwaqo2 in relocation camp of Baidoa and 1 mobile in Baidoa 2. PHC in Bakool Wajid and 2 PHC in Elberde as per EPHS through PHC approach with a strong referral component. 2. A network of 9 Mobile health teams (each linked with fixed PHC) are deployed for outreach points, targeting U5 and PLWs. 3. Nutrition services such as OTP/SFP and IYCF/ Nutrition education are integrated into the PHCs (MCHs) and mobile clinics units to enhance the therapeutic efficacy of health and nutrition services. 4. Strengthen Health information system through training staff on utilizing existing MOH/Health cluster data tools and databases, disaggregating data by age and sex to improve quality of data and enhance services for women, men and children of various age groups in line with MoH Essential package of Health Services. 5. Train and supervise medical staff on sexual and reproductive health, maternal-child health, child spacing and Basic Emergency Maternal Obstetric and Neonatal Care in fixed facilities and Mobile team units. 6. Referral services are strengthened through the provision of ambulance services in all areas for prompt emergency treatment of critically ill prioritizing PLWs and U5. 7. Integrated surveillance, referral and epidemic response mechanisms to strengthen and promote early detection and response of outbreaks. 8. 110 CHW and 22 nurses of MCHs are trained on techniques to deliver health/nutrition/protection related awareness messages and support the referral to the nearest PHCs, Mobile clinic or secondary health facility. 9. Strengthen community ownership by training community Health committees as the link between health facility and community and vice versa. 10. Community volunteers referred to SAM cases and accessing health facilities receive comprehensive Medical treatments in line with set standards.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion : READO will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. READO, will ensure that the lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services. In addition, READO will identify the power dynamics in the area which included who has access to health care, who manages facilities, who is able to inﬂuence decisions on the provision of health services. READO will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. This information will be considered when determining locations for health interventions. In regards to avoiding tensions between the host and displaced communities, READO will provide the local/host community with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. In addition, READO will consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conﬂict between the two communities. Flood or other emergencies: Target communities affected by ﬂooding, AWD, inﬂux or IDPs or forced IDP evictions etc., unforeseen by EWS. To mitigate this, READO will scale-up the beneﬁciaries targeted with health interventions, to provide emergency assistance to those worst affected.Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age READO will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. This will be done through introducing special arrangements for persons unable to access health facilities eg. Mobile health services, facilitate accompaniment arrangements (e.g. youth groups assisting the elderly/persons with disability; community networks). In addition, READO will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. Furthermore, READO will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to health needs. READO will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their speciﬁc needs and preferences for health center location, design and services. Moreover, READO will engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. READO will also have have a proportionate number of female health staff. READO will also establish complaints and response mechanism. READO will use information boards to display Complaint Response Mechanism phone number during training sessions activities/beneﬁciaryselection and at health centers. Beneﬁciaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaint. READO will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaints will be documented and reviewed regularly. In turn, the call center will be in charge of calling beneﬁciaries as part of the monitoring system. Protection of civilians: READO will ensure, that the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe. This will be done by identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets. READO will not share information on the identity of patients unless informed consent has been given (e.g. names, addresses, or traits)
