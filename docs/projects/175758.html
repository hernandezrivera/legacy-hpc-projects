Project Summary

Since the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) in September 2018, active conflict has noticeably reduced, and an increase in returns has been observed. Humanitarian organizations continue to provide support and assistance as prospects for peace and development improve, and confidence is generated for durable solutions, including returns, relocations, or local integrations. Unfortunately, a lack of accommodation, workspace, and other key facilities often hinders humanitarian operations and a sustained presence in strategic deep field locations. In response, IOM will establish humanitarian hubs to enable the expansion of operations across hard-to-reach areas of South Sudan through an increase in static humanitarian presence in key strategic deep field locations. Based on lengthy consultations with field offices and State Inter Cluster Working Groups (ICWG), sixteen potential locations were proposed, of which eight have been selected as priority locations, namely Jiech, Kajo Keji, Kodok, Leer, Mandeng, New Fangak, Pieri, and Raja. IOM will provide field working facilities, accommodation, and warehousing to enable agencies to maintain a continuous presence on the ground and international staff to overnight in these locations, as well as allow care of the overflow of staff who are unable to find any other accommodation. Based on the eight proposed locations and further security/technical assessments undertaken, five pilot locations have been identified for 2021, namely Kajo Keji, Kodok, Jiech,Raja and Leer, at which the initial hubs will be set up; best practices and lessons learned will inform the establishment of humanitarian hubs in the other five sites identified. The establishment of the pilot hubs at Kodok and Jiech are being funded by DFID and Jiech by SSHF, so IOM will support the establishment of hubs in Kajo Keji, Raja and Leer under this project. IOM will work closely alongside the Swedish Civil Contingencies Agency (MSB) and UN OCHA, as well as NGOs that will assume responsibility for the running of the hubs. IOM will hand-over the management of the hubs and work on building the capacity of NGOs and local partners to ensure sustainable management of the humanitarian hubs subsequent to the conclusion of the intervention.

1 - Needs

South Sudan remains one of the most challenging service delivery environments in the world as agencies continue to operate in a context of limited access, persistent insecurity, including deliberate targeting of humanitarian assets and staff. Various intrusions on humanitarian space, ranging from violence to bureaucratic impediments, were seen in 2020. Effective, sustainable humanitarian delivery is dependent on strengthened engagement with local-duty bearers and partners, enhanced understanding of and appreciation for humanitarian space, and improved measures that promote the safety and security of humanitarian workers.Effective, agile coordination that seeks innovative ways to reach underserved vulnerable people will be critical to an effective humanitarian response in South Sudan. In order to meet the needs of vulnerable populations, overall humanitarian interventions will need to be ‘as local as possible, and as international as necessary’ through enabling equal and impartial access to assistance and services, and to maintain a continuous presence as close to the affected population being served. Given the likely negative impact of COVID-19 on the prospective humanitarian funding for 2021, and high levels of humanitarian need across the country, it will be more important than ever to have strong, joint needs analysis and assessment to guide strategic response planning for effective, well-coordinated humanitarian delivery to the most vulnerable people (South Sudan HNO 2021). At present, limited facilities are in place to enable a static or temporary humanitarian presence in key deep field strategic locations, including those impacted by floods. Through this project, IOM will provide common service to enable humanitarian agencies to respond to the lifesaving needs of the flood affected population through the set up and provision of safe and dignified humanitarian hubs with working and accommodation spaces that will support scale up an efficient, effective and timely humanitarian response to the most vulnerable communities living in hard to reach area of South Sudan.

2 - Project activities

IOM South Sudan aims to ensure that structures are in place to facilitate and support the effective, targeted delivery and monitoring of services to displaced populations by making the response as local as possible. To facilitate and meet this objective, IOM will establish three out of eight planned humanitarian hubs under this project in 2021 in South Sudan. The project will be undertaken in four phases as follows: 1) Mobilization and Technical Scoping: Sites at the proposed locations have already been identified, with a focus on building cost-effective semi-permanent durable structures. The structures will be built using local materials and labour. IOM will assume responsibility for engagement with the local government and partners, as appropriate, as well as the design of the humanitarian hub site itself. Each humanitarian Hub will provide sectioned accommodation for women and men, warehousing, communication services, security, and office space to humanitarian workers. All humanitarian agencies and workers will have access to full accommodation, generators for power and electricity, latrines and ablutions, water purification, bunkers, fuel storage, a common kitchen, and sufficient office space to maintain communications with colleagues and co-agencies. Each Humanitarian Hub is envisioned to host and accommodate up to an additional 20 humanitarian workers at a time, and continuous rotation will allow the entire humanitarian community to benefit from the establishment of such hubs.2) Hub Establishment: IOM will facilitate local procurement, logistics, and construction of the humanitarian hubs. 3) Operationalization and Management of Humanitarian Hubs: Upon the completion of the humanitarian hubs, IOM and the identified implementing partner (IP) will assume responsibility during the initial phase for administrationmanagement, and maintenance of the Humanitarian Hubs. The Hubs will eventually be operated on a cost-recovery basis, and all agencies accommodated at the hubs will sign a standardized Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with IOM. However, at the initial stage of operationalization and up to six-months thereafter, running costs will require support from donors.It is foreseen that IOM and the IP will manage each Humanitarian Hub for approximately six months, during which time IOM will provide ongoing training to those agencies selected to assume management of the Hub. Therefore, the budget includes all costs associated with start-up, running, and maintenance of the Hubs for six months.4) Hand-over: One agency per site will be selected as Humanitarian Hub Manager and will assume responsibility for administering, managing, and maintaining the Humanitarian Hub. IOM will launch an open call for interest among humanitarian organizations which will be reviewed by a technical team – encompassing IOM, MSB, and UN OCHA. Agencies will be prioritized based on their prior experience/presence in the targeted counties, logistical capacity to set up and maintain the Humanitarian Hub (e.g., maintenance and facility upkeep); and administrative capacity to manage the Humanitarian Hub, such as resource management and staffing levels. During the hand-over phase, IOM will sign an MOU with the selected Organization, detailing roles and responsibilities during the hand-over phase, as well as subsequent to the Organization assuming sole responsibility for hub management. IOM will subsequently partner with the respective Organization at each hub location to capacitate on hub management and prepare the eventual hand-over of the hub management, which will principally take the form of on-the-job training. If required, IOM may request the secondment of standby personnel, including MSB technical staff, to support the organizations taking final ownership of the hubs during the first three to six months of hand-over, to ensure proper running/management of the hubs and long-term sustainability herein.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The humanitarian hubs will enable humanitarian agencies to rapidly establish or reestablish presence in areas in which people have been heavily affected by the ongoing conflict and subsequent outcomes, enhancing overall efficiency by providing stable and secure living and working conditions. Establishing the hubs will support the entire humanitarian community, providing common spaces to enable agencies to establish a sustained presence and deliver services to the most vulnerable. They will serve to lessen the scope for communities to leave their habitual place of residence for the purpose of receiving assistance, as humanitarian and development agencies will be able to expand operations across hard-to-reach areas of South Sudan through an increase in static humanitarian presence in key strategic deep field locations. This will also serve to lessen the exposure of women and girls to sexual violence in relation to access to humanitarian assistance, and more particularly as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services.
