Project Summary

Palestine refugees – already suffering from the humanitarian and socio-economic impacts of a 16-year blockade, and the consequences of recurrent hostilities – were confronted with another round of escalation of violence in August 2022, which left 49 Palestinians dead of whom 17 were children and 360 injured (Source: Ministry of Health).
Repeated exposure to conflict, compounded with the effects of continued restrictions on the movement of persons and goods, has created a mental health crisis in Gaza. According to the MSNA, 40% of households reported having at least one family member suffering from psychosocial distress of trauma. Children are the group mostly affected and a recent MHPSS assessment undertaken by UNRWA identified that 42% of new first grade students show signs of trauma and require intervention.
The impact of a deteriorating socio-economic situation, including extremely high-unemployment rates and related poverty, has resulted in a rise in cases of GBV including early marriage and domestic violence.
Another consequence of the recurrent hostilities is the presence of explosive remnants of war, which pose significant threat to civilians. According to UNMAS, in 2021 60 people were injured by ERW, and 1 boy killed. Since the beginning of 2022, 12 people, including 7 children have been injured by ERWs. While much of the explosive ordnances from the escalation have been removed, the potential for renewed conflict remains high, giving rise to the need for further Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE).
To mitigate and respond to the protection concerns and risk factors arising from the repeated escalation of hostilities , UNRWA will increase protection capacities and response through a series of multifaceted initiatives focusing on MHPSS, Child Protection, GBV and EORE.
Underpinning the initiatives will be a significant coordination with the Protection cluster and a community-based approach that ensures Palestine refugees are partners in decision making.

1 - Needs

Palestine refugees -already suffering with the humanitarian and socio-economic impacts of a 16-year blockade, and still suffering the consequences of the May 2021conflict, which has aggravated Gaza’s chronic shelter, infrastructure and energy deficits, were confronted again with another round of escalation in hostilities in August 2022. The recurrent exposure to conflict and violence, the fear for life, the loss of family members and the witnessing of wide-scale damage and destruction have only increased the level of trauma and other mental health consequences. According to the MSNA 2022, 40.0% of households reported at least one member of their household experiencing signs of psychosocial distress of trauma in the year prior to data collection.There has also be an increase in the perception of a lack of protection. Specific population groups, including women, children, and persons with disability are considered particularly at risk for experiencing protection threats, with 25.3% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns for children, 38.4% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns for children with a disability, and 15.7% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns for women. To mitigate existing protection risks and strengthen response to protection cases, UNRWA will increase its protection capacities through a series of multifaceted initiatives focusing on MHPSS, Child Protection, GBV and ERW risk reduction. GBV, Child Protection and MHPSS The constant threat of an escalation in violence significantly affects the mental health of all Gazans and leads to strong feelings of insecurity, anxiety and a lack of protection. Since May 2021, over 18,000 new cases requiring psychosocial support have been identified by UNRWA’s Relief and Social Services programme. UNRWA social workers have provided support and referral services to around 1,380 individuals, of which 640 are children. A recent MHPSS study conducted by UNRWA as well as this year’s MSNA highlight a deepening mental health crisis, with a majority of the population in Gaza experiencing various levels of trauma and other mental health symptoms. Children are the group mostly affected and a recent study conducted in UNRWA schools showed that at least 42% of new entry students show signs of trauma and require MHPSS intervention. Nine months later, 35 per cent of these children were still suffering from reactions to trauma, with some even resorting to suicide. To respond to the increasing psychosocial support needs of children, individual interventions as well as recreational group activities need to be scaled up.Women and girls in Gaza face multiple layers of violence and discrimination, both in the domestic and public sphere. Traditional gender norms and expectations that are deeply entrenched within the society are key factors accounting for the GBV risks that women and girls face. Contributing factors are high levels of unemployment and poverty, which are direct consequences of decades of economic blockade, as well as psychological trauma caused by recurrent hostilities. This situation leads to a behavioral dynamic of men being frustrated, unable to fulfil their expected role as breadwinners for their families, as their opportunities to provide and protect are extremely limited. Limitation of services and reluctance to report GBV due to fear of stigma, social exclusion, so-called honor killings or reprisal restrict survivors’ access to and utilization of critical GBV services. There is a need to both, scale up prevention efforts and improve the quantity and quality of services for women and girls at risk and survivors of GBV. ERW risk awarenessAs noted in the MSNA and figures provided by UNMAS, the recent escalation in Gaza has left severe contamination of explosive remnants of war (ERW), explosive ordnance and exacerbated an already volatile living space with ERW from past escalations. Since the beginning of the year, 12 people, including 7 children (4 women, 1 man, 1 girl, 6 boys) were injured by ERWs. Damage to basic infrastructure in Gaza is severely affecting the provision of electricity, piped water and essential services. Explosive ordnance which has failed to explode, and now lies buried in the rubble of damaged buildings or in farmland and poses an immediate risk to the population. In addition, the potential for renewed conflict remains high, giving rise to the need for further ERW Risk and Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) messaging, especially but not limited to children.

2 - Activities

In line with key needs highlighted in the MSNA, to mitigate and respond to targeted protection concerns and risk factors arising from escalation of hostilities and general protection crisis in Gaza, UNRWA will increase its protection capacities through multifaceted initiatives focusing on CP, GBV and EORE, to prevent and mitigate effects of conflict related violence: a) Child Protection and GBV response:All interventions will be based on IASC guidelines on CPMS and GBV as well as on UNRWAs internal Protection policies. Inclusive participation of women, men, girls and boys as partners, including the elderly and people with disabilities, in needs assessments, design and targeting of interventions will be ensured through engagement of community-based Youth Committees, Parent Councils and School Parliaments, Womens Programme Centres, Community Based Rehabilitation Centers and other Community Based Organizations. Where gaps exist, particularly in disability inclusion and elderly groups, UNRWA will engage the community to identify new potential local partners. Collaboration with local relevant NGOs will also be fostered to better address needs identified.Specific gender, age and disability targets/indicators to be further finessed after needs assessment completed. Geographic areas of focus to be determined based on MSNA analysis, current UNRWA data and planned needs assessment. Per MSNA, disability target is set at 21%. Through training of its staff and expanded coordination with Protection Cluster and sub-cluster members, UNRWA will expand its case management and referral system mechanisms across all programmes in general and schools and health centres in particular. Special attention will be given to child protection and GBV cases. In particular children experiencing high and critical protection risks such as child abuse and neglect, as well as children severely affected by conflict related violence and trauma, will receive targeted child protection interventions including, but not limited to structured PSS through individual case management and external referrals. b) Protection MainstreamingUNRWA will establish area based Protection Mainstreaming Working Groups across the Gaza Strip aimed at strengthening protection mainstreaming and monitoring throughout UNRWA’s humanitarian response and service delivery. This will build on areas of improvements identified in UNRWA’s Protection Alignment Review and ensure that service delivery is provided in a safe and dignified manner, available and accessible to all Palestine refugees and that beneficiaries are able to provide feedback and are actively consulted and involved in all decisions affecting them. Staff involved in the Area Protection Mainstreaming Working Groups will receive training and mentoring. Collaboration with local partners will also be strengthened to address identified gaps. c) Explosive Ordnance Risk EducationUNRWA will expand its Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) by training its emergency shelter staff and key school staff, strengthening its partnership with UNMAS to meet its objectives. School counsellors in the 50 schools identified as most at risk of ERWs will receive a Training of Trainers on EORE, which will be rolled out to all students in the concerned schools. d) Protection AdvocacyUNRWA is in the process of strengthening its protection monitoring system to increase evidence-based advocacy on issues affecting Palestine refugees. At least 25 advocacy initiatives to highlight and raise awareness on the protection context affecting Palestine refugees will be provided. e) PSEAUNRWA will increase its training capacity on PSEA by having additional certified PSEA trainers. This will help scale up training efforts for UNRWA staff to provide regular trainings on PSEA to its 13'000 staff. UNRWA will also continue to have awareness raising sessions with beneficiaries on PSEA and available reporting mechanisms. Furthermore, UNRWA will continue to closely engage with the PSEA Network and other relevant local partners. f) Monitoring and Evaluation UNRWA’s Monitoring and Evaluation Unit and implementing partners will be engaged to design a framework of monitoring evaluation, including regular in person visits to project locations by M&E and UNRWA Protection staff. UNRWA will employ surveys, focus group discussions and interviews with beneficiaries to monitor the progress and results of the activities. Feedback will be incorporated into the project cycle. Direct (in person) and indirect (press, social media, etc) outreach will be further undertaken from project design to review by project staff to disseminate the information on the above mechanisms.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

UNRWA is unique among United Nations agencies in directly providing essential services, mainly primary education, primary health care, relief and social services, infrastructure and camp improvement and microfinance. Of the two million Palestinians living in the besieged Gaza Strip, some 70 per cent are refugees, dependent on UNRWA for humanitarian assistance. Given its mandate of direct service delivery, the most effective way to reach beneficiaries and address the protection risks described is through direct service provision by staff such as Teachers, Counsellors, Social Workers, Nurses and Doctors.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Generally, in UNRWA's Protection Strategy the prioritization of the do no harm principle is explicitly stated as the key protection standard of safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. On a biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit includes extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. Specifically in relation to this project, UNRWA will collect disaggregated data (by gender or specific groups) as part of project monitoring to detect the effects of activities on inequality or exclusion in these groups; introduce specific DNH indicators into the project monitoring framework, depending on the project and its context; disseminate Do No Harm tools; invoke its well established process during its monitoring and documentation procedures of child protection and gbv cases of obtaining informed consent (and assent in the case of minors) for recording and use of information that affects them. UNRWA will employ an enhanced risk matrix prior to embarking on any individual response deemed high risk or critical. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. Finally, UNRWA developed guidelines for remote documentation and monitoring and has also established guidelines to prioritise field work in the context of COVID-19 risks.
