Project Summary

This project will support the resilience and safety of children through school embedded of safe, child-friendly and motivating spaces. These spaces shall serve as a venue for identifying vulnerable and at-risk children, and facilitating their referral for immediate response as well.
This project aims to prepare 5 child-friendly spaces (CFSs) in 5 schools in 5 villages which will benefit the children of 50 villages (10 villages’ children per Space) as each location will have other villages in its immediate area. Each CFS will contain areas for:
• different activities type of activities (Indoor and outdoor, creative, imaginative, physical, nonphysical)
• spaces for indoor activities such as Painting, drawing, clay, making dolls, puppets, and animals, pasting pictures, greeting cards from dried flowers, finger painting, posters, Dance, music, singing, role play acting.
• A place for facilitators.
• A place for playing, and other recreational activities
This project will contribute to the protection and well-being of children, and will provide multi- sectoral support for all children, the identification of vulnerable and at-risk children and facilitating their referral for immediate response.
The primary target beneficiaries of the CFS are all children affected by emergencies, particularly the most vulnerable children, to involve them in recreation games and other group activities.
The breakdown of the target children is the following:
1- Out-of-school children.
2- Children with disabilities.
3- Children living and working on the streets.
4- Child who underwent labor.
5- Children without appropriate care.
In addition, psychosocial support for the parents and other family members of the children in the CFS will be provided, so the family can be more involved in providing psychosocial support to their children inside of the CFS.

4 - Needs

According to recent data collected by HNO, 2.1 million children in Syria are out of school, 1.3 million children are at risk of dropping out, and 85% of assessed communities reported a high occurrence of child labor preventing school attendance, 20% of victims of explosive hazards accidents reported were children, 47% of children were hurt or killed while playing. It is estimated that over 2.6 million children remain displaced, and separation from caregivers is a reported child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria putting children at risk of exploitation, abuse or neglect. The disaster and its influence on children and families have powered harmful coping mechanisms such as child labor, early marriage and child recruitment, putting adolescent boys and girls at particular risk, while straining the capacities of families and communities to protect children. Child labor is reported to occur across all 14 governorates of Syria. Child labor interferes with children’s schooling by depriving them of the opportunity to attend school, obliging them to leave school prematurely, or requiring them to attempt to combine school attendance with excessively long and heavy work. The key needs identified in all locations that have undergone significant changes and needs are child-friendly spaces to hold those children and protect them from harm and to open a glance of chance for their improvement in life.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In line with SWEDO’s strategic priorities and objectives for SGBV prevention, mitigation and response across sectoral responses/thematic areas, SWEDO will pursue the following actions/interventions to improve the quality of programming by ensuring the most vulnerable beneficiaries access assistance that is appropriate and relevant to their needs and delivered in safe and dignified ways. The proposed actions/intervention will include among others:1- Promotion of SGBV risk integration and prevention across all sectoral response in accordance with UN AGD Policy, the IASC guideline for integrating SGBV into humanitarian action and protection mainstreaming principles. Preventing sexual and gender-based violence requires changes in gender relations and power dynamics within the community and families. UN’s Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) approach guides operations on how to contribute to gender equality, help eliminate gender-based discrimination, and empower women and girls. 2- SWEDO will systematically undertake regular assessments including participatory assessments with the aim to identify risks, needs and concerns of women, girls, men and boys and have these reflected in planning and programming processes for all sectors. SWEDO will mainstream SGBV and PSEA prevention throughout the assessment and distribution processes of core NFI and makes sure to prevent SGBV and SEA. This will be done through:• Training new staff members, contractors and their personnel, consultants and service providers.• Arranging refresher training at least once a year.• Requesting staff members to read and sign SWEDO’s Standards of Code of Conduct.• Ensuring there are posters in all office premises that remind staff members with SWEDO’s zero tolerance on SGBV, PSEA and corruption.• Giving handouts to beneficiaries so that they know their rights on how to approach the Complaint and Feedback hotlines to complain about any staff member who may admit any unacceptable act.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

At the startup of each project and soon after hiring the key personnel, SWEDO will arrange a risk mitigation workshop to further studying the risks and identifying mitigation measures. As a result, a risk mitigation matrix will be prepared determining the most challenging risks, their impact and responsible officials to take mitigation measures. The matrix will be updated on a monthly basis during project progress meetings. The project is expected to face the following risks, mitigation measures are also identified for each risk: RISK: protection activities may not cover all IDPs in the targeted locations, since the number of the IDPs is changing. MITIGATION: SWEDO will coordinate closely with the Protection cluster to cover the most vulnerable IDPs by arranging the protection activities with other NGOs to avoid duplication. RISK: Interference of local authorities and armed militias in beneficiary selections.MITIGATION: SWEDO will make sure that all involved beneficiaries and stakeholders are well briefed about the impartiality, integrity and non-political foundation of SWEDO and the good level of transparency it follows in serving its beneficiaries that will reject any interference or pressure (relevant policies and guidelines are applicable and in place). RISK: Project staff could be involved in corruption and making deals with supplies, beneficiaries and other stakeholders.MITIGATION: Training will be provided to prevent any improper act (SWEDO Code of Conduct and whistleblower policy). RISK: Project staff could be involved in sexual exploitation and abuse with beneficiaries and among the staff.MITIGATION: Training will be provided to prevent improper act. SWEDO applies Zero tolerance for Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.
