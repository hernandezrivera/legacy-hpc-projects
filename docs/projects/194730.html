Project Summary

Proposal accepted in 2022-2023 plan but unfunded, thus; Rescate is re-submitting the project due to farmers’ urgent need to retain their normalcy via food security & sustainable livelihood on their land. Most areas original population did not return to their homes yet due to lack of farming facilitating gear, mainly irrigation, hence they still live in other areas in pursuit of livelihood. This exposes women & girls to grave GBV violations& discrimination during work to secure food.
The optimal target of this project is to revive the food security chain via improved agricultural productivity & support sustainable agricultural & irrigation practices among the targeted 880 HHs farmers in the targeted project's areas 920 Hectares in Maskaneh where complementarity with the undergoing INGO partners’ project will be boosted if the missing part of Farm 6 is completed by Rescate.
Rescate team visited the two targeted sites Maskaneh & Hatla (DEZ) with MAAR & MWR to work to meet the peoples’ priorities because the areas are classified as 4th category in rain precipitation which increased land salinization. Hatla, Euphrates low level deems pumping an urgent prerequisite. Although Hatla 2 pumps are rehabilitated, yet not operational due to long electricity outages & stopped generators added to expensive repair costs, thus; result was nonfunctional pumps that did not serve the purpose. Studies gave weight to irrigation as a priority lifesaving need highlighting that agriculture & livestock are the major community source of living. Target is installing solar power system, cleaning the canals, maintain the suction pipes, install & maintain the suction valves at 6.5 Km.
In Maskaneh, intervention is to rehabilitate the main irrigation canals including damaged concrete covers, which cause irrigation water flow leakage leading to cavities due to the Gypseous soil. 1,400 LM rehabilitation includes Metal gates, Expansion joints, Clean concrete channels, Providing & installing concrete bases & neck, Rubber gaskets, Long & short siphons, and Water inlets.
With 2023 intervention plan, covering water flow & distribution of agricultural inputs in Halta for 2 seasons per the FAS kit composition including seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, equipment.
In Maskaneh, reviving agriculture as a major profession for the population in the remaining 920 hectares forming 42 farms/880 HHs 6160 direct & 595 indirect BENs within the 6th Farm will increase the intervention sustainability & will enhance the inhabitants protection & resilience with food security & livelihoods. 880 HHs will receive agricultural inputs wheat, fertilizer& pesticides 250 kg /ha for irrigated cultivation.
Securing sustainable irrigation & distribution of kits will secure immediate livelihoods to HHs to work on cultivating their lands for the next season. Training on both irrigation canals sustainability,15 days to returnee farmers on sustaining the new irrigation system & irrigation equity, investment of land & the pumping station & conduct timely maintenance cycle. Agriculture training on climate smart agriculture (CSA), compost making, natural resources management, & waste management. Coordinate with stakeholders to ensure Early Warning System multi-hazard, people-centered based on Knowledge of the risks faced; Prediction, technical monitoring &warning services; Dissemination of meaningful &timely warnings; Awareness &preparedness to act.
Creating jobs for women, the core farmers, will protect them from GBV & PSEA violations when they seize seeking income as workers on others' land. Ensure food security in these areas with outreach to cities will contribute to control food prices, hence; enrich food basket & with wheat seeds will sustain the bread production value chain as encouraged by MAAR & sectors. This project responds to the need of FSA boost resilience to help vulnerable smallholder crop farmers become more self-reliant thru protection & improvement of their livelihoods’ assets.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Activities might create some inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery or service types if one community is served and another is not. Confusion of the community has been identified in rural areas where no protection services exist, so population shall understand the purpose & goal of the activities, it might create a negative effect & prevent the project quality implementation. PSEA & GBV minors are some of the most vulnerable in the war in Syria, especially girls. Tensions linked to stressful situations may lead to domestic violence. 1) Rescate & Partner will ensure consultations with the targeted communities & neighboring ones; 2) create a mapping of services & involve the community in the activities’ design; 3) Undertake explanation to the community & encourage acceptance. 4) Rescate will monitor all population movement & community tensions to be aware of the situation in the field to design the program respecting “Do No Harm”; 5) PSEA & GBV risk, training is provided to staff & suppliers to ensure knowledge & commitment to the signed Code of Conduct; 6) field staff already communicate on regular basis & will continue to explain to the community about the services available & the activities implemented; 7) community focal points are consulted & can call Rescate team within the frame of the complaint mechanism in the course of the project.8) Lack official IDs, Rescate will accept 2 official identity dox duly ratified by any official Dpt.

As part of ongoing efforts to improve accountability, Rescate put in place a beneficiary Complaints system (CRM) in all projects for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints & give feedback on Rescate & suppliers’ work & to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issues. When needed, the HoM will scale the issue to Rescate HQ in Spain, where the Director, in consultation with the gender specialists’ team, will look for potential solutions and/or an appropriate way to proceed. Rescate team attend AAP trainings to ensure staff induction is well informed. Tensions linked to stressful situations may lead to domestic violence. 9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Security situation as well as economic situation remain stable; and areas accessible to Rescate team and contractors.- Required materials are available in the market especially for solar energy & Agri kits, - All approvals are granted for project implementation; - No delays on fund transfers in country, -No delays on procurement and service provisions.Risks:- The areas of intervention are no longer accessible, NCE to defer program until released - Rescate staff & contractors will commit to the safe access procedures as stated by UNDSS and other concerned partners in this context.- High inflation is impacting contractors’ ability in securing items needed for the rehabilitation works, installations of solar energy; and commodities for the kits.- Exchange rate fluctuation and inflation effect procurement procedures and contractors are not applying to tenders.Mitigation: down payment 30% to contractors to purchase materials at the onset of the project.- Shortages of fuel disrupts the project activitiesMitigation, store fuel for emergency. Delays in procurement approvals.Mitigation by dedicated follow up and support from ST.Conflict on the community level between the project’s BENs & those who did not benefit.Rescate conducts conflict sensitivity analysis prior project activities & will ensure inclusion of the community in the assessment phase through participatory approach involving different stakeholders to develop & clarify eligibility.
