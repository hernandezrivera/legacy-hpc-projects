Project Summary

The proposed project will adopt an integrated approach through intervention mix and it will target 500 poorly resourced food insecure farmers who are in Integrated Phase Classification 3 and 4 (IPC 3 and 4). It will minimize the risk of further food insecurity deterioration by improving access to basic crop and livestock inputs at the same time enhancing access, functionality and availability of critical community assets for crops and livestock production through infrastructure rehabilitation and construction. The project is also aimed to optimize the benefit of agricultural support through extension and advisory services to improve agricultural yields and manage crop pests and livestock diseases in Mwenezi district by December 2020.
Zimbabwe carries a cyclical burden of food insecurity exacerbated by economic instability and climate-induced shocks amongst others. About 7.7 million people in rural and urban areas are at risk of food insecurity in which Mwenezi district can not be exempted. The district lies within natural region v which receives annual average rainfall of less than 650mm, not sufficient enough to sustain crop production under dryland agriculture resulting in extremely low crop yields. The district was positioned on 14th position nationally in terms of food insecurity according to the Rural Livelihood Assessment conducted by Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) in 2019. About 68.6% of the population (121,639 individuals) were projected to be food insecure. The district was classified in crisis and emergent need of food assistance (IPC 3 and 4). The livestock situation in Mwenezi has also been deteriorating and the mortality rate of 6.1% for cattle reported by ZimVAC report of 2019 is alarming as it is far above the nation’s recommended rates between 3-5%. Also, the goat mortality rate of 17% for the province is higher than the country’s acceptable rates of 8-10%. The livestock situation in the district has worsened due to EliNino induced drought and as of November 2019, the district had the highest livestock poverty deaths of 4,016 cattle (59% of all deaths) in Masvingo Province according to Masvingo Provincial Livestock Report of November 2019.
It is against this background that there is need for adoption of climate smart agriculture technology and production of drought tolerant crops such (sorghum and millet), and diversification of livelihoods activities such as nutrition gardening and small livestock production. The proposed project will provide agriculture and livestock input support as well as rehabilitating productive assets consisting of water sources and dip tanks. Further, the project will provide livestock fodder to guarantee improved livestock feed for farmers in the coming seasons. The project will also offer livestock production and animal health trainings to targeted farmers through the departments of Crops and Livestock Production, and Veterinary Services.
Beneficiaries of the project in IPC 3 and 4 are to be targeted using a participatory community-based selection process that will be agreed between all the stakeholders and the community. Beneficiaries will be selected using the agreed community-based targeting selection criteria which will be guided by IPC 3 and 4 indicators, household access to land and water amongst others. Special considerations will be given to marginalised groups such as people living with disabilities, chronical illness, the elderly, widowed, orphans and child headed.
Christian Care have been present in Mwenezi district since 2004 implementing agriculture and livelihoods project funded by the Canadian Foodgrains Bank (CFGB). The organisation has a current running, “Conservation Farming and Livelihood Program” funded by Act for Peace and the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) both of Australia. The project has reached its 10th year (phase out year) since its inception in 2009.

1 - Activities

The project will conduct multiple activities in a holistic manner to maximise the benefits for the targeted farmers. As such a total of 3 water sources will be identified, rehabilitated and solarised for supplying water to 3 nutrition gardens that will also be established in the first 6 months. A sum of 150 farmers participating in nutrition gardens will be supported with garden seed suitable to the local context. This kit will comprise of 20g tomatoes, 20g carrots, 20g leaf vegetable rape and cabbage, 100g butternuts, 20 g cucumber, 20g onions and 500g beans or peas per farmer. Furthermore the project will provide 20 cutting livestock fodder packs per farmer for fodder production. The livestock restocking activity will provide 6 hens, a cock indigenous chickens and 50 kg poultry feed per farmer, 2 females goats per farmer and 1 buck in community at a ratio of 1buck per 13 females. The project will rehabilitate 2 diptanks and livestock handling facilities at dip tanks. For the component of field crops, the project will provide drought tolerant crop seeds in the form of 5kg maize drought tolerate variety, 5 kg sorghum and 5 kg millet per farmer for a 0.4-0.5 hectare piece of land. Complementary trainings will be conducted to enhance farmer knowledge and skills. Under these farmers will be trained on nutrition garden production; livestock fodder production, livestock production and animal health; climate smart agriculture, diseases and pest management; small grains value chain development, post-harvest handling, storage, processing and marketing. The project will take into account environmental issues during the implementation of all the activities. As such practices that may cause harm or damage to the environment such as the use of chemicals and pesticides will be discouraged. Planned activities will be implemented in coordination with line government departments, local authority, food security humanitarian organisations and local leadership. Government technical department will offer technical expertise in facilitation of project activities. The project will coordinate its activities with other programs in the district to minimise jublication of efforts and ensuring that the most vulnerable people are targeted. The organisation will attend all the meetings that will be invited through the Food Security Cluster.

2 - Indicators

The success of the project will be determined by measuring the number of households receiving season sensitive emergency crops and livestock inputs, critical community assets for crop and livestock production rehabilitated and built as well as the number of vulnerable small holder farmers receiving extension and advisory services to manage crop pests and livestock diseases.
