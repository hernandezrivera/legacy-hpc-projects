Project Summary

According FSNAU post Gu 2021 assessment the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and SAM both remained Serious. The situation then rapidly deteriorated, between December 2020 and October 2021, due to below average Deyr rains and emerging drought like conditions, which had been predicted would lead to La Nina between April and May 2021 (FAO, 2021; ECHO, 2021; FEWSNET, 2021). Of extreme concern are people in Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Phase 3 who are quickly slipping to phase 4 or Emergency levels of acute hunger and are already experiencing mortality and irreversible stripping of vital livelihood assets (FAO, 2021). The social economic impact of COVID 19, including reduced remittance, couple with drought like conditions largely contributed to the poor household food insecurity experienced. It is still predicted that more than half the population is expected to experience deterioration in their food security state from stressed (IPC Phase 2) to Crisis IPC Phase 3. This phenomena has rapidly unfolded in Elwak. For vulnerable children and women, hunger quickly translates to acute malnutrition placing them at a very high risk of mortality.
Besides these, the district also continues to have one of the worst infant and young child feeding and micro-nutrient indicators in the country (HARD 2019; IPC, 2020).
Critical water shortages and scarcity has also been reported in Elwak. This has fueled population movement, increase in prevalence of water related diseases and high water prices increasing economic pressure on households. Disease, as an immediate cause of acute malnutrition, in turn contributed to prevalence at the back drop of a poorly functioning health system. Access to health facilities is limited, most areas lacking vital health services and qualified personnel. Besides, the health system is still largely dependent on humanitarian funding (UNICEF, 2020; OCHA, 2020). These factors have largely contributed to sustained high prevalence of severe acute malnutrition in the area.
The proposed interventions will align with cluster 2021 HRP objectives, HRP strategic objectives and Government and strategies to providing lifesaving interventions and protect lives in the target districts, especially among the most vulnerable.. Interventions will focus on providing adequate nutritional support to bring up and avoid further deterioration of already existing situation. To achieve this and realize sustainability, HARD will provide both treatment and prevention interventions. HARD will work closely with government entities, both at local and federal level including MOH at all levels. The organization will also intensify community action in order to include affected population in interventions affecting them. Interventions will also integrate with those of WASH, Food security and protection. All nutrition interventions will integrate with health interventions and provided through health facilities.
Serious protection issues observed in these districts will be addressed working with the protection cluster and any protection risks likely to emanate from projects will be mitigated. Priority will be given to the disabled in all treatment sites. Staff will be trained to work with the disabled. Physically disabled who qualify will be employed. Community members will be encouraged to bring out disabled children and PLW to access services. Those will be followed up by CHW.
All data will be disaggregated by age, sex and disability status.
HARD developed a PSEA policy which will guide staff. All staff will be trained on PSEA. Access to services will be improved by use of mobile teams that will go to rural areas and hard to reach areas.
HARD Somalia intends to respond to the above intervention through scaling up with 2 static sites and 1 stabilization centers (SC) in Elwak district as well as two mobile/outreach team covering the affected rural location in Elwak district.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

HARD has gender policy that ensure that when hiring staff that women are given 50%consideration in different positions. In addition, the project target populations aresegregated into men, women, boys and girls hence giving emphasis to all gendergroups in the community. During the screening and admission of children into nutritionprograms, boys and girls will be given equal chances. Gender considerations will betaken into account throughout the assessment, planning and design of theinterventions and significant consideration will be given to pregnant women, mothers,and women of child-bearing age. In the provision of health and nutrition promotioncampaigns, messaging will be targeted for specific gender and age groups and asappropriate the events will be organized separately. Equitable and gender-sensitiveparticipation and decision-making approaches will be adopted throughout the project,including women’s participation in the community nutrition committees and access togender-specific health and nutrition information. Community consultations throughfocus-group discussions will take place during the project implementation, andstakeholders will be involved in decision-making processes to tailor the project to theneeds of different gender and age groups. Furthermore, data will be dis-aggregated byage and gender in all recording and reporting.
