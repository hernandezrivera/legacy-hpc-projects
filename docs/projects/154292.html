Project Summary

In Chad, the humanitarian situation is characterized by recurrent emergencies: flooding, droughts, diseases outbreaks, food insecurity, influx of refugees from neighbouring countries and a protracted conflict in the Lake Chad Basin area. Chad ranked 186 out of 189 on the 2018 Human Development Index. Poor governance, poverty, low status of women, insufficient funding of basic social services remain barriers in improving children’s and women’s lives and constrain the development of the country. The nutrition SMART suvey carried out in Agust 2018 revealed that acute malnutrition remains a public health concern in many regions. It was found that 12 regions namly (Kanem (25%), Ennedi Ouest (22.8%), Wadi Fira (20.3%), Borkou (19.4%), Bahr El Ghazel (18.7%), Hadjer Lamis (18%), Sila (17.9%), Ouaddai (17.8%), Batha (17.1%), Ennedi Est (17.7%), Guera (17.6%) and Salamat (15.9%) exceed the emergency threshold as defined by WHO. These regions bear more than 80% of the national burden. This situation is exacerbated by an influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan, Central African Republic and Nigeria. Based on the caseload estimates of 2019, it is expected that over 349,660 children will suffer from severe acute malnutrition and will need care within 750 health facilities. The nutrition cluster target is to treat over 300,440 children within the 684 supported health facilities. In order to respond to the needs of children under 5 , it will be essential to secure the Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) pipeline and essential medicines in order to provide the most needed treatment in the target regions. UNICEF’s intervention is lifesaving and will directly contribute towards the achievement of the objective 2 of the Sustainable Development Goals through the following outcomes: (i) Scaled up treatment of severe acute malnutrition in the most affected regions; (ii) Capacity building of frontline health staff on SAM management, and (iii) Promotion of improved infant and young child feeding (IYCF) knowledge and practice of caregivers in the targeted communities. On top of accessibility, the project will focus on gender balance and equity in service delivery.

Needs

Drought, floods and epidemics mostly affect the populations living in the Sahel belt. In addition, population movements due to political and military conflicts in neighboring countries continue to jeopardize the main sources of income in rural areas. This situation, compounded by chronic poverty and limited access to basic services, has exhausted the livelihoods of the most deprived communities and undermined household resilience in general. In Chad, 1 out of every 6 children die before 5 years old. According to the 2014 -2015 Multiple Indicators Cluster Survey (MICS), infant and child mortality rates are very high with 65 and 133 deaths %o live births respectively; and the maternal mortality rate is also unacceptably high at 860 deaths per 100,000 live births. The main direct causes of mortality in under-five children in Chad are preventable: malaria (20.4%), pneumonia (15.7%) and diarrhea (13.5%) are the top causes, while malnutrition, micronutrient deficiencies and poor child care such as sub-optimal infant feeding practices contribute to at least a third of these deaths. The nutritional situation remains worrying. The results of the SMART 2018 survey reveal a stagnation in the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) of 13.5% compared to 13.9% in 2017. The emergency threshold of 15% is exceeded in 12 of the 23 regions of Chad. These are Kanem (25.0%), Ennedi West (22.8%), Wadi Fira (20.3%), Borkou (19.4%) and Barh el Gazel (18%). 7%), Hadjer Lamis (18.0%), Sila (17.9%), Ouaddai (17.8%), Guera (17.6%), Batha (17.1%), Ennedi East (16.7%) and Salamat (15.9%).Severe acute malnutrition is also stationary at 4.0% in 2018 (3.9% in 2017), a rate above the emergency threshold of 2%. 13 out of 23 regions exceed the emergency threshold. These regions are Ennedi West (6.1%), Hadjer Lamis (5.5%), Sila (5.5%), Kanem (5.3%) and Ouaddai (5.1%), Borkou (4.8%), Wadi Fira (4.4%), Barh el Gazal (4.3%), Chari Baguirmi (4.3%), Batha (3.8%), Tibesti (3.7%) %), Guéra (3.2%) and Salamat (2.8%). The nutritional situation is also worrying in the Lac region with a 1.8% MAS rate close to 2% of the emergency threshold in a context of population movements and insecurity.despite MAS and MAG rates below the emergency thresholds, this city is considered to be in urgent need of views of its population density (9.75% of the national population).In total, 16 regions, mainly located in the Sahelian belt, are declared in a nutritional emergency: these are the Barh El Ghazel, Batha, Borkou, Chari Baguirmi, Ennedi East, Ennedi West, Guéra, Hadjer Lamis, Kanem, Lake, N'Djamena, Waddai, Salamat, Sila, Tibesti and Wadi fira.Chronic malnutrition remained steady at 31.9% compared to 32.4% in 2017. It exceeds the critical threshold of 40% set by WHO in 2 regions Lake (48.2%), Wadifira (41.2%). Some regions have rates close to the emergency threshold of 40%, namely Sila (39.6%), Mayo Kebbi West (39.1%), Ouaddaï (38.8%), Kanem (37.6%), Tandjile (36.8%) and Hadjer Lamis (35.9%).Based on all of the above data, it is estimated that over 293,849 children will be severely malnourished this year in the 16 regions. Taking into consideration the alarming level of food insecurity, the nutrition sector does not anticipate any improvement on the malnutrition rate or may even further deteriorate during the hunger gap season in the Sahel belt and in the refugee, IDP, and returnee settlement areas, possibly leading to higher mortality rates. There is therefore an urgent need to scale up life-saving nutrition interventions in order to address unmet needs and prevent associated mortality and morbidity.
