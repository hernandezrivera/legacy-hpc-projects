Project Summary

Norwegian Church Aid (NCA) proposes to implement nine months project to strengthen GBV referral and provision of psycho-social support services to respond to the Gender based Violence ( GBV) needs of the most vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities in Tonj North and Pibor counties, aid their recovery and build their resilience to shocks they face from crisis. The main objective of the project is to reduce risk of GBV and strengthen response to GBV services for the most vulnerable women, adolescent girls, child survivors, the elderly and persons with disabilities. The project targets a population of 10,000 people of whom 4,175 (2,456 women, 296 men, 240 boys, 1,183 girls) people are from Pibor, and 5,825 (3,545 women, 494 men, 340 boys, 1,446 girls) are from Tonj North all of whom are in critical need of GBV services. The project will also support 150 persons of disability from the target population. NCA is implementing people with disability project, and in this project GBV already has been identified as a protection risk for persons with disability. The project also targets 200 elderly persons with critical GBV needs.
NCA has GBV programmes across South Sudan with presence in Pibor( Gumuruk), Wau (Jur River), Kuajok and Warap, Torit and Juba. NCA supports Women and Girls friendly spaces with recreational psychosocial support activities and counselling to GBV survivors, referrals of survivors to service providers for life saving services. NCA has also integrated GBV in its WASH interventions by providing technical GBV training to water management and community protection committee structures who are charged with the role of carrying out GBV awareness in communities. The GBV has also integrated very well the GBV and peace messages awareness ‘’Tamar’’ campaigns with faith-based organizations and civil society organisations. In addition, NCA is a member of state and national GBV, NFI and WASH clusters where specific GBV needs and gaps have been identified.
The key outcomes include IDPs, returnees, host communities mostly women, adolescent girls, child survivors, elderly and persons with disability have improved access to existing services for GBV survivors through a secure and confidential referral and reporting system, increased community engagement in GBV prevention and response activities. The intervention aims to achieve these outcomes through provision of individual and group psycho-social support, raising awareness amongst rights holders on GBV, addressing stigma that GBV survivors are subjected to, capacity building of service providers on effective response to GBV survivors a as well as empowering and strengthening community based structures such as protection committees, women groups and school clubs in GBV prevention and response, empowering and building resilience to GBV survivors through life skills support services, strengthening safe, confidential and accessible referral systems for survivors of GBV. Through this intervention, NCA will identify and address barriers to reporting with survivors and how they feel service provision can be enhanced. NCA will use an inter-agency approach in the implementation of the project to ensure that comprehensive referral and case management services are accessible to survivors. NCA will liaise with WASH, Health and Livelihood and peacebuilding clusters to ensure that GBV survivors and vulnerable persons of concern have access to the services through referral pathways in Tonj North and Pibor. NCA will update the referral pathway to include services for persons with disability, child survivors and the elderly.

1 - Needs

The high prevalence of GBV remain a major threat in Tonj North and Pibor counties. A recent baseline survey conducted by NCA in 2020 show that cases of gender-based violence are widespread and this includes rape, sexual exploitation and abuse, physical violence, sexual assault, forced and early marriages, intimate partner violence, emotional abuse, as well as controlling women’s access to resources. According to the GBV cluster GBViMs information, approximately 97% of GBV reported cases affected women and girls. Further, NCA’s Gender rapid analysis conducted in 2019 in Warrap, Western Bar el Ghazal, Eastern Equatoria and central equatorial, show that sexual abuse and exploitation are widely reported, especially intimate partner violence. Out of every 10 women and girls, at least 6 had reported about being sexually or physically assaulted. Violence against women and girls is aggravated by famine, floods, and the recent Covid-19 crisis that have heightened women and girls risks to GBV especially sexual exploitation. The Covid-19 lock down measures have affected the local markets negatively, and reduced people’s access to social amenities including education and medical care. Women and girls particularly face high risks of maternal deaths due to lack of access to maternal care services. Adolescent school-going girls have been confined at home hence eventually fall victims of sexual exploitation and early pregnancies(REACH Humanitarian situation monitoring report, February 2021). In addition, the covid-19 measures of social distance, lockdown and curfew times have widened the gap of women and girls ‘access to basic services like water, food, markets and medical and maternal care services.All forms of GBV continue to be under reported due to stigma and customary laws which are strongly embedded in Tonj North and Pibor counties. The NCA survey further shows that 33 % of the respondents indicated that GBV cases usually go unreported while only 12 % reported that perpetrators of GBV are often taken to the police. Still only 0.5% of the cases (mostly rape) are taken to the hospital while 3% are taken to women safe centres. Cases taken to the faith leaders comprised 30% showing the level of trust the community has bestowed on the religious leaders. In terms of support to survivors, a majority of the respondents (82%) confirmed that survivors of GBV normally seek help from various institutions including the police, and GBV centres. The widespread GBV constitutes a significant impediment to women and girls participation in recovery and development initiatives in Tonj North and Pibor counties. The prevalent violence against women and girls in Tonj North and Pibor are due to the gender inequality exacerbated by displacements arising from recent floods and conflicts. Presently, there are information and knowledge gaps on GBV as communities lack adequate platforms to discuss and address GBV issues. In addition, women, and girls in Tonj North and Pibor walk long distances to access markets, services and fetching firewood and water which exposes them to further risks and threats such as attacks. The elderly and persons with disabilities are exposed to safety and health risks as they are often left behind during active conflicts and floods. Women, girls continually face high incidents of trauma coupled with very limited opportunities for social and economic opportunities. Furthermore, due to food insecurity and lack of livelihood opportunities, women and girls are vulnerable to negative coping strategies such as transactional sex and child labour. The family’s ability to provide sufficient clothes, shoes, menstrual hygiene products, and access to medications are also affected by their economic situation. Menstruation also undermine the school attendance of adolescent girls with the cost of menstrual hygiene becoming prohibitive as a result of the current economic crisis exacerbated by conflicts and Covid-19 pandemic.

2 - Project activities

Train 50 community leaders, age sets, religious leaders, women and men groups, law enforcement agencies, water management committees to address issues of GBV. The training will focus on Tamar and modalities of engaging men in accountable practices including identifying women, girls, men, and boys as champions for change (2) Train peer to peer groups in six schools in Tonj North and Pibor on the negative effects of GBV . The prevention activities on GBV shall target schoolboys and girls or girls from the communities in the event that schools are not operational due Covid-19. Prevention activities include awareness on GBV and sexual reproductive health rights. (3) Provide professional skills training on case management/PSS for 50 frontline NCA staff (counsellors) (4) Provide specialised individual and group psychosocial support and case management services to GBV survivors, women and girls, child survivors, men and boys of all ages including persons with disability. (5) Provide material and cash and voucher support to extremely vulnerable GBV survivors, women and girls of all ages; NCA also intends to engage its WASH emergency programme to train women and girls survivors of GBV, and other vulnerable groups in life skills training such as making covid-19 face masks and menstrual hygiene kits, which NCA has already been distributing so as to reproduce these materials locally to make income and meet their basic needs. (6) Distribute 1500 dignity kits to GBV survivors in Tonj North and Pibor. (7) Strengthen referral pathways for local community-based groups, grassroots women groups: NCA will work closely with the GBV subcluster at grassroot level to strengthen and establish safe, accessible, and confidential GBV referral systems by establishing standard operating procedures for GBV referral, including developing standard referral and case management tools for partners (8) Carry community awareness outreach on harmful practices of GBV (rape, early child marriage, assault, domestic violence, etc) for 2500 community members. The GBV prevention and response initiative will aim to reduce the vulnerability risk through protection activities through TAMAR campaigns and engaging men through accountable approaches. Adolescent girls and vulnerable women will receive menstrual hygiene kits. (9) Hold advocacy meetings with state line Ministry of Education and Ministry of Gender, child, and social welfare, payam and Boma administrators, traditional leaders to develop GBV polices and by-laws in schools and in the community. (10) Hold 3 radio talk shows on GBV prevention in Tonj North and Pibor. (11) Establish and support two Women and Girls Friendly Spaces in Tonj North and Gumuruk; NCA intends to strengthen provision of psychosocial support services in already established Women and Girls Safe Spaces being supported by other donors and agencies as well as establish 2 additional ones in the two locations. In addition, NCA will equip Women and girls safe spaces with WASH related services such as hygiene materials and Non Food Items (NFIs).

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

NCA has considered operational issues of access especially during wet season where costs of transportation of humanitarian materials becomes difficult due to bad roads. The challenges related to costs for transport as well as costs and risks associated with in kind donations and impact on local businesses have been considered. The the existing markets and their ability to supply goods from big towns e.g., Juba and Wau have been assessed. The significance of promoting local businesses, the importance of not disrupting the function of markets by introducing external goods which are already in markets have been analyse. In addition, NCA considered the need for sustainable access to GBV services as well as the need to build resilience of communities and markets and factors for safety, market supplies and costs. NCA also recognised that markets are disrupted by road access constraint but also considered that local businesses are more resilient and more adaptive to shocks. We considered the impact of the economic crisis and how humanitarian responses can also play a role in supporting markets for delivering goods.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Gumuruk in Pibor and Warrap in Tonj North have vibrant local markets where dignity kits for women and adolescent girls and elderly women can be sourced. NCA has conducted a preliminary cash and markets survey which has shown viability of using vouchers for targeted beneficiaries to receive GBV services through vouchers. There is a safe mechanism for payment of traders through the existing local Money transfer modalities within NCA which would enable NCA to transfer cash safely. Discussions with Women and Girls has shown that there are no barriers to receiving hygiene materials from shops and that cash vouchers are generally safe. The distances and routes for right holders’ access to markets are safe. Traders are able to guarantee a steady supply of goods based on market demand and have a price range that does not fluctuate beyond a certain threshold. Overall, NCA has established that using cash and vouchers will give right holders better value and choices.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

NCA provides information about PSEA in diversified methods to suit the audience of the information, including persons with disabilities and those with literacy inabilities. NCA produces IEC materials such as posters and billboards educating the audience on methods of protection from sexual abuse and exploitation. NCA also has a code of conduct against SEA which is available to all partners and key stakeholders. Women, men, girls, and boys are engaged in focused group discussions to create awareness about SEA. During counselling sessions, survivors of GBV are briefed about SEA and preventive measures to SEA. Staff will be made aware about their expected conduct while working at the community level. All NCA and partners staff will be trained in different polices on code of conduct and safeguarding children, CHS and humanitarian principles. Any breaches to the code of conduct will be addressed according to HR and employment terms. Community members and their leaders will be informed about their right to complain if any member of the staff or partner staffs does not uphold their values, breach any policies according to the expectations of the community and according to the employment guidelines and policies. Community members and structures within the communities will be provided with avenues for reporting any breaches and complaints of misconduct through a complaints reporting mechanism.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NCA has established diversified feedback and response mechanisms that are safe, confidential, culturally acceptable and accessible to all including persons with disabilities. NCA has established community protection committees, Water management committees, which the communities can use to complain about program quality and any sensitive feedback is channeled to our field safeguarding team and safely and confidentially escalated to the Juba safeguarding team for response. NCA often receives feedback from survivors during the counseling sessions and during monitoring visits by the M& E teams. NCA communicates the availability of these channels through visual aid such as posters, verbal announcements, and group discussion. sessions to capture diversified audience, including children, illiterate, and persons with disability. Messages are adopted to the local languages of the contexts,

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NCA has a Complaints Response mechanism at the country level which highlights communication modalities with communities. The CRM has contact details of focal points at project, country and HQ level where complaints are handled. There is a working email and telephone number for sensitive complaints. In areas where communication by telephone and email is not possible, NCA CRM focal points will consult with communities on best alternative channels for communication. Before projects are started or implemented, communities will be provided with information about the project, details about its scope, funding, objectives, beneficiaries including how the targeting of the beneficiaries of the project was derived. Implementation methodologies including entitlements and rights to complain will be discussed with the communities.GBV survivors always receive feedback during counselling sessions, follow up visits and at the center during the recreational activities sessions by engaging women, adolescent girls in conversations about how they feel about the services at the center, the quality of the dignity kits, availability and quality of referral services, the counseling sessions.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback from the beneficiaries will be used to improve the services for beneficiaries in service points such as safe spaces. The feedback about implementation modalities would require NCA to examine implementation approaches and modalities to align them to beneficiaries needs and priorities. Feedback on breaches of code of conduct will be handled on the basis of outcome of thorough investigations. Feedback on quality and quantity of services will be reviewed against standard procedures and guidelines including product specifications and planning documents such as budgets, log frames and proposals, needs assessment reports etc. In addition, NCA monitoring processes are undertaken monthly and quarterly while midterm reviews and valuation are undertaken annually. Complaints response and feedback mechanism is a component of NCA programme monitoring framework.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

NCA has a safe, confidential, and culturally appropriate and accessible reporting channels for staff and beneficiaries. Available options for reporting are clearly communicated to beneficiaries in diversified formats and languages including verbal communications, use of pictorials posters, banners, during focused group discussions, and individual counselling sessions. NCA has trained its staff on the Act Alliance code of conduct and child safeguarding policy and always encourages staff to do refresher on-line trainings monthly. NCA intends to hold refresher trainings for the existing implementing partners on Act alliance code of conduct and child safeguarding policies. has internal reporting channels that are safe and accessible for staff including, emails, the whistle blower sites with accessibility options for persons with disabilities. At the community level, NCA has IEC materials translated into the local languages. NCA has also carried out a conflict analysis of the area of our project implementation which gives us a very good understanding of the social , cultural, and political dimensions . This will help NCA to adopt conflict sensitive approaches such as do no harm policy in the execution of GBV and other programmes. In addition, NCA has a strong working relationship across all humanitarian clusters including government line ministries and GBV sub working group, child protection, general protection, Wash cluster, food security and livelihood, among others, at all levels, through participatory cluster meetings, reporting on GBViMs outcomes, joint cluster needs assessments. NCA participates in establishment and strengthening of the referral systems with its partners and always creates awareness to beneficiaries about the available referral services. Through safe, confidential, and accessible referral systems, NCA has been able to refer survivors of GBV and other vulnerable population groups to service providers for life-saving services like clinical management of rape and safe maternal care services. Periodically, the established feedback and response mechanisms are evaluated for effectiveness and efficiency. Beneficiaries are consulted during establishment of these channels for the best options that suit their contexts, continually NCA seeks beneficiaries ‘feedback about the effectiveness of these channels and necessary changes are adopted based on the gaps identified. NCA has trained its staff on data safety and security as well as confidentiality. A particular staff has been assigned to manage the GBViMs which is password-controlled. All survivor’s information that is in hard copy is kept in files and locked up in a cabinet. Survivor’s personal information is entered using codes with no disclosure of names. Additionally, all staff including implementing partners’ case workers and community mobilizers are trained on case management and GBV case management operating procedures, including the GBV guidelines for case management adopted from IASC and UNFPA GBV guidelines for case management tool kits. NCA is committed to maintaining zero tolerance to corruption and fraud through training and creating an awareness to its staff and implementing partners, contractors, affiliates on the anti-corruption and fraud policy and signing it. The staff are obliged to maintain this standard in their direct service to the communities according to the reporting systems in place for reporting corruption and fraud.
