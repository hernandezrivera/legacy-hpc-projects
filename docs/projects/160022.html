Project Summary

Based on results of assessments conducted Protection cluster there is the increase in internal displacement, as well as forced evictions, protection concerns are intensifying. Gender-based violence (GBV) is rampant, mostly affecting women and girls. Abductions and other grave violations against children, including recruitment and use of children by armed forces or armed groups, and cases of unaccompanied/separated children are all on the rise. According to the GBV sub-cluster, a total of 3,200 GBV cases were reported from September 2016 to March 2017 in the drought-affected region and actual numbers are estimated to be far higher. The protection crisis in Somalia continues to be characterized by a multi-faceted armed conflict and is compounded by the persistent drought, constrained effective governance, eroded resilience and economic vulnerability of families and communities, and new as well as protracted displacement often resulting in undignified living circumstances with severe protection implications for affected men, women, and children. Highly localized conflict dynamics, consisting of inter-communal rivalries and competition, including regarding access to land and political power, further compound the conflict, displacement, and overall protection situation. Acute protection needs in Somalia stem from acts of violence, exploitation, abuse, coercion, and deprivation, especially in situations of conflict and displacement, violating International Human Rights Law and International Humanitarian Law, including grave violations against children and GBV.
The crisis is aggravated due to impediments on access to services, low awareness of basic rights, and existing gender disThe organization has two stop centers and a well-equipped hospital in Luuq district. The stop centers are where SGBV is brought for aid and screening and records then transferred to USWRO’s main referral Hospital in Luuq district. USWRO has the experience needed for the success of this its main focus is to address gender inequality, eradication of traditional harmful practices against female gender especially young girls, maternal health care and advocating and lobbying for the empowerment of women, hence the organization has the capacity to implement this program and partner with protection cluster. Furthermore, the organization has created a sense of ownership in the communities’ development programs where the community’s ideas are incorporated and involved in all USWRO’s program right from planning, design and implementation and monitoring and evaluation stages. As USWRO identified issues to be addressed by this program, the organization drew from experience and lessons of on-going projects relating to women and young girls empowerment, education, child protection, governance, and health care services.
In addition, USWRO possesses a well-qualified and talented staffs that are specialized in the programs & financial management, sexual reproductive health, program management, GBV and gender equalities, peace promotion, monitoring & evaluation, and the programs will be integrated in the already existing USWRO structure and there will be a committed team that will be responsible for the successful implementation of the programs. From our community empowerment program, we have been implementing gender-related activities to reproductive health, empower women to take part in development activities and also decision making positions. USWRO has also promoted women’s access to household income through the promotion of income generating activities schemes. USWRO is currently also undertaking its gender assessment study that will see gender mainstreaming in all program interventions and operations. In addition, the experience of SRH rights has also been focused on safe motherhood targeting women and adolescent girls. This included safe contraceptive practices for family planning and general sexual reproductive health messages integrated into the HIV/AIDS program.

Project Activities

Support the training of 4,200 adolescents from Luuq district in the Gedo region on their right to women empowerment, gender, and sexual reproductive health services.  Support the mentoring of 1,620 adolescents from Luuq district in the Gedo region in advocacy and leadership skills to become advocates, leaders and change agents on girls’ gender equality, women empowerment, and sexual reproductive health. These adolescents will become peer educators who will go on to train other youth on their right to empowerment, gender and SRH services.  Support adolescent girls against harmful practices against adolescent girls including FGM/C Support adolescent girls’ visits to different offices for them to see and appreciate how female managers and others work, and to be inspired to work hard to empower to achieve this.  Support maternal health care services to young parents by bringing maternal health care closer to hard to reach and vulnerable minority community in rural areas Raising awareness among parents, teachers, community & traditional leaders and district government officials on girls’ participation and their role in addressing barriers to reproductive in their communities.  Support structured interface meetings/forums between adolescent girls on one hand and teachers, community leaders and government officials, on the other hand, to lobby for the integration of girls into the governance structures including development Committee (DCs) in their districts.  Mobilize women and adolescent girls to form groups that would be engaged in micro-finance interventions. Mitigation Measures

In general, the operating environment in terms of security and access has improved significantly over the last one year owing to the continued improvement of the security and establishment of local administrations. However, even with this, the organization anticipate experiencing some of the following constraints and propose to manage them as indicated:  Insecurity and constrained movement of staff: USWRO will make use of local staff that understand the local political, security and social dynamics and will continuously engage with stakeholders positively to create space to operate.  Insurgency hit and run attacks: USWRO enjoys community good will and employs local staff from local areas. While utilizing its security plan, USWRO will also actively engage and participate in coordination forums where information on security is shared.  Accessibility to beneficiaries: Some of the beneficiaries are in hard to reach regions some which have been cut off by floods. The organization hopes that the occurrence of floods will have gone down during the program implementation period and destroyed roads repaired.  Full Accountability & Transparency: the organization directly oversees the implementation of its programs, requiring full access to the communities it assists. USWRO is committed to a policy of transparency and disclosure by ensuring that key financial information is publicly available and that its programs undergo external impact evaluations and is committed to being fully accountable to project participants, communities, partners, supporters and donors for the work we undertake and for the resources entrusted to us  Traditional leaders’ resistance to change due to enshrined cultural beliefs; elders may be reluctant to include adolescent in decision making because of culture; Working with traditional leaders and other community leaders to raise awareness on the importance of involving adolescent in decision making and as agents of change
