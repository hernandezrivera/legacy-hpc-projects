Project Summary

In Ethiopia increased food insecurity across the country along with below-average crop production, and food price increases caused Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) admissions to stagnate at very high levels since February 2021. Between January to April, country-wide SAM admissions were 25 per cent higher compared to the same period last year, with all regions apart from Afar and Gambella reporting increases.
As per the nutrition cluster update report, Majority acute malnutrition cases are concentrated in the displaced population both in camps as well as those in the host communities in the conflict affected areas of Amhara Afar and Tigray these regions account to more than 25% of the total burden. Nutrition screening assessments conducted in Tigray, Afar and Amhara showed that proxy-wasting prevalence consistently was above 20 percent for children 6 to 59 months, and over 40 percent for PLW. For Tigray region, where the conflict started, acute malnutrition for 2021 was estimated to be ten-times higher than the previous year burden. Rural areas have also a significant burden of acute malnutrition these areas of Oromia, Somalia and SNNP are more prone to droughts and have limited access to adequate health and nutrition services. Within these locations’ children under the age of 5 yeas account for more than 70% of the nutrition people in need and women account over two third of the PIN for PLWs. Acute malnutrition among women is of significant concern in Northern Ethiopia, cases of adolescent pregnancies are rampart this is further complicated by the breakdown of the health system. The disabled, elderly, orphans and child headed household have also been identified to be vulnerable to malnutrition, they are more likely to have a physical disability, face barriers to accessing assistance and services, IDPs face an increased vulnerability to increased risk of disease outbreaks, epidemic due to poor access to prevention and treatment of common morbidity and childhood illnesses, and elevated malnutrition rates where children PLWs and elderly are particularly at risk, for instance the recent screening data on (Feb , 2022) shows among the total screened children of 715,605 under five 12,249 and 79,514 of them are SAM and MAM respectively, this showed the GAM rate of 13%, where as from the total screened of 19,301 PLW 10,704 of them are identified under MAM, this showed 55% of them are affected by malnutrition.
On-going armed conflict, intercommoned violence, climatic shocks, and disease outbreaks including COVID-19 continued to be the key drivers of humanitarian needs in Ethiopia during the first quarter of 2022. Conflicts have erupted, deteriorated, or escalated in various parts of the country, with severe impacts on civilians and particularly on vulnerable and marginalized group.
The project will cover all kebeles of the targeted woredas of Afar and Tigray regional state with enhancing the local health system including OTP and SC sites to deliver quality CMAM and IYCF-E service provision to conflict-affected communities. This project primarily focuses on providing life-saving Acute Malnutrition treatment, care, and support to affected and displaced under-five age girls and boys, and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) who are suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). The project will also strengthen further the local health system and build the capacity of health workers (HWs) and health extension workers (HEWs) to respond to already doubled primary health care and nutrition services in all targeted intervention woredas. Besides, the project strongly works with district health offices in maximizing Family MUAC, best practices of IYCF throughout the implementation period of this project. MCMDO will deploy Nutrition technical project staff in all of the targeted woredas in order to tackle the above mentioned activity.

1. - Projects activities

The major activities of the project are; Restore and expand capacity for life-saving nutrition interventions through health facilities and outreach activities for children under five suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) either through the MHNT in communities with high influx of IDPs (including host communities) and accommodation centres.  Treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) of children under five, both from IDPs and host communities living in the affected districts;  Logistic support and transportation for nutrition supplies for therapeutic feeding including, RUTF and therapeutic milks (F75 and F100) and other supplements and nutrition equipment from Regional/ Zonal to the target district.  Support the supply chain logistics for nutrition;  Improve the quality of the24 hours inpatient treatment and care in stabilization centre through on the job training and mentoring with MCMDO technical staff Strengthen the HEW using the MCMDO technical staff on the management of acute malnutrition at community level Provide basic OTP and IYCF-E training for health extension worker in the targeted woredas Provide basic 7 days long IMAM training for district health professionals  Implement Active case finding through MUAC screening for detection, referral, and follow-up, and also monitoring of the nutrition situation;  Undertake nutrition assessments/ surveys for assessing the nutritional status of the populations at risk, and for Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) acute food insecurity and nutrition analysis; Promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices, and overall Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) best practices messages for caregivers of children under two either though the awareness sessions and cooking demonstration in the communities with high influx of IDPs (including host families) or through IYCF Corners that will be associated with the health fix posts at the accommodation centres. Support any campaign with the right procedure thorough Technical and logistic manner.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

To achieve a coordinated response, OCHA continued to bring humanitarian actors together to ensure coordinated, coherent multi-sectorial emergency responses. As part and parcel of such responses, MCMDO will closely work and provide all the necessary information to the Health cluster coordinators at national and regional levels so to be used during the inter-cluster coordination meetings. UNICEF, WFP, WHO, UNFPA will provide different kits, medicines and medical supplies (EHF funded project) and MCMDO deployes qualified nutrition experts to implement the community level activities. UNICEF in coordination with RHB will support standard training of the CMAM teams on SAM, MAM and IYCF. WFP and Nutrition cluster will complement the project by the provision of the nutritional inputs for the treatment of malnutrition while the MCMDO will ensure all information on health and nutrition is shared with the relevant authorities and clusters. The MCMDO supported MHNTs/surge support in overallping woredas will be coordinated to ensure that affected communities within the woredas are covered and complements the interventions for SAM facility-based care through timely referral. MCMDO uses similar protocols, modality, and coordination mechanisms to ensure that relevant data are shared with the cluster, donor community, and other reports, like Zonal as well as Woredas, Nutrition cluster, DRMO, and Plan and economic development office. MCMDO will also coordinate with other NGOs implementing different activities in the targeted woredas.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Beneficiary accountability is one of MCMDO quality standards reflected as a cross-cutting issue throughout the project cycle. MCMDO has engaged the targeted beneficiaries and local actors in all stages of the project. In this response, there is a witnessed challenge to conduct a detailed assessment and engage the affected communities due to the challenges related to access and blackouts. However, MCMDO in collaboration with UN OCHA, Health and nutrition clusters had brainstormed to prioritize the key emergency nutrition activities compromised due to the war crisis. All CMAM team members will be trained or refreshed on the use of a participatory approach especially on how to receive feedback from the communities and specifically the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) on quality and accountability standards keeping the dignity of the beneficiaries. Apart from this approach Beneficiaries will be involved in monitoring the project. They will also continue being part of the community-based structures/committees that contribute to matters that affect them such as the days/timings/locations for community-based events, malnourished therapeutic service, and capacity building. Likewise, in the field sites, beneficiary accountability is reinforced getting quality health is one of their rights is a basic need, selection of beneficiaries is discussed with beneficiaries in community meetings for massive and comprehensives community awareness sessions so that it is clear that our services are based and we treat patients on a needs only basis and lifesaving. MCMDO believes that community participation is very crucial and helpful for the success of the project. Otherwise, during implementation, community members are involved as Health and nutrition volunteers for household visits during which they gather feedback and data to report back to project management staff. Finally, survey reports are shared with community leaders, and a meeting to present the results will be arranged with the local authorities. MCMDO will ensure the DNH principle that requires to endeavor not to cause further damage and suffering as a result of its actions.
