Project Summary

COVID-19 in Syria added to the complex emergency, further weakened the fragile health system and increased population vulnerabilitySyria declared the first COVID-19 case on March 22, 20, and at the same time establish COVID-19 Task Force aiming to carry out activities under identified pillars in collaboration with implementing partners. In NWS, the current health system is fragmented and has very limited capacity across the defined pillars of the COVID-19 response: surveillance, laboratory testing, contact tracing, human resources for health and case management and measures at points of entry (PoEs), etc.
A total of 200 776 confirmed cases of COVID-19 were reported in Syria between 22 March 2020 and 31 October 2022, of which 104 160 cases (51.88%) were reported from northwest Syria (NWS), 57 358 cases (28.57%) from government-controlled areas (GoS), and the remaining 39 258 cases (19.55%) were from northeast Syria (NES).
During the same period, a total of 7 246 related deaths were reported, of which 3 163 (43.65%) were from GoS, 2 505 (34.57%) were from NWS, and 1 578 (21.78%) were from NES. The overall Case Fatality Rate (CFR) is 3.6% (no change in the CFR compared with the previous month). The CFR was highest in GoS (5.5%), NES (4.0%) and in NWS the CFR was 2.4%.
The overall aim of the project is to reduce morbidity and mortality resulting from COVID-19 among the population of Syria by scaling up the country’s preparedness and response operations, reducing transmission of COVID-19 and protecting all persons within Syria from the disease, and strengthening health systems to attain resilience and respond to emerging and re-emerging public health threats. The project aligns with the Health sector strategy and WHO global strategy and adopts the 11 pillars approach. The main objectives are:
• Strengthen existing national multisectoral coordination mechanism to deliver timely and integrated response and to contain the pandemic, by actively engaging Directorates of Health, line-ministries, UN agencies, INGOs and NNGOs.
• Enhancing Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities aiming to ensure people’s understanding of the situation and their compliance to social and public health measures.
• Strengthen surveillance for early detection and response of COVID-19 infections to reduce person-to-person transmission.
• Strengthen the public health laboratory network central and directorate-level public health laboratories for virologic testing of COVID-19 samples and sequencing of SARS-CoV-2.
• Strengthen and sustain infection prevention and control (IPC) measures to ensure safety of patients, healthcare workers and the caregiving environment.
• Establish and maintain designated isolation facilities across the country.
• Maintain stockpile of supplies required for the response.
• The continuity of essential health services as to avoid substantial disruptions is secured.
• Support continued COVID-19 vaccine administration and expanded coverage efforts across all 14 governorates using hospitals, PHC and mobile teams.
For COVID-19 vaccination, kindly note:
-Across Syria, in rural areas and camps 341 mobile teams will be deployed to cover that targeted population. Services will be provided by trained hospital teams and routine immunization personnel in mobile teams.
In NWS, in the absence of the MoH, WHO in partnership with the Syrian immunization group (SIG) and partners will support COVID-19 vaccination services, inclusive of independent qualitative and quantitative assessment and monitoring of performance, coverage, and quality of campaign-related activities for all targeted communities. The project will expand vaccination sites and fully support the human resources, operation costs including logistics, transportation and maintaining technical support, supervision, training at the governorate, district, and vaccination site level. This project will support & sustain the operation cost of 200 C-19 vaccination sites

3 - Needs

The social and economic impact of the crisis and the COVID pandemic is large and growing. A lack of sustained access to health care has exacerbated the effects of the conflict and pushed millions of people into unemployment and poverty. With a severely degraded healthcare system, Syrians remain extremely vulnerable to additional shocks, such as the COVID19 outbreak still unfolding. In addition, the introduction of sanctions has further restrained Syria’s external economic ties, leading to difficulties in procurement of lab supplies and medicines, fuel shortages for the operation of RRTs, and a rapid depreciation in local currency. MoH struggles to keep up with the spread of the epidemic and to significantly scale up the surveillance and testing capacity. Protection of healthcare workers and frontlines is still crucial. In NW Syria there is a high risk of transmission. In a scenario in which sustained community transmission has been detected, surveillance system will need to be expanded to include monitoring the geographical spread of the virus, transmission intensity, disease trends, characterization of virologic features, and the assessment of impacts on healthcare services. COVID-19 community transmission will continue in 2022, driven, in part, by low vaccination coverage, low adherence to public health and social measures, and testing capacity which remains inadequate. The COVID-19 pandemic has been accompanied by widely circulating misinformation and rumors.

4 - Implementation of activities

While strengthening the response activities carried out in the acute phase of the pandemic, it is important to meet longer-term needs. In parallel with this, more permanent, medium- and long-term solutions should be produced on top of the successes in initial capacity building process in Syria in humanitarian crisis. In Country preparedness and response plan for 2022, main activities that will support COVID-19 preparedness and response under the following domains: • Leadership, coordination, planning, and monitoring through facilitating COVID 19 taskforce committees at different levels. • Risk communication and community engagement• Surveillance: support operation cost of RRT for sample collect and field visits, support MoH/NGOs and community to establish points of care/tests through the provision of RDTs, case investigation and contact tracing. • Travel, trade, points of entry (PoE), and referral system.• Diagnostics and testing: procurement of COVID-19 lab supplies, testing kits, PCR machines, GeneX machine, capacity building on sequencing, and rehabilitation of 3 labs in rural areas. • Infection prevention and control• Case management and therapeutics: provision of life-saving medicines, enhance oxygen supplies, ICU equipment. •Operational support and logistics• Essential health systems and services• Research, Innovation, and evidence

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Hostilities in Syria continue to take a heavy toll on the lives and livelihoods of civilians who have already endured a decade of violence, multiple displacements, and a major economic downturn. The COVID-19 pandemic has only worsened the situation, adding new layers of complexity and hardship. Therefore, the availability, access and sustainability of essential health services and medical supplies was and continues to be always in risk due to the security situation, internal displacement, and donors’ support. According to the HNO 2020, Attacks on health care have left 14.5 million people in 42 districts below emergency standards for hospital beds. Additionally, up to70% of health workers have left Syria and many others have been injured or killed. Lack of health personnel is particularly acute in areas affected by recent hostilities and displacement, with 64% (13.2 M) of the population living below minimum standards for healthcare workers. Overcrowding and long waiting times are top access barriers reported by patients. Regarding the health system risks the recent assessments indicate the need for referral capacity, including ambulances to overcome gaps in secondary care and connect patients to life-saving care, with this regard WHO regularly conducts Prioritization Exercise in collaboration with health cluster partners aiming to identify needs, target population, accessibility, availability of services, monthly caseload and overlapping with other facilities.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Low vaccine acceptance might be the main risk beyond project capacity. WHO and UNICEF continuously adapt communication strategy for demand generation. Cross-border operations in NWS depend heavily on the current UNSCR2504 which authorizes cross-border access to northwest Syria through Turkey until January 2023. WHO is engaged in contingency planning to enable business continuity in the event of non-renewal. • Frequent population movement and new displacement due to ongoing clashes are mitigated by contingency planning and adaptation. Change in severity and/or infectiousness of COVID-19 may require revision of overall strategy
