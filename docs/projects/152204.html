Needs

3.4 million people need support to access safe water and basic sanitation facilities across the BAY states. The main drivers of vulnerabilities related to WASH are closely linked to the conflict, including recurrent, unpredictable and continuous population displacements. They are also a result of pre-existing low levels of WASH services.Ongoing and protracted displacement creates continued need for WASH and puts an additional stress on existing WASH services and facilities, especially in 43 LGAs across Borno and Adamawa states. With congested populations in displacement sites, especially in 15 LGAs and wards in Borno State, water-borne diseases spread rapidly.Spiraling cholera outbreaks have taken hold each year. In 2018, the cholera outbreak has affected 18 LGAs with a total of 4,250 cases . In addition, 1.5 million people in the affected LGAs , including IDPs and host communities, are considered at risk and require cholera prevention interventions. Flooding, fierce winds and sand storms have resulted into substantial damages of WASH infrastructures. In 2018, at least 20 per cent and 28 per cent of IDPs living in camps in Borno and Adamawa states respectively have been seriously affected as a sizeable number of latrines, showers, handwashing points, and solar panels got partially or totally destroyed, affecting access to WASH facilities and services.New arrivals from hard-to-reach areas, especially women and children, are particularly at risk and need urgent WASH support. They include at least 100,000 children under five and 30,000 pregnant and lactating women across 9 LGAs in Borno State and 2 LGAs in Yobe State.
