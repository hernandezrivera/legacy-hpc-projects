Project Summary

The proposed project’s objective is to enable conflict-affected populations to meet their basic needs and provide vulnerable individuals with support when they experience harm, while promoting a transition towards early recovery and resilience-strengthening in the medium term with support to livelihoods restoration (cash support for agricultural inputs; cash support + entrepreneurship training for non-agricultural livelihoods). This is the year 3 of a project which started in 2018 and its strategy is aligned with the signature outcomes of the IRC Strategic Action Plan.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The crisis in the Far North (FN), which is currently experiencing an upsurge in violence and attacks, is characterized by risks of violence and violations of the fundamental rights of the population as well as limited access to basic services, including for health services for survivors of GBV, safe access to water and economic means to meet households’ basic needs. Since January 2020, the increase in acts of violence such as repeated attacks and incursions against communities, particularly in the departments of Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga (between August and September, 82% of the incidents recorded by INSO concerned these two departments and this trend can be extended the entire 2020 year), are causing frequent displacement of populations and a considerable increase in protection risks and needs. In June 2020, a total of 321,886 displaced persons were registered in the FN, or 24,506 additional people since December 2019 (+8%) following multiple attacks by organized armed groups (OAG). During these attacks, which are the cause of 89% of displacements, OAGs commit grave violations against civilian populations, with the most vulnerable people left further exposed to risks. Between March and August 2020, IRC received nearly 23 alerts of security incidents committed by armed groups or men engaged in acts of looting, sexual violence, murder and other violations and affronts to human dignity. In August 2020, IRC protection teams observed a doubling of the number of cases of unlawful killings and illegal taxation compared to the number of cases reported for the entire previous quarter. In addition, between April and June 2020, 500 cases of gender-based violence (GBV - 99% of cases being women and girls) were recorded by UNFPA in the Far North, including 333 cases reported by IRC. The multiplication of attacks coupled with the multiple displacements in intervention areas puts populations at risk of looting, theft, killings, and physical violence creating urgent medical, social, and material assistance needs. Displaced persons are also at increased risk of water born and hygiene-related diseases as they do not have access to water, sanitation, and hygiene infrastructure due to an already difficult situation in the Far North regarding access to water. Access to water is the priority need for the majority of the displaced population, with 44% of IDPs noting as their biggest need (IOM DTM 21) in the Far North. There is a high percentage of non-functional water points in Logone-et-Chari (27%), Mayo-Sava (35%), and Mayo-Tsanaga (33%), while over 88% of displaced in Mayo-Sava having to travel over 45 mins to access water, exposing them to protection risks along the way (IOM DTM 21). Furthermore, displaced populations who have forced to flee are in need of immediate NFI items to improve their living conditions and meet hygiene needs. The COVID-19 epidemic also had important impact on households’ capacities to meet their basic needs and generate income; according to an IRC evaluation led in the Far North in June 2020, 85% reported that capacity of covering basic needs has been decreased as a result of COVID 19. According to the September 2020 fewsnet report, the cumulative effect of widespread flooding and ongoing price increases in the Far North is further limiting access to food for poor households already made vulnerable by insecurity and the COVID-19 pandemic and exposing them to Stress food insecurity (IPC Phase 2).In order to respond to the most urgent needs, the IRC Cameroon programme will prioritize lifesaving and life-sustaining assistance to under-served, crisis-affected areas, including hard-to-reach areas in the Far North in three most affected departments – Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo-Tsanaga, but will prioritize its response according to changing needs and zones with populations that are most impacted by armed group incursions and violence targeting civilian populations and, so, will remain flexible to shift its programm
