Project Summary

Ongoing political instability, conflict and insecurity in Libya, has resulted into a protracted crisis, leaving 893,000 people in need of assistance. As a cross cutting issue in a protracted crisis providing Accountability to affected populations has been a challenge with many members of the affected community unable to find information and provide feedback about humanitarian services. A common feedback mechanism has been established which has four main objectives:
i. Access to Information
o Enabling affected populations to easily access vital information related to humanitarian aid;
ii. Feedback Mechanism
o Enabling two-way communication between the affected population and the humanitarian community; including receiving and referring issues of sexual exploitation and abuse
iii. Trends Analysis
o Providing humanitarians with insight into communities’ key concerns, needs and challenges based on region, gender and age to help humanitarians take better informed programming decisions;
o Informing future community engagement program design and interventions;
o Supporting the provision of trends analysis to key government counterparts through the sectors and sub-sector leads when appropriate.
iv. Accountability and Efficiency
o Facilitating collective accountability to affected populations and consolidating limited resources that are currently only available to humanitarian organizations individually while putting the affected populations at the centre of humanitarian response.

1 - Needs

While a number of humanitarian organizations responding to the crisis in Libya already have their own individual hotlines aimed at enabling affected populations to access information, affected populations are often not aware of the contact numbers of all United Nations (UN) Agencies, international and local Non-Governmental Organizations (NGO)s. 54% of respondents to an Emergency Telecommunications Sector (ETS) survey stated that they did not have sufficient access to information on the humanitarian response.In emergencies where a Cluster Approach or Sector is activated, the Inter-Agency Standing Committee’s (IASC) best practice recommends using a single phone number as an entry point for queries from affected populations, and a coordinated mechanism to later channel these queries to individual mandated organizations. This single number enables members of the affected population to have easier access to information about the response and assistance available to them, speeds up the resolution of issues and allows greater access for affected population to decision makers within NGOs, UN and the government, thereby improving aid delivery efficiency and accountability to affected populations.

2 - Activities

• Provide affected communities with timely and accurate information and referrals;• Identify trends in information requests and information provision from affected communities;• Allow analysis of common themes of concerns• Allow data to be recorded in a coherent manner, and all queries recorded and analysed by a single set of standards for overall tracking of sectoral trends;• Enable dissemination of common messaging;• Ensure complaint/feedback/queries arrive at the appropriate agency when a beneficiary cannot or does not distinguish between agencies;• Reinforce the all-important perception of neutrality and objectivity of centralized complaint mechanism;• Minimize resources among individual agencies to set up and operationalize the whole mechanism;• Enable data to be recorded in a coherent manner, and all queries recorded and analysed by a single set of standards as opposed to agencies applying discrete criteria case recording.

4 - Protection risk analysis

Data Privacy Protection Risks. While aiming to help affected populations through a CFM call centre, ETS acknowledges that indiscriminate disclosure of personal data can potentially expose the affected population to violence, abuse and discrimination. In addition, unauthorized third-party access to such personal data carries an equally high risk of putting the affected populations in danger. Access to affected vulnerable populations. Insecurity, discriminatory gender norms and bureaucratic challenges may impede humanitarian access into and within Libya, preventing ETS from reaching the affected population. To mitigate this risk, ETS will continue to work closely with humanitarian partners and the donor community in order to advocate for unrestricted humanitarian access.

5 - Mitigation measures

Data protection risks. To mitigate the risk, the ETS team conducted a Data Protection Impact Assessment (PIA) prior to commencing the project with the goal of identifying, evaluating and addressing the risks arising from the processing of personal data related to this project.
