Project Summary

OCHA will in 2020 continue to support the Humanitarian Coordinator (HC) and the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) in coordinating the humanitarian response to meet the (protection) needs of the most vulnerable people affected by the conflict / crisis in Libya. Through Inter-Sector and Area-Based coordination, OCHA will provide mechanisms and support to ensure that the interventions of humanitarian organisations are well coordinated and based on robust and timely evidence. OCHA will further facilitate and advocate for humanitarian access, the respect of humanitarian principles and ensure that the protection of people in need is effective.

1 - Needs

The territorial gains made by LNA in 2019, particularly in Libya’s south and latest with the outbreak of the armed conflict in and around Tripoli, have made the humanitarian situation more fragile and unpredictable. The armed conflict in and around Tripoli has compounded the vulnerabilities and humanitarian needs already resulting from seven years of instability and insecurity.While the overall impacts of the crisis on people in all affected groups have been severe, there are varied needs according to the varied contexts of different populations in Libya. Refugees and migrants face specific protection issues, including exposure to grave human rights violations as a result of their irregular status, targeting by criminal gangs, and exploitation and abuse linked to the control of mixed migration flows to Europe.Parties to the conflict have failed to consistently observe their obligations under international humanitarian law to refrain from indiscriminate attacks and to take constant care to spare civilians and critical civilian infrastructure, including electricity and water distribution networks. In an extremely worrying trend, health personnel and facilities have been repeatedly impacted by the armed conflict.Currently, there are no signs of improvement in the humanitarian situation in Libya and neither in the security situation, protection nor public services. On the contrary, the humanitarian situation is expected to further deteriorate as the conflict becomes entrenched, causing further constraints on humanitarian access and posing further challenges for humanitarian actors looking to deliver conflict-sensitive assistance. As the situation is likely to deteriorate, Libya will continue to remain of concern and require OCHA’s presence to provide a coordinated support to the humanitarian community. The military offensive launched by the LNA against Tripoli on 4 April 2019 led to a severe deterioration in the security situation. As a result, a decision was taken by the UN leadership to temporarily reduce the international staffing level according to programme criticality. Nine months later, the situation has not improved; on the contrary, the analysis is that instability and insecurity will prevail for a protracted period that likely will last through 2020 and maybe even longer. In this context, OCHA Libya’s cost and work plan for 2020 aims at ensuring that the changed set-up of OCHA’s country office in Libya is fit for purpose despite the significant reduction of international staff inside Libya, and yet in line with the current and anticipated deterioration of the humanitarian context and meeting expectations of HCT partners for effective coordination support. In the very unpredictable and volatile environment, the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) currently operates with three scenarios; 1) prolonged military stalemate, 2) general confrontation / civil war and 3) cease fire / negotiated change of control. While the 2nd scenario is considered a worst case with massive anticipated displacement, protection and increased access concerns, each of the two others are also anticipated to generate humanitarian and protection needs, which the humanitarian community would need to prepare itself for. In a protracted situation, the existing vulnerabilities will be exacerbated, making it more difficult for people to cope and sustain themselves, and a humanitarian crisis could unfold. Women and children and vulnerable groups are particularly at risk.

2 - Activities

OCHA Libya will in 2020 continue to be guided by the recommendations of the IASC peer-to-peer mission to Tunis and Libya in September 2017, which identified the strong need for increased coordination support and capacity, and those of the subsequent high-level directors’ mission in October 2018. However, taking the changed operational context into account, the humanitarian operation and, consequently, OCHA’s coordination support, has to adjust to these ‘new normal’ conditions for the foreseeable future. Through the leadership provided by OCHA, common approaches will be developed under the auspices of HCT in order to underpin effectiveness in humanitarian response. Activities in 2020 will include: Support to the Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC), including. on a strengthening on the monitoring and evaluation part. Discussion are currently ongoing as to prepare for a multi-year planning framework starting from 2021 with an action plan to be incorporated into 2020 HRP.  Humanitarian Access in line with the humanitarian principles that reinforces recent progress in establishing access mechanisms with the LNA and avoids individual ‘quick fix’ approaches that may jeopardize these mechanisms;  A prioritized approach to Fundraising for humanitarian operations, in line with the prioritization set out in the HRP (or any alternative humanitarian planning framework). Also, efforts to establish a pooled funding mechanism will continue to remain a priority to better support in particular national NGO partners in responding to needs in ‘hard to reach’ areas;  “Speaking with one voice” and “telling the story” on critical humanitarian issues remains important for the HCT to amplify the Advocacy efforts on behalf of the most vulnerable people affected by the conflict / crisis. Better data collection and analysis is imperative in this respect.  Effective and timely Preparedness / Contingency Planning is imperative in the volatile and unpredictable context of Libya. 4 - Protection risk analysis

 A deteriorating security situation causing further constraints on humanitarian access An increased threat against UN personnel operating inside Libya leading to further reduction of staff or in worst case evacuation of all staff Lack of political and financial support to address humanitarian challenges in Libya undermines OCHA’s position and reduces its ability to facilitate effective humanitarian response Increased politicisation of humanitarian needs and priorities as a result of power shift which can change the political conditions for humanitarian action, incl. collapse in public sector affecting delivery of public services Internal disagreement within the humanitarian community can lead to fragmentation and inefficiency in the overall response

5 - Mitigation measures

 Development and implementation of an Access strategy. Revise Programme Criticality in Libya; engage with UNDSS for better analysis and dissemination of security information relevant to access. Continue negotiations and advocacy with all concerned parties. Maintain the support office in Tunis to function as backup base for remote coordination of operation. Maintain and ensure that a flexible and complementary staffing system is in place to avoid critical gaps and shortcoming in coordination support. Proactive engagement with the international diplomatic community and Government of Libya to raise awareness of humanitarian needs and to mobilize the necessary resources and ensure their IHL/HRuF responsibilities. Ensure accurate analysis of needs and gaps is produced to underpin advocacy efforts. Ensure proper contingency planning is in place to enable a swift response by the humanitarian community to changes in the context and needs of affected people. Increase networking / relations with rapidly changing political counterparts with support of UNSMIL. Ensure proper consultation and full transparency in planning and decision-making through HCT and ISCG and bilateral relations with partners. Ensure proper leadership support to HC/RC for quick resolve.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is a commitment by humanitarian actors to be accountable to the people we seek to assist. In 2020, this includes the establishment of a Common Feedback Mechanism (CFM), to improve transparency with affected communities and enable them to easily access information related to humanitarian assistance. This two-way communication ensures that humanitarian agencies’ decisions are informed by affected communities’ views while enabling them to assess and comment on organizations’ performance, including on sensitive matters such as sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). In this regard, the PSEA Network in Libya will support a coordinated approach to delivering on commitments to minimize the risk of SEA by humanitarian workers and ensure an effective mechanism for reporting that respect the principles of informed consent and confidentiality, and provides appropriate assistance where required.
