Project Summary

With funds mobilized through JRP in 2020, UNICEF in partnership with 7 national, 2 international NGOs and one Government partner will reach 98,200 boys and girls with child protection and adolescents services both in the Rohingya camp and Host Communities. The services will include Case Management, including support to foster families, response to children who are victims or at risk of trafficking, Mental Health and Psychosocial support services (MHPSS), Life skills and strengthening adolescents participation in Peacebuilding and Social Cohesion. The UNICEF's Child Protection project will also support system strengthening jointly with government and partners including access to justice for children and birth registration services as a strategic approach to foster the emergency and development nexus. It is anticiapted that 75% of the interventions will be in camps and 25% will be in host communities with girls constituting 52% of the planned child beneficiaries and boys 48%.

Needs

The September 2019 report of UNHCR estimates 913,981 refugees are currently hosted in 35 refugee camps in Cox's Bazar with children constituting 55% of the population. The refugees continues to live in congested and unsafe camps, with nearly 100% dependency aid which continues to be compounded by Government's restrictions, limited access to education and livelihood opportunities, particularly for adolescents boys and girls. The Child Protection situation for children in both the camp and host community remains of serious concerns. The CPSS CPIMS+ report revealed a high number of children more than 24000, who experienced one or more forms of violence or exploitation , exposure to harmful cultural social norms and practices, . There are also high numbers of Separated and Unaccompanied children who continue to require family based support. Children with disabilities are exposed to isolation that reduces their e.g. social network, inclusion ,access to resources, equity, opportunity to participate. There is high need for mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) as children continue to face stressful situations in the camp due to disruptions of normal life, violence associated with displacement, including uncertainties about their future. Girls, particularly those who are facing challenges in carrying daily activities, including participating in Multi-Purpose centre will need continued support to access these services. Adolescent boys, particularly those above the age of 15 years continue to face much greater challenge as education comensurate their ages is not available.The Rohingya crisis has created a challenging situation, particularly for the host communities , considering Cox's Bazar being one of the worst performing district in terms of child related development indicators and vulnerability to natural disasters. While the arrival of Rohingya refugees since 25 August 2017 is not the root cause of the problem, their presence has contributed to a vicious cycle which will only get worse in the immediate and long term. Of the 2,569,000 people living in Cox's Bazar district, 530,000 (21%) of population live in the immediate environment (Teknaf & Ukhiya) of the camps and are subject to difficult situations due to high influx of refugees which surpasses the local population. In the district, 33% of the population live below the poverty line and 17% below the extreme poverty line. In a joint UNICEF and Government of Bangladesh assessment on basic services, only 37% of households who participated considers marriage below 18 years as Child marriage. Child labor prevalence is reported at 9% including 8% in Ukhiya and 10% in Teknaf. The refugee influx added strains in social services and exacerbated safety risks for adolescents and children including growing reported cases of thefts, traffic accidents and security incidences. Access to justice for children for both Rohingya and Bangladeshi remains an issue of concern due to limited logistics and human resources faced by Police and the inability of courts to deal with cases. Birth Registration into the Birth and Death Registration System (BRIS) is suspended in Cox’s Bazar for both host community and the refugee camp since the refugee influx in 2017. While advocacy efforts are continuing at different levels, the backlog of children without birth certificates continue to rise in both the refugees camps and host community in Cox's Bazar district.

Activities or outputs

Under Objective 1, UNICEF will support a range of services in the camps and in the host community to ensure children and adolescents have access to well-coordinated and gender-sensitive quality child protection services.1. Case Management interventions: Partners will be supported on Case Management services for child vulnerable and at risk of child protection concerns. Key activities will include alternative care arrangements, social protection , family tracing and reunifications, strengthening of referral systems ; 2. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support interventions: Structured MHPSS for girls and boys age 6-18 years will be provided through a network of 136 Multi-Purpose Child and Adolescents Centers (108 in camps and 28 in host community). UNICEF will also support positive parenting programs and recreation for children, including access for children with disabilities. 3. Adolescent resilience, peacebuilding and Life-Skills interventions: Adolescent participation and empowerment programme targeting girls and boys age 10-18 years will be through a network of 5 Social Hubs and MPCs using Peace-building and life-skills modules. 4. Child Protection Coordination and Capacity Building initiatives: Will continue to support the Child Protection coordination efforts including strengthening CPIMS+. Support capacity development of staff using both global and locally contextualized training resources. Continue to support Department of Social Services (DSS) as a strategic approach for sustaining the current CP interventions . Under Objective 2, UNICEF will support a range of system strengthening initiatives with government and local institutions in Cox's Bazar district:1. Justice for Children: Support 3 Children’s Court to deliver child sensitive justice including diversions, promotion of non-custodial measures and trainings of judicial actors in accordance to Children Act 2013( amended 2018). 2. Child Welfare Board: Through partnership with the DSS, will reinforce the functioning of 9 Child Welfare Boards both at district and subdistrict level to strengthen prevention and response to child protection concerns ;3. Strengthening the Child Affairs Police Desk : Will ensure the functionality of Child Friendly Police Desks in 8 police stations with logistic and technical assistance, capacity development, advocacy for deployment of female police officers , infrastructure support.4. Birth Registration: Will partner with the Office of Deputy Commissioner to strengthen the Birth Registration Information System to ensure access to birth registration for both Refugee and Bangladeshi children. The support will include the provision of technical and logistical support to district , sub district and union level local government units; 5. Strengthening of the Social Service Workforce: Will support DSS to increase its Social Workforce and also invest in the coordination capacity of the DSS as part of its exit strategy and in supporting localization agenda. Will reach community members with capacity building support, including strengthen 100 Community Based Child Protection Committees.Strengthen its accountability mechanism to the programme participants by providing a forum for feedback and inputs in the activities. Ensure partners adheres to relevant CPHA Minimum Standards , Child Safeguarding and PSEA standards, develop CP policy and displayed.
