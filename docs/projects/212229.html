Project Summary

HEKS/EPER Strategy in Syria focuses on three basic needs: Water, bread, and work. HEKS/EPER prioritizes strengthening the work of the pre-existing structures without substituting them. Therefore, HEKS/EPER mainly supports public livelihood infrastructures and implements some training and livelihood activities for individuals inside those infrastructures to ensure sustainable impact.
Under this project, HEKS/EPER is planning to implement the following activities to enhance access to basic livelihood services to support at least 458 PoCs across three governorates as follows:
A) Aleppo:
1. Suliman AlHalabi, rehabilitation of parts of Al-Amal Vocational Training Center for PWDs building, and equipping the kitchen to host vocational training, supporting 75 PoC who are people with disability
B) Lattakia:
1. Jablah, Beit Yashout, rehabilitation of parts of the Rural Development Center (RDC) including the carpet production unit and providing equipment and tools for establishing an aluminum workshop, supporting 33 PoC through creating long-term jobs
2. Daleih, conduct the following inside the Daleih production unit:
Rehabilitation work, install solar system, equip a sewing workshop and conduct vocational and business management trainings, provide small grants and toolkits distribution, 220 PoC (160 VT + 60 SBGs/toolkits) - Note: grants will be provided in-kind after internal procurement.
3. Al-Qardaha, in Elmseitra production unit, light rehabilitation and equip soap production workshop, 25 PoC -jobs
4. Bahlolieh, inside the Bahlolieh production unit, equip soap production workshop for 25 PoC-jobs
D) Hama:
1. Tebt Al Emam, rehabilitation works for Sewing Production Unit , possibly supporting 5 PoC-jobs
2. As-Saan, rehabilitation works for Dairy and cheese Production Unit, possibly supporting 65 PoC-jobs
3. Hama, rehabilitating works and solar system installation for The Institute for People with Mental Disabilities, possibly supporting 10 PoC-jobs
The total estimated cost for the above activities is around 480,000$ while the total project cost is around 657,534$.
Project Assumptions: 1) Stable security situation 2) Consistent humanitarian access 3) No mass movements of populations 4) Availability of material in the local market 5) Acceptance of community.
Protection risks and mitigation measures:
1. Exclusion -physical access - mitigation: Design the infrastructure to allow for safe and easy access for everyone including PWDs – i.e: creating ramps.
2. Children conduct work on behalf of the humanitarian organization or its contractors - Mitigation: 1- Add zero-tolerance disclosure on child labor in contracts. 2- Check civil documentation
3. Conflict due to the project – mitigation: advertise transparent selection criteria based on vulnerability and ensure all key informants understand the selection process well.
PSEA strategy:
Risk: PoCs exposed to SEA harm - mitigation: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism and distribute hot-line numbers to key informants and community leaders 3- Address any SEA complaints. 4- Train staff on code of conduct, safeguarding principles and GBV risk mitigation measures; 5- Raise the awareness of existing service providers where victims can seek help 6- Enforce policies to prevent PoC exposure to harm.
Risks:
1) Theft of rehabilitation materials or equipment- Mitigation: Coordinate with local ministries to ensure local ownership of materials & facilities to promote cost recovery systems for longer-term maintenance and to promote community ownership.
2) Delays in obtaining needed approvals for implementation. - Mitigation: keep close coordination with the line ministries at different levels to speed up the processes.
5) Lack of capacity of authorities to operate or manage the infrastructure - Mitigation: ensure authorities' willingness & ability to manage from the assessment phase –provisioning of technical training, spare parts, and tools for public staff.
