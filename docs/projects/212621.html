Project Summary

The crisis in Syria has resulted in the displacements of 6.5 million people, and 1,7 million IDPs ended up in camps and collective centers where they desperately need life-saving multi-sectoral assistance. Most of these IDPs reside in IDP sites in Aleppo and Idleb governorates.
Children and women constitute the majority of IDP residents in IDP sites in Northern Syria Increase in the number of wash, and hygiene-related diseases are considerable for IDPs, particularly women and children.
Most of these camps were established on agricultural lands. So one of the most critical issues these camps suffer from is the dire conditions of roads, such as; the spread of mud, dust, mudslides, and water pools. So graveling and paving camps' roads enhance the resilience of both the IDPs (men, women, boys, and girls) and the host communities and have a great significance on IDPs' life as follows:
• Creating a clean environment devoid of dust and dirt, and all other contaminants for IDPs and host communities
• Facilitating movement of IDPs and vehicles to the camps.
• Reducing the spread of dust in summer that has terrible effects such as asthma and allergies, especially on children and women
• Preventing the spread of mud in winter and the related adverse effects such as mudslides
• Preventing the spread of water pools on the roads
• Reducing traffic accidents resulting from the roughness of roads
• Creating a safe, friendly, clean environment for children to practice their activities.
The project also aims to empower through an intervention that responds to their identified needs by enhancing their psychological, educational, and vocational status and raising their awareness. Moreover, the project will increase the community's awareness to create a more enabling environment through establishing the Development Center and conducting a need amendment and discussion sessions to verify capacity-building and empowerment activities.
Vocational training will be provided to targeted communities and camps through offering various types of training. This includes profession training such as tailoring, barbering, mobile and laptop repair, as well as technical and administrative training like Microsoft Office programs, design software, and digital marketing, among others. These training sessions will be tailored to the specific needs of each community and groups. Assessment will take into account the diverse needs of each gender, with particular attention given to training individuals with disabilities.
The aim is to ensure sustainable income for the targeted individuals and their families, thereby improving their financial and economic situation and reducing reliance on humanitarian aid.
The beneficiaries of road maintenance activities will be 45,500 individuals (8,500 men, 10,000 women, 14,000 boys, 13,000 girls, and 4,000 PWD).
The beneficiaries of the training activities and income opportunities will be 500 individuals (200 men, 200 women, and 100 PWD).
During the year 2025, the SDI plans to work within the same activities, targeting the areas most in need and covering the gaps. SDI is planning to work with a budget consistent with or close to the 2024 budget plan.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Poor road conditions worsen annually post-rainy season, leading to difficulties for all demographics: men struggle with mobility in and out of camps for work, women face challenges in movement and hygiene upkeep, and children find it harder to reach schools. Road damage poses injury risks and delays, especially for sick travelers needing urgent medical care. Around 45,500 people regularly travel on these roads. Also highlights the urgent need for vocational training and other interventions, including adolescent awareness and psychological support. Project assessments will ensure activities meet beneficiaries' needs across genders.Activities:Mobilize and sensitize the community.Assess target roads for rehabilitation needs.Contract road work according to SDI guidelines.Repair approximately 7 KM of road, including asphalt resurfacing and pothole fixing.Monitor progress through site visits.Equip vocational training halls.Select beneficiaries.Coordinate with stakeholders and local authorities transparently.Assist beneficiaries in marketing their products, including e-marketing principles.Tension among host communities, IDPs, and returnees poses a high likelihood of occurrence with a high impact. Mitigation measures include ensuring needs-based distribution, transparent selection criteria, and providing vocational training for income enhancement. Another high likelihood, medium impact risk is the influence of local authorities on selection criteria, potentially leading to nepotism and community tension. To mitigate this, standardize assistance packages, ensure uniform beneficiary selection criteria.Beneficiaries face risks of sexual misconduct, often hesitant to report due to various factors like lack of knowledge about reporting methods, fear of retaliation, stigma, or losing assistance. Also, illiteracy or visual impairments may hinder understanding, especially for women or children in remote areas. To mitigate these risks, SDI will: clearly publicize the PSEA hotline and email through banners, social media, and phone messages; provide PSEA training to staff and contractors, emphasizing zero tolerance; hold community awareness sessions on reporting and confidentiality; conduct child-friendly awareness sessions; install complaint boxes; use PSEA checklists; ensure accessibility for PwDs.A 1: Target location is secure; risk: Armed conflict affecting access; likelihood: medium; effect: medium. Mitigation: Monitor security, advise alternatives, report to SNFI cluster and OCHA, and consider activity delay or relocation if needed. A 2: HLP documents are authentic; risk: Fake documentation; likelihood: medium; effect: medium. Mitigation: Provide staff with HLP rights awareness and engage local authorities. A 3: Approval for site access; risk: Hindrance from local authorities; likelihood: moderate; impact: high. Mitigation: Coordinate with local authorities, review HLP documents, and conduct further verification before activities.
