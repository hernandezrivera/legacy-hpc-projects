Project Summary

Following liberation from the Islamic State at the end of 2017, Iraq entered a transition period from emergency humanitarian relief to emergency recovery. Despite this transition, there are areas still possessing significant humanitarian needs in the fields of WASH and health.
There is an immediate health risk caused by inadequate access to safe water, poor sanitary conditions in camp and out of camp settings, and bad hygiene practices. Affected communities have insufficient access to safe water and sanitation, inadequate supply of safe drinking water, lack of functioning latrines, and limited availability of basic hygiene items. The general situation presents a significant risk of waterborne and diarrheal diseases, with an increased risk of infections such as cholera arising in areas where local authorities do not have the capacity to cover the needs, and where only a limited number of humanitarian actors are currently operating. In particular, the lack of WASH infrastructure in schools is a huge challenge as the current situation prevents children from returning to education, or exposes those who do return to the risk of communicable diseases.
Primary healthcare provision remains insufficient in several areas which continue to be overwhelmed and in critical need of support, particularly for children, pregnant women and neonates. There is a lack of medical qualified staff, inadequate medical supplies and pharmaceuticals, a lack of referral pathways for critical and severe patients, and lack of secondary healthcare services. Communities suffering these conditions, do not have any established Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCs), and continue to rely on the provision of healthcare through Mobile Medical Teams (MMTs).

Considering the needs, Relief International (RI) aims at addressing the three areas of concern through an integrated program approach. It will build upon its current WASH, and health interventions to continue providing emergency lifesaving health assistance to the most vulnerable and underserved populations in the areas of Anbar, Kirkuk and Salahaldin governorates. For WASH, RI will focus on the provision of safe drinking water and sanitation facilities, and the promotion of hygiene, specifically in schools and health facilities; the overall intervention will target 133,000 beneficiaries. In terms of Health, RI will continue its intervention with Static Primary Healthcare Centers in camp settings; the overall intervention will target 470,583 beneficiaries.
In terms of targeted population, both male and female, IDPs, returnees and host communities, will be targeted. The intervention will be tailored considering that each gender group requires a specific response; therefore, specific attention will be posed to the needs of women and children to design a more effective response. The description of the activities related to each program will be developed in the following dedicated section.

Needs

The acknowledged needs have been corroborated by the findings of RI’s Multi Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), finalised in early November. The MSNA took into account RI’s programme implementation in Western Anbar, Kirkuk and Salahaldin, and gathered data from RI’s partners and external sources. The MSNA was cross-checked with the recent assessments from REACH Initiative in Anbar, Kirkuk and Salahaldin governorates to conclude that the following priority needs are: 1. Access to quality health care services in remote areas, provision of essential drugs, vaccines, provision of healthcare through static PHCs in camp settings and operating static Delivery Rooms;2. Upgrading/installing new water and sanitation facilities and provision of sanitation services, provision of sustainable water networks areas of return, strengthening community-based hygiene promotion, provision of integrated WASH interventions in schools and health facilities, and sustainable, cost-effective approaches for wastewater treatment, desludging and solid waste. The 1st Standard Allocation Strategy for 2019 states that 0.5 million of returnees do not have access to sufficient livelihood opportunities and basic services. According to the REACH Initiative overview of Western Anbar in August 2018, the main reasons preventing people from returning to their locations of origin are a lack of basic services, with damage to the water pipes resulting from IS occupation a particular concern; however, repair work has begun on this to some extent. At the time of data collection, 20 water pump stations reportedly still needed to be repaired, while eight were said to be functional. Furthermore, equipment, such as generators, were reported to have been looted during the recent crisis. During the data collection, there was reportedly no functioning water treatment plant, with water being sourced directly from the Euphrates River. People in Western Anbar, particularly in Qaim and surrounding areas, only have access to water from the piped water network for three to four hours every two to three days, and the limited availability is caused by a lack of public electricity for running the pumps. As a result, the pumps were running on generators, for which there was reportedly limited fuel available. There was a reported need of 300,000 liters of fuel each month, but only 120,000 liters were being provided by the GoI. Around 10% of the population in the surrounding area of Qaim are either not connected to the piped water network or the pipes were damaged. As per the REACH report, there is need for the provision of new generators, fuel, and water treatment materials, as well as other equipment to improve access to portable water. The lack of tools and support is jeopardizing the coping strategies of returnees and, as a consequence, their reintegration within the community. According to the HRP 2019, 2.3 million Iraqis do not have equal access to proper WASH services. Also, 0.5 million people in camps are in need of WASH support with less than 35 liters of water per day provided to 230,000 IDPs, more than 20 people sharing one latrine, and the absence of appropriate wastewater treatment facilities worsening the water and sanitation conditions in the camps. Communities in remote locations are able to access piped water, however this supply cannot be considered as regular, and there is a need to further rehabilitate WASH infrastructure in these areas to increase water supply. The outbreak of cholera in winter will increase the need of basic health services. Therefore, the continuation of PHCs is required, together with medicines provision, referral mechanisms, reproductive health services, maternal, neonatal and child health care services to provide healthcare services in camps settings. As a result, RI will aim to continue implementing programming in these areas given the huge presence of needs, and the lack of actors available to take over with a potential exit of RI.

Activities

HEALTH RI will focus on Anbar, Kirkuk, Salahaldin, and Baghdad by implementing healthcare services through existing PHCs in camp settings. RI will: 1. Carry out consultations, identify diseases, and treat and provide case management for common communicable and non-communicable diseases; 2. Strengthen the management of essential medicines and supplies through good storage practices, proper handling and monitoring the consumption;3. Initiate surveillance where it still does not exist and strengthen the already existing, to support the improvement of national facilities in monitoring and responding to diseases outbreaks. RI will facilitate training on EWARN, disseminate weekly EWARN reports and send alert for notifiable and outbreak prone diseases; 4. Deploy qualified medical personnel to support PHCs with technical oversight and in coordiantion with the Health Cluster and MoH; 5. Ensure that the project staff has the necessary skills, and train them on treatment guidelines, Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), chronic diseases, Psychological First Aid, Reproductive health, outbreak response and any other training needs identified; 6. Identify, treat and provide case management for epidemic prone diseases by improving access to basic and essential primary health services for crisis affected populations in frontline, recently liberated locations;7. Raise awareness, screen and assess disability in the conflict affected districts;8. Deploy qualified medical personnel to oversee the implementation of rehabilitation and PSS services at DoH supported facilities;9. Work with MoH and DoH to implement system strengthening and capacity building efforts to strengthen the quality of care and improve health outcomes. Handover of activities to the DoH, including static PHCs in camp settings, in the respective governorates will be a gradual process where RI aim to slowly handover activities towards the end of the funding cycle. However, a full closeout will always bring issues related to exit; until the government is able to fully support PHC service provision in all locations, or until there is an ability for the population to afford minimal costs sharing, the full transition to the DoH cannot be fully envisioned in the short- or medium-term. RI will work closely with the DoH during 2019 to ensure that they have technical capacity to take over facilities, and are also planing to work on system strengthening and the role out of peer monitoring for all supported PHC's. WASH The proposed WASH activities are designed to improve the access to quality water and the improvement of public health within vulnerable displaced and host communities through a comprehensive package of WASH services including the provision of safe drinking water and sanitation facilities, hygiene promotion and materials, and environmental sanitation activities. WASH activities will both respond to emergency WASH needs and support longer term public health. RI will:1. Repair, extend and upgrade Water Treatment Plants, Water Compact Units, and Reverse Osmosis systems;2. Provide integrated WASH services in public buildings; this includes latrine and sanitation blocks rehabilitation and construction, installation of water tanks, and hygiene promotion;3. Distribute NFI focusing on hygiene kits and emergency relief items;4. Provide training sessions to WASH implementers on water quality testing, including how to monitor it, chlorinations, FRC testing, Potatest training; 5. Monitor water quality in health facilities.

Protection Risk Analysis

RI has conducted an analysis of the protection risks that potentially threaten RI’s ability to provide assistance that is impartial, does not endanger or harm beneficiaries, allows for feedback to be incorporated into the program monitoring plan, and promotes community empowerment. 1. The first protection risk facing the target population is the risk of being targeted for attacks by armed groups due to association with organizations receiving a grant from donors associated to countries perceived as their enemies. All beneficiaries are vulnerable to this risk. 2. The second main protection risk is the risk of beneficiaries being asked to provide sex in exchange for goods and services. The most vulnerable group to this risk is women, especially girls and young women. RI believes the risk is low but recognizes, that the problem of sexual exploitation and abuse exists within humanitarian spaces, and will therefore, take all measures to prevent its occurrence on the part of its staff, partners, vendors and other associates. 3. The third main protection risk is exclusion, denial of assistance or services or other factors that render accessing the services dangerous or impossible. Those who are most vulnerable to this threat are older persons, persons with disabilities, children and unaccompanied youth. 4. The security situation can deteriorate putting both staff and beneficiaries in danger. Also, in the event of increased insecurity, some routes might be inaccessible, making it difficult for RI to implement the project or deliver supplies.

Mitigation Measures

1. In order to minimize this risk, RI will operate in low profile with minimal or no visibility in such context. RI will ensure that the aid delivered is seen as ‘Iraqis helping Iraqis’ by supporting teams from the local community. In addition, RI will make sure that the interventions are conducted in a low-profile fashion, that does not draw attention to the amount or value of the resources being provided, transported to the program sites, etc. To the possible extent, program goods and supplies delivered to beneficiaries will be locally-procured, inside Iraq. 2. RI has a fully adapted Policy on Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and has operationalized PSEA throughout its recruitment and procurement practices. RI requires all partners and contractors, to sign its Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) policy at the time of agreement, and all staff and consultants at the time of offer letter, so that the contracts are not valid without a signed Code of Conduct in place. 3. RI will ensure that the health assistance provided across all the project-supported health facilities are accessible to these vulnerable populations by targeting these groups within the needs assessments; monitoring patient data to ensure that all age and gender groups are represented and persons with disabilities are being served; providing the aforementioned complaints mechanism; assessing the HFs and their locations to ensure that physical and security challenges do not prevent access for any population group. 4. RI's Safety and Security Manager will monitor security developments on an ongoing basis and will provide advice. In the case of a significant deterioration in security or access, RI may briefly suspend activities, or in extreme and sustained cases, may relocate activities to other areas where similar needs exist. In the event of increased conflict or inaccessible roads, RI will use identified alternative routes, and has created relationships with private contractors capable of transporting supplies on behalf of RI. It is therefore expected that even with a serious deterioration in security, RI will be able to continue rehabilitation/minor repairing activities. RI will also closely observe the existing safety and security measures being successfully implemented by other humanitarian organizations, delivering aid in similar capacities, and will maintain the flexibility that allows the organization to adapt, change or adopt new best practice strategies.

How is your project accountable to affected population

RI exercises downward accountability, involving beneficiaries and the DoH of each governorate at all levels of the project cycle, starting from the needs assessment, to the project planning, execution and closure/handing over of the project. RI piloted a complaints and feedback mechanism, allowing beneficiaries to rate the level of care received and to provide any feedback about how to improve the level of care provided, at the health facilities. A mechanism will be put in place to allow patients to register complaints at any time via the RI hotline or UNOPS monitored hotlines, complaint boxes, and face to face. It is within this premise that RI promotes the participation of beneficiaries in every area of decision-making. Moreover, a mechanism is already set out in current RI supported locations, where beneficiaries participate in joint monitoring activities, allowing beneficiary inputs to be incorporated to improve the quality of project implementation and cater quality service deliveries. In order to ensure accountability to beneficiaries, RI will have visibility/banners explaining RI’s specific activities, working days/hours and with the complaints hotline number visibly displayed within the supported facility. This will assist RI in ensuring that each health facility is delivering assistance accountability and giving beneficiaries an independent method to provide information confidentially. RI staff will also be in frequent contact with local leaders and authorities to check in on the progress of the project and collect feedbacks that will be shared with RI Senior Management Team. RI will continually monitor patients’ satisfaction with the provided services, in order for the latter to be better tailored and meet needs more efficiently. Community feedback will be critical to the direction of the program. Through the adoption of Do No Harm approach, efforts are made to ensure that relationships with the targeted population and its local leaders are enhanced and programs are implemented to improve relationships, reduce tensions, and promote peaceful pathways out of conflict situations. RI believes that understanding the context in which we operate is paramount to ensure that interventions are successful and to avoid negative consequences in the operation area. RI’s monitoring activities will play a vital role in assessing the context and conflict dynamics, especially the role of dividers and connectors. By doing this, RI can attempt to link the conflict analysis with the program cycle of the project. This will help RI to re-plan, implement and monitor the intervention with a conflict sensitive approach. RI's program will aim to build resilience and reduce future risks by supporting existing institutional facilities, thereby, helping the government fulfill its obligations to its citizens, strengthening the capacity of the healthcare personnel, and serving the basic health needs of the population, thereby, improving the health of the citizens and allowing them to build a more positive future.
