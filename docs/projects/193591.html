Project Summary

Physicians for Human Rights Israel (PHR) will contribute to Health and Nutrition Cluster objectives 1 and 2.
By providing lifesaving and essential services to the most vulnerable Palestinian communities, while strengthening the local health system through trainings in specialties lacking in the Occupied Palestinian Territory (oPt), we will contribute to the first objective.
Through mobile clinics in the West Bank and medical delegations to Gaza, PHR’s volunteer medical professionals will provide direct medical assistance through 10,000 consultations in both the Gaza Strip and the West Bank; will provide free medication and medical equipment; and will issue and/or fund medical referrals to hospitals in East Jerusalem and Israel for over 200 cases. The specialist mobile clinics will visit West Bank villages and areas with limited access to specialized care once a week, conducting health screenings in some of the clinics. Approximately once a month, PHR will deploy medical delegations to the Gaza Strip where at least 60 % of the patients visiting the mobile clinic will be women.
As part of the medical delegations to the Gaza Strip, PHR surgeons will carry out at least 100 surgeries. Patients will be selected from the Ministry of Health’s waiting list and in coordination with local surgeons. PHR surgeons will bring necessary surgical equipment, disposables, and tools lacking in the Gaza Strip - both for use during surgeries and in order to increase the capacity of local public hospitals.
While providing medical aid, the intervention also aims to enhance preparedness within the health sector and reinforce the capacity of the Palestinian health system. Health professionals will be trained as per their own priorities, based on academic and practical gaps identified in collaboration with the Ministry of Health and local NGOs. In Gaza, trainings will take place through hands-on surgical coaching, workshops, and conferences. The surgery coaching in the key fields of orthopedics, vascular, and chest surgery (among others) will benefit at least 150 local Palestinian surgeons and healthcare staff. In between visits, PHR’s surgeons will continue to provide expert advice and support to their colleagues in Gaza via calls and emails. PHR will also facilitate 3 medical conferences in Gaza with 500 attendances, bringing up-to-date medical knowledge to meet the needs of medical professionals, particularly in the field of mental health.
Our interventions will also address the Health and Nutrition Cluster strategic objective 2, carrying out advocacy activities to protect the right to health in the long term. PHR will intervene to reverse denials of exit permits in 220 cases, for patients who require treatment unavailable in the oPt, their companions, as well as for health staff needing to exit the oPt for professional training (approximately 90% of whom will be from Gaza and 10% from the West Bank). When justified, PHR will use legal action combined with a range of advocacy tools to tackle individual cases and will also employ strategic litigation to challenge general criteria according to which permits are denied. We will focus particularly on children in our advocacy work. Additionally, PHR will advocate for the right to health of cancer patients residing in Gaza who require life-saving interventions such as chemotherapy, and whose permits have been consistently and wrongly denied on grounds that the expertise for these is available in Gaza. PHR will also continue to focus on advocacy surrounding long-term and multiple-entry permits, which are rarely issued and impact patients who must frequently exit the Gaza Strip for treatment, in particular oncological patients.

1 - Needs

As indicated in the Health Cluster draft HNO 2023 matrixes and narratives, the oPt’s humanitarian health crisis is driven by cross-cutting challenges, which have placed a greater burden on an already burdened healthcare system; namely the COVID-19 pandemic, flare-ups of military hostilities and the decades-long occupation of the oPt and Israel’s blockade on the Gaza Strip. In the West Bank, the local health system is particularly challenged to respond to patient needs for the most disconnected communities living in Area C, which are cut off from the main health service providers in Areas A and B due to Israeli movement restrictions. As the humanitarian situation deteriorates, women’s needs for specialized services, including reproductive health screenings and breast cancer awareness, has increased. In Gaza, the chronic depletion of essential medical supplies continues to pose a considerable challenge: as per the MoH’s most recent available numbers, at the end of 2022, 40 percent of essential drugs and 19 percent of medical disposables were reportedly at ‘zero stock’ in Gaza . This issue will be addressed through medication and equipment deliveries. Further, Israeli approval rates of exit permits for treatment unavailable in Gaza and the West Bank have been plummeting, and PHR receives many more requests for assistance in these areas than it can handle. Specific challenges are identified for cancer patients – in particular women - as Israel rejects permit applications on grounds that the necessary treatment is available in Gaza, which is often incorrect and does not account for the patient load and lack of equipment and medication. Palestinian physicians face pronounced challenges in their pursuit of studies, continuing education, and specialized training and they often fall victim to Israel's non-transparent movement and access restrictions, preventing them from reaching residencies, training opportunities, and jobs. PHR’s activities on the ground allow us to track developing trends, and our needs assessment mechanism involves both continued cooperation and contact with stakeholders such as village councils and MoH officials, as well as an analysis of the data gathered on the ground through our medical delegation of specialist doctors. PHR has observed growing needs for referrals in the fields of Internal and family medicine, orthopedics, rehabilitation, gynaecology, nephrology, neurology, and cardiology, among others. PHR’s needs assessment is based on a variety of sources: WHO situation reports, the Humanitarian Response Plan for the oPt (HRP), the Health Cluster’s Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), reports from other organizations, and our own practical assessments based on our contact with thousands of beneficiaries on the ground. PHR is in constant dialogue with local partners, the MoH and physicians themselves. PHR has been handling permit denials for 25 years.

2 - Activities

PHRI has over 33 years’ experience providing direct healthcare and humanitarian aid to the Palestinian population of the oPt and advocating for Palestinians’ right to health at the principled level. Our mobile clinic was established in 1988 to provide healthcare to communities in rural West Bank villages and to underserved populations in Gaza. Following the increased movement restrictions introduced against residents of the oPt in the 1990s and 2000s, we responded by offering assistance to Palestinians who require permits to access medical care outside their areas of residence. We are currently the only Israeli organization able to operate in the Strip on a regular basis.PHRI has an unparalleled knowledge regarding the impact of Israel’s permit regime on the right to health in the oPt, and a strong network of local partners in both Gaza and the West Bank, allowing us to continue our activities despite external pressures and challenges. Our local connections also grant us a unique awareness of the changing needs on the ground – we receive regular updates and communications from Palestinian organizations and the Palestinian Ministry of Health (MoH) regarding the medical care, equipment, and medication most urgently needed. Activity 1: Mobile Clinics and Women Mobile ClinicMobile clinics and medical delegations will offer specialized care in 10,000 patients visits (over 60% of them for women and girls) in both the West Bank and Gaza, in the most vulnerable communities. PHRI’s clinic is operated by Israeli and Palestinian volunteer physicians and medical staff that travels weekly to the West Bank, and every six weeks to Gaza. These clinics consist of teams of 10-15 physicians and health professionals. Specialties include neurology, endocrinology, orthopaedics, surgery, internal medicine, emergency medicine, paediatrics, gynaecology, urology, ophthalmology, immunology, ear nose throat, family medicine, physiotherapy, and mental health consultations, on top of nurses, nutritionists and a mobile pharmacy. The clinics travel on a pre-announced basis, are set up in local public facilities, and provide services to the individuals in need from the morning to afternoon, for around 6 hours. The needs of people with disabilities will be addressed by making all clinics and medical services accessible.Specialized women’s mobile clinics will be conducted under this activity once a month. Visiting the same rural community 3 times in a row, the women’s clinics create a space where Palestinian women can speak about their health needs and access care and consultations. In addition, the women’s clinics often include a lecture or information session on health, touching on topics such as breast cancer screening, nutrition, and exercise. Female health-staffed clinics will specifically address women’s health needs and local women grassroots organizations will help ensure high patient turnout and that marginalized beneficiaries will not get overlooked. Activity 2: Referrals to medical specialists and tests200 mobile clinic patients in need of treatment unavailable either in the West Bank or Gaza will be supported with referrals to hospitals or appointments in East Jerusalem or Israel.Activity 3: Medication and Medical Equipment Chronic depletion of essential medical supplies and drugs will be addressed through medication and equipment deliveries based on Ministry of Health lists. The mobile clinic will also distribute free medication during consultations, while PHR surgeons will bring equipment needed for surgeries. Activity 4: Provision of Surgeries in the Gaza StripThrough its team of highly skilled surgeons, 100 essential surgeries will be conducted. In some cases, PHR surgeons may conduct emergency surgeries if our delegation happens to be present in the operating theatre and may be able to assist. In 2022 the main sub-specialties needed and provided through our activities were General surgery (39%) , Orthopedics surgery (33%), Gastrointestinal surgery (19%), and Urology surgery (6%).Activity 5: Training WorkshopsIn partnership with the Palestinian Ministries of Health in both the West Bank and Gaza, PHRI will organize conferences and workshops for health professionals, bringing volunteer experts from Israel to present recent advancements in their fields – from surgical practices to mental health, nutrition, and diabetes. The project will enhance the skills and knowledge of over 500 Palestinian medical staff to improve health services in their areas of residence. 150 of the trainees will be trained during surgeries. Activity 6: Freedom of Movement CaseworkIsrael’s movement restrictions bar medication and equipment from entering Gaza on the one hand, and bar physicians and patients from traveling out of the Strip on the other. It is thus vital for PHRI to assist Palestinian patients and healthcare professionals to obtain permits to exit Gaza and the West Bank for healthcare purposes, allowing them to access the care they need and hone their professional skills. PHR will support patients whose permits to exit Gaza and the West Bank for treatment have been denied. Particular attention will be given to female patients, as well as parents who need to accompany their children. New restrictive regulations issued by Israel will be examined from a gender perspective, highlighting how they affect women’s access to health. PHR may take legal action to appeal refusals and use strategic litigation when it becomes aware of a restriction affecting a cluster of patients.Activity 7: AdvocacyThe project will protect the right to health of Palestinians through advocacy activities targeting Israeli duty bearers, including security officials and Members of Knesset, combined with media work to increase pressure on decision makers. International advocacy activities will challenge Israel's policies and their adverse effect on Palestinians' health.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The direct inquiries, issues, and complaints raised by our beneficiaries constitute the basis for the project’s needs assessment, design, and implementation. In all our work in Gaza and the West Bank, PHR strives to respond to patients’ needs, which are shared with us through our Mobile Clinic, through complaints received on the phone, and through our contact with the medical community. The Palestinian Ministry of Health and hospitals in Gaza are key partner in many of the project’s activities, especially the surgical operations and capacity building activities in Gaza. We regularly receive lists of patients awaiting surgery from the Gazan Ministry of Health and from local Gaza hospitals, and this is how we select patients for surgery during our medical delegations to Gaza. Further, Palestinian organizations, most of which are grassroots NGOs well embedded in the host communities, play an important role in pointing at patient needs. Among them is Basmat el Amal, a well-known cancer organization active in Gaza for many years who refers patients to PHR; the women’s union in Khan Younis - a rehabilitation organization in El Burredj refugee camp; and the Support and Hope cancer organization. The Director of PHR’s Mobile Clinic is in constant dialogue with our vast network of local partners, including grassroots organizations, Palestinian hospitals and physicians, and the Ministry of Health. Finally, direct contact between PHR’s volunteer physicians and local physicians in Gaza is a key component of the project and forms the basis for many of the training and capacity building activities we implement.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

we maintain an open channel for feedback and complaints from our beneficiaries. Beneficiary complaints can be transferred through local doctors and partners or through a form with contact details of and our main office in Jaffa, offering an avenue for complaint about the service beneficiaries have received. Forms are also made available to medical staff attending conferences. Regarding freedom of movement cases, PHR regularly conducts in depth interviews with two beneficiaries every month, to get a better understanding of how our working modalities are perceived.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Do No Harm principles are considered through all PHR’s projects. Our work with the beneficiaries we serve relies on our in-depth familiarity with the local context and the unique challenges faced by this population, which we have developed through more than three decades of work on these issues. In addition, we ensure our interventions are culturally sensitive by employing a staff over 1/3 of whom are themselves Palestinians who speak native Arabic and have the capacity to identify culturally-sensitive issues in our work. Some of our interventions specficially target women and aim to promote and protect their rights and wellbeing, as detailed above. In addition, PHR recently reviewed and updated its code of conduct, which contains strict safeguarding and anti-sexual harassment policies to ensure protection of our beneficiaries, staff, and volunteers. The updated code was approved by our Board of Directors in November 2021, and includes increased background checks and training for all incoming staff and volunteers. All PHR staff members and volunteers are required to sign a pledge indicating they have never been accused of harassment, they acknowledge PHR’s zero-tolerance policy for harassment, and they commit to protecting beneficiaries’ privacy and refrain from exploiting their positions for sexual and other favors. Volunteer training modules have also recently been updated to ensure they address safeguarding issues. We have an internal complaints mechanism for staff and volunteers, and provide beneficiaries with information regarding avenues for complaint if they have encountered any misconduct in their dealings with us. PHR prioritizes the safety and dignity of its patients PHRas its work is guided by our safeguarding policy document, which describes how PHR is committed to preventing and responding appropriately to harm to children and vulnerable adults; how PHR openly communicates a lack of tolerance towards abuse; commits to preventing a person from working with children and/or vulnerable people if he/she poses an unacceptable risk; places clear responsibilities and expectations on PHR’s staff and associates and supports them in understanding and acting in line with the safeguarding policy. PHR’s activities support the development of self-protection capacities and help empower beneficiaries, allowing them to claim their right to health. Patients who approach PHR needing referrals and appointments outside the West Bank, who ask PHR to try to obtain an exit permit after their application has been turned down, must claim their rights themselves – this is particularly true of female patients which are empowered to own and defend their cases.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PHR strives to remove all access barriers to our activities, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups. PHR provides assistance in accordance with basic humanitarian principles, based on identified needs (impartiality), without any adverse distinction, as indicated by the different categories of patients that are treated by our clinics and medical delegations, have undergone surgeries, or received referrals or permits in the past decade. Our beneficiaries have included women, men and children, with a special focus on the most vulnerable people. Vulnerable groups are identified and targeted through our network of well-recognized partners on the ground: women grassroots organizations, rehabilitation organizations, cancer organizations, and others. Acceptance levels within the target population are high: PHR is well known and trusted in the oPt, after over three decades of regular activities and ongoing cooperation with partner organisations on the ground. PHR – an Israeli organization - is perceived as a professional, trustworthy and neutral. Among the vulnerable groups targeted by our services are women, who are especially targeted by our women's-only mobile clinics in the West Bank and our focus on women's health needs in Gaza. People with disabilities are also targeted by PHR's mobile clinics, all of which are made wheelchair accessible, and which strive to provide specialist healthcare to people with disabilities, including by helping them to obtain medical equipment (such as wheelchairs) which is otherwise unavailable to them.
