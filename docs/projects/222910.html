Project Summary

In 2025, Plan International Bangladesh (PIB) will prioritize fostering safe, equitable, and inclusive learning opportunities that are gender-transformative for 17,398 learners (F: 8,999; M: 8,399) aged 3 to 18, among Rohingya refugees and children from host communities. This includes 522 learners with disabilities (3%), adolescents, and youth. Through various education interventions implemented by local partners, PIB will reach 5,396 learners aged 3-4, 3,360 learners aged 5-14 (F: 1,929; M: 1,431), and 8,642 learners aged 15-18 (F: 4,373; M: 4,269). PIB will continue using the Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLF) model, expanding and refurbishing Early Childhood Development (ECD) centers and introducing new curriculum: the Myanmar Curriculum for KG, primary, and secondary levels, and the Functional Literacy and Numeracy (FLN) Program for adolescent and youth learning facilities.
The project will focus on the most vulnerable learners, particularly adolescent girls and learners with disabilities. Gender-segregated classrooms, female facilitators for adolescent girls, and female monitors will support adolescent girls’ safe travel to and from learning facilities and ensure regular attendance. Additionally, 37,991 parents, community members, and stakeholders (F: 19,617; M: 18,374) will be engaged in efforts to promote girls' education, empower adolescent girls, and mitigate gender-based violence (GBV) risks, bullying, and harassment.
PIB will strengthen the skills of teachers and facilitators in ECD, Myanmar Curriculum (KG, primary, and secondary), FLN, and host primary and secondary schools on gender-responsive pedagogy, inclusive education, theme-based training, and crisis-sensitive planning to improve learning outcomes for refugee and host community learners. PIB will also expand the Pre-Service Capacity Development (PSCD) program to train Rohingya women to teach the Myanmar Curriculum, creating a pool of potential female facilitators. To enhance classroom support, PIB will build coaching and mentoring capacities for Rohingya Resource Instructors, master trainers, and education supervisors to assist teachers in real classroom settings. Additionally, PIB will implement a system for regular learning assessments and formative evaluations to monitor students' progress, enhancing teachers' assessment skills and tracking learners' development.
Under the Joint Response Plan (JRP) 2025, PIB will further strengthen community engagement and capacity-building for Education Sector partners and affected communities to enhance their involvement in children’s education. PIB will train Community Education Support Groups (CESGs) in Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) to collaboratively develop emergency preparedness and response plans, equipping learning facilities and communities with vital skills for crisis response. PIB's education partners will receive training in gender-responsive pedagogy, gender mainstreaming, Social and Emotional Learning (SEL), inclusive education, and crisis-sensitive planning and management. This initiative will support education partners throughout the project, enhance community consultations and advocacy, and foster social cohesion and coexistence by engaging both host and camp communities and key community leaders, including CESG members.
PIB has secured funding from institutional donors like Education Cannot Wait, DFAT-AHP (Australia), BMZ (Germany), and JPF (Japan) for the next year. Efforts will continue to attract additional resources to support both technical and operational needs, enhancing the education sector’s overall goals.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Through this proposal, PIB aims to discourse the main barriers and needs of Rohingya refugees and host community children, especially girls and children with disability, to access gender transformative quality inclusive quality education.According to the ISNA- 2024, 54% of early education-aged children aged from 3-4 years not attending early education program during the 2023-2024 school year. Besides, education enrollment in particular among adolescents (12-18 years), especially girl remained low (only 49%) in the camps in enrolment. It’s been found that adolescent girls aged of 12 to 18 are more likely than boys to be missing school. J-MSNA 2023 data shows that the reason behind missing schools by girls is due to education not being considered as priority, household chores, protection risks, cultural norms etc. whereas boys are missing school due to attending Madrasha. Limited data exists on the number of Rohingya adolescents are currently engaged with education opportunities including pre-vocational program, especially life skill. However, the limited range of educational opportunities exposes them to exploitation, increased exposure to protection risks, and can lead to negative coping mechanisms. For the holistic development of early childhood learners (age 3-4), a common curriculum has been developed for the whole sector. However, the significant additional materials like TG, routine, assessment guidelines, etc. need to be aligned/developed with this curriculum and PIB intends to support the sector with this activity through ECEWG. In Cox’s Bazar response, there is a need to develop appropriate guidelines for supporting and enabling young children’s holistic development in camp and host communities following the context of Bangladesh and customizing the Early Learning competency framework of the education sector, Cox’s Bazar. Additionally, climate-smart education modalities piloting will also be a key intervention area for PIB EIE projects.In the Camps, there has been some success in recruiting increase percentage of female teachers, with 48% of teachers being female. However, the majority of female teachers are from the host community and are Bangladeshi, with female Rohingya teachers only comprising 20% of the total (education Sector Dashboard, Sep,2024). This presents a challenge in the roll out of the Myanmar curriculum, with greater numbers of female teachers with sufficient Myanmar language skills needing to be recruited and trained. Besides, children with disabilities face significant challenges who attend learning centers at much lower rates than their peers across all age groups for both girls and boys. This is contributed to by insufficient skills and training of the educators, and poor awareness, negative attitudes and stigma related to children with disabilities attending the same learning centers as children without disabilities. A lack of inclusive learning and teaching materials, including teaching materials suitable for children with learning difficulties, is an additional barrier.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project integrates protection principles to ensure access, safety, and dignity, and promote and protect the rights of refugees, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. The project will strongly follow FOUR GUIDING PRINCIPLES in all of its activities and will be followed at every stage of the project cycle. The project will strongly Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended adverse effects of its intervention, to enhance people’s well-being and to reduce the vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Access to assistance and services will be identified and ensured in proportion to specific needs and without discrimination, gender, disability or age-related barriers. Protection mainstreaming will be ensured throughout the project by training and sensitizing the project and partner staff to equip with Knowledge and support to act in line with CP minimum standards. The safety of program activities and sites, and access to them will be assessed and monitored and activities will be adapted and/or advocacy conducted with relevant authorities to reduce risks and promote safety. To protect children, adolescents and young people girls and boys, as well as parents and caregivers, the program will link to the Plan International’s established Community-based CP and GBV mechanisms to ensure safe, effective feedback and complaint processes. Community Education Support Group (CESG) and parents will be engaged to help monitor, report, and refer identified protection cases within education interventions through the mechanisms and ensure timely response as required. Additionally, to address the risks and to reduce overlapping of the protection-related activities, the project will also ensure collaboration with other relevant actors and strengthen the referral linkage services.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Following age and disability inclusion strategy, this project prioritizes establishing safe, accessible, and inclusive educational environments for all children and adolescents aged 3–18, with a mandatory target to enroll at least 3% of children with disabilities. Major initiatives include ensuring safe access to community-based learning facilities with reasonable accommodations for learners with disabilities, providing specialized trainings and coaching-mentoring for facilitators, administrators, and supervisors on disability inclusion, disability screening, referral pathways, developing IEP and accessible materials etc. With the help of these initiatives, educators and other stakeholders will be able to create inclusive learning environments for people with disabilities. In order to accomplish the strategic goals, a disability-inclusive monitoring and reporting system is designed, which aims to collect and analyze data on disability using WGQ/CFM tools. This approach allows for the continuous improvement of initiatives by enabling targeted interventions that address specific barriers faced by children with disabilities. By empowering communities to play an active role, the program aims to ensure an inclusive environment that encourages the regular attendance and retention of children with disabilities. Additionally, in partnership with Humanity and Inclusion (HI) , the provision of assistive devices and inclusive learning materials will further enhance the accessibility and quality of education for these children. These collaborations provide the technical expertise needed to develop inclusive resources and facilities and ensure that all activities align with best practices in disability inclusion. Through these coordinated efforts, the program not only seeks to promote academic achievement but also to foster social inclusion for all children, creating a solid foundation for a more inclusive society.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To support the fundamental principles of the AAP (Accountability to Affected People) and to ensure accountability towards the affected people, the project will implement several key interventions throughout the project cycle. The interventions include rolling out PIB's child-friendly accountability and feedback mechanism, collection of feedback and timely response accordingly through phone calls, SMS, Community consultation, and post-distribution monitoring. Community consultations will be arranged to hear the voices of children, young people, and relevant community members. Besides, targeted communities will be informed about the feedback channels beforehand so that they know how to share their feedback. The accountability focal in each location will be trained on CFRM and will proactively engage with project beneficiaries for suggestions and feedback on the project interventions. Addressing a spectrum of pertinent issues such as stakeholder participation, access to information, capacity building, and compliance, the project team is committed to fostering appropriate practices among its members.Overall, all these interventions will serve as integral components in ensuring that the project remains responsive to the needs and concerns of the affected individuals to foster a culture of accountability, transparency, and responsible conduct within the team.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Plan International Bangladesh acknowledges the root cause of PSHEA and safeguarding incidents is an abuse of power by the perpetrator over the survivor. Mainstreaming of Safeguarding PSEA refers to integrating measures to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse into the core practices, policies, and procedures of organizations.This project aims to take a comprehensive approach to address Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming by embedding principles of safety and accountability throughout its program. Plan International Bangladesh fully committed to:• Creating a safe and inclusive culture that allows children, programme participants, staff, associates and visitors to thrive and feel secure while engaging with Plan International.• Addressing the drivers of gender inequality and unequal power relations that can lead to harm and violence, sexual harassment, sexual exploitation and sexual abuse (SHEA);• Strengthening, cultivating and sharing the capacity of all staff, associates and visitors so they understand, and are supported in meeting, their PSHEA and safeguarding roles, responsibilities, and their duty to do no harm.• Promoting child and program participant safe practices, approaches, interventions and environments which respect, recognize and respond to the specific PSEA and safeguarding needs.• Encouraging, facilitating and receiving all reports related to safeguarding or SHEA concerns.• Responding in a timely, effective, safe, comprehensive, procedurally fair and confidential manner to all PSEA and Safeguarding complaints, allegations. We have zero tolerance to inaction on safeguarding and SHEA reports. By integrating Safeguarding and PSEA Plan International Bangladesh will safeguard vulnerable groups (Children and adult program participant), improve organization's efficacy & reputation, and build a safer environment for everyone involved by incorporating safeguarding and PSEA into the organizational structures.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project integrates climate change adaptation and mitigation into the Rohingya refugee education system by incorporating Climate Action within the Myanmar Curriculum and I-ECD/ECE programs, with a focus on disaster risk reduction (DRR), disaster risk management (DRM), and building community resilience. Given the camps’ vulnerability to frequent hazards like floods, landslides, and cyclones, this initiative aims to empower learners, educators, and community members to better understand and address climate risks. Activities include developing ToT module on Climate change and educatio, Training of Trainers (ToT) on climate resilience, orient learners on child centric risk assessment, child-led climate action planning, and establishing climate corners at learning facilities to engage learners in environmental stewardship. Collaboration with local stakeholders strengthens community-wide advocacy for climate adaptation, enhancing preparedness and response at all levels. Inclusive approaches will address the specific needs of diverse groups, including gender, age, and disability, ensuring meaningful participation and promoting a positive transformation in community roles. Expected outcomes include heightened climate awareness, increased community engagement in resilience practices, and greater involvement of learners in climate-smart actions. Through this comprehensive approach, the project aims to strengthen resilience and equips the Rohingya community to face climate-related challenges more effectively.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, Plan International’s Education in Emergencies (EiE) program in the Cox's Bazar Rohingya context has embedded comprehensive Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures. The program includes a Business Continuity Plan (BCP) to ensure that educational activities can be maintained or quickly restored during disruptions. It follows standardized Emergency Response Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), outlining clear roles, responsibilities, and protocols to efficiently address urgent needs. Additionally, contingency funds are in place to allow rapid response to emergencies, ensuring immediate support for critical educational services. These EPR measures enable the program to adapt swiftly, safeguarding children's right to education in a fragile, crisis-prone environment.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project will reach 8,642 (15-18) youth under education sector to support them with Functional Literacy and Numeracy along with Life skill sessions and create enabling environment considering gender equality in their community through different activities throughout the project period.Besides, Functional literacy and Numeracy (FLN) intervention focuses on adolescent girls and boys aged between 15 and 24 who drop out at early grade or have never been engaged in any educational program. The core purpose of the educational program is to develop the functional literacy and numeracy skills of learners as well as the capacity building of staff and facilitators who work directly with this age group. The project also aims to support youths in developing socio-emotional skills and improving awareness of social issues. Awareness sessions cover various topics such as inclusive education, child marriage, safeguarding, the importance of education, child labor, human trafficking, etc. Through these sessions, the projects create awareness among learners which helps them play crucial roles in cascading these messages among their friends and family as well as the community. To support youths in organizing different community engagement activities, there are life skills sessions to develop essential life skills such as communication, leadership, interpersonal skills, creative thinking etc. These activities cover sports events, social work, community awareness-raising campaigns, and household visits. Besides, Projects have co-curricular initiatives to engage youths in thought-provoking activities on different themes. Youths participate in drawing competitions and design LC magazines on diverse issues like drug addiction, clean community, and green society to make them conscious and responsible for addressing these issues.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, Plan International’s Education in Emergencies (EiE) program in the Cox's Bazar Rohingya context actively promotes self-reliance and resilience among refugees. Through the program, children and adolescents receive life skills and vocational training, equipping them with practical abilities that enhance their ability to support themselves and their families. PIB’s EIE programs integrates resilience-building elements, such as problem-solving, critical thinking, and conflict resolution, to help learners cope with the challenges of displacement. Additionally, the program engages parents and community members in awareness sessions, fostering a supportive environment where education becomes a cornerstone for resilience and future opportunities. These initiatives collectively contribute to creating a more empowered refugee community capable of adapting to ongoing challenges.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, Plan International’s Education in Emergencies (EiE) program in the Cox's Bazar host community aligns with local development priorities and district-level plans. Supporting the District Development and Growth Priorities (Dec 2022), the program enhances educational access and quality for local children, addresses learning gaps, and promotes skills for sustainable development. In collaboration with local authorities, including Government Primary Schools, it provides infrastructure support and essential materials to host community schools. Functional literacy and numeracy training for adolescents and youth further enable participation in livelihood and vocational programs, strengthening economic resilience and reducing dependence on aid. Aligned with district goals, the program builds educational capacity and infrastructure, benefiting both refugees and locals. ECD programs will help early learners to engage in primary education. PIB will also support building GPS infrastructure.
