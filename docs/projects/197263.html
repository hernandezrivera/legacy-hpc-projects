Provide life-saving and life-sustaining NFI support to cover the needs among conflict-affected households (HHs) living in Northwest Syria (NWS)

Basic Info

Project Name

Provide life-saving and life-sustaining NFI support to cover the needs among conflict-affected households (HHs) living in Northwest Syria (NWS)

Start Date

01/01/2023

End Date

31/12/2023

Project Summary

The project's purpose will be achieved by distributing NFI kits and one-off cash for winter assistance to the most vulnerable HHs in NWS. The intervention will improve the availability of core and seasonal non-food items and enable current and newly displaced HHs and vulnerable communities to meet their immediate non-food needs with dignity and flexibility, and will further support the overall intervention goal by addressing life-saving needs.
SR will provide 25,000 displaced individuals (5,000 HHs) across northwest Syria with in-kind NFI kits and 50,000 individuals (10,000 HHs) with one-off cash for winter assistance 150$. Through the cash for winter assistance, beneficiaries will have the flexibility to choose the items that meet their basic emergency non-food items needs with dignity from local markets. The harmonized transfer value for the cash for winter to these most vulnerable HHs is determined based on the recent SNFI cluster recommendation where any changes in the design and the exact MPCA value will be reconfirmed based on monthly REACH updates and reflected in REACH price monitoring reports.
The assistance will be provided to the most vulnerable HHs in NWS, it will be distributed according to a process of registration and verification using a scoring system.

3 - Needs

According to HNO, Shelter and NFI sectors, and with the security environment remaining somewhat volatile, deterioration in economic conditions, compounded by a depreciating Syrian pound, has led to further intensification of the severity of the need for NFI support. As such, the overall NFI PiN has increased by 5 per cent to 5.7 million (from 4.90 million in 2022). Winter clothing, mattresses and blankets have witnessed a significant increase in inaccessibility in 2022 when compared to the previous year. In particular, clothing expenditure ranks as the highest monthly SNFI expenditure, i.e. three times more than rent and four times more than electricity. Such items are crucial to ensure the well-being of the most vulnerable, particularly during harsh winter seasons when access to basic NFIs is considered lifesaving. Nearly 85 per cent of the overall population reported a deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs.30 per cent of the population rely on either communal or private generators, exposing households to highfuel costs and driving a need for quality winter clothing. Consequently, basic items are further out of reachas families’ average monthly income ‘v’ expenditure gap reaches 60 per cent. The number of people needing NFI support is expected to increase further in 2023, with a projected worsening of the economicsituation.

4 - Implementation of activities

SR will provide 25,000 displaced individuals (5,000 HHs) across northwest Syria with in-kind NFI kits and 50,000 individuals (10,000 HHs) with one-off seasonal cash for winter assistance of 150$. Through cash for winter assistance, beneficiaries will have the flexibility to choose the items that meet their basic emergency non-food needs with dignity from local markets. The harmonized transfer value for the cash for winter to these most vulnerable HHs is determined based on the recent SNFI cluster recommendations, where any changes in the design and the exact cash for winter value will be reconfirmed based on monthly REACH updates and reflected in REACH price monitoring reports.NFIs will be provided to the most vulnerable HHs in NWS; they will be distributed according to a process of registration and verification using a scoring system.SR organization will follow its standard operating procedures and policies to implement the proposed activities; SR field team will register all the families and implement a verification process to ensure all potential beneficiaries are in line with SR eligibility and vulnerability criteria. In addition, all recruitment and procurement processes will be according to SR policies that will ensure transparency and equal opportunity for all project stakeholders. SR MEAL team will be involved in all the response stages, from registration mentoring activities and beneficiaries verification to baseline/endline surveys and PDMs reports.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

SR will do many actions to mainstream protection and gender across all project stages as following: SR conducted a rapid needs assessment by using Focus Group Discussions (FGD) with males and females separately as well as the most vulnerable groups such as disabled and PLW to ensure the program design meets the exact need for beneficiaries to avoid doing harm in the implementation stages. Female staff will be hired for distribution/redemption and MEAL to ensure equal access for all beneficiary categories, including females headed of household, girls and widowed. Beneficiaries will be clearly informed that all distributed value-based vouchers are free and that cash distributed is provided with no obligations on their end. Options for home delivery of the needed food commodities for vouchers which will be redeemed on behalf of vulnerable persons (e.g., PWD who cannot access the markets, elderly, etc.) confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system will be designed and beneficiaries will be clearly informed where to refer or report incidents or violations. The day, time, and location of the distribution of the vouchers will be discussed with different community members and leaders to determine the safest most accessible location to allow all individuals including women, children, and the elderly, to access the service with dignity and without exposure to further risk of harm.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risks: 1-SEA risk against beneficiaries especially women 2-GBV Risk against beneficiaries 3-Not equal access for services from marginalized groups such as disabled and women (Bias and discrimination in distributions process) Mitigating Measures: All staff and vendors will be trained, acknowledge, and sign a code of conduct for sexual exploitation and abuse, moreover, SR has zero-tolerance against SEA. PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (PSEA posters will be distributed to beneficiaries, PSEA banners will be placed at the distribution site, PSEA messaging/videos and hotline information will be shared with beneficiaries through SMS/WhatsApp). All project staff will be trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies, to ensure full adherence with the Do No Harm principle and to stress that PSEA and GBV issues will be dealt with firmly and expeditiously. Female staff will always be present at the distribution/ redemption places and involved in the distribution process All markets populated by beneficiaries will have access to educational posters/banners for the appropriate use. Ensuring safe and equal access to inclusive, accessible, and non-discriminatory list of markets to get their food assistance. Protection cases including GBV will be referred to the respective focal point NGOs in the respective locations and sensitive data will be handled and kept confidentially with the PSEA field FP.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Operational R: A-Markets are not functioning which may affect the voucher redemption, Likelihood: M, Impact: H B-Exchange rate fluctuations between USD and TRY and SYP which affects the price in NWS, Likelihood: H, Impact: M C-Lockdown due to Spread of C19 pandemic in NWS. Likelihood: M, Impact: H. Mitigation measures: 1-Regular price monitoring and markets assessment to ensure this intervention will not cause harm to the local markets 2- Monitor REACH Report for SMEB price monitoring 3- All voucher value will be loaded in USD and all contracts with service providers will be signed in USD 4- SR will apply precautionary measures which are in the FSL guidance about COVID-19 as well as WHO recommendations for staff and contractors in terms of hygiene practices, use PPE, and maintain physical distance for safety. Security R: A-General daily security threats including robbery and looting Likelihood: M, Impact: M B- Macro level conflict related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors, shifting alliances, border closure, and chemical attack. Likelihood: H, Impact: H. Mitigation measures: 1. SR will use vouchers modality so the looting will not an issue 2. Following SR safety and security guidelines and established safety protocols. 3. If the project location is no longer accessible, SR has plan B to shift the allocated quantity to other locations
