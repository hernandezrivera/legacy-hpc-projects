Project Summary

Rationale:
8 years of conflict in Syria has not only led to destruction and reduced functionality of infrastructure, but also affected socio-economic patterns, and diminished Syria’s population’s ability to sustain living standards, to benefit from decent and gainful livelihood opportunities and to access essential services. Consequently, protraction of this situation has led to a further strain on social cohesion and community security, stressing the Social Capital of Syrian communities.
Needs assessments through various sources indicate that currently more than 8.6 million Syrians are in acute need of Early Recovery and Livelihood support.
Based on the humanitarian needs analysis done by the ERL sector, needs have been categorized under the three interrelated components: infrastructure and basic services; livelihood and community security.
Through the area-based approach, UN-Habitat aims to provide sustainable housing, restoration of essential infrastructure and access to services to displaced-, host- and returnee- populations in selected neighborhoods in Daraa, Deir-Ezzor, Homs, Rural Damascus and Aleppo. In practice, the approach consists in rehabilitation and reinforcement of repairable buildings and infrastructure, restoration of basic services and promotion of livelihood activities in damage-affected neighborhoods, selected through ‘Neighborhood Action Plans”, developed through the municipality-led MTOS (Municipal Technical Office Support) program. The holistic notion to serve entire conflict-affected, vulnerable communities through a comprehensive package of housing, essential infrastructure and related services, as well as early recovery and livelihood under the objective to revive neighborhoods, is a t the core of this project.
Objectives:
This area-based livelihood project, derived from experience gained in previous pilot projects and evaluation of similar interventions, aims to:
• Restore essential infrastructure and services, promote the rehabilitation of living space, key factors in reviving affected neighborhoods and contributing to improve stability in areas,
• Provide productive assets in addition to shops rehabilitation in neighborhoods, to contribute in reviving local economies and to support residents and returnees to rebuild their life,
• Enhance security in communities through provision of street lighting,
• Involve the community members and main stakeholders in identifying community-based needs, planning of suitable response options and implementation of activities, thus to promote neighborhood ownership
• Enhance municipalities capacity to assess, plan and implement adequate programs to respond to communities’ most urgent needs, thus to reinforce local coping capacity.
Project components:
The project targets 250,000 vulnerable returnees, IDPs and Host families in Daraa, Deir-Ezzor, Homs, Rural Damascus and Aleppo. Planned and implemented through neighbourhood action plans developed through MTOS (Municipal Technical Office Support) programs, its main components comprise
• Procurement and installation of solar street lighting systems,
• Public – space-, sidewalks- and road- rehabilitation, implemented to the best possible extent through local workforce, especially unemployed youth; also, to make places available to promote social cohesion between IDPs/refugees/ returnees and host communities.
• Rehabilitation of market areas and shops, including support for productive assets and employment of local workforce,
• Capacitating municipal staff to improve service delivery at the municipality- and neighbourhood- level through training workshops and provision of technical equipment to support the technical services, improve the data collection, analysis, planning and monitoring.
All activities will be carried out in consideration of environmental- and aspects of protection of cultural heritage

Needs

The ERL Sector analysis of humanitarian needs is categorized in three inter-related themes: Infrastructure and basic services: Estimated 3.4 million people need humanitarian assistance to make their houses habitable, to restore accessibility and safety in areas, especially newly accessible, e.g. through debris clearance, and to restore functionality and performance of basic WASH-, electricity- infrastructure and related services. In addition, health- and education infrastructure and public space, is in critical need of attention to provide essential services and to facilitate socio-economic recovery not only for 6.5 million internally displaced persons, but also for host communities and spontaneous returnees. Livelihood: According to sector estimates, more than half of 4.9 million potential members of the workforce (between 18 and 65 years of age) lack access to sustained employment, a fact exacerbated by severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills and working capital. One of the consequences is high likelihood of youth to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as joining extremist organizations, crime, trafficking etc. Social cohesion and community security: Prolonged conflict impacts the social fabric and equally affects host families and IDPs. This situation is likely to aggravate due to returnees adding strain to already overburdened neighbourhoods and communities. Selection of locations and activities is based on the criteria for projects to be included in HRP, besides on criteria of:• Being part of Government / UN Habitat MTOS 2019 Program• Activity lines and locations already funded through donor commitments or with expressed donor interest • High Number of people in need, vulnerable, and expected returnees in target locations. Implementation of activities

In the governorates and sub-districts mentioned above, the project will implement the following activity lines: 1. Restoration of and access to basic infrastructure: • Cleaning the neighbourhood from debris and waste and sterilizing the roads and streets;• Rehabilitation of roads and footbaths;• Rehabilitation of public spaces• Provide streetlighting • Creating a learning open spaces for women, youth and children to conduct awareness campaigns and sessions. 2. Restoration of markets, shops and provision of productive assets and livelihood opportunities: • Market rehabilitation;• Workshops rehabilitation (repaired the shutters doors, connect shops to critical infrastructure (water, electricity, and sewage); 3. Improve effective local public services delivery: • Building the capacity of municipality staff through the implementing the MTOS (Municipality Technical Office Support) programme to strengthen service delivery at municipality and neighbourhood level. • Provision of technical equipment to for data collection, analysis planning and monitoring in the municipalities; • Conducting training workshops for community representatives; and • Refurbishment of municipal services premises and workspace. Following the respective ministries’ procedural requirements, projects will be implemented through local resources – communities, municipal technical departments, private sector entities, and workforce engaged from affected populations, in particular unemployed youth, as well as NGOs, in areas and thematic sectors identified through municipality-led MTOS processes. As much as possible, implementation of this project will be linked to other sectorial projects in the same target areas.
