Project Summary

The 2022 HNO identifies 2.4 million IDPs, returnees and vulnerable residents as having shelter and NFI needs, driven by the multitude of conflict-related, ecological, and economic factors, further exacerbated by the real threat of COVID-19. The sector will target 1.3 million people with most severe needs (56% of the PiN) in 77 localities across Darfur, Kordofan, Blue Nile and flood-affected states, by delivering timely emergency assistance to newly displaced as well as addressing mid to longer-term needs of returnees and communities living in protracted displacement, through the following two objectives:
• Provide lifesaving NFI assistance to crisis affected populations
• Enable crisis affected populations to access adequate shelter solutions that provide protection, safety, security, and space to live in a dignified manner
Immediate emergency response will continue to be critical in light of the deteriorating security and economic situation and recurrent flooding events that drive displacement and will require country-wide prepositioning of contingency stocks of non-food items and emergency shelter kits.
UNDP is ensuring that displaced, hosting communities and vulnerable population have access to the standard household NFI kit, other NFIs and and clean energy services which will ensure livelihood and protection in an emergency setting. The main output of the project is to provide NFIs and install Solar Photovoltaic (PV) systems in different community services centers (provide “schools, health clinics, streets, and women centers) besides providing solar cookers (to reduce dependence on firewood and cutting of trees, but also to reduce women’s vulnerability and exposure to harassment usually associated with firewood collection in locations far away from residential areas), solar lantern and solar streetlights for installation in communities hosting displaced populations (lighting for vulnerable/displaced populations at night provides protection of civilians in terms of minimizing the risks of harassment, robbery, etc.), solar mobile chargers to secure communication channels and generate nominal income even during emergency situations, and other relevant solar items.
In general, this sector will focus on serving small-scale settlements’ development and maintenance activities to improve accessibility, access to essential energy services, and increase resilience to hazards which will benefit both displaced and surrounding hosting populations, decrease the strain on already limited resources as well as support peaceful coexistence and contribute to overall peacebuilding efforts.

1 - Needs

New displacement has occurred in West Darfur and eastern Sudan due to inter-communal conflictamong the Bani Amer and Nuba tribes. New IDPs that were previously not known to the sectorwere identified as a result of assessments that reached new areas including the areas that armed factions control. The macro-economic instability continues to be a driver of deteriorating humanitarian needs, exacerbated by COVID-19, and heighten pre-chronic vulnerabilities. In 2021, flooding affected large areas across the country again. Heavy rains caused damage to houses, livestock, farmland, infrastructure, and services. There is a need for durable solutions (return and urban integration) for IDPs, and returneesalike including a need to secure access to clean and renewable energy, together with community training on building techniques and livelihood opportunities to promote self-reliance and resilience, while ensuring the availability of basic services in their area of choice. These supports help restore a minimal sense of dignity and protection against exposure to the elements, mitigate health risks and provide some privacy and security to those in need. The standard household NFI kit and other NFIs such as working equipment kits combined with “Cash for Work” approach will act as a stabilizing influence and allow displaced and vulnerable populations to recover from the crises and resume their family and community life by providing them with safety, protection, financial means, health risks and other factors that could increase their vulnerability. Displaced women and girls are particularly vulnerable without secure environment, basic equipment and resources for survival and maintaining their dignity. Addressing gender specific needs and capacities ensures an adequate standard of living for women and girls. Camps and settlement areas need adequate lighting to improve the quality of life and provide protection. Solar lanterns and streetlights can reduce crime and improve security at night. Based on the sectors' detailed analysis of key indicators, an estimated 58 most vulnerable localities will have acute needs that are either extreme or catastrophic (severity 4 or 5). The ongoing economic crisis may worsen the impact of emergency situations and the sector may need to increase the number of beneficiaries, as people’s loss of purchasing power reduces their ability to cope and adapt.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Provision of standardized household NFI kits and solar items to access clean and sustainable energy: Intervention will focus on the provision of standardized household NFI kits (Blankets, Jerry Cans, Mosquito Nets, Plastic Sheeting, Sleeping Mats, Solar Lamps, Kitchen Sets, and Scouring Pad) and various types of solar item to displaced and vulnerable population. Solar Photovoltaic (PV) systems will be installed in different community services centers (schools, health clinics, streets, women centers, etc.). Solar streets lights will also be provided to areas where beneficiaries are prone to protection risks during night periods. This will help to illuminate the households and areas for the protection and safe movement of women and children in particular. Solar mobile chargers with basic mobile phones will secure communication channels and help generate nominal income even during emergency situations. The fabrication and distribution of solar cooker (which relies 100% on solar radiation) with cooking utensils to displaced and vulnerable population to replace inefficient 3-stone stoves for preparation of household meals but will also train individual displaced persons on how to fabricate, use and maintain these solar stoves using locally available materials. The provision of solar stoves will reduce the time spent by women and children to collect firewood, but also reduces the protection risks associated with firewood collection. Awareness and sensitization initiatives on the sustainable use of forest resources and environmental protection will also be carried out. This will increase the awareness level of displaced population on the sustainable use of forest resources. 2. Provision of working equipment kit along with “Cash for Work” modality:Where applicable, cash for work arrangement will be pursued mainly for vulnerable youth and women with provision of working equipment kit (wheelbarrow, shovel, spade, claw hammer, hand saw, safety boots and gloves, florescent jackets and safety helmets, raincoat, umbrella, hazards marking tape, First Aid Kit, donkey cart). 21,200 households (suppose 1 household have 5 people) is given USD 300 package of standard household NFI kit and solar cooker (21200*300=6.4 mln) plus each community is given USD 30,000 valued community PV, solar streetlight, solar charger, working equipment in cash for work modality, and community cooker (in total, 30000*58 communities=1.8mln).

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNDP’s various assessments and studies revealed that unsustainably harvesing and ravaging local forests and other natural resources were among the coping mechanisms of the displaced and vulnerable members of communities. Actions such as firewood collection, use of burnt bricks for housing, collection of wild fruits, uncontrolled clearing and burning of farm sites, improper disposal of refuse, to name but a few were among the environmental hazards practiced in the proposed localities. The proposed interventions will incorporate environmental safeguards in all aspects of the project activities to reduce vulnerability to climate change-induced risks. Interventions will seek to promote improved natural resources management, efficient energy utilization and environmental protection through training on NRM and community environmental action plan (CEAP) development and implementation. Proper refuse disposal will be promoted among the target groups, for example the creation and strengthening of waste disposal committees, waste collectors identification and use of disposal sites will be used as a livelihood activity. The use of solar stoves will be promoted especially among women who are frequent users of natural and forest resources. Soil and water conservation techniques such as minimum tillage, controlled burning of farm sites, mulching, the use of organic manure for backyard gardening, etc. will be promoted.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will contribute to Sudan’s protection environment by conducting awareness raising trainings and campaigns on civil, social and economic rights of displaced population to increase self-reliance and opportunities for solutions. Focus will be on awareness and sensitization initiatives on basic rights with local authorities, host communities and service providers to reduce social tension and promote social cohesion. Capacity building trainings of community-based committees/organizations, beneficiaries and key community members will include topics with focus on human rights, displaced population's rights, gender equity, rights of people living with disabilities among others. UNDP has adopted the strategic plan (2022-2025) which aligns with the 2030 agenda and SDGs. The principle of equity and or “leaving no one behind”, targeting the most vulnerable and most in need with all its interventions and proection. Targeting criteria will be used throughout to ensure the most vulnerable are targeted. Protection specific activities have been incorporated in planned activities. The project will ensure vulnerable people have access to project assistance in addition to ensuring the use of a well-defined selection/identification criteria of beneficiaries for project assistance. The project will also ensure to share information on protection issues that the project will come across with humanitarian and protection partners working in the state. The project will also ensure stronger coordination with other UN agencies working in the same geographical areas to avoid missed opportunities for integrating protection information in displaced population's profiling for project assistance. The project will seek for cooperation with NGOs, civil society and community-based organizations on any protection issues that will be countered during project implementation.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

UNDP will work with other humanitarian actors on the implementation of this project, in particular with IOM. Since the provision of NFI/solar items needs to be closely aligned with the provision of shelter and coordinated with the Durable Solution, synergies between UNDP and actors in shelter and durable solution should be sought as much as possible. The proposed project will incorporate a participatory approach to ensure target populations and communities can participate in needs assessment, planning, and implementation. Accountability will be ensured among all stakeholders including government, host communities, displaced populations, and other vulnerable groups. The project will establish community management committees represented by all groups including youth, women, persons with disability, etc. and responsible for coordinating and monitoring project implementation and sustaining the project outputs.
