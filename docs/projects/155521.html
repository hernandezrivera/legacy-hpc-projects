Needs

The big waves of CAR refugees to Cameroon were in 2003, 2008, 2013, 2015 and recently in 2017 but the minor influxes have continued. Around 90% of the CAR refugees have settled in the East, North and Adamaoua regions, and the East region has hosted the most refugees (about 50%) on Cameroon soil and has suffered the most sociodemographic pressure with about 25% of its current population that is refugees (one in four inhabitants is refugees). CAR refugees have stayed in Cameroon relatively (old CAR refugees have stayed for more than ten years) but many CAR refugees (65-80% of households) still depend on food distribution. The Eastern region has been experiencing tensions between herders and farmers before the CAR refugee crisis and several and massive influxes of CAR refugees to Cameroon have further fragilized social issues in the East region. 70% of CAR refugees have stayed in host communities and they need to share limited infrastructures, natural resources (e.g. land, wood, water) and economic opportunities.Therefore, CAR refugees and vulnerable host communities need to be economically empowered and social cohesion between them needs to be strengthened.
