Project Summary

CDHI is planning to implement a 12-month integrated Nutrition lifesaving emergency response in crisis affected communities of Michika, Nangere and Tarmuwa LGAs of Adamawa and Yobe state respectively. This action will provide life-saving nutrition services while also preventing malnutrition and addressing its root causes. The planned activities will leverage the existing gains made by CDHI in addressing malnutrition and water access issues. CDHI will provide comprehensive CMAM package interventions both at facility and community levels.
The project will use existing government system - PHCs at community level to deliver treatment services to nutritional vulnerable groups identified with uncomplicated severe acute malnutrition SAM through outpatient therapeutic feeding programme. In locations that are hardly accessible due to insecurity and infrastructural deficit, Identified SAM children will receive treatment through the outreach approach. MAM pregnant and Lactating Women - PLWs and children 6-59 months identified using MUAC screening, will receive treatment using Tom Brown at the community level. The MAM management using Tom Brown model will be implemented in close collaboration with the OTP programme and appropriately linked with other health and nutrition services such as MNP supplementation, routine immunizations, and medications and with appropriate complementary referral mechanism between both programmes in place. Both organizations will strengthen the referral of SAM children with complications to stabilization center through the facilitation of transportation for timely access to services to prevent mortality.
Community based promotion of maternal, infant, and young child feeding - MIYCF will be integrated into the nutrition service package both at community and facility level, to ensure that malnutrition prevention is an integral component of service delivery. Similarly, children 6-23 months will receive micro-nutrient supplementation The SBCC activities will focus on improving behaviors on MIYCF and increased demand for consumption of nutrient dense locally available food and integrated with WaSH and hygiene promotion. This project will adapt innovative ideas to facilitate disability inclusion, localization, accountability, and sustainability. During the implementation of the project CDHI will sure that people with disability appropriately participate in the project as they will be given priority in households targeted for nutrition intervention, selection in management committees and meaningful involvement in all project activities. To further ensure sustainability, this action will involve key stakeholders and both local and state level to ensure buy in and participation. Capacity of select locals will be built to facilitate continuous step-down of key messages even after this action for continuous impact

1 - Needs

The IPC acute malnutrition (May 2022 – April 2023) published on 23 November 2023, it is projected that nearly 6 million children aged 0-59 months have likely been suffering and will likely continue to suffer from acute malnutrition in the period of May 2022 – April 2023 in Northwest and Northeast Nigeria. This includes 1,623,130 Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases and 4,308,404 Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) cases. In addition, 511,890 pregnant and lactating women will likely be acutely malnourished and in need of nutrition interventions.Contextual analysis of contributing factors to acute malnutrition in the IPC analysis implies strong impact of WASH in malnutrition. Shortages of WASH supplies such as soap were reported across locations, especially in cholera hotspots that have been identified as priority locations for preparedness measures. Many borehole facilities require disinfection and rehabilitation ahead of the rainy season. The WASH sector is coordinating amongst agencies that have been supported by the Nigeria Humanitarian Fund (NHF) while advocating for additional funding support to scale-up rehabilitation and disinfection activities (Adamawa SitRep) Women and children continue to be at the receiving end of this crisis, given their vulnerabilities aggravated by prolonged socio-cultural gender inequality dynamics that continued to put this vulnerable group at the disadvantage side. Government of Nigeria at all levels continue to identify the importance of strengthening maternal and infant and young child health and nutrition as it is evidently the bedrock for national development. The Borno state 25 years development plan and Yobe state social protection policy have all prioritize women and girls’ health and nutrition as the entry point for sustainable development.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CDHI employ the usage of a Gender-sensitive and Inclusive programming framework, addressing exclusion in target populations, impacting health behaviours and service access. CDHI Uses GESI frameworks/tools, integrating gender/power analysis to understand social norms, power dynamics and barriers to continually adapt our strategies and activities. CDHI will promote inclusion and participation in decision making for People with Disabilities (PWD). This aims to minimize unintended negative effects/ do-no-harm. We tailor activities to respond to populations’ specific needs, disaggregating/analyzing data by gender, age, sex, disability. Specific gender mainstreaming strategies will be employed from planning to delivery. A critical Protection monitoring, and community-based protection mechanism will be employed to support the identification and referral of the most vulnerable individuals. Continuous monitoring would be ensured, and assessment would be conducted to identify the cultural norms and provide ways in which the program will achieve effective results. Information will be disseminated timely and appropriately to enable informed decisions. Protection team will work alongside the MEAL team to come up with approaches on reporting, developing specific banners/messages on PSEA and Protection for easy dissemination of information. Continuous training of staff on PSEA, Safeguarding and the Code of Conduct would be provided to prevent occurrences of abuse. Downward accountability/participatory monitoring will be integrated in the M&E framework, collecting data/feedback. Special attention will be paid to the protection of women, those with disability, adolescent, and children during the implementation of this project. In the setting up of any committee, gender balance will be ensured to promote the meaningful and safe participation, leadership and actions of women and respond to the unique needs of diverse groups. Mainstreaming strategies would be incorporated throughout the life span of project activities by ensuring the rights of beneficiaries are upheld. We will integrate in the sensitization messages mechanism to report issues related to sexual exploitation and Abuse. Those systems and mechanism will be made available to the communities. Community members will also have the complaint box, and a dedicated community level help desk which could be utilized in raising concerns of SEA or suggestions on how to prevent SEA.
