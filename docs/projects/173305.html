Project Summary

This project will prioritize the needs of women and adolescent girls by targeting the practical issues of addressing GBV by using the existing 6 WGSS (another one is proposed) at 5 camps, where women and girls will be able to access first level PSS, referral coordinated survivor centered case management, MHPSS services and skills development. 23 Community Cooking and Learning Center (CCLC) will also be used for providing orientation on GBV issues to women and adolescent girls on menstrual hygiene management. Women including those living with disability will be encouraged to get those services of WV with partnership of CBM and CDD. Men and boys will engage with different activities and transformational change approaches like, Male Engagement and faith leader engagement models to reduce GBV and encourage them to support women and girls to seek GBV services. The 10 People’s Corner will be used men and boys and other community people engagement activities. Empowering the existing community leadership structures, local CSO, Govt. stakeholders through trainings and GBV sensitization in the camps and host communities, who will be sustained as agent of us for those communities. The project will integrate environment and disaster response and environmental hazards during the disaster and COVID-19 pandemic. GBV will be mainstreaming through GBV component and dedicated Gender and Inclusion staff among all sectors of World Vision at 32 camps and Host Communities. All staff and stakeholders will be oriented on complaints and community feedback and PSEA and those issues will be gathered on a continuous basis and responded to promptly to ensure accountability to the beneficiaries. Internal Complaint, Feedback and Response will be tracked and reported weekly basis and beneficiaries will be involved and consulted in the different stages of the project and inform to programme management, who will take decisions on program adaptation.

1 - Identified Needs

GBV is a key area of work to improve long term protection in the Bangladeshi host and Rohingya communities as there has been significant improvement in the humanitarian situation over the preceding three years to meet the immediate lifesaving needs of the population. But, protection risks and related unmet needs specially GBV remain at the core of this humanitarian response till now. Findings of a Rapid Gender Analysis 2020 conducted among the Rohingya and Bangladeshi host communities, indicate that COVID-19 pandemic, has increased gendered safety and security risks for the women, girls, and boys. Specially Rape and sexual violence has been increased rapidly in Bangladesh during COVID period. From January to October 2020 total 1349 women and girls were raped (277 gang raped) and 271 were attempt to rape. 46 women and girls were killed after rape and13 committed suicide after rape, 180 has been harassed and 14 committed suicide being harassed, Moreover, According to the Violence Against Women (VAW) Survey , 73% of married women in Bangladesh have experienced some form of violence by their current husband; with, 55% experiencing some form of violence in the 12 months prior to the survey, and 50% reporting physical violence in their lifetime. Recent assessment findings show that violence, petty crime, bullying, harassment, abduction, inadequate lighting in latrines and bathing facilities are among the reasons mentioned by females for feeling unsafe in certain locations in their neighborhoods. In addition, 21% of assessed households reported safety concerns being a barrier to accessing services and facilities such as health, education, and markets. Despite the presence of government run services such as hospital based One Stop Crisis Centers (OCCs), women police desks, and legal aid services, as well as GBV service entry points set up by NGOs, gaps are still in ensuring adequate coverage of safe and quality GBV services for host communities. In addition, low awareness about available services and deep-rooted socio-cultural barriers, limit the ability of survivors to access services. Access to justice for women is still of concern due to cost and long waiting times. Furthermore, due to the COVID 19 pandemic situation, GBV partners are reporting an increase in domestic violence, intimate partner violence and physical violence. The stay home restrictions have affected the livelihoods of poor families, straining household finances as most people have lost their income sources. This has increased tensions within families, worsening vulnerabilities for women, with various forms of GBV as the resultant consequence. Findings of a Rapid Gender Analysis 2020 conducted among the Rohingya and Bangladeshi host communities, indicate that COVID-19 pandemic, has increased gendered safety and security risks for the women, girls, and boys. The findings show increases in intimate partner violence (IPV), polygamy, transphobic violence, and violence against sex workers, as well as a rise in both girls and boy's child marriages. GBVIMS incidence reports of the third quarter 2020 shows, 2.2% Rape, 2.3% Sexual Assault, 49.1% Physical Assault, 1.1% Forced Marriage, 19.1% Denial of Resources, 26.1% Psychosocial/Emotional Abuse and 84% intimate partner violence. Though the actual incidents are higher than the reported cases. The increased focus on the COVID 19 pandemic, has overwhelmed the service delivery system, diverting both attention and resources away from the ever- present threat of GBV and other reproductive health needs of women and girls.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Conduct awareness raising sessions among women, girls, boys & men by using IEC & Audio-visual materials on GBV, COVID and others Emergency to enhance beneficiaries knowledge will on GBV, COVID and resilience 2. Provide age and gender sensitive case management services to at-risk women, girls, men and boys survivors of GBV in a safe and dignified manner3. Provide PFA, PSS, Referral support as per beneficiaries needs along with structured Mental Health and Psychosocial Support to at risk women, men girls and boys and adolescent survivors4. All staff and stakeholders will be oriented PSEA 5. Identify the most vulnerable beneficiaries and provide Hope Kits/ NFI/Dignity Kit to vulnerable women and girl survivors as needed as part of their recovery/risk mitigation 6. Women and women with disabilities trained and sensitized to take up community leadership7. Women and girls including people with disability will be engaged in skills training of choice at the WGSS and CCLC8. Engaged vulnerable women and girls with reusable cloth mask and menstrual pad production9. Train WV staff and facilitators on GBV contextualized guidelines addressing gaps and ensure need basis support on protection concerns within the community, different GBV aspects and referral pathways10. Identify and equip existing community based protection groups with information and materials on referral pathways for women, girls and boys needing specialized care such as MHPSS, case management, nutrition and health services11. Facilitate community dialogue sessions on GBV, gender equity, COVID, disaster community cohesion and peace12. Identify and train resource persons among faith leaders form the host communities as ToT’s in Channels of Hope (WV global model)13. Identify and conduct activity base educational session to ensure gender equitable relationships at household and community level through WV Male Engagement Model and sensitize families to support survivors of SGBV14. Calendar Event celebration for mass awareness (16 days of activism, women's day, Refugee day etc.)15. Adapt relevant IEC materials to conduct awareness session with community people on SGBV and COVID-19 prevention and response
