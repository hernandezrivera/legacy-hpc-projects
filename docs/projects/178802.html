Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to contribute towards addressing critical food insecurity aspects through enhancing access to bread and developing essential household agricultural livelihood assets support to households in Joseph,Ablin, Marata,Rami (Ehsem), Kafr Haya, Ariha, Darkosh,Al Hamia, Almilk (Ariha and Jisr-Ash-Shugur districts) in Idleb
In addition, the project will select 700 women out of 4500 targeted families with previous experience in the vegetable cultivation to be provided with agricultural / kitchen garden inputs (winter and summer vegetables) including seeds, fertilizers and basic tools.
The project will encompass four areas of humanitarian intervention which are:
1-WSA will conduct a detailed food security need assessment at household level to identify 4500 families (65% host community and 35% Internally displaced persons) with focusing on most vulnerable segments of the population including( Female-HH caring for children, elderly, pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities, family with no source of income, as well as families with the largest number of women and girls.
2-Daily free bread distribution for period of 6 months, targeting 4500 of the most vulnerable household 1 kg/day/HH This activity will be materialized through supporting one bakery in Ariha with 102 MT flour/month and associated materials for bread production (yeast, salt), as well as covering the operational cost (fuel and labor)
3.Inputs/Support for 700 farmer women (in kind and vouchers with value 70 USD/per HH) for Kitchen Gardens targeting particularly women HH.
4. Enhance Agricultural knowledge and skills of 700 women, though supporting technical training. The project will support 6 sessions to cover all phases of crop production .
According to the latest developments regarding the COVID-19, the WHO guidelines, and recommendations FSL cluster, WSA will be to provide the services by reducing the number of beneficiaries during the sessions and distribution and increase the outreach activities, taking into account the recommended prevention measures

4 - Needs

WSA conducted primary assessment in the targeted areas, . FDGs and KIIs were conducted with M, W, B and G to figure out the key priorities of food security and livelihood sector. The collected data in the communities interviewed demonstrated families are forced to ration their food and send their children to bed with empty stomachs, all because food insecurity is rampant. Low-income people in particular tended to rely on bread as the cheapest and most filling food staple. But the armed conflict led to a decline in domestic wheat production and at the same time drove a lot of people into povertyIn addition, agricultural livelihoods are under immense pressure. Many households have been forced to abandon crop and livestock production or scale back their engagement dramatically due to the unavailability or expense of key inputs (seeds, fertilizer and tools), households named the unavailability or high price of seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, and tools as their main reasons for ceasing production. Also, kitchen garden interventions are among the most suitable agricultural interventions for women, these types of interventions enhance women's economic empowerment through self- reliance. the assessment indicated that market-based management training is among the priority for women.As of February 2021, at least 12.4 million Syrians were food insecure, according to the World Food Programme WFP. This represents an alarming increase of 3.1 million compare to the previous year. The UN Food and Agriculture Organization FAO and WFP estimate that 46 percent of Syrian households have cut down on their daily food rations, and 38 percent of adults have reduced their consumption to ensure that children have enough to eat HNO, 2021.

5 - Implementation of activities

WSA will consult men, women, boys, girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities to understand their needs and preferences for location, design, and methodology of assistance. This project will be directly implemented by WSA in coordination and cooperation with the local councils of the selected target districts. The community mobilizers will be in charge of beneficiary selection from both IDPs and host community, based on the following criteria: most vulnerable households, households headed by women, household with elderly, disabled, households with sick care takers. WSA will implement full market assessment and identify traders and evaluate their capacity and reputation, to sign the MOU with selected traders. many awareness sessions will implement with traders and beneficiaries to identify vouchers and how benefices redeem it. Voucher will provide with many information (ID, code,signed of .. etc) to avoid fraud. The beneficiaries will be verified and integrate IDPs with the host community through the establishment of clear mechanisms in cooperation with the local councils to distribute bread and identify the targeted women farmers for agricultural input support and the establishment of mechanisms for monitoring and supervision by coordination with local authorities. The project will be managed by a qualified FSL technical adviser and oversee the local staff by providing technical advice and guidance.WSA will integrate gender-based violence prevention activities into project activities through the development of an action plan that will incorporate the principles of protectionAlso- WSA well raise awareness among the beneficiaries about complaints mechanisms regularly

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Conflict between the beneficiaries and non-beneficiariesTo prevent the conflict between the beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, WSA will ask the local councils/actors and the residents of these areas to nominate a community representative to represent the people in the area. WSA, in cooperation with the Community Representative will choose the beneficiaries according to specific criteria to serve this community and will adopt a clear and transparent policy in the implementation of the project. Risk 2: Security concernsWSA team will make continuous assessment on the accessible safe routes before the movement of the bread. Appropriate time of truck movement will be assessed and chosen.Risk 3: Diversion of support by the belligerent parties. WSA field team assesses any area before commencing any type of activities. If there is any probability of aid diversion in a certain area, WSA refrains from working in it. Additionally, WSA has good networks with local communities and stakeholders, which enables WSA to know about any potential risks of such type and take the necessary precautions.Risk 4: Movement restriction to basic services due to the high increase in the number of COVID cases and the high level of risk.Mitigation Measure: adopt all COVID preventive measures. Follow the guidance for FSL cluster for distribution in COVID context.Risk 5 :Persons with disabilities, the elderly, and widows have difficulty accessing to distribution points. Mitigation Measure:- Provide transportation support for them.- WSA will adopt door to door distribution modality.Assumption 1: All requested items (flour, yeast, and salt) are locally available.Assumption 2: There is no high exchange rate volatility.
