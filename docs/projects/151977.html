Needs

Libya’s current migration crisis is characterized by different migratory flows and patterns in and through an increasingly fragile and conflict-ridden country. The roots and drivers of the country crisis are complex, involving underdevelopment, state fragility, marginalization and security threats in West Africa, East Africa and the Middle East. Political insecurity and conflict in Libya has led to failing governance, social and economic inefficiencies, collapse of service provision and direct threats to the life and liberty of all populations within Libya. Economic prosperity pre-2011, porous borders, and the complex realities of the political and economic situation in Libya and other regional countries have seen Libya hosting various mixed migration flows, consisting of forced migrants, labour migrants, migrants seeking onwards travel to Europe and migrants who are long term residents of the country. Following the crisis since 2014, Libya increasingly became a transit country towards Europe. The absence of powerful central authorities, effective border control and lack of rule of law provide fertile ground for international smuggling and trafficking networks in Libya. Migrants and refugees have become another commodity to be exploited for profit. Of the three main sea routes used by refugees and irregular migrants to reach Europe, the central Mediterranean route is currently most used and numbers have been increasing since 2014. Libya is currently the most important migration gate for refugees and irregular migrants aiming to reach Europe by sea, representing over 90% of sea arrivals to Italy. Whether or not intending to move onwards, migrants and refugees transiting or remaining in Libya face particularly dire conditions and are victims of physical and mental abuse, discrimination, forced and unpaid labour, financial exploitation, gender based violence, arbitrary arrest and detention, and marginalization. As per the Libyan law which criminalises entering, exiting and staying in Libya.Children on the more are the most vulnerable, armed groups hold migrant and refugee children in an unknown number of unofficial detention centers across the country. Migrant and refugee children do not undergo any kind of formal registration and don't have access to legal process before and while being in detention. Conditions in detention are generally inhumane: severely overcrowded, without adequate access to toilets or washing facilities, food, or clean water. In several detention centers, migrants are held in large numbers in a single room without sufficient space to lie down. Outside of detention, children on the move (migrant and refugee children) has no access to basic services, undocumented, and face lots of protection gaps that they need to cope with before keeping on moving or being intercepted. In summary children on the move (migrants and refugees) are in need to PSS and specialized services (especially for the victims of violence)REACH, in the framework of a partnership with UNICEF, conducted an assessment of the profile, drivers and journey of refugee and migrant unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) who arrived in Italy in 2016 and 2017 who departed from Libya. 47% of the UASC interviewed mentioned that violence was the main driver for migration.

Activities

• Community-based Child Protection (CP) and Psychosocial Support (PSS) services. Including but not limited to focusing on PSS and case management services for vulnerable children (including children on the move, migrants and refugees children). Children on the move are a cross-sectoral focus of the UNICEF LCO country programme. All elements of the Child Protection component will also target increasing protection for this group of exceptionally vulnerable children: programmes addressed to PSS and protection in schools will be implemented in schools and non-formal education facilities attended by migrant children; community-based child protection implementation strategies will be located in conflict-affected areas and area with a high population density of migrant children; efforts to strengthen the legislative and procedural framework of justice for children will include a particular focus on procedures of migrant children, with a particular focus on alternatives to detention (transitional centers and departure and gathering UNHCR center), support to best interest determination to identify durable solutions, and cross-country coordination between UNICEF offices will ensure that repatriated migrant children are provided support on their return to their home country. • Provision of specialised child protection services to referred vulnerable children. • Raising awareness on child protection through community-based activities promoting social cohesion. The Child Protection programme will contribute with Youth and Education components to ensure the integration of the most vulnerable youth and Children (including migrant youth, including adolescents, and children associated with armed conflict) have access to life skill training and to TVET through a protective and inclusive environment.
