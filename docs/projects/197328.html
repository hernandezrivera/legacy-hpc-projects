Project Summary

Through this project, CATAI intends to work within selected locations in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe with the aim of improving access to safe, protected, and quality education for conflict-affected children. The project will facilitate access to education for vulnerable children to enable them to obtain be enrolled and retained in schools.
CATAI will complement one of the present projects it's implementing to address the following:
The provision of learning supplies, training of teachers, provision of basic education supports to conflict-affected children, children to attend classes where teachers have received PSS, training of SBMC members, and provision of temporary learning centers.

The project's result will be achieved through activity packages aimed at strengthening
the capacity of education activities at the community level and ensuring the protection of children and
adolescents. To ensure a consistent and comprehensive response.
Through the project, CATAI will have coverage across Gwoza, Ngala, and Bama In Borno state while having Mubi and Furfore, and Yola In Adamawa state, the project aims at targeting 7,000 beneficiaries.
CATAI will in achieving the target through increasing access to informal and vocational skills opportunities required to address the critical learning needs of children who have had years of disrupted learning: catch-up classes, accelerated learning programs, vocational skills training, mental health, and psychosocial support services (MHPSS). The response will prioritize the most deprived population groups, in particular, particularly children associated with arm groups. The target population will be out-of-school children. Children living in communities or displacement sites where there are few or no available education services will be prioritized.

1 - Needs

According to the Sector specific needs analysis, the Education Sector Since the start of the crisis in Borno and Adamawa States, the impact of the crisis on the education system has been severe, leaving generations of children without learning opportunities. This, in turn, increases their vulnerability. Access to education, especially for displaced children, remains a major need. IOM observed that in camps and camp-like settings, no children were attending school in 6% of sites and less than 25% of children were attending school in 25% of sites. Strengthening the education system, promoting access to inclusive quality education services, and protecting schools from attacks are key priorities in the northeast. Many schools have been damaged, destroyed, and or forced to close. In 2020, UNICEF estimated that some 1,400 schools have been damaged by the conflict across the BAY states, most of which have not been rehabilitated. In Adamawa State, only 30% of schools have sufficient learning materials, with lower proportions in Borno (26%). Only 47% of schools in Borno have adequate furniture, and 32% and 26% in Adamawa. According to UNICEF, some 2,295 teachers have been killed across the BAY states since the start of the conflict, while many others have been displaced, contributing to severe shortages of qualified teachers. According to JENA 2021, the average student-teacher ratio across the BAY states is 69:1; Borno State has the second highest (67:1) and followed by Adamawa (42:1) As a result of the protracted crisis in north-east Nigeria, IDPs and returnees – many of whom have suffered multiple displacements – have lost productive assets and livelihoods, and now rely on basic assistance from partners and the Government. With critical needs competing for meager resources, IDPs and returnees are forced to make difficult decisions and prioritize life-saving needs such as food, with little or no resources left for education. The continued displacement creates access barriers to both formal and informal education services. This has led to high dropout rates and large numbers of out-of-school children. An estimated 900,000 children have limited access to education services. Fifty-six percent of displaced children across the BAY states do not attend school. Girls are also affected by cultural barriers to education that precede the crisis; The delivery of vital education services to the most affected and vulnerable people in hard-to-reach areas is hampered by access constraints caused by frequent attacks and clashes targeting civilians and school facilities. Several roads are unsafe, and service providers and vendors are unable to reach affected areas. Additionally, road access challenges due to flooding during the rainy season often increase the cost of services such as the delivery of teaching and learning items, and school construction and rehabilitation materials.The delivery of vital education services to the most affected and vulnerable people in hard-to-reach areas is hampered by access constraints caused by frequent attacks and clashes targeting civilians and school facilities. 2 - Activities

The proposed activities under this project directly link with the allocation strategy presented to partners by OCHA/NHF and the prioritization by Education Sector. Prioritized activities: Prioritized activities under this project, will contribute positively to the protection of children• Enhancing the skills of students and teachers through the provision of PSS training • Provide basic education for conflict-affected children through accelerated lessons and classes. • Renovation and Reconstruction of temporary learning spaces or classrooms • Providing learning supplies to pupils through school in a box • Training of teachers on school room management, PSEA, and student management. • Provide training for school-based management committee members • Provide basic Education and literacy for children. CATAI will work closely by paying special to persons with disabilities. This group will be assisted through consultations with specialists and referral support. Based on sector priorities, CATAI will support school-based management committees (SBMCs) through training and capacity building, with a special focus on improving participation in school management and the myriad key issues in protection, MHPSS, risk reduction, and child safeguarding that have been identified as being prevalent in communities and school.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will work with young girls between the age of 12-18 who fall within the category of at risk groups and would require needs before, during and after the project implementation. Some of the specific vulnerabilities and needs of at risk young girls who will participate in the project implementation may include; safeguarding, supervision, mentorship, and communication. In ensuring that all specific vulnerabilities listed above are taken care of, CATAI will mainstream the principles of Conflict Sensitivity and Do No Harm throughout the project lifespan. The principles maximize the positive impact of the project while minimizing the potential negative impact. In implementing this project. Secondly, CATAI will train the project staff and facilitators on its child protection policy and ensure that all project and support staff sign-up to ensuring full compliance with the policy. Thirdly, CATAI will disseminate mechanisms for receiving whistle blower reports on the conduct of its staff. To achieve this, CATAI will ensure that all reports received are treated with utmost confidentiality while ensuring proper investigation of all complaints received and implementation of appropriate penalties. CATAI will continuously assess risks and the changing conflict dynamics at the Community, LGA and State levels. Additionally, the Best Interest of the Child (BIC) principle as articulated in Article 3 of the Convention on the Rights of the Child will be the key and core guiding principle for the project.
