Project Summary

This project aims at increase access to life-saving and life sustaining basic health services for those most in need population during humanitarian situations in Sudan, 2022.
The project is targeting Khartoum, Sennar, Blue Nile, North Darfur, West Darfur, Central Darfur, North Kordofan, West Kordofan, South Kordofan, and Red Sea.
This project will strengthen health sector capacity to prevent, prepare for and deliver timely response to population affected by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease outbreaks. This will include a) procurement, prepositioning and rational use of essential medicines, equipment and medical and laboratory supplies including PPE and strengthen capacity on rational use of drugs and supply chain management; b) building the capacity of healthcare providers and community health workers on provision of maternal, neonatal and child healthcare services, communicable and non- communicable diseases healthcare services and COVID19 prevention and vaccination services; c) expanding and maintaining disease surveillance system with early warning component for communicable and notifiable diseases and strengthening related response and information system; d) strengthening the rapid response to emergencies including setting-up emergency health teams, supporting RRTs and treatment capacity; e) improving health facility set-up to provide an acceptable quality of health services including the supply of water and power and basic needs of quality of care; f) support system for early referral of complicated cases; g) supporting community mobilization, health education and preventive messages including for COVID19.
The project implementation plan will be detailed, implemented and monitored jointly with targeted communities and ministries of health at national and state levels and local health authorities. The project will be implemented in 12 months with estimated budget at $ 13,437,576

1 - Needs

Sudan is currently going through severe and growing complex crisis, characterized by severe economic deterioration and inflation with extremely low public spending on social services, growing food insecurity, volatile political context, intercommunal conflicts across several parts of the country, refugee influxes and multiple health system related shocks including flooding, disease outbreaks including COVID19 and challenges with health system governance, financing and needed human resources. Whilst the interagency Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 is not yet published, data released so far demonstrates that the People in Need of humanitarian assistance has risen by 1.2 million from 2021 to a total of 14.6 million people in 2022 representing 55% increase from 2020 figures. Whilst there are certainly areas with higher levels of humanitarian need, the needs are chronic across country, reflecting multiple local drivers, and the impact of country-wide economic and political challenges. Several diseases are endemic in Sudan such as malaria, cholera, dengue fever, and chikungunya with a tendency to cause annual outbreaks across several states. In 2021 there have been outbreaks of Hepatitis E in 8 states and Measles reported in 4 states including among refugee population.Flood season is the peak season for water and vector-borne diseases resulting in increased need for health services. 86.7% of population classified as at high risk of malaria, and as of 03 October 2021, 13 states have malaria caseloads at epidemic levels while 11 states report typhoid caseloads at epidemic levels (Emergency Committee of the Federal Ministry of HealthThe healthcare system remains fragile and continuously stressed by the cycles of humanitarian crises including conflicts, civil unrest, floods, diseases outbreaks and deteriorating economy. The economic difficulties facing Sudan with historic levels of inflation across the country, and the effects of COVID19 pandemic have made it difficult to maintain medicine stocks across the country. During 2021, the availability of emergency medicines declined steadily to reach 43% compared to 57% during 2020. This has been especially hard on vulnerable groups including refugees. During 2021, the immunization coverage has dropped as reflected by the 8% drop in PENTA3 immunization coverage, measles immunization stands at 76% and the human resources for health availability is severely compromise (available HRH are enough to serve only 17% of the country’s population according to the WHO’s minimum standard). As of Oct 2021, the total number of COVID19 confirmed cases across the country is 40,238, with 3,099 confirmed COVID19 deaths since the first positive cases was confirmed in March 2019. Over the last few weeks, the country is witnessing the Furth surge in COVID19 cases marking the start of a new wave. Amid the current economic and political situation, the response to COVID19 pandemic continue to face lack of designated treatment facilities, health staff, necessary equipment and minimal surveillance and response capacity that’s ultimately will affect the health system’s ability to minimize the COVID19 related high fatality rate. In Sudan, 1.5 million women of reproductive age lack access to both Basic Emergency Obstetric services. Around 70% of delivers occurs at home with clinical management and psychological support to gender-based violence survivors, including clinical management of rape, is still impacted by a weak referral system and lack of qualified staff at the facility and community level. Children under five years of age are exposed to vaccine-preventable diseases due to reduced coverage of the expanded program of immunization and a high prevalence of malnutrition.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The climate crisis is an intergenerational child rights crisis, and the greatest threat to children's survival, learning and protection. Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy, and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programs and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks.Environment will be considered as an integral part of the project with a number of factors that will be implemented to improve the environmental friendliness of the health humanitarian response. This will include the coordination among different actors including communities on environmental friendly health programs; enhancing awareness raising amongst health practitioners and communities on environmental protection.Certain measures will be implemented as an integral part of this project to mitigate possible environmental effects of health interventions such as effective waste disposal, careful planning of the emergency waste disposal sites and using appropriate methods, safe and hygienic disposal of needles and other medical supplies, safe management of solid and human waste to prevent spread of communicable diseases.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all programming, Save the Children aims to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. Program design and implementation is informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics. The project will implement purposeful awareness raising for targeted beneficiaries and communities on the community level protection measures, availability of GBV services and importance of timely access to service. GBV survivors will have safe access to health facilities and health providers will be equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services. Health staff will be trained on first line GBV management and CMR will be conducted in coordination with the protection and GBV. Further, information sharing of violence, IDP movements, and GBV incidents will be shared among sectors to ensure timely response

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Stakeholders’ participation and accountability to affected people is part of SCI’s core values. In practice, our participation and accountability approaches covers the following: a) Regular, timely and accessible information sharing, b) Participation – discussing together, deciding together and working together, c) Proactively seeking feedback from children and communities and handling their complaints, d) Generate data and evidence for continuous learning. SCI is signatory to Core Humanitarian Standard, and considering this SCI Sudan has already established strong accountability mechanisms which include three critical components of information sharing, participation, and complaints and feedback for the targeted communities and children. SCI will provide regular, timely and accessible information to targeted communities about: a) Who we are and the principles we adhere to; b) Project interventions and what SCI intends to deliver (e.g. what people are entitled to receive and when); c) Our staff and how we expect our staff to behave (including our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct); d) How communities can be involved. This information will be communicated in the local language and medium that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the targeted communities, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups. To ensure accountability to the beneficiaries during the consultation phase, SCI has will consult and engage the existing community structures/bodies (traditional leaders, representatives from IDPs, returnees, refugees, host communities, youth groups, women groups, and local government authorities). During the implementation phase, SCI will ensure that targeted communities have been provided with opportunities to express their views and influence decision-making through discussing together, deciding together and working together. Particular attention will be given to the vulnerable groups to enable for active participation in the implementation of the project activities. Key stakeholders such as partners and the community (women, men, boys, and girls)
