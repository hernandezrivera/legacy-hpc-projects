Project Summary

Education:
The project will support the 9 schools (with 14 shifts) VO already oversees in the IDP camps in the sub-districts of Salqin and Maaret Tamsrin, currently supported until March 2024. continue the delivery of services to more than 3.329 students (1680 boys, 1640 girls) by covering incentives for 169 school staff (104 Males, 65 Females, 25 CWDs(10 girls)) and creating sustained, safe learning environments through the distribution of education kits, and small rehabilitation works, payment of running costs, promotion of community engagement through parent-teacher associations (PTA) and student council as well as cash assistance to families with children at risk of school drop-out. VO recognizes that successful education is rooted in alleviating protection risks and concerns for the children. VO aims at serving the same communities, families, and children with specialized protection services, incl. case management and referral to provide families facing crises with immediate financial support. The component has been designed with the active participation of the local participation, which identified mental health as one of the key needs. Protection services will be accompanied with special attention to MHPSS awareness to highlight and acknowledge positive coping mechanisms. VO will provide formal education during the academic year, and non-formal education during the summer program for the same enrolled children. VO will identify the educational needs of registered students based on their records in the supported schools, conducting ASER assessment for the newly registered girls and boys. Based on the needs analysis, VO will provide Summer catch-up or remedial classes from the SIG curriculum or basic literacy and numeracy using the BLN curriculum.
To summarize, the logic of the intervention is to provide the IDP communities in the districts of Salqin and Maaret Tamsrin with continuous humanitarian support. While we would ensure the extension of the formal education system in the schools already supported by VO, the project will secure additional support for the local communities, including protection programs.
The project reflects the priorities based on the severity scale and community feedback. The project is demand-driven, as is the result of the consultation with local stakeholders (incl. community committees), and is people-centered, as VO and the targeted IDP communities worked together to ensure a proper accountability system, according to which local communities and schools’ representatives will be constantly engaged in the monitoring activities.


The project will intervene in the strategic sectors contributing to the needs of the population. The education component will represent a solution to both short and long-term needs, as it will support continued education services in nine IDP camps, already supported by VO. Services will be expanded with protection services, targeting the same children and families and border communities to reduce school dropouts and negative coping mechanisms.
Winterization
Based on Violet's experience in the NWS, markets are accessible and offer affordable winter items. Fuel, stoves, and tanks emerged as top priorities. Additionally, the headteachers of the targeted schools reported that all schools require winterization items to enhance the learning environment. This involves providing the mentioned winterization items. VO plans the Provision of winterization to schools to ensure educational continuity in the academic year. VO will distribute the winterization items to 91 schools needing winterization. The winterization items include heating fuel for 88 days, stoves, and fuel tanks for 25,828 individuals, 11197 boys, 13331 girls, 602 male teachers, and 698 female teachers, including 385 CwDs (215 girls), to improve the learning environment in the targeted schools/learning spaces.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Needs: The HNO report outlines the sectoral overview for education noting there are currently 6.9 million people in need (education sector PIN) including children and education personnel. 15 percent of these include people with disabilities amounting to over 1 million. With increasing out of school populations, retention in schools – especially transition from non-formal to formal education remains a vital challenge. This is further exacerbated by a lack of secondary institutions and deteriorating state of existing school structures. The capacity and effectiveness of teachers across the country remains constrained. The shortfall of teachers has been reduced through the induction of temporary teachers but there is lack of continuous professional development (CPD) support. Payments to teachers are not yet regularized and involve long waits in many parts of the country. Protection Risk Analysis1) Risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if the education service is provided to a specific group, e.g. IDP Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Before implementation, Violet conducts assessments and selects most vulnerable communities based on severity; selection criteria (eligibility/vulnerability) transparently communicated through the AAP team. AAP team has access to service mapping to communicate informed with communities about alternative services. 2) Children with special needs might be excluded from education and PSS. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Ensure facilities and services are suitable for special needs, e.g. mobility or learning materials. 3) Child labor, early marriage. Conduct awareness PSEA Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Risks1. Risk; Inaccessibility to target areas. Mitigation; Violet would triangulate information with available sources, use rigorous monitoring mechanisms, suspend; relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; switch to distance learning. 2. Risk; Insufficient stipends continue to result in teachers and education personnel absenteeism. Mitigation; A standardized and harmonized stipend scale that ensures adequate remuneration should reduce turnover and absenteeism and support teachers with other sectors and resources. 3. Risk: Increased COVID-19 and cholera cases in the targeted location. Mitigation: Protection Violet staff will consider WHO and Health sector guidelines. online activities. 4. Risk; students are dropping out of school. M. Provide PSS activities and involve parents. Risk: The CP activities will be post-ponded or stopped. Mitigation: Re-coordinate with the local authorities in link with community leaders' support to clarify projects` importance and goal.
