Project Summary

The situation of shelter has worsened during 2019. The damage caused by the ongoing conflict in Syria has led to millions of homes being destroyed. Shelter and infrastructure damage have occurred on a massive scale, with the level of rebuilding and reconstruction needed which goes beyond the focus and capacity of the Shelter sector and the humanitarian response in general. Taking into consideration the needs identified in the field and in line with the Shelter sector strategy, the project aims at strengthening the resilience and cohesion of vulnerable communities by improving housing. Repair and rehabilitation of damaged houses will improve safe and decent living conditions of vulnerable conflict-affected populations (returnees and host communities) selected based on vulnerability criteria with priority to families with PLWs and/or children under 2 and under 5, elderly and disables. AHH identified vulnerable households living in poor and unsafe housing conditions. Returnees willing to return to their pre-crisis accommodation and host communities have the same needs.
Housing Land and Property (HLP) issues will be integral to the shelter sector ensuring that interventions are founded on legal and customary ownership and tenancy. The proposed project mainstreams and incorporates protection principles in the activities to promote meaningful access, safety and dignity. Indeed, the rehabilitation works will take into account the unique needs of men, women, children, elderly and disabled persons; the technical assessment and the residential unit design will be completed to ensure safety, privacy and dignity for all targeted beneﬁciaries. AAH plans to rehabilitate 325 damaged apartments in Aleppo for the benefit of 1,625 individuals. Approvals have been already granted by the Authorities.

4 - Needs

The shelter situation has continued to deteriorate due to the cumulative impact of the crisis. Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute, resulting in deterioration of living standard condition and distress for the physical and mental well-being of vulnerable households, thus affecting the protection of affected populations, while a significant portion of the population continues to experience protracted displacement. Overall, shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20% to an estimate total of 5.65 million people in need. Of this, 3.8 million (69%) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically, these areas are concentrated in Idleb, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Raqqa city and camps in the North East. Up to one million IDPs reside in last-resort informal and spontaneous settlements. Emergency shelter rehabilitation and WASH infrastructure repair to areas in reception of influxes of internally displaced people, as well as those to which people are returning in high numbers, will continue to be a critical life-saving priority. Moreover, the majority of people living in damaged shelters and houses cannot afford repairs due to the high cost while the upward trend in rent prices can be expected to further degrade the ability to make safe and adequate repairs. In many houses the structural safety is maintained but the level of destruction and looting is so extensive that requires rehabilitation works above the average cost to, for example, rebuilt completely partitions and facilities with the aim of ensuring safe, decent and dignified housing conditions. Indeed, the assessments conducted by AAH indicate that, as consequence of poor living conditions, women and girls are reporting inadequate space, loss of privacy and lack of water relating to sanitation and hygiene practices that is causing mental distress and increasing their protection risks as long as they are unable to conform to social norms.

5 - Implementation of activities

AHH will improve safe and decent housing conditions of vulnerable conflict-affected populations selected based on vulnerability criteria with priority to women-headed HHs, families with PLWs and/or children under 2 and under 5, elderly, with a member chronically ills and/or with physical/mental disability, hosting IDPs for more than 3 months, and/or with more than 7 members. The type of shelter targeted for rehabilitation works are damaged apartments (owner orientated support for returnees/ host community). The privacy and dignity of separate households will be considered during the design and implementation of the works and essential shelter needs will be covered through basic civil repairs. Housing Land and Property (HLP) issues are integral to the shelter sector. The rehabilitation works activities will take into account the unique needs of men, women, children, elderly and disabled persons; the technical assessment and the residential unit design will be completed to ensure safety, privacy and dignity for all beneﬁciaries Moreover, AAH engages in repair/rehabilitation of buildings only if the building is found structurally safe; as such, AAH works closely with local authorities, communities and agencies involved in ERW clearance. Targeted household will participate in the needs assessments and directly involved in the decision-making related to the rehabilitation. Local authorities will be also regularly involved during identification, execution and handover of works, thus monitoring the timely implementation and quality of the repairs. Overall, AAH technical teams will carry out regular onsite monitoring to supervise the quality of the works; undertake a comparison of pre/post completion to measure the effect of the intervention and ensure that the objective has been met; ensure works are done in respect of the safety and dignity of target beneficiaries. Relevant age/sex groups are considered with all data sex disaggregated throughout the project cycle.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: physical safety of beneﬁciaries/communities is at risk due to structural integrity of the building and/or the presence of ERWs in or in close proximity to potential work sites. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: structural assessment of buildings is done by qualiﬁed personnel. Repair/rehabilitation of buildings only if the building is found structurally safe. AAH works closely with local authorities, communities and agencies involved in ERW clearance; contractors/workers are aware of risks associated with the presence of ERW and safety protocols are in place. Protection risk: beneficiaries selection criteria is unclear or is inﬂuence by local authorities may contribute to nepotism, tension in the community and risk of most vulnerable being left out and/or abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). Likelihood: high. Impact: medium. Mitigation: assistance package is in line with sector standards and guidelines. AAH engages with community people (both men and women), to support in disseminating information. Feedback and complaint mechanism at community level allowing women to express their complaints. AAH staff is trained on conﬂict mitigation techniques, awareness of relevance of social structures for aid programs and neutral acceptance of AAH programs. The selection process for rehabilitation follows sector guidelines ensuring all steps are followed in a transparent and compliant way. Already established country commission to identify and act in case a staff is identiﬁed as perpetrator of abuse and exploitation. Protection risk: lack of documents to prove property/occupancy rights incl civil documentation excludes beneﬁting from shelter repair/rehabilitation works. Likelihood: high. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AAH communicate to beneﬁciaries the list of alternative documents to prove ownership and engages with the local authorities to establish a mechanism to deal with such issues at local level.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: security situation allows safe response; access to target areas and beneficiaries is granted; partners are willing to collaborate on timely manner; availability of suppliers and items in the market, prices remain stable; no major displacements occur. Risk: security situation is unstable and/or worsens, AAH has no access; there are additional administrative procedures imposed on D-INGOs, and restrictions on access to beneﬁciaries; lengthy processes to obtain approvals. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: AAH coordinates with local authorities, Ministries, and stakeholders to monitor the situation, to maintain access to target areas and beneﬁciaries, to develop relationships and avoid or mitigate restrictions. Security rules and plans are in place and regularly reviewed. Re-planning of activities to recover eventual delays. Advocacy and coordination with the humanitarian community. Risk: significant devaluation of EURO/ USD against SYP; International bank transfers are not ﬂuid and timely. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: signiﬁcant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be monitored and, if materialized, communicated to donors and options discussed. Risk: aid diversion, financial fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: strict policies and procedures in place for procurement, financial management and risk management; internal controls set up. Monthly monitoring of financial spending. Risk: markets and services are not functional for planned deliveries; procurement of equipment/materials are delayed. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: diversify procurement according to AAH guidelines. Launch framework agreements or tenders for medium time frames. Risk: any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc.). Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: compulsory trainings on organizational ethical policies. AAH mission has a policy for PSEA and a reference person against harassment at country level.
