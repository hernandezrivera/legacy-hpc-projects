Project Summary

Kutum is witnessing protracted displacement since 2011, where WASH gab is highly critical, widespread & urgent, especially due to ‎ security deterioration in the month of February 2016 serious conflict erupted in Jabal Mara. In Wash the highest priority of the new IDPs is that the WASH is poor & immediate water supply & sanitation are required. In Kutum water consumption is 5.8l/p/d, (Umlayona, Hasahba . The ‎situation of sanitation services is severely deteriorated. Dignity & gender sensitivity in emergency community latrines are rarely ‎considered. According to recent data obtained from WES (Feb 2019) there is a dire WASH needs including lack of water resources, need for water filtration,and sanitation facilities, where there is no latrines so it is an area of need. It was stated that there is a need to repair one water yard in hashaba village, and rehabilitate 6 hand pumps in um layona. On the other hand, BN is a host for IDPs, and vulnerable residents where water consumption is 3.8l/p/d. Ongoing conflict in BNS since 2011, accelerated in the first quarter of 2015. The displacement is primarily from parts of Bau, Ge Kurmuk localities due to conflict between SAF and SPLM-N. Newly displaced IDPs have moved to more secure locations, predominately Damazine, Rosaries, Giessen, and Bau localities. According to State Water Corporation (SWC)/(WES)/Nov/2018 El Kurmuk locality have a total of 632,400 people not accessing adequate sanitation services, and having a significant gaps in pit latrines coverage. There are huge WASH gaps among vulnerable IDPs and host communities showing that 80% of the targeted population practice open defecation. There are no active plans to reduce open defecation, there is lack of monitoring and follow-up plan on CATS/CLTS, no qualified trained TOT hygiene promoters, limited access to safe drinking water and no trained mason on design, plan and construction of latrines slabs. Founded on a sanitation unit capacity analysis done by UNICEF in 2018; there are 4 established sanitation and hygiene units in BN and 2 in ND none of them has the minimum number of required staff (7 staff). Consequently open defecation is a major factor to a multiplicity of water sanitation related diseases such as diarrhea, cholera and typhoid, Not only is open defecation & lack of improved sanitation facilities detrimental to human health but also to economic and social development. . And regarding access to sanitation facilities only 33% are using improved latrines. in BN there is a total of 1,676 hand pumps, 157 water yards, 5 treatment plants, and 1966 BASIC water facilities, out of which 41%, 22%, 67%, 40%, and 45% are respectively functioning (WASH 2018-2021 Strategic Plan, Nov 2018). Both localities will be needing a CLTS approach to be injected within the intervention.

2 - Activities / Outputs

rehabilitation of 12 hand pumps, in Kutum/Um layoona, and construction of 3 new HPs in BN/Alkurmuk, Jorot west, and bulang villages, rehabilitate 6 water yards one in hahsba/ND, 2 , and the other 2 in BN, besides upgrading of 2 hafirs in Bulang as recommended by WES the sanitation component will mainly target schools, and health centers, by constructing a 3 drop hole latrines in the centers, and a 4 drop holes gender sensitive latrines in abubakr alsideeg, and khadija bit khuwalid schools, and in 3 schools. the project will promote the hygiene practices through formation of new school hygiene clubs, and strengthening the already existing ones, cleaning campaigns for schools and the communities,distribution of soap, and sanitary kits for girls at schools, and women at reproductive age. FPDO will adapt CLTS approach in the 3 localities of alkurmuk, and kutum targeting 6 villages /communities. 100 hygiene promoters will be trained to undertake the house visits, and the hygiene promotion sessions (40 in ND, 40 in SK and 20 in BN). as an exit strategy and to grantee the sustainability of the provided services or construction/rehabilitation hand pups, and water yards FPDO will train 10 persons from each community on managing the water yards, and hand pumps (O & M). FPDO will implement all the above-mentioned activities is close coordination with WES, MOH, and the targeted communities, as it will also consider the gender, and environmental factors during all project's phases.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

the environmental factor will be highly considered on this intervention through using environmental friendly construction materials. and by organizing cleaning campaigns for all the targeted villages. Moreover FPDO will formulate 6 Environmental/hygiene school clubs to promote concepts of environmental conservation and preservation targeting 50 students per school. also the distribution of garbage containers in health centers, and educational institutions will directly reduce the negative impact of Territory waste disposal, beside the community led total sanitation approach that in turn will reduce the rate of open deification, as new latrines would be built, and old ones will be rehabilitated, and peoples' awareness increases through the hygiene promotion activities.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The designation of this project activities are devoted to promote gender equity in stage of planning, assessment and implementation stages. Planning incorporates separate focus group discussions with women, men, boys and girls as well as a breakdown of daily activities by gender to ensure access by key groups. FPDO will provide services in a manner which addresses any gender inequalities in the beneficiary population, without exacerbating tensions. women are at serious risk if they leave the confines of IDP camps,having a restrict access to better opportunities and best chances to ensure gender equality which will negatively effect on the community. FPDO took into account all relevant gender aspects as following: In the assessment stage FPDO collected the information from both men and women regarding their mobility patterns due to their roles and responsibilities. As women and girls are mostly affected by conflict, the project will allow more access to live saving education facilities to these group. Access to local resources and facilities will be improved significantly for men, women, boys and girls in places where FPDO works. Female teachers and girl students will be considered to enjoy WASH facilities in consultation of latrines locations in the schools. In addition FPDO will encourage women to participate fully in WASH committees and all established activities. FPDO will sensitize community through trainings and awareness sessions on the management of water points and sanitation facilities will be subject of particular attention. overall, FPDO will apply the Do No Harm approach when implementing different projects components.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

During planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation phases, FPDO will adhere to the principles of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) adopted by Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership guidelines (HAP) and Inter-agency Standard Committees (IASC). FPDO mainstreaming approach will focus on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities including men, women, boy, elderly those living with disabilities by increasing their participation through consultation, sharing the information about the project in a timely, accessible and inclusive manner. Moreover, during the implementation of project activities FPDO will ensure that people receive information relating to the project start and end dates, time and place of training, campaigns and other relevant facts. Affected population will be consulted during the whole project design steps, communication will be applied before and during the assessment stages to facilitate mission to Kutum locality. The beneficiaries will be identified with their needs clearly. As a result of this action affected population will contribute positively to the project design and will continue to participate in the implementation. Kutum education offices, Teachers and PTAs are very cooperative and welling to work hand in hand in collaboration with FPDO to implement the activities. The community is very helpful and motivated to work on facilitating access to equal gender and live saving and quality education among affected population. ‎FPDO ‎will ensure consistent two way ‎‎of communication getting the community feed back during focus group discussions (FGD) with the community. Advocacy and sensitization ‎meetings will be ‎conducted at Kutum with stakeholders (including ‎beneficiaries) and ‎community leaders and will further share the information to the communities. complaints mechanisms will be em-placed through the complaints boxes, and assigning a dedicated staff member only to receive issues and concerns reported from community members, considering people's privacy, and protection.
