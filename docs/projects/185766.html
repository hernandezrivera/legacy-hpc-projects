Project Summary

Over many decades, Rohingya refugees have been fleeing to Bangladesh from Rakhine State, Myanmar. Since the August 2017 influx, 911,359 Rohingya refugees have settled in Cox's Bazar (904,706 identified in camps and 6,653 estimated to be dispersed in host communities) in Ukhiya/Teknaf Upazila, leading to what has been defined as one of the largest and the fastest-growing refugee crisis in the world. The situation remains critical and access to basic services is still challenging especially on WASH service. The rapid and massive increase of the refugee population has also had a substantial impact on Bangladeshi host communities.
Due to the pure drinking water crisis throughout the camps, the WASH Sector will prioritize providing access to chlorinated piped water to all Rohingya refugees in the camps. Hand pump water points will be upgraded or decommissioned, in order to optimize the use of water resources and reduce public health hazards.
The proposed project will upgrade the WASH facilities such as DTW for adequate, improve the latrine and awareness on hygiene issues. The project will contribute to achieving objective-1 of section Selection 1 (Objective) and objective -2 of Selection 2 (Objective). The project will be implemented 03 camps namely camp 14 (block B and D), camp 16 (block G & D) and camp 19 (Block B, C) and one Union of Host community - Palongkhali Union. Total household coverage will be Water supply 600 HH's, Improve Latrines (150 HH's), bathing cubicles (150 HH's), hand washing (150 HH's) etc.
Activities of proposed project : (1) Upgrade of existing Water supply source especially Deep Tube Well (DTW) (12 Nos - 600 HH’s), (2) Improve existing Latrines both Host (Union : Palongkhali) and Rohingya Camps (150 Nos – 750 HH’s) (3) Upgrade existing bathing cubicles both Host and Rohingya Camps (150 Nos – 750 HH’s), (4) Improve existing hand washing facilities both Host and Rohingya Camps (150 Nos – 750 HH’s), (5) Behavior change door-to-door sessions on health and hygiene both Host and Rohingya Camps – 600 Sessions (200 Sessions/Camp) (6) Capacity building of project Staff and WASH committee members (780 HH’s), (7) Solid waste management (SWM) Operation & Maintenance (collection & dumping through cash for work) - 5000 Population, (8) Hygiene kit distribution to the Host community focusing on PWD – 1500 HH’s (9) Latrine kit distribution both Host and Rohingya Camps (150 Nos), (10) Celebrate Day Observance (World water day and Hand washing day, etc.
Beneficiary’s target : A total of 29,750 beneficiaries will be achieved by the project in which male 14,280 and female 15470. The project will provide services to both male and female of children, adolescents, youth, adults and older. It will special care for PWD and also children.

1 - Identified Needs

The Sector maintains that there are 50,711 functional latrines and 893,551 out of the 1.05 million (85%) in need of immediate WASH support are benefitting from safe water to agreed standards (ISCG Situation Report, Apr 2019 and WASH Sector Situation Report, Jan 2019). However, water quality issues, highly uneven coverage and lack of standardization of inclusion, gender and vulnerably-based design restrict access to safe WASH services. On the other hand, the total number of functional latrines within a 100m radius of fecal sludge management (FSM) is very low to the WASH cluster. Hygiene practices remain low and the coverage of hygiene promoters, particularly women and persons with disabilities. According to solid waste management in camps have become a growing concern, with most refugees dumping waste in drainages, clogging them further. The top problems related to the bathing facility are the lack of separate units for men and women. According to JRP 2021, the WASH Sector will prioritize providing access to chlorinated piped water to all Rohingya refugees in the camps. Hand pump water points will be upgraded or decommissioned, in order to optimize the use of water resources and reduce public health hazards. Fecal sludge treatment facilities will be upgraded to improve efficiency and coverage and minimize maintenance costs. The Sector will strengthen solid waste management systems, including through the establishment of safely-managed disposal sites, promotion of waste segregation, safe disposal and recycling, and composting of waste, where possible. The Sector will strive to harmonize solid waste management systems and engage partners in sustainable gender-responsive strategies by increasing female participation and engagement. Host Community WASH situation reported that 23% of Drinking, 22% for Cooking, 25% of Personal hygiene at the bathing location, 25% of Other domestic purposes households and 35% of At least one purpose reporting not having had enough water at the time of data collection. (Ref : J-MSNA : KEY FINDINGS -Host Community WASH). According to JRP 2021, the WASH target has not been achieved and still now needs to be provided the same requirement. So the project has been considered based on the progress and achievement of the JRP 2021 statement and progress as of period.
