Project Summary

This project aims at ensuring the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas of Tonj North, Ayod and Nyirol Counties and Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. HRSS will provide critical GBV prevention and mitigation services to vulnerable women, men, girls and boys among Internally Displaced People and affected host communities through right based, participatory and integrated approach. HRSS will provide survivor centered psycho-social support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery, Provision of GBV case management services including coordinated care and support, Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support, Implementing Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment, Provision of legal services that protect the rights of GBV survivors and promote their access to justice, Implement community engagement and awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle, Engage men and boys for accountable practices to transform harmful social norms that perpetuate gender inequality, and to promote the health and safety of women and girls. Establish and / strengthen support to safe house to provide immediate security, temporary refuge, and support to GBV survivors who are escaping violent or abusive situations and capacity building trainings on various topics of GBV to frontline serve providers. The target project beneficiaries is 88,621 (57988 in Tonj South, 15702 in Ayod and 14,931 in Uror Counties respectively. The purpose of this project is to champion preventive measures for post war domestic violence, collaborate with community elders, members of the displaced populations and promote the adoption of legal measures to address the problems of associated with Gender Based Violence. HRSS also hopes to lead efforts to raise awareness as to the protection and access to services for IDPs and internally displaced women and girls, strengthen and support partnerships between local governments, state and community levels in awareness raising, promoting legal measures and programs to help GBV survivors. HRSS will collaborate on programmers and link appropriate government ministries at County level including health, interior, justice and social welfare, supporting systematic integration of gender-based violence prevention and response mandates to the ministries of social services and protection.

1 - Needs

An estimated 2.6 million people risk facing gender-based violence in 2022, which represents a 25 per cent increase compared to 2021 estimates. Climate shocks, severe drought, massive flooding, and conflicts have contributed to the increase in GBV risks. GBV needs are highest in thirteen counties: Ayod, Cueibet, Fangak, Gogrial East, Kapoeta East, Mayendit, Mayom, Pibor, Rubkona, Rumbek North, Terekeka, Tonj East, and Tonj North. 48 per cent of households (HHs) surveyed expressed fear/concern about GBV.155 Among the population groups surveyed, IDPs (57 per cent) expressed greater concern/fear about GBV, specifically rape and forced marriage. Women and girls are exposed to different forms of GBV in their routine activities. The FSNMS+ findings indicate that 38 percent of HHs reported that women and girls avoid more than one area in their communities such as water points, latrines, distribution areas (food and non-food items), markets and/or firewood collection sites, among others, because they feel unsafe. Findings further show that 32 percent of HHs also face an increased risk of GBV as they travel more than 30 minutes to fetch water. The FSMNS+HNOi survey reveals that only 6 percent of the community members are aware of GBV response services available in their neighbourhoods. Participants in Focus Group Discussions (FGDs)/Key Informant Interviews (KII) conducted in host communities, IDPs, and returnees indicated that their community members did not feel safe from various forms of gender-based violence.156 Sexual violence was raised as a principal safety and protection concern for women and girls, while revenge killings, abductions, and forced recruitment were identified as GBV risks for young men, men, and boys.157 Regarding the mechanisms available to women and girls facing protection issues, participants cited their family members as the initial support group, followed by community chiefs. A women’s group was mentioned as a support mechanism by some of the respondents. The availability of GBV-specific services, such as psychosocial support and medical services, however, was mentioned by a few respondents. Participants expressed the need for more reporting mechanisms, medical support, and other services, as well as the need for more female staff in such services. The 2021 analysis of GBV survivors who were reported and accessed services revealed that 97 per cent of survivors were women and girls.158 Of the reported GBV incidents, physical assault accounted for 36 percent, sexual violence for 24 per cent, and emotional abuse for 23 per cent. The 2021 FSNMS+ qualitative assessment identified rape, physical assault, sexual abuse, forced and early marriage as GBV concerns in more than two-thirds of the interviews held with both male and female participants. As a coping strategy, women and girls limited their mobility to humanitarian distribution sites, water points, farmlands, etc. The increased risk of GBV and gaps in response services, as revealed by the assessments, highlight an urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and scaling up of specialized GBV response services. Specifically, there is a need to strengthen GBV case management, medical care, psychosocial support, safety and security, legal service, safe houses, livelihood, economic support, and GBV referral pathways. Primary GBV prevention interventions such as EMAP, SASA, and the Community Cares program, integration of GBV into other sectoral responses including safety audits, strengthening of GBV coordination, and provision of dignity kits are high priority interventions for the GBV Area of Responsibility

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

HRSS GBV project will seek to equip beneficiaries with skills and knowledge they can utilize to come up with their own protection solutions. In the proposed intervention, women, girls, boys and men will be provided with information on their rights and need to protect their rights. Community Based Protection networks will play a critical role of mitigating children right abuses while advocating for increased participation of the community in protection of children. HRSS will also continue to fund raise to support GBV activities in targeted locations during and after the 12 months’ period. HRSS uses beneciaries’ feedbacks throughout the programming cycle to assist in making necessary adjustments and nally adapt those important changes to the project activities and services to the satisfaction of the right owner/beneciaries. HRSS will train all the staff on the acceptable code of conduct required of them while implementing the project. Based on the reports of the beneciaries feedbacks, the intervention will ensure that their concerns are included in the project program activities and adopted. HRSS will also use the beneciary’s feedbacks to strengthen the voice of the affected population and target beneciaries during project implementation. The beneciaries feedback will allow HRSS to identify human resource gaps and needs in project implementation and ensure project staff are well trained in project’s implementation. HRSS will also use the beneciaries’ feedback for creating links between donors and the affected communities therefore holding to account the implementing partner to both the donors and the beneficiaries. And by building the resilience of community members, the intervention will strengthen the resilience of households’ thus expanding household coping mechanism.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Complaint and Feedback Mechanism is embedded in the project right from project inception to conclusion. Women, Girls, individuals who are not literate, Individuals living with disability will be sufficiently informed of the mechanism and channels they can use to provide any complaint regarding the project. Project staffs will receive, acknowledge and address the complaints, provide feedback and safely document steps taken to address the complaints. HRSS works with beneficiaries in all its intervention to assist in improving and evaluating the quality of its services to the targeted beneciaries. Under the proposed intervention, HRSS will employ numerous tools to provide feedback from the vulnerable members that will include: community meetings with target groups especially women girls elderly and people with disabilities during which beneciaries are able to discuss about the project impacts and challenges facing them and possible lasting solution that will make the project a success that is most likely to change and improve their lives. Focus group discussions that will be conducted to different genders, age and people with disabilities and during the discussions, participants are able to lament on challenges facing them and those response will be taken note by the project staff for action. The proposed intervention will also have a one to one interviews with vulnerable members of the affected communities to collect their personal perceptions concern the project, challenges facing them and possible solutions. HRSS will install Complaints box/suggestion boxes in places accessible and secured to all the vulnerable people. Where there is telecommunication networks available, HRSS will introduce hotlines and free toll text massaging targeting the vulnerable people where women, girls and individuals with disabilities and those who not literate will be able to echo their concerns through a dialect understood by them. For those who are not able to write, HRSS has one help desk in Ayod County and will introduce in the other two counties in all the target project locations with a local female staff who has the knowledge of the language and culture to help in interpreting and recording the beneficiaries concerns in the complain log sheets.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HRSS will use Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) approach which is a system blending both formal and informal community structures, where individuals will be able and encouraged to safely report incidents of SEA. Local communities will be involved in developing and approving the CBCM so that the structure is both culturally and gender-sensitive.The mechanism will have multiple entry points, allowing both beneficiaries and staff the opportunity to report at the organizational level – internally through the network’s and field agency focal points at the community level. The primary Concern of the mechanism is to aid known and potential SEA survivors, and also fulfill a prevention function through awareness-raising efforts. PSEA and CBCM will not be a separate, parallel system to other complaints and feedback structures but rather link to and build on existing structures to create one system for handling feedback and complaints. HRSS will also ensure that a person who receives assistance as part of either emergency relief or development aid through assistance programs. Persons under this title include members of affected populations including refugees, internally displaced persons and other vulnerable individuals, as well as host community members. HRSS will also make sure, all the project staffs undertake PSEA training and tailor PSEA messages in community awareness engagement activities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HRSS will conduct community capacity building training with community members such as community leaders, women leaders, church leaders, chiefs, stakeholders and local authorities in various technical areas linked to the project implementation strategic owners for smooth phase out of the project. Further skills training will be enhanced to the strategic service providers as a way of sustaining the projects’ achievements and the structures developed. HRSS will conduct capacity training of the local authorities who will fully take over responsibilities of the project specially flood preparedness and mitigation in the future. In practice a lot of feedback received by HRSS at the frontline will be dealt with at the point of contact and field implementation level. Some feedback will be referred onwards to state actors and GBV sub-cluster as and when it relates to issues outside the control of HRSS. HRSS shall engage beneficiaries particularly in GBV emergency interventions and in the fields of community and participatory development. All information gathered by HRSS team from beneficiaries will be used throughout the program cycle for validation, sense-checking to test assumptions, surfacing issues missed by other parts of the monitoring system, identifying red flags, keeping stakeholders engaged through a regular reporting process on project progress, and for enabling learning loops. Feedback will be used to make adaptations to a project based on changing contexts or needs.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

61% of displaced persons in South Sudan are women and girls. Negative gendered norms, safety concerns and domestic chores can exclude women and girls from accessing humanitarian assistance. To ensure equal participation of women and girls in this project, project consultations and planning meetings will be conducted in locations that women and girls can easily and safely access and during time of day they can attend. Women and girls will be engaged in project monitoring to embed their views and concerns in project activities. To mitigate occurrence of GBV, Hope Restoration will deploy a GBV officer who will provide guidance to project staffs on existing and potential GBV risks to ensure activities do not pose additional risks to women and girls. To mitigate exposure to violence during selection of beneficiaries, HRSS will conduct consultation with the community members and leaders to provide information on the project including selection criteria, number of beneficiaries per location. Also, selection of beneficiary’s exercises will be conducted in collaboration with community leaders, local authorities, beneficiaries’ representatives to ensure fairness. All complaints received on the project will be safely, impartially and swiftly acted on with complainants being provided with feedback within the shortest time possible. Prior to commencing project activities, a safety audit will be conducted in each target location to map existing or potential protection concerns posed to beneficiaries and project staffs. The Safety Audit report will be utilized by project staffs, community leaders and members in deciding registration, distribution and training sites, where beneficiaries can access easily and safely. Project data will be dis aggregated by age and gender to measure level of participation of females.

12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

HRSS will design questionnaires to assess the needs of the community and to receive feedback from stakeholders to be considered in the Trauma and Psychosocial Support delivery and the Trauma and Psychosocial development of the curriculum training materials. This will include Trauma/Mental Health and Psychosocial Support for the community and Trauma/Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Curriculum Training/Trainer of the Trainerson building their capacity. On case management, HRSS will work with health centres and also set up as an alternative to enable identification of cases, take complaints, guarding against the possibility of missed diagnosis of more urgent conditions that will potentially result in increased fatalities. In areas where the treatment capacity for serious and critical cases is extremely limited, HRSS will support and advocate for facilities be reinforced to enhance in-patient capacity so as to provide palliative, end of life care. Screening sites will be established close to designated Ready Facilities and will be undertaken by health professionals to prevent missed diagnosis. Screening sites will implement Infection Prevention Control (IPC) measures to protect staff and patients

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The relationship between the host and IDPs is usually normal but tensions are expected over grazing land as people continue moving to highland areas with their livestock every day. HRSS will target beneficiaries in newly displaced IDP population with few hosted by host communities. HRSS will also carry out registration of continued displacement of population by flood. HRSS will respond to flood-affected people’s urgent needs include food and livelihood support, emergency shelter and non-food items (ES/NFI), and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), health and protection services. HRSS will lobby for additional logistic support with dedicated air assets and riverine transport to transport cargo to multiple locations prioritized for flood response.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

This project seeks to respond to chronic food insecurity in target locations which is a result of combined pressures as a result of poor production methods, environmental degradation and Climate Change. HRSS intends to contribute to enhancing long-term sustainable household food and income security through addressing environment drivers of food insecurity in the target counties. HRSS will also work with existing community structures using a multi-sectoral approach by building on the already existing programs implemented by HRSS through food distributions targeting food insecure households especially women, girls and persons with specific needs hence reducing IPC level by 10%
