Project Summary

The project will target 12500 households in four districts of Lower Shabelle region affected by droughts and conflicts with displacements (Wanlaweyn 2825 HHs), (Kurtunwareey 2050HHs), (Qoryooleey 3125 HHs) and (Marka districts 4500 HHs), districts in Lower Shabelle region, The primary focus will be access to food & Safety Nets, livestock asset protection and delivering agriculture inputs to increase and diversify sources of income among targeted communities, As for FSNAU seasonal assessment informed the determination of People in Need and severity analysis of food in need. The acute food insecure population table (Oct-December 2019) shared by FSNAU included in living standard consequence in calculating the inter-cluster PIN1 for 2020 HNO.
Taking to the consideration of an estimated 2.1 million Somalis are in Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 & 4) and face significant consumption gaps through December 2019, an extended hunger season following the poor performance of the 2019 Gu season rainfall. The food security situation reflect the cumulative effects of below-average rains experienced across most parts of Somalia, the project targeted districts, there are poor food consumption scores in the IDPs, mainly the children under 5 and PLW, alarming to starvation and malnutrition concerns referenced by AYUUB multi-sectoral assessment carried out on July 2019 due to conflict, lack of livelihood opportunities and drought. Widespread acute food insecurity and malnutrition persist in many parts of Lower Shabelle and affected by protracted conflicts and droughts, despite concerted efforts to reversing this trend.
The project will support the communities affected by conﬂicts and droughts and displacements through provision of unconditional cash transfers, agricultural inputs and food assistance and restoration of livelihood opportunities, helping vulnerable people and building opportunities of life through sustainable intervention like income generation activities through asset creation.
The project will struggle to build the capacities of community structures to be able to respond to disaster early warnings and all these will be tended towards improving food security and building community pliability while fostering disaster risk reduction.
Aiming at to lessen chronic poverty and reverse vulnerability to drought shocks thus promoting self-reliance in economics, social and cultural needs of project area populations as well as enable them live in harmony with their neighbors, AYUUB will seek collaboration with the ministry lines both Federal and state level and focus will be paid on system strengthening to ensure sustainability and enhancing capacity particularly with focus on strengthening and self suﬃcient in emergencies preparedness, response planning, this strategy will support the Ministry of Agriculture in Emergencies and engagement of community to realize effective response and fundraising opportunities.
AYUUB NGO will put efforts to the community to be self-reliant, as well to have equal opportunities of having support from humanitarian service/livelihood interventions to ﬁght against food insecure in the environment where the communities affected by conﬂict and drought escaped whose 65% are IDPs escaped from conﬂicts and evictions of AS in search of restoration of community productive assets and livelihoods.

Project Activities

Activity 1. In line with the FSC & HRP 2020 objectives, AYUUB aims to improve households’ immediate access to food through transfer of unconditional cash (Cash Plus) to 3500 HHs (also targeted for in activity 3 below) selected by AYUUB beneficiary selection criteria such as women headed HHs, child headed HHs, aged persons, persons with disabilities from the four districts. Each district will select 3500 HHs and will receive US$60/month through mobile transfer for a period of four months in areas where markets are viable; are physically and socially accessible to all segments of the population. The Cash Plus will help the families through the farming periods before the harvest of their farm produce. Activity 2. AYUUB in consultation with all stakeholders will identify most suitable beneficiaries for CFW to ensure that the nature of work is selected in consideration of speciﬁc gender needs & cultural practices for women in particular. Bush clearing activities along roads leading to major markets locally will be the main activity under CFW where 500 HHs from each district will be targeted. Cash transfers will be done through mobile money transfer (Hormuud) as it will not only ensure the safety, dignity, and integrity of recipients but limit fraud and diversion of funds. For those unable to do labor-intensive work, specifically female headed HHs with young children, the old and disabled, the transfer will be unconditional. Activity 3. Protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through provision of seasonally appropriate Farm inputs and technical support in the four districts under the project. AYUUB will distribute seeds package which are readily available in the local markets to the 3500 HHs with each household receiving 50 kg of various compound fertilizers, 10 kg of cow-pea seeds, 20 kg of Maize seeds and 240 grams of different vegetable seed varieties. Each HHs will also receive a watering can, Knapsack sprayer, a Panga, a Spade, a hoe and an axe as hand tools for farming. The farmers will also undergo trainings on GAP, IPM and Post harvest operational training. These farmers will be supported by 2 Agronomists and 4 Agricultural Field officers over the duration of their farming season.

Project Indicators

Indicator 1: Percentage of people in severe acute food insecurity phases (crisis and worse) from the total population in Somali from post Gu 2019 level (baseline 14%) with improved Household Dietary Diversity. Indicator 2: Percentage reduction of people in acute food insecurity phases due to provision of unconditional cash transfers from the total population in Somalia from post Gu 2019 level (baseline 34%) to 25%. Indicator 3: Percentage of beneficiaries (HHs) supported with livelihoods inputs (agriculture, livestock and fishers) to enhance Coping Strategy Index compared to target beneficiaries (HH) per season.

Please provide further explanation

The choice of activities such as transfer of cash, CFW, canal rehabilitation, water catchment rehabilitation and provision of farm inputs are intended to reduce poverty among IDPs and host communities thus leading to the achievements of NDP goal 1: End poverty in all its forms everywhere. The approach concerning the internally displaced will be to shift from classic‘ humanitarian assistance towards more durable solutions‘ that provide them with a realistic set of opportunities to (re)organize their lives through capacity building to impart knowledge and life skills to youth and women to have alternative livelihoods.
