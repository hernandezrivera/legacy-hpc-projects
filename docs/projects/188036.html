Project Summary

The proposed project aims at providing comprehensive (S)GBV services in already established Women & Girls Safe Space (WGSS) and Men & Youth Center (MYC) including case management in a safe, accessible, and private rooms which includes the initial/intake assessment for cases of beneficiaries with (S)GBV concerns such as; early marriage, sexual violence, and domestic violence, identifying strengths and assets that support (S)GBV survivors, following up referrals (internal or external) to health, MHPSS, nutrition, livelihood programs, and education services as needed based on a referral pathway developed by the GBV Sub-cluster. In addition to providing PSS activities aim to promote psycho-social wellbeing of (S)GBV survivors and other vulnerable individuals, which include topics such as family and community relationships engagement in meaningful roles and religious or spiritual practices, meeting basic needs, physical security, sense of identity, dignity, and positive self-esteem and physical wellbeing. Primary PSS activities include: Life skills for adolescents, parenting skills for adults, and "My Safety, My Well-being" for adolescents provided in line with (S)GBV sub-cluster recommendations. Also, providing group awareness raising sessions for adults, the GBV toolkit by the GBV Sub-cluster including 7 messages. In addition, the project includes vocational trainings for (S)GBV survivors, hairdressing and knitting for women in the WGSS and hairdressing for men in the MYC to empower the survivors to be well-integrated in their communities. The project intends also to support one mobile protection team, considering constraints that women and girls face in accessing services as well as the importance of raising awareness on (S)GBV for women, men, adolescent boys, and girls. The mobile team will contribute to increase the availability of high-quality protection services in the region, ensuring the confidentiality of provided services to avoid stigmatization and encourage beneficiaries to use them. The mobile team will implement awareness-raising activities, which include information-sharing sessions to raise awareness on specific topics depending on the perceived need of the target population. It will also consider topics shared through consultations with stakeholders and other humanitarian actors. Altogether, topics will especially focus on (S)GBV and domestic violence, gender equality, child marriage, and the importance of education, since low education and poverty, alongside the overall stressful and volatile environment, are either root causes or accentuate die risk of (S)GBV.
The project is estimated to target in total; 5256 beneficiaries

4 - Needs

According to, 2022 Voices from Syria, Assessment Findings of the Humanitarian Needs Overview, In 2021, women and girls across all governorates of Syria were subjected to physical violence (hitting, beating, physical injuries, torture, murder, and femicide), psychological and emotional violence (verbal abuse, forced isolation, gaslighting, social stigmatisation and ostracization), sexual violence (sexual assault, blackmail, exploitation, harassment, and rape), social violence (stigma and discrimination), as well as forced and early marriage, systemic denial of economic resources and education, movement restrictions, and exploitation at work. Although mentioned less frequently, women and girls also continued to face threats of kidnapping, arrest, and detention. These various forms of violence were most commonly perpetrated by women’s and girls’ male partners, male family members, and men and boys in public. They were also upheld by patriarchal norms, customs, and institutions that maintain gender inequality. Women and girls faced compounded forms of violence when exposed to overlapping and mutually reinforcing forms of discrimination and social exclusion, such as for divorced and widowed women who are not only exposed to heightened social policing, but also systematically deprived of inheritance and resource ownership. In addition, during the COVID-19 lockdown, reports of various forms of violence against women significantly increased.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented in an established and equipped WGSS & MYC where 2 (S)GBV case workers will provide case management services for beneficiaries of (S)GBV concerns reach the centres such as; early marriage, sexual violence, and domestic violence, identifying strengths and assets that support (S)GBV survivors, following up referrals (internal or external). 4 PSS animators, 2 females and 2 males, will provide non-specialized PSS activities aim to promote psycho-social wellbeing of (S)GBV survivors and other vulnerable individuals, which include topics such as; My Safety, My Well-being for adolescents consisting of 5 sessions for each group and Parenting Skills for adults consisting of 6 sessions for each group. The PSS animators will also provide group awareness raising sessions for adults on the 7-message by the GBV-sub-Cluster consisting of 4 sessions for each message for each group. 3 vocational trainers, 2 females and 1 male, will facilitate hairdressing and knitting trainings for the survivors in the WGSS & MYC targeting 20 beneficiaries for each vocational training each 3 months. Also, 2 outreach workers, 1 female and 1 male, will implement awareness-raising activities in the community and surrounding camps, which include information-sharing sessions to raise awareness on specific topics depending on the perceived need of the target population.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Sexual Harassment: among employees, volunteers and in work environment contextLevel: LowMitigation: All UOSSM staff have been trained on PSEA and above all has signed a TOR and aware of the internal policy of PSEA which has been monitored by both the HR & MEAL teams. Risk: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: between service delivery personnel, towards beneficiariesLevel: LowMitigation: All UOSSM staff (employees and volunteers) signed on a code of conduct that includes of refers to the commitment to PSEA, that is issued by the UN Secretary General Bulletin for the PSEA procedure. In addition, UOSSM has a PSEA focal point in addition to a dedicated hotline to receive complaints from beneficiaries in case of PSEA incidents. Risk: Gender Based Violence: targeting the vulnerable groups with communities (i.e., women, elderly, children, special needs …)Level: MediumMitigation: The project design is built on robust need assessment and is also targeting the most vulnerable groups in the community without discrimination or exclusion. Above, the project is a multi-sectoral one where all sectors are aware and acknowledged of detection and referral of GBV either internally to the protection department or externally to other service providers.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Rise in COVID-19 Cases which in turn imposes Restrictions on MovementLevel: MediumMitigation: As a mitigation action will be taken against this expected pandemic situation and in the purpose of the awareness-raising service not to be shut down completely for the beneficiaries, UOSSM will distribute the awareness-raising via telephone for beneficiaries. Also, posters in the centers in addition to the distribution of brochures in public places, medical centers, camps, and schools including messages on COVID-19. Risk: Interference with humanitarian workLevel: MediumMitigation: Conduct agreements with the Civil Local Authorities to guarantee the neutralization of humanitarian facilities and avoid interference with its activities. Also, implementing the principles of humanitarian action in providing all services and promoting their application to all employees. In addition, take full preparedness for all scenarios through preparing emergency and contingency plans to spare the organization interventions by any party.
