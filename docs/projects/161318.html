1 - Needs

In its sixth year, the conflict in eastern Ukraine continues to take a significant toll on the lives of five million people, 3.4 million of whom require humanitarian assistance. It has negatively impacted the physical and mental well-being of the affected population; the provision of and access to basic services; people’s livelihoods and incomes as well as the overall economic situation of the affected areas. Regular shelling and active armed violence continue to threaten the safety and well-being of some 600,000 civilians who live within five kilometres of the “contact line”. Widespread mine contamination along the “contact line” and beyond puts the lives of some 2 million people at risk. Protection risks associated with access to social entitlements and services as well as freedom of movement, of what is now an increasingly protracted conflict continues to impact the most vulnerable, including the elderly, the people with disabilities, the women and children. While humanitarian needs are acute in settlements close to the “contact line”, the degrading living standards and socio-economic conditions over time, of those living even leaving beyond the “contact line”, including in NGCA, has generated severe humanitarian situation. Meanwhile, humanitarians continue facing challenges to deliver aid and protection to everyone in need. Accessing people in hard to reach settlements and in NGCA is still a challenge, while funding for the humanitarian action in the east continues to fall short. The 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan has been funded only at 47 per cent ($77 million out of $164 million required). In the last two years, at least six humanitarian organisations were forced to terminate their operations due to underfunding. The Ukraine Humanitarian Fund (UHF) was set up in February 2019 to support the mobilization of additional funding to address some of the most critical humanitarian needs. The given complex context as well as the presence of around 130 partners in eastern Ukraine warrants a fully operational and capacitated OCHA. While systems are in place and well-functioning, coordination in eastern Ukraine requires adapting to the changing context, while protection concerns and challenging operational environment require an honest broker and an advocate, which OCHA has taken a lead on.

2 - Activities & outcomes

• Conduct regular monitoring and analysis of events impacting the humanitarian response, including reporting, advocacy, needs/gaps and response analysis, to better support the Humanitarian Coordinator (HC) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) in its traditional role as secretariat. • Coordinate and lead joint assessment processes, including missions. Ensure regular reporting on results to better inform decision making processes. • Develop and maintain information products, ensuring the sex and age disaggregated data is collected and used to enhance gender sensitive analysis and action. • Lead and provide coordination platform for efficient multi cluster coordination, including assessments, response and monitoring. • Ensure regular monitoring on funding, including identifying and reporting on funding gaps. • Support the timely allocation and disbursement of donor resources to the most critical humanitarian needs.• Strengthen donor engagement through regular meetings, as well as briefings and visits.• Increase awareness and support for the humanitarian community to increase operational capacity and guarantee unimpeded humanitarian access. • Support capacity of agencies to effectively mainstream gender into needs assessments and overall project activities in order to ensure positive gender equality outcome. • Conduct and put in place emergency preparedness measures in support of humanitarian operations. • Sharing and circulating of timely analytical and situational reports to increase awareness and identify pitfalls for humanitarian operations. • Perform civil-military coordination to bring together civilian and military actors and the UN and humanitarian partners, including facilitation of humanitarian notification system for a safe and sustained humanitarian access in hard to reach areas.• Work hand in hand with development actors to better align recovery/development and humanitarian activities, through the humanitarian and development nexus (HDN) approach • Increase awareness of the crisis through regular public information and communication engagement (campaigns, audio-visual products, media and social media) through the existing Humanitarian Communication Sub-Group (HCSG).• Support the HC and the HCT to advocate for the decisions and a change that revert the protection risks affecting millions of people through relevant private and public advocacy.

3 - Indicators & targets

• Production of quality reports, advocacy and communication documents shared with stakeholders – Regular • Data updated and shared with stakeholders, and disaggregated by age and gender – Regularly • Joint assessment missions occur on a regular basis • Increased number of assessments reflecting gender issues and concerns – Regularly • Humanitarian information products are updated regularly – Monthly • Multi cluster assessments are regularly used as a tool for assessments – As needed • Regular humanitarian overview reports include humanitarian funding status – Monthly • Donor meetings occur on a regular basis (Kyiv and field levels) – Monthly/Quarterly • UN Agencies and other humanitarian partners cross the “contact line” and freely access conflict affected areas to deliver humanitarian assistance – As needed • Situational and analytical reports are regularly submitted and circulated to humanitarian partners – Daily, monthly and as needed • Preparedness and resilience aspects of emergency response are regularly reviewed, and planning processes are in place, including the Inter-Agency Contingency Plan, UN Partnership Framework (UNPF), MPTF, etc. The Inter-Agency Contingency Plan for Ukraine is regularly updated –annually • Sustaining civil-military coordination and follow up for movement of humanitarian goods and personnel conducted – daily, monthly and as required ad hoc • Improving and maintenance of working relationship with the de facto and Ukrainian authorities to enable humanitarian operations – daily, monthly • Regular meetings on HDN related matters to ensure alignment of humanitarian and development interventions - Regularly • Release, dissemination and widespread implementation of joint public information and communication campaigns, media and social media engagement and human interest stories (audio-visual) – daily, weekly, monthly and ad-hoc as required.
