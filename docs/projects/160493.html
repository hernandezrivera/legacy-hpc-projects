Project Summary

The project focuses on delivering a Comprehensive Nutrition Program in Yei, Morobo and Lainya counties, and will also support nutrition activities in hard to reach areas Kassingor and Kapoeta East. The project has three objectives: (1) reduce infant mortality rates caused by malnutrition; (2) decrease the prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women; (3) widely promote good health, hygiene and nutrition practices to prevent malnutrition from occurring;
ACROSS is a faith based South Sudanese organisation with regional reach and has been supporting the positive and holistic transformation of communities in South Sudan since 1972. ACROSS is currently operating in some of the most isolated, hardest to reach areas in South Sudan and enjoys strong community relationships at multiple levels from the grassroots to State and National levels. Our mandate is holistic transformation, and we specialize in delivering multi-sectoral interventions with proven results in these hardest to reach areas.
This project builds off our current nutrition and integrated projects. ACROSS currently manages 7 static nutrition sites in Yei, 2 in Morobo and 3 in Lainya County, and conducts outreach to reach communities that do not have static sites. In this project for 2020, we will scale up our efforts to run a total of 19 nutrition centres in Greater Yei: 8 in Yei, 7 in Lainya and 4 in Morobo counties. In these centres we will provide nutrition treatment, counselling and follow up. All admissions to our nutrition program are screened for malaria and all positive cases are referred for treatment to the nearest health facility. Any cases presenting with complicated medical conditions are referred for medical attention and any women or children presenting with suspected GBV or Child Protection concerns are referred to relevant services. All patients and their families are educated with relevant health, hygiene and nutrition messages. We collaborate with other health and WASH partners to provide patients discharged from the nutrition program with preventative health/WASH kits containing treated mosquito nets, soap and water treatment for integrated health outcomes.
In addition to providing services in the facilities, ACROSS integrates nutrition support with health by providing institutional feeding to malnourished cases in the hospital and also conducts outreach and community education activities. In a highly successful Ebola preparedness project targeting Yei and Morobo Counties, ACROSS distributed 400 solar powered digital audio players (DAPs) loaded with prerecorded messages on Ebola awareness and other messages on health, hygiene, nutrition, protection and peacebuilding. The DAPs provided communities with consistent, high quality messaging in local languages that can be played repeatedly and shared between households and communities for highly effective health promotion, especially to remote and isolated or hard to reach areas. In this project, we will update the existing DAPs with new and more relevant messages and distribute more DAPs to areas that didn’t receive them from the previous project, especially Tore and Lainya. Messages will emphasize healthy diets for young children and mothers and other preventative measures, as well as how to tell malnutrition symptoms.
This project will collaborate with FSL partners on the ground to establish kitchen gardens at the nutrition sites, for delivering nutrition education on dietary diversity, home vegetable cultivation and cooking classes.
In Kassingor and Kapoeta East, ACROSS is not the Nutrition Cluster partner, however is delivering integrated FSL and WASH programs in these extremely remote communities, and will support the Nutrition Partners to broaden their reach. ACROSS's trained staff will conduct outreach activities for widespread screenings and refer identified malnutrition cases for treatment.

1 - Needs

Protracted government forces’ operations against armed groups have contributed to displacement of people within the three areas of Yei, Lainya and Morobo. With the destruction of the already existing underdeveloped structures like markets, schools, PHCU/Cs, and massive population displacement from one county to the other seeking safe haven, humanitarian needs are high and expected to remain so in 2020.In Yei, there are currently about 4,553 people internally displaced based on the recording from Yei RRC office. With increased displacement of the local population, acute malnutrition in children under five remains a major concern in many parts of Yei, as well as among the IDPs and the communities within the affected areas, especially with shortfalls in funding food rations and disrupted livelihoods. All this has collectively led to a deepening humanitarian catastrophe in various parts of the counties, especially for the children under five years, and for pregnant and lactating mothers. The high food cost and low economic income has largely contributed to alarming prevalence of malnutrition and elevated morbidity and mortality among the vulnerable groups including children and PLW as well as TB and HIV epidemic. Last quarter, the Nutrition Assessment, Counseling and Support (NACS) reports from Yei Hospital revealed a caseload of 966 (464 male, 502 female) malnourished HIV/AIDS and TB patients. The malnourished children Pregnant and lactating mothers and vulnerable people are in dire situation who need continuous nutritional support and treatment. With the disruption of the primary health care services, access to nutrition service, health care, clean and safe water, continued loss of properties and inaccessibility to farming lands all these factors necessitate an inter-sectorial collective collaboration and intervention in addressing issues regarding the well-being of the affected population. According to an inter-sector assessment conducted in May in Wuji Payam in Lainya it was found that mothers have poor knowledge on exclusive breastfeeding, whereby they introduce salt and water for newborn on the first three months after birth. Mothers start introducing complementary feeding at early age (2-5 month) due to frequent movement. There was lack of active nutrition services program coupled with poor hygiene, lack of health service and poor knowledge on child spacing contributed to malnutrition. Most children did not receive vaccination in the past one year and mothers were not attending ANC. The provision of integrated nutrition service will serve to reduce morbidity and mortality among children under five pregnant and lactating mothers and other vulnerable persons, such as those living with HIV/AIDS or TB. ACROSS has been actively promoting exclusive breastfeeding and other good nutrition habits, but more work needs to be done in 2020. ACROSS is not the Nutrition Partner in Kassengor payam Pibor County (RMF) or in Kapoeta East County (Andre Foods South Sudan), however ACROSS is working on the ground in these very remote and difficult to reach communities where there are very few humanitarian partners. Our staff are aware of the high rates of hunger and malnutrition in the communities. Throughout 2019, ACROSS trained nutrition staff have been conducing malnutrition screenings in Kauto Plateau in Kapoeta East, which has resulted in high numbers being referred to the nutrition program who may not have been reached otherwise, due to limited resources of the nutrition partners. ACROSS will continue these activities in 2020 in Kapoeta East and expand this model also to Kassingor.

2 - Activities

ACROSS is currently providing nutrition services established in 7 static nutrition sites and 7 outreach sites to ensure nutrition services are accessible to vulnerable conflict affected girls and boys, PLWs and other vulnerable groups. ACROSS will adopt the SS MoH approach to community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and MIYCN guidelines in addressing malnutrition in children under 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Women PLWs. Conflict affected vulnerable people will also be included. In 2020 the project will target 12978 beneficiaries in Yei, 6515 in Lainya, 3472 in Morobo and will focus on the following activities: Nutrition treatment to Children U5 (6-59 months old), PLWs with SAM and/or MAM provided at OTP and TSFP respectively. Identification and referral of cases with SAM and MAM through active case finding in community and at the health facilities. Provision of Malaria RDT testing to children with SAM admitted in OTP and providing linkage to malaria treatment for SAM children testing positive for malaria.Conduct screening of SAM children admitted in OTP for medical complication and referral of children with SAM with medical complication to SC for nutrition and medical treatmentLinking PLWs to ANC/PNC services provided at the health facilities.Linking mothers/caretakers of SAM children with provision of LLITN for malaria prevention.Provision of safe drinking water and hand washing infrastructure at all nutrition sites.Provision of safe waste disposal at nutrition sites.Training of all nutrition staffs on WASH including hygiene and sanitation.Provision of community mobilization activities and provision of integrated messaging on health, nutrition, education, protection, FSL and WASH through trained network of CNVs.Train community volunteers in nutrition and protection.Promotion of optimal MIYCN programs promoted to PLW to prevent malnutrition among children under 5 years. Conduct community messaging and sensitization to men and women on nutrition management and optimum MIYCN practices. Promote exclusive breastfeeding and mother to mother support groups for peer support. Conducting comprehensive Nutrition education among target communities. Provide training to community leaders to sustain community nutrition initiatives Provide access to integrated Nutrition, Health, WASH, Education, Protection and FSL services geared toward prevention of malnutrition in children under five and PLWsProvide nutrition education and community messaging using other sector platforms (schools, Food distributions, etc.). Integrate protection components/services to children and PLW under nutrition programs. Train Nutrition staff on identification and referral of children with protection concerns (separated and unaccompanied children). Train Child Protection field staff to refer all nutrition related cases identified in CFS/outreach, to nutrition service providers. Conduct GBV safety audits in at least 3 sites.Work with FSL partners to provide kitchen gardening demonstration at Nutrition sites to create awareness on food diversity and consumption of micro-nutrients.Create linkage with partners to provide agricultural inputs and livelihood kits to caretakers of SAM children to support household production of nutritious foods.Distribute new and/or update existing solar powered Digital Audio Players loaded with messages on health, hygiene, nutrition, protection and peace building. Establish listening groups to engage in community dialogue on the different topics on the DAPs. Design and record any further messages based on input from the local community, and reload the DAPs with new messages.The project will conduct widespread screening activities to remote areas in Kapoeta East and Kassingor, and refer identified cases to Nutrition Partners. Conduct community awareness and education on Nutrition and protection in these locations, and support Nutrition/protection partners to access communities to provide services.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project includes the self-identified needs, priorities and perspectives of the displacement-affected people. In Morobo, Lainya and Yei, ACROSS has an ongoing presence and relationship as trusted Nutrition partner. In Kassingor, the target population are the recently displaced Jie community, who have almost no services by other partners or government. Last month, our project staff was advised by the community of their needs, which is why we are including this location within this project. In Kapoeta East, ACROSS is delivering integrated FSL, WASH and Nutrition program in very remote communities. The nutrition component integrates with the other program, but has been included in this project proposal on advice of the Nutrition Cluster and Peer Review Team.The project is designed to include effective community consultation and Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms where perspectives and challenges from displaced affected communities are systematically collected, acknowledged, assessed, referred, and responded to in a timely, confidential, and effective manner. The project will allocate dedicated staff for this purpose and ensure the staff is accessible to the affected people. This will effectively ensure the project and its implementation serves and benefits the affected people.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The Nutrition Cluster has a well established coordination system, which ACROSS' activities are integrated into. In Yei, Lainya and Morobo, ACROSS is the designated Nutrition Partner to deliver the nutrition program in these counties. In Kassingor and Kapoeta East, there are other partners who are delivering the Nutrition Program, but have limited resources to access these very remote and hard to reach areas. ACROSS' proposed activities are coordinated with the local nutrition partners and the Cluster to ensure efforts are not duplicated, but rather to ensure more communities are reached by outreach screening and referrals. The government doesn't have the capacity to run the health and nutrition services independently, however, ACROSS always involves Ministry of Health officials in any trainings we deliver so we are building the capacity of health and nutrition departments at the State and County levels. We also invite government staff to conduct joint supportive supervision and we meet with them regularly in planning and review meetings to ensure our services are aligned with their strategies and policies.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The Project is linked with national objectives. The project will be implemented in alignment with the cluster strategic objectives which are complimentary with national objectives. The project implementation will follow the national nutrition guidelines (CMAM, SAM and MIYCN) in treatment and management of acute malnutrition.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Currently the target areas in Yei, Lainya and Morobo are experiencing sporadic clashes between government forces and armed groups within the counties, at times making certain areas inaccessible to humanitarian assistance. ACROSS is working and providing assistance in relatively stable areas where affected vulnerable people can access humanitarian assistance, and providing outreach to the more inaccessible areas, when security allows. Kassengor and Kapoeta East are relatively stable, sometimes experience occasional cattle raiding, but project activities can be carried out safely.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

ACROSS is closely working with CHD and SMOH and building capacity of their staff. ACROSS hires staff from the local community as much as possible and provides training, with the hope that when the project ends there will be increased knowledge and capacity to continue community mobilization activities and awareness. The project also supports the beneficiaries in developing kitchen garden demonstration plots, with the hope that the beneficiaries will use the skills at home to grow their own nutritious food. ACROSS will look for partners from FSL cluster to provide seeds and implements to support the beneficiaries with kitchen gardening. This will improve food diversification and help prevent malnutrition. Distributing the DAPs loaded up with integrated messages on nutrition, health, hygiene, protection and other messages will build capacity for longer term results even in the most isolated and hardest to reach communities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, the project will embrace information sharing and feedback mechanisms regarding the overall project design, scope of work, budget and personnel involved in implementation. Various approaches tailored towards supporting feedback/complaint mechanisms will be in place and will include: individual surveys of most vulnerable beneficiaries throughout the project cycle collected via mobile data collection (KoboCollect) to seek feedback on participation, satisfaction, any complaints. Focus group discussions where beneficiaries' views and ideas will be taken into account in the project cycle. Members from the most vulnerable will be considered in recruitment of staff to implement the project. This way the vulnerable people will have a voice on how the project is implemented.ACROSS will allocate human and financial resources to ensure that feedback from vulnerable members in the community is systematically collected, acknowledged, assessed, referred, and responded to in a timely, confidential, and effective manner. ACROSS staff are trained annually in safeguarding, AAP, PSEA and other core humanitarian principles, as well as ACROSS' internal policies and code of conduct. ACROSS has a trained and dedicated AAP Officer who will furthermore ensure appropriate feedback mechanisms are in place and that minority groups are actively sought out for feedback. The AAP officer will also ensure the target communities in all locations understand their rights, and our staffs' obligations according to humanitarian principles, and that the community are made aware of how to report any inappropriate behaviour and breaches of code of conduct.
