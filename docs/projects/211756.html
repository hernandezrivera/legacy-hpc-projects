Project Summary

DRC carried out a market assessment in Hanano city of Aleppo governorate in close coordination with UNDP and the Chamber of Commerce. The assessment was conducted in November 2023 and consisted of a series of qualitative Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) with private and public institutions and micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs), alongside physical observations of the local market infrastructure. Based on the needs, the project aims to promote self-reliance and resilience among crisis-affected communities in Aleppo through increased access to decent and sustainable livelihood opportunities within the newly restored Hanano marketplace, including self- and wage employment. DRC will rehabilitate 95 damaged shops and common areas through private contractors to restore access to and functionality of the local marketplace. Once the works are completed, MSMEs supported under this project will benefit from rental agreements, including a tenancy fee waiver for a minimum of two years, to utilize the rehabilitated facilities for their business endeavours without the financial strain of initial rental costs. DRC will provide technical and financial support to improve MSMEs’ capacities to increase their income and generate employment, placing priority on enterprises that are socially and economically viable with potential for sustainable growth and job creation. Business Development Skills trainings will be delivered to 118 MSMEs by accredited trainers to strengthen participants’ foundational business management knowledge and skills. Upon completion of the training, a subsect of MSMEs (95) will be selected through a competitive process to receive conditional cash grants based on their approved business plans. To maximize the impact of business investments, regular mentorship and coaching will be provided alongside market events to promote the reopening of Hanano marketplace and expose MSMEs to new market actors and collaboration opportunities. DRC will provide soft skills trainings and subsidized apprenticeships to 40 unemployed individuals to expand their skills and work experience to enter the labor market. Over a three-month period, host MSMEs will integrate on-the job learning within their daily operations in Hanano marketplace to contribute to apprentices’ overall skills development and employability, with the intention of remaining employed or securing a new job after the placement. Safeguarding measures are embedded in DRC’s mandatory training packages, policies (Code of Conduct, PSEA, child protection), and procedures for the recruitment of staff, reporting and investigation routines, as well as in the roles/responsibilities of senior management and managers. Staff are aware of their obligation to report suspected misconduct and to direct all SEA-related cases to the HQ-based Code of Conduct team for investigation and management. DRC will use in-house capacities to continue the provision of trainings to all staff and contractors under this project.
