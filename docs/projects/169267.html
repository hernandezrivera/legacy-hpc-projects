Project Summary

Following the Beirut Port Explosion, which claimed 180 lives, caused more than 6000 injuries and devastated an important part of the city, there exists an urgent need for psychosocial support services to reduce the traumatic impact it had on Beirut’s population. On top of that, increased protection risks, especially for children, make it crucial to ensure the most at-risk individuals have access to adequate services. HI’s outreach-based approach 1. identifies all urgent and unmet needs using Psychological First Aid (PFA) outreach and the HI emergency hotline as entry points; 2. directly provides community-based PSS and 3. refers individuals and/or households to humanitarian actors through a cross-sectorial referral mechanism, including to Child Protection, SGBV and Legal Aid service providers as well as HI’s physical rehabilitation services. Referrals to other actors are closely followed up by HI teams to ensure affected individuals are receiving essential services necessary for a holistic recovery.
Vulnerable areas are prioritized based on a combination of factors including the level of damage sustained from the explosion, pre-existing socio-economic vulnerabilities, and areas under-served by other humanitarian actors. Indeed, the traumatic impact of the explosions on the overall population intensifies underlying anxiety, stress and resort to negative coping mechanisms. HI’s intervention aims at directly supporting individuals showing such signs of distress to increase their well-being. By linking them up with adequate services through a strong cross-sectorial referral mechanism, this intervention intends to guarantee a safe and efficient access to services in order to further increase the well-being of the targeted individuals as well as to preserve their dignity and help them cover their most basic needs.
Given the imminent protection risks that exist following disasters, HI’s approach systematically considers the intersectionality of factors - including gender, age, disability, legal status, nationality - which can heighten an individuals’ vulnerability. By using PFA as an entry point, information is gathered on individual factors and/or household demographics which capture and highlight protection risks, thus enabling teams to ensure timely, safe and effective referrals (internal and external). This approach, coupled with active door-to-door outreach, ensures populations most at-risk of being excluded are consistently identified and included in the response, regardless of nationality, gender, age, disability, or legal status. Finally, the project targets 300 individuals, according to the following breakdown: 250 individuals will receive PSS services and 100 individuals will receive assistive devices based on their needs. There is no double-counting as only some individuals (expected 50) will receive both PSS and AD.

1 - Needs

The social and psychological impacts of the explosion are undeniably high on all individuals. However, those with underlying vulnerabilities are at a greater risk of developing longer-term mental health issues and lasting trauma. Hence the need for a variety of services such as PFA, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and specialized mental health interventions combined. Indeed, so as not to overburden specialized mental health service providers as well as to ensure that all levels of needs are addressed, it is crucial that individuals have access to the most appropriate service. Assessments show that children in particular experience long-term trauma, anxiety and sleeplessness. While protection issues existed prior to the explosion, they are likely to increase consequently. Indeed, COVID-19 lockdown measures and the socio-economic situation already compounded domestic violence problems as women and children found themselves trapped in sometimes-unsafe family environments. With the damage and destruction of houses, relocation to other crowded living sites and increased stress, trauma and mental health conditions, incidents of violence and abuse are expected to rise subsequently. Moreover, vulnerable groups not typically reached by the humanitarian community such as domestic and migrant workers (DMW), stateless persons and refugees of other nationalities are at-risk of being left aside by the response. Some of the socio-economically vulnerable neighbourhoods directly impacted by the explosion are host of such populations. However, cases of DMW abandoned by the families they were working for are reported, and not only in less favoured neighbourhoods (Jeitawi, Gemmayzeh for instance). These vulnerable groups have less access to information and services, including in regards their basic human rights.

2 - Activities

HI activities that align with FA Protection Response Priority 1: Ensure protection mainstreaming and community participation:1. Ensure that a protection lens is applied through all aspects of the response by:• Collaborate with Child Protection, SGBV and Legal Aid specialized actors to ensure teams adequately address such cases;• Support intersectional identification of vulnerability and of disability using the Washington Group Questions;2. Expanding existing protection hotline with an updated service mapping and referral pathways across sectors by:• Contributing to a strengthened referral system across sectors including coordinated multi-sectoral service mapping based on field realities in order to ensure timely and accurate referrals (e.g. selection criteria of humanitarian actors)3. Expand community outreach and existing community-based protection programmes to identify and refer cases in need of protection services by:• As an entry point to the broader outreach community-based PSS intervention, ensuring continued active outreach by PFA teams. HI activities that align with FA Protection Response Priority 2: provide appropriate and efficient protection services:1. Provide MHPSS to vulnerable populations through communities and referrals to service providers with a focus on the most vulnerable groups by:• Provision of outreach community-based PSS (individual, group and community sessions) to persons showing signs of anxiety and distress following the blast, including persons with disability.• Referral to specialized mental health service providers 2. Ensure access to specialized services for persons with new and pre-existing disabilities and ensure relocation sites address the needs of people with disabilities, elderly and other persons with specific needs by:• Replacement of lost or damaged assistive devices for persons with pre-existing disabilities The provision of outreach community-based PSS services targets all individuals living in blast-affected areas that show signs of anxiety, distress or trauma, including Persons with Disabilities (PWD) (newly acquired and pre-existing disabilities). As part of this broader PSS approach and as an entry point for the identification of beneficiaries, HI outreach PFA teams, individually assess persons in needs of PSS services, including PWD, to identify urgent and unmet needs as well as disability using the Washington Group Questions. Internal and/or external referrals are then made to relevant service providers via an assigned sector-specific referral focal point within the team. They ensure all individuals are referred to as many actors needed to ensure a comprehensive coverage of their multiple needs. For specialized mental health services, HI refers to specifically identified actors with which the organization already collaborates strongly to ensure access to such specialized services for the most vulnerable individuals identified. Finally, internal referrals are also made to HI physical rehabilitation team upon identification of corresponding needs. A similar process is followed for individuals contacting HI via the emergency hotline.In addition, when needs for psychosocial support are identified, HI team of Psychosocial Support Worker provide direct support at community-level. Based on the level and suitable modality for each individual, sessions can be either individual or group. Such groups will be defined alongside common characteristics of individuals identified such as gender, age and of course needs.Finally, the replacement of assistive devices (AD) for persons with pre-existing disabilities is done through individual donations based on a technical assessment to include potential modification of needs. Indeed, the provided device should meet the current needs of the individuals whether resulting from the sudden change of environment and potential new needs or from the level of needs that might be different from when the person initially received an AD.
