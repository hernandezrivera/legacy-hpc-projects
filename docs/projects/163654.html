Project Summary

The project aims to support humanitarian information management in Libya via sustained implementation of the IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) programme and via capacity building of local and national Libyan counterparts. DTM supports the humanitarian community in Libya by provision of data, information and analysis on the population mobility and its impact on the affected populations. The information and analysis provided by DTM in Libya covers internal displacement as well as international migration. Under this project DTM will continue to monitor internal displacement via emergency tracking, assess the priority needs of the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) via rapid assessments, and by continued implementation of Mobility Tracking publish bi-monthly information packages on IDPs and returnees in Libya. Similarly, DTM will continue to monitor migration trends and dynamics, and conduct assessments of migrant vulnerability and humanitarian needs in Libya. Information products such as flash updates, detailed analytical reports and public datasets in addition to bilateral sharing of operational information with OCHA and partners of Rapid Response Mechanism (IOM, UNFPA, UNICEF, and WFP) will facilitate enhanced humanitarian information management.
Furthermore, DTM will undertake capacity building activities aimed towards supporting local and national Libyan counterparts in the implementation of improved humanitarian information management.

1 - Needs

During 2019 Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) programme in Libya published over 50 information products including analytical reports on displacement and migration in Libya. The assessments conducted and the subsequent reports published were crucial in identifying the vulnerabilities and humanitarian needs of IDPs, Returnees (returning IDPs), and migrants in Libya. DTM initiated emergency tracking of displacement upon the onset of Tripoli armed conflict (April 2019) and published 22 flash updates on displacement. Furthermore, DTM also remains critical in providing information to the Rapid Response Mechanism (an interagency initiative with IOM, UNFPA, UNICEF, and WFP participating) via timely verification of the displaced populations in need, and by supporting on the ground coordination of the subsequent response. DTM in Libya is the primary source of data on displacement and migration, including the population figures used in the Libya Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNOs), and figures used for targeting humanitarian response under the Humanitarian Response Plan. Under the working assumption for Libya HRP 2020, the humanitarian context related to the migration flows to Libya and, the political crisis including the associated armed conflict is not expected to change. Neither is an increase in the national or local capacity for information management is projected to the extent to fulfill the data and analysis needs of the humanitarian community. Therefore, the need for data, information and analysis provided by DTM towards the aim of enhancing humanitarian information management, and for evidence based operational and strategic planning purposes will remain. On the other hand there is a well documented need for capacity building of the relevant national counterparts in Information Management (as documented in ACG West and ACG South meeting minutes). DTM has been supporting local partners including municipal level authorities on ground, and working in close collaboration with several national authorities in building their capacity. In specific as part of this HRP 2020 proposal DTM will support the national and local authorities by building their capacity for data collection, management, analysis and reporting. During 2019 DTM conducted several workshops and series of focused bilateral meetings aimed towards identifying key needs of Libyan counterparts and potential areas of collaboration. In specific capacity building of Ministry of Local Governance (MoLG), and municipality level counterparts in geographical data collection (GIS) and mapping was identified as a key need. Furthermore, need of capacity building related to collection of displacement data for the Ministry of Displacement, Social Solidarity Fund, and of Higher IDP Committee under the auspices of the Office of the Deputy Prime Minister has also been identified. DTM aims to incorporate this need, and will continue to support these national authorities via capacity building efforts. Analysis of 2019 DTM Mobility Tracking and Flow Monitoring data indicates substantial differences in the needs and vulnerabilities of girls, boys, men and women. Specifically, adult female migrants showed substantially higher vulnerabilities and needs across multiple sectors in comparison to their male counterparts (2019 DTM Libya Migrant Vulnerabilities and Humanitarian Needs Assessment). Moreover, 47% of school-age migrant children in interviewed households in Libya were assessed to have limited or no access to education in their location of residence while elderly migrants who migrated irregularly were found to be particular affected by limited access to public health services and social safety nets. All assessment activities involving micro-data collection will collect sex and age disaggregated data across population groups to facilitate better understanding of the specific needs and vulnerabilities. Additionally, questions on disabilities will be added to 2020 Flow Monitoring Surveys.

2 - Activities

DTM will implement the following set of activities under this project: Activities aimed at provision of data, information, and analysis: 1. Emergency Tracking of Displacement with Rapid Needs Assessments to determine needs of displaced and affected populations;2. Implementation of DTM Mobility Tracking (including Multisectoral Location Assessments - MSLA) to update baseline IDP and returnee population figures, and multisectoral needs data; 3. Migrant Stock Population Baseline Assessments;4. Migrant Flow Monitoring and Intention Surveys (Durable Solutions). Activities aimed at capacity building of local and national counterparts: 5. Joint workshops on narrowing down specific needs and areas of focused collaboration. 6. Training sessions via field experts covering the identified focused areas of collaboration. 7. Joint and collaborative field activities aimed at building capacity for data collection. 8. Workshops on joint data analysis and information management as part of local and national counterparts' capacity building. Key project-level indicators contributing to sector indicators include, but are not limited to: # of internally displaced persons in Libya (disaggregated by age and sex), # of returnees (disaggregated by age and sex), # of migrants present in Libya (disaggregated by age and sex).

4 - Protection risk analysis

IOM in the overall implementation of its Displacement Tracking Matrix programme in Libya ensures strict adherence to the Do No Harm principle. In specific the following key protection risks are identified, that DTM programming aims to reduce, mitigate, and remedy: 1. Risk of protection harm emanating from gathering of personal information from respondents (key informants, migrants, and head of displaced households); 2. Risk of protection harm to DTM enumerators and implementing partners (humanitarians) via their engagement in data collection activities linked to humanitarian relief / aid provision.

5 - Mitigation measures

DTM programming will adhere to following mitigation measures for the protection risks presented under the section 4 of this project: For the mitigation of protection risks to respondents (key informants, migrants, and head of displaced households interviewed): 1. The personal information of respondents (key informants and migrants) is primarily aimed to be not gathered or collected if not absolutely essential. 2. If essential that this personal information be collected then IOM data protection principles are closely followed to mitigate any protection risks that may arise. (For further details on the risk mitigation measures followed, please consult the IOM data protection manual at https://www.iom.int/data-protection) For the mitigation of protection risks to humanitarians (DTM enumerators and implementing partners): 1. Clear communication on the purposes of data collection and its links with the subsequent provision of humanitarian relief / aid provision (if any) is one of the first risk mitigation measures undertaken by DTM in Libya. 2. DTM enumerators will be provided with adequate training on measures they may take at personal level (via effective communication with respondents / beneficiaries) on reducing protection risks that may arise during their field data collection activities. 3. DTM enumerators are bound to adhere to institutional protocols on safety and security, and on external communication as an additional mitigating measure to the protection risks that they may face.
