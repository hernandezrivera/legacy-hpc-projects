Project Summary

Since the beginning of COVID-19 imposed restrictive measures in South Africa and other neighbouring countries, Zimbabwe has been overwhelmed with appeals of Zimbabwe migrants wishing to return to their homes. To date over 10,000 Zimbabwean migrants have returned to the country mainly through Beitbridge, Plumtree, Chirundu, Forbes, Nyamapanda, Kazungula, Sango and Kariba border posts, with a projection of 20,000 new arrivals expected in the next coming months. If the rapid spread of the virus continues to affect neighbouring countries, new restrictive measure will most probably further exacerbate the socio-economic impact in vulnerable migrants unable to access host Governments support, significantly increasing the number of migrants’ returnees into Zimbabwe.
It is clear that returnee numbers represent a challenge for Government and communities of origin/destination. Apart from the need to have COVID-19 preventive/response measures observed including mandatory quarantine, the anticipated numbers will place extra pressure on already constrained basic services. Heightened risk for women and children is also a real concern. In addition, the lack of information on COVID-19 in many communities poses the prospect of discrimination and stigmatization.
Migrants and mobile populations bear a high risk of infection due to several factors and barriers, such as: lack or inadequate access to proper information or prevention, limitation or exclusion from accessing diagnosis and treatment services, crowded living conditions, and stigma and discrimination. These vulnerabilities coupled with high levels of internal and cross-border migration and upcoming winter season place Zimbabwe at significant risk in the face of the evolving COVID-19 pandemic with exponential rates of infection.
To cater for the returnees, the Government of Zimbabwe established quarantine and isolation centres throughout the country. The returning migrants are expected to be quarantined in their respective provinces of origin for a mandatory 7 mandatory days. The returnees represent an additional challenge for the country already burdened by the ongoing food security and economic crisis. In addition, the receiving communities are themselves already vulnerable, facing food crisis, droughts, and another protracted crisis. Competition for resources and the lack of information on COVID-19 in many communities will likely lead to the discrimination and stigmatization of the returned migrants. Lack of viable livelihoods options for a higher number of vulnerable returnees to the country will result in them adopting negative coping mechanism making them vulnerable to protection issues, particularly smuggling and trafficking.
IOM Zimbabwe is aligned to the IOM regional response to contain and interrupt the virus from spreading and save lives at risk, particularly migrants and migrants host communities.The overall objective of this intervention is to contain and interrupt the transmission of COVID-2019 to save lives at risk with particular focus on migrants returnees and mobile vulnerable populations. This will be achieved by reinforcing disease surveillance and prevention activities and ensuring national border control agencies and civil society have the resources and capacity to control this pandemic. Through this action, IOM will also ensure the returnees have access to life-saving post-arrival assistance, which includes provision of immediate livelihood for the 3 months after arrival.

Plan fields

1 - Activities

1) Strengthen cross border coordination in health management to assist migrant and mobile populations. 2) Ensure mobility is taken into account in public health messaging, and that migrants and mobile communities have access to timely and correct information by providing risk communication and community engagement activities. 3) Engage Environmental Health Practitioners (EHPs) to support a secondary screening phase at POEs and conduct a Participatory mapping exercise, Population mobility mapping (PMM) at cross border points to detect high-risk transmission mobility corridors, hot spots and conduct health assessments to mobile population that could be at risk due to their vulnerability, communicating relevant information to performed the correspondent follow up in the place of origin. 4) Increase capacity of border officials and health workers in awareness and management of Points of entry (POEs) through capacity building in active surveillance, health screening, referral and data collection at POE/ the development of Standard operating Procedures (SOPs’), referral mechanisms, detection, notification, isolation and, including the training curricula and manuals. 5)Increase Infection Prevention and Control at cross border points and surrounding communities by enhancing provision of water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services at POEs. 6) Strengthen case management capacity by training health workers, establishing isolation centres to all provinces and districts and providing adequate PPEs. 7) Strengthening of the logistics, procurement and management system through mapping of available resources and review supply chain and management system. 8) Continue and expand the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) intervention, to enable and improve registration, profiling and monitoring of migrants’ returnees and IDPs, including the provision of age, sex, and disability disaggregated data and information on needs, gaps, and vulnerabilities.9) Continue and expand Points of Entry (POE) assessments, to regular monitor the management conditions, needs and gaps at border posts to strengthen post-arrival COVID-19 humanitarian response.
