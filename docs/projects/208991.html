Project Summary

The project will aim to ensure 130,593 people affected by conflict, flooding, drought, and waterborne diseases have dignified and sustainable, meaningful access to sufficient safe water, appropriate sanitation facilities, and a healthier environment. The same interventions will aim to ensure increased durability of targeted positive hygiene behaviours.
The project interventions will target White Nile, Al-Jazira, Gedaref, South Kordofan, and Sennar states through an integrated approach focusing on internally displaced persons (IDPs) and vulnerable host communities in and out of camp or gathering sites. The project will focus on life-saving interventions in newly displaced populations, closely coordinated with the rapid response mechanism (RRM) to ensure optimal utilisation of resources. Using a strong presence in the target areas, NRC will aim to restore access to basic WASH services, with markets guiding the design of interventions. In water supply, the planned interventions will improve access to equitable, sustainable, and affordable safe water with the aim of at least 15 litres per person per day (lpd) for drinking and domestic purposes. This will be through quick-fix rehabilitation of existing water systems based on a rapid technical assessment. This will be through the repair of damaged water pumping stations and water treatment chemicals like coagulants (for surface water sources) and chlorine. The rehabilitation will also entail the repair of damaged water distribution networks and water points or the replacement of solar panels in systems using renewable energy. In locations served by shallow wells equipped with handpumps like South Kordofan and Gedaref, the NRC WASH team will provide fast-moving spare parts to trained technicians to ensure their continued functionality during the crisis. To ensure water quality, household water treatment and safe storage (HWTSS) will be prioritised by providing water purification and treatment tablets for water systems without centralized chlorination and ensuring adequate water collection and storage containers. Residual chlorine will be monitored in collaboration with the government line ministries to ensure a minimum of 0.2 to 0.5 mg/l at the point of use. Land tenure for water supply infrastructure, in close collaboration with ICLA, will be prioritized to minimise service disruptions and disputes. Gender and protection assessments will inform the locations of water points to mitigate risks associated with water collection and ensure meaningful access for vulnerable or marginalised individuals.
To ensure an environment free of human excreta, which is essential for people’s dignity, safety, health, and well-being, NRC, in the first phase of an emergency, due to land tenure and space availability, will aim to rehabilitate existing latrines to restore them to functionality; this could be through infrastructure improvement or desludging. Proper disposal and transport of the excreta will be ensured at designated sites. The emergency latrines will be constructed in consultation with the government to ensure compliance and will be equipped with handwashing stations. NRC will use participatory approaches that aim to adopt positive behaviour change by the target population. The focus will include raising awareness about handwashing with soap, sand, or ash at critical times. NRC will undertake mass hygiene campaigns during emergencies like an outbreak of diseases and then focus on behaviour change. Various communication channels will be explored, including social media, to ensure wider reach and coverage. The campaigns will be coupled with the provision of hygiene-related NFIs after thorough market surveys, with the option of market-based programming. Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) will form a key component of the hygiene promotion targeting adolescent girls to address barriers related to hygiene, access, and disposable.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To employ Do No Harm principles and mitigate risks of abuse and exploitation, NRC will continue to consult with participants, especially marginal groups, and monitor protection risks related to service delivery. NRC will mainstream protection and GBV in all trainings and use standard, approved materials adapted to the Sudan context. Understanding that improperly located water points can contribute to inequality of access and be sources of conflict and security risks, such as harassment and abuse, NRC will involve women, girls, boys, and men in consultations when locating the sites for the water points. These consultations will solicit information to help determine further strategies to mitigate barriers to inclusion. Hygiene promotion (including menstrual hygiene promotion) will use a door-to-door approach, which reaches women and girls in culturally appropriate ways, minimizes protection concerns, and reaches PwD who may have difficulty accessing public spaces. The selection of project participants will be based on well-defined vulnerability criteria, exposure to protection risks, people at risk of eviction, and people with less access to humanitarian assistance. NRC will target the areas most affected by displacement in accordance with the HRP and data collected from local NGOs, local and religious leaders. NRC will carry out this project regardless of status and will provide assistance to all categories of vulnerable and at-risk people affected by the crisis.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

NRC has conducted gendered FGDs with communities and liaises with leaders. A feedback exercise with program staff and CHVs also provided insight. NRC has made use of qualitative data collected by other actors, including those empowered to collect sensitive data, to ensure high-risk groups have been accounted for in the design. Responsive program alterations are also conducted through our community reporting process. Communities will be engaged to help with the selection of participants and rehabilitation work, using processes to capture varied voices. Engagement is conducted with marginal groups in mind; for example, CHVs use a variety of pictorial, audio, and written materials to ensure the inclusion of PwDs and individuals with limited literacy.Male and female CHVs are selected from the community and are included in the monitoring process as data collectors.In regards to PSEA, NRC has zero tolerance to PSEA, and requires immediate reporting of any suspicions involving staff, partners, or other agencies.
