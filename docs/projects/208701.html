Project Summary

Northeast Nigeria has been grappling with numerous challenges due to the activities of the militant group Boko Haram. This executive summary outlines the pressing issues faced by the region and proposes a comprehensive plan to address them through Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) interventions. These interventions aim to empower the local population, particularly the youth, by fostering a conducive environment for entrepreneurship.
With over 1.7 million people displaced and a significant number seeking refuge in neighbouring countries, the region is in dire need of assistance. Out-of-camp Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities, comprising more than half a million individuals, require support. The provision of sustainable livelihoods and economic opportunities is crucial for their well-being and successful integration.
The rise of Boko Haram can be attributed, in part, to the recruitment of uneducated youths whom the insurgent group exploits. This executive summary recognizes that the conflicts in the region encompass Violent Extremism, Political Violence, Ethno-Social, Intra-religious, Inter-religious, and Youth Restiveness. Addressing these issues requires a multifaceted approach that targets not only the symptoms but also the underlying causes.
Youth unemployment poses a significant challenge in Northeast Nigeria. However, self-employment is viewed as a viable career path that can uplift individuals and contribute to the overall economic growth and prosperity of the community. To harness the potential of the youth, there is an urgent need to establish an entrepreneurship ecosystem that supports and nurtures their innovative ideas.
The proposal suggests forming beneficiaries into Village Savings and Loan Association (VSLA) groups, providing entrepreneurship training, mentoring, start-up kits, and cash grants. These initiatives aim to empower small business owners and entrepreneurs, improve livelihoods, and create economic opportunities. The primary goal is to foster an environment that enables sustainable development and reduces dependency on external aid.
To achieve the proposed objectives, a comprehensive set of activities has been identified. These include conducting entrepreneurship training workshops to equip individuals with the necessary skills and knowledge, providing mentoring sessions to guide aspiring entrepreneurs, facilitating the development of business plans to ensure sustainability, and distributing start-up kits and cash grants to support the establishment of new ventures.
To assess the effectiveness of the interventions, a robust monitoring and evaluation framework has been proposed. This includes collecting quantitative and qualitative data, conducting monitoring visits and interviews with beneficiaries, reviewing business accounting books to measure financial performance, conducting follow-up assessments to track progress, and maintaining records of networking event attendance.
The proposed performance indicators will be verified through various means. Quantitative data will be collected through surveys and assessments, while qualitative data will be gathered through interviews and focus group discussions. Monitoring visits and interviews will provide valuable insights into the impact of the interventions. Furthermore, the review of business accounting books will enable an assessment of financial sustainability, and networking event attendance records will reflect the entrepreneurial ecosystem's engagement and growth.

1 - Needs

Northeast Nigeria has witnessed widespread destruction of both infrastructure and livelihoods by the militant group Boko Haram. Over 1.7 million people remain displaced across the region and hundreds have fled into neighbouring Chad, Niger and Cameroun. Not only have skilled labour become rare in communities because of mass displacement, but victims have either lost their only sources of livelihood or have had their breadwinners killed by the insurgent group – remain have remained dependent on humanitarian aid. (UNDP March 2018) In 2023, nearly 3.5 million people are identified to need Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) interventions across the BAY states. The ERL Sector identified over half a million out-of-camp IDPs (503,496 people) whose needs are not being sufficiently met. The sector estimates over 2.1 million people in host communities need support, as well as most of the 830,236 returnees (2022 MSNA). The terrorist activity in the northeast is perpetuated by youths who are being used to carry out all manners of atrocities without recourse. This is largely due to the unorganized manner the youths were, most of them never had formal education and there was no common ground for any form of tutelage by an experienced adult which is directed to sharing their experience with the younger ones. This terror group capitalized on the weak connections and organizing that exist to exploit these youths and present themselves as a person with better options and alternatives. Many Youths fell prey and saw these tactics by insurgents as an organized way to bring them together (creative International 2017). The Boko Haram insurgency has affected almost every aspect of human lives in Yobe state (Northeast). However, various types of conflicts also lie within the state and manifest from time to time, even most recently during the years of insurgency attacks. The complex conflict landscape in Yobe state provides a more in-depth understanding of some of the different conflicts, which often tend to hide under the cover of the Boko Haram insurgency and virtually pass by unnoticed. While some of the conflicts are more peculiar to a specific location, at their peak, they all tend to get violent and none of them should be completely ignored. These conflicts are broadly categorized under; (1) Violent Extremism [VE] (2) Political Violence (3) Ethno-Social (4) Intra-religious (5) Inter-religious and (6) Youth Restiveness. (Mercy Corps 2018) While youth unemployment has become the most pressing issue of the contemporary world, self-employment is growing as a viable career path that is available to most youth. Yet, an entrepreneurship ecosystem conducive to youth to start, grow, and sustain their businesses is still lacking. Entrepreneurship skills are needed to ensure that those with vocational skills only who either own businesses or wish to start new ones can run their enterprises successfully to counter the possibility of recruitment of youth for any form of social vices. Thus, it is essential to act as soon as possible to reverse the deteriorating situation of the youth at least to maintain the sustenance of our generation. Their potential could be used for the development of the nation if attention is paid to them. They could also contribute to the attainment of Sustainable Development Goal SDG 8.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The Empowering Communities for Sustainable Livelihoods and Economic Growth project in Yobe State, Northeast Nigeria, implemented by the Wadata Relief Care Initiative (WRCI), aligns with the Humanitarian-Development-Peace (HDP) nexus approach by forming part of a coherent package that strengthens the resilience of affected people and advances towards lasting solutions. The goal of the HDP package in this context, facilitated by the WRCI, is to empower communities, promote sustainable livelihoods, and drive economic growth in Yobe State. The project's components include: 1. Humanitarian Interventions: Recognizing the immediate needs of affected populations, WRCI, in collaboration with humanitarian organizations, provides essential services such as healthcare, food, and shelter. This ensures the safety and well-being of the communities, addressing their most pressing concerns. 2. Development Interventions: WRCI focuses on long-term resilience and sustainable development by addressing the underlying causes of vulnerability in Yobe State. Through capacity building, skills development, and support for entrepreneurship, the project improves livelihoods, access to basic services, and economic opportunities. 3. Peace-Building Interventions: WRCI, alongside peace-building institutions and local communities, fosters social cohesion, promotes dialogue, and addresses the root causes of conflict. By doing so, the project contributes to the overall peace-building efforts in Yobe State, creating an enabling environment for sustainable development. Key actors involved in the HDP nexus implementation in Yobe State, including WRCI, humanitarian organizations, development agencies, peace-building institutions, the local government, non-governmental organizations, and the communities themselves, collaborate and coordinate efforts. This enables the successful implementation of the project and the realization of the HDP approach's objectives. WRCI, as the implementing organization, actively engages with these key actors in planning, implementation, and coordination. By leveraging the expertise, resources, and networks of humanitarian, development, and peace-building actors, WRCI maximizes the impact and sustainability of its interventions. Aligned with the principles and objectives of the HDP nexus, the project implemented by WRCI strengthens the resilience of affected communities in Yobe State. By empowering them with skills, resources, and opportunities for sustainable livelihoods and economic growth, the project supports their journey towards lasting solutions. Through this comprehensive approach, WRCI aims to create a positive and transformative impact in Yobe State, promoting resilience, peace, and prosperity for the communities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

This project aims to promote livelihood and economic development with a strong focus on gender and age markers in protection mainstreaming. It seeks to provide access to assistance and services without discrimination based on sex, age, or diversity, ensuring that the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, and persons with special needs, receive appropriate support while maintaining confidentiality. Through advocacy, empowerment of targeted groups, and the establishment of community-based committees, the project aims to enhance the population's ability to reclaim their rights.The project places a particular emphasis on empowering vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, and persons with special needs, ensuring that they have equal access to services and support. It aims to raise awareness of the specific consequences of conflict-related protection (CP) issues faced by these groups and enhance their utilization of available services.To reduce the stigma attached to violence and support the reintegration of children, particularly girls and boys formerly associated with non-state armed groups (NSAG), back into the community, community-based support teams will be established. These teams will provide tailored assistance and guidance, ensuring that all individuals affected by conflict, regardless of gender or age, are fully accepted and supported by their communities.The project also aims to build resilience in communities by enhancing their capacity to support women, men, girls, and boys affected by conflict, while considering their specific gender and age needs. This involves providing targeted training and resources to community members, empowering them to provide effective support and assistance. By strengthening the community's ability to respond to the challenges of conflict, the project aims to promote long-term stability and sustainable development.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members, the project will employ several strategies:1. Inclusive and participatory approaches: The project will actively engage with vulnerable community members, including women, girls, persons with special needs, and marginalized individuals, to ensure their voices are heard. This will involve conducting consultations, focus group discussions, and community meetings specifically targeting these groups.2. Tailored feedback mechanisms: The project will establish feedback mechanisms that are accessible and appropriate for the vulnerable community members. This may include using multiple channels such as in-person meetings, phone calls, written feedback forms, or digital platforms, depending on the specific needs and preferences of the target groups.3. Sensitivity and empathy: The project staff will be trained to approach feedback collection with sensitivity and empathy, creating a safe and supportive environment for vulnerable community members to share their experiences, concerns, and suggestions.4. Clear communication: The project will clearly communicate the purpose and importance of feedback to the vulnerable community members. They will be informed about how their feedback will be used to improve project activities and outcomes, fostering a sense of ownership and empowerment.5. Regular monitoring and evaluation: The project will establish a robust monitoring and evaluation system to track the feedback received from vulnerable community members. This will ensure that their concerns and suggestions are considered, and appropriate actions are taken based on their feedback.By implementing these strategies, the project will prioritize the inclusion and participation of vulnerable community members, ensuring that their voices are heard, their needs are addressed, and their perspectives influence the project's implementation and outcomes.
