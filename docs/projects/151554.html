Project Summary

The project will seek to repair housing units damaged by the conflict, with a focus on highly vulnerable households (elderly, people with mobility difficulties, households headed by single mothers and households with children, including those headed by women). A mixed modality approach will be adopted (in-kind or monetized assistance), based on market conditions (availability) and the specific vulnerabilities of target beneficiaries (especially considering gender, household composition, and age factors). The overall objective of the proposed intervention is to address essential life-saving and life-preserving shelter and winterization needs.

Needs

Ninety-four percent of conflict-related incidents (average 175/week, Jan-June 2018; INSO) take place within 5km of the line of contact (LoC; 44% GCA and 50% NGCA), with a further 5% in the 5-20km zone. Conflict risks are thus significantly higher close to the LoC: 86% of households living within 5km report shelling as a security concern, compared to 29% and 9% in 5-20km zone and beyond 20km respectively (REACH, Analysis of Humanitarian Trends 2018).REACH data (2016, 2017, 2018) show the proportion of households with partially damaged or destroyed houses remaining significant. In GCA 5km zone, this proportion was 27% in summer 2016, 35% in summer 2017, and 25% in winter 2018 (suggesting the positive impact of shelter interventions preceding the winter season). In summer 2018, it was 29%, higher than the 5-20km zone (12%) and beyond 20km (9%). Data from NGCA (summer 2017) has this figure at 17%. Shelter needs on both sides of the LoC are thus significant, especially during harsh winter months.Wood and gas remain a primary source of fuel for heating in rural areas, (46% in Luhansk; REACH, Trends Analysis 2018). ACTED-led market monitoring in GCA shows that, in November-December 2017, prices for coal and firewood had increased by 12% and 25%, respectively, as compared to August-September. In February 2018, they had further increased by 8% and 7% respectively.In the 5km zone, 42% of households spend part of cash assistance on shelter needs, and 45% on NFI (ACCESS Multi-Purpose Cash (MPC) Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM), 2018). Shelter is the second largest reported expenditure in Donetsk (after health), and the third largest in Luhansk (after health and food).While spending patterns are relatively similar for men and women headed households overall, PDM reveals that more women headed households in Luhansk spend assistance on shelter than men headed households (52% vs 38%), and more men headed households spend assistance on NFIs in Donetsk (56% vs 34%). Minimum heating costs (winter) have been tagged at 280, 115 and 320 USD/season for coal, firewood, and gas, respectively (2018-2019 Winterization Guidance), which are the three most-used fuel types (REACH, Trends Analysis 2018). This represents 38%, 16% and 44%, respectively, of the average annual income of vulnerable single-income households in GCA 5km area (August 2018 value), with firewood experiencing steepest price increase during winter (ACCESS Market Monitoring 2017-2018).Seasonal heating costs for coal, firewood and gas represent 96%, 39% and 110%, respectively, of average annual income for households with unemployed members aged 50-59; 73%, 30% and 84% for women headed households with children; 75%, 31% and 86% for all households with children; and 36%, 15% and 41% for households with elderly members (ACTED food security beneficiary data, 2018).The population along the contact line is disproportionately female (57%) and elderly (35%); 64% of those 60+ are women. Women headed households make up 49% of all households. Women headed household with children have the second lowest income per person in the 5km zone at 901 UAH (28% of actual subsistence level), have the lowest ability to make utility and rent payments, and the most debt (ACCESS vulnerability analysis, 2017).Households with members who are elderly, children, chronically ill, or have mobility limitations are also particularly vulnerable, noting that people with limited mobility are concentrated within households of elderly. As per ACCESS MPC vulnerability analysis, single elderly women (22% of the population along the contact line) have the lowest ability to undertake shelter renovations. Households with unemployed members of pre-pension age, have the lowest average income per person per month at 893 UAH (27% of actual subsistence level).

Activities & outcomes

ShelterACTED/PIN’s current project (funded by Global Affairs Canada) supports 100 households in Luhansk NGCA through installation of new plastic windows in houses damaged by conflict.ACTED/PIN propose to support thus 200 more households with houses damaged because of conflict (Luhansk NGCA). The installation of new windows will improve the heating efficiency of the housing units (environmental sustainability; PIN PDM data show that 93% of beneficiaries pay less for heating after windows are installed). This modality is informed by beneficiary input: 91% note windows are a priority (ACTED/PIN needs analysis, 2017).200 households will be targeted, approximately 400 individual beneficiaries will benefit (37% elderly, 17% children, 58% women – recognizing that vulnerable women are more likely to be elderly, less able to undertake renovation works).Beneficiary selection will be done in consultation with the Shelter Cluster, communities and local authorities. Where possible in NGCA, vulnerability assessment will be undertaken to ensure prioritization of the most vulnerable – elderly and single mothers will be a key focus.Under the ongoing intervention, ACTED/PIN’s shelter beneficiary profile includes:- 62% women (incl. nursing mothers, single mothers and women heads of households);- 25% elderly women. WinterizationIn partnership with Arbeiter Samariter Bund (ASB), and funded by the Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany, ACTED is providing 1,000 most vulnerable households in GCA with winterization cash (6,500 UAH/household eq. 230 USD: amount might be revised as per shelter recommendations).ACTED proposes to support 900 more households with winterization assistance in 2019 through appropriate modalities. ACCESS market monitoring data show that the main barrier faced by households to access goods is high prices; fuel is either fully or partially available in a majority of locations (some locations in semi-periphery and periphery report no availability). ACTED will assist households with cash support for areas connected to utility supply or where fuel is available in local markets, and consider in-kind support to mitigate protection concerns where necessary.Women headed households – 58% elderly isolated women – spend more multi-purpose assistance on shelter than men headed households in Luhansk (21% vs 15%; ACCESS PDM). Significantly more women headed households (34%) report limited access to fuel for winter than men headed households (21%; ACTED gender analysis, 2017).Beneficiaries will be selected in coordination with shelter partners, and referrals will be sought from humanitarian (incl. protection) actors. ACTED works closely with communities and local authorities – e.g. seeks referrals – to ensure aid is delivered as per needs and vulnerabilities of affected populations.ACTED ensures that beneficiaries have access to Complaints and Response Mechanism, managed by an independent AME team, e.g. through posters and contact cards distribution. Community representatives are informed of internal ACTED and donor processes to ensure accountability. During project implementation, ACTED’s AME team conducts spot check monitoring with beneficiaries, and community focus group discussions upon assistance delivery to receive and incorporate beneficiary feedback.The project will focus on:- Women headed households, who tend to employ income-based coping strategies (ACCESS CSI baseline 2017) e.g. collecting materials to burn or food in forest which can be contaminated with mines and ERWs;- Elderly households, who are more likely to be women facing chronic diseases and isolation yet spend a significant amount of income or assistance on fuel costs during winter, as shown by (ACCESS PDM 2018);- Men headed households with children, given that the majority of children reside in men headed households, and that they report spending more assistance on NFIs than women.
