Project Summary

The protracted crisis in Somalia, compounded by COVID-19, floods and droughts, has resulted in massive challenges in meeting the WASH needs of the Somali population. According to the JMCNA, approximately 8.9 million people are affected by poor WASH conditions. For 2021, it is estimated that 4.6 million people are in catastrophic (10 per cent), severe (9 per cent) or extreme (18 per cent) need of humanitarian WASH assistance could be in acute life-saving WASH needs along the Shabelle and Juba river basins in case of above normal rainfall performance during the GU and Deyr 2021 rainy seasons.
According to the JMCNA 2020, 41 per cent of Somalis do not have access to a regular and stable improved water source for drinking, while over 30 per cent walk more than 15 minutes to reach their main drinking water source. Due to these constraints, one in five Somalis do not have enough water per day to cover their basic needs for both for drinking and domestic use. Challenges to access a minimum quantity of safe water per day are reflected at national level (locations with darker shades reflect higher severity). Availability of water containers is also limited, with 92 per cent and 8 per cent of household respectively reporting their non-availability for transporting and household storage, further impacting their regular access to water. According to various rapid assessments. Hirsh belle and southwest States are the worst affected, accounting for nearly 91 percent of the caseload 164,000 people (Ocha-Situation Report 26 July 2020). According to the findings of the 20 July rapid multi-sector assessment up to 71, 000 people have either been affected or were at risk of flash floods in Afgooye and Wanla Weyne districts in Lower Shabelle in South West State, since 5 July when river banks broke along the Shabelle River. The affected people include 13,000 who have been displaced in the Afgooye district and 29,000 who are displaced in Wanla Weyn district. The most affected areas include four low-lying villages in Afgooye and 16 villages in Wanla Weyn district. Fourteen wells, 291 latrines, and six houses were damaged. As a consequence of recent flood disasters, population displacements, and the chronic lack of investment in quality WASH infrastructure, Somalia continues to suffer from AWD/Cholera and risk factors for outbreaks persist in several communities affected by the flood disasters. Early Warning Alert and Response Network report indicates that 69 new cases of AWD/cholera with no death were reported mainly from Banadir, Hiran, and Middle Shabelle regions, in epidemiological week 36.
The project will enhance access to safe water through construction of 2 elevated water tanks and rehabilitation of 6 boreholes/wells, ensure adequate and appropriate sanitation facilities and Hygiene services by constructing 200 pit latrines and distributing 3000 hygiene kits to vulnerable household. The project also aims to ensure that the targeted communities have the necessary information, knowledge and understanding to prevent water and sanitation related diseases by conducting by community hygiene outreach activities through 90 (men and women) community hygiene promoters.The project shall promote the equitable participation of both men and women in the planning and decision making processes, this will ultimately help to ensure that the entire affected population has safe and easy access to water supply, sanitation facilities and services, and that such services and facilities are appropriate. Special focus shall be given to the needs of women and children who bear greater risk of SGBV in the use communal sanitation facilities. The intervention shall support gender sensitive participatory processes for operation, maintenance and management of the sanitation facilities and services. The project also seeks to promote good hygiene by conducting handwashing promotion to the villages and schools.

1. - Projects activities

1. Supply of clean, safe, and treated water through two water construction of elevated water tank in Jowhar districts benefiting 3500 men, women, and children making up 583 households shall have access to approximately 15 liters of safe drinking water per person per day for twelve month.2. Water distribution point construction and or rehabilitation; with the agreement of borehole owners; 6 boreholes/wells shall be fitted with an appropriate solar pumping system to reduce maintenance and operating costs long-term.3. Distribution of 3,000 hygiene kits to the most vulnerable households.4. 9000 women and 5000 men targeted by Community hygiene outreach activities, implemented by 90 (men and women) selected and trained community hygiene promoters. Participatory methods of hygiene education shall be employed to enable targeted beneficiaries develop the knowledge, attitudes and skills required for them to adopt healthy lifestyles. The promotion of positive behaviour towards general environmental cleanliness, hand-washing with soap (at least before eating and after using the latrine). The correct use of latrines, cleaning of latrines, collection and proper disposal of solid waste shall also be promoted. Selected Community Hygiene Promoters shall help to monitor and encourage proper hygiene practice, as well as provide positive role models by consistently demonstrating appropriate hygiene behavior.5. Construction of 200 latrines to serve the needs of 8,000 men, women, and children. Latrines shall be fitted with lockable doors; lockable from the inside to protect people while they are using them. An appropriate hand-washing point with soap, water and adequate drainage shall be made available. Latrines will also be separated into males and females.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project will ensure different people including the minority groups are involved and consulted all project life cycles. The women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women, and marginalized persons will be part of the group engaged to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for sanitation and water facilities location, design and services. Besides, the project will engage the community and committee representative who play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response.In addition, the project will ensure the location of the WASH facilities are constructed in a safe location where women and children can safely access.
