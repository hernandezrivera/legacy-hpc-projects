Project Summary

This action is an immediate response to ensure that girls, boys, women, men, elderly with and without disabilities have sufficient means to respond to their WASH related needs. Specifically, the action aims at improving access to drinking water supply by installing solar panel systems for 13 private desalination plants (PDPs), In Gaza Strip (GS), drinking water is largely supplied by PDPs, which provide more than 4 MCM/year to Gaza households, representing 95% of the water used for drinking and 67% of the water used for cooking. The action directly contributes to the 2019 oPt Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) Strategic Objective 1: “The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL), while duty-bearers are increasingly held to account”; and Strategic Objective 2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic service and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”. Specifically, this action directly contributes to the WASH Cluster Strategic Objective 1, by ensuring equitable access to drinking water services to the most vulnerable people in GS; and WASH Cluster Objective 2: “Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the un-served, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank, to reduce threats and to cope with emergencies and shocks” through provision of alternative energy sources (solar) including alternative solutions to water access, and support critical PDPs located in the un-served, underserved and most vulnerable communities in the GS.
The action aims to achieve the above objectives through the installation of solar panel systems for 13 PDPs which are committed to work during emergency, and willing to contribute on 20% of the total amount of the solar panel systems. The action also envisages a capacity building programme on operation and maintenance of desalination plants and solar panels systems and water quality and safety of water trucking. This action’s purpose is to maintain minimum operations of private water desalination plants in the GS, which will lead to guarantee drinking water access in communities who live in deteriorated public health conditions and to enhance the drinking water safety and quality. The provision of alternative source of energy to the 13 selected plants is the result of a the 2018 GVC study titled “Drinking Water Distribution Mapping During Crises in the Gaza Strip, with Desalination Plants of Private Vendors”, endorsed by the WASH cluster and PWA, The study analysed the capacity and the cost of drinking water production in the GS during large and medium scale crisis. The analysis, which targeted 96 PDPs in the GS to be used in case of emergency, has led to the selection of 21 PDPs as the most suitable to respond. Further, the study recommended to improve and upgrade the selected private desalinations plants in terms of potable water quality and safety and to conduct practical technical trainings to improve staff technical capacity and management during crises for the selected plants.
The activities will be coordinated with the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), WASH cluster, and the relevant working groups (e.g. Renewable and Sustainable Solar Energy Coordination Task Force, WASH Monitoring Working Group (WMWG), Water Quality Working Group (WQWG)).
The 13 PDPs are located in Beit Hanoun (2), Bet Lahia (1), Jabalia (1), Gaza (Al Shejaiya) (2), Beach Camp (2), Al Nussirat Camp (1), Khan Yunis Camp (2), Al Mawasi (Khan Yunis) (1) and Rafah (1).
It is expected that this action will directly benefit 216,000 people (57,161 girls, 59,706 boys, 49,086 women, 50,047 men), 67% out of them are refugees and IDPs, while it will indirectly target 360,000 individuals (95,628 girls, 99,509 boys, 81,810 women, 83,412 men) during emergencies.
Coordination with UNRWA infrastructure & camp improvement programme was ensured to avoid overlapping and ensure complementary to the services provided by the Agency

Needs

Since January 2017 electricity power blackouts - up to 20 hours/day has impeded the provision of a set of basic services, especially in the WASH sector, such as drinking and domestic water supply, wastewater and sanitation. Water and wastewater facilities with electrical generators are heavily used and overloaded by long hours of operations. Over 96% of the water extracted from the aquifer is unfit for human consumption, with the result that 95% of people in GS rely on purchasing desalinated water from private trucking, posing a heavy financial burden on already impoverished families and health risks due to widespread contamination of that source. Increased electricity cuts have further reduced the drinkable/ piped water supply and the operation of approximately 130 critical water and sanitation facilities (HNO, 2018). In the GS there are a total of 286 desalination plants with different capacities and technologies (PWA 2016), and only 21% are officially licensed by the competent authorities. The brackish water desalination private business has also expanded and reached 96 PDPs to cover the drinking water demand which cannot be covered by the public desalination plants only. The electricity shortage affects different drinking water providers, the produced drinking water supplied by 48 public desalination plants shall be reduced by 80%, where only 4,000m3/day shall be produced out of 20,000m3/day plants nominal capacities; the PDPs’ owners are forced to operate their plants with electrical generators which are heavily used and overloaded by long hours of operations, resulting in an increase in the production cost. Quality of distributed drinking water is critical in Gaza. During the first quarter of 2018, the drinking water monitoring proved a significant deterioration in quality of water and huge gap in quantities. The test results indicate high levels of microbiological contamination of desalination plants and its reservoirs (33%) at water source level. GVC -in coordination with PWA, CMWU, UNICEF, WASH Cluster and WQWG in line with the WASH Cluster Contingency Plan 2016- carried out an assessment titled “Drinking Water Distribution Mapping during Crisis in the Gaza Strip with Desalination Plants of Private Vendors” in Jan-Nov 2018. The assessment aims to re-evaluate the current situation of PDPs in the GS. In GS, with more than 43% of the population unemployed, the cost of purchasing drinking water increases the burden on those who are most vulnerable limiting their ability to access safe water. The unregulated production, transportation and storage of water results in the potential contamination of up to 68 % of household drinking water supplies, exposing nearly 60 % of the population to further public health risks. The installation of solar panel systems for 13 PDPs will improve the access to drinking water services, overall efficiency of the existing water trucking, reduce the quantities of required fuels, minimize the operation cost, and thus it will ensure sustainable access to sufficient water quantity for large segment of the population in the GS. The inadequate access to drinking water generates differentiated impact among the family members, especially women, which prioritize its use for domestic tasks in the house, resulting in deteriorating hygiene conditions for the whole family and an extra burden, especially for women and girls. The 13 PDPs present high capacity in terms of production, storage and distribution, water quality and the ability to distribute water and cover more than one governorate and are committed to work under crisis conditions. They were also selected based on their location in the un-served, underserved and most vulnerable communities and willingness to cover 20% of the total cost of the solar panel systems

Activities

A.1. Installing of solar panel systems for 13 private desalination plants, including the provision of Capacity building programme on operation and maintenance of desalination plants and solar panels systems.At an early stage, GVC and RWDS will carry out an in-depth technical assessment and will organize introductory meetings with PDPs owners to provide accessible and timely information on organizational procedures, types of project activities, and any other relevant information that may interest them in order to ensure the full integration of the owners of the selected PDPs all stages of the project cycle, to guarantee/increase ownership, maintenance and sustainability. This programme includes distributing a manual that will be use as a guideline and to train the operators and workers, how to deal with this system for a long term to ensure that solar system life is preserved for as long as possible and the original conditions of the system are sustained.A.2. Maintenance and upgrading of 13 private desalination plant, including the pre and post treatment units such as pH adjustment unit, disinfection unit, dual media filter, and mineralization unit in order to improve the produced water quality and safety.A.3. Provision of capacity building programme on water quality and safety of water trucking. This programme introduces water quality standards, monitoring and assessment, and safe water storage and distribution, etc. GVC and RWDS in collaboration with PWA will carry out day to day management of project activities with proper monitoring as well as works quality assurance GVC’s technical team.GVC and RWDS are responsible of carrying out in-depth technical assessment; designing BoQs, preparation of tender documents and following the tender process; following up installation of solar panels; providing capacity building to ensure proper maintenance and sustainability; monitoring of the activity.PDPs will commit to facilitate the implementation of the activities, attending the capacity building sessions and contributing with a 20% of the total cost of the solar panel system.

The analysis will allow to identify if PDPs are producing safer and cheaper water and if this has a positive impact on consumers’ daily life.
