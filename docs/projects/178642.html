Project Summary

The overall goal of the War Child programme is to increase the resilience and wellbeing among vulnerable children and youth in North East and North West Syria. Under this action, War Child would respond to urgent Child Protection needs in Idleb and Aleppo, and expand the service provision for highly vulnerable children in Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor through CFS, structured PSS, awareness raising, and developing specialized service capacity within local partner organizations, so that they can deliver a quality case management service.
This prevention and response approach will address immediate and urgent identified child protection risks while contributing to long-term change. The project aims to reach parents and caregivers as well as local leaders and communities to build a more protective environment for vulnerable children affected by conflict. The project will contribute to multi-sector initiatives (specifically in Education) to support the mainstreaming of Protection outcomes. It will also focus on local organisations to build sustainable technical CP capacity and enhance the quality of the overall response.

4 - Needs

According to WCH need assessment conducted in NES, complex protection vulnerabilities for children, adolescents, and communities are not being addressed – with profound results. A gender and age analysis demonstrates multiple issues affecting different sections of the population. Boys 0-12 are forced to support the household financially and subject to intense peer pressure. Boys 13-17 years experience early marriage, and are more at risk of engaging with armed groups. Girls 0-12 face additional schooling barriers through societal roles and risks perceived in traveling long distances to schools. For girls 13-17 years, early marriage results in negative health and psychosocial outcomes. Protection services are extremely limited due to the broad geographic scope, small numbers of specialized agencies, and trained technical staff. Only six INGOs provide specialized CP services across NES. 71% of KIIs in rural settlements did not know of any services to support children. According to the NWS Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSN 2020), 73% of KIs reported that early marriage occurs within the community as one of the top coping mechanism among girls (47%) and boys (36%). War Child's NWS needs assessment conducted in April 2021 highlights that several psychosocial wellbeing challenges, which impact children, were repeatedly reported by participants across all targeted locations and which result from poverty, loss of a caregiver, lack of access to safe spaces and dropping out of school. Interviewed community leaders and parents outlined several protection gaps, and a prevalence of children engaging in the worst forms of child labor and children subjected to physical abuse at home, on the streets, and on the way to school.

5 - Implementation of activities

Community-based child protection: Due to the needs and lack of psychosocial support (PSS) services in the targeted areas, WCH will provide comprehensive PSS interventions by implementing structured age- and gender-appropriate PSS activities (Deals/6-18 years). For adolescent boys and girls WCH will run Win-Win Deal to enhance conflict awareness, and peacebuilding. For caregivers WCH will offer support to reduce stress, improve wellbeing. Specialized child protection services: Recognising the lack of specialised case management (CM) services in the targeted governorates, WCH and partners will identify caseworkers, and provide intensive CP CM and CPMS+ training based on the inter-agency standards, NES/NWS SOPs, and information standard procedures. Children with specific CP concerns will then be assigned to dedicated caseworkers in each location. WCH has developed internal guidance for CM, including adaptations and recommendations for COVID-19. As a member of the CAAFAG TF in NES, WCH will also support the implementation of the UN Action Plan in both prevention and response capacities.Vulnerability criteria: Beneficiaries will be identified based on vulnerability criteria outline in the case management SOPs and PSS guidelines the criteria will prioritise: UASC, Child engaged in worst form of child labor, CAAFAG, Children exposed to or at risk of any forms of abuse, violence, neglect and/or exploitation; including child survivors of sexual violence. War Child regularly conducts internal monitoring through FGDs with children, parents and CBO staff members. War Child has gender-sensitive and child-focused participatory feedback mechanisms, alongside more general mechanisms for caregivers and the community.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Unsafe CFSspaces Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigation measures: Each of the project locations will be identied and set up using War Child CFS guidelines, which will also be used to train partners on minimum standards for assessing the risk and establishing safe spaces. The spaces will be monitored with contextualised tools, including assessment using the War Child CFS checklist. In all cases War Child will ensure the sites and their surrounding entry and egress points for families and children alike are cleared of ERW. Protection risk: No inclusion and/or participation of disadvantaged groups Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measures: To increase equitable access to the facilities, an inclusion checklist will be applied, so that the needs of disadvantaged groups are considered into program design (IDPs, CwDs, adolescents, girls). The checklist will assess accessibility and meaningful participation in the needs assessment; outreach activities and inclusive development of activities; engagement of marginalised groups; and representation in monitoring and evaluation activities. Protection risk: In areas with IDPs, potential tension between host and IDPs Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measure: Targeting both groups in project activities, and ensure work aims at reducing tensions. Protection Risk: Changing dynamics and sudden displacement may not allow WCH to continue delivering case management services to the same beneficiaries due to loss of communication or inability to access the cases in their new destination Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measure: continue following up with the cases remotely via phone.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection risk: Sexual abuse, or attempt of sexual abuse of a child Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation measures: (1) Train partners to review or set anew a child safeguarding (CS) policy for their respective organisations (2) WCH program team monitors partners and general media postings on activities and alerts child protection/safety focal point at WCH whenever there are concerns (directly discussed with partners and action plan agreed upon) (3) Staff of partners on the ground have one CS focal point who reports to WCH any breach (4) Child safety concerns identified through internal monitoring and evaluation are discussed and followed up with partners For all field staff (including facilitators and teachers) and before activities begin, WCH will provide trainings on CS/PSEA. Trainings will be provided on WCH Safeguarding policy, to ensure awareness, response and compliance across all partners. WCH has developed child-friendly PSEA materials that can be displayed in classrooms and CFS, as well as child-friendly identification and reporting mechanisms, to ensure the safety of children. These are constantly monitored through the project lifecycle by the Child Protection team at WCH, and regular training and updates are conducted with partner field staff and management teams. To avoid inefficient referrals that do not result in service provision, referral pathways will be mapped for all project locations and regularly updated throughout the project lifecycle.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Protection risk: Security incidents directly affecting WCH program and/or partners (staff, facilities, beneficiaries)Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation measures: Partner analyses local conflict dynamics and can anticipate developments impacting humanitarian response. Follow up by WCH on security situation (trends) and measures; security fund included in budget to support cost of material damage to facilities or cost of measure to increase safety; Development of a Duty of Care Policy for partners in coordination with other INGOs; Train CBO partners in security management and analysis of security threats; Ensure contingency measures are in place and agreed upon. Protection risk: Financial mismanagement or fraud by partnersLikelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation measures: Closely follow implementation of financial monitoring procedures agreed with partners; Ensure monthly reporting of all financial expenditure with multiple forms of verification (videos and photos of activities and material with dates, talking to people on the ground). Verify monthly financial report (by programs and financial staff) before next monthly funding transfer. Protection risk: Intervention strategy is not supported by the community/resistance by children, caregivers and/or community membersLikelihood: High. Impact: LowMitigation measures: Train partners to involve children in the design and development of the program, and to listen to their concerns (participatory methodologies). Involve partners in the development of the program – through regular discussion and structured meetings. Ensure involvement and support from parents and community members throughout the program. Continuous dialogue with local leaders.
