Project Summary

The UNICEF child protection program provides support to extremely vulnerable, conflict-affected girls boys and women in South Sudan who are exposed to child protection risks and gender-based violence. The program includes providing case management and family tracing and re-unification (FTR), mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), Reintegration interventions for children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG), Monitoring of grave child rights violations through the monitoring and reporting mechanism (MRM) and critical frontline services. The UNICEF strategy for the implementation will contribute to the component of localization. Within the program budget, 16,298,850 $ is required for 12 months period.

1. Needs

According to the Inter –sectoral severity analysis of the 2024 HNO a total of 79 counties face extreme needs while two are in severe need. Protection concerns remain significant, with affected populations expressing fear over persistent insecurity, protection threats including child protection and gender-based violence, human rights violations. According to IPC report October 2022 – July 2023, the most severe acute food insecurity conditions are in counties where chronic vulnerabilities have been exacerbated by a combination of shocks including floods, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis. According to HNO 2024 3.5 million children in need are at risk of violence, exploitation and abuse requiring lifesaving child protection services. 15 percent of the total target represents people with disabilities. High levels of displacement and family separation affect over one million, causing severe psychological distress and leaving them vulnerable to exploitation, violence abuse and neglect. Displacements separated children from their families. Between January and June 2021, DTM reported on 268 spontaneous and 11 organized instances of population movement comprising of 411,105IDPs (82%) and 90,764 returnees (18%) across ten states and Abyei administrative area. Communal/localized conflicts accounted for most displacement (60%) which further 20 per cent was the result of national disaster (flooding). UNICEF will work in 42 identified counties where IRNAs in 2023 and other assessments at state level have highlighted child protection concerns such as psychosocial distress, family separation, GBV, EORE and recruitment in armed forces, neglect, during intercommunal violence, floods and nutrition insecure counties (phase5) in collaboration with the nutrition and health sectors will also be targeted. More than 18,225 children have been registered as unaccompanied, separated and missing since December 2013 with over 3000 still needing to be re-unified with their families. Since 2013 over 6400 children have been recruited or used by parties in the same period. UNICEF will integrate MHPSS into other sectors like education and health. 650,000 people are estimated to be at risk of injury or death from Land mines and ERWs. The continuous flooding in some areas, inter-tribal conflict and clashes coupled with high levels of food insecurity continues to expose women and girls to GBV including sexual violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. Women and girls in South Sudan experience pervasive violence throughout their lives, within their own homes and as a result of the conflict. While the full magnitude of GBV is unclear, studies indicate that some 65 per cent of the approximately 5.59 million women and girls in South Sudan, have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetimes; this is among the highest rates in the world. Most girls and women experience sexual violence for the first time under the age of 18. While the root cause of GBV is gender inequality, other drivers in South Sudan include low levels of education, the use of violence as a socially accepted norm, poverty, and alcohol consumption. Even in locations where there is high awareness of where to report GBV, there are multiple barriers to survivors seeking support, including self-blame, fear of reprisals, mistrust of authorities, and risk of retaliatory attacks. Stigma and shame impede access to the limited services available for GBV survivors, resulting in untreated sexually transmitted infections, including HIV, and other negative consequences. In addition to gender-based violence concerns, South Sudan continues to grapple with persistent nutrition insecurity stemming largely from disabling socio-economic and political conditions, exacerbated by a long history of shocks, including years of conflict, droughts and flooding as well as suboptimal maternal and childcare practices and gender disparities on the other hands.

2. Project activities

Child protection. Provide community based MHPSS services for girls, boys and caregivers in extremely vulnerable situations and for those facing multiple protection risks and provide specialized activities for adolescents using the UNICEF adolescent kit. [Target: 82500children]. Provide comprehensive case management services and referrals for extremely vulnerable children, including for UASC, including identification, registration and documentation, interim care, family tracing, follow up visits and reunification [Target: 4200]. Provide capacity building to I/LNGOs on CPiE and case management, including utilization of CPIMS+ (4000 sessions). Promote awareness on CPiE for families and care givers (700 sessions).
