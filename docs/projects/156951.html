Project Summary

The project will continue to contribute to reducing stigma and to promoting dignity of the survivors by harnessing a concept of ‘capabilities’ opposed to ‘disabilities’ among the survivors, their families and community members. Also, will contribute in increasing the war survivors’ access to services through multidisciplinary approach and enhanced referral system.
This project will also address acute vulnerability of newly arrived IDPs and the most neglected and least served groups in hosting communities through multi-sectoral approach. Linking up with other existing life-saving interventions (health, nutrition, survivor assistance) of Hand in Hand for Aid and Development , the project will demonstrate high complementarity to serve the urgent needs and long-term supports of the IDPs and communities in northern Syria. In absence of protection institutions and social and family protection networks and structures, the project will introduce alternative social protection to the most vulnerable families through one-off material supports (assistive devices, modiﬁed bedding, communication devices, transportation) which enable to address diversiﬁed needs of individual households to prevent the vulnerable households to be engaged in negative coping mechanism. In addition, the mobile outreach unit can serve as a point of detection/referral any suspected cases of gender based violence. Through in-house health referral mechanism as well as collaboration with other GBV actors, survivors of GBV will have access to service in a timely manner.
Additionally, due to lack of understandings on disability mainstreamed interventions across humanitarian assistance providers in northern Syria, persons and children with impairments and injuries may live inaccessible or dangerous environments and may face discrimination, marginalization and exclusion. The project will address this challenge
this project also aim to protect the civilians in Idleb, Aleppo governorates form the risks of Mines and ERWs (Explosive Remnants of War) through: conducting Risk Education session, build other NGOs capacities, conducting CIS. in addition to provide comprehensive protection service through school-based approach that will increase the access to protection service in schools and increasing the people with disabilities access to the schools

Needs

According to the HNO 2019"1 in 2 people in Syria are potentially at risk from the threat of explosive hazards. Based on current population estimates, 10.2 million men, women, boys, and girls live in the 1,980 communities reporting explosive hazard contamination in the last two years.77 These communities are spread throughout the country and correlate to areas that have seen most explosive incidents over the past 12 months. The impact of explosive hazards on communities is immediate and devastating, with the loss of community members, an increased number of people with permanent injuries and loss of access to properties, basic services and livelihoods. It also prevents the safe delivery of humanitarian assistance, affects population movement and limits the scope for recovery and reconstruction efforts. A wide range of infrastructure, such as private property, schools, hospitals, markets, agricultural land, wells and roads may be blocked by explosive hazards, impeding access to education, health services, marketplaces and safe livelihood opportunities. HIHFAD conducted a survey on “war victims” contracted by “Association for Aid and Relief, Japan” (AAR Japan) in Northern Syria, outreaching over 5,000 men, women and children that sustained injuries and impairment. The survey revealed that almost 60% of these were wounded in airstrikes. Many survivors suffered from severe multiple injuries, including complex fractures, debris penetration or trauma wounds. Despite the very serious nature of these injuries, including the loss of limbs, permanent paralysis caused by spinal cord injuries or sensory impairment, many of the wounded have been unable to access adequate medical care. The medical and rehabilitation needs of those injured remain largely unmet, with an overwhelming demand for medical treatment, rehabilitation and prosthetics. In the absence of appropriate care; untreated injuries will potentially lead to permanent impairment and a more signiﬁcant degree of disability for many. Only 35% of those injured have access to either rehabilitation services or to an assistive device and only 5% have access to both rehabilitation services and an assistive device. Furthermore, only 10% of survivors receive ﬁnancial support or in-kind assistance, facing huge socioeconomic challenges in their lives. During the survey, the HIHS survey team also encountered a high percentage of survivors and their families expressing serious needs of mental health and psychosocial supports (MHPSS). With no access to support, most of the survivors felt ‘forgotten’ or ‘burden’ to their families. Specialized care such as MHPSS and rehabilitation service are particularly limited in the hard-hit areas by the airstrikes. Given the situation above, the project uniquely focuses on outreaching the survivors at their homes, and teaches them how to provide self-care (physical and psychosocial support activities). The impact evaluation that HIHFAD conducted by the end of the ﬁrst phase of the previous survivors assistance project indicates, the beneﬁciaries face diﬃculties to visit the referred facilities, due to the ﬁnancial constrains to cover transportation or lack of mean to reach the facilities. In order to ensure the beneﬁciairies having an access to the required service, the project will provide shuttle service which allow the beneﬁciaries to reach the facilities. Children with disabilities tend to be out of school, not only by their medical conditions, but more social stigma related with disabilities. Missing educational opportunities and disability will create double burden for children to fulﬁll their potentials. Parents, teachers and community members have to be guided through inclusive approaches, not to limit chances of social participation for children. Particularly, teachers have to be taught how to engage children with disabilities in their class rooms.

Implementation of activities

This project will provide the services though the following: 1- Culturally-adapted and age-appropriate self-care guidance booklet on physiotherapy/MHPSS activities which have been developed and tested in previous projects will be implemented in extended area 2- mobile teams staff trained on how to equip survivors and their family members with self-care knowledge and skills will conduct home visits to outreach the survivors. 3- Survivors/family members will be trained on self-care (physio-therapy and MHPSS activities) and their conditions monitored over 12 months 4- referral system will be enhanced by providing transportation and adopting automated system to follow up referred cases. 5- GBV center will be established attached to Atma maternity hospital ,Atme hospital is specialized in providing healthcare for women- GBV and CMR have been identified as an increasing concern and gap in service provision for women in the catchment area. The majority of the hospital staff are trained on basic GBV health interventions but this new department will help to provide greatly needed more advanced protection services. A new physical space will be allocated for this activity in the hospital, a community referral system with be established from the women’s clinic and the WAYCs center to the GBV center. Privacy and confidentiality are paramount to ensure the survivor’s safety and staff will follow strict standard operating procedures. Dedicated and specialized health and psychosocial support will be provided for the survivors of GBV and the Clinical Management of Rape. Three staff members will be dedicated to this approach; one GBV specialized Health Provider, one PSS Worker and one MHGAP trained Doctor who also will provide needed psychological support for other cases referred from other departments when needed (the pediatric and women’s clinics and from the local community itself). the center will Provide the following services: Individual and group counselling / Focused PSSPsychological First Aid (PFA)Focused PSS for GBV survivors (only)Case management for GBV survivorsReferrals of survivorsCMR and other health services for survivorsSpecialized mental health for GBV survivors . 6- Advocacy materials on self-care approach with success stories will be developed and disseminated (to the main stockholders (Donors, UN agencies, INGs, NGos, and local representative.5- Mainstreaming inclusive interventions across humanitarian actors and awareness raising in communities, 6- Making schools accessible for children with disabilities 7- Train teachers on how to teachers engage children with disabilities in their class rooms. 8- Psychological Social Support. 9- Risk Education. 10- Hygiene promotions. 10- provide in kind support to the schools) ﬁrst aid kits and lice shampoo (to enhance the health and hygiene conditions11- Conducting Enhanced Contamination Impact Survey. 12- Encourage safety behavior and increase the knowledge of the risks of Mines and ERWs by conducting RE session. 13- Supporting other NGOs delivering RE to vulnerable communities and IDPs, improving the relevance, integration and reach of RE messaging across all relevant sectors by building their capacities.

Protection Risk Analysis

1-External support to local communities undermines long-term capacity sustainability. mitigation: Develop projects that can be sustained on a long-term basis without NGO support. 2. Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in servicse. 2. GBV service providers safety and security because of their role and PTS for dealing with violence. 3.Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response servicesMitigation: 1. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centred services 2. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral 3. Traditional legal systems put women more at risk, message to be mainstreamed 4. Self care strategies as part of programmes and organization HR policy 5. Include supervisory structures in response services 6. GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. Risk #1 The people attend the RE sessions, particularly males and boys may feel overconﬁdent to respond to explosive hazards without having proper knowledge and equipment to deal with. Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. Mitigation strategy : emphasis on the safety messages (stop , Warn, inform the others). Conduct regular KAPB surveys to monitor changes in risk behaviour, as well as qualitative evaluations to understand the issues underpinning risk behaviour. 3- Risk that local authorities may dictate mine action priorities not in line with humanitarian objectives. Likelihood: High , Impact: medium. Mitigation strategy : Identify and highlight humanitarian priorities independently when cooperating with local councils.4- Impact of activity is minimal on children's protection: Mitigation: 1. Ensure training programs are designed to assess impact of the trainings (Conduct qualitative training for frontline child protection workers) 2. Link with other capacity building initiatives to ensure synergy 3. Support development of a cohort of core child protection trainers in Syria 4. Encourage follow-up, mentoring and coaching initiatives 2-Familiarisation may cause the civilian populace to become overconﬁdent or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards. Mitigation: Emphasis on "Stop, Warn, and Report" messagging in risk education.
