Project Summary

The project aims to support 5 primary health centers (level 2) in Idleb governorate as the following:
- Idleb: Harim, Dana, Deir Hassan, Deir Hasssan PHC G0703145
- Idleb: Harim, Dana, Hezri, Hezri PHC G0703144
- Idleb: Harim, Salqin, Eskat, Eskat PHC.
- Idleb: Harim, Armanaz, Hafsarjah, Hafsarjah PHC.
- Idleb: Harim, Kafr Takharim, Kafr Takharim, Kafr Takharim PHC
The project also aims to contribute to the overall efforts in rural Idleb to reduce mortality and morbidity rates and increase the resilience of the target communities through supporting Aqrabat hospital in Idleb, Harim, Dana, Aqrabat.
The main services delivered are primary and secondary care in multiple wards in the hospital including:
- Internal Medicine
- Maternity and obstetrics.
- ICU
- Pediatrics
- Radiology
- Laboratory
- Emergency Department
- Oxygen Generator
- Pharmacy
- Operations and Trauma management department
- Outpatient Department
- Medical warehouse

4 - Needs

According to OCHA, In January 2020, the humanitarian community tracked about 464,800 IDPs movements across Syria. The largest population movement recorded in Idleb governorate with about 320,600 IDPs,including 297,000 IDPs displaced from within Idleb governorate and 23,500 IDPs displaced from Aleppo governorate. The second-largest population movement was recorded in Aleppo governorate with around 132,600 IDPs, including 86,100 people arriving from within Aleppo governorate and 46,400 IDPs from Idleb governorate. Aqrabat hospital is not funded although it is the second important hospital after Bab Al Hawa hospital in Idleb.The area is crowded with IDPs, most of them are elderly persons and women.The percentage of people with disabilities is very high (around 30%).The health provision is collapsed due to the massive displacementswomen, elderly persons, U5 children, and NCD patients are the most vulnerable groupsAccording to an assessment conducted in 2020, intensified hostilities between government forces and non-state armed groups in the north-western governorate of Idlib have caused the displacement of 700,000 people since the beginning of December 2019. This adds to the 400,000 already displaced between May and August. IDPs are moving further north towards cities and IDP camps. The cold and rains exacerbate humanitarian needs with tents flooded and IDPs exposed to low temperatures while sleeping in precarious shelters or in the open air. Increased fuel prices and a fuel shortage hinder transportation for civilians, but also for aid delivery. Other access difficulties are due to the volatile security situation and muddy roads. Protection, food, shelter and NFI, and health are all areas of concern.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. To provide IDPs and host communities with high quality and free secondary health services at Aqrabat hospital by covering the salaries, running costs and providing medicines and consumables.2. Provision of gynecological and obstetrical services (antenatal and postnatal care, births, cesarean sections, etc.)3. Introduction of a quality management framework and training and coaching (on the job) of health personnel based on current needs.4. Providing surgical services in the fields of orthopedics, general surgery, ophthalmology, ENT, urology, etc.).5. To provide IDPs and host communities with high quality and free primary health services in 5 basic health structures by assuming the running costs and providing medicines and consumables.6. Collection of health and epidemiological data for the Early Warning and Response Network (EWARN) and activation of emergency plans for epidemics.During the project implementation, the monitoring team ensures the regular delivering of the activities and verifies the quality and accessibility of the services, through collecting feedback of the affected population and beneficiaries by interview, checklist and surveys. With note that the sampling methodology considers the most vulnerable people.Beneficiaries, mainly IDPs, are strongly engaged in activities, sessions, and in the field coordination meetings as well. Their experiences are shared, their views/suggestions are considered. Moreover, they were made aware on all services provided. IDA has an effective feedback and complaints mechanism policies, and procedures are in place to facilitate receiving the complaints/concerns safely and with confidentiality through multiple channels/tools.Feedback on performance and best practices, challenges, and lessons learned are thoroughly discussed among the project team and MEAL department.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The team will be trained on how they best deal with serious and life-threatening situations. They will be provided with regular updates and recommendations to assure their safety and security. Coordination with Syrian Civil Defense in areas of implementation will be followed up to make sure their prompt access and response whenever needed. Another coordination with the health facilities, camp managements, and emergency response focal points will be enhanced. In the case of extremely severe insecurity, the work will be suspended based on safety and security focal point instructions with immediate coordination with the donor. Risk management plan considers the following: 1. Attacks on patients and/or project personnel due to a deteriorated security situation. Likelihood: lowMitigation: Immediate cessation of activities and waiting for a change in the situation.2. Destruction of health structures, plundering of furnishings due to a deteriorated security situation.Likelihood: lowMitigation: Suspension of activities; analysis of the situation, rehabilitation and/or replacement of missing equipment.3. Closure of Turkish border crossings for persons and goods, resulting in delays in project implementation.Likelihood: middleMitigation: Stockpiling (medicines, consumables); Sufficient number of medical staff living in Syria.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

To mitigate any risk that could affect the project implementation, IDA will develop Risk Management Matrix to set and plan for any possible risk that could be faced. Access:1- Risk: Access to project location is getting more harder or prevented by the local council or the controlling armed group. Mitigation: IDA has a valid MOU with LC covering project implementation duration. Operational:1- Risk: overlapping of services whether during the planning or implementation phase. Mitigation: IDA is active NGO since 2012, and has active projects in the areas. Also, the locations of these facilities will be shared and reported with the health cluster through HeRAMS system. IDA will manage this issue with the cluster, WHO and local council. 2- Risk: a shortage of needed supplies ( fuel, drugs, medical consumables) Mitigation: IDA has the authorization to deliver any humanitarian assistant through border gates.3- Risk: facility targeting and shifting control-lines. Mitigation: IDA will implement a specific security plan and will make sure the buildings are fortified against targeting. Moreover, IDA will share the locations of the facilities with a de-escalation mechanism to avoid targeting from conflict parties. IDA can evacuate the facilities and shift to other project locations in case of front-lines changes. Financial: 1- Risk: corruption and abuse use of cash. Mitigating measures: IDA will assure that all accounting and financial management practices are in line with IDA financial instruction.
