Project Summary

This project focuses on UNRWA's comprehensive response to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 and protect Palestine refugees and health care workers in the West Bank from the virus while continuing to provide primary health service and addressing the needs of the most vulnerable.
The response includes measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 during primary health delivery at UNRWA health centers through: A) infection prevention and control measures and protection of the health workforce, B) COVID-19 vaccinations; C) continuation of of essential health services with adaptation of services to limit exposure to the virus or isolation for pregnant and lactating women, NCD patients and persons with disabilities; D) mitigating the impact of COVID-19 on mental health; E) community outreach and awareness raising on COVID-19 mitigation measures and; F) training and support to PSEA and complaints mechanisms.

1 - Needs

As the primary humanitarian organization providing health services to Palestine refugees in the West Bank, UNRWA health centres and health points register more than 1,000,000 patient visits every year through its network of 43 health centres, health points and Qalqilyia hospital. 669,150 refugees (approximately 57% women) have active files with UNRWA West Bank Health Programme, and directly benefit from UNRWA's health services. UNRWA provides quality health services to to fulfill the health needs of Palestine refugees in the West Bank, in partnerships with the Ministry of Health (MoH) and other stakeholders. The COVID-19 emergency has put an increased pressure on UNRWA’s already overburdened health system. As of 7 October 2021, 273 health staff (39 per cent of UNRWA health workers) had been infected by COVID-19. Since the beginning of the pandemic, 40,232 refugees in the West Bank had been infected by COVID-19 including 113 new cases the first week of October 2021. Despite these trends, the introduction of triage and rotation systems in UNRWA’s health centers have allowed UNRWA to provide over 620,000 consultations in the West Bank and East Jerusalem from January – September 2021. It is estimated that in 2022, over a period of 12 months, UNRWA will continue providing over 1,000,000 consultations as the situation improves and more refugees are vaccinated. From the beginning of the campaign, 47,846 Palestinians received their first dose at UNRWA health centers and 22,925 persons have received the second dose. It is believed that while the number of visits to health centers was reduced during initial lock-downs, patients are only holding off their visits to health centers. At the same time, misinformation has contributed to change behaviours and altered perceptions of health facilities, and reduced demand for health services including support to high risk pregnancies. Thanks to provision of PPE to all health workers and a steady supply of infection control material to sanitize health centers, UNRWA has been able to continue provision of basic health services through 2021. As the pandemic continues, prevention measures remain critical for ensuring UNRWA ability to provide primary health and mental health services. According to WHO global guidelines, infection prevention and control (IPC) measures are among the most effective tools available to contain the spread of COVID-19 both in health facilities and among the communities. According to UN Women (2021 Gender Alert-presentation) 25% of pregnant women in the State of Palestine are at risk of death during childbirth while also 25% suffer from anemia. Severe lack of resources in the Palestinian healthcare system have further deprioritized women’s access to SRHR. Equipment shortages and restricted mobility negatively impacted maternal healthcare, with essential natal care services being inaccessible particularly to women in remote or restricted areas. UNRWA is in the process of setting up 11 breastfeeding rooms/Women and Girls Safe Spaces in selected health centers where women and girls could learn about PSEA, GBV, reproductive health, breast feeding and other women health related issues. NCD patients and persons with disabilities will also be prioritized in order to ensure continuation of primary health services to these groups while avoiding exposure to COVID-19. Furthermore, the vulnerability of the target population to protection risks following rights violations and the relative power imbalance between humanitarian workers and refugees requires UNRWA to ensure that safe complaints and feedback channels based on consultations with beneficiaries, are accessible and known to the target population, including feedback and complaints on goods and services and more serious complaints related to misconduct and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Consultations undertaken by the PSEA Network for the oPt confirm the need for all organisations to strengthen SEA policies and processes, in addition to MSNA 2021 data which found that 23.8% of refugees (vs 11.1% of non-R) knew how to access complaint mechanisms. Information gaps remain on the needs, preferences, safety and confidentiality concerns of refugee women and adolescent girls, especially women with disabilities, single headed households, pregnant/lactating women and new mothers and elderly women. This information will be obtained through surveys, interviews and focus group discussions UNRWA plans to implement across the West Bank in 2022 to inform and strengthen UNRWA SEA channels and processes based on the consultation and participation of affected groups.

2 - Activities

Under this intervention, UNRWA seeks to provide a comprehensive response aiming to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 and protect Palestine refugees and UNRWA health care workers in the West Bank including East Jerusalem from the virus, while continuing to provide primary health service and addressing the needs of the most vulnerable, including those facing access barriers to health services. The proposed interventions are in response to the COVID-19 emergency. 1. COVID-19 response) Infection Prevention and Control and Protection of the health workforce at UNRWA health centers These activities aim to prevent the spread of COVID-19 infection during primary healthcare delivery at UNRWA health centers, protect health workers and patients, and maintain safe essential health services.1.1 Provision of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) to all 43 UNRWA's Health centers and Health clinics and Qualqilia Hospital for health workers and patients.1.2 Ensuring adequate health staff to patients ratio by introduction a triage system: one additional staff per Health Center to ensure triage. 60 health staff will serve in 43 health centers (an average of 1.2 staff per health center) and 8 staff for Qualqilia Hospital.1.3 Adequate supply of infection control material for all UNRWA health centers and Qualqilia hospital. 2. Vaccination) Accelerate widespread and equitable access to and delivery of safe and effective COVID-19 vaccinations for refugee and non refugee populations.2.1 Additional 26 health staff, including nurses and clerks to implement the vaccination campaign. 2.2 Equipment to ensure safe delivery of vaccines to end users including freezers where needed. 3. Maintaining essential health services while adapting to ensure safe delivery of essential health services for all conditions and vulnerabilities.3.1 Home delivery of medication to certain NCD patients categories (bed-redden, high risk NCD cases etc)3.2 Strengthening of health hotline to limit unnecessary exposure to COVID-19. 3.3 Introduction of 24 community midwives who will work to improve the maternal care services and to enroll women in MCH services and visit high risk pregnant cases at home to limit exposure to COVID-19. Community midwives will play a key role in educating women on prenatal health and nutrition, responsible for the physical, mental, and emotional care as well as providing counselling and education for the women and her family throughout pregnancy and childbirth, and promoting health in the wider community.3.3.1 Training and community consultations by midwives on the use of Women and Girls Safe Spaces and awareness raising on sexual and reproductive health topics, as well as GBV.3.4 Provision of additional support to persons with disabilities whose vulnerability and isolation might have increased during COVID-19 and strengthening of integrated services.3.4.1 Training for frontline health staff on disability inclusion; 3.4.2 Training on disability screening to improve identification; 3.4.3 Devices in health centers to facilitate visits of PwDs3.4.4 Referral for up to 200 vulnerable PwDs for additional services 4. Mitigate the impact of COVID-19 on mental health.4.1. Introduce 19 psychosocial counselors in UNRWA health centers 4.2 Training of 19 Psychosocial Counselors, 24 midwives and 6 medical officers on PSEA. 5. Risk communication, community engagement (RCCE) in partnership with WHO, UNICEF and MoH 6. Safe, accessible and child-friendly mechanisms are in place for reporting complaints and feedback, including complaints of SEA, particularly in high-risk areas. 6.1 Consultations are held with women and girls, including women with disabilities, single headed households and elderly women to inform the feedback and complaint channels for SEA. This includes a survey, interviews and focus group discussions identifying reporting barriers and mitigation measures. Existing complaints channels, SOPs and PSEA strategy will be adapted to reflect feedback from consultations with beneficiaries. 6.2 This project has built-in clear process for receipt and referral of complaints of PSEA in accordance with CBCM SOPs and internal SOPs on Case Management which support frontline staff to safely identify and refer protection cases within the agency. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor centered approach in how the complaint is handled and support is offered to the survivor. UNRWA works closely with the PSEA Network, including compulsory reporting and will also strengthen existing processes and raise issues requiring technical support and/or external referral through this forum. 6.3 Age and gender-sensitive PSEA awareness raising materials are disseminated to all Women and Girls Safe Spaces containing information on rights, safe complaint channels and how to access survivor-centered assistance. 6.4 24 midwives,19 counselors and six medical officers including those operating complaint channels and feedback reporting mechanisms will complete at least one PSEA training during the project period, to strengthen SEA safeguards for reporting SEA and referrals. 6.5 Midwives, psychosocial counselors and dedicates medical officers will indirectly engage in the HCT oPt PSEA Network, through the active participation of UNRWA PSEA focal points and senior management, including contribution to the development of interagency workplans and SOPs.

3 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured by continuous provision of health services to Palestine refugees despite the pandemic. More specifically it will be measured by the following indicators: - 100% of UNRWA healthcare workers are protected with the provision of PPE - All 43 UNRWA health centers and Qualqilia Hospital remain operational during COVID-19 outbreak - 667,215 refugees have continued access to primary health care despite COVID-19 - 5,000 women received sexual reproductive health services - Community Midwives conduct 72 community awareness session per month - 100% of relevant front-line staff receive training on PSEA - 50% of vulnerable persons with disabilities referred by UNRWA social workers are supported with assistive devices - 100 frontline health staff receive training on disability inclusion and integrated services - UNRWA Standard Operating Procedures on PSEA, linked through the interagency CBCM are in place and endorsed by senior management - 40% of children and adults have access to a safe channel to report sexual exploitation and abuse. - 100% of established Women and Girls Safe Spaces provide information on the prohibition of SEA, how to report and receive assistance - 60% of health center visitors are reached with key messages and awareness-raising material on PSEA (disaggregated by type of PSEA communication materials developed for each population group identified). - 49 midwives, psychosocial counselors and medical officers are trained to recognize SEA and know where to report - A standard clause on PSEA included in UNRWA contracts - PSEA technical focal points at UNRWA attend PSEA Network meetings and actively contribute to network outcomes

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback and complaints relating to specific cases is followed up according to internal SOPs used by health staff to adapt services, interventions and referrals for support services to meet the needs of families and individuals. In addition, project staff collects and analyses feedback and recommendations from the affected populations to inform decision making about the scope and modality of service provision and overall design of the project. As part of the satisfaction survey, beneficiaries are provided with details about the feedback hotline as well as specific information about UNRWA’s zero tolerance policy on sexual exploitation and abuse and how to safely report SEA cases and support services available. Feedback obtained through consultations on SEA complaints channels will also inform improvements to accessibility for vulnerable populations.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The project adopts a variety of mechanisms to facilitate the meaningful participation, feedback and complaints from all population groups. This includes a dedicated free phone hotline, in-person satisfaction surveys, email and phone hotlines for SEA and serious misconduct complaints. Recognizing the limitations of these mechanisms to persons with disabilities, children and the elderly, and Bedouin/herder communities, UNRWA staff also reach out to these populations through dedicated focus group discussions and interviews conducted by relief services social workers. Technical protection staff working thematically on SEA, child protection, gender and disability inclusion will also advise where additional mechanisms should be developed and existing mechanisms strengthened to improve access to these populations, including through training of health staff, awareness raising of feedback and complaints channels with community based organisations representing these groups, linking with community-based complaint mechanisms and improving cross-programmatic referrals within UNRWA departments (education, infrastructure and camp improvement and relief and social services). The UNRWA Protection Department has also taken the lead in enhancing the accountability to affected persons (AAP) in UNRWA's service delivery in the context of COVID-19, establishing online platforms for information sharing and feedback, hotlines, and conducted dedicated outreach. Finally, the COVID-19 emergency has increased demands on UNRWA and its services and presented challenges to establishing appropriate communication with communities, as well as to accountability mechanisms in the context of COVID-19 restrictions. The Protection Department has led the establishment of dedicated AAP lens to the COVID19 response, including drafting key communications to the refugee community.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project adopts a do no harm and conflict sensitivity approach, including specific measures to ensure that identified risks are addressed within the action and unintended negative consequences are minimised. UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. UNRWA’s Case Management SOPs ensure that protection cases are identified and referred to specialised support services. SOPs cover compulsory reporting on SEA and support to survivors. A dedicated psychological consultation hotline is available to all refugees. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff, including risk mitigation for SEA. On a biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. In 2019 UNRWA developed a new information management system for the collection of sensitive protection monitoring and documentation data; specifically the system now separates out biographical data from incident related data, with individual cases being only referred to through generated case code. In 2019/8 UNRWA developed a free feedback and complaints hotline for the use of all beneficiaries under this project. For the purposes of ensuring that briefings involving affected persons, are carried out in a way that prioritises the safety and dignity of these persons, UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. This includes a checklist to guide all staff working in this capacity. UNRWA has adapted its case management in response to difficulty of physical access to concerned individuals/households. UNRWA has established a dedicated psychological consultation hotline available to all refugees. In addition, UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is dedicated to the application of the humanitarian principles in all of its service delivery. Specific measures are taken to ensure that UNRWA's services are neutral and impartial in nature, including through regular monitoring throughout its operations. For the purposes of this project, UNRWA has developed, in coordination with the West Bank Protection Consortium, a dedicated vulnerability assessment tool in order to prioritise assistance to the most vulnerable refugees. This tool was pioneered by UNRWA and has since been adopted by other members of the Consortium and is to be applied throughout the West Bank. UNRWA takes measures to ensure that its assistance under this project is available to all persons, and takes dedicated action to ensure that individuals with specific needs are reached. UNRWA takes actions to ensure that persons with disability, with movement and access restrictions, the elderly, women and children are targeted during outreach and communication - and also are key stakeholders in all community interactions and in accountability processes. Services delivered are tailored to the needs of individuals, including for those with specific needs, the case management services delivered are developed in coordination with the affected persons and are guided by their specific needs. Specific needs according to age, gender and disability are identified at the initial assessment stage of case management, for those affected by damage to residential property, and those exposed to displacement. The individual case plans are tailored to respond to these needs, for example provision of services linked to mobility issues amongst the elderly.
