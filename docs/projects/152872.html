Project Summary

The 2019, SSHF is responding to deteriorating Health situation that resulted from multiple displacements and has compounded the Health needs of an already vulnerable population across the country. Conflict and economic crisis have taken a toll on health in South Sudan. Disease outbreaks have lasted longer than ever and reached previously unaffected areas, weakening already vulnerable people’s ability to cope with multiple shocks (UNOCHA 2018). From mid-2018 there is a looming threat of Ebola and other outbreaks like rift valley fever that has ravaged different parts of the country amidst seasonal upsurge in epidemic prone diseases like malaria, AWD, meningitis etc.
It is estimated that 3,571,191 individuals consisting of women, men and children who are vulnerable and will be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2019. The number of people targeted for health care presents an 8% increase from that in 2018, this is worsened by limited access to quality water, infection prevention and control measures, health care waste management, food insecurity and deteriorating nutrition indicators necessitating the need for integrated approach to service delivery with a focus on health security to mitigate burden from infection and improve the quality of life of the beneficiaries.
Maiwut County is one of the counties that have been disrupted by the civil war. The violence that erupted in December 2013 disrupted health system, farming cycle and livelihood of most people in the county. A county of over 116,244 people was seen deserted with over 280,000 individuals crossed to Ethiopia. This made the international NGO running the health services in the county to evacuate leaving the provision of health services in the hand of the CHD and some National NGO with weak capacity. Currently CASS is supporting one PHCU with Support from SSHF. The lead health partner in the county is Nile hope supporting 7 health facilities, they took over from Goal but they are not yet fully on ground leaving a huge gap in terms of health service provision, NIDO support two health facilities, Relief international and ADRA are other partners on ground. The total population targeted for humanitarian assistance is 57,152. CASS target for 2019 is 30% of the targeted population which is 17,146.
In Rubkona county, Bentiu POC which was established in December 2013 with 117,046 people seeking protection in the site as of May 2017 (IOM data base), is getting depopulated steadily. Between the years, the population in the POC kept fluctuating; the cluster projected a population of 76,110 as of 2018. The latest BMR data from IOM for Bentiu and Rubkona towns stands at 36,617 individuals.
Currently the population of IDPS outside the POC in Rubkona is increasing tremendously and with the peace agreement signed, more of the population in the POC is expected to move out to settle in Rubkona and the surrounding areas. The available health facilities in the area are not sufficient to meet the needs of this population.
The targeted population for humanitarian need is about 136,603 (38,555 females and 37,947males). The IDP population outside the POC is estimated at 54,090 which is expected to increase with the peace agreement signed. CASS being the lead health partner in the County is targeting the whole population in need targeted for humanitarian assistance in 2019.

Needs

The protracted violence and displacement combined with a high disease burden, poor access to sanitation (less than 7%) high illiteracy rates (as high as 88% for women and 63% for men), and high levels of poverty has led to very poor health care provision across the country. Greater Upper Nile and Unity state takes the lead.Rubkona CountyIn Rubkona County, Health response activities services which were mainly humanitarian driven; has been interrupted and frozen due to erupted fire arm clashes between the arm opposition and the government forces in Nhialdu Payam during the month of August 2017 which led to loss of facility assets and access constraints due to insecurity. Health service delivery has been on and off since June 2015 with only one health facility (Rubkona PHCC) serving constantly. As a result, health care service delivery has been compounded. The uptake of Skilled Birth Attendance (SBA) is very poor at 16.4% and only 18.3% of pregnant women deliver in a health facility. These factors contribute to a maternal mortality ratio of 789 per 100,000 live births (adjusted MMR, Unicef, 2017), among the highest in the world. Malaria, acute respiratory diseases, and diarrheal diseases are the leading causes of death and are especially dangerous for children under the age of five. Vaccination coverage for children is abysmal. The SHHS reported that only 3.5% of children in Unity State received all of their necessary vaccinations on time, and coverage data for the different antigens included in the routine EPI program reached 25% of infants between 0 and 12 months of age. Moreover, the SHHS reports that 23.0% of children in Unity State were severely malnourished, the highest rate in the country by nearly eight percentage points. A lack of adequate access to clean drinking water and sanitation facilities exacerbates risk of poor health. Through this Project, CASS will support 3 PHCCs and 2 PHCU and 4 Mobile clinic in Rubkona County. This will be backed with the iCCM and the Community Health Programs to complete the package of the essential health care in the county. Maiwut CountyMaiwut County is one of the counties in former upper nile State now Latjor state. It has an estimated population of 116,244 individuals, predominantly occupied by the Nuer Jikany and exclusively controlled by the SPLM IO forces. It is characterized by medium to unknown risk of displacements. Throughout the conflict displaced persons have travelled to Maiwut county for refuge or through it into Ethiopia, OCHA consistently report the presence of IDP’s along Maiwut – Ethiopian boarder as of 2016 about 280,000 crossed along that boarder into Ethiopia. The County is served with a network of 9 health facilities, 1 PHCC and nine PHCU’s. The development support to health services is from the world bank. CASS is supporting 1 mobile Clinic and 1 PHCU to provide comprehensive primary health care services for the fluid population in Maiwut. This project is targeting both men and women though the priorities remain women and children. The project is environmental friendly.

Beneficiary accountability is an attitude and an approach that promotes the dignity of those affected by enabling them to influence decision-making and play a more active role in their own recovery. To be truly accountable, project staff must accept responsibility for their actions and agree that they can be called upon to give an account of how and why they have acted / failed to act. For the purposes of this project, beneficiary accountability is understood to be implemented across 5 standard practices: Participation, Transparency, Feedback and Complaints, Monitoring and Evaluation, and Staff Competencies and Attitudes.

Briefly describe how

CASS believes that employees should be afforded the opportunity to work in an environment free of sexual harassment. Sexual harassment is a form of misconduct that undermines the employment relationship. No employee, either male or female, should be subjected verbally or physically to unsolicited and unwelcome sexual overtures or conduct. CASS will not tolerate any behavior that amounts to sexual harassment and any officer or employee found to have committed sexual harassment shall be subjected to disciplinary action, up to and including dismissal. Any officer or employee, who experiences or witnesses any act of sexual harassment in the workplace, shall report the same immediately to the Committee on Decorum and Investigation. They may also report acts of sexual harassment to any other member of CASS's management or ownership. All allegations of sexual harassment will be quickly investigated. To the extent possible, the identity of the officer or employee shall remain confidential and that of any witnesses and the alleged harasser will be protected against unnecessary disclosure. When the investigation is completed, all parties will be informed of the outcome of the investigation. A Committee on Decorum and Investigation shall be constituted and shall be composed of the management and the employees’ representative to receive complaints, investigate and hear sexual harassment cases. The Committee shall develop its own rules in the settlement and disposition of sexual harassment cases. The Committee shall also develop and implement programs to increase understanding and awareness about sexual harassment.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Risk assessments will regularly be conducted by CASS Field Coordinator and all movement to project site will be coordinated with the relevant Cluster leads, UNDSS, UNMISS and local authorities/RRC to ensure clearance for safe passage, flexible response and effective team work. The CASS Juba security advisor support will be available at all times to respond to queries and provide advice. CASS field teams will also be sensitive towards staffing and the underlying conflict tensions. Deployment of national staff will be done within their home areas, where the risk of ethnic violence is minimal. In addition, all staff working in the field are already provided with hostile environments awareness training (HEAT) and regular security briefings. Surge capacity will be used where appropriate in order to reduce risks in areas with a high potential for ethnic conflict.
