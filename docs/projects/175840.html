Project Summary

In response to identified priority protection needs, including HLP needs, DRC will deliver General Protection services for conflict and displacement affected communities. DRC will implement protection activities in a variety of locations including hard to reach locations through both static, outreach and mobile modalities. In doing so DRC aims to contribute to the overarching intersectoral strategic objectives for the 2021 HRP through contributing to the four protection cluster strategic objectives of:
- Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas
- Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience
- Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other population
- Enhance protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response
DRC general protection activities will ensure protection services are available to women and girls, boys and men. DRC will facilitate access to life-saving protection assistance by working with community networks to identify and strengthen positive coping mechanisms and develop mitigation strategies for identified protection risks. DRC will strengthen referral mechanisms for specialised services; conduct awareness raising and information campaigns focused on rights including HLP rights, assistance opportunities and threats; establish or support Community Based Protection Networks (CBPN) for primary prevention and monitoring; provide capacity building support to duty bearers, including on HLP and provide IPA based on needs, including for resolution of HLP related concerns. Evidence-based programming and advocacy across the response will be be supported through regular protection monitoring and protection assessments, including HLP monitoring and HLP assessments, based on pre-identified triggers, with mechanisms to ensure inclusion of persons with specific needs such as persons living with disabilities, elderly people, adolescent girls at risk, and female headed households.
DRC currently has strong static presence in Unity and Upper Nile State. DRC will continue to provide static and outreach services in these locations. DRC also currently has 2 Mobile Protection Teams that respond to emergencies in hard to reach locations where there are limited services and high needs. These mobile teams will also provide General Protection services outlined below. As such the locations listed as targeted areas for intervention include those locations where DRC has static presence as well as locations where DRC may respond as part of the country wide mobile protection response team capacity.
DRC activities in the sectors of SNFI, FSL, CCCM and other sub sectors of protection including GBV will complement protection services and allow for multi-sectoral service delivery in response to multi-faceted protection needs. DRCs multi sectoral capacity will allow for opportunities for strong integration between SNFI and HLP in particular which is crucial to ensure a do no harm approach to service delivery.
Overall, DRC aims to reach approximately 34,800 vulnerable individuals affected by displacement and conflict.

1 - Needs

Despite the advent of the September 2018 revitalized peace agreement, South Sudan will continue to experience alarming humanitarian needs in 2021. By April 2021, it is predicted 7.24 million people, 60% of the population will be facing severe acute food insecurity (IPC Report). In December 2020 1,042,000 people were affected by floods (ICCG Flood Report). Given political, economic and social developments, it is anticipated that 8.3 Million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance or protection according to the 2021 HNO, as human rights abuses continue to be perpetrated by all parties to the conflict with near impunity (UNHCR, Position on Returns). While at a national level the overall security situation has improved, in 2020 there was an increase in intercommunal clashes, localized violence and rising tensions fueled by political parties in Juba. The global COVID-19 pandemic’s negative impact on South Sudan’s economy and individual people’s purchasing power and coping mechanisms, has contributed to rise in violence and increased already present vulnerabilities. This triple shock of violence, flooding and covid-19 has severely impacted peoples physical and mental wellbeing and safety especially already marginalized communities are expected to be affected by the deteriorating conditions in 2021. The protective environment in South Sudan remains adversely impacted by conflict-related displacement and violence, rights violations against civilians (especially child’s rights), sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), housing, land and property (HLP) rights and weather-related displacement. In certain states the political vacuum continues to perpetuate localized organized violence, impacting the safety and security context, affecting the coping mechanisms, psychosocial capacities and vulnerabilities of women, men, girls and boys. This is further exacerbated by the lack of access to justice and general rule of law, fueling local tensions for people residing in POC sites. This lack of access to justice and general rule of law leaves both individual and community level HLP concerns unresolved and only a few County Land Authorities (CLAs) and Payam Land Councils (PLCs) established. HLP rights have played a major role in perpetuating conflict in Unity and Upper Nile state in particular and will continue to adversely impact the protective environment if remain unresolved. In hard-to-reach areas a serious challenge remains access to information regarding available services as well as a lack of female representation in self-governance structures to address gender-sensitive issues, particular issues related to women’s access to their HLP rights. This insecure environment and lack of access to basic services, as well as specialized services such as legal/HLP assistance services, has led to high prevalence of protection risks particularly among women, children and persons living with disabilities, including survival sex, child labor, child or forced marriage, separation, psychological distress and mental health problems. Based on a recent Protection assessment conducted in Panyikang and Fashoda in January 2021, in two counties which are predicted IPC level 4 in the lean season, there are already significant protection including HLP, safety and displacement concerns due to severe flooding, exacerbated food insecurity and increased pressure on diminished household resources. The transition of the POC sites to IDP camps, and sustainability of peace, may trigger increased return movements into target locations, thereby increasing the pre-existing needs and gaps in these locations, as well as place increased pressure on already insufficient services. People movement associated with the transition of the POC sites to IDP camps will also likely see a rise in HLP issues, particularly in Malakal county (a target location of this HLP project) which is classified as catastrophic HLP (severity 5) in the 2021 South Sudan HNO

2 - Project activities

The specific detailed activities that DRC will implement under the sector of general protection includes mobile and static protection assessments and monitoring, including HLP assessments and monitoring, in order to identify protection risks and trends to inform prevention and response programming as well as support evidence based advocacy. Protection monitoring and assessment activities will include direct observation, FGDs, KIIs and documentation of protection incidents at protection desks. Protection monitoring reports and protection needs assessments will be shared on a need-to-know basis with partners and clusters for evidence informed programming and advocacy. DRC will also facilitate safe quality referrals to relevant services for individuals in need. DRC will use protection monitoring to identify trends related to access to services and undertake advocacy to address identified gaps in service provision and the referral pathway. DRC will also provide IPA, in the form of cash or in-kind assistance, to address protection risks that require immediate action. This includes the provision of IPA alongside HLP counselling or HLP legal aid to facilitate resolution of an HLP issue. DRC will conduct information dissemination on available services, prevention and mitigation of specific protection risks and relevant rights and entitlements. This includes HLP legal awareness raising activities which will focus on disseminating information on South Sudanese customary law and processes, South Sudanese legal frameworks (such as the Land Act, Local Government Act, and Draft Land Policy) and relevant international laws related to HLP rights. Given the patriarchal societal norms in South Sudan, and the gendered consequences of conflict and displacement, additional focus will be given to women’s HLP rights. DRC will also engage in community-based protection preparedness and risk mitigation activities given traditional community-based protection mechanisms have been damaged by conflict and displacement. DRC seeks to continue capacity building of community based structures, including authority figures and community leaders. This will include capacity building on HLP subjects. DRC also seeks to continue strengthening CBPNs through capacity building, mentorship and development of Community Action Plans for improving self-protection capacities and strengthening positive coping mechanisms. DRC will provide legal counselling and legal aid to persons in need with a focus on HLP. Legal counselling will be provided on an individual or household basis and will focus on providing targeted information to a specific HLP issue. Legal aid may be in the form of enabling access to security of tenure by engaging with local authorities who can provide land documents, referrals to administrative bodies who can provide assistance with land disputes; engaging with authorities to prevent forced evictions and seek alternative solutions. These forms of assistance are localised and depend on the contextual analysis and needs in a specific location. A combination of these forms of assistance may be appropriate and necessary. In many situations, several people are affected by the same HLP issue, which requires a community-based solution and response.DRC will provide focused non specialized PSS for those in need through the provision of PFA during times of crisis. DRC will also support community based protection networks and other structures with capacity building on PFA to promote the sustainability of the enhanced protective environment. During the provision of safe quality referrals and awareness raising activities DRC will also engage in discussions on social and emotional wellbeing that further support individuals social and emotional wellbeing.Activities will be conducted by static teams who also conduct outreach as well as DRC Mobile Response Teams that respond to emergencies in hard to reach locations where there are limited services and high needs.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Cash IPA is based on market functionality as cash is only provided, instead of in-kind, when the support required to meet the protection outcome is feasibly able to be purchased (eg cash for transportation services where such services are present).

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash will be provided as IPA support of required to address an HLP need where another form of support such as information provision, referrals, advocacy and similar are noyt appropriate. Cash will be provided only if no other actor can provide support, and the HLP issue can only be resolved or reduced through this means. The cash assistance is provided on an individual basis and only following a thorough risk analysis.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DRC conducts regular awareness raising with beneficiaries on PSEA, including reporting mechanisms. DRC has IEC materials available in multiple formats that are displayed/shared in order to raise awareness on PSEA and ensure that those with different disabilities and other diversities have varying options for accessing information on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC has IEC materials available in multiple formats that are displayed/shared in order to raise awareness on the feedback and complaints mechanisms and ensure that those with different disabilities and other diversities have varying options for accessing information on the FCRM. DRC also conducts regular in person awareness raising with communities on the presence of the complaints and feedback mechanisms. DRC has several entry points for lodging complaints comprising of in-person office visits, Let us know box (suggestion box) and through community visits (either orally at or formal written complaints, at both the FCRM desks or to other DRC staff), and through intermediaries working with DRC e.g. implementing partners and community representatives. Having several mechanisms allows for beneficiaries to choose a method that is most comfortable and practical for them, acknowledging the different barriers to accessing FCRM mechanism, and therefore different preferences, for those living with disabilities and other diversities. Several mechanisms also ensure there is at least one mechanism in place in all locations.. Apart from the aforementioned channels, DRC will also gather feedback from the vulnerable groups through community meetings and surveys. DRC Staff have been trained to handle personal complaints and inquiries in a confidential manner, and protection issues are referred to DRC protection team or to protection partners to ensure sensitive issues are handled by qualified staff only. DRC MEAL staff are also trained on AGD mainstreaming which seeks to ensure the FCRM mechanisms managed by these staff is inclusive. Beneficiary data, including MEAL data is always disaggregated by age and gender (including qualitative and quantitative data) and therefore DRC is able to track trends in the age and gender of those submitting feedback and complaints and therefore identify any challenges for engaging with the FCRM faced by different populations.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

DRC has IEC materials available in multiple formats that are displayed/shared in order to raise awareness on the feedback and complaints mechanisms. DRC conducts regular awareness raising with communities on the presence of the complaints and feedback mechanisms.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

DRC has a functional Feedback, Complaints, Response Mechanism (FCRM) that allows beneficiaries to give feedback and complaints regarding the activities being implemented in their respective locations. The DRC F-CRM desks are set up to receive complaints and questions from beneficiaries and follow-up with feedback, including referrals to external service-providers or to the DRC protection desks when appropriate, either immediately or within a reasonable timeframe, depending on the nature of the complaint. DRC has a standardized FCRM form where all feedback and complaints are captured. All data captured on the FCRM forms is then uploaded onto a password protected database where all feedback and complaints are tracked until they are closed. Furthermore, beneficiary complaints and feedback will also be captured when conducting post distribution monitoring (PDM). Beneficiaries will be able to provide their opinions on how DRC interventions can be improved and they will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or make a complaint.
