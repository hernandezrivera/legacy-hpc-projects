Project Summary

Medical Teams aims to reduce excess morbidity and mortality among vulnerable populations, with a focus on migrants and displaced mothers and children, through community-based interventions linked with increased access to quality essential health services.
Community Health Interventions. Medical Teams will provide ongoing training and support for 10 Community Health Volunteers (CHVs), to identify households in need and support health education and referrals to clinics. Medical Teams will exceed SPHERE standards of 500-1000 beneficiaries per CHV. The CHVs will be equipped to deliver education (both in person and virtually) to households on 1) MHPSS and psychological first aid (PFA), 2) Sexual and Reproductive health and health rights, including contraception, SGBV treatment and prevention, 3) maternal and child health, including infant and young child feeding (IYCF), and, 4) community based surveillance for quick identification and reporting of outbreaks of illnesses such as COVID-19, dengue, and vaccine-preventable diseases, as well as monitor trends in maternal and child health (including acute malnutrition) to elicit a response of local health authorities in the community (Medical Teams is a member of the MOH Working Group on Community Based Surveillance). CHVs will also use tools such as the Maleta Mavex pedagogical kit to provide SHRH education and SGBV prevention. The community health program will support enrollment in EPS through the provision of information and multisectoral Health Points in collaboration with other I/NGOs working in protection and legal services.
Access to essential health services and affiliation with the health system. Beneficiares without EPS will be provided essential primary healthcare services – Maternal Health, Sexual and reproductive Health, Non-communicable diseases, treatment for childhood illnesses based on Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses, and MHPSS – at no cost through contracted public and private clinics. Costs are either subsidized by Medical Teams or by other funding sources available to the clinics (such as through UNHCR or IOM). Subsidized care includes consultations, laboratory or radiological diagnostics, and medications, and Medical Teams staff are present at facilities as patient advocates to ensure access.
Those who do not have full affiliation with the health system will be provided with education, advice and general support on how, when and where to do this. Medical Teams staff and volunteers help to identify and address local barriers to affiliation to enable the target population to access health care services in the long term.
