Project Summary

Solidarités International (SI) and its national partner Action for Humanity (AFH), through ongoing project implementation and participatory needs assessments, have found that most IDPs (in camps, informal settlements and among host community members in villages) continue to face critical WaSH needs and their Shelter locations lack water tanks and sustainable solid waste solutions
(garbage bins, sewage networks). Addressing these needs is particularly pertinent in light of the COVID-19 pandemic.

The proposed action applies a basic needs approach, by combining an integrated response package of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WaSH) and Shelter & Settlements services to IDPs and vulnerable members of host communities in Azaz, Afrin, Al Bab, Ghandhora and Jarablus. Beneficiaries vulnerability is decided based on objective criteria such as living conditions (unsafe, undignified) and or lack of income capacity. Targeting criteria used in this project are aimed at selecting vulnerable households directly affected by the conflict, often by using a geographic blanket approach.

In addressing the most urgent WaSH needs, SI and AFH have identified the following response activities.
1. Providing a full WaSH package in IDP sites to ensure water supply access and corresponding water quality monitoring, operational maintenance of sanitation facilities, Hygiene Promotion activities, solid waste management and rehabilitation of water networks.
COVID-19 prevention measures are integrated and mainstreamed by the provision of COVID-19 kits, and the availability of hygiene materials, as per cluster recommendations.

Both SI and AFH are targeting water stations and network and are therefore able to reach a much higher number of direct beneficiaries than in the current BHA funded project.

4 - Needs

More than 2.7 million people remain displaced in NWS. According to the CCCM Cluster,there were more than 32,000 displacements in northwest Syria in the month of December2020, with departures mainly from Afrin, Idleb and Ariha. The main locations receivingnewly displaced people include Afrin, Sharan and Raju sub-districts. The top three needsreported for newly displaced persons were winterisation (23%), NFIs (20%), and shelter(18%). Significant gaps in water and sanitation services have led to an increase of peoplein need of these services, to over 2.6 million people in the last 3 months. Further gaps areexpected to compound with the existing gaps in the coming months. SI Rapid Needs andSecurity Assessments in NWS have shown a continuous need for humanitarian responsein the sectors of Shelter & Settlements and Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WaSH), inparticular for newly displaced persons, as several families now share the same shelters,and some are living in tents. The main needs assessed included Shelter and winterizationassistance in addition to WaSH services and infrastructure networks.

5 - Implementation of activities

SI and its partner AFH will apply the most pertinent and appropriate modality ofintervention to ensure timely, effective and relevance assistance, in full respect of thehumanitarian principles and Do No Harm-related concerns. SI and AFH are proposing alife-sustaining implementation strategy in response to the protracted nature of the crisis.IDPs coming from different parts of Syria have been staying in NWS with little perspectiveof return in the short- to medium term; the situation now calls for a new approach to findmore sustainable and cost-efficient ways to provide vital services while limiting itsimpacts on the IDPs decision to stay or return. Activities under this Action will allow foralternative ways to provide services at lower cost, by working with Community HealthPromoters and including maintenance of latrines. Strengthening community mobilizationto foster appropriate practices and ownership over the WaSH facilities management iscritical for this shift to materialize. Based on SI’s experience in NWS under previous BHAfunding, cash distribution is the more suitable modality of intervention for some sectorsof intervention, such as winterization assistance. SI recently finalized its cash for winterdistributions in its current BHA funded program; feedback from beneficiaries confirms it isthe preferred modality of assistance as it gives the beneficiaries the right to prioritize theirmost urgent basic needs.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks include: • Inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages acrosslocations • Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees due to variedassistance packages • Abuse of power by staff distributing goods or delivering services tovulnerable populations. • Road accidents, physical harm • Restricted access (due toinsecurity of COVID-19 lockdowns) • COVID-19 public health concerns. Mitigationmeasures for the identified risks are integrated in SI’s implementation strategy, startingwith transparent selection criteria agreed upon in coordination with the relevant clusters.The risk of abuse of power is mitigated by thorough vetting of locally recruited staff, whoall abide by the SI Code of Conduct. SI also has a Feedback and Complaints ResponseMechanism (FCRM) in place that allows for real-time feedback from beneficiaries andother stakeholders. The FCRM is an accountability tool that simultaneously facilitates thecollection of lessons learnt that will be used to improve future interventions.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks: Gender-based violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), tendsto increase during times of crisis. Risks of this occurring further increase insituations of aid dependency and power imbalances. SI is cognizant of the inherentrisks related to service delivery, notably for cash and in-kind distributions. To ensurethat assistance delivered is safe, relevant and timely, SI has accountability systemsand procedures in place. Mitigation and prevention measures: SI is determined toprevent the sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workersand reaffirms its goal of achieving full implementation of the six core principlesadopted by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee Task Force on Prevention andResponse to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. SI's PSEA Policy complements theCode of Conduct and Protection Policy, both of which state that it is strictlyforbidden for any employee i) to use its position to obtain any favor, includingsexual favors, and ii) to have sexual intercourse with minors. SI’s contracts andpartnership agreements include a standard clause requiring sub-contractors toadopt policies that prohibit SEA and to take adequate measures to prevent andrespond when required. A Whistleblowing Policy and a Feedback and ComplaintsResponse Mechanisms Framework are key pillars of SOLIDARITESINTERNATIONAL Policies package. SI has PSEA focal points to identify andaddress any PSEA concerns and implements a referral system when require
