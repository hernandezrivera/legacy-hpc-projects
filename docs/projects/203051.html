Project Summary

In recent years, the protection context in Lebanon has drastically deteriorated. Lebanon has witnessed a series of compounded crises, placing the country on the brink. On top of the ongoing refugee crisis, Lebanon now faces the most severe economic and financial collapse in its recent history, further compounded by COVID-19, political turmoil, and the Beirut Port explosion. These conditions have deepened pre-existing vulnerabilities and compounded underlying issues of gender inequality and discrimination in Lebanon, resulting in heightened risk of gender-based violence (GBV), and in households being forced to adopt negative coping mechanisms. These problems have also increased tensions among communities and placed burdens on women and girls. Recent assessments point to increased safety risks for women and girls, who describe a general environment of fear and security deterioration in the places where they live.
According to the data collected through the Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS), Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) reporting continued to increase in 2022, with 5% increase in third quarter of 2022 accounting for 62% of reported GBV cases compared to the second quarter. Physical and emotional abuse were the most commonly reported type of violence against women and girls, accounting for 36% and 34% of the incidents respectively; Sexual abuse remains underreported due to the stigma associated with it and incidents of rape and sexual assault constitute 17% of all reported incidents.
When it comes to adolescent girls, they are often excluded, marginalized and at risk of GBV and negative health outcomes; they are disproportionately affected by the multi-layered crises and their access to education and other essential services have been disrupted, increasing their exposure to certain harmful practices, such as child marriage, representing a direct manifestation of gender inequality; recent data shows an increase in percentage of this harmful practice among Syrian refugee girls with 21.9% in 2022 compared to 20.4% in 2021.
Responding quickly and effectively to GBV can save lives and is a critical first step toward starting the healing process for survivors. UNICEF, through this project, will deliver urgent care and referrals for 4,400 GBV survivors and works with communities and institutions to challenge social norms that tolerate violence and abuse.
UNICEF, in partnership with local women-led organizations and international non-governmental organizations (NGOs), will provide safe spaces for 4,400 women and girls to come together for support. Through supporting women and girls to enhance their psychosocial well-being, gain better economic independence and play a meaningful role in the decision-making that affects their lives, UNICEF will foster conditions in which women and girls not only survive and heal the effects of violence but also ultimately thrive. On the other hand, UNICEF GBV program will also engage around 6,700 women, girls, men and boys through the GBV risk mitigation activities to contribute to a more secure environment, through sensitizing them to GBV in collaboration with UNICEF partners.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

In the light of current situation in Lebanon where GBV risks multiply exponentially, driving a scale-up of UNICEF’s GBV in Emergencies (GBViE) programming has become extremely important. Building on the experience gained by UNICEF and its partners through the previous years and the continuous learning that has informed UNICEF’s GBV programme adaptation, UNICEF will prioritise the outreach to the diverse target population including the most vulnerable women and girls who are at heightened risk of GBV, using current GBV programming, to provide them with integrated response package of services that promote their wellbeing, rights, agency, recovery from violence and enhance their skills. As such UNICEF will be reaching 4,400 women and girls through response services. Furthermore, to ensure strong links between prevention and response interventions, the project will build on community engagement and mobilization initiatives through UNICEF’s social and behavioral communication strategy, “Qudwa”. Qudwa employs an integrated approach to ensure environmental and transformative change for children and women, to eliminate social expectations that normalize child marriage and violence against children and women. All these activities will be complemented with risk mitigation activities and efforts targeting men, boys, women and girls, to mitigate possible GBV risks and make the community where women and girls are living “safer” reaching 10,000 individuals.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

UNICEF is an active member of the National PSEA TF and has set up a number of mitigation measures to ensure the prevention against SEA. Under this project the following will be ensured:- Review of internal complaints handling procedures to ensure existing feedback mechanisms are functional and can respond to reported issues- Ensure affected communities, especially women and girls, are consulted on changes to complaints and feedback mechanisms while ensuring that changes are communicated to affected communities in appropriate formats.- Promote safe, confidential complaints and feedback mechanisms with affected communities, making increased efforts to ensure those who are particularly at risk are aware of and have access to safe and confidential means for them to report concerns over sexual exploitation and abuse and other types of misconduct.- Continue roll-out of mandatory PSEA trainings for UNICEF staff and for partner staff and ensure adherence to PSEA standards and guideline.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

To strengthen its AAP efforts, UNICEF systematically incorporates processes for affected population to promote participation and equitable, two-way communications between communities and service providers. Moreover, UNICEF builds on existing planning,monitoring and evaluation capacity in development, humanitarian preparedness and response work, before, during and after a crisis.By leveraging local, national and international partnerships aimed at expanding the reach and scope through coordinatedresponses, UNICEF continuously supports vulnerable and affected people to strengthen their resilience, the capacity of service providers and authorities to respond effectively to affected populations needs. More specifically, under this project, through its outreach work UNICEF will increase access to timely, accurate and reliable life-saving information to the most vulnerable population in urban and rural areas and strengthen community engagement as agents in their own survival and recovery.
