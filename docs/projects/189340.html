Project Summary

There has been decrease in Armed conflict in South Sudan since the parties to the civil war signed a peace agreement in September 2018; although few locations with reported cases of armed conflicts exacerbated by the 2021 flooding with a fragile political situation still exist in the country. The humanitarian situation in South Sudan continue to deteriorate due to the increase of vulnerability drivers including inter communal violence, climatic shock, severe flood leading to population displacement, commodity price fluctuations, and pre-existing vulnerabilities such as malnutrition. The above mentioned triggers has worsen and limited WASH access to the vulnerable populations in South Sudan therein increasing WASH related diseases such as Hep-E posing more protection risks to the vulnerable people. The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) indicated that about 6.1 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. This intervention targets underserved and vulnerable population in Bentiu/Rubkona and Pariang County which is at catastrophic level according to the WASH cluster 2022 severity map and stressed severity stage in humanitarian conditions shown in 2022 HNO.
Through this proposed intervention, the IRC seeks to improve access to safe water supply and sanitation for vulnerable communities across Bentiu/Rubkona as well as Pariang, and 2 static primary health care centers (PHCC) and 2 primary schools through the rehabilitation and maintenance of non-functional community water systems, provide safe access to household and or communal sanitation facilities and hand washing stations at strategic locations, provide hygiene promotion activities targeting diseases outbreak hotspots, and provide trainings to WASH committees.
The intervention will work closely with the County WASH department structure, community leadership, and WASH actors present in the targeted location for coordinated response as the custodian of water and sanitation services at the County level to foster coordination and collaboration with key actors in WASH and health in the targeted County. This will also support county led services mapping to ensure complementarity of interventions proposed by this project with other ongoing WASH services delivery partners.
The intervention will mainstream COVID-19 risk reduction measures across all service delivery channels to ensure reduction in transmission.

1 - Needs

South Sudanese continued to witness a fragile stability resulted from the formation of the transitional government of national unity (TGoNU) in Feb 2020. There is an increasing humanitarian needs as a result of cumulative effects of years of recurring conflicts and violence and aggravated by subsequent shocks including exacerbated by large scale flood that is threatening the wellbeing and livelihood of the vulnerable people in South Sudan. With the high poverty rate and internal displacement in the country, South Sudanese continue to leave in severe humanitarian needs situation that requires urgent humanitarian response to alleviate and improved the lives and wellbeing of the people. The HNO 2022 indicated an estimated 6 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions were found to be most severe in the Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, and Eastern Equatorial and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. As access to water sources is predominately communal in South Sudan, the limited access to WASH services impacts on all the population groups. In Bentiu, the WASH needs around IDPs, non-displaced and host community members, returnees from internal displacement remains a catastrophic with limited access to safe WATSAN infrastructures compounded by disease out breaks such are Hep-E and Acute Watery Diarrhea. The main drivers of WASH needs include continues flooding resulting to limited physical infrastructure, subnational violence. Increased displacement due to large flood also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in official IDP camps in Bentiu displacement sites and it creates barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities. Pariang is still currently at security level 3 in the WASH cluster base on the 2021 severity map and stressed severity stage in humanitarian conditions shown in 2021 HRP. However, Pariang hosts a significant number of refugees whose needs require kin attention. Through this proposed intervention, the IRC will support by providing access to WASH facilities targeting Jamjang host communities, primary health care centers (PHCC) and primary schools through rehabilitation and maintenance of non-functional community water systems, provide safe access to communal or household sanitation facilities and hand washing stations at strategic locations, provide hygiene promotion activities as well as provide trainings to WASH committees.

2 - Project activities

The IRC plans to implement emergency WASH outputs aligning to the South Sudan WASH cluster strategy to improve access to safe and adequate water, sanitation and improve hygiene practices among individuals and institutions in Bentiu/Rubkona and Pariang (Jamjang) communities. This will be achieved through the rehabilitation and maintenance of exiting non-functional water supply systems in Bentiu, Rubkona and Pariang (Jamjang County); and provision of latrines facilities for targeted vulnerable households in the targeted communities, static primary health care centers (PHCC) and primary schools, rehabilitation and maintenance of communal sanitation facilities and hand washing stations at strategic locations. -provide hygiene promotion activities including safe practice messages. -provide trainings to WASH committees and respective government authorities.

3 - Project indicators

Success will be measured using the IRC’s IMPACT standards which identifies a set of monitoring actions across the project life cycle. The project will dedicate funds to recruit monitoring and evaluation staff that will be charged with setting up project M&E systems and check points to track project implementation progress and to document learning. The project will be implemented in line with the overall strategic objectives of the Humanitarian Needs Overview and WASH Cluster strategic objectives to ensure that activities align with the overall prioritized needs of affected populations. The indicators tracked will be in line with the WASH Cluster priorities and will be disaggregated by age, sex, nationality, residency and disability. This will further inform access and utilization of project assistance by vulnerable groups in the community. Quantitative and qualitative data will be collected during project implementation using standardized tools used by the WASH cluster and reported on a monthly basis. Regular review meetings will be held at the project locations where project staff and partners will review data on all indicators and use the data to make critical decisions about how to best deliver on the project outcomes and make necessary adjustments. Project cycle meetings will be held on a monthly basis to discuss performance across service delivery sites, share experiences and learnings and make high-level evidence-based decisions geared towards strengthening the project delivery mechanisms and success pathways.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Experience and research has shown that if beneficiaries do not know what has become of their feedback, then they will lose trust in the feedback process. At the IRC, we respond to beneficiaries’ feedback by asking them how we should respond, selecting the appropriate channels, identifying the timing for the response, planning and allocating resources, recording and keeping track of client feedback, and monitoring and reviewing our feedback mechanisms. Complaints and feedback will be analyzed and presented during weekly field site meetings and monthly project cycle meetings where practical actions to address them will put in place with clear timelines and responsibility allocated. These actions will then be followed up in subsequent meetings to ensure they are implemented and to measure the results. Key actions to adapt project assistance will include, but not limited to, making programmatic changes on assistance delivery, changing policy, provision of goods and services, providing information on available assistance, providing linkages with other projects, providing external referrals and including the needs of clients in project funding proposals.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We will collect, analyze and use feedback from beneficiaries regardless of their age, nationality, gender, residency status, education status, disability status and other diversity factors. We will use the Washington Group Short Set of Questions to disaggregate feedback and complaints by disability status. The IRC’s feedback channels will disaggregate feedback and complaints by gender, age, nationality, residency status and disability status to enable us to understand the needs and perspectives of different clients, including the most vulnerable members in the communities we serve. We will pay special attention to the most vulnerable and gather their feedback through key informant interviews, focus group discussions and community meetings. The community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements include persons with special needs to ensure their needs are into account.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The IRC will maintain the random sampling of clients accessing services through the project cycle. The IRC will prioritize FGD session with representation from vulnerable group to provide opportunities for their feedback to be incorporated in the project implementation. Also, the most vulnerable in the populations will be accorded a chance to share feedback through the feedback mechanisms. The administered feedback questionnaires will be in English and translated in local languages to make it simple and easy to understand and the intervention will employ the use of easy to access helpdesk where persons with specialized needs including clients and caregivers with disability, mental health needs and illiterate community members.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be informed on the scope of the project, timelines, key decision gates and community accountability mechanisms and systems during project opening meetings, project review and coordination meetings, stakeholder and community forums, information boards and using IEC materials. IRC has established community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements who will inform community members on the available client feedback and complaints mechanisms that they can use to provide feedback about project assistance. The community-led feedback management committees will also follow up with IRC on actions taken on the feedback and complaints provided to ensure that the feedback and complaints received from clients are included in adaptive program management. As a client-centered organization, the IRC has a well-structured client responsiveness and accountability system in place where clients’ complaints and feedbacks are collected, analyzed, and used to inform program decision making. We will use both the proactive and reactive feedback channels to solicit client feedback that will guide project implementation and strategies for improvement. This feedback will be used to inform decision-making and adapt interventions to achieve better outcomes for beneficiaries in line with their priority needs and preferences.
