Project Summary

GOAL’s primary objective for 2019 is to provide quality and essential healthcare services, scale up responsiveness to needs of targeted vulnerable populations in Melut County, Upper Nile, through a network of 3 primary health care facilities (Dethoma and Koradar IDP clinics and 1 mobile clinic- Malek) by delivering the basic PHCC, PHCU and mobile health care packages. GOAL will target 20,329 internally displaced persons (IDPs), with a focus on children under 5 years (target 4274 children) and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs – target 1,628 PLWs).

Needs

GOAL proposes to focus on provision of primary health and nutrition services for vulnerable groups, namely IDPs with a focus on pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and children under 5. Approximately 39,182 IDPs have settled in Melut following displacement from Baliet in 2013; after the closing of the Protection of Civilians (PoC) site in December 2017, when many actors left the county, GOAL remained to continue providing an integrated package of health and nutrition services. The most recent SMART survey conducted in March 2018 indicates high morbidity levels in the county, particularly in children under 5 years (with 47% reporting sickness within two weeks, 42.8% fever, 30% cough, 19.8% diarrhoea) coupled with the GAM prevalence of 21.7% (SAM 4.9%, MAM 16.8%) (WVI SMART Survey, March 2018). Further, uptake of reproductive health services remains low; the number of women who attend their 4th antenatal care visit is half the number of women who attended their 1st antenatal care visit, with women frequently delivering in the community instead of seeking delivery in a health facility. Lastly, considerable distances between health facilities pose a barrier to access to health care services, especially for those with limited mobility, such as pregnant women, elderly or disabled. Such factors contribute to a worsening health burden in Melut; GOAL seeks to address these challenges through provision of integrated primary health and nutrition services through both static and mobile sites, to enhance equitable access to quality care. In 2019 GOAL will offer this package through a network of 2 static health facilities, 1 mobile clinic, and 9 TSFP sites; integrated health and nutrition will be provided at 3 GOAL health facilities, while GOAL will offer TSFP at an additional 6 health facilities supported by other partners (WVI and Ministry of Health); WVI, the World Bank funded health actor, will provide health services at these sites for the host community alongside GOAL’s IDP-targeted implementation. GOAL will enhance linkages to nutrition and food security programming through referral of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) to outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP) managed by WVI, while also strengthening referral of beneficiaries into GOAL’s DFID-funded RECOVER resilience project, in order to better address root causes of poor health and nutrition.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Awareness raising sessions will be conducted regularly within target communities to inform programme participants of GOAL’s Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) across all sites, to ensure communities have a strong understanding of their rights as beneficiaries, what to expect from GOAL programming, how adequacy of interventions is evaluated, and provide an opportunity to address concerns and complaints.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Promotion messaging is tailored to relevant audiences to provide gender and age sensitive information regarding sexual and reproductive health, MIYCN practices, hygiene promotion, among others; these messages are designed to improve community health, nutrition and hygiene practices.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL will incorporate protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in all levels of programming; it will conduct routine PSEA and safeguarding training for all staff to educate on what constitutes sexual exploitation, proper reporting mechanisms if SEA is suspected, and ways to mitigate risk of SEA in programming, to better safeguard beneficiaries at all times; following training, all staff will sign the PSEA, Child Protection, Safeguarding and Code of Conduct policies to reflect their understanding of the consequences of breaches to these policies. During awareness campaigns, special emphasis will be placed on SEA so that communities are well-informed on various methods they may use to report suspected or perceived cases of SEA, which include GOAL’s internal reporting email (speakup@goal.ie), external mechanism (goal@safecall.co.uk) and South Sudan’s countrywide reporting through the IASC PSEA Task Force.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

GOAL conducts periodic review meetings with stakeholders in its field sites to inform them about programme implementation and provide an opportunity for feedback about its projects. These consultations are used to improve subsequent programming and inform project design. Throughout the life of the project, awareness raising sessions will be conducted regularly within target communities to inform programme participants of GOAL’s Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) across all sites, to ensure communities have a strong understanding of their rights as beneficiaries, what to expect from GOAL programming, how adequacy of interventions is evaluated, and provide an opportunity to address concerns and complaints about the project.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

GOAL has an established feedback, complaint and response mechanism (FCRM) in place in Melut, through which beneficiaries have the opportunity to raise questions, comments, or issues in programming. Feedback/complaints are addressed by the Area Coordinator or escalated as necessary to the Country Director to ensure appropriate and sufficient response to community members.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL conducts periodic review meetings with communities to discuss programme implementation, achievements, and opportunities for improvement; additionally, community members may utilize the FCRM to provide feedback or complaints, which are routinely monitored for appropriate response.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL’s programming will focus on enhanced integration of protection into project activities to safeguard beneficiaries. GOAL has already trained a midwife in Melut on clinical management of rape (CMR), and health facilities have requisite kits to respond to and manage any cases of gender-based violence (GBV) presenting at the facilities. In 2019, GOAL seeks to extend CMR initial and refresher training and safeguarding training to additional cadres of staff as well as strengthen the referral mechanism to InterHealth, the county Protection partner.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project considers barriers vulnerable groups, including pregnant women, children, the elderly or the disabled may face in accessing care; it seeks to address these barriers through provision of a mobile clinic to provide treatment to those unable to access health facilities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL’s humanitarian responses will routinely assess, analyze and monitor the risks to civilian safety, and take appropriate action to mitigate risks. Programme managers will ensure the programme brings human rights standards and principles to the centre of all interventions, recognizes programme participants as rights-holders, as well as focus on vulnerable and marginalized groups to ensure their safety and dignity. Adequate measure will be put in place at the distribution sites to ensure distribution takes minimal amount of time, including adequate staffing, clear communication to beneficiaries about when to come for supplies, and triaging the area to make sure sufficient supplies are available at the time of distribution. Furthermore, GOAL will ensure that adequate gender-disaggregated latrine facilities are available at facilities where programming is implemented to protect dignity of the users.
