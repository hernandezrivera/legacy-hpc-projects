Project Summary

The project aims at addressing food insecurity and building resilience of the Conflict-affected communities (Madagali. Maiha, Mubi north) in Adamawa state and (Damaturu&Gujba) of Yobe state North East of Nigeria.
The project targets an estimated 5,350 households, particularly women, children the elderly and the poorest of the poor families, living in the aforementioned communities in Adamawa and Yobe states, This is to assist affected households to meet their immediate and long-term food and cash needs, as well as provide appropriate capacity building to the target communities.

1 - Needs

In Boko Haram conflict-affected areas of the northeast, livelihoods for most households remain heavily disrupted. Agricultural and other income-earning opportunities remain restricted due to the direct impacts of the conflict and by the high levels of displacement. This is exacerbated by a typical market functioning and high staple prices. In many cases, households remain highly dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic food needs, particularly the IDPs camps as they do not receive substantial humanitarian assistance and are using strategies that may deplete their livelihoods, such as sale of farm tools, fishing nets and farmlands. Worst-affected areas, where there are highest restrictions on agriculture, other livelihoods activities, access to markets, and assistance provision, are expected to face larger food consumption gaps and they will continue to face Emergency. Areas where populations are affected by complete loss of livelihoods activities and who remain inaccessible to humanitarian actors are likely facing similar or worse food security outcomes as neighbouring, accessible areas. Extreme levels of acute food insecurity are possible in a worst-case scenario where displaced populations become cut off due to a shift in conflict and emergency assistance provision is halted. A recent VSO assessment of the needs in the NE indicate that VSO unique relational volunteer approach to working with community volunteers, supported by national volunteers will help lay the foundation for a health early recovery programme.This project is built around community volunteers as key actors for building back better their own devastated communities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

VSO will establish a feedback mechanism that primary actors to openly make their concerns known. We will hold quarterly feed back sessions to hear out any issues. We will encourage the use of Community score cards to gauge community appreciation of our work and the quality, impartiality, effectiveness and rapidity of the the services provided. We will encourage the use of the existing VSO hotlines and emails for reporting cases , suspicions around the violation of primary actors right. We will display at all community meeting , in our offices and in our partner office, the various contacts for reporting cases of protection, fraud, abuse and how primary actors can seek redress. We have built into the project various awareness raising sessions on the rights of our primary actors to access information and the mechanism for complaints. We will increase awareness sessions for primary actors, on their right to complain and most importantly assure them of their protection. We will investigate and report back and take actions on any complaints. The lessons from the investigation will feed into improving our processes.
