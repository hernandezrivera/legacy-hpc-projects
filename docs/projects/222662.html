Project Summary

The John Dau Foundation (JDF) is committed to strengthening integrated primary healthcare services for vulnerable populations in Duk County, Jonglei State, South Sudan, in 2025. This project seeks to reduce health-related mortality and morbidity by improving access to lifesaving emergency health interventions, focusing on underserved groups, including women, children, internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and individuals with disabilities.
Duk County faces numerous challenges, including recurrent flooding, intercommunal conflict, and inadequate healthcare infrastructure, leading to some of the worst health outcomes globally. The maternal mortality rate stands at 1,223 per 100,000 live births, and the under-five mortality rate is 98.7 per 1,000 live births. Immunization coverage remains critically low, with Penta3 at just 32%, contributing to frequent outbreaks of preventable diseases such as measles. Malaria is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality, particularly during the rainy season. These issues are compounded by poor WASH conditions, high malnutrition rates, and limited access to mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services.
The project will target 25,000 individuals (12,400 men and 12,600 women), delivering essential health services such as immunizations, maternal and child healthcare, disease prevention and management, emergency preparedness, and MHPSS. JDF will integrate these services with nutrition, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) programs to ensure a holistic and sustainable approach to health service delivery. Emphasis will be placed on community-based interventions to enhance resilience and ensure equitable access to care.
JDF is committed to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) by engaging beneficiaries throughout the project lifecycle. Transparent communication, inclusive community consultations, and robust feedback mechanisms will ensure services are responsive to the needs of the most vulnerable, including persons with disabilities, women, and children. Beneficiary feedback will guide adaptive project implementation, fostering trust and improving outcomes.
The project also prioritizes the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) by enforcing robust policies, providing staff training, and establishing accessible, confidential reporting mechanisms. Survivors of SEA will receive medical, psychosocial, and legal support through defined referral pathways. By upholding these principles, JDF ensures a secure and dignified environment for delivering lifesaving healthcare services.
Through integrated, community-focused efforts, this project aims to address critical health gaps, improve maternal and child health outcomes, and build the resilience of Duk County’s healthcare system, ensuring sustainable benefits for its most vulnerable populations.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to rank among the countries with the worst health indicators globally. The under-five mortality rate stands at 98.7 deaths per 1,000 live births, and the maternal mortality rate is 1,223 per 100,000 live births, highlighting significant gaps in child survival and maternal health. Preventable diseases such as malaria, diarrhea, and pneumonia remain the leading causes of death, with malaria alone accounting for more than 60% of illnesses among children under five.Immunization coverage is critically low, with only 32% of children completing the Penta3 vaccination schedule, leaving many vulnerable to vaccine-preventable diseases such as measles. The ongoing risk of disease outbreaks, including cholera and Hepatitis E, underscores the fragility of public health systems, particularly in regions like Duk County, where WASH conditions are severely compromised.Duk County is categorized as IPC Phase 1 (Emergency), reflecting high levels of food insecurity and acute malnutrition among children under five. Recurrent flooding has displaced thousands, destroyed infrastructure, and disrupted healthcare delivery, leaving large segments of the population without access to essential services. As of 2023, only 44% of South Sudan’s population is within walking distance of a functional health facility, and in Duk County, this figure is likely even lower due to poor road networks and geographic isolation.Maternal health indicators reveal stark disparities, with less than 26% of deliveries attended by skilled personnel, contributing to preventable maternal deaths. Antenatal care (ANC) services remain inadequate, with ANC1 coverage at 30% and ANC4 at 28%, highlighting the need for improved maternal and newborn care services.Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) needs are acute, particularly among displaced populations who have experienced conflict and trauma. Despite this, access to MHPSS services remains negligible. Similarly, gender-based violence (GBV) is a pervasive issue, particularly for women and girls in vulnerable settings such as IDP camps, where services for survivors are extremely limited.This project seeks to address these critical gaps through integrated health services, focusing on maternal and child health, immunization, emergency care, and disease prevention. By targeting 35,700 people, including IDPs, returnees, and host communities, the project aims to improve health outcomes, strengthen local health systems, and build resilience in Duk County’s most vulnerable communities.

2. Project activities

1. Conducting Immunization Campaigns: Deliver immunization services targeting vaccine-preventable diseases, with a focus on measles, polio, and other antigens. Procure and distribute outbreak response commodities, emergency antigens, and cold chain equipment to ensure the preservation and efficacy of vaccines. These activities aim to improve immunization coverage, including reactive measles vaccination for 11,403 children (M=5,473; F=5,930).2. Infection Prevention and Control (IPC): Implement IPC measures in six targeted health facilities through regular assessments using IPC scorecards. Provide facilities with supplies, protocols, and training on IPC best practices. Continuous monitoring of WASH conditions will ensure a hygienic and safe environment for patients and staff.3. Strengthening Community Health Services: Link facility-based services with community-level care through initiatives like the Boma Health Initiative (BHI) and Integrated Community Case Management (ICCM). This will enable effective management of childhood illnesses, maternal health services, and referrals for severe cases.4. Provision of Life-Saving Healthcare Services: Deliver essential healthcare services, including the management of non-communicable diseases (NCDs), maternal and child health, and sexual and reproductive health, through static facilities (hospitals, PHCCs, and PHCUs) and mobile healthcare units. Procure medical kits, equipment, and supplies while training healthcare workers to enhance service delivery.5. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS): Provide MHPSS services to address the mental health needs of vulnerable populations. This includes individual and group counseling sessions, targeting 1,120 individuals (M=537; F=583) to reduce psychological distress caused by displacement, violence, and trauma.6. Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Response Services: Offer clinical management of rape (CMR) services and other support for survivors of GBV. Train healthcare workers on GBV case management and provide accessible services to 2,850 survivors (M=1,368; F=1,482).7. Support for Persons with Disabilities: Deliver rehabilitation services to 2,280 individuals with disabilities (M=538; F=1,186), ensuring tailored interventions to address their health needs.8. Behavioral Change Communication (BCC): Conduct 120 community health events to promote awareness and adoption of positive health behaviors, focusing on disease prevention and health-seeking practices.9. Health Education During Disease Outbreaks: Reach 25,000 people (M=12,400; F=12,600) with health education and promotion during outbreaks to enhance community knowledge and preparedness.10. Community Engagement and Feedback Mechanisms: Conduct 36 community consultative meetings to identify needs, engage the community in project planning and implementation, and ensure accountability. Establish feedback mechanisms at six health facilities to track community perceptions and improve service delivery.11. Supportive Supervision of Health Facilities: Provide technical and operational assistance to six health facilities through quarterly supportive supervision visits, ensuring the quality and accountability of health services.12. Emergency Preparedness and Response: Preposition emergency medical supplies and strengthen disease surveillance systems to prepare for and respond to potential outbreaks and emergencies.13. Maternal and Neonatal Health Interventions: Scale up antenatal care (ANC) services to increase ANC1 and ANC4 coverage, while improving the percentage of skilled deliveries to at least 26%. Provide comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care (BEmONC) at selected facilities.14. Health Worker Capacity Building: Train healthcare workers on clinical protocols, disease management, MHPSS, GBV case management, and WASH to strengthen the capacity of the local health system.15.
