Project Summary

In 2021, CAID will work closely for providing technical guidance to its partners, ensure the targeted activities with quality, support on protection intervention and play role for coordinate with PWG sector for up to date information sharing necessary reports for strengthening coordination as well as camp level. Partners have been implementing several projects in the targeted camp and its existing structures (WGSS, training centre, Health centre, etc) will used the proposed activities. The purpose is to strengthen and ensure protection of refugee vulnerable women, men, girls and boys including elderly, and PwD. The process will be completed by strengthening community participation approaches, capacity building of community groups, IPs, awareness through religious leaders/ faith base leaders as well as protection messaging.
CAID will facilitate to identify high risk area and distribute solar light, Covid-19 prevention martials for women and girls, older people and PWDs are key beneficiary those are in high risk and possible treat of protection risks of Rohingya and Host community on the basis need assessment. CAID will maintain coordination for protection mainstreaming with other sectors and refer to the relevant agencies regarding Legal Aid support such as legal counselling, case representation, awareness on legal concept will be key option to support and survivor of GBV and other conflict of community for mediation, as well as resolve conflicts among the community. Besides, Covid-19 prevention awareness, referral, and other need basis initiative specially for women and girls including PSNs, hijra, and disability will be key priority for reaching messages and services as well address their needs. 1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya are a stateless Muslim minority group who have been subjected to systematic persecution and grave human rights abuses by Myanmar authorities. In August 2018, a study estimated that more than 24,000 Rohingya were killed by the Myanmar military and local Buddhists militia since the "clearance operations" started on 25 August 2017. It also estimated that at least 18,000 Rohingya Muslim women and girls were raped, 116,000 Rohingya were beaten, and 36,000 Rohingya were thrown into fires set alight. Currently Rohingya response shifted to restoring dignity, safety, rights but protection crisis remain still underrated. Further Covid-19 crisis deterred protection service and other addressing protection service retrograde in the camps. Due to the reduced footprint of protection actors as well as authorities, access to formal and informal justice mechanisms, psychosocial support including MHPSS, as well as mediation and legal/physical protection services have been negatively affected, leaving space for Majhis to mediate disputes and conflicts with practices not in line with protection principles. Some misinterpretation about Rohingya refugees and host community over troublemaker groups evolving around the camps created misperception among both communities. Additionally, Host communities shows negative impression on Rohingya volunteers’ involvement in in IGA activities. So, social stability and mitigate risk of both community social cohesion activities are much needed. Hereafter, movement restriction due to barbed wired around the camp is going on rapidly by responsibility of BD Army and some representative from camps visited Bhasan char Island where they showed negative impression and created psychological and mental pressure. Furthermore, Covid-19 pandemic brings more sufferings particularly for the women and adolescent girls to access services such as health, education, and other basic needs. And this panic situation impacting their mental health and misapprehension for future lifestyle. Strengthening networking with other sector will play vital role for empower women and girls to be familiar with SSWG as currently very few women and girls living with disabilities attend the Safe Spaces for Women and Girls (SSWG). As per ADWG, Elderly and disable persons are usually left out from awareness and have diverse specific needs. The access to CFRM for people with disabilities and older people should be improved. Moreover, JMSN findings (October 2020), shows-community conflict and cultural rumors, delay of judgment, unstable community resolution hampering utmost among the refugees and host community. Several risks reported such robbery, kidnapping, child marriage, sexual harassment, threats by local people/armed groups, mahjee demanding money/using violence against those reporting problems that household without men, child missing, child marriage produce dilemma among the refugees.The pandemic has also forced many humanitarian programs in the camp scale back. Basic needs shortage at market and lack of availability of goods placed mental pressure, high risk of vulnerability women and girls, elderly peoples and person with disabilities. Moreover, nearly half of the incidents were committed during the night and protection risks have been widely reported in the camps. Contributing factors have included the living conditions in the camps, lack of income generating activities, insufficient lighting and gender segregated toilets facilities in the camps, absence of security patrolling during the night. Protection risk mitigation through mainstreaming in WASH, Shelter and Site Management sectors is essential. Christian Aid has own CFRM system through COMPASS to address PSEA reporting, referring, disabilities and older people’s concern, and monitoring as well ensuring accountability of AAP through hot line number & voice recorder.
