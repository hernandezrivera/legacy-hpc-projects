Project Summary

As part of continued efforts to improve access to life-saving health care services among internally displaced, conflict-affected and vulnerable population, International Medical Corps (IMC) will continue to provide a comprehensive package of health care services following static and mobile approaches in South Sudan. IMC’s main strategy is to support static essential health services that is complemented by regular outreach to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for mobile or hard to reach populations with intermittent periods of stability and weather-constrained access. Aligned with the 2021 HRP for South Sudan, the National Action Plan for Health Security (NAPHS) 2020 – 2022, and the Global HRP for COVID-19, IMC’s health care interventions are designed to: (1) increase access of approximately 139,731 conflict-affected, displaced and vulnerable people in Juba, Malakal and Wau displacement camps, as well as in Canal/Pigi, Akoka, Baliet and Panyikang Counties to preventive and curative health services, (2) improve the capacity of the country’s health system to cope with the COVID-19 pandemic, other disease outbreaks and emergencies, and ultimately, (3) to prevent and reduce excess mortality and morbidity in the country.
IMC will continue to provide 24/7 free primary and secondary integrated health care services across its 26 supported health facilities in the aforesaid locations. These services will include outpatient consultation for the management of common causes of morbidities and mortalities, endemic diseases, trauma and injuries; health education at the facility and community level; provision of essential pharmaceuticals and medical commodities; support to the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI); sexual and reproductive health services; and community surveillance and referral services to ensure continuum of care. IMC will also support the health facilities through maintaining trained health care providers; further capacitating them and carrying out monthly clinical supervisions by IMC Medical Doctors, Clinicians and Site Managers. Moreover, IMC will also integrate MHPSS into primary health care activities across its 26 supported health facilities, in line with the WHO and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines.
Additionally, while ensuring the continued provision of lifesaving health care services to the populace, IMC, being the co-lead of TWG on Case Management, IPC and Safe Burial, will further bolster the capacity of 3 COVID-19 supportive care facilities / isolation units in the country to cope with the increasing number of COVID-19 cases, namely, Dr John Garang Infectious Disease Unit in Juba, Malakal Teaching Hospital and Al Muktah PHCC in Wau. IMC will also form and deploy an Emergency Rapid Response Team (ERRT), in close coordination with MoH, that can quickly respond to health emergency/potential outbreaks, including Cholera, AWD, Measles, COVID-19, Ebola and conflict-related injuries/wounds, as well as to natural calamities.
Meanwhile, due to the volatile security, continuous displacement of the population, and difficulty in accessing static health care facilities in Canal/Pigi, IMC will deploy four Mobile Medical Units (MMUs) across the five Payams of Korwach, Wunlith, Alam, Khorfulus and Atar. The MMUs will support the need for increased capacity and accessibility of locally available health services in six static facilities, which IMC is currently supporting under the UNICEF/World Bank-funded program. Each MMU will consist of 1 Clinical Officer, 1 Nurse, 1 Midwife, 1 Vaccinator, 1 Data Recorder and 1 CHW. They will be integrated with the Mobile Nutrition and Protection teams of IMC, which will also be positioned in Canal/Pigi. The integrated Units will visit the different hard-to-reach Bomas and IDP camps within the five Payams. To increase the chances of patients coming from distant communities, each Unit will remain on the spot for 2 full days, providing services.

1 - Needs

Juba, Malakal and Wau Displacement Camps: Since 2014, IMC has been providing comprehensive health services in the displacement camps of Juba, Malakal and Wau. These camps are currently in the process of transitioning to conventional IDP sites under jurisdiction of the government. In November 2020, the sites in Juba, including Bor and Wau were transitioned, with Malakal and Bentiu PoC sites planned to transition in 2021. According to HNO 2021, the re-designation of the PoC sites may further entrench protracted displacement and the associated burden on displaced people, returnees and host communities. Now more than ever, a continued support in terms of healthcare, MHPSS, and other lifesaving interventions is needed to not let the IDPs further face the brunt of this transition, and support them for a gradual safe return to their homesteads. Recent headcounts state that there are about 32,113 IDPs sheltering in Juba IDP camps, 33,137 IDPs in Malakal PoC, and 17,732 in Wau IDP camp. These populations rely on IMC’s support in terms of primary and secondary healthcare services in its 2 health facilities in Juba IDP camps, 1 health facility in Wau IDP camp, and 1 health facility in Malakal PoC, including an Operating Theatre (OT). The morbidity attributed to common diseases, Malaria (46%), Diarrhea (40%) and Acute Respiratory Tract Infection (39%) remains a challenge in all the camps. Out of the 50,721 cases diagnosed for Malaria, Diarrhea and ARI in 2020, 16,300 or 32% of them were under 5 children. The RNA conducted by IMC in the camps highlighted the critical need to increase the availability of health services, particularly focusing on CMR and Minimum Integrated Services Package (MISP) for Sexual Reproductive Health for IDP women. Another critical need is on the renovation of the health facilities, particularly adding handwashing facilities and sex-segregated toilets. Upper Nile and Jonglei States: In relation to health service delivery, Jonglei and Upper Nile remain extremely challenging due to widespread insecurity and looting of health supplies and assets. Access to primary health care has been difficult for a large proportion of IDPs and host community populations. According to available Health Management Information System data, consultation utilization rates for all ages were lowest in Jonglei state (0.2), and coverage of ANC 1st and 4th visits as well as deliveries by skilled birth attendants were the lowest in Upper Nile and Jonglei States (ANC 1 – Upper Nile 14.8% and Jonglei 21.5%; ANC 4 – Upper Nile 7.8% and Jonglei 9.5%; SBA – Upper Nile 2.2% and Jonglei 2.3%). Immunization coverage is poor, with Penta3 coverage of only 16% in Jonglei and 24% in Upper Nile. This situation is further complicated by looting of cold chain equipment. Specifically, in Canal/Pigi County of Jonglei, only 3% of pregnant women go for at least four antenatal visits; live births attended by skilled health personnel is at 0%, and only 2% of newborns receive postnatal visit within 72 hours of childbirth. IMC is currently running six health facilities in Canal/Pigi. However, there is a significant number of population, especially IDPs and those who have fled to remote areas, who are unable to access healthcare services in these static facilities due to far distance, continued displacement, frequent flooding, and volatile security. MHPSS: Displaced and conflict-affected people experience elevated levels of psychological distress, which may cause or exacerbate mental health conditions. The COVID-19 pandemic has worsened the mental health situation for the population, and constrained the availability of already limited mental health services in particular for persons with pre-existing mental health problems. Furthermore, given the dearth of a qualified mental health work force, those with mental health conditions are largely neglected and untreated in South Sudan. IMC is one of the few actors providing comprehensive MHPSS services in the country

2 - Project activities

Communicable Diseases: Monitoring of communicable disease through IDSR; community health education with a particular focus on the top four diseases in the targeted locations: malaria, respiratory infections, diarrhea, and malnutrition-related conditions; conduct of facility-based and outreach vaccination services per the WHO and national MoH Guidelines; integration of HIV/AIDs and TB program with the medical service package, and submission of EWARN surveillance weekly reports to the MoH and WHO. Training of health care workers and prepositioning of supply to be able to respond to an outbreak of communicable diseases. Reproductive Health: Provision of MISP for life-saving SRH services, including ANC, BeMONC, CEmONC, health facility delivery, postnatal care services, FP services, and STI prevention/protection and management; upgrading of maternity and operating theatres for CEmONC interventions in Juba and Malakal camps; distribution of clean delivery kits to all pregnant women in their 3rd trimesters during mobile units outreaches; provision of CMR services; prepositioning of PEP Kits; provision of FP services with special focus on family planning counselling, and reproductive health outreach through CHWs. Non-Communicable Diseases: Carrying-out consultations for NCDs; prepositioning and distribution of essential medicines for NCDs in accordance with MOH clinical guidelines; training of health care providers on the prevention and management of NCDs, as per national guidelines; 24/7 clinical care for people with priority mental, neurological and substance use disorders in the static health facilities, and referrals of MMUs to health facilities in Canal/Pigi, as well as provision of consultations and care for trauma-related injuries. MHPSS: Provision of integrated MHPSS services, including psychiatric emergency services for both in- and out-patients in all static health facilities through skilled MHPSS Specialists. IMC’s MHPSS workers trained on mhGAP-HIG will conduct regular field visits to follow up on all cases treated in facilities to ensure quality of case management. IMC and MoH clinical staff will be trained on selected priority mental, neurological and substance use disorders, and will be provided with routine supportive supervisions. The facilities will also be supported with psychotropic and neurotropic drug supply. Community Health: Strengthening of community surveillance and referrals and regular home visits and follow-ups for patients-at-risk; conduct of defaulter tracing for HIV, TB, mental health, EPI, ANC, PNC and other diseases; employing SBCC strategies by facilitating health education and rumor tracking at both the facilities and community level, and dissemination of information on preventive measures against epidemic prone diseases using the available services through community radio, distribution of IEC material and mass campaigns. Higher Level of Care: IMC is currently providing advanced emergency obstetric and newborn care, trauma care, trauma surgery, and rehabilitation for major injuries and basic surgical care at the Operating Theatres located in Malakal PoC, Malakal Teaching Hospital and Juba IDP camps. These OTs will continue to serve as final referral points for general surgery and obstetric emergencies in the displacement camps and beyond. IMC will expand the capacity of the OTs through hiring and training of qualified surgeons and anesthetists, as well as procuring additional surgical equipment and medical supplies. Emergency Preparedness and Response: IMC will also form an ERRT, that can quickly respond to health emergency/potential outbreaks. The ERRT will be rapidly deployed in the event of an outbreak of diseases/conflict/natural calamity in IMC operational areas and beyond. They will do initial investigations and treatment of suspected cases through sample collections, contact tracing and isolation, while coordinating with the health cluster and authorities to mobilize a full response

3 - Project indicators

IMC will adopt the indicators set by the Health Cluster, as outlined in the succeeding section, and as appropriate in this proposed project. To measure the achievement of the expected outcomes and ensure increased program quality, IMC has developed a robust system for monitoring, including setting up patient tracking systems within health facilities, registering daily community health activities and conducting routine quality control interviews with targeted beneficiaries. Impact-level indicators will inform more specifically the change and extent to which high impact and high-quality essential medical services for vulnerable populations, as well as community engagement services, have become available as a result of the project. IMC will disaggregate and centralize results for activity-level indicators on a monthly basis and track output/outcome-level indicators. The project will use performance data gathered through the monitoring of activities implemented. IMC will ensure close monitoring and reporting of project implementation to assess progress and data quality, identify challenges during implementation, and arrange contingency measures, as appropriate. IMC will utilize a range of monitoring tools including Project Management Tool (PMT) to ensure that project activities are implemented as per the plan and resources are utilized efficiently. In addition, a MEAL Plan and an Indicator Performance Tracking Table (IPTT) will be developed to assess progress of indicators against targets on a monthly basis. The analysis from the IPTT will be shared to the program team and used to inform the extent of the achievement of results and objectives, and to improve project implementation. With the technical support and guidance from IMC’s Medical Director and MEAL Specialist, the field team will be guided by detailed implementation plan to ensure timely execution of program activities and review. Health program data will be captured routinely, and as defined in sectoral-specific guidelines including the disability monitoring report mechanism. IMC has a well-established monitoring and evaluation system. At the field level, the Program Managers use the developed work plan that will guide implementation of the program. At Juba level, the Technical Coordinators, working closely with the M&E Coordinator, will provide technical support to the field teams on data collection, reporting and analysis pointing areas that need extra efforts to improve quality of services. Using the existing reporting tools (i.e. checklist, daily, weekly, monthly reporting forms, DHIS2, etc.), program training reports and supplies consumption reports will be shared internally and externally with the Health Cluster, MoH, donor and other stakeholders. In addition to the formal reporting system, field program team will conduct quarterly joint supportive supervision, as well as review meetings to review the project’s progress against planned activities, as well as share best practices and experiences. The MEAL Coordinator will carry out quarterly monitoring visits to support Data Quality Assessments (DQA), with an aim to improve the data collected and used to assess program performance. The DQA process will include the participation of MEAL officers and program staff at the field. Findings will be discussed with program teams during the project quarterly review and planning meetings. During the monitoring visits, Program staff will continue to receive on the job training on collection of quality data, and how to analyze and use the information to produce positive changes during implementation period. Data collected will be disaggregated according to location, gender, age and target group (male, female, girls, boys), including disability; analysis will look at how different population groups are affected, involved and /participating in the program.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IMC in South Sudan has developed country-specific guidance to protect beneficiaries from exploitation and abuse in humanitarian crisis. This Guidance complements IMC’s global safeguarding policies. All IMC employee has to undergo a training on PSEA, and sign a pre-employment commitment against sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as report any suspected cases. IMC will conduct regular awareness raising sessions with the community on PSEA through QAAPCs and CHWs. The project team will incorporate PSEA messages into CBFRM activities, especially during monthly AAP meetings with the community. The project team will also ensure posting/distribution of IEC materials at strategic locations in the community, including how to report PSEA cases. Regular sessions at the health facilities and other services point will be done on a monthly basis. Community focal points will be empowered through trainings to be able to pass on key PSEA messages, jointly with IMC project teams. For sensitive cases that arise, communities will be sensitized to report to the field site manager/designated focal point. The field site manager is responsible to log in the complaints into IMC reporting system, Ethics point, for further investigation. Communities will be sensitized to report any sensitive cases directly to Ethics point reporting systems for investigation. Ethics point contacts will be shared during community sensitizations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IMC will further strengthen and/or put in place various CBFRM mechanisms that will ensure the most vulnerable members of the community are catered for. IMC ensures accountability to affected population is incorporated from the design phase by consulting affected communities, with equal representation from women, girls, PWDs, elderly, etc., on their specific needs and vulnerabilities, that should be adapted by the project. During project implementation, the project will use different channels, such as AAP monthly meetings, suggestion boxes, hotlines, house-to-house visits, etc., to inform affected communities on their rights and entitlements, and mechanisms available to them to share feedback and complaints. The project will also consult communities on their preferred feedback modalities, and support set up of these channels where they are not functional. The mechanisms will be robust to ensure collection of all forms of complaints including cases of PSEA. Feedback and complaints coming from the various channels will be analyzed, and patterns identified to inform program adaptation where necessary. In addition, regular community consultations will be conducted to ensure participation of communities in the ongoing implementation and M&E activities. These will include assessing level of satisfaction with services provided, unmet needs, identification of lesson learned and recommendations for project improvement.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IMC utilizes community-based feedback and response mechanisms (CBFRM) to solicit feedback and receive complaints. IMC’s CBFRM procedures provides guidance on: (1) effective community consultations – building upon community preferences on how to raise concerns and provide feedback; (2) major steps on managing feedback and complaints and how to explain our actions and decisions to the communities (including vulnerable and marginalised people); (3) Clear informed consent - IMC will provide all necessary information on how to manage feedback and complaint process, explain people’s rights and entitlements, as well as key steps that IMC will take to keep confidentiality of sensitive complaints, and (4) Routing and management of sensitive complaints, including SEA and safeguarding, including, protection measures and investigation pathways. CBFRM is aligned closely with the organization’s broader safeguarding and accountability policies; together, these procedures help ensure the systematic use and management of the feedback and complaint handling process across all field locations and programs. Building on the existing feedback and complaints mechanism, IMC will ultimately promote community participation on management and use of the feedback and complaints through community learning meetings, community Help desks, Post distribution Monitoring, AAP meetings and field registers as a safe and user friendly channels to collect, process and respond to the complaints according to IMC and community’ preferences, as well as disseminate information about the project. During program implementation, IMC will continue to enhance these already existing community accountability structures in the interventions guided by IMC South Sudan CBFRM country local procedures. IMC will share relevant program information including information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure meaningful participation. Regular information on the program performance and monitoring results, staff code of conduct and PSEA reporting lines staff will also be shared. Communities will be sensitized on existing AAP structures as a means of sharing complaints and feedback. During these sensitizations, communities will also be consulted on the most context-appropriate and safe avenue to share complaints and feedback. The information will be used to strengthen the CBFRM in the implementation sites. Further, the existing AAP committees will be trained on receiving, handling and responding to complaints and feedback on the project interventions.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback and Complaints arising from the communities will be processed, analyzed and discussed during program management meetings. The meetings will develop responses and action plans to address the complaints and feedback raised. These responses will be provided through the AAP committees in each project location. In addition, the information will be keyed into a complaints and feedback response database for analysis and learning. During monthly/quarterly program review meetings, the information from the database will form part of the meeting reflections and will inform necessary programmatic corrections.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

• IMC will maintain alertness on the security situation in all its health facilities and project locations, in close coordination with the camp/community leaders, local and national authorities, and UN agencies. IMC will also maintain its 24/7 security guards across all its supported health facilities, which also help in assessing the daily security situation together with IMC Site Managers and Security Director. IMC has also Security Risks Analysis (SRAs), Standard Operating Procedures (SoPs) and Contingency Plans in all its project locations, which are being updated bi-annually, or as need arise.• In IMC’s health facilities, there are separate waiting areas, wards, latrines and WASH facilities for women, men, girls and boys. The CHWs and community volunteers go around the facility to maintain orderliness in queues, and ensure that no untoward incidents of abuse and exploitation may happen to anyone accessing the health facilities. Huge banners about PSEA are also posted in different corners of the health facilities and in the community. Key messages are also disseminated by CHWs on the importance of reporting PSEA cases, including where and how to report such cases.• IMC ensures that a multi-sectoral referral pathways are in place in all its project locations, especially for survivors of protection incidents. Monthly safety audits are being conducted, which results are shared to and discussed with relevant national and local authorities and other NGOs to draw timely, actionable solutions, and provide targeted support to the survivors.• IMC will continue to implement all planned activities in full equity, without any discrimination based on race, color, origin, religion, age, gender nor disability. Particular attention will be given to ensure that children, PLW, vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors have access to quality health and protection services. Full access and preventive activities will also integrate persons with disabilities, elderly and youth. Special adjustments are being done by IMC to accommodate the special needs of PWDs and elderly, such as doing house-to-house visits/consultations to them; ensuring that health facilities are accessible to patients with movement constraints and physical disabilities, supporting rehabilitation where necessary such as ramps and grab bars, among others.• IMC will further strengthen existing and/or establish Quality Accountability of the affected population (QAAP) committees and other CBFRM modalities, which were selected by the community, to ensure that everyone has equitable and easy access to context-specific complaint and feedback mechanisms.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

IMC incorporates protection principles in its all its programs and its daily work by prioritizing the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries served. Consultations and other activities will take place in secure areas that are also easily accessible for groups who are extremely vulnerable and/or with limited mobility. IMC healthcare workers will be trained with a focus on gender-based violence prevention and survivor-centered treatment core concepts (clinical management of rape), considering the challenging environment that poses high barriers to vulnerable people, especially women and girls, in terms of reporting GBV cases, as well to counter the general lack of information regarding this topic. This proposed project will integrate GBV medical and MHPSS services to survivors, including counselling, management and proper referral for survivors of GBV, including rape survivors. All staff will be trained on protection, GBV Core Concepts and safe referrals. Service mapping and medical referral protocols are in place across IMC’s health facilities and project locations to ensure meaningful and safe referrals, including ensuring patient information shared with third-party service providers is only done so after informed consent of the patient is secured. Furthermore, IMC provides integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, including psychiatric emergency services for both in- and out-patients in all its supported health facilities. Other PSS interventions that IMC provides include psychoeducation, support groups and peer support sessions for the most vulnerable groups, including community leaders, women, children, GBV survivors and PWDs of the displaced and conflict-affected population in the target sites. These are conducted through small group discussions, tea sessions and community outreaches with the aim of bringing vulnerable individuals and group of people together, create social opportunities for their families and care givers of persons with psychosocial disabilities, and the wider community structures, which are tailored to ensure promotion of psychosocial wellbeing is achieved and the human rights of socially disadvantaged vulnerable persons are promoted. In addition, IMC will implement/pilot Self-help -plus (SH+) in all its project locations. SH+ is a stress management intervention based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) principles, for use by persons and group of people experiencing psychosocial distress and other stress-related conditions.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Through an integrated and comprehensive health, nutrition and protection services delivery, and through working with partners, IMC will ensure optimal and sustainable service quality for acute malnutrition management, as well as improving IYCF practices through nutrition education, counseling, facilitation of support groups, complementary feeding and kitchen gardening. A sustained delivery of such interventions will then significantly contribute to the decrease of proportion of populations facing IPC 3, 4 and 5.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

IMC will conduct a Vulnerability Analysis to understand the needs, roles and dynamics of women, girls, boys, men, PWDs and elderly in different age groups. The project scope will also include coverage surveys and bottleneck analysis to identify barriers to accessing different interventions and identify solutions to overcome these barriers. IMC will work closely with protection actors to incorporate community awareness to address inequalities or protection threats. Furthermore, IMC will adopt SBCC approaches to eradicate stigma and norms against women, girls, PWDs and older persons. Specifically, IMC will ensure separation of male and female in the admission ward, and WASH facilities will be separated for male and female patients. Access to health services will be improved to all women and girls that reside in the project target locations, they will be oriented on the concept of GBV as well. Regardless of community social norms, women will be engaged through the program activities and all women will have access to community feedback and complaint mechanisms to share their needs and issues regarding access to services. IMC will put in place safe and friendly systems for women to respond to their needs. IMC will also mainstream gender equality in program review and planning through deep analysis of the program results to assess impact of health activities to target beneficiaries, particularly women and girls. This proposed project is also designed and will be implemented to account for the challenges that may be faced by PWDs and Older People. IMC will promote accessibility of the project’s services by defining cross-impairment indicators for monitoring and ensuring that support is given to people with different impairments. All IMC staff and volunteers will receive protection training that highlights the special needs of these groups. Risk communication activities will also emphasize the role of the community on ensuring protection for PWDs and older people, and their immediate referrals to health facilities in case of any illnesses. IMC will use the IASC Guidelines on the inclusions of PWDs in Humanitarian Actions.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

IMC will adopt a multi-sectoral approach in services delivery in all its target locations, integrating health, nutrition and protection services. IMC directly implements nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive interventions, as well as strengthening the enabling environment for nutrition to address the immediate, underlying and basic causes of undernutrition through provision of both curative and preventive nutrition services. In areas/facilities where IMC does not implement nutrition programs, IMC will closely work with nutrition partners on the ground to establish a well-coordinated and coherent referral systems. Through this, IMC will contribute to decreasing the incidence and prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 and PLWs.
