Project Summary

The CARE project will respond to the vulnerable health needs of vulnerable Syrian population in Deir AL Zor, Homs, Idlib, Hama and Lattakia. These areas have been considered to have high number people of needs and severity scales (3 and above) according to Health Sector severity and PiN 2022 where services, resources and assistance are limited. As a result, substantial responses are needed to reduce the suffering of those who have lived through considerable hardship, especially during COVID-19 pandemic. The objective of the CARE project is to improve the access of men, women, boys and girls, including people with disabilities to health services in the above-mentioned governorates. The CARE project will benefit 376,485 highly vulnerable individuals, in 2 years, including 105,416 men, 105,416 women, 82,826 boys and 82,827 girls through the restoration of access health services through the rehabilitation of 10 health centers in the governorates of Deir Al Zor, Homs, Hama, Idlib and Lattakia, and then providing health materials to the same rehabilitated centers. In addition, 510 disability devices will be distributed to PWDs in the same targeted governorates. The proposed activities are under objective 3 of the Health Sector log frame (Strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements and changes in context) activity 3.2 (Increase access to quality health services by establishing functional health facilities and mobile medical units and supporting referral), indicator 3.2.1 (# of health facilities refurbished or rehabilitated). And objective 1 (Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need), activity 1.5 (Provide health facilities with essential medicines and medical supplies, including personal protective equipment (PPE)), indicators : 1.5.2 # of medical masks (including N95 respirators) provided to health facilities, 1.5.3 # of medical gloves provided to health facilities, 1.5.4 # of protective gowns provided to health facilities, activity 1.5 ( Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, and referral), indicator 1.1.4 ( # of physical rehabilitation sessions supported).These centers provide out of charge services for population and provided materials will help to increase the quality of provided services to beneficiaries.ADRA will prioritize areas which include largest number of children under five years, women of reproductive age (15 – 49 years), older persons (age 60 and above), displaced persons –including refugees, IDPs and returnees, and persons living with disabilities and chronic disease. ADRA will further prioritize sub-districts with highest health severity scale (3 and above), dense urban settings, areas with lower/ limited functional health facilities. Where possible, ADRA will select the areas where ADRA is already present in order to provide an integrated approach including the sectors of WASH, livelihood, food security or education.

4 - Needs

A combination of conflict in Syria and deteriorating economic conditions, combined with the COVID-19 pandemic, resulted in increased vulnerability of the Syrian population (HNAP Syria 2021), putting the health system at risk. In 2022, 12.23 million people are in need of health services including over 4.4 million displaced people, 1.33 million children under 5 years old, 503,000 children are expected to be born (2022 HNO), and according to MSNA (Multi-sectoral need assessment) 2021, 1.3 million people with disabilities among Syria are most likely to live more than an hour away from a functional health facility, which force them to pay additional costs for mobility, face lack of availability of services, and fear of COVID-19 at the facility. With just 63% of hospitals and 56% of Primary Healthcare Centers are fully functional (WHO Whole of Syria combined HERAMS Q2 2021), forcing already vulnerable individuals to cover long distances in search of adequate health care facilities and services, especially with the critical situation of COVID-19 pandemic. Most of the increased severity was observed in Deir-ez-Zor, Hama, Homs (2022 SYRIA HNO). In Idlib, the lack of functional health services continued causing increased pressure on local health services, particularly in the areas of high returnees’ population.

5 - Implementation of activities

ADRA will conduct community-based needs assessments and will meet with men, women, boys, girls, (children, adolescents, young adults, adults and older adults), and PDWs and pre implementation surveys will include community’s feedback ensuring the needs of the local population are taken into consideration. ADRA will share the contact number of the complaint mechanism making sure the beneficiaries and stakeholders are aware of it and are able to provide anonymous complaints and to report any feedback to the organization.ADRA, in coordination with MoLAE, SARC and sectors (Health) will conduct a detailed technical assessment of the planned infrastructure projects (health centers). The technical assessment will be the basis of the BoQ needed for the tender process to identify a qualified and experienced contractor. A number of stakeholders (such as the staff of the selected center and community members) will be consulted ensuring that the needs of the community are met, including men, women, boys, girls, PWDs and the elderly, and appropriate approvals obtained. For the health supplies, ADRA will conduct the needs in coordination with SARC and hand the supplies directly to the centers. The same procurement process will be followed as for the rehabilitation of centers. The disability devices activity will be advertised through local partners inviting the PWDs to apply for the required device to be included in the beneficiary list who will the receive the required devices.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Likelihood: low, Impact: high. ADRA will assure the safety of intervene areas. ADRA will report on attacks against health care through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), ADRA providing preparedness trainings for the staff (including first aid courses) in case of any incidents.Beneficiaries may be placed at risk due to project interventions including the possibility to tensions between beneficiaries and community members. Likelihood: low, Impact: high. ADRA will work closely with local partners to ensure that the planned interventions are culturally appropriate, sensitive to local customs and traditions and harmonized with other forms of assistance to ‘do no harm’ and minimize the risks to beneficiaries. COVID-19 continues to spread and impact on project staff / beneficiaries. Likelihood: low, Impact: high. ADRA will assure the social distancing during all stages of the project, PPEs will be available for ADRA team, partners, stakeholders as well.An increase in GBV if the abuser finds out about the reported cases. Likelihood: low, Impact: high. ADRA will ensure that the ADRA staff present at the center are aware of the risk and know the safe referral pathways in place in the area. The staff will also be trained on communication with persons (male, female, boys and girls) with diverse disabilities.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The risk of SEA by staff: likelihood: low, impact: high. Mitigation: ADRA ensured all staff are trained on SPHEA and sign the code of conduct and safeguarding on a yearly basis. In addition, the functional complaint and feedback mechanism allows for reporting of such cases directly to the MEAL department.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Delays in obtaining the necessary approvals to implement activities and security approvals to access project areas in a timely manner from relevant authorities. ADRA will maintain strong connections with all levels of stakeholders to follow-up on necessary permissions and approvals and discuss any delays with the Ministry of local Administration and environment (MOLAE) and SARC.Changes in the security situation especially with the COVID-19 status in Syria, which might impact ADRA team, partners and the community particularly in project locations. Regular monitoring of the security situation with UNDSS, INGOs, sectors, stakeholders and staff. ADRA has an established and proven safety and security plan which is shared with all staff and will provide necessary PPE for staff to help prevent the spread of the virus and protection of staff, beneficiaries and stakeholders.Extended delays in fund transfers to Syria or devaluation of SYP may place project activities outside the planned budget. Closely monitor exchange rates, use competitive tender processes, adjust targets or search for additional funding. Due to recent challenges to transfer funds to Syria, ADRA has opened a variety of different channels and approaches to money transfer to help ensure the smooth transfer of project funds.
