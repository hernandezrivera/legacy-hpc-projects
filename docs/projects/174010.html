Project Summary

Context/ Needs
Pakistan hosts approximately 1.4 million registered Afghan refugees and between 600,000 and one million unregistered Afghans. Roughly 32% of the registered Afghan refugees live in refugee camps known as Refugee Villages (RV), whereas 68% live outside the RVs, in urban and rural areas along with host communities. Approximately 39% of the registered Afghan refugees in Pakistan are school-aged children between 5-18 years. Out of these 544,102 children, only 22% (119,403) received a formal education in 2017 despite the fact that all registered refugee children have access to free education in Pakistani public schools and UNHCR schools in the RVs. Only 30% of all school-aged children in the RVs are enrolled in school; the rate is even lower among girls and stands at only 18% (UNHCR Mapping, 2017).
The UNHCR and other partners are primarily focusing on primary education and there is lack of opportunities for post primary education, particularly for girls. These issues further complicated by cultural norms, negative perceptions about girls’ education and gender disparities which all increase the sufferings of adolescent girls.
The gap between the enrolment of girls and boys increases exponentially when moving beyond lower secondary to higher secondary education with zero enrolment of girls at the higher secondary level in the urban settlements in KP. Only 4% of adolescent girls are known to attend secondary schools (UNHCR Education strategy 2016-18).
In UNHCR’s assessment, key reasons cited by refugees for poor school attendance, retention, and completion include: 1) Distance of schools, particularly secondary schools, which is especially challenging for girls due to traditional movement restrictions, 2) Inability to pay private Afghan/Pakistani/NGO-run school fees, 3) Lack of qualified teachers in RVs, and 4) Socio-cultural traditions that undervalue education, particularly for girls, as well as an insufficient number of segregated schools for girls. Most children not attending school are involved in labor or domestic responsibilities to support families, and children’s attendance is sometimes seen as an economic loss by poorer households. The findings of RI’s multiple assessments and studies (January 2019, April 2019, February 2020, and April 2020) with Afghan refugees support UNHCR’s conclusions.
Analysis of ‘Population and enrolment data’ from selected RVs in district Peshawar reflects a critical situation. Out of 16,553 primary-age children in selected RVs, only 2,397 (less than 15%) are enrolled in RVs schools while more than 85% out of school. A total of 11,671 post-primary age children (12-17 years) in selected five RVs are assumed as out-of-school as there is no facility available for post-primary education at RVs level. Analysis of enrolment data of RVs school reflects exceptionally high dropout rate. 28% children enroll in Grade-1 drop down to 9% in Grade-5.
Project Summary
The proposed action aim to support out-of-school Afghan children residing in Refugees villages (RVs) to fulfil their right to education. In response to know afghan children education barriers and needs particularly being faced by children in selected RVs, RI propose an integrated and multi-faceted program using combination of tested, new, innovative and contextually appropriate approaches. Align with the Government of Pakistan ‘Single National Curriculum’ strategy and UNHCR’s strategy of inclusion and integration, RI plan to enroll, mainstream and retain Afghan refugee children in nearby government primary and middle schools. RI comprehensive project model include mobilization strategy and facilitation for Afghan communities to encourage and prepare them to enroll children in government schools outside RVs. The objective of the program is to improve girls’ and boys’ equitable access and enrolment in primary / post-primary education in government schools, with a particular emphasis on girls and marginalized children.

1- Project Activities

Over a 12-month project, the program will increase enrolment and retention of out-of-school afghan children in public schools (primary, middle), ‘situated nearby or around selected RVs’ through a combination of activities, that seek to break down socio-cultural barriers preventing children, particularly Afghan girls, from entering the schools. RI will target public schools, Basic Education Community Schools (BECS), Elementary Education Foundation (EEF) and National Commission for Human Development (NCHD) schools to mainstream and enroll Afghan children via below mentioned activities: Objective 1: Afghan children have increased access to safe, quality education services in target government schools: Objective 1 aim to roll out RI’s School Readiness Program (SRP) for primary age children. RI is implementing SRP program for Afghan refugees, which is designed to support out of school (OOS) children to enroll (and remain in) Pakistani government schools. SRP centers prepare out-of-school Afghan children age 5-9 for enrolment in age-appropriate grades through specially designed and approved content in an eight-week program. Likewise, for middle school age children RI will implement its Post-Primary Readiness Program. Analysis of enrolment data suggests a huge gap in the transition rate from primary to middle school with a 70% drop out rate. To address this gap, RI propose to replicate its ongoing PPRP in target RVs to promote and support enrolment post-primary grades. The activities includes: 1) comprehensive awareness and mobilization sessions with target children and their caregivers; 2) facilitation of admission process through coordination with PTCs and school management; 3) Provision of school supplies and learning material to target children, 4) Catch-up classes for children in grade-5 to cover learning losses and prepare children for transition and; 5) A stipend of PKR 1,200 ($8) per month for Afghan girls to cover cost of transportation during the academic year (conditional on a minimum 80% attendance rate). RI will organize Community enrolment campaigns in March-April 2021 and September 2021 at provincial, district and community/RV levels to align with government annual enrolment campaigns. RI will work with existing Afghan committees to strengthen their capacities and develop linkages school management to support ARs children enrolment and protection in schools. Objective 2: Afghan children are retained in safe, quality learning environments in government target schools: Objective 2 aim to target and address supply-side barriers through activities like CPD program for government teacher in target schools to improve learning outcomes, school environment and promote retention. RI will deliver comprehensive and context specific CPD program to the teachers of public, NCHD, BECS and EEF target schools. Based on need assessment in target government schools, RI will deliver essential school improvements, repair and rehabilitation in target government schools. This is especially important where parents of girls in particular may be concerned about the adequate basic facilities and security, such as boundary walls. In order to improve community buy in for Afghan refugee enrollment, RI will strengthen existing PTCs to enable them to better conduct outreach activities with the target refugee communities. Specifically, RI will upgrade the existing training and policies around PTCs to focus on girls and refugees and will also support them to develop school improvement plans more effectively. And to support children’s physical and mental wellbeing in schools, RI will work with PTCs and school leaders to establish after-school clubs. The clubs will support the development of children’s life skills and healthy living and develop their understanding of gender discrimination. The clubs will also support integration and friendships between Afghan refugee and Pakistani children.
