Project Summary

The project of " Provision of Specialized Mental Health and Free Telephone Counseling (HL) Services to Women, Men, Boys and Girls Affected by Violence in Gaza Strip" aims at contributing to alleviate the effects of the Israeli occupation practices through delivery of quality specialized mental health services to the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys who are affected in the context of the Great March of Return (GMR) and living in the most affected and marginalized socio-economically vulnerable and under-served communities. The project has three main interventions as follows:
A1) Provision of specialized mental health services for 400 children (boys and girls) and 600 adults/parents (women and men) who have displayed symptoms of severe psychosocial distress. By using the existing referral system, those cases will be detected and referred by the case managers and psychosocial support staff working in family centers and Child Protection and MHPSS networks. Additionally, other cases will be detected through structured activities implemented through other venues such as GCMHP’s community centers and their field teams. The clinical services will be provided by specialized multi-disciplinary team including psychiatrists, psychologists, psychiatric nurses, social workers, and rehabilitation specialists at GCMHP three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah and Khanyounis. Therapy plans include psychotherapy, play therapy, medication, family counseling and home visits. GCMHP has procedures that offer practical guidance and assistance in taking protection issues seriously in the workplace, and to make staff aware of their roles and responsibilities while interacting directly with women and children.
A 2) Provision of free telephone counseling (Hot Line) service for 400 persons including children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who couldn’t reach mental health centres due to economic or social barriers. Through this service, the telephone counselors will provide psycho education, individual and family counseling, and also detect and refer children and adults with severe symptoms to GCMHP community centres to receive specialized mental health services including medications if necessary. This service helps overcome stigma attached to mental health problems. This service provides another differentiated aspect, where some cases will be detected and referred to legal and protection services provided by other service providers in Gaza Strip. GCMHP through its awareness raising activities including (public meetings, radio spots and programs, street billboards, publications, etc,) enhances access of the target group to this service.
A3) Provide training courses to 50 child protection workers (50% women) who are working in the field of child protection and MHPSS sector. The training topics will address the most common disorders among children and adults, and detection and referral skills.
A4) Produce one fact sheet, including gender and age-disaggregated data, to document trends/patterns of psychological disorders among those assisted to be shared with all stakeholders to help improve the prevention and protection response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview, that issued by the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs in 2019, showed that the violence has already generated widespread mental health and psychosocial consequences affecting approximately 52,098 people, including 26,049 children, in need of MHPSS responses; this is in addition to the estimated 210,000 already acutely vulnerable suffering from severe or moderate mental disorders. According to OCHA database, from 01 January 2018 to 17 August 2019, violence and ongoing conflict resulted in the killing of 66 children; and the injury of 7,832 children in Gaza. The vast majority of Gaza injuries (536 girls and 7,143 boys) were in the context of GMR demonstrations from 30 March 2018 to 17 August 2019. The GMR demonstrations have also resulted in high levels of psychosocial distress, with an estimated additional 25,000 children in need of moderate and severe MHPSS support. This adds to the already significant mental health burden of 250,689 children in Gaza in need of MHPSS and individual case management support. GCMHP issued a factsheet in 2019 about the mental health situation in the context of GMR. The factsheet confirmed that 14.8% of family members, 34% of injured persons, and 57% of injured persons with amputation (who received PFA by its mobile teams) have needed specialized mental health services and they were referred to GCMHP community centers and mobile stations. With a fact that GCMHP is the only NGO provides these services, and that sever cases have not been responded by other actors. In fact, GCMHP witnessed a significant increase in the number of cases who received its therapeutic services in 2019, as GCMHP’s specialized therapeutic services reached in the first half of 2019 to 2,212 new and follow up cases including 426 children (176 were between 1-10 years and 250 were between 10-18 years, and the total number of girls was 162) and 1,786 adults (983 men and 803 women) the numbers of men were 442 between 19-30 years, and 499 were between 31-60 years, 42 above 60 years while the numbers of women were 345 between 19-30 years, and 400 were between 31-60 years and 58 above 60 years. In 2018, PC's members referred monthly around 30-40 of vulnerable children and adults facing protection risks such as GBV, psychological distress and mental disorders. While in 2019, the PC members referred monthly around 70-100 of vulnerable children and adults facing protection risks. Within the last three months (July- September 2019), they referred 205 cases to GCMHP community centers (for example, 71 cases from Maan’s family centers, 22 cases from Terre des hommes -TDH, 24 from MSF, 8 from Al Mezan center for HR, 10 from Tamer Institute’s family centers, et.). GCMHP is committed to involve both men and women in data collection and needs assessment phase considering their environments, economic situations, gender and age group needs. GCMHP teams observed high levels of trauma, depression and anxiety symptoms among injured people and their families including children and adults. Moreover, women reported high levels of violence practiced by the injured men towards wives and children. To enhance identification and engagement of people at risk, directing them to appropriate services, and enhancing the continuity of care provided to individuals at risk. GCMHP provides its free telephone counseling which is the only operating one in Gaza strip. Within the first half of the year 2019, the GCMHP records showed that 710 callers benefited from this service, and the vast majority of them were women and girls (58%) including (3% under age 18 years, 80% aged 19-59 years, and 17% aged 60+), while men and boys were (42 %) including (3% under age 18 years, 96% aged 19-59 years, and 1% aged 60+).

2 - Activities

Clashes and GMR demonstrations exacerbate existing protection and MHPSS needs, resulting in humanitarian needs to ensure psychosocial support interventions. This project will support the delivery of quality specialized mental health services to the women and men, girls and boys, affected by the current crisis taking place at the border areas in the Gaza Strip through the following: A1) Provision of specialized mental health services for children (boys and girls) and adults/parents (women and men) who have displayed symptoms of severe psychosocial distress. By using the existing referral system, those cases will be detected and referred by the GCMHP professionals as well as case managers and psychosocial support staff working in family centers and Child Protection and MHPSS networks located in Rafah, Khan Younis, Middle Zone, Gaza and North governorates. Accordingly, this service will target cases referred from all areas in Gaza Strip. GCMHP has already a follow up/tracking system of referrals and cases received in addition to feedback mechanism to referring organizations. The clinical services will be provided by specialized multi-disciplinary teams at GCMHP three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah and Khanyounis.Therapy plans include psychotherapy, medications, counseling and home visits. GCMHP has procedures that offer practical guidance and assistance in taking protection issues seriously in the workplace, and to make staff aware of their roles and responsibilities while interacting directly with women and children. A 2) Provision of free telephone counseling (Hot Line) service for children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who couldn’t reach mental health centres due to economic or social barriers. Through this service, the telephone counselors will provide psycho education, individual and family counseling, and also detect and refer children and adults with severe symptoms to GCMHP community centres to receive specialized mental health services including medications if necessary. This service receives calls from all areas at Gaza strip. A3) Provide training courses which will target new 50 child protection workers (50% women) who are working in the ﬁeld of child protection and MHPSS sector. The training topics will address the most common disorders among children and adults, and detection and referral skills. A4) Produce one fact sheet, including gender and age- disaggregated data, to document trends/patterns of psychological disorders among those assisted to be shared with all stakeholders to help improve the prevention and protection response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions. GCMHP Users: the users of GCMHP community centers are adults/parents and children who are direct or indirect victims of human rights violations, e.g. any individual has direct/indirect personal experience in the context of Great March of Return. The access of beneﬁciaries to the community centers is ensured through addressing any economic, physical or social limitations ( eg. providing transportation for cases, conducting home visits and availability of free telephone counseling (hotline). Complaints Mechanism: GCMHP is always in place to respond to and address complaints through the following :1) Complaints boxes at community centers and Head Quarter of GCMHP. All these boxes are easily accessed by beneﬁciaries. 2)A speciﬁc part at its account page on oﬃcial website and Facebook to receive complaints and inquires, these tools are available for all audience). 3)An employee is allocated and responsible for following up these complaints if founded and sent them to the High management. 4) All issues and publications of GCMHP such as brochures, billboards, radio spots, magazines and newsletters, etc.. include Full Contact Information of GCMHP (addresses of centers, telephone numbers, mail, and Hotline number).

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

First: GCMHP will implement the project activities at its community centers which are distant from any threats of violence. The GCMHP community centers are located in the middle and west of cities (the least affected areas within times of emergencies in Gaza Strip). The GCMHP facilities are distant from possible perpetrators such as police and armed forces to provide a reassuring feeling or instill fear depends on the context. Second: GCMHP made adaptations such as fitting ramps and railings to its facilities so that all individuals and groups can access and use the facilities in safety and with dignity. Third: all GCMHP facilities, policies, clinical procedures, systems, forms, templates and instructions ensure that confidentiality and privacy of patients and respected all forms of consultation, counseling and personal information sharing. On the other hands, the therapy rooms are well separated from public spaces or the waiting area. GCMHP facilities and services respect the gender needs and concerns especially the women and girls’ needs, (GCMHP services can be accessed by women and girls, as more than 50% of GCMHP beneficiaries are women and girls). GCMHP was and still is requesting consent to collect and use data and makes sure the beneficiary is clearly informed of all services and has the capacity to give informed consent. GCMHP has also a well-designed and protected MIS system for patients that secure data storage. Fifth: GCMHP has many policies such as a gender mainstreaming policy, child safety policy and security policy that make sure that there is a variety of staff (male, female) who are well trained on Gender policy. Sixth: GCMHP was and still distributing PFA toolkits/gifts to the children at home away from the public eye. Seventh: The GCMHP services including telephone counseling ensure that services are provided at an adequate time when individuals can realistically access them. Eighth: GCMHP has M&E system that considers data disaggregated by age, gender, and location or specific group (e.g. persons with disabilities).

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

GCMHP has a new strategy 2019-2021 that states clearly that equitable access to mental health services should be through accessing networks and awareness materials, encouraging referral for those in need of mental health support. A special emphasis is placed on reaching out to the most vulnerable including persons living with a disability or suffering from high levels of trauma. A flexible response approach ensuring marginalized groups, areas impacted by new eruptions of violence, or emerging and urgent needs can be responded to. This strategy has also another value regarding the independence and non-political affiliation, as GCMHP is a non-political, independent organization. It is not influenced by political parties or ideologies. Mental health care is provided solely on the basis of need, without discrimination or preference. GCMHP is a key member in many clusters such as PC, HP, MHPSS and CP WGs, and PNGO’s rehabilitation cluster. GCMHP is always consults with the Protection Cluster including the GBV and Child Protection sub-clusters, about power dynamics in the area of intervention and use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access or discrimination against particular groups. GCMHP coordinate also with specialized agencies to identify individuals and include them in the program interventions. On the other hands, GCMHP has an existing referral system to receive referrals from other organizations. This system is generalized among all PC members and well operated by GCMHP and partners. GCMHP was and still is sharing factsheets and information with the Protection cluster, including the Child Protection sub-clusters by using many forms like 4 Ws- matrix and other templates. GCMHP do not assume an “open door” policy is enough. It makes sure that there are other possibilities for submitting complaints that do not require the beneficiaries to expose themselves to project staff. It opened all social media platforms and websites to public to provide any feedback or complaints.
