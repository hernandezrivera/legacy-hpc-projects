Needs

From a human rights organization’s perspective, the most pressing protection concerns in Gaza in recent months were: (1) Excessive and lethal use of force against protesters partaking in the Great March of Return (GMR) demonstrations in ARA along the Gaza-Israel border fence where protesters, paramedics and journalists, who did not pose a serious threat to life or injury against Israeli soldiers barricaded on the other side of the border, were routinely targeted with live ammunition; and (2) Restriction on freedom of movement of medical patients seeking treatment outside Gaza, with additional measures applied extensively in 2018 by Israeli authorities to deprive patients of travel permits on the grounds of being relatives of Hamas operatives or for taking part in GMR protests, a manifestation of collective punishment affecting the most vulnerable. As of 13 Oct. 2018, Al Mezan’s documentation shows that from the start of the GMR protests on 30 Mar. 2018, 215 Palestinians have been killed in the Gaza Strip. Of those, 162 were killed at the protests designated gathering sites —including 33 children, 1 woman, 2 journalists, 3 paramedics, and 4 PwD. Another 10,778 persons were treated at local hospitals for injuries sustained in the protests, including 1,948 children, 438 women, 120 paramedics, and 124 journalists. Of those injured, 6,151 were hit by live fire, including 981 children and 118 women. The Israeli forces continue to keep the dead bodies of 10 fatalities, including two minors. At the time of writing, GMR protests are showing no signs of waning and are likely to continue at the same pace in 2019 if the de-escalation efforts led by Egypt continue to falter. This will require human rights organizations working on monitoring and documentation of IHL/IHRL violations to maintain their personnel increased presence in the field to monitor incidents of violations against protesters, and farming and fishing communities working in the ARA. Israel also continued to impose strict measures on freedom of movement. Although restrictions affect the entire population of the Gaza Strip, they are particularly detrimental to the life of patients referred to medical facilities outside Gaza to receive treatment unavailable in the costal enclave. In the first half of 2018, patients submitted 11,846 applications for exit permits via Erez crossing to Israeli Coordination and Liaison Administration (CLA), less than 60% of these applications were approved. Of the delayed/rejected patients’ applications, 2,682 were submitted by male patients and 2,192 by female patients ¬— ¬including 889 submitted for child patients, according to WHO monthly reports covering the first half of 2018. As approval rate for patients permits in 2018 is largly equivalent to 2017 rate (both less than 60%), this rate is likely to remain unchanged next year and thus over 8000 application are expected to be rejected/delayed in 2019. Considering that rejected/delayed patients are likely to submit multiple application (one for every new hospital appointment) and by assuming 3-4 applications per patient, it is expected that at least 2000 patients (45% females, 55%males) will need legal interventions in 2019 to secure travel permits via Erez. Shortly after the GMR protests ignited in 30 March, Israeli authorities started imposing a blanket denial of permits for patients injured in the protests who were seeking medical treatment outside Gaza. A joint-petition submitted by Al Mezan and Adalah to the Israeli High Court in April 2018 managed to secure a ruling in favor of a Palestinian patient who had been denied permits to access hospital in Ramallah on the grounds of his participation in the protests. Since the court ruling, over 30 Palestinians injured in the protests were granted travel permits to receive medical care outside Gaza.

Activities

Activities under PC objectives #1. (1) In 2019, Al Mezan field workers will continue to monitor and document IHL/IHRL violations committed against Palestinians, with focus on violations against protestors, fishermen and farmers. (2) In 2018, Al Mezan field workers have fully documented (i.e. collected personal info, testimonies, and supporting evidence) on almost all GMR protests’ fatalities and on major cases of injuries, and monitored (i.e. compiled data) on the rest of cases of injuries that were treated at local hospital. In 2019, Al Mezan will deploy 5 additional field workers to fully document cases of injuries affecting children and women that were monitored in 2018. The will also assist the existing field workers in in monitoring and documentation of violations. (3) Documentation data will feed into the database for analysis of violation trends and will be utilized to draft press releases, reports, factsheets, monthly statistical updates on IHL/IHRL violations and violations against children in conflicts. Produced materials will be disseminated to over 700 professionals, media organizations, and NGOs through existing mailing list, and will also be shared via Al Mezan’s website and social media platforms. Data will also be used to answers requests for sharing information received on weekly basis from UN agencies working in oPt. (4) Locally, representatives of Al Mezan will meet in Gaza with members of the international community to brief them on violation trends and protection concerns. Field visits will also be arranged. Activities under PC objective #2: (5) Al Mezan lawyers will provide legal aid (counselling and interventions) for patients delayed/denied exit permits to access treatment outside Gaza. The legal intervention will be in the form of official correspondence sent with supporting material to Israeli CLA at Erez requesting an exit permit for the patient. (6) Al Mezan will also join human rights organization in Israel to bring petition to the High Court similar to two successful petitions made in 2018 (one on GMR injured protestors and the second on female patients deprived of access to medical care on grounds of their family ties with Hamas operatives). This type of intervention benefits victims beyond those directly represented and can reduce the amount of required responsive interventions (complaints to CLA) if the court ruled in favor of the plaintiffs. (7) provision of legal aid to casualties of GRM protests: first, though civil notifications which are sent to the Israeli Ministry of Defense within 60 days of the incident to secure the victim’s right to seek reparation through Israeli justice system, and second, by sending complaints to the Military Advocate General requesting a criminal investigation into the incident in question. (8) legal aid will also be provided to other victims of IHL/IHRL violations, particularly farmers and fishermen who are most at-risk of being shot, fall victim to CIDTP, or have their equipment and fishing boats destroyed or seized thereby jeopardizing their only means of livelihood. They will receive legal counselling and will be provided with legal assistance to address the violation, in case their equipment or boat had been seized a complaint will be sent on their behalf to Israeli authorities to demand its return. (9) farmers and fishermen will be invited to awareness sessions carried out by lawyers from Al Mezan in Gaza. The participants will be advised on the violations they are exposed to, the legal mechanisms they can pursue to seek protection and redress, and the specific obstacles impacting protection as posed by the Israeli justice system. Total number of beneficiaries is expected to be at least 2,320= 1000 cases of IHL/IHRL violations occurring in 2019 monitored + 1000 cases of child injuries occurring in 2018 in context of GMR protests fully documented + 30 members of the international community in Gaza + 250 patients + 20 fishermen + 20 farmers.

Outcome indicators and targets:10 statements, policy, resolution from UN agencies, EU and third states in support of protection concerns raised during the course of the project;1 Israeli policy challenges successfully though petitions to Israeli High Court;125 patients, who had been denied/delayed access to medical care outside Gaza, are granted travel permit via Erez. (55 women, 40 men, and 30 children); Activities monitoring: Beneficiary of legal counseling and representation are solicited to provide feedback and suggestions using a questionnaire specifically designed to measure satisfaction of legal aid recipients. LTF working group members are updated on monthly basis about development of legal intervention on the subjects of accountability and freedom of movement.Al Mezan will strive, where possible, to coordinate with local HR organization working on monitoring and documentation of violations. Evaluation: Al Mezan organizes at least one evaluation session annually (in January) whereby a gender balanced group of beneficiaries is invited to participate in focus group discussions to elicit their feedback for Al Mezan’s annual evaluation process. Al Mezan’s training staff is responsible for collection of data with support from internal focal points, including the fundraising officer and project coordinator. Data is analyzed and used for learning, planning and reporting. Community Engagement:Given the fact that affected communities are the main source of data for Monitoring and Documentation, their involvement starts at an early stage when fieldworkers obtain testimonies from victims and eyewitnesses, and in the process, victims are provided with explanation of the work and its expected outcome. Further, victims of violations whose cases fall under the project’s legal services are referred to Al Mezan’s Legal Aid Unit to discuss their legal options. Legal services are implemented in a manner that ensures respect of protection mainstreaming principles, most importantly ‘do no harm’ principle. The recipients of legal services are informed of possible ramifications of the legal actions, and it will be made clear that the final decisions to initiate any legal action is theirs to take. Finally, NGOs/CBOs which provide services to affected communities regularly express their interest, including during Al Mezan evaluation session, in Monitoring and Documentation data which facilitate their work and inform decisions on prioritization and allocation of resources.
