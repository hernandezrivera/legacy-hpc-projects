Project Summary

The intervention focuses on ensuring access to quality education for affected children, with a special emphasis on the well-being and empowerment of women in these crisis-affected areas. The program aims to overcome barriers to education caused by emergencies, providing a holistic approach that includes academic support, emotional well-being, and skill development. By targeting both children and women, the intervention seeks to create a positive impact on the entire community, fostering resilience and contributing to the long-term recovery and development of these regions

1 - Needs

Nigeria, the most populous country in Africa, has a population of approximately 206 million people, with an estimated 51% being female. While the Nigerian government has made efforts to improve access to education for all, significant challenges remain. According to the United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF), Nigeria has the highest number of out-of-school children in the world. The situation is particularly dire for girls, who face additional barriers to education such as early marriage, gender-based violence, cultural beliefs, and poverty. Despite the progress made in recent years, girls in Nigeria continue to have significantly lower enrollment rates, completion rates, and literacy rates than boys. In emergency affected areas, the education situation gets worse as a result of conflict, pandemics or other risk and hazards. School closures as a result of emergencies increases the number of out of school children and dropouts. Displacements also disrupts education as several children have become IDPs and are not able to gain access to education. Security concerns in areas affected by conflicts have impacted education with many parents not willing to send their children to school due to risk of abduction, sexual violence and forced marriage. The teacher shortages in emergency affected communities have reduced the quality of education with several school operating with inadequate number of teachers. Adequate funding for education is essential to improving education opportunities in Nigeria. The Nigerian government has committed to investing 20% of its annual budget in education, but in reality, the funding falls far short of this target. As a result, there is a need for increased investment in education from both the government and the private sector to improve infrastructure, teacher training, and access to educational resources.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Drawing from the elements outlined in the global protection cluster guidance on protection mainstreaming, the project will support the enrolment of boys in schools and provides school supplies, in addition to focusing on girls and women who are the main beneficiaries to prevent potential harm or conflicts on the girls and women. Safety considerations for girls and women enrolled in the non-formal education program include establishing learning spaces in secure areas within the communities. Where possible, school classrooms will be utilised, particularly since lessons are conducted after formal school hours. To preserve the dignity of girls and women at schools, visibility labeling stickers will be utilised to clearly distinguish between male and female latrines, preventing any confusion. Additionally, suitable services will be implemented to ensure that water is readily accessible to girls near the latrines. Furthermore, the project will provide specialised support items for women and girls with various forms of disabilities, ensuring their access to services and enabling their active participation with their peers. For vulnerable girls who are unable to attend either formal schools or non-formal schools, an assessment will be conducted to determine if they have access to a mobile phone. Those who do will be supported in their learning through the IVR learning platform.The beneficiaries will have access to reporting platforms to provide feedback and claim their rights.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will organise a community inception meeting, promoting child participation with representation from children with disabilities and at-risk women and girls. This meeting will serve as a platform for participants to actively influence the project design and collaboratively plan its implementation.The carefully selected participants of the inception meeting, representing the communities of implementation, will constitute the project steering committee. This committee will hold regular meetings with project staff to present project reports and gather community feedback.Working in collaboration with relevant government agencies, the project steering committee will support monitoring activities alongside project staff. Regular monitoring review meetings will be conducted to share insights, discuss learnings, and address feedback. All feedback received will be shared with the project and organisational management team for consideration and further action.
