Project Summary

"The proposed 12-month project will be implemented directly by World Vision and target 9040 vulnerable individuals in camps 12, 13, and 19 of Cox’s Bazar to address protection and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) issues which will work towards the achievement Enhanced community-based protection systems to prevent and respond to GBV, particularly for at-risk and vulnerable women and adolescent girls. The Objective/ Outcome level actions will address change from an interlinked two-pronged approach focusing on both prevention and response as detailed under the activities sections below. Thus, the project will target women and adolascent girls who will be identified but also encouraged to receive services in a dignified manner, at the WV Women girl safe spaces which will provide both target PSS/couneling. For any cases that require advanced service they will be referred to clinics through the referral system. Cognizent of the fact that at community effort is required to erradiate GBV and to mitigate any such issues n the camps, WV will faciliate and get buy in from commnuity leaders and faith leaders to educate people on GBV prevention, women rights and services that ensure safety and dignity of all benficiaries, among other relevant topics geared toward GBV prevention.

Objectives

Objective 2: Promote a community-based approach to the response, support community self-protection mechanisms and facilitate meaningful access to specialized services for persons at heightened protection risk, including girls, boys, women and men of all ages and with diverse needs and vulnerabilities, with the ultimate aim of mitigating exposure to protection risks, strengthen the resilience of affected communities, and place communities at the center of the response in line with AAP principles. Objective 4: Boys and girls including adolescents facing life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress have access to well-coordinated and gender-sensitive quality child protection services by 2022. Objective 5: Improve access to quality survivor-centered services by responding to individual needs, preventing and mitigating of GBV risks, and empowering women, girls and survivors of GBV in Rohingya refugee camps and targeted host community sites

Needs

"At its heart, the Rohingya humanitarian crisis is a protection crisis with over 904,000 displaced people living in Cox’s Bazar and as such, is the reason this project will focus extensively on protection programming for children and women. Children, representing 55%4 of the refugee population, continue to face severe protection risks as a result of the emergency, including, psychological distress, neglect, and violence, among others. Additionally, lack of integrated programming for adolescents, as a result of programming gaps and governmental restrictions, has resulted in an increased risk of trafficking, child marriage and child labor, among other harmful practices. There continues to be insufficient access to GBV services for women and girls due to lack of information, inadequate services and social stigma, all the while domestic violence continues to be an acceptable norm. Instances of GBV continue to be reported in high numbers and instances of sexual exploitation and abuse continues to be severely under reported as a result of sensitivity concerns, social stigma and lack of knowledge of services provided, resulting in the employment of negative coping mechanisms on the part of the survivors. There is also a strong need to engage men and boys both as survivors and as agents of change on GBV in addition to women, girls and community leaders in behavior change activities around gender quality and GBV prevention. Considering that GBV is generally under-reported, the recorded cases represent only a small fraction of the overall caseload. Community Focus Group Discussions indicated that stigma, feelings of shame, self-blame, threats of retaliation, rejection by family and community, low awareness about the benefits of services are among the reasons for under reporting. Adolescent girls, who are more vulnerable to child/forced marriage, trafficking, and sexual violence face heightened obstacles to access services. Several studies also found that Rohingya men and boys are subjected to different forms of sexual violence and the multifaceted impact of violence (Women' Refugee Commission report 2018)

Beneficiaries: WV will target some of the most vulnerable, girls, boys, women and men in the target camp blocks and the beneficiary selection will be carried out in a fair, participatory process led by WV project staff and local facilitators. The project cannot at this juncture provide a specific beneficiary target for host communities given the lack of clarity on which communities WV will target. A need assessment will be conducted under GBV and in coordination with other partners in potential host communities that will. decide the target in early 2020. Gender mainstreaming: All project activities as mentioned above and as reflected in needs section will be catering to all gender types. Understanding the gender social relations in this context, WV will ensure that the larger community/or household understands the various nuances around gender relations such as power dynamics and through adequate awareness raising and implement and design activities to ensure that discords are addressed and all functions of the community I.e., faith leaders are engaged in managing issues surrounding GBV.Disaster reduction and social cohesion: WV has worked with site management in certain camps to form and capacitate disaster management committees including in the proposed project target camps such as camp 13 &19. Thus, lessons learned from the DRR projects in the previous year WV will incorporate education components on DRR and mitigation in its awareness sessions in the proposed project as well. Through its existing livelihood projects being implemented in the host communities, linkages will be made between beneficiaries in this project as well as vendors in the host communities to promote economic development which as a result will also encourage social cohesion. Further, where WGSSs or WV’s community kitchens are functioning in the target camps, any host community women living in the near vicinity will also be invited to be a part of the various communal activities including obtain services from the centers.Capacity Building: WV project staff including other cross functional staff will be educated on basic GBV concepts, especially on the referral mechanisms/pathway to that efficient referring can be done between all projects and partners in the target camps.PSEA policy/commitment for WV: As a commitment to the realization of child and adult safeguarding, WV seeks to do no harm to children or adult beneficiaries, to keep the interests of community members especially children at the centre of our activities, and to utilize opportunities to help children be safer within their families and communities. This includes consideration during programme design of local child protection threats and issues and influencing local actors and groups to be safer organizations for children and adult beneficiaries. All project implementation take into consideration of a Community Feedback and Complaints Mechanisms where Children, parents, and other adults are aware of established complaint mechanisms in WV projects and their right to be safe from abuse and exploitation in WV programmes.
