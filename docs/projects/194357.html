Project Summary

Grassroots Relief and Development Agency (GREDA) intends to implement sustainable agriculture, fisheries, livestock and honey processing project for sustainable economic recovery and resilience in Western Equatoria State targeting 10,750 poorest vulnerable Internal Displaced Person (IDPs)/returnees and host community members most of whom are widows, single mothers, pregnant/lactating mothers, youth, old aged person and persons with disabilities living in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties in Western Equatoria State because these counties were badly affected by the conflict between the periods 2013-2016 and unprecedented flooding between the months July-October 2022 that resulted in displacement, many houses collapsed and destroyed livestock and agricultural production that severely affected the livelihoods of the IDPs/returnees and host community members in three targeted counties.
In Western Equatoria State these three (3) Counties (Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo) are in (IPC phase 3) in acute food insecurity.
1. Mundri East County with total population of 100,275 people ( 20,055 people are in acute need of food)
2. Mundri West County with total population of 49,776 people (12,440 people are in acute need of food).
3. Mvolo County with the total population of 75,350 people (18,837 people are in acute need of food)
Thus; although the needs are overwhelming the target beneficiaries of this project is 10,750 poorest vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community members most of whom are widows, single mothers, pregnant/lactating mothers, old aged people and persons with disabilities living in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties in Western Equatoria State.
The average of 60% population in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties are farmers (FAO 2016). The crops grown successfully in these targeted counties include maize, sorghum, beans, pumpkins, okra, tomatoes, groundnuts, and sim-sim. The counties fall within the Green Belt in Western Equatoria State include some of the most fertile lands in the country and farmers in the area can reap two or three harvests per annum from the same plots. However; due to insecurity and flooding the targeted counties in Western Equatoria State experienced one of its worst crop harvests in recent history. The level of food insecurity reached was historical lowest in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties that one person out of four being considered food insecure. The livelihoods inputs/equipment in the conflict affected parts of Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties were looted in several Payams including Bangolo, Medewu, Yeri, Lessi, Dari, Kotobi, Lui, Lozoh Witto Kediba, and Amadi Payams. The signing of the revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCISS) in September 2018 has relatively brought peace in South Sudan including Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties. Many IDPs and returnees have returned in their original home land. Unfortunately they lost all their agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing inputs/equipment. There is high need to supply them with agricultural tools, improved seeds, and seed processing equipment, fisheries kits, veterinary drugs, cold chain, and honey processing equipment, sun-drying racks, and solar driers, smoking ovens, post-harvest technology trainings on food packing, handling, hygiene and storage to empower 10750 IDPs/returnees and host communities most of whom are widows, single mothers, pregnant/lactating mothers, youth, old aged persons and persons with disabilities to produce their own food to reduce malnutrition, hunger and gain strength and resiliency against macroeconomic crisis, climatic shocks and humanitarian access challenges.

1 - Needs

About 6.6 million people, or over half of South Sudan’s population (54%), are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity, classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse between October and November. Of those, 2.2 million people are experiencing worse conditions in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and an estimated 61,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Akobo of Jonglei State; Pibor County in the Greater Pibor Administrative Area. The most food insecure states between October and November 2022, where more than 50% of their populations are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, are Jonglei (68%), Unity (66%), Northern Bahr el Ghazal (62%), Upper Nile (58%), Warrap (57%), and Lakes (57%). In the post-harvest projection period of December 2022 to March 2023, an estimated 6.31 million people (51.0% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 33,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo and Fangak of Jonglei State; and Pibor County of Greater Pibor Administrative Area. During this period, an estimated 1.97 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. In the lean season projection period of April to July 2023, an estimated 7.8 million people (63% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 43,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo, Canal/Pigi and Fangak counties of Jonglei State; and Leer and Mayendit counties of Unity State. During this period, an estimated 2.90 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Given the high levels of severe acute food insecurity in South Sudan, there is a need for immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent the total collapse of livelihoods in the affected counties, particularly those with a high share of populations in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. For populations in Minimal (IPC Phase 1) and Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity, there is a need for resilience-building support and enhanced disaster risk reduction (DRR) strategies to mitigate the effects of climate change. Urgent action is also required for populations classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity to protect their livelihoods and reduce household-level food consumption gaps.The highly food insecure populations are in locations with chronic vulnerabilities worsened by frequent climate-related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells), the macro-economic crisis, conflict and insecurity, and low agricultural production. In the lean-season projection period of April to July 2023, 47 counties will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, 29 counties will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and one county will likely be in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity. Thus; narrowing this analysis in Western Equatoria State three (3) Counties ( Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo) Counties are in (IPC phase 3) acute food insecurity as follows:-1. Mundri East County with total population of 100,275 people ( 20,055 people are in acute need of food)2. Mundri West County with total population of 49,776 people (12,440 people are in acute need of food). 3. Mvolo County with the total population of 75,350 people (18,837 people are in acute need of food)Thus; the target beneficiaries of this project is 10,750 poorest vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community members most of whom are widows, single mothers, pregnant/lactating mothers, youth, old aged persons and persons with disabilities living in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties in Western Equatoria State.

2 - Project activities

To ensure successful implementation of this project the following activities are essentially important for the implementation of this project and the activities are as follows: - 1. Recruit qualified 3 agricultural officers, 3 fishery officers, 3 livestock officers as field coordinators (three officer per county) in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties. 2. Carryout community based participatory planning mobilization to assess beneficiaries’ critical needs, priorities and choices through analyzing technical, financial, environmental and social feasibility to ensure beneficiaries understand the project are prepared to take ownership of the project to enhance successful implementation and sustainability of the project in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties 3. Procure and distribute assorted agricultural inputs (vegetable,crop,and fishing kits) to 10,750 beneficiaries in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties. 4. Setup 60 Kitchen gardens at Nutrition facilities in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties. 5. GREDA animal health workers to carryout vaccination and treatments to 6,300 livestock beneficiaries who are unable to access veterinary drugs to their livestock in Mvolo,Mundri East and West Counties 6. Conduct capacity building and training,asset building and market support targeting 500 beneficiaries in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo counties. 7. Conduct continuous project impact monitoring and evaluations in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties targeting the direct beneficiaries of this project.
