Project Summary

Prior to the conflict, both community members and local stakeholders report that women had a very limited ability to independently enjoy property rights in Northeast Nigeria. These restrictions persist in the conflict period, and there is evidence that the conflict may have made the situation worse for some women, while shifting practices as a result of the conflict might be creating an opportunity for others. Exception to unequal access to rights prior to the conflict occurred for women with high levels of education and wealth, but for most women, enjoyment of housing, and property rights depended on their family relationships. This is despite equal protection of women’s HLP rights under Nigerian law. According to a NRC report, in an interview conducted for research on women’s HLP rights a lawyer in Maiduguri stated: “In most households, decisions about the house, the land or the property such as renovations, repairs, relocations, and sales of land, this is usually made by the man of the house, that is the father or the husband.
Boko Haram crisis in Nigeria has been worsened by the 2024 floods in Borno State. The floods displaced many people who had already been displaced by the Boko Haram insurgency. Some people were forced to move into overcrowded schools and camps. Nigeria's infrastructure is poorly maintained or non-existent, which contributes to the vulnerability of the country to floods. Homes, schools, and farmlands were submerged. More than 300 people died, more than 673,000 people were displaced, Gender-based violence increased. Families were separated, including some unaccompanied children.
On 10 September, 40% of Maiduguri, Borno State’s capital, in northeastern Nigeria, was flooded due to a breach of the nearby Alau Dam. The numbers of internally displaced persons (IDPs) who registered in one of some 30 camps increased to close to 400,000 people within a week. At least 18 of these camps were schools, which contributed to the government’s decision to close some of the camps within a week’s time, to enable reopening of schools in late September. IDPs whose homes were not inhabitable were encouraged to seek shelter in host communities, or in one of the camps that would remain open the longest – Bakassi, Teacher’s village, and Gubio. After ten days, access to affected areas remained a challenge. The 3 main bridges in the city were damaged (Fori, Lagos, and Customs Bridges). Additionally, Muna IDP camp housing approximately 57,000 conflicts affected IDPs, of whom 6,000 were new arrivals, was cut off by water for one week. As a result, people have lost homes, documents and livelihoods as farms were equally washed away by the flood.
The flooding occurred at the height of the lean season in the north, which was already exacerbated by the unprecedented socio-economic crisis country-wide as a result, there is a surge of 40% food inflation rate due to spikes in fuel and therefore affecting food prices.
It is against this backdrop that Care First Community Initiative has opted to offer Housing Land and Property services to those who lost belongings, houses, documentations, psychosocial support at a level that will bring succor to the persons of concern.

1 - Needs

Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) issues are common during crises and can arise in a variety of contexts, including conflicts and natural disasters which is evident in the recent flood situation in Borno State. The needs that arose as a result of the flood are as follows; Displaced people have had difficulty finding housing or accessing land, and some have returned to find their homes destroyed or occupied. Displacement has led to the loss of documents that prove ownership or rights to land or property. This has also led to inequitable assistance. Women and children are disproportionately affected by disinheritance. Land grabbing is another problem displaced persons are battling with during this period of crises and flood. Displaced people should be able to have a home free from the fear of forced eviction. HLP issues are a vital component of humanitarian response and solutions. To address these issues, Care First Community Initiative will; Ensure that through various authorities, land and property registries, cadastral records, and personal documents are kept safe through advocacy. We will advocate for joint ownership where applicable and possible. Responses to HLP issues often involve multiple sectors and actors, including those working in the humanitarian, transitional, and development fields which is why we will make referrals when necessary . Care First Community Initiative wishes to provide services to defend the right to a safe home and to have access to land especially for women and girls.Female restrictions to HLP services stem from women’s diminished ability to exercise legal rights under Islamic and customary law in general and the perception that they are less valuable than men. One internally displaced participant in NRC research stated: “Women inherit less than men because men are greater generally in life and in the scheme of things.” This unequal access to property rights has been particularly exacerbated by the conflict and flood by increasing the number of women who are residing on their own and who may not be able to access land. These women often find it particularly challenging to exercise their HLP rights.

2 - Activities

Following discussions about HLP, five themes are looked at: (a) HLP rights in emergency contexts; (b) HLP in peace processes, peacekeeping and peacebuilding; (c) HLP rights in rule of law and development programming and durable solutions; (d) access to HLP rights for vulnerable groups; and (e) HLP in disaster risk reduction and climate change. Each theme presents a brief overview of relevant planning processes and possible entry points for HLP. This is followed by suggestions on the type of activities that could be undertaken, which may be stand-alone HLP activities or part of other types of programming. Drawn from international humanitarian and human rights law, HLP rights entitle displaced women and girls to having a safe home, free from the fear of forced eviction, a place that offers safety, and the ability to seek livelihood opportunities. Access to HLP rights is foundational to socio-economic inclusion and an essential steppingstone for displaced people to rebuild their lives. Disputes over land and property tenure are often at the centre of conflict. When conflict ends, disputes over occupied land or property are a continued source of instability and may cause recurring displacement. They often undermine long-term solutions for displaced people and may threaten fragile peace agreements. HLP rights also protect people’s relationship or ‘tenure’ over a plot of land, a home or property, and entitle people to claim their land, home or property or get compensation for the loss of it (right to restitution or compensation). It is key for all women and girls to understand the underlying HLP tenure issues or disputes (such as housing construction or rehabilitation, WASH infrastructure, or agriculture/rural livelihoods) and to address these issues and related challenges. Care First Community Initiative will carry out activities to support the HLP rights of mostly women and girls. Even before conflict, women are disadvantaged when it comes to accessing their HLP rights. Land and property ownership remains largely restricted to men, by both tradition and law. Conflict affects women in particular ways and might make existing inequalities worse. That is why our HLP work has a specific focus on displaced women’s HLP rights.
