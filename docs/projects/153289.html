Project Summary

Juba and Terekeka Counties of South Sudan has consistently remained below sphere minimum standards in terms of WASH infrastructure and services due to the conflicts and economic crises. Having access to safe water and basic sanitation and adopting good hygiene practices has remained extremely below the recommended standards. Improving access to safe drinking water, appropriate sanitation and proper hygiene practices plays a critical role in preventing water related diseases often associated with conflicts.
This project will focus on improving quality of life among 15,900 female and male individuals who are directly affected by conflict and having very limited access to basic WASH services; which exposes them to the risk of outbreak of WASH related diseases in Juba and Terekeka Counties. The project will target beneficiaries at health facilities, schools, market places and households for improvement of WASH services and fixing gaps for mitigation of WASH related diseases. Water supply, sanitation and hygiene services will be provided for timely access at locations where community members remain vulnerable at sites of high risks identified during joint assessment with Payam authorities.
11,000 individuals will benefit from regained access to clean water supply through rehabilitation of communal water collection points. 3 communal latrines will be rehabilitated and handwashing facilities with shops will be provided for 13 primary schools and 4 health units. Also 4000 community members will have access to 8 newly constructed water collection points.
For effectiveness of the WASH services, provision of basic WASH facilities will be accompanied by capacity building through training of 20 health care workers as hygiene focal persons. 13 school hygiene clubs will be formed and pupils trained including 26 female and male teachers to support hygiene promotion sessions in schools. 30 community hygiene promoters will be trained and enormous participatory hygiene and sanitation promotion sessions, water quality monitoring, disinfection and chlorination will be conducted at times of needs. Training will be given for 208 female and male Water User Committees at the rehabilitated and newly constructed water points to support efficient use and maintenance.
During the project cycle, NSDO field team will coordinate with key partners on ground who are providing WASH, health, nutrition and protection services at other sites in same Counties Regular joint activity monitoring sessions will be conducted and will involve County Department of Water and Sanitation and Public Health and Environment and partners.
NSDO will also prioritize daily and weekly reporting and information sharing.
Protection mainstreaming will consider design improvements to accommodate the disabled, small boys and girls, and considering privacy, dignity and protection. Hand washing facilities will be fixed and soaps provided to promote the habit of hand washing at critical times among school pupils. Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits will be provided for girls of reproductive age accompanied with Menstrual Hygiene Management sessions so that girls will not miss classes during their periods.

Needs

During the five years of the most recent conflict, nearly 4.5 million people fled their homes in search of safety; and of which nearly 2 million of them internally. While the intensity of conflict may have reduced, and clashes contained to certain regions, vulnerable people will experience the impacts of the conflict through 2019. The conflict, associated with economic decline have eroded the Government’s ability to provide consistent basic services to its people. According to the 2019 HNO, the number of people in need represents about 64% increase from 2018. For example, currently one primary health center serves an average of 50,000 people which provides need for hygiene promotion and disease prevention at the health facilities. About 75% among the population of households’ members reported having being self-diagnosed with a water-borne illness. The number of people requiring emergency WASH services in 2019 increased by 7% from the previous year whereby Juba County in Central Equatoria has shifted to having high needs. 5,712,000 South Sudanese women, men and children are estimated not to have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene services in 2019. Only 29 per cent of the population has access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30-minute distance without facing protection concerns. The remaining two thirds of the population are thus required to take greater lengths to access water or are reliant on surface water or unprotected water sources. While the UNICEF-WHO 2017 Joint Monitoring Programme indicates over 61% of the population of South Sudan practice open defecation and few existing sanitation facilities are mostly in disrepair, more than 90 per cent of the population practices are estimated be practicing open defecation, either because they do not have access to or are not accustomed to using a basic sanitation facility. Women and girls face increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water and using communal latrines, and access to menstrual hygiene products, and appropriate and dignified washing locations remains their key need.With the risk of Ebola outbreak which is already present in the neighboring Democratic Republic of Congo, an outbreak in South Sudan could be devastating if community members are not reached with key hygiene messages for adoption of habits for prevention especially in Juba which is the capital City and center to regions like Terekeka and other nearby towns. NSDO has been implementing WASH in schools projects since 2016; during these interventions, NSDO conducted a comprehensive needs assessment to identify gaps in host communities and institutions including schools and health facilities. The assessment also involved the representatives from the Counties water and sanitation authorities, Focused Group Discussions were conducted with to review needs and existing gaps in WASH service provision. Existing Water Supply facilities in Terekeka County especially remain unattended to, many partners concentrate within the City council yet settlements outside the Juba City Council and surrounding Payams and Counties seem to be ignored. Most of the community members, schools and health facilities targeted do not have access to safe water supply or even practice safe hygiene and sanitation habits, some schools have existing facilities but the facilities are dilapidated and non-functional.

Activities

Activities:- Conducting hygiene promotion training for 20 female and male health worker staff members in four (4) primary health Care Units, and 26 primary school teachers who will be focal persons of hygiene at health facilities and schools.- Formation and training of school hygiene promotion clubs in thirteen (13) identified community primary schools.-Training of thirty (30) community hygiene promoters in target sites of Northern Bari Payam, Rejaf Payam of Juba and Terekeka Payam in County and Terekeka.-Conducting hygiene promotion sessions for prevention of cholera, Ebola and other WASH related diseases among approximately 15,900 people targeted at health facilities, primary schools, and market places and at house hold levels.-Rehabilitation of 18 boreholes (16 hand-pumps and 2 Mini Water Yards) for 9000 target beneficiaries in 4 health facilities, 8 schools and 8 communal water collection points of host communities.-Construction of 8 communal water collection points for 4000 boys, girls, women and men at 3 primary schools and 5 host community places; and training of Water User Management Committees.-Rehabilitation of 3 latrines for 540 male and females with design improvement for dignity, gender segregation, safety and room for MHM in 2 primary schools and 1 health facility; and establishment of handwashing stations in 13 primary schools and 4 health facilities.-Establishment of referral pathways in host communities, schools and health facilities; referral to cholera treatment/health centers by school and community based cholera response teams including community hygiene promoters attached to the schools and school health club members will carry out monitoring and referrals in case of suspected outbreak.-Focused Group Discussion with different beneficiary groups at each project site and joint monitoring of project activities with local authorities of beneficiary groups.

The messages are context specific and tailored for specific gender. Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

NSDO implements activities on a human rights-based approach, considering gender balance, gender equality and children protections rights. NSDO has a children protection policy where each child has a right for protection and basic needs, NSDO works towards achieving that in every activity implemented. Timely access to safe drinking water will improve life of boys and girls in schools. NSDO will work with protection colleagues/CLUSTER to provide support for victims of abuse and exploitation. NSDO also will work with GBV working group for further protection guidance and support.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

NSDO has an open-door policy where community members are welcomed to give feedback; and will create platform for community members to give their feed-backs anytime. A suitable information sharing environment will be created. Beneficiaries will be informed of the contact details/telephone numbers to call or focal persons to report complains to. NSDO will establish a complaints mechanism during the project period for beneficiaries to give feedback. Beneficiaries will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or raise issues during the provision of services, whereby the hygiene promoters and volunteers will gather information daily and report to the WASH Officer. Additionally, NSDO will gather complaints and feedback through the weekly FGD. The NSDO gender focal person will be dedicated train volunteers and will support to activities of feedback once in a week and work with protection colleagues and the Protection Cluster to provide further support that improves dignity of females. NSDO will also work together with the Education cluster to make sure there is adequate information sharing at cluster partner level. Impact assessment of deliverables, appropriateness, timeliness and quality of this project component will be ensured; and post-completion lessons learned meeting will be held to brainstorm lesions learned. Activity plans will be made together with the representatives of the line ministry in accordance to the submitted master plan. The weekly, monthly, quarterly and final reports will also be shared and matters arising discussed.
