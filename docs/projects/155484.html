Project Summary

Globally significant strides have been made in HIV diagnosis and prognosis but there remains a significant gap in addressing the challenges related to adherence in order to attain virological suppression for children, adolescents and young people living with HIV (CAYPLHIV). Nationally, apart form there being a gap in Anti-Retrorival Therapy (ART) coverage for this age group, challenges with adherence militate against efforts to attain the ambitious UNAIDS 95-95-95 HIV cascade targets by 2020.
the coming in of flash floods this 2018-19 agricultural season further presents a challenge and physical barrier for CAYPLHIV to access health facilities and remain in care. This response plan therefore aims to increase access to child friendly reporting and response on HIV sensitive protection cases for 18,910 children affected by the emergency humanitarian crisis in 7 targeted districts.
The response action will facilitate identification of needy CAYPLHIV (0-24 years) affected by HIV and provide linkages with social protection programmes in an integrated approach. The response action will also provide sensitization of humanitarian staff, Child Case Care Workers (CCWs), 210 Community Adolescents Treatment Supporters (CATS) and 210 Child Protection Committee (CPC) members on special considerations for children living with HIV.
Resultantly, there will be case conferencing in the targeted districts through CATS coordination meetings and ultimately strengthened tracking, follow up, counselling and support for CAYPLHIV.
Finally, the 18,910 CAYPLHIV will remain in care and therefore attain virologic suppression.

Needs
