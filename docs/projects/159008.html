Project Summary

This project focuses on conflict-affected vulnerable households engaged in farming or fishery in the Access Restricted Area (ARA). It is designed to protect the livelihoods of ARA-dependent households and build their resilience to cope with protracted protection threats and economic shocks. Vulnerable households affected by the IHL/IHRL violations and/or other types of shock such as economic or environmental and engaged in farming or fishery will receive conditional cash assistance (CCT) to help them restore their production capacity and sustain their livelihoods, while the most acutely vulnerable beneficiary households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities will be supported with unconditional cash-based assistance to cover their immediate basic needs. PUI focus also on the infrastructural challenges for resuming agricultural livelihoods in the ARA such as shortages of electricity and water in agriculture through provision of irrigation water using solar PV system on agricultural wells. The Action will enable vulnerable households engaged in farming or fishery, including vulnerable women, to re-launch or enhance their agricultural activities through different activities such as solar energy for irrigation and CCT to purchase agricultural inputs. The proposed intervention is part of a multi-year program. The planned action was closely coordinated with the Food Security Sector and the Ministry of Agriculture, as well as with other relevant local actors. The project will contribute to the following overarching country strategic objectives: SO2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL. SO3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. CO1: Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced CO2: Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs.
PUI coordinated its planned interventions with FSS and MoA. Coordination with key actors in the area of intervention took place with ICRC, Action against Hunger, ACAD, PARC and UAWC.

1 - Needs

The ongoing deterioration of humanitarian situation has markedly affected the households’ ability to generate a decent income to meet their basic needs, with the weak purchasing power and limited cash flow. People who are dependent for their livelihoods on the ARA at land and sea are exposed to another layer of vulnerabilities due to the enforcement of these areas. Vulnerabilities are acknowledged and reflected in the HNO and the HRP, and place them among the most vulnerable groups in Gaza. In September 2019, PUI/UAWC conducted FGDs with 24 farmers working in the ARA. The majority reported that the restrictions and blockade imposed on Gaza affected their ability to generate income. Due to the ongoing economic crisis as a result of civil servants’ salary cut by PA as well as the delay of MoSD subsidies, farmers are forced to sell their products at lower prices resulting in low profit. Some farmers reported incurring losses due to selling at prices lower than the production cost. 60% of surveyed farmers reported to have a reduced income, while 15% reported having no income at all. All farmers confirmed that they have accumulated debts that they cannot pay back, and that most agro-suppliers are not providing assets or input on credit anymore.Access to water for irrigation is considered among key challenges for farmers in the ARA. This can be attributed to the infrastructure challenge related to the shortages of electricity, which directly affect the irrigation water availability and supply due to the fact that the majority of the agricultural wells are operated by electricity. PUI carried out an impact survey in May 2019, with affected farmers by the electricity crisis in its area of intervention. Results showed that farmers were struggling to irrigate their crops due to the acute electricity crisis, which affected the irrigation water supply. On average, irrigation water supply was reduced by 70% in comparison with the previous planting seasons. The lack of irrigation water caused damages to the crops in terms of quality and quantity, where the magnitude of damages ranges between 50 and 90%. As a result, some farmers skipped planting the whole season, while others lost their all their crops. In addition, the farmers confirmed that the cost of irrigation water increased by 30 to 50% due to using alternative sources (e.g. fuel-operated generators). The above-mentioned effects led to a significant reduction in the generated income and/or contracting more debts with the suppliers of agricultural inputs and the owners of the water wells.PUI/UAWC conducted a FGD in May 2019 with 17 affected fishermen by protection incidents, who stated that their main priority is to be able to re-launch their livelihoods. They highlighted that if they are not able to catch enough to sell, the caught fish will still be an important source of protein that will contribute to the food security of their household. In June 2019, PUI/UAWC assessed the vulnerability of 66 affected fishermen from recent protection incidents and their needs. According to the assessment, the incidents negatively affected the household’s ability to generate income.

2 - Activities

PUI will target the following target groups: (1) vulnerable households engaged in farming or fishery in the ARA who are affected by IHL/IHRL violations and/or other types of shock such as economic or environmental, (2) acutely vulnerable households suffering from limited access to food and income opportunities in the ARA, (3) farmers who have problems in accessing affordable energy and water. All targeted households will be selected according to the shocks incurred, protection-related, and socio-economic vulnerability criteria. Selection criteria will include: households who are affected by the ongoing deterioration of the economic situation and/or protection incidents. PUI will partner with UAWC in this project (PUI has already collaborated with UAWC during 2018-19 on a FSS project). UAWC will have a direct role in co-implementing the different planned activities and will participate in the monitoring and evaluation process. Activity 1: Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for farmers, herders and breeders. a. Provision of conditional cash assistance (CCT) to 100 (10 F, 90 M) vulnerable households engaged in farming or fishery and affected by protection threats and/or other types of shock such as economic or environmental in the ARA. Cash assistance will be mainly used to buy inputs and repay agricultural debts. b. Set up of two solar panel systems on two agricultural wells which will serve 100 (10 F, 90 M) farmers in average (23 kw/well). Activity 2: Cash-based support. a. Provision of unconditional cash transfer (UCT) to 100 (30F, 70M) acutely vulnerable households affected by economic shocks to cover their immediate basic needs. Through PUI’s work in the ARA the differences in the effects on groups of age, gender and diversity have been identified. The male farmers and fishermen are expected to be the main providers and “protectors” of the households, while their exposure to re-current protection incidents combined with the cash crisis and its effect on the local market challenges their possibilities of succeeding in providing for the needs of their families. The women often contribute directly to the livelihood activities but with limited access to and/or control over productive assets. The high poverty rates among the group leads to potential harmful coping strategies, of which engaging children in many hours of labor affecting their learnings and in worst case leads to the withdrawal from school, as well as using early marriage as a strategy to reduce household expenses. Additionally, the exacerbated exhausted protection environment has been reported by the communities of the ARA to heightening the risks of domestic violence, divorces and heritage disputes within the families. Moreover, most of the households are at risk of food insecurity and malnutrition.The planned action will target women as well as men as income providers, to ensure equal access to productive resources, facilitate access to market while promoting joint decision-making in income use. Obstacles to female headed households’ economic participation will be tackled, thus improving their food security. Specific attention will be given to ensuring the fulfillment of the four protection mainstreaming principles in order to ensure the best “good programming” standards: (1) Do no harm principle; (2) Meaningful access; (3) Accountability to beneficiaries; (4) Participation and EmpowermentFinally, PUI is co-chairing the ARA core-group with the Protection cluster, which aims at joining efforts of all key stakeholders working in the ARA to better coordinate the response to the affected population.

3 - Indicators

PUI and UAWC’s M&E teams will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets. This plan will describe the whole M&E system, including indicators, tools, breakdown of responsibilities and communication channels within the organizations and between them. Regular monitoring visits will be conducted to follow up the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are being implemented in accordance with the work plan developed at the beginning of the activity. Focus group discussions and/or semi structured surveys will be carried out to assess the impact of project activities against baseline survey results. Below is more information about the amount and modalities of the CBT activities: Farmers and fishermen households will receive conditional cash assistance over two installments. The selected farmer households will receive livelihoods support of an average amount of 1000 USD, equivalent to the average cost of agricultural inputs to cultivate two dunums. While, the selected fishermen households will receive livelihoods support of an average of 800 USD, which represents the average amount of assistance to secure a level of recovery necessary to re-launch the livelihoods. The amount represents the average amount of four scenarios as follow: (Scenario 1) Maintenance Cost for boats; (Scenario 2) Maintenance Cost for boats engine; (Scenario 3) Fishing Tools/Equipment cost and (Scenario 4) Fishing Nets cost. The most acutely vulnerable beneficiary households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities will be supported with unconditional cash-based assistance to cover their immediate basic needs and provide for their families, with an average amount per household of 834 USD for three months. The amount of assistance was calculated based on the intention to cover 50% of the amount of the deep poverty line threshold for a family of 6 members in Gaza. The deep poverty line (determined by PCBS, 2017) served as a basis for calculating the standard transfer value per person and per month. This applied calculation methodology is in line with the calculation methodology followed by the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) to estimate the amount of cash transfers under the social safety net program. The cash based assistance transfer value was calculated to partially cover (50%) the gap between the deep poverty line for a family of 6 members and the average household income (mainly food assistance from UNRWA/WFP and cash subsidies from MoSD). The amount of cash to be received per month will be tailored to the number of the households’ members, while the duration of cash assistance will be for three months.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

PUI has a long established presence in the ARA, a deep knowledge of its context and close collaboration with its population, local CBOs, community leaders and municipalities. PUI’s accountability to the ARA population has been well-recognized for years, including during the last war of 2014.The Action’s primary concern is to respond to the affected population’s needs, expressed and identified during the needs assessment phase, and through the FGDs. Hence, community members and potential targeted beneficiaries are involved in the design phase of the action and also in identifying vulnerabilities and related needs. As soon as the action starts, to ensure transparency PUI will meet with all selected beneficiaries and community representative to inform them about the final accepted project and explain all different steps where beneficiaries will be asked to actively participate and contribute to the success of the Action. This informative meeting will also allow targeted beneficiaries to inform PUI/UAWC about their situation, needs, aspiration and involve them through a participatory approach. During those meetings, PUI / UAWC staff members will ensure to handout information cards on the feedback and complaint mechanism (FCM) and explain the mechanism. They will pay attention to highlighting that feedback, whether positive or negative, will all be taken into consideration and addressed by PUI representatives independent from project team and be dealt with in respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. Beneficiaries will be invited to use boxes and templates located in the premises of UAWC for that purpose as well. The FCM will directly contribute to the improvement of the project implementation. It will assist also in understanding beneficiaries’ perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in the early detection of problems such as in regard to targeting.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PUI/UAWC is fully committed to the do no harm principle, and promoting safety and dignity. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks associated with the action. This will be done through assessments, continuous evaluation and consultations with the targeted groups as well as through the FCM. The targeted communities and beneficiaries will be actively involved in all phases of the Action and all feedback will be taken into account in best possible manners. The inclusive approach and the close cooperation with communities, CBOs and DPOs and beneficiaries is designed to ensure privacy, safety, dignity and accessibility for men and women, with or without disability and to contribute to the enhancement of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and contribute to the Action at all stages of the implementation. Lastly all involved staff is trained in PSEA policies and the action continuously engage in GBV mainstreaming by as an example having staff trained in identifying and referring GBV survivors as non-specialized actors.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, the Action aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle. The Action’s participatory assessment is specifically designed to involve vulnerable groups traditionally excluded from decision making. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI/UAWC will analyze possible barriers and enablers in cooperation with the community, CBOs, PDOs and beneficiaries with a specific focus on age, gender and diversity. Furthermore the Action will pay particular attention to identifying beneficiaries most at need by attributing special attention to inclusiveness of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs. The planned action will target women as well as men with and without disabilities as income providers, to ensure equal access to productive resources, facilitate access to market while promoting joint decision-making in income use. Obstacles to as an example female headed households’ economic participation will be tackled, through engaging actively with the involved beneficiaries on challenges and possible solutions. PUI is working to ensure that programmes and services are available and accessible to persons with and without disabilities. Therefore, PUI work to on a non-discriminatory approach, where a person with a disability is able to exercise their protected rights on an equal basis with other members of the community. PUI as humanitarian organization is also committed to protect Rights of Persons with Disabilities and inclusion principles and practices of the most vulnerable. PUI ensures that persons with disabilities and their families are involved and consulted at all stages of the project cycle, for instance, during the needs assessment phase, PUI includes persons who identifies with one or several of the Washington Group Short Set of Questions on disabilities to gather information on barriers, needs and priorities. As a result, PUI will identify and work to remove any barriers that may prevent persons with disabilities from effectively and safely using services and participating in projects as well as build on identified enablers. During the planning phase, PUI ensured that the disability-related findings and recommendations from the assessment stage have been carried through into the project design. PUI then, make adaptations when necessary to ensure accessibility and inclusiveness of project services. Further, during the implementation phase, service delivery mechanisms can be adapted to improve access for persons with disabilities.
