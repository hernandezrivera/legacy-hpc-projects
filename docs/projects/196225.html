Project Summary

Overall objective: Improved transferable skills and self-reliance of persons with disabilities and older people of Rohingya refugees in preparation for voluntary repatriation and sustainable reintegration in Myanmar aligning with the UN/GoB framework on skills development for Rohingya Refugees and Host Community.
The proposed project will target people over the age of 18 among the Rohingya and Host community (60% women and 40% men). They represent an excluded age group that are often forgotten during livelihood, skills development and basic literacy activities. Targeting older refugees and person with disabilities in livelihood activities is important given these additional roles as it allows them to meet their basic & specific needs, live dignified lives, as well as a means for supporting families and communities.
We are expecting the achievement of the outcome in the following way.
Pre-vocational Life skills training: Considering the low-level literacy and self-esteem of persons with disabilities and older people among the Rohingyas and host community, HelpAge will provide pre-vocational training on basic literacy, numeracy and life-skills training for basic knowledge of gender-related issues, DRR, health and hygiene, service linkage, physical exercise, home-based care, stress management, and nutrition etc to 2500 Participants. Each selected beneficiaries will receive life skills training of 72 hours. For persons with hearing impairment, sign language with be used for basic literacy. For host community OP, non-formal adult literacy curriculum will be followed. Life skills training is very important for the Older People and person with disabilities to increase their self-esteem, to empower them inculcating their rights, building their capability in decision making and accessing to the market and to keep them active i.e. giving confidence for self-reliance.
Vocational/technical skills Training: After completing pre-vocational training, beneficiaries will be eligible to receive vocational training on need-based technical subject like Handicraft, bamboo craft, Gas burner repairing, plastic recycling, solar lamp production, Jute products, sewing, handicraft, mat making, fishing net making, liquid soap and handwashing etc. 2500 participants will be targeted for livelihood and skills developme among the plder people and person with disabilities. 60 percent total target beneficiary will be selected from older people aged 50 and above and remaining will be selected from aged over 18 group as person with disability criteria. All the Rohingya beneficiary will be provided some inputs and instrument so that they can practice and produce something using their skills gained from the training. For host community members both on-farm and off-farm topics will be selected based on individual choice, availability of raw materials and market viability. Host community beneficiaries will be provided with input and capital support to start-up income generating activities.
Wage earning and Self-Employment: The project will create access to opportunities for refugees who successfully complete the pre-vocational and vocational training. HelpAge will create linkages with the humanitarian service providers at camp level to engage 500 graduate beneficiaries in volunteer works in the various sector like health, WASH, site management and site development, DRR etc. The project will also support the 1500 graduate participants to establish self-employment through in kind.
Social cohesion issues will be considered during the implementation of this proposal. Participants will receive the information about the skills development activities, income generation, entitlements as well as the criteria to enroll themselves in the programme that will reduce the risk of creating conflict among them. Awareness sesions and day observation event will be organized with them that will create the environment of sharing and working together relationship.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Capability and needs of Person with Disability and Older People are often undermined by the humanitarian actors. As a result, they struggle to meet their essential needs (including protection, food, water, shelter, personal safety, health and information) in a sustainable manner and with dignity. They do not get access to Economic Self Reliance- input/materials and monetary assets. They remain unable to afford the critical needs, engage in social activities and address their issues by themselves. people with disabilities, and Older people reflected that, all aspects of self-reliance are very poor and increasingly viewed as driven by two factors: lack of commitment by agencies, undermining their contribution and capacity in the community. 936,733 Rohingya refugees currently reside in 33 extremely congested camps formally established by the Government of Bangladesh in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’s Bazar District (UNHCR population Factsheet, July-2022). According to the REACH report, 12% of the total population has either a single or multiple disabilities, this number rises to 46% among the older people. The geo-physical structure of the camps is hilly with difficult terrain, which is a particular challenge for older people and people with disability and inhibits their ability to access humanitarian services. According to the J-MSNA-2021, 76 percent HH without access to self-reliance activities were found to have unmet food security and livelihood needs. Person with disabilities and older people participation in the self-reliance interventions are very low which left them more vulnerable situation. 17 percent HH are engaged in self-reliance activities in the camps among them 6 percent individual aged 60+ having been involved in the self-reliance activities. According to the REVA-5, A higher proportion of Rohingya female-led households reported having at least one family member living with a disability (3.2 percent). For male heads, the proportion reporting at least one family member living with a disability was 7.3 percent for the refugee. However, with limited access to self-reliance activities, the refugee population in camps remains highly reliant on humanitarian assistance. There is lack of intervention to provide appropriate life skills to enhance knowledge and information that would enable them to take adaptive and positive behaviours. Such support would strengthen existing coping mechanisms and build resilience to address challenges in their surrounding context to perform daily activities and participate in the decision-making process. It would enhance their ability to adapt to the circumstances and increase their self-esteem and confidence by being able to communicate with the community. It would enhance their ability to take decision and claims their rights and entitlement, ability to solve problems at all levels of personal, family and community level and overcome the challenges. In the proposed project area (10 camps and 5 locations of host community), we have established Age Friendly Spaces (Multi-purpose service center) with well-furnished training rooms, age and disable friendly WASH facilities, storeroom, breastfeeding corner, child corner, nutrition room etc. Thus, can implement project with minimum expenditure for facilities development.HelpAge will implement this project to provide livelihood and skills support to 2500 Beneficiaries among 2000 will be targeted from Rohingya and 500 from Host community. Beneficiaries will receive soft skill, technical skills and wage employment opportunities throughout this project that will increase their self-reliance. During this project implementation, HelpAge will follow the UN/GoB framework on skills development for Rohingya Refugees and Host Community.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Since 1991, HelpAge International is implementing humanitarian programmes in Bangladesh. Immediately after the influx of Rohingya refugees, from 2017 it has been implementing humanitarian programme including self-reliance programmes in 10 camps and adjacent host community to provide support to older people and persons with disability. HelpAge International is very familiar with the implementation process in the camp and has strong representation and acceptance to the ISCG, RRRC and different sectoral working groups. Since 2021, HelpAge has been implementing self-reliance projects with an integrated approach through funding from WFP. There are numerous successful strategies approaches already adapted in the project e.g. introduce sign language in the training curriculum, doing accessibility audit for the facilities and screening and providing necessary assistive device to the PwD. Also, lessons learned have been incorporated in the design of this project. HelpAge and its partners have designed and are already implementing a highly agile and adaptable model which addresses the significant challenges of older men, women and People with Disabilities have in accessing in livelihood options through gaining skills and input support. All successful strategies and approaches will be replicated in the proposed project. HelpAge is planning to provide integrated support to the project participants such as rehabilitation assistance, physiotherapy for functional capacity, skills development and livelihood opportunities, soft skills for decision making as well as aware them on their rights, PSEA, DRR etc. Through this project, HelpAge will reach 2500 Beneficiaries among 60 percent will be women and all participants will be selected from Aged 18 and above with or without disabilities. HelpAge will coordinate with the sector and will submit the activity update regularly through 4W/5W report and will play collaborative role with the government and other humanitarian stakeholders.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will be implemented in the 10 camps and 2 Unions of Ukhiya and Teknaf with an integrated approach of livelihood and skills development maintaining do no harm and social cohesion.HelpAge always engage programme participants in the project management cycle from design to evaluation as part of appropriate and accountable programme. HelpAge will enhance support to other humanitarian actors on accessibility as well as mainstreaming disability and older people in the humanitarian actions as a core member of Age and disability working group. HelpAge will take initiatives through this project to share information and awareness raising on humanitarian services provided to OP via AFS and CBP structure, Age Friendly kit distribution (Walking stick, prayer mat, urine pot, winter clothes), support to accessing humanitarian services without discrimination and exploitation and closely work with other GBV and protection focal organization.

3.2 - Gender

Gender equality is a programmatic priority, and gender-sensitive and appropriate interventions have been designed to ensure gender mainstreaming following HelpAge’s gender policy. 60% of beneficiaries will be older women and their inter-section of persons with disability. HelpAge has designed this project to provide livelihood and skills training to 1500 Women and 1000 Men. Women will received life skills and transferable skills training according the needs identified at camp level and skills that can be use in the Myanmar when they repartriated. Gender, age and disability issues are duly considered in all steps of HelpAge Project’s design, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. HelpAge and its partners strictly follow a referral pathway set for each camp that includes creating a trusted platform where GBV survivors feel safe to disclose the case, maintain a code of conduct at working area, and always practice the survivor-centered approach.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

HelpAge will create the safe environments for the children those who come along with the participants. It has dedicated staffs, volunteers to accompany the children with toys and other facilities. It will also organize orientation to staffs, volunteers and other relevant service provider such as vendors, network members on safeguarding and PSEA to ensure safe environments for all. It will not tolerate staff or representatives carrying out any form of harm and abuse, including sexual harassment, abuse, and exploitation and will take appropriate action. It protects the rights of adults to live in safety, free from abuse and neglect and children from abuse and maltreatment. This means that the safety and wellbeing of the survivor are paramount, and although we encourage survivors to report safeguarding concerns, no-one who has directly suffered harm and abuse is obliged to make a direct report.

3.4 - Diversity

HelpAge respects the diversity of its staff. Valuing diversity means that we recognise that we all have complex identities made up of many strands and that these can include but are not limited to gender, race, disability, age, nationality, ethnic/national origin, sexual orientation, religious beliefs, marital status, pregnancy, social status and political beliefs. The project will target 2500 participants from Rohingya and Host community where 60 percent will be older women and their inter-section of persons with disability. HelpAge is committed to provide humanitarian services to the diversified population. This project will ensure the participation of both women and men among the older people and person with disabilities. HelpAge will ensure the access of person with disabilities from aged 18 and above in this project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To strengthen the accountability process HelpAge highlight the Complaints and Feedback response mechanism process as this creates the opportunity for communities (both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) to provide their feedback and complaints to HelpAge, and in exchange to receive feedback or response from HelpAge, is an important part of being accountable. HelpAge believes that no one should left behind from participating in the livelihood and skills intervention due to their impairments and barriers. Participants will receive awareness session of CFRM, PSEA as well as service mapping of respective targeted camps. Participants will engage in the project management cycle as their needs and activities have been idenfied based on their feedback. HelpAge always ensure beneficiaries dignity and respect in its all activities and share all the project entitlements to the participants so that they can participate in the implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, The project has activities on soft skills training where all participants will receive awareness sessions on disaster risk reduction considering the camp and host context. HelpAge has life skills module and each project participants received sessions on cyclone preparedness, early warning, fire rescue & response, landslide, lightening etc. HelpAge will also provide vocational training on on bamboo products that can be used against plastic products at camp level which will act as a climate change mitigation measures.
