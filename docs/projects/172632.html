Project Summary

This project will focus on improving access to safe water, improved sanitation service and hygiene promotion among refugees and communities. This is aimed to reduce the incidence of waterborne diseases. The provision of improved safe water and sanitation facilities both at refugee camp and community will be accompanied by the transfer of knowledge on how to use them, together with sustainable behavior change given through the training of Environmental Health Technician, Community Health Volunteers and Water Point Committees on the Community Based Management concept.

1 - Activities

• Provision of clean potable water to 15,000 refugees and 5,000 non-refugee population for drinking and other domestic uses through upgrading and extension of 4 solar piped water schemes and rehabilitation of 10 non-functional boreholes in the camp areas and host wards. Water from the boreholes will be subjected to chlorination through an inline chlorinator system which have been designed by Oxfam in Zimbabwe as well as through a filtration system. • Support water quality testing in the host ward. Routine analysis of agreed relevant parameters shall be done with the MoHCC focusing on WASH related communicable disease surveillance. The project shall support MoHCC with water testing consumables and portable water treatment kit (Wagtech). • In collobation with its local partners in the district, Oxfam will support the formation and mentoring of Water Point Management Committees and refresher training of hand pump mechanics. Capacity building on this regard shall also include conflict management at water points, water governance and water right and this shall be done with support from other key partners.  Distribution of mobile refugee’s specific handwashing stations to household, schools and health institutions in the camp and host wards. Oxfam have designed a COVID-19 compliance portable handwashing station specifically for refugee camps.  Promote Community Led Total Sanitation with hygiene and social marketing through incentives, limited materials or market support linking with livelihood/food security support for Host Population. Support refugees and institutions with WASH NFI’S mainly comprising of bucket with tap, soap, plastic jerry can, IEC materials, water treatment chemicals. The kit will also comprise of latrine cleaning materials and detergent. Training of Community Health Volunteers on Participatory Health and Hygiene Education (PHHE) and facilitate the establishment of health clubs in the camp and host wards. The volunteers shall be chosen from the refugees and community members. The training shall be done in vernacular language of the refugees such as Swahili which was observed during the rapid assessment with the assistance of an interpreter. The pictorial approach of the Participatory Health and Hygiene Education shall be used. Volunteers shall be supported with tools kits for facilitation of weekly sessions. Health club members shall also be trained on various income generating activities such as detergent production. Conduct hygiene promotion targeting 20,000 people (15,000 refugees and 5,000 community members) through door to door awareness targeting all refugees as well host ward community members. Focus shall be on key issues such as COVID-19 safe water chain, hand washing days, proper use of latrines and early health seeking behavior. Theater performance shall also be used for mass awareness Production and distribution of culturally and context specific Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials in line with identified key hygiene messages. Construction of COVID-19 compliance laundry stations. Security through a fence shall be erected around the station which shall have a drying line as well as shade.  Awareness raising to churches, marketplaces and tuck-shop in the camp Construction of a small waste recycling centre)  Installation of 5 skip bins at designated areas in the residential and market areas and supporting refugees with waste receptacles

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Throughout the project AAP will be mainstreamed into the activities in a manner that ensures the affected people are at the center of our work, and this will be achieved by ensuring the following; 1. Communities participate in the project in a safe and appropriate manner that ensures equal opportunities for all including the vulnerable. 2. Communication and information with the affected populations will be shared in a two-way communication channel that ensures the targeted communities are aware of their rights in the project and the obligations of the Partner while working in their community. Community dialogues and inception meetings from District level down to camp and ward level will be some of the tools to be utilised. 3. Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be established and utilised throughout the project, to get feedback on experiences and perspectives of the project implementation. Mechanisms such as help desks, suggestion boxes and suggestion boxes feedback sessions will be implemented at a frequency to be determined by communities. All feedback received will be recorded and adequately addressed in a manner that also safeguards the communities. 4. Oxfam will also work with other partners at the camp and ward to ensure that communities are not overburdened, and efforts are also not duplicated.5. Oxfam has an active toll-free line that has been active since Cyclone Idai emergency response inception. Inception, mid-term and end of project shall also be used to engage the community and stakeholders. Oxfam will continue to participate in the monthly District Water and Sanitation Sub-Committee meeting
