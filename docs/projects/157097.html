Project Summary

The project interventions will focus on supporting 26,440 people who are affected by the unpredictable conflict in Golo, Rekoro, Nertiti, Umdukhun locality’s in (central Darfur) and Habila, keirinik in (west Darfur) area through provision of ESNFIs intervention. The project will support 16440 beneficiaries (3288 households) of Returns and protracted IDPs in Central (60%) and west Darfur state (40%). and 10,000 (2000 HHs) of newly displaced in desperate need of ESNFIs will be supported through the common pipe line. The project will ensure that the target communities will have adequate ESNFIs items to ensure their most basic human needs as it relates to the maintenance of health, privacy and dignity and its easily within their reach. SORC will undertake periodic joint inter-agency assessments with special consideration to female headed households to inform the project on the new and emerging situations in the targeted areas. Post-distribution surveys will be an integral part of the project undertaken with ESNFI project or other partners of the sector in the targeted locations to evaluate the effectiveness of the distributions. SORC is a partner in the ESNFI sector and has previously been working in the area distributing NFIs and constructing ES and transitional shelters. SORC plans to provide NFI support to some 2000 HHs (50% women, girls and 50% mans, boys) of newly displaced and disaster affected households in both west and Central Darfur and further 3288 households of returns and protracted IDPs will be supported with both NFI items 2000 HHs (50% women, girls and 50% mans, boys), transitional shelters for 300HHs (50% women, girls and 50% mans, boys) in areas of return and 988 HHs (50% women, girls and 50% mans, boys) of protracted IDPs (PWSNs) will be provided with NFI items in all targeted locations. 50% female headed households will be involved in all interventions. SORC will conduct needs assessments and verification of beneficiaries of ES/NFI to ensure priority assistance is given to people with specific needs (PSNs), EVIS and the most vulnerable in line with agreed sector guidelines. Assessments and distributions will be done in collaboration with stakeholders, community, women’s associations, on the ground. fire and flood awareness and environmental protection will be undertaken to ensure capacity is built with special consideration to role of gender, skills built and environment is protected. SORC will coordinate with stake holders like UNHCR, NCA, UNOCHA and partners on the ground.

Needs

According to HNO 2019, New population movements (either IDPs or returnees, or both) are to be expected and a response in terms of ESNFI and Shelter is important. Prepositioning buffer stocks is crucial in remote areas inaccessible by road during rainy season and prone to population movements, natural disasters or close to conflict areas. It is almost impossible to describe more precisely the specific needs of beneficiaries in terms of ESINFI since such needs will depend on population movements. However, according to SORC SWOT assessment and field surveys on 20-27/10/2018 in the targeted locations ESNFI needs remains uncovered in Golo, Nertiti, Rokoro, umdukhun Locality (Central Darfur state) and Habila, kerenik locality (west Darfur state) area in September 2018. They rebuilt their homes with local materials found in the area like chopping down trees which affects the environment. These houses do not protect the returnees in the autumn and rainy seasons. Regarding to their living conditions, as they have lost everything, they also need basic NFI items. Moreover, Seasonal returnees come back to their origin villages for the agricultural period (June to November). After the agricultural season and the harvest, they go back to the IDP camp, and do so every year. They mainly need plastic sheeting to protect them from the rains when they stay in their home village during the cultivation period. Those people left their village between 2017 - 2018. The villages had been completely devastated and houses looted during their absence, meaning an intervention in terms of ES/NFIs is highly needed. In the assessed areas, most of them are living either outside or in a relative’s house. priority FOR ESNFI assistance will be given to Newly displaced, returnees, disaster affected and protracted IDPs wit 50% female haded household, Previous SORC experiences have highlighted that there is need for full basket of ESNFI items. Simultaneous vulnerable returnee households are also ineed of transitional shelters in the targeted locations. SORC is active in education and livelihood sectors in WD and CD, as well as implementing RRR project in Um Dukhun since 2017, 2 Years Project and DCPSF with undp. All projects are complementary and participate in SORC's approach, by ensuring a timely emergency assistance, early recovery mechanisms and capacity building for a longer-term impact. SORC coordinates its program with TGH, UNHCR, DRC and partners on the ground to avoid duplication and pays particular attention to accountability towards affected population. Complaints mechanisms are already in place, based on SORC accountability strategy, and will be strengthened throughout this project. SORC conducts Focus Group Discussions, as well as meetings with relevant stakeholders to collect needs, beneficiaries’ recommendations and feedback.
