Project Summary

WATAN Shelter/NFI Programme 2019, seeks to contribute in CCCM Sector by developing and reviewing integration methodologies to be applied in a clear and simple operational strategy respecting HLP and HAP standards, decreasing families inability to secure shelters, also providing daily living assistance for humanitarian purposes.
The project aims to create suitable environmental conditions for camp residents; evacuate informal settlements by providing land paving and rehabilitation services to the vulnerable existing camps and improve its capacity to receive more inhabitants.
Also, conducting a new methodology "identifying the altitude and inclination of each work site reaching a fine mitigation mechanism to protect tent sites from sudden changes of weather/geographical effects".
WATAN Had started a pilot study to indicate the topographic elevation of the sites and the drawing of the settlement lines to achieve greater safety for the movement of rainwater and natural tendencies of the contour lines.
One additional plan is to secure Resilience Shelter - 200 Prefabricated Units to receive vulnerable families IDPs who live in informal settlements or camps and ranked as highly sever/in-need households (pilot project - Khayr Village).
Reaching the most severe communities the main outputs of this plan are:
- Providing Resilience Shelter - 200 shelter units (Prefabricated Units) for 200 Households "1200 individuals"
Azaz Camp Neighbourhood - Urban ِExpansion Works to support Informal Settlements Evacuation.
Camp Exit Strategy Pilot Project.
- Camp Extention and Rehabilitation works for 6,000 Inhabitants/1000 Households/Tents
Evacuation of Informal Settlements.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
- Gravelling and insulation works for 4000 tents/households "24000 individuals".
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)

Needs

In North-West of Syria Aleppo and Idleb Governorates had been marked by several conflicts throughout 2018, also weather bad conditions in the informal settlements, unplanned camps had its effect on the vulnerable community in both Aleppo and Idleb.As the summarized result of 2019 initial HNO statistics, there has been worsening of shelter situation, so WATAN SNFI 2019 submitting this study aligning with CCCM Cluster's objectives with its operational in scope and seeking more durable solutions.Continuing with 2019 HNO, the following statistics will be the main targeted group in this study: - Aleppo 883,834 PiN - (Severity: 4 - Severe) - Idleb 23,813 PiN - (Severity: 5 - Critical) Other Statistics form 30 December 2018 with the affected tents/camps by rain floods, Reports listed more than 7,687 affected tents/12,064 affected families. The project represents a collaboration with the affected population and local authorities.WATAN had conducted extensive field surveys as a baseline for the current response, carrying out investigations in different targeted areas. https://watansyria.sharepoint.com/:b:/s/Shelter/ETa2qJU7IshHpJrPJOl_VrAB-fgojbMQ6EHm6TQcOgkewA?e=duC07fThe needs survey revealed the following results: 1. There is a lack of adequate income sources to finance the basic humanitarian needs for 90% of the newly displaced people in targeted provinces.2. Dealing with the extreme cold is the greatest concern among 80% of the families interviewed.3. 90% of the families suffered from cold-related diseases during last winter and they still live in the same place.4. All respondents confirmed that they do not have any solutions to cope with the cold which will severely affect them as they continue to live in tents, shelters or residential buildings that neither are equipped nor insulated.Moreover, given the high population density "especially in Afrin and Rural Aleppo", access to shelter, basic services and livelihood opportunities is severely constrained, with particularly worsening overcrowding especially in camps and informal settlements.Many IDPs are currently living in open spaces or unsuitable buildings that have been transformed into a shelter, including tents. These locations do not take into consideration the needs of women, men, girls and boys and expose people to degradant, unsafe and unhealthy conditions. Also, host communities require support especially those who are hosting IDPs.Random camps suffering from poor environmental conditions including muddy grounds, tents placed in agricultural land, lack of water and sanitation, and lack of regular water networks, affecting any possible solutions to enhance its conditions, also lack-of financial resources to pay for maintenance and construction work in both informal settlements and city neighbourhoods Through a clear scoring system, priority will begiven to the following groups of beneficiaries: - IDPs families:> Families with 4 or more children; > Families with pregnant and lactating women; > Female-headed families; > Elderly-headed families; > Child-headed families; > Families with persons with disabilities;> Child caring for another child unattended by their parents;

Implementation of activities

1- Community Selection:• As mentioned, the targetted areas will be communities with 4/5 severity scale.• Conduct needs assessment and identifies in-need locations in different IDP camps in both Aleppo and Idlib governorates.• Conduct pre and post assessments using mobile data collection applications. • Coordinate response with different CCCM cluster members. 2- Community Engagement:Procedures and policies to involve targeted communities in action were built to build partnerships with IDPs women and men of all ages and backgrounds by promoting meaningful participation through structured dialogue. Separate discussions with women, girls, boys, and men, including adolescents, enable to gather accurate information on the specific protection risks that different groups face, identify underlying causes, understand refugees' capacities, and hear their proposed solutions. 3- Beneficiary and Criteria Selection:Eligibility and vulnerability indicators will be used in a specific scoring system. Eligibility Group:IDPs - Households who lost their shelter and do not have suitable living conditions. Beneficiaries from the Eligible group will be selected based on the ranking of the vulnerability index:1- living in the IDP temporary settlement site.2- Household no source of income or no other means of financial support.3- Female and/or a single person head the household.4- Household headed by an elderly, minor or person with a disability.5- Household host orphaned, unaccompanied children, or homeless relatives due to the conflict.6- Household member/s with a physical disability, mental disability/illness, special needs or limiting conditions. 4- Agreements with Beneficiary/Owners:Agreement of the beneficiary to the following:-- Guarantee not to remove or otherwise take possession of any of the items providedthrough the Repair and Rehabilitation works.-- Confirm that any repair work does not confer any ownership rights.-- Take the responsibility for the safety of all non-construction affiliated individualsin the household, especially women and children and when necessary keep them from the site during repair works.-- Facilitate access to the site for the staff to monitor progress and quality of the repair works.-- Complete the works at the quality specified in the BoQ and within the timeframeWATAN Applying HLP principles; investigating the ownership of any targeted land and discuss with owners and available site management through MoUs, Panners, and for sure it will be in accordance with CCCM Cluster coordination before the registration period. 5- Quality Assurance and implementation follow-up:Quality Assurance (QA) is required to ensure that work is undertaken according to the Programme Standards while achieving the levels of equitable support, safety, durability and efficiency required by the Programme, and it will be on two baselines:QUALITY ASSURANCE THROUGHOUT THE PROGRAMMEQUALITY ASSURANCE AND SUPPORT DURING IMPLEMENTATION 6- Completion of Works and Completion of receiving certificates:Once work has finished, an independent review of the work should be completed by a member of the staff not previously involved in the project or a Third Party Monitoring, or the MEAL Team which is not involved in the implementation activities.A technical advisor will be consulted before closing any activity. 7- Monitoring and Evaluation:- The M&E system will include the relevant quantitative and qualitative indicators to track project progress, results and outcomes of the planned overall objective. Data will be disaggregated by sex and age. The M&E unit will support also the capitalization and lessons learning exercise and formulate recommendations for the dissemination and replication of good practices in resilience programming CCCM interventions.- The monitoring team will inform CCCM and Third Party Monitoring (TPM) team in advance of the any implemented activity to ensure accountability to affected populations.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat 1 B. The tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees Mitigating Measure 1 1. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 2. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. 3. Make effort to target a % of the vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs 4. Establish a feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be 5. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process Protection Risk/Threat 2 F. Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain population. Mitigating Measure 2 1. People are informed about their rights to receive assistance regardless of the documents they have. 2. Presence and information about complaints mechanism. Protection Risk/Threat 3 J. Closure of the border for commercial shipments or not the renewal of SC would have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities for both NGOs and UN agencies as it would prevent the transhipment possible aids across the border from Turkey into Syria. Mitigating Measure 3 1. Explore alternative supply lines through local procurement Protection Risk/Threat 4 K. Depletion of the market and inflation of the prices. As a consequence of the closure of the border or other internal security situations, agencies might start to resort to local procurement. If not well conducted, this could have a negative effect on market prices and items availability. Mitigating Measure 4 1. Conduct a strong and constant market monitoring before starting any local procurement 2. Keep the prices of the market monitoring with short and periodic frequency 3. Liaise with Cash WG before shifting to local procurement

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Key challenges and expected solutions to work in Aleppo an Idleb will be as the following: Assumptions: 1- BAH and BAS borders will continue to remain open. 2- HLP will be taken into consideration before delivering field works or any assistance. 3- Flexible in-site implementation/reallocation, when certain areas are inaccessible. 4- Good collaboration with the IDPs and Host communities. 5- Access will be conducive to deliver assistance. Risks: 1- Borders closure can affect money transferring or any cross-border and local activities. 2- Application of humanitarian accountability principles HAP where the risk of aid diversion caused by armed groups exists although thus far not reported officially. (HAP Reference: https://watansyria.sharepoint.com/:b:/s/Shelter/EcbaMVcSanFLofNMdGGXnnYB-CFnFQemUdoar7gQ8JW1xw?e=7d3KTs) 3- Duplication in distribution activities and distribution locations with other organizations. 4- Some sites are on land privately owned, and private owners may refuse physical works. (Housing, Land and Property issues). 5- Staff mobility and access restrictions to the affected population, with active fighting, shifting of the conflict lines might prevent delivery of materials and workers. Risk Mitigation: 1- Coordinate with CCCM Cluster, local authorities, and funding partners to avoid any delay or lack of approvals. 2- WATAN will report back to CCCM Cluster for any incident.3- WATAN will report back to CCCM Cluster for any possible aid diversion incidents. 4- WATAN will utilize HLP methodologies to investigate ownership of land and discuss with owners and available site management. 5.1- Deconfliction referencing with UN OCHA. 5.2- Coordinate with CCCM Cluster to highlight vulnerable sites, also to identify reallocation sites in case of a security situation.
