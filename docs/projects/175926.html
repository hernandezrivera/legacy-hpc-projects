Project Summary


Justice for Children Organization started operating in Leer and Mayom counties in 2017, implementing Education in Emergencies, the organization has been very successful and achieved a lot within the short spell in term of training of teachers and PTAs/SMCs, supporting teachers’ incentives, construction of TLSs and segregated latrines, peacebuilding, protection and GBV training of teachers on teaching methods, classroom management, ethic the major activities under the previous project with UNICEF,
What was imminent is that, throughout the county, there was a lack of school feeding programs in schools, which according to J4CO, is imperative for promoting education as well meeting the dietary requirement of these children.
Therefore, under this intervention, it plans to incorporate school feeding targeting 2445 (1000 Girls) in 20 (7) in Leer and Mayom counties especially those that will meet the selection criteria according to WFP standard.
J4CO will conduct BTL together with the community and civil mobilization in the two counties in order to attack more children, pupils, adults re-joining schools. Through this mass mobilization, the local authorities, the chiefs, County education Departments, youth and women organizations will take the lead, making the project community owned.
The project will support the payment of incentives to 60(20 F) teachers in all the 20 schools for a period of 12 months which will lead to an improvement in the quality of the services they provide because of motivational factors and due to their capacities development program.
The WASH components (sanitation and hygiene promotion) and CP basic services (Psychosocial Support and Life Skills), will be provided through capacity building training and support supervision of volunteer teachers to improve quality learning outcome.
J4CO will also work closely with Nutrition Partners in Leer and Mayom Counties to integrate the Early Childhood Development (ECD) program into Nutrition Centers to support moderate and severe malnourished children in selected centers.
The project will support the establishment of inclusive Temporary Learning Spaces5 (TLS), 2 in Mayom -yet to be agreed with the community, county authorities and strategic places as can be decided by the county authority, partners on the ground for the benefit of all including establishment of 6 kitchens for schools that met the criteria for selection of school feeding program
J4CO will conduct capacity-building training for the teachers in the area of teaching methodologies, teacher’s code of conducts, peacebuilding among others. This will help improve teachers’ skills in delivering knowledge and skills to the young ones.40 (10F) School Management Committees (SMCs) and 40(15F) Parents- Teachers Association (PTAs), will also have trainings in management and administrative skills to help the Head Teachers in handling disputes arising in the schools.
The project will also support the formation and facilitation of 20 peace clubs, in the old and the new TLS (SZOP) to teach pupils peace education, Gender-Based Violence (GBV) through social events like sports, debates so that they grow up as peace ambassadors in the country
J4CO will conduct training for 8 school inspectors who will be charged with monitoring, supervising, and evaluating the performance of the teachers/schools in different institutions and recommend ways of improving quality education in the county.
The project will ensure the integration of school feeding program in 20 schools previously under its support with support from UNICEF and the World Food Programme
The 217-2021 Severity Needs level with regard to education stands at 3.4 m people, of which 29% are minimal, 36% stressed, 36% Severe, 18% in extreme needs of education services, while 3% are in catastrophe. This requires immediate response to the needs of children in Leer which is placed under IPC 3 and Mayom (IPC 4) emergency.

1 - Needs

According to Joint three UN IPC release (FAO/WFP and UNICEF as of Dec 18, 2020, 6.5m people will be severely Acute food insecure countrywide (IPC 3). The worst food insecurity scenario is reported in Pibor and Akobo, Aweil South, Tonj East, South and South where households have run out of food. These same figures are expected to increase between April and July to 7.2m people that will be food insecure due to the factors of insecurity, drought, and economic crisis, and the impacts of the Covid 19 pandemic. According to the report, lack of food at school is the number one reason for drop out. During this time of food shortage, children are more likely to be pulled out of school to help the family find food and will simply be kept home if they have no energy to attend. For girls, in particular, earlier dropout and marriage has been an ongoing effect of the economic and food crisis, as families look at them as a source of dowry which would be used for household survival. School children will also be able to access protective factors of school (psychosocial support, lifesaving messages, and access to other services. The combined effects of displacement, low-income levels, weak productivity, hyperinflation, loss of production, and climatic shocks are jeopardizing the lives, livelihoods, and dignity of vulnerable IDPs and host community households in Leer and Mayom Counties. The population figures in the whole of Unity state according to the last counts, IPC Oct to Nov 2020, puts it to 1,096,229. Out of these, 297,000 people are stressed (24%), 500000 in crisis (50%), 204000 in an emergency (19%), and the rest of the population are in IPC 1 and 2 mainly due to the minimal food assistance is given to them last year.School enrollment in the state has been affected during the 2020/2021 academic years due to the impacts of the covid 19 pandemic which brought about the country lockdown. Statistically, the number of enrolled pupils are as follows; ECD 5489 (2254 F), Primary 22011 (10,078 F), ALP 4719(2458F), Sec, 4450(1258 F), the total teachers in the area; ECD, ALP, and Primary 1387 (212F), Sec 272 (25F). These are the major targets and other indirect beneficiaries under these interventions.Leer County is under IPC level 3 According to IPC analysis as of Oct-Nov 2020- April –July 2021 Suggesting that slightly over 50% of the population will be severely food insecure and harvest from 2020 depleted. A total of 9,739 HHs returnees have been registered by ROSS in the area of Leer (Touchriak, Yang, Thonyor, Panhiam, Dindin , Pilieny) and this figure is expected to have risen due to relative peace brought about by the formation of the Revitalized Transitional Government of National Unity (RTGoFNU) T. Similar population returns have also been reported in Mayom, according to IOM Data Tracking Matrix of large returns.Food security is linked to school attendance as shown by Education Cluster Assessment. According to the report, lack of food at school is the number one reason for drop out. During this time of food shortage, children are likely to be pulled out of school to help the family find food and will simply be kept home if they have no energy to attend. For girls, in particular, drop out and early marriage has been ongoing effects of economic and food crisis, as families look at them as the source of dowry which would be used for household survivalThe county continues to face a humanitarian crisis, resulting from the inter-communal conflict and lack of food remains a big challenge, which has been further exacerbated by the harsh economic conditions as a result of conflict and high inflation currently being experienced in the country. In addition, the economic crisis, poorly functioning markets, climatic shocks which include prolonged dry spells, and flooding have further worsened the household livelihood capacities are depending heavily on school attendance as most households are adopting negative coping strategies.

2 - Project activities

The project aims at providing male and female children, adolescents, and school-aged youths with equitable access to safe, protective, and inclusive education, improving cognitive skills of male and female children, adolescents, and school-aged youths through education in emergency ECD, basic education, youth education, and ensuring girls and boys are protected through increased access to psychosocial support, life skills, and referral pathways. J4CO) will provide an integrated enabling environment to increase enrollment, retention and reduce vulnerability against hunger and malnutrition among school children through the establishment of a gender-segregated block of 5 latrines each (2 for boys, 2 for girls, and 1 for teachers) in 3 schools in Leer County and 2 in Mayom county to ensure safe disposal of feces for girls and boys, installation of hand-washing facilities and distribution of soap and chlorine in schools to reduce the rate of waterborne diseases and the Covid 19 Pandemic. The funds will help in establishing school stores and basic kitchens blocks for meal preparation to make them ready for school feeding program including facilitating the training of cooks and storekeepers on the enriched supplementary food preparation, handling, cooking, and storage of food in 5 schools in Leer and 2 in Mayom County- targetting (1445 boys, 1000 girls). As a crosscutting activity mental health and psychosocial support services to help youth deal with trauma and social pressures which are heightened by movement restriction, health, and hygiene promotion, including Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) to 1000 adolescent girls, and youth training on leadership, peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence for 100 youths.Justice for Children Organization (J4CO) in a need assessment conducted before submission of this proposal showed a poor education system in all the participating schools in the two Counties, evidence of several and different problems that are negatively affecting enrollment and reinforcing dropout rates result from early marriage, poverty, GBV, food insecurity among others. The majority of the schools assessed have unsuitable learning spaces environments, lack WASH facilities, lack kitchens, and cooking utensils, and untrained trained volunteer teachers. Thus, the most relevant needs identified concern these three main issues. Firstly,the data concerning the targeted schools show an alarming lack of the most basic scholastic materials, such as blackboards, textbooks, and other stationery, which are pivotal necessities for ensuring a proficient education for pupils, but also a decent teaching environment for teachers. The collected data show also a compelling need for training teachers on teaching pedagogy, ethics/code of conducts, classroom management, Protection, GBV, and inclusion topics. Out of 20 assessed schools in Leer County, 10 of them are highly recommended for onsite feeding activities, 8 schools are recommended for establishment of new semi-permanent sex-segregated latrines, 6(2) in Mayom are recommended for medium-scale renovation including its sex-segregated latrines to tackle girls’ extremely high dropout, due to the elevated risk of sexual harassment lack of safe spaces for changing menstrual pads. J4CO has been implementing Education for Children in Emergencies in Leer/Unity since 2017 and at the same time conducting needs assessment in the whole Unity state especially in the field of Education, health, Food security and Livelihood, WASH, and Protection. The findings in especially in education indicate that there are schools that still operate in the open air or under trees with no hygiene facilities. Trained teachers are also lacking and the few who are there opt to look for better jobs since they are not being paid on regular basis. Similar gaps exist in Mayom county as a result of the withdrawal of CHIDO because their project ended, therefore need intervention to continue promoting children's education.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

J4CO is aware of the risks and threats associated with humanitarian response in the context of South Sudan and Mayom county in particular, through our strongly established presence, the organization has been able to gain the trust of the community, and successfully implemented past and ongoing projects in the area that has given us credibility in managing risks that arise, and through our accountability, full transparency approach with the local authorities, partners on the ground and at the national level, we’ve managed to coordinate response and in case of crises emergence, the necessary precautions Assumptions & Risks• High cost of transport: Leer and mayom counties are very far apart and some areas have poor or no feeder roads that vehicles can move to transport food items and other non-food items. The roads become impassable during rainy seasons J4CO has included a separate budget in the proposal to cater for plane chatter and will organize with WFP to put some contingencies to cater for any abrupt increase in the cost of transport from the airstrip• Inadequate Storage facilities: J4CO storage capacity is limited and may not accommodate all the shipments at once but this can be addressed through collaboration with the state ministry of education and other humanitarian partners in the state so that some food materials are temporarily kept in their facilities. Alternatively, J4CO can plan some money to construct temporal stores.• Inter-Communal Violence: Sometimes there are fights among the communities and this can interrupt project activities because children and the staff feel insecure. Already, UNICEF and other partners have come up with the declaration of schools as the zone of peace and that prohibits the use of pupils and the schools for any purpose other than learning. The organization will always monitor security updates of the areas through NGOs forum/UNDSS to facilitate where possible re-locatable staff or try to mitigate conflict through local conflict mechanism• Conflict of interest from the community leaders. It is very common to see local authorities having an interest in a project like this can be averted by early clarity on the nature of this project and making it community-owned to be involved in every effort including decision-making processes• Threats of covid 19 in the country. The pandemic has been a major challenge to most economic, social, political, and religious activities worldwide, farcing lockdowns. This can continue to hinder the smooth implementation of project activities given that, the rollout of vaccines is still very slow and not yet in South Sudan. J4CO will, however, adhere to sticks health regulatory measures put forward by the government, health institutions,s, and other agencies concerning covid 19 preventive measures.
