Project Summary

The HALO Trust’s (HALO) ultimate goal is to clear Somalia’s landmine contaminated border with Ethiopia, in order to improve access to water, pasture, and infrastructure, and promote peacebuilding. Somalia’s landmine contamination is a legacy of 40 years of sustained conflict, and it threatens the recovery of Somalia’s most fragile rural communities. This contamination restricts access to water, pasture, and other essential services, in regions where clan conflicts and grievances exacerbate an already desperate competition for resources. This contamination has resulted in recent and severe accidents, killing civilians and destroying livelihoods. So great is the need for landmine clearance that some communities have attempted their own demining, without training, safety equipment, or adequate detection equipment.
The project seeks funding to support the deployment of non-technical survey teams along the Somali-Ethiopian border. Non-technical survey (NTS) is the process through which a team gathers information on the presence and impact of mines and other explosive ordnance, without using any specific capital equipment or technical process. The NTS relies on a range of discussions with key informants like affected communities, ex combatants, or explosive ordnance victims. This process gathers important information that will help the team decide whether the area is indeed contaminated; and what the boundaries of the suspected hazardous area are. It also helps gathering information on the type of contamination in the area, which will affect the clearance method chosen in the future.
NTS is a vital element of the mine action sector, as it allows operators and authorities to have an accurate picture of the contamination landscape in the country. It can then be used to effectively plan, prioritize, implement and monitor all explosive hazards activities such as clearance and risk education. Delineating the dangerous areas can also save lives and alleviate suffering, by clearly indicating where the hazardous areas are. Until 2021, most NTS focused on localised areas along the Ethiopian-Somali border in the South West, Hirshabelle and Galmudug States. Little is known about the contamination in Puntland and Jubaland State and extending survey activities there will enable the mine action operators and the Somali Federal Government to have a better grasp of the work needed. In addition, there are still many knowledge gaps regarding the contamination in South West, Hirshabelle and Galmudug States, and this grant will allow for a more comprehensive survey of the area, including remote locations.
The project seeks to deploy 8 NTS mobile teams along the Ethiopian border between January and December 2022 (12 months). Each team will be composed of 4 members (1 man, 3 women), and will roam around the region to identify and map potentially hazardous areas. Geodata will be shared with the Somali Explosive Management Authority (SEMA), to help them build a comprehensive picture of the contamination. In addition, the teams will provide crucial risk education and awareness sessions to all communities visited, in order to minimize the likelihood of explosive hazards-related deaths and accidents.
The overarching objectives of this project are to:
• Enhance security and stability, as well as reduce risk of death and injury to individuals and communities;
• Improving the knowledge of the contamination landscape in Somalia, for all mine action operators; and
• Help SEMA improve their planning and monitoring capacities.

1. - Projects activities

HALO Trust will target affected communities across the Ethiopian-Somali border in the Jubaland, South West, Galmudug, Hirshabelle and Puntland states. This project will allow the surveying and mapping of contamination along the border area, therefore reducing the threat to lives and increasing the efficiency of stakeholders within the Protection Cluster and within SEMA. In the long-term, this mapping will contribute to the prioritising of clearance activities, which will facilitate access to land, reduce resource-based clan conflicts, and encourage stability. The intervention is relying on the following activities: training and deployment of 8 NTS mobile teams to conduct non-technical survey and collect data on the contamination along the Somali-Ethiopian border; delivery of risk education sessions for 480 communities along the border (reaching up to 14,000 people of all genders, age groups and disability status); sharing of information with and monitoring of the geomapping process of the Somali Explosive Management Authority (SEMA)

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Risk 4.4 HALO will ensure that teams are operating in safe areas, and avoid any territory controlled by Al-Shabab or other non-state armed actors. Journeys will be planned ahead and consent will be collected from village elders, to ensure that teams are welcomed in a community and will be allowed to operate safely. HALO also has an extensive network of community liaison officers who monitor all areas of operations and help building trust and acceptance from local communities. Risk 4.5 Teams will receive an operational training, which will allow them to identify explosive ordnance and adopt safe behaviours when surveying an area. This includes: never entering a suspected contaminated area; always planning a journey ahead and making sure the roads are safe and clear of explosive items, etc. If need be, teams will be able to call other HALO operatives to help safely remove or dispose of explosive ordnances.
