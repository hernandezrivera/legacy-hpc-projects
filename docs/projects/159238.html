Project Summary

Floods and droughts are among the most dangerous and costly of all natural disasters that have had profound direct and indirect effects on the economy of Somalia. The last 10 years has seen more than 50% of failed rainy seasons, two major drought events including a famine in 2011 and a severe drought in 2016/17. About four major flood events have also been witnessed in the country during the same period in 2010, 2013, 2015 and 2018. Such sequential events have had hazardous impacts, eroding resilience, economic reserves, livestock herd size and health.

The project objective is to reduce flood and drought impacts through the use of FAO Early Warning information and foster a better understanding of the Juba and Shabelle river basins in Somalia through detailed river surveys that will see durable and sustainable measures to reduce frequent occurrence of floods.
The project aims to scale up the use of climate information and early warning systems such as Flood Risk and Response Management Information System (FRRMIS) and Combined Drought Index (CDI) for saving lives and enhancing livelihoods in vulnerable communities. The project will support the expansion of hydro-meteorological monitoring network that will generate data which will be used in turn in the production of early warning information for early action. Dissemination of the early warning information will be done through e-mail, FAO-SWALIM website (FRMMIS), direct SMS and media broadcast to vulnerable communities. To sustain this, FAO will strengthen institutional and indigenous capacities for use of Early Warning Systems (EWS) and Climate Information (CI) in preparedness for response to climate related disasters.
The project will also undertake river survey and morphological study for sustainable flood risk management of the Juba and Shabelle river basins. The study results will be used to build institutional and indigenous capacities to mitigate flood risks along the two rivers.
The project will strengthen and create awareness on the best practice in water use among the communities through capacity building, citizen science and information dissemination. This will see an improvement in the way different communities utilize and reserve water during rainy seasons. The project will advocate for simple rain water harvesting methods and soil conservation measures that will see optimal use of rain water and thus reduce water scarcity in a selected pilot study areas.

Project Activities

1. Expansion of networks that generate climate-related data to save lives and safeguard livelihoods from extreme climate events (January – December 2020). 2. Development of platforms including the web based FRRMIS, CDI and localized mobile applications; dissemination of climate related information/services through radio, SMS, print media and e-mail to communities engaged in agriculture based livelihoods (January – December 2020). 3. Contribute and benefit from national, regional and international platforms for joint UN humanitarian response and FAO resilience programme (January – December 2020). 4. Strengthening institutional and indigenous capacities for use of Early Warning Systems (EWS) and Climate Information (CI) in preparedness for response to climate related disasters (January – December 2020). 5. Enhancement of flood (FRRMIS) and drought (CDI) monitoring tools (January – December 2020) . 6. Continuous monitoring of monitoring river breakages and flooded areas during the 2020 Gu and Deyr rains using very-high resolution satellite imagery and field verification (March to November 2020). 7. Carry out a river morphological study to plan river training works, support flood management and flood forecasting along the two rivers (March to October 2020). 8. Strengthening institutional and local communities capacities to mitigate flood risks along the two rivers (September to December 2020). 9. Strengthening capacity and create awareness on best practice in water use/management in a selected basin (February to December 2020).

Project Indicators

• Number of telemetric (online) monitoring stations supplied and installed in selected areas;• Number of flood and drought risk and early warning information (SMS alerts, bulletins and radio messages) produced, disseminated and used to inform vulnerable communities;• Online Flood Risk and Response Information Management System (FRRIMS) and Drought monitoring tool enhanced and updated; • Reduction of flooded acreage and number of people vulnerable to flood risks along the Juba and Shabelle Rivers;• Number of flood maps produced during the Gu and Deyr seasons;• One report of river morphology for Juba and Shabelle Rivers produced and disseminated;.• One report contributing to joint UN humanitarian response and FAO geospatial resilience platform documenting the technical approach for monitoring flood and drought;• Number of persons by gender benefiting from capacity building activities;• Number of capacity building and awareness workshops held.

Mitigation Measures

Lens 1: 1a. Exposure to landmines or explosive remnants of war Humanitarian access to some areas within Somalia is limited to conflict/security threats including presence of landmines or explosive remnants of war. The use of high resolution satellite images will be emphasized in areas with limited access for ground truthing. FAO has also invested in local staff who knows the security issues/risks of their respective areas. The staff also have very close relationships with the local communities. FAO will monitor closely the security of local staff and partner organizations with guidance from the United Nations Department of Safety and Security (UNDSS) in Somalia. Lens 2: 2b. Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processesIn times of floods and droughts women and children are most affected in the community. The early warning information generated during this project will target the most vulnerable members of the community who are women and children. The participation of women in workshops targeted to promote early warning information will be encouraged. Lens 3: 3a. Protection concerns stemming from displacementThe ultimate beneficiary of the SWALIM early warning information generated through this project include vulnerable communities. In times of high floods there is a high risk of the vulnerable communities being excluded from getting early warning information due to displacement. This project aims at scaling up efforts to reduce disaster and climate risk and strengthening the resilience and adaptive capacity of people at risk of displacement by investing in early warning for early action information systems and strengthening institutional and indigenous capacities to mitigate flood risks along the Juba and Shabelle Rivers. Further, the project will establishing capacity for preparedness and community-based early warning systems in order to prepare for safe and timely evacuations if displacement becomes unavoidable, thus ensuring the protection of all displaced persons.
