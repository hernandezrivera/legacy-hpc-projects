Project Summary

Since 2016, the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon have been involved in a crisis which started as a teacher-lawyer strike and degenerated into what has been dubbed an armed conflict.
In North West and South West, 53% of women are exposed to risk of violence during travelling, 33% of the avoided sites by women and girls are water points and 32% are latrines. In terms of GBV, 18% of the protection issues in the accessed villages were identified to face GBV 29% exposed to sexual violence, 17% forced into marriages, 10% child marriages, 10% domestic violence and 19% unable to access services. 30% of accessed locations do not have women and girls safe space.
Incidences of emotional violence, denial of resources, opportunities and services, physical violence, rape and sexual assault, and forced marriage continue to be reported mostly by women and girls, as intimate partner violence or committed by known or unknown persons including armed men. As per the GBV IMS data a total of 7,996 GBV survivors received specialized GBV services from January to September in North West, South West and the Far North. It includes 79% of IPV, 20% of sexual violence. More 90% of GBV survivors in NW and SW and 100% in FN are women. Reported incidents are a sign of response capacity but not of the prevalence, as many barriers including closure of services in most parts of the North West and South West (NW, SW), lack of access to services, lack of awareness about available services hamper survivors to report and seek services. Despite efforts deployed, GBV protection services and GBV prevention mechanisms are insufficient or totally absent in many localities. The limited funding allocated to the GBV sector in 2022 resulted in the suspension of services in some locations. Many GBV survivors did not have access to specialised services, risking infection of STI or HIV, mental distress and unwanted pregnancy. From 2021-2022 there was 35% decrease in GBV services delivery. In both NWSW and FN some health facilities are not functional, or don’t have resources to provide Clinical Management of Rape services. In 2022 only 0,01% (as per FTS) of the requested GBV funding was granted. The situation in 2023 will get worse for 953,524 women and girls, the most exposed to GBV risks and 33,854 men and boys as well, if no attention and adequate support and resources are allocated to address GBV needs
The main activities will focus on sensitization and awareness raising for communities, emphasizing the participation of men and boys, strengthen referral and legal assistance mechanisms for internally displaced persons and host communities with legal protection needs (detention/documentation, GBV survivors etc.), provide basic vocational training and economic/Empowerment support to women and girls, Provide cash transfer assistance, build the capacity of operational organizations on the holistic response to GBV. UNFPA and partners will continue providing individualized case management and psychosocial support to survivors of GBV and other vulnerable people. Finally, UNFPA, in collaboration with the Ministry of Women Empowerment and the Family (MINPROFF), will also continue to reinforce close collaboration between actors and service providers involved in responding to the needs of women and girls. Ensure that safe and ethical systems for the transfer of data including confidential personal records of GBV survivors are put in place by organizations and authorities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Gender-Base Violence (GBV) constitutes a violation of the fundamental human rights and is one of the main symptoms of the unequal power relationship between men and women. GBV seriously affects all aspects of a woman’s life, especially their health, their participation in economic life, and their social integrity. Women victims of violence are more likely to have physical, mental and reproductive health problems. Indeed, the sociological and legal context of GBV is characterized by a spirit of tolerance towards this phenomenon in Cameroon and especially in the North West, South West where there is a large number of displaced populations as a result of the Anglophone crises. In order to look for sustainable solutions to this situation, UNFPA has been carrying out GBV prevention and response activities in the North West, South West regions in Cameroon. UNFPA’s efforts are aimed to improve the quality of basic services for survivors. Although UNFPA's interventions have had a positive impact in terms of behavior change and the support from community members to survivors and other vulnerable women and girls, there are still gaps to cover. In 2023 UNFPA plans to continue activities in the sub divisions listed above. The main activities will focus on sensitization and awareness raising for communities, emphasizing the participation of men and boys, strengthen referral and legal assistance mechanisms for internally displaced persons and host communities with legal protection needs (detention/documentation, GBV survivors etc.), provide basic vocational training and economic/Empowerment support to women and girls, Provide cash transfer assistance, build the capacity of operational organizations on the holistic response to GBV. UNFPA and partners will continue providing individualized case management and psychosocial support to survivors of GBV and other vulnerable people. Finally, UNFPA, in collaboration with the Ministry of Women Empowerment and the Family (MINPROFF), will also continue to reinforce close collaboration between actors and service providers involved in responding to the needs of women and girls. Ensure that safe and ethical systems for the transfer of data including confidential personal records of GBV survivors are put in place by organizations and authorities. Expected Results: Risk of GBV and SRH is reduced and quality of response improved and sustained.
