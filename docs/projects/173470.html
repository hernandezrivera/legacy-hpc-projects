Project Summary

IMC will continue to implement GBV prevention and response services in Benghazi and in Misrata, which include the provision of specialized GBV case management services based on a survivor-centered
approach. IMC has established awareness and case management services in Misrata and Benghazi and intends to expand to ensure comprehensive services in line with GBV guiding principles and a survivor centered approach through static (women and girl safe spaces (WGSS)), mobile and remote service delivery modalties. The main target population will be women and girls but men and boys will be engaged in awareness sessions to facilitate social behaviour change. To ensure complementary services under this project,
health staff will also be trained on GBV core concepts and safe referrals of GBV cases to GBV
caseworkers. IMC will also maintain a field-level referral system with other nongovernmental
organizations (NGOs) and actors providing relevant services like emergency cash
assistance, child protection, and shelter. IMC will train community based focal points to support community based awareness and referrals and work closely with local organisations to ensure local ownership and support. Moreover, local partners will be trained on GBV case management and awareness and life skills curricula, such as the adolescent toolkit.
IMC will provide the target communities with increased knowledge on their health rights, including SRHR, and service availability. In addition to the GBV team, also the health and MHPSS staff will be trained on Protection and GBV concepts and ensure gender balance of staff in facilities with clear referral pathways in line with the IASC Guidelines for Integrating GBV Intervention in Humanitarian Actions and the IASC Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action, giving clear guidelines to increase inclusive access and reduce barriers to care. IMC will continue to collect disaggregated sex and age data and conduct focus group discussions and key informant interviews with both mixed and single-sex groups and, when possible and relevant, with different age groups as well as with people with disabilities, to gather vital information and to allow for deeper analysis of the gender and inclusion implications that activities and services offered by IMC have in the specific areas of intervention and to ensure adjustments are made accordingly and continuously throughout the program cycle.

1 - Needs

The huge gaps in GBV services means that health consequences, including sexually transmitted infections, unplanned pregnancies, and other reproductive and possibly life-threatening health complications remain unaddressed. It is crucial to scale up and establish holistic and comprehensive GBV prevention and response services, especially in geographic locations that lack any specialized GBV services and are affected by increased conflict and displacement. Inclusive access to remote and mobile service delivery, in terms of language sensitivity, geographical reach and different abilities, needs to be ensured. In parallel, women and girls need to be provided with resources, knowledge and monetary assistance to safely access adapted service modalities. Shelter and independent safe houses for GBV survivors are urgently needed. The MSNA findings highlighted a gap of information about rights, safe access opportunities and service availability among all population groups. Prioritizing safe access to GBV and sexual and reproductive health services amidst the protracted crisis and COVID-19 pandemic is key along with capacity building and advocacy for safer access to health services, respecting GBV Guiding Principles and CMR guidelines. Furthermore, advocacy on protective legal mechanisms for women and girls, to end impunity for perpetrators through the legal loopholes and unlimited humanitarian access to detention facilities needs to be strengthened.

2 - Activities

Providing GBV awareness sessions, PSS group activities, Case Managament inlcuding referral services for women and girls and men and boys through static and mobile/remote response modalities, ensuring accessibility for all age groups and for people with diasbilities. Trainings on GBV core concepts and GBV case management will be offered for community focal points and local stakeholders.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

IMC plans to mitigate the risk of increased tensions and conflicts in the communitydue to perceived or actual unequal distribution of assistance through ensuring clearcommunication to the affected population about how, where, when, and whyassistance is provided and. IMC also consults with communities before, during, and afteractivities to more accurately gauge, and then respond to, the needs of each communitywith a particular focus on vulnerable groups including women, children, and peoplewith disabilities. IMC plans to mitigate the risk of increased tensions and conflicts inthe community due to perceived or actual affiliation to one group or another byensuring that all IMC staff working on this project follow guiding humanitarianprinciples of impartiality and neutrality. IMC also communicates with local, regional, and nationalgovernment bodies to explain our status as an impartial INGO who is not affliated ordeferential to any particular group. Related to COVID-19 risks, IMC will ensure that all staff are trained on proper infection, prevention, control standards and are give the necessary PPE to ensure theirsafety and the safety of beneficiaries. Any group activities will be led ensuring socialdistance and proper use of PPE are maintained. As a medical organization, we areparticularly well suited to address these concerns. These measures act as mitigationto the risk of increased exposure to COVID-19 transmission. Moreover, IMC will ensure PSEA training and awareness for all staff and beneficiaries as well as training staff on GBV, PFA and referral pathways to support awareness and assistance to affected individuals. IMC has PSEA focal points in all site locations to ensure access to reporting mechanisms. Furthermore, IMC is training all relevant staff on data protection, safe torage of data and handling of sensitive data in line with GBV guiding principles.
