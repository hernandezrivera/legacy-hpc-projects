Project Summary

Mercy-USA for Aid and Development (Mercy-USA) aims to improve the quality of life and protect vulnerable communities from morbidity and mortality associated with water-related diseases through water, sanitation and hygiene activities. This 12 month WASH project will target vulnerable pastoralists affected by drought and conflict in Hiiraan, Galgadud, Mudug and Banadir regions by improving access to safe water and sanitation facilities and facilitating the uptake of acceptable hygiene practices. The project activities are in line with relevant strategic plans, including the Somalia National Development Plan (2020-2024), UN Strategic Framework 2017-2020 (UNSF) – SP3 and SP4 and WASH Cluster priorities and guidance.
The target populations in Hiraan, Banadir and Gal-mudug have been facing simultaneously multiple barriers and inequalities in accessing WASH services. These barriers include, geographical barriers, where some of the target communities live in rural settings, physical barriers, where persons with disabilities, children, older persons, pregnant women and others vulnerable groups are unable to access WASH services due to inappropriate design, and economic barriers i.e. high construction costs, connection charges and tariffs that limit people's access to safe and affordable services. In particular, the IDP communities have been experiencing abject poverty, population density, contaminated environments, and a lack of formal land tenure, which all combine to limit their access to safe water and sanitation. An increase in population contributes to water shortage, with only 66.7% having access to drinking water sources, resulting in increased watery diarrhea (Mercy-USA Rapid Needs Assessment, April 2020).
In light of the COVID-19 pandemic, already vulnerable people in Galmuug, Hiraan, and Banadir regions are unable to ensure preventive measures and protect themselves from the virus due to a lack of reliable and sufficient water and sanitation services not only at home and in the community level but also in health facilities, putting both health workers and patients at a high risk of infection.
Mercy-USA thus seeks to increase access to sustainable WASH services for disadvantaged individuals and groups, including IDPs and people living in rural areas and increase their resilience to emergencies, including current COVID-19 pandemic.
Mercy-USA's proposed WASH response is immediate and designed to control the spread of water-borne diseases and preserve the health of the target populations, focusing on the severely affected and hard-to-reach. Proposed interventions include the provision of free, safe and sustainable drinking water, routine chlorination of existing water sources, testing of communal water sources for chemical and microbiological hazards by use of a portable water testing kit, construction of gender-segregated VIP latrines for underserved public primary schools and public health facilities and hygiene promotion. Mercy-USA will directly implement project activities.
Mercy-USA will ensure that people with disabilities participate in implementation of project activities by ensuring their representation in all WASH committees and community meetings. The project will consult with PWDs about location, design, safety, and facilities' appropriateness. During project implementation, segregated focus group discussions will be held with the community to identify gender inequalities, discuss all the genders' needs, and understand the risks faced by PWDs in accessing WASH services. Activities will target to address these needs during program implementation.
During routine and regular program monitoring and evaluation, Mercy-USA staff will collect disaggregated data by disability, age and gender to monitor the impact of programs on vulnerable groups. Mercy-USA, during both monthly and quarterly data reviews, will seek to monitor inclusiveness of programming by comparing achievements versus available population data.

Project Activities

WATER SUPPLY SUB SECTOR • Rehabilitation and protection of 42 shallow wells. Shallow well designs will be flood-resistant, and all wells will be equipped with hand pumps and 1 animal watering trough per well. • Rehabilitation of 5 boreholes (2- Gal-mudug and 3-Hiraan regions), including conversion of inefficient generator-powered boreholes to hybrid models by installing solar-powered pumping systems, construction of elevated tanks, piping extensions of water sources and construction of water kiosks in IDP settlements and primary schools in areas with high rates of malnutrition and AWD to improve access to safe drinking water. • Shallow well chlorination campaigns targeting 60 shallow wells.• Construct rainwater harvesting systems in 12 primary schools.• Training of 29 Water Management Committees (WMC) to ensure that targeted water sources (12 shallow wells, 5 boreholes and 12 rainwater harvesting systems) remain safe, effective and operational during and after disasters to provide safe and free drinking water for vulnerable IDPs. • Provision of water through water voucher system during periods of water shortage targeting 1,800 HHs in IDP camps for 3 months.SANITATION SUB SECTOR • Construction of 100 gender segregated VIP latrines and 100 handwashing stations for IDP communities. • Construction of 8 solar powered latrine protection street lights to protect IDP communities. • Construction of 40 gender segregated VIP latrines and 40 handwashing stations for rural Health Facilities providing free MCH services. • Construction of 48 gender-segregated VIP latrines and 48 handwashing stations for primary schools in communities affected by high malnutrition rates.• Provide cleaning kits (brooms, buckets, liquid soap, and gloves) to 12 primary schools, 20 MCHs and 7 IDP settlements targeted with sanitation infrastructure.• Provide continuous on-the-job training for health workers, teachers and school WASH club members. • Promote sanitation and safe solid waste disposal in households through hygiene education. • Provision for appropriate disposal of menstrual material will be provided in all proposed sanitation facilities. • Separate VIP latrines will be constructed for males and females, sited in safe locations and illuminated at night. HYGIENE SUB SECTOR • At the community level, the program will train 9 Hygiene promoters (HP) 55% men and 45% women who will supervise the work of other 91 community hygiene promoters.• All hygiene promotion staff (100) will undergo a 5-day comprehensive training on PHAST. • Distribution of 2,000 standard hygiene kits to guardians of children who have been admitted to the nutrition SFP/OTP program, mothers who have delivered at the health facilities and people affected by AWD in 17 health facilities. Mercy-USA will use Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) for hygiene items such as soap, menstrual hygiene products, chlorine for household water treatment, and water transport and storage containers, and/ or for water and sanitation services.• All health facility staff and teachers will be facilitated to come up with an AWD/Cholera preparedness plan based on their existing resources. Household Water Treatment and Storage (HHWTS) networking group will be formed at each health facility. • Support for one KAP/coverage survey to measure program indicators. COORDINATION • Regular update of 4W matrix;• Participating in regional and district level coordination meetings. Initiate district coordination meetings in locations where there are none. • Participating in cluster technical working groups when required.ENVIRONMENTAL HEALTH • Construction of one standard incinerator with a waste separation zone and waste bin for each of the 17 health facilities in need of such items. • The operator of the incinerator will receive appropriate training, demonstration and a complete set of protective clothing and equipment.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The voucher system allows beneficiaries to choose from a list of WASH items approved by the WASH Cluster. This increased autonomy enhances the dignity of beneficiaries and enables them to select items in line with household needs. Moreover, Mercy-USA favors the voucher transfer modality as it: • enhances beneficiary dignity: making choices for themselves empowers the beneficiaries and can help restore a sense of dignity that is often disrupted by forced displacement as HHs able to prioritize based on HH needs; • supports local markets through cash injection for small/medium size vendors and therefore create multiplier effects for the benefits of the humanitarian intervention; • increases the cost-effectiveness of Mercy-USA's response.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Through Mercy-USA's field offices, Mercy-USA has carried out an assessment of the local market and determined the proposed WASH items are available through vendors in the markets which remain functional and accessible. Mercy-USA will continuously conduct market surveys in the target areas to asses markets and vendors` capacities and to finalize the list of suppliers where the beneficiaries could redeem the vouchers. Mercy-USA will support markets through providing technical support or training, small grants/loans to WASH service providers and generating demand for the products/services among consumers.

Mitigation Measures

Risk that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA:• Mercy-USA, guided by internal PSEA and Child Safeguarding Policy and international best practices, will safeguard gender justice and equity and promote gender equality. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk:• Mercy-USA will carry out mapping exercises on a semi-annual basis to understand marginalized communities in the areas of operation and their needs. • Mercy-USA will ensure that community selection committees comprise at least 50% representation of various marginalized people, including IDPs, minority tribes, and people with disabilities. • Data collected for program implementation will be segregated in gender and disability, ensuring that the program reaches marginalized communities. • The design of WASH infrastructure will factor in the needs of the marginalized communities. For example, boreholes will be solar-powered to reduce the running cost hence providing free drinking water. Zero tariffs/costs on drinking water will ensure that no vulnerable members of the community are left without water. The WMC will charge a nominal fee for livestock owners. • Mercy-USA will ensure that formal confidential feedback and complaints mechanisms are accessible to all community members, especially marginalized communities. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk. Mercy-USA will have three forms of confidential feedback mechanisms to ensure that all community members are reached, including illiterate people, those who cannot physically travel, and finally, those who do not have resources to feedback. The follow mechanisms will be in place:• Suggestion boxes • Hotline numbers• Focal persons at the community level. • Community mobilization will be intensively carried out to ensure the uptake of the formal feedback mechanisms established.• Mercy-USA will continuously monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of the mechanisms established and make improvements continually.
