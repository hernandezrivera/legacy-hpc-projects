Project Summary

The Danish Refugee Council (DRC) is appealing for support to implement CP and GBV prevention and response programming targeting vulnerable Lebanese households (HHs) in the Bekaa and North Lebanon. The rapid and devastating deterioration of the Lebanese economy since 2020 has both impacted the value of the Lebanese currency and the already struggling labour market. More than ever before the host community struggled to meet their basic needs. With a lack of access to employment and income generation activities, vulnerable Lebanese HHs have become increasingly been becoming more reliant on humanitarian assistance, at levels previously unwitnessed by DRC during the response. The inability of HHs to meet their daily needs has also had a devastating impact on child labour, with more children and younger children engaging in daily work, a negative coping mechanism that had previously been most witnessed more with refugees is now becoming more evident within the Lebanese community. The threat of eviction with more HHs living under an eviction order, and families further relying on debt to avoid eviction. During implementation of case management in 2021 DRC witnessed rates of Lebanese children requiring and intervention double compared to the previous year, indicating that an increase in specialised attention to child protection prevention and response must be delivered in a tailored fashion to these communities to ensure that Lebanese children have the right to grow in safety.
The current events in Lebanon have also had a devastating effect on rates of GBV for women and girls. An impact survey into the effects of COVID-19 and GBV rates and perceptions on refugee women and girls found that an overall decrease in reporting, down 8%, indicating during periods of lockdown it was difficult for survivors to access support. At the same time incidents of domestic violence and intimate partner violence have been on the rise since COVID-19 and the implementation of lockdown measures. Data collected through the GBVIMS highlights this trend, with 78% of incidents perpetrated by an intimate partner.
DRC will provide urgent protection services in governorates where the greatest numbers of vulnerable Lebanese reside. This will be achieved through the implementation of an integrated package of protection activities. DRC will target individuals at risk or survivors of GBV, with a focus on women at risk of violence and abuse, and adolescent girls at risk of early marriage as the most prevalent protection concerns observed, and for whom individual capacity to overcome without intervention is extremely limited, and high risk. Adolescents survivors will also be identified through FPSS. For emotional support groups (ESG), survivors engaging in case management will be automatically advised about the sessions. Other women in the communities will also be considered eligible depending on dynamics and risk factors within the communities. DRC will target children at risk or victims of abuse, neglect, exploitation or violence with a specific focus on children engaged in WFCL and exposed to violence in the home. Children will be identified through internal and external direct referrals through geographic coordination with other CP actors, through FPSS sessions targeting children and adolescents and CBPSS targeting children and caregiver more broadly, with a particular focus on reducing child labor and addressing harmful gender norms. Legal assistance will be embedded within the CP and GBV process.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

DRC will deliver GBV services using these mixed and flexible modalities: survivor centered GBV CM to support informed decision making about how to address the GBV incident, risk, or violation, and understand which services are available and to facilitate access to these services in a holistic and dignified manner; and 2) ESG to ensure survivors have access to a supportive network. Legal counselling will also be available to survivors who require individual legal advice from a lawyer. DRC will deliver CP CM will focus on the identification of children and caregivers who would benefit from an integrated approach to CM, where DRC will also leverage internal Economic Recovery activities that will contribute to finding economic solutions to the households needs, thereby decreasing a reliance on negative coping strategies like child labour, child marriage.DRC will implement FPSS that is based on the “I Am Here” approach, that supports outreach in identifying the most marginalised adolescent boys and girls through a door-to-door assessment methodology. Adolescents participate in the design of participatory curricula whereby adolescents prioritise their own needs in terms of skills, knowledge and aptitudes in pre-defined thematic areas. DRC’s partners will implement CBPSS in the most vulnerable areas with sessions being prioritized based on the needs of the community. Beneficiaries will remain engaged in sessions for three months with a minimum of four sessions attended.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Gender, diversity and inclusion has been integrated into the design and are in line with the IA Standing Committee GMT. DRC will disaggregate all beneficiary data collected by sex and age. DRC will protect the identity and data of its beneficiaries as per IA GBV data protection protocols. DRC maintains a high level of confidentiality, ensuring services are provided discreetly, and never identifying the survivor. DRC ensures that all staff are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for PSEA, and the internal reporting mechanisms for SEA cases, through the mandatory trainings and the signature of the code of conduct. DRC will work on enhancing its awareness raising activities with communities to disseminate information on SEA key messages, available reporting channels and how the overall procedure functions, from the point of receiving the complaint, to investigation, resolution and feedback to communities on the outcomes of complaints and actions taken.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

DRC ensures AAP by ensuring the voices of PoCs are heard and that they have an opportunity to participate in and influence relevant decisions affecting how we work with them. DRC’s methodology involves PoCs in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation phases. DRC will ensure opportunities to offer a response or redress where PoCs raise Feedback and Complaints (F&C). Beneficiary F&C will be gathered through different M&E tools, and DRC will ensure that F&C are incorporated to provide PoCs with a voice in program design and implementation. F&C are currently collected through Feedback Boxes (FB), a hotline and email . Information on these F&C channels to PoCs are disseminated by DRC staff through business cards. An information poster is installed next to each FB explaining its purpose. DRC staff are trained to explain the existence and importance of these channels. F&C are then compiled, logged, categorized and then referred to relevant staff for their action.
