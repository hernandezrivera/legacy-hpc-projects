Project Summary

A rapid gender assessment will be conducted to measure the impact of COVID-19 on women, girls, men and boys in Rohingya and host communities and the effectiveness of the response plan. The assessment will be conducted at the onset of the outbreak to identify immediate impact on women, girls, men and boys and other vulnerable populations of COVID-19, and how social and gender norms and roles affect their ability to protect themselves. A report s will be developed to provide evidence to support the design of new interventions and to identify whether any adjustments in response plans need to be made.

Needs

The normative framework that governs the lives of Rohingya refugees unequally affects women and girls by limiting their mobility, their agency to make decisions about their lives and access lifesaving services, and de-prioritizes their needs compared to men’s. These gender norms as well as a lack of gender-friendly facilities and services and security threats have hindered women’s and girls’ ability to meet basic needs even before the crisis. The COVID-19 crisis and lockdown measures will most likely exacerbate these pre-existing social and gender norms with negative consequences for women and girls. Other vulnerable populations, such as transgender, older women and men, and persons living with disabilities may face additional challenges in accessing information, services and support during COVID-19 crisis and, as their particular social characteristics makes them harder to reach, their needs may not be addressed by response and recovery plans. Without understanding pre-existing gender norms and roles and the gendered impact of COVID-19, sectors and organizations will not be able to plan and implement effective and inclusive response to COVID-19. Similarly, the long-term consequences of COVID-19, even after the outbreak may not be the same for women, girls, men and boys, and the ability of Rohingya communities to recover from this crisis will be linked to their gender, age and other characteristics. Long-term recovery interventions therefore need to be guided by evidence in order to ensure these benefit women, girls, men and boys equally, as well as other vulnerable populations.

Activities or outputs

1. Conduct a Rapid Gender Assessment (RGA) at the onset of the COVID-19 outbreak in the camps 2. Develop an RGA report on initial evidence of the different impact of COVID-19 on women, girls, men and boys, with recommendations for sectors and organizations to mitigate the gendered impact of COVID-19 and ensure COVID-19 response equally benefits women, girls, men and boys3. Widely disseminate the findings of the report through various channels
