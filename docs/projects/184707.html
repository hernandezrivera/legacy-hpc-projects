Project Summary

GBV remains significantly under-reported and undocumented in Somalia. Lack of data means the prevalence of GBV could be higher than available statistics. Silence also is one major challenge for survivors as well as stigma and discrimination. A legitimate concern is the weakness of prosecution of GBV perpetrators which contributed to impunity. This is due to a weakened rule of law and lack of legislation. With the intensification of the conflict due to the ongoing elections, drought/Floods and multiple armed actors fighting, the prevalence of GBV incidents is likely to increase. This is brought about by giving attention to elections and forgetting what is at stake regarding the IDPs in general. The main protection risks to GBV survivors are limited access to health services, lack of information, and insecurity in the camps.
GBV remains the greatest threat to women’s reproductive/sexual health and their emotional well-being. Women and girls suffer from GBV and reproductive health conditions that are preventable and treatable with better chances of recovery if medical advice is sought earlier. Unfortunately, many barriers keep the majority of women accessing different services of GBV, such as:
GBV response services are rarely available and scattered in areas. GBV victims face problems in accessing complete care packages included a requirement for management of traumatic complications as it receives very little support.
Lack of comprehensive GBV services, Psychological consequences of rape and sexual abuse are rarely addressed. If the problem is not addressed it can cause ruptures within society, as symptoms are persistent over years. Victims fear publicity and the resulting stigmatization.
Despite Many risks faced by people with disability, women, children, and youth with disabilities report a lack of information and awareness on GBV-related activities and how to access case management services, due to both physical and attitudinal barriers to accessing such services.
Traditional mechanisms are often used to solve all GBV cases, these results in increased stigmatization against women and girls. Traditional approaches to address rape tended to ignore survivor’s situations and instead seeks resolution or compensation for rape through negotiation between elders of the perpetrator’s and survivor’s clans. Some survivors were forced to marry perpetrators.
In this project, SWDC seeks to strengthen comprehensive, holistic, and multi-sectorial response and prevention activities for GBV survivors in Kaxda, Deynile and Dharkenley districts of Banadir region. SWDC’s strategy will support women, girls, boys, men, and GBV survivors to contribute to creating an environment of equality among them, ability to meaningfully contribute to peacebuilding and transitional justice mechanisms, and ultimately live free from violence. The project seeks to improve protection for 30,000 IDPs, returnees, host community, and refugees and GBV survivors to receive quality services, with particular emphasis placed on information sharing among GBV stakeholders, provide linkages to service points through hotlines, legal counseling, and representation. Advocacy and sensitization to mobilize communities including targeting political and traditional leaders and civil society, religious leaders to fight against GBV by assisting in prevention and protection will also be prioritized.
SWDC will closely coordinate and complement its activities in the district and sites, coordination will be sought with Shelter, CCCM, General protection, and HLP implementing partners. This approach will be useful to strengthen referrals and provision of quality support within the locations and the same population leading to achieving a greater impact.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Distance to distribution sites and other services often exposes beneficiaries to the risks of being robbed or violated. This is worsened when the beneficiaries have to walk through bushy areas that are not inhabited by anybody or conflict zones. SWDC will avoid such by bringing the services closer to the people. In the case when a survivor is referred for further assistance either to other SWDC centres or other service providers, the organization will provide the transport to reach the next point of assistance. Distributions will be planned in consultation with the beneficiaries to agree on appropriate date and site for the distribution. The distribution will be done at a place close to the beneficiaries homes and the persons with disabilities and elderlies who cannot carry their entitlements will be provided with a means of transport.
