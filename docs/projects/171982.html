Project Summary

The protracted conflict in eastern Ukraine has a devastating impact on the health and well-being of the affected population showing signs of long-term consequences. The conflict has isolated rural health facilities in Government-Controlled Areas (GCA) from major health centers located in urban areas in Non-Government Controlled Areas (NGCA). Of the approximately 3.3 million people living within the 20 kilometres of the contact line, 38 per cent of households in the 20 km area along the ‘contact line’ and 57 per cent of households in the 5 km area along the ‘contact line’ (GCA) reported having problems accessing healthcare and require life-saving and essential health services. The insecurity, lack of maintenance of outdated health facilities and medical equipment, shortage of medicines and medical supplies, understaffing and curtailed access to referral hospitals and pharmacies undermine the health care system’s ability to cope with both new and pre-existing health care needs.
The ongoing process of health care reform in Ukraine impacts the access of the population to essential medical services on both sides of the ‘contact line.’
The lack of access to primary, secondary and tertiary health care services particularly affects vulnerable populations such as women, children, elderly, people with disabilities, those living in isolated settlements. This is further compounded by the lack of qualified medical staff and brain drain. Those who remained are confronted with the significant challenges to access quality professional education and continuous training. Health care workers are also exposed to traumatic events and are being overlooked by the health system and humanitarian organizations.
A pandemic of COVID-19 has seriously affected the health conditions of the population in both GCA and NGCA directly and indirectly. People observed
received additional stressing factors: fear of getting infected with unknown pathogen; many controversial information about the disease; strict isolation measures introduced by authorities; closure of health care facilities to reserve beds for COVID patients, and many others. Health services received additional burden that seriously affected health workers as well as the organization in a whole. Growing numbers of new cases of infection and deaths of COVID, shortage of laboratory capacities, test systems, personal protection equipment, oxygen supply, and other vital equipment as well as lack of the knowledge and practice in treating patients are keeping health care workers in a very stressed condition for months.
It is difficult to ensure quality management of the healthcare system, since standardized protocols and operational procedures, based on the globally accepted guidelines are not being widely deployed or even tailored to the local context. Patient safety is also a major source for concern, given that little standardization occurs in the management of diseases, infection prevention and control measures, administration of the healthcare facilities. Essential medicines, renewables, equipment, and core medical technologies are also lacking.
To respond to the humanitarian needs, WHO will continue implementing the following key activities, which are based on the strong cooperation with health authorities:

2. Support to Public Health sector through the capacity building of the laboratories with a primary focus on improvement of detection of COVID-19
3. Reinforcing technical capacity of health care workers in mental health and psychosocial support

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in eastern Ukraine has a devastating impact on the health and well-being of the affected population showings signs of long-term consequences. Of the approximately 3.3 million people living within the 20 kilometres of the contact line, 38 per cent of households in the 20 km area along the ‘contact line’ and 57 per cent of households in the 5 km area along the ‘contact line’ (GCA) reported having problems accessing healthcare and require life-saving and essential health services. The decentralization of the healthcare system in GCA has the potential to further exacerbate the difficulties to access health care services.Hostilities and persisting security threats although decreasing during the end of 2020, are still remaining as barriers for isolated populations to access essential and specialized health care services at primary, secondary and tertiary levels. This lack of access to health care particularly affects vulnerable populations such as women, children, elderly, people with disabilities, those living in isolated settlements. Lack of evidence-based health data, equipment, essential medicines, trained staff, standardized treatment protocols and insufficient infrastructure for trauma care and emergency medical services are especially critical in eastern Ukraine. Conflict-affected individuals, families, and communities experience an intensified need in the mental health and psycho-social support services, whereas the public medical infrastructure has become more limited. Physiological signs of distress (70%) and emotional difficulties (58%) are most prevalent within 0-20 km of the ‘line of contact. Quality, evidence-based MHPSS at the community level are largely unavailable, which is in part due to the low level of integration of MH in community-based supports (i.e., PHC) with health care providers being trained in early identification and prevention of MHPSS conditions and a low-functioning referral system. People with moderate to severe MHPSS conditions face difficulties in accessing focused-psychosocial support, specialized MH services and medications. TB and HIV/AIDS has been an issue in Donetska and Luhanska Oblasts, even before the conflict. These two oblasts were among the most affected in Ukraine by HIV and TB, especially multidrug-resistant TB. There is a high dependence on international aid for HIV and TB treatment in both GCA and NGCAs. An estimated 35 000 people are living with HIV in NGCAs, but only 16 000 know their status and are followed up in health settings. Each year more than 1500 new TB cases are registered in Donetsk NGCA, of which more than 30 percent of cases are multidrug-resistant TB.
