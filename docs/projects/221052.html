Project Summary

Twelve years after independence, South Sudan continues to face chronic underdevelopment and the impact of multiple, interlocking crisis drivers. Whilst the South Sudan conflict and violence continue to cause insecurity and displacement, the Sudan crisis has led to a significant influx of almost 794,000 arrivals in South Sudan by mid-June, straining already limited services at border points and destination areas. The great challenge in the State is now short-term humanitarian support to the returnees who reached their home areas and intend to resettle.
This crisis has undoubtedly exacerbated the education situation in Upper Nile State. As the State continues to grapple with the burden of bringing more than 120,000 out of school children particularly girls and the marginalized back to school, with the returnees arriving into a weak education system including un-conducive learning environment, lack of qualified teachers, inadequate teaching and learning materials and a different medium of instruction than what is used in Sudan
The provision of safe and protective education in emergency (EiE) services for the children of South Sudanese host communities & returnees and the Sudanese Refugees in areas of Manyo, Fashoda and Malakal Counties of Upper Nile State, scheduled from January 1, 2025, to December 31, 2025. The program, with a total budget of $649,819 aiming to address educational challenges exacerbated by conflict, displacement, and natural disasters
The program targets various vulnerable groups, including children with disabilities, IDPs, and returnees, aiming to improve access to quality education. The work plan includes measurable targets such as community mobilization, training workshops, and the establishment of integrated ECE services
A. a total of 8,102 individual will be targeted including children, adolescents and youth 3-17 years old are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle (in Fashoda = 1,807 Male – 1,150F, in Malakal = 342 Male – 373 Female, in Manyo = 2,544 Male - 1,696 Female) respectively. 15 temporary learning spaces (2 TLS in Fashoda, 2 TLS in Malakal and 4 TLS in Manyo) established/rehabilitated in line with INEE's Minimum. 20 gender sensitive (3 in Fashoda, 3 in Malakal and 5 in Manyo) WASH facilities built or rehabilitated in line with MoGEI/Education Cluster. 176 (41 Male – 41 Female) in Fashoda, (27 Male – 27 Female) in Malakal, (24 Male – 16 Female) in Manyo Counties respectively trained on EiE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills, a total of 7,612 children and Youth between 6-17 years old in Fashoda (2,000 Male – 1,250 Female), in Malakal (250 Male – 280 Female), and in Manyo (2,299 Male -1,533 Female) respectively benefited from distribution of learning materials, 1,806 Children (3-5 years old) in Manyo County (245 boys – 163 girls), Fashoda (661 boys – 441 girls) and Malakal (177 boys – 118 girls) benefited from distribution of ECD kits and 15 school management committees (SMC) members, Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and education authorities (4M – 3F) in Fashoda County, (3M-2F) in Malakal County and 2M-1F) in Manyo County trained on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)
The program also emphasizes gender equality and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Overall, the program seeks to enhance the education sector's capacity to deliver inclusive and quality education, particularly in emergency contexts, while ensuring community involvement and sustainability
