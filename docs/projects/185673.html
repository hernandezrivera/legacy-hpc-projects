Project Summary

The aim of the project is to strengthen disaster resilience among vulnerable Rohingya refugees and host-communities in Cox’s Bazar through effective multi-hazard risk management. The project will target both Rohingya refugees (55.03%) and host-communities (44.97%) in Cox’s Bazar. The Project will work in close collaboration with ISCG Sectors, Local Government and other relevant stakeholders to support ISCG's overall disaster risk management strategy through: i) localized weather forecasting, landslide early warning and monsoon preparedness; ii) multi-hazard risk assessment, preparedness and response planning; iii) community-based landslide and flash flood risk reduction, including implementation of natural hazard mitigation and nature-based solutions in vulnerable camps and host communities; iii) facilitate and support multi-hazard DRM analysis, plans and products for local government and ISCG (SMSD, EPRWG, Shelter, CwCWG); iv) strengthened community capacity for inclusive DRR and preparedness in host communities and camps; v) synergy and coordination of DRR initiatives in the host communities and camps. The project targets the most vulnerable Rohingya refugees (55.03%) and host-communities (44.97%), especially women (51.62%), children (7.19%), elderly (3.61%), persons with disabilities (0.4%), LGBT groups, and ethnic communities.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar is one of the most disaster-prone districts in Bangladesh and is highly exposed to natural hazards and extreme weather impacts including cyclones, landslides, flash floods, storm surges and extreme temperatures. The district is exposed to frequent cyclones in the recent years such as Cyclone YAAS (2021), Cyclones Amphan (2020), Bulbul (2019), Mora (2017), Roanu (2016) and Komen (2015). Cox’s Bazar district also remains amongst the poorest districts in Bangladesh and has long struggled with food security, livelihood opportunities, insufficient water and sanitation facilities, weak infrastructure, poor health and education indicators.In addition, Cox’s Bazar is now home to around 1 million Rohingya refugees since 2017. The Rohingya crisis has increased the size of the population at risks, as well as added pressures on environment which undermine resilience. Due to congestion, typography, and temporary shelter structures, Rohingya households are highly impacted by severe natural hazards and weather events. Between January to September 2021, weather events such as windstorms, heavy rains, slope failure and flooding have impacted 206,820 individuals (44,190 households), displaced 29,292 individuals, and killed 35 individuals in the camps (SMSD incident report, 18 Sept’21). Even in the absence of a major disaster, this climatic context has considerable impact on the refugee response operation in Cox’s Bazar. This year has also seen increasing number of natural hazards and weather-related incidents in camps and hosting unions. For instance, on 22 March 2021, a massive fire broke out in Kutupalong Balukhali which destroyed and damaged 12,000 shelters, injured 563 individuals, killed 13 individuals, and affected 61,000 Rohingya refugees and host communities. Moreover, between 26 and 27 July 2021, a heavy monsoon rain triggered landslides and flooding which affected 21,606 refugees, displaced 13,432 refugees, and killed 6 refugees in camps (ISCG, 01 Aug’21). The women, girls, children, elderly, persons with disabilities, and other minorities were particularly affected to these natural hazards and extreme weather events in Rohingya camps and host communities.The Local Government, first response agencies and volunteers lack adequate capacity to effectively prepare for and respond to extreme weather events and disasters. According to the recent DRR KAP Survey (Sept 2020), only 28% of the host communities and 58% of the refugees reported to have enough information about what to do if a cyclone occurs. 67% respondents from both the host communities and refugees also reported that there are people who need extra help or are at greater risk before and during a cyclone. The project will aim to improve localized weather forecasting, landslide early warning and strengthen community capacity for disaster risk reduction, preparedness and response incorporating special needs of the extremely vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities, children, elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers.On the development side, while Government has been investing in various social safety net programs, public works and local infrastructure development, these are not risk informed. The project will enhance multi-hazard risk assessment and planning, capacity building of local government and disaster management committees for integration of DRR in public works and development interventions. Project will also demonstrate landslide and flash flood risk mitigation and nature-based solutions in vulnerable camps and host-communities.Project contributes to mitigate potential tensions by targeting both Rohingya refugees and host-communities. Project will provide DRR technical support and products to government and ISCG (SMSD, EPRWG, Shelter, CwCWG) ensuring localized, gender-sensitive and coordinated advice and efforts. Project will improve synergies and coordination of DRR initiatives in host community and camps.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Development of localized weather-forecasting and products with Bangladesh Meteorological Department for improved monsoon preparedness, contingency, and response planning by humanitarian stakeholders;2. Development of a landslide early warning system through the installation of automated rainfall gauges in the camps;3. Support Community Risk Assessment, Disaster Management and Contingency Planning for vulnerable hosting Unions and Upazilas, including the inclusion of the needs and capacities of the most excluded groups in these DRM planning;4. Support testing Contingency Plans of hosting Unions and Upazilas through simulation exercises 5. Implementation of community-based disaster reduction activities in the Rohingya camps and host-communities at risk of landslide and flash floods, including implementation of natural hazard mitigation and nature-based solutions through SMS partner/NGO;6.
