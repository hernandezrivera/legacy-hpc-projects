Project Summary

The objectives and purpose of WVI’s SNFI intervention are to:
1. Provide lifesaving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support.
2. Reinforce an enabling protection environment and the cohesion of communities by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.
The intervention will include the following activities:
1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs (e.g. mattress/mat, plastic sheet, blankets, jerry can, kitchen set, solar lamps; including cash/voucher for these items) for 36,000 individuals (6,000 core NFI kits, 6,000 kitchen kits).
1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (e.g. winter clothing, fuel, winter-speciﬁc shelter upgrades; including cash/voucher for these items) for 30,000 individuals (5,000 units of fuel, 5,000 blanket and mattress kits, 5,000 adult winter clothing and 5,000 children winter clothing).
1.3 Provision of emergency shelter (e.g. tent installation in camps, transit sites, and spontaneous settlements with associated infrastructure, distribution of emergency shelter materials and kits; rental assistance, including cash/voucher for these items) for 9,000 individuals (1.500 tent installations, 1,500 shelter kits, 6,000 cash grants).
1.4 Rehabilitation of collective centres and transitional shelters (including in-kind, cash, voucher, physical repair, etc.) for 1,770 individuals (259 HHs).
2.1 Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/returnees/host families to 7,545 individuals. (1,257 HHs)
2.2 Training of stakeholders on resilience and quality-oriented shelter / NFI skills and capacities
These activities will target directly households, collective centers, and camps in districts A'zaz, and Idleb.
Overall, apart from 1.2.2 which provides specific clothing, the initiatives target households and does not specifically target any demographic. 13,656 households will be offered assistance which will include 13,656 men, 13,656 women, and 27,312 girls, and 27,312 boys.

4 - Needs

In north-west Syria, there are critical levels of overcrowding, putting signiﬁcant pressure on overburdened communities, with few if any remaining shelter options available.Overcrowding exacerbates the risk of personal safety and protection especially for females. The living conditions of households in Syria may further deteriorate if higher quality, integrated and more sustainable shelter solutions cannot be implemented due to lack of funding and approval. Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute resulting in signiﬁcant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being and protection of affected populations. Female headed households are at greater risk of protection and shelter vulnerability one reason being the likelihood of having lower incomes meaning they are less likely to be in adequate shelters. Overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with an increase in PiN from 2019 by 20 per cent to 5.65 million people. From 1-21 January 2020, FAST conducted two rapid need assessment to capture the situation of IDPs in Ariha, Idleb, Harim, and Jebel Samaan districts. Overall, shelter has been described by all KII’s as one of the most urgent needs for newly displaced individuals in all locations. 86% of respondents prioritized cash to pay for rent and the same 86% stated that they have not yet received any shelter assistance. In CARE’s RNA, only 9% of affected individuals reported that they were living in an appropriate shelter, one free from overcrowding and protective from weather conditions. The vast majority of shelters are being shared by three families and half of all new IDP’s are living in tents. The most urgent shelter interventions are: heating, shelter replacement and rehabilitation, and electricity. The most urgent NFI interventions are: cash assistance, fuel for heating, winter kits, children’s and adult’s clothes, and cooking materials

5 - Implementation of activities

For the provision of core and essential NFIs (1.1.1), new arrival kits and kitchen kits will be delivered to new IDPs within our target region. WVI will support IDPs who need protection from adverse weather through the provision of winter shelter kits, SOKs, and materials (1.2.1 & 1.2.2). WVI will ensure that households are provided with support through the installation of tents and surfacing (1.3.2) and that vulnerable population groups including female heads of household, the elderly, and disabled individuals are given additional support. WVI will rehabilitate collective centres and damaged HHs (1.4.1 & 2.1.1) to ensure that affected individuals have access to temporary shelter needs. All necessary activities will undergo a rigorous due diligence process which will include background checks for staff, contractors and landlords, approvals of property rights and tenure and signed MoUs with landlords and local councils prior any rehabilitation works. WVI will provide training for stakeholders on resilience, shelter, NFI, and related capacities. Selection and validation tools will identify target populations and their priority needs. Beneﬁciary lists will be coordinated with local councils. Winterization kits will be to the SNFI sector standards. Targets groups will include informal settlements and camps. All projects will be directly implemented through WVI staff who have direct experience from 3 years of undertaking shelter and NFI projects in Syria. Staff have been hired from target communities in Syria, this builds better local acceptance and increases WVIs contextual understanding. WVI will have a full warehousing system to ensure all NFIs are stored to the correct standards and accurately monitored. For the rehabilitation of damaged houses: WVI will continue to build upon its previous work and implement HH shelter rehabilitation projects, expanding towards A'zaz countryside.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

For the provision of core and essential NFI’s and the provision of emergency shelter,- inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members; - abuse of authority by distribution actors; risk of physical harm at distribution points; - a lack of accepted documentation unfairly excluding certain populations; - GBV or sexual exploitation within crowded living conditions The likelihood and impact of each risk tends to be high. As such, appropriate mitigating measures are: - ensure that distribution is based on needs and that selection criteria is transparent; - instruct distribution partners to engage with communities; - make sector partners aware of the code of conduct and ensure they have the measures in place to identify staff who are acting inappropriately; - provide access points for individuals with specific needs; - inform people of their rights For the rehabilitation of collective centres, the sustainable repair and rehabilitation of housing- increased risk of inter or intra-community tension due to varied assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria, or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination; - GBV or sexual exploitation within crowded living conditions, areas lacking lighting, and gender segregated sanitation facilities; - Eviction from camps/transit sites/spontaneous settlement due to lack of approval from property owner/conflict in property ownership; - exclusion from shelter assistance due to lack of civil status document/ID. The likelihood and impact of these risks tends to be high. As such, appropriate mitigating measures include: - ensure beneficiary selection criteria is uniform and widely agreed upon and clearly communicated to the community; - establish complaint mechanisms; provide training to humanitarian staff; - provide home delivery;

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: WVI is not able to mitigate risks around shelling and airstrikes. However, WVI will assess the capacity of partners and advise appropriately - Limited monitoring by WVI’s 3PM. - Changes in the areas of control - Temporary closure of borders for shipments either Turkey or within Syria. - Potential diversion of supplies in areas under the control of armed groups. - Damage of supplies due to transport - Climate conditions may exasperate ﬂooding or diﬃcult road conditions. - Where local procurement is the only option, markets can become depleted Mitigation Measures: - WVI has a vast experience in delivering services and supplies in conﬂict situations, - WVI’s partners are equipped with warehouses in strategic locations and have strategic partnership with local councils. - Partners will provide monitoring plans with speciﬁc operational context. - Third party monitoring visits will monitor the progress. - Implementing partners will provide situational feedback. - WVI’s partners are advised to closely monitor utilization of supplies and to monitor what is out of stock in local markets. - WVI and its partners carry out continuous security assessments drawing from multiple security sources including OCHA and has a dedicated security and context analysis team. - WVI will advise its partners to train ﬁeld staff in security awareness and management. - Partner staff “lock down”, restricted movements or suspension of activities can be required in times of conﬂict escalation. - Partners will liaise with local councils and obtain all necessary approvals prior to commencement of activities. Where possible WVI will seek to have LC presence at certain activities for additional monitoring. – WVI will ensure all civil documentation is in place such as ownership contracts prior to starting rehabilitation works. - Rehabilitation of housing and collective centers will aim at reducing overcrowding lowering risk of GBV and protection concerns.
