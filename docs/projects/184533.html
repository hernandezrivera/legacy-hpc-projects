Project Summary

The project will ensure that vulnerable communities, IDP and non IDPs incl. people with disabilities, get access to safe, timely, dignified, and coordinated GBV and CP specialized services through integrated referrals and case management.
The proposed project will provide survivor-centered multi-sectorial support and protection services through the establishment of a safe house in Erigavo which will strengthen response services and provide timely access to life-saving services for GBV and CP survivors in the eastern regions of Somaliland.
WAAPO currently operates the only existing safe houses in Somaliland with presence in Haregisa, Borama, and Burao. However, due to the referral case load reported by WAAPO staff located in Sool and Sanaag, owing to clan conflicts, distances and displacement, it is crucial to establish a safe house.
Survivors admitted into the Safe House will be provided with safety and security with dignity and wellbeing, commendation and meals along with PSS, psychosocial counselling, psychological first aid, case management, and medical and legal assistance through referrals.
Moreover, the safe house will also admit FGM/C complication cases, adolescents, vulnerable female migrants (Human trafficking in person), and survivors of SEA incl. people with disabilities. Additionally, The project will set-up three compassionate survivor-centered care and Inter-agency counseling desks in LasAnod Ceynaba, and Buuhoodle to increase access to routine PSS, MHPSS, counseling and case management.
The proposed project will support mobile and remote GBV service delivery to crisis-affected locations through mobile unit protection team to conduct timely identification of survivors of GBV, CP, SEA. The mobile unit will provide PSS, safe, dignified and meaningful access to protection and response services such as first-line support, case management, medical-legal assistance, and specialized services such as MHPSS through referral to WAAPO Safe Houses, MESAF, MOJ, Baahikoob, and other GBV-Actors in Somaliland. Additionally, the mobile unit will facilitate protection monitoring to identify trends and patterns of violations of rights to inform WAAPO’s programming.
The project will also increase access to FGM/C response services by offering critical care and timely responses such as medical assistance, counseling and psychological support, and safe house if needed to alleviate the associated FGM/C complications.
WAAPO will conduct public awareness-raising, advocacy and campaign on SGBV and CP prevention, response, and mitigation to reduce the risk of SGBV, improve the capacity and knowledge of the local communities to address the various root causes incl. gender inequality and discrimination, and to create community dialogues around harmful social norms and practices.
The capacity building workshops and trainings will also be conducted for the WAAPO staff, CP and GBV actors, national actors, and CBCP among the camp committees to increase the knowledge and skills in providing inclusive, non-discriminatory, age- and gender- sensitive GBV, SEA, CP mitigation, coordination, response strategies for vulnerable communities, incl. IDPs and host communities.
The project will establish Child-Friendly Spaces (CFSs) in Erigavo and Burao districts and will innovate mobile outreach actives that will be conducted home visits to deliver CP-Service delivery, MHPSS and PSS to children with severe signs of distress and children with disablities and to support caregivers and parents. The proposed project will provide Case management & (FTR) through an Individual case management system that meets each child’s unique needs including FTR services, family strengthening programs, and family-based alternative care arrangements (preferably community-based care) to ensure children separated from their caregivers receive care and support. Additionally, the project will mobilize trainings on positive and protective parenting for the caregivers.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

o WAAPO will ensure differential needs, concerns, and priorities for individuals from minority groups through the application of the AGDM approach; and will maintain beneficiary selection tailored fair beneficiary disaggregate data through participatory approach, vulnerability assessment, outreach missions, mediate power of dynamic, accountability mechanism system. WAAPO will empower individuals from minority groups by promoting their rights, building their capacities, and providing them with concrete platforms to be actively engaged and serve as role models to their communities. WAAPO will identify community volunteers (outreach volunteers and paralegals) with a strong personal commitment to end discrimination against minority groups to participate in the activities and empower them through building their capacities and promoting the values and spirit of volunteerism.Another group of people that are usually excluded from humanitarian assistances and projects are people with disabilities. WAAPO works closely with the Disability department under the MESAF to provide services that cater to the specific needs of people with disability in Somaliland. Additionally, WAAPO closely collaborates with key disability organizations such as Disability Action Network (DAN) to ensure that the best interest of PWC are maintained in WAAPO programing. Regarding the prevention of abuse that people with disability might encounter, WAAPO operates a hotline and help desk to provide information and for any incident to be reported immediately. Currently, WAAPO has a safe house/shelter for gender based violence victims and WAAPO is in the process of constructing rams to make the spaces more accessible for those using wheelchairs. o This risk will be mitigated by enforcing a policy of zero tolerance for sexual misconduct. In 2020 UNICEF Somali has conducted a PSEA Capacity Assessment exercise to WAAPO (Find attached is the report) to identify PSEA organizational capacity gaps and relevant and organizational practice. Women Action for Advocacy and Progress Organization scored 13 points in the assessment, which indicates that WAAPO’s PSEA organizational capacities need improvement and their SEA risks are moderate. The organization has hired a consultant and we have developed PSEA SOP, gender policy, PSEA training material, communication material, and revised all the contracts and staff code of conduct. The organization is also running a hotline and safe house that provide assistance and support to survivors of SEA - referral and reporting tools and forms is also available in both local languages. The organization has also received almost 5-workshops led by the attached UNICEF consultant.All the WAAPO staff incl. SWs, CWs and outreach workers attended the E-Learning PSEA program shared by UNHCR and UNICEF and obtained their certificates. The all-sub-contract from WAAPO is also applied to ensure zero tolerance of the SEA and WAAPO drivers and security guards were also oriented, their contract 5/9 was also translated into Somali. WAAPO nominated and trained SEA investigators and provide their roles and responsibility. All WAAPO program activities will obliged to create an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Whenever an incident is reported indicating sexual exploitation and sexual abuse, or any serious breach of the code of conducts, WAAPO will to report the incident to UNOCHA without delay. To ensure proper reporting, WAAPO will put compliance desk in the protection desk and will sensitize communities to report through the established hotline and child help-line. All the WAAPO employees signed a code of conduct that outlined their responsibility to report any SEA related incident.
