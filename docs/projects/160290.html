Project Summary

Grassroots Relief and Development Agency (GREDA) intends to implement sustainable agriculture, fisheries, livestock and honey processing project for sustainable economic recovery and resilience in Western Equatoria State targeting IDPs/returnees in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties because these counties were badly affected by the conflict that erupted in Juba in 2013 and 2016 respectively. The targeted three (3) counties are currently hosting high numbers of IDPs and returnees. In accordance with Mid-2019 population (NBS) report/records the populations of the three (3) targeted counties are as follows:-
(1) Mvolo County Total population is 71,586 (14,000 people are at crisis level and 7,000 people at emergency level). 21,000 people are critically food insecure
(2) Mundri East County total population is 95,873 (29,000 people are at crisis level and 14,000 people at emergency level). 43,000 people are critically food insecure
(3) Mundri West County total population is 46,760 (16,000 people are at crisis level and 5,000 people at emergency level). 21,000 people are critically food insecure.
The average of 60% population in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties are farmers (FAO 2016). The crops grown successfully in these targeted counties include maize, sorghum, beans, pumpkins, okra, tomatoes, groundnuts, and sim-sim. The counties fall within the Green Belt in Western Equatoria State include some of the most fertile lands in the country and farmers in the area can reap two or three harvests per annum from the same plots. In November 2015, Western Equatoria State as a whole experienced one of its worst crop harvests in recent history. The level of food insecurity reached was historical lowest in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties that one person out of four being considered food insecure. The livelihoods inputs/equipment in the conflict affected parts of Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties were looted in several Payams including Bangolo, Medewu, Yeri, Lessi, Dari, Kotobi, Lui, Lozoh Witto Kediba, and Amadi Payams. The signing of the revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCISS) in September 2018 has relatively brought peace in South Sudan including Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties. The IDPs and returnees are returning in their original home land. Unfortunately they lost all their agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing inputs/equipment. There is high need to supply them with agricultural tools, improved seeds, and seed processing equipment, fisheries kits, veterinary drugs, cold chain, and honey processing equipment, sun-drying racks, and solar driers, smoking ovens, post-harvest technology trainings on food packing, handling, hygiene and storage to empower 10750 IDPs/returnees and host communities to produce their own food to reduce malnutrition, hunger and gain strength and resiliency against macroeconomic crisis, climatic shocks and humanitarian access challenges

1 - Needs

In January 2019, an estimated 6.17 million people in South Sudan faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity or worse, with an estimated 30,000 people classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). In the projection period of February-April 2019, an estimated 6.45 million people would face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity or worse, with an estimated 45,000 people classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). This is expected to increase to an estimated 6.87 million people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity or worse at the peak of the lean season (May-July 2019), with an estimated 50,000 people classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). Food insecurity is largely driven by conflict and related displacement, low crop production and ongoing macro-economic crisis, climatic shocks and humanitarian access challenges. In particular focusing on Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties malnutrition and hunger has become the primary causes of many deaths among the IDPs/returnees and host communities in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties in Western Equatoria State. In accordance with Mid-2019 population (NBS) report/records the populations of the three (3) targeted counties are reported/recorded as follows:-(1) Mvolo County Total population is 71,586 (14,000 people are at crisis level and 7,000 people are at emergency level). 21,000 people are critically food insecure.(2) Mundri East County total population is 95,873 (29,000 people are at crisis level and 14,000 people are at emergency level). 43,000 people are critically food insecure.(3) Mundri West County total population is 46,760 (16,000 people are at crisis level and 5,000 people are at emergency level). 21,000 people are critically food insecure.Lactating/pregnant mothers, children, old aged people and people with disability are vulnerable groups severely affected by food insecurity. There is insufficient distribution of inputs, tools and equipment for IDPs/returnees and host communities to implement livelihood projects. They lack agricultural tools, improved seeds, vegetable irrigation cans, seed processing equipment, fishing kits and veterinary supplies. They also lack post-harvest equipment, tools and inputs such as grinding mills, peanut butter machines, cold chain, solar driers, and honey processing equipment, Sun-drying racks, smoking ovens, milk processing equipment in Mvolo, Mundri East, and Mundri West Counties. The supply of agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing inputs will help beneficiaries to produce their own food and they will gain strength and resiliency against macroeconomic crisis, climatic shocks and humanitarian access challenges. Therefore; this project seeks to supply agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing inputs and equipment to enable 10750 IDPs, returnees and host communities to produce their own balanced-diet food to become healthy, self-reliant and improved their economic well-being. To ensure sustainability and market-food-value the beneficiaries will also be trained through field mobile trainings to improve skills of beneficiaries in modern food production technologies i.e. food production, processing, packing, handling, hygiene and storage systems to enhance availability of households/market food supply throughout the year.

2 - Activities

To ensure successful implementation of this project the following activities are essentially important for the implementation of this project and the activities are as follows:-1. Recruit qualified 3 agricultural officers, 3 fishery officers, 3 livestock officers and 3 Bee-keeping officers as field coordinators (one officer per county). 2. Carryout community based participatory planning mobilization to assess beneficiaries’ critical needs, priorities and choices through analyzing technical, financial, environmental and social feasibility to ensure beneficiaries understand the project and take ownership of the project to enhance successful implementation and sustainability of the project in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties3. Procure and distribute assorted agricultural tool, improved seeds, and new genetic crops, vegetable irrigation cans to 3050 beneficiaries in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties.4. Procure and distribute fishing kits to 750 beneficiaries’ fisher folks in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties. 5. GREDA animal health workers to vaccine and carryout treatment to over 10,000 livestock belonging to 1200 beneficiaries who are unable to access veterinary drugs to their livestock in Mvolo,Mundri East and West Counties6. Procure and distribute post-harvest equipment, tools and inputs (grinding mills, peanut butter machines, cold chains, solar driers, honey processing equipment, sun-dry-racks, smoking oven, milk processing equipment targeting 4250 beneficiaries in Mundri East,Mundri West and Mvolo counties.7. Conduct mobile field trainings to the 10750 beneficiaries to foster peaceful co-existence, good governance and accountability among the beneficiaries, improve business skills of the beneficiaries in modern food producing, processing, handling, hygiene and storage and also to enable the beneficiaries to form agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing groups to enable them work together to produce outputs of economic scale that will be sufficient to ensure availability of food supply for households’ consumption and marketing throughout the year; to enhance better health and beneficiaries economic well-being in Mvolo,Mundri East and Mundri Counties.8. Conduct continuous project impact monitoring and evaluations targeting 10750 beneficiaries.The project will enhance formation of aggro-based dealers, fisheries, engage veterinary drugs for livestock vaccination and treatment and training on utilization, operation and maintenance of equipment and tools and intensive technical, hygiene and food safety and different trainings targeting entire beneficiaries 10,750 IDPs/returnees and host communities.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

This project is seeking to provided livelihood inputs to the beneficiaries to have rights to produce their own food in order to have abundant food in their households to become self-reliant. Since the targeted livelihood inputs will be procured and distributed to the beneficiaries as their inputs; their rights are 100% legally recognized. Thus; beneficiaries rights in this project are recognized and their voice will be heard because they are the owners of this project and they will be fully included in the project decision-making and implementation.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The targeted internal displaced persons/returnees and host communities lost all of their livelihoods inputs through the persistent conflict that erupted in South Sudan in December 2013 and July 2016The Revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) signed on 12th September 2018 ended the conflict and provided the opportunity for peaceful resettlement. Now the population in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties that were affected by the displacement are returning for resettlement in their original home-lands. They are in dire needs of livelihood inputs that will enable them resettle to produce their own food to become self-reliant. This project will provide adequate livelihood inputs that beneficiaries will receive/access as start-up means to enable them resettle to produce their own food to regain strength and resiliency against macroeconomic crisis, climatic shocks and humanitarian access challenges.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is designed to be implemented by the 10750 IDPs/returnees and host communities in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties to enable the beneficiaries to take the ownership of this project to become their own project to enhance long-term sustainability.The project is linked with the government because the line Ministry such as Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Animal Wealth will provide moral and technical support throughout the implementation period of this project. The trainers for agricultural modern technologies, fisheries, livestock and honey processing will come from the Ministry mentioned above.Therefore; the project is linked to both government and community systems and will have great impact in the community for sustainability, since the community are the direct beneficiaries of this project. Government on other hand will provide training guidelines and security for the project and beneficiaries.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with the national objectives because the project aims to increase food security, reduce malnutrition and hunger that has become the primary causes of many deaths of vulnerable people in South Sudan; most of whom are lactating/pregnant mothers, children, old aged persons and persons with disabilities.Most importantly this project aims to provide agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing inputs (agricultural tools, seeds, fishing kits, veterinary supplies/drugs and honey processing equipment) to IDPs/returnees and host communities who lost their livelihoods inputs through the persistent conflict in South Sudan 2013/2016. The project will provide livelihood inputs to the IDP/returnees and host communities beneficiaries to enable them to resettle to produce their own food to become self-reliant, reduce malnutrition, hunger and poverty among the direct beneficiaries of this project.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

In 2017 GREDA received funding from Al Khair Foundation (AKF) UK and procured 500 metric tons of assorted humanitarian relief food assistance (maize, beans, oil and salt) from Uganda and distributed these foods for lifesaving to internal displaced persons (IDPs) displaced from Kediba the Headquarters of Mundri East County who were settled in displaced camps in Lui and Mundri West County. Since that time GREDA maintained field office in Mundri West County with strong visibility presence in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties.Hence; since the signing of the Revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) in September 12th, 2018 the hostilities between government forces and SPLM/A-IO in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties got stopped. The environments for the implementation of this project are safe and stable. At the moment the opportunity created by peaceful co-existence between government forces and SPLM/A-IO will allow the activities of this project to proceed with no interruption. As GREDA; we had worked in the three targeted counties since 2017 and we are conversant with the communities in Mvolo,Mundri East and West Counties.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The livelihoods inputs to be provided by this project to the IDPs/returnees and host communities were intended to provide start-up means to enable the direct beneficiaries to produce their own food by themselves. The project is designed and structured to be owned by the direct beneficiaries. The beneficiaries will be trained on modern agricultural food production techniques, fish catching methodologies, livestock vaccination and treatment, livestock product processing, bee-keeping and honey product processing techniques/methodologies. These trainings in agriculture, fishing, livestock and honey processing will enable the beneficiaries to know the modern techniques/methodologies of food production to enable them take the charge of expanding the project in future and to ensure sustainability of the project. Therefore; after expiration of one year duration of this project, the project responsibility will be fully handed to the beneficiaries to continue with the project implementation. However; GREDA will continue providing them technical support and will continue to look for them more donors to help them to expand at large scale.
