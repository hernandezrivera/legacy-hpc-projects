Project Summary

DRC's objective is to prevent and respond to protection threats identified in camps 6, 10, 8W, 8E, 11, 12, 26, 27, Palongkhali, and Rajapalong host communities. DRC will implement General Protection services, including case management, provision of Individual Protection Assistance, legal activities, protection information and support, community-based protection/ community engagement, protection monitoring, awareness raising, capacity building in camps 6, 8E, 8W, 10, 11, 12, 26, and 27 with a focus on avoiding service overlaps. The project aims to enhance synergies by integrating with other protection and non-protection actors, providing case management services, and making individual protection assistance available wherever possible and appropriate.
DRC will facilitate community-led initiatives, awareness and sensitization programs, Individual case management, protection information and support, referrals and follow-ups, protection monitoring, and community engagement. These efforts will strengthen individual and community groups to better detect, prevent, and respond to reduce the protection risks. Additionally, given the increasing trends of new arrivals after the worsening situation in Myanmar from the beginning of this year, DRC will ensure support through protection programming.
DRC will conduct disaggregated age, gender, and diversity analysis to inform and enhance its programming response across all protection initiatives. The organization will expand protection monitoring initiatives to contribute to the Joint Protection Monitoring platform and advocacy efforts. Improved sampling methodologies and enhanced capacity of staff and volunteers will ensure systematic data collection on specific vulnerabilities such as Persons with Disabilities (PwDs), single-headed households, and minorities.
DRC is committed to making all centers accessible to persons with disabilities and setting targets focusing on their needs. Increasing the inclusion of women in the community's volunteer force is also a priority. To avoid duplication of effort, DRC will maintain close communication with existing complaint and feedback procedures, collaborating with the CFM and CFP to ensure clients are not already receiving assistance and forwarding non-protection situations appropriately.
DRC will provide Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training to its employees, with volunteers actively contributing to the network's SEA data. Active participation in the Protection Sector and related sub-sectors is part of DRC's strategy.
DRC plans to increase the frequency of sharing protection monitoring data with the sector and sub-sectors, providing summarized overviews of observed risks in the camps and host communities while complying with routine reporting requirements.
To ensure refugees have access to assistance without harm, DRC will continue providing protection mainstreaming training to non-protection actors, aligning with the resources and guidance provided by the protection sector.
Ongoing coordination efforts with RRRC and CiCs will prioritize timely access and avoid resource gaps and duplications. Regular briefings on DRC's operations and advocacy efforts are shared with CiCs to ensure alignment and sustainability, fostering an integrated approach with other actors and partners.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Based on recent ISNA 2024 findings, DRC protection teams, including protection monitoring, community-based protection, and case management in camps and host communities, have observed an alarming rise in protection challenges and risks. Refugee households expressed greater concern about protection risks than host community households, with refugees identifying crime, violence, and potential affiliation with organized groups as primary concerns (33%, JMSNA 2023). Gender plays a significant role in shaping these risks. Women and girls face heightened vulnerability to gender-based violence (GBV), including sexual exploitation, harassment, and abuse, particularly due to the unsafe reporting channels and the dominance of organized groups, which further inhibit survivors from seeking help. While reported GBV incidents have decreased from 12.24% in 2023 to 9.53% as of October 2024, this decline is likely attributable to underreporting rather than an actual reduction in cases, given the fear and stigma survivors face. Male refugees, particularly adolescent boys and young men are disproportionately targeted for forced recruitment, abduction, and violent attacks, with abduction/kidnapping reported at 38% and direct threats from organized groups at 17% (ISNA 2024). These dynamics exacerbate psychological distress, with 33% of refugees reporting symptoms such as fearfulness and excessive worry about safety, where women are especially impacted due to their heightened dependency on insecure environments. In addition, violations related to Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) disproportionately affect female-headed households, who face challenges asserting claims and accessing justice due to structural inequalities and male-dominated governance systems in the camps. This adds to their economic marginalization and risks of exploitation. Although HLP violations have decreased slightly from 17.72% in 2023 to 16.3% as of October 2024, these incidents remain a critical concern, particularly for widows and women living without male relatives, who are more vulnerable to dispossession and abuse. The presence of organized groups creates a pervasive atmosphere of fear and insecurity, affecting all genders differently. Women and girls face barriers to mobility and accessing services due to harassment and safety concerns, while men and boys are at greater risk of being forcibly recruited or coerced into participating in criminal activities. Anonymized data from DRC’s case management further reveals an increase in reports of threats, intimidation, neglect, coercion, abduction, physical abuse, and denial of access to services, with women disproportionately affected. Protection monitoring by DRC continues to ensure systematic data collection on safety and security, GBV, population movements, HLP violations, violence against children, and access to humanitarian and protection assistance. Strengthening gender analysis and tailored interventions is crucial to address the unique risks faced by women, men, boys, and girls, ensuring that assistance is inclusive and equitable across all population groups.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

DRC has been strategically integrating protection across various programs within the organization, including CCCM and economic recovery initiatives. This integration extends to encompass Gender-Based Violence (GBV) alongside other critical protection components such as case management, community-based protection, protection monitoring, and economic recovery efforts.DRC protection team is dedicated to ensuring the seamless integration of these aspects into all the aforementioned sectors and at the camp level where DRC operates. This involves conducting thorough needs assessments and delivering comprehensive training sessions for both protection and non-protection staff (such as CCCM and EcRec), collaboration with EcRec. The goal is to enhance awareness and understanding of protection issues across diverse sectors, and in locations where DRC implements a full package such in camp 8W, 8E, 10, 11 and 12.Furthermore, the DRC protection team is actively engaged in producing information management products tailored to the protection sector. These products serve to streamline and optimize the collection and dissemination of crucial protection-related information.To fortify the capacity of sectors at the camp level, the protection focal points receive Training of Trainers (ToT) facilitated by trained protection experts. This approach not only empowers the focal points but also contributes to a cascading effect, fostering a more knowledgeable and capable workforce within the camp-level sectors.In essence, DRC's commitment to mainstreaming protection extends beyond theoretical integration to practical implementation, leveraging assessments, training initiatives, and information management strategies to create a robust and comprehensive approach across diverse programs and at the camp level.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

DRC conduct comprehensive awareness-raising sessions for refugees and local communities, emphasizing the importance of preventing and addressing sexual exploitation and abuse. These sessions focus on recognizing warning signs and identifying appropriate reporting channels.DRC has established clear and confidential reporting systems, enabling individuals to express concerns or report incidents without fear of retaliation. Dedicated response teams, including PSEAH focal points in each camp and host community, ensure swift reporting to the national focal point, who then involves HQ for timely and suitable responses. In cases where necessary, thorough investigations are conducted to ensure accountability.All team members and focal points undergo training on their safeguarding responsibilities. This includes recognizing signs of exploitation, harassment, and abuse, and understanding the necessary steps when such incidents are identified.DRC has developed comprehensive PSEAH guidance and a robust code of conduct, clearly outlining expected behaviors and prohibiting any form of exploitation, harassment, or abuse. These guidelines serve as a transparent framework for all stakeholders.DRC collaborates with the PSEA Network within the country, facilitated by the UN, to ensure compliance with international standards and meaningful information sharing that strengthens the system.DRC's commitment to safeguarding surpasses mere compliance—it is a fundamental principle ingrained in DRC's global operations. Fostering a safe environment is a shared responsibility. Through ongoing training, transparent communication, and proactive measures, we aim to uphold the protection of all involved parties.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Over 15 DRC protection staff are trained in the Nexus Environment Assessment Tool (NEAT+). DRC does NEAT+ analysis during the project design. DRC's approach to incorporate climate actions lies in four areas: a) climate change adaptation, b) mitigation of environmental degradation, c) climate mainstreaming, and d) policy advocacy. DRC is an active Energy and Environment Network (EEN) member. In 2023, DRC established baseline data for its climate footprint through programmatic and operational works and has set an ambitious target to reduce its carbon footprint by 50% by 2030. DRC advances nature-based solutions for landslide risk mitigations, conducts tree plantation, and recycles plastic wastes and other organic waste products. DRC's protection sector works with other sectors to mainstream climate action into protection programming. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) is seamlessly integrated into the activities implemented through community-based protection programming. Furthermore, DRC teams are part of PERU, and the emergency response teams are coordinated by the sector and mobilized in the event of natural hazards. This integration ensures that DRR measures are embedded within community-based protection activities, enhancing the overall resilience and preparedness of the communities served by DRC. The active participation of DRC teams in PERU showcases a proactive approach to emergency response, enabling swift and effective interventions in the face of natural disasters to mitigate their impact on the affected populations.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Youth refugees remain a social group at heightened risk of marginalization and exclusion regarding access to education, employability, resilient livelihoods, civic engagement, and participation in decision-making that affects their lives. Despite youth refugees representing a growing proportion of the total number of persons displaced and affected by conflict and crisis, they are often overlooked, and their specific needs are insufficiently considered when policies and programs are designed. Therefore, increasing protection and enhancing inclusion are at the core of the DRC Youth Approach, which focuses on three main pillars: I. Youth Participation & Engagement II. Youth Focused & Inclusive Programming III. Advocacy and mobilization on the Youth Agenda. DRC is committed to increasingly better accommodating the needs, challenges, and aspirations of young refugees in contextualized, comprehensive, and consistent programming and in the organization itself. Danish Refugee Council (DRC) actively incorporates youth engagement as a transformative component within DRC protection programming. Recognizing the pivotal role of youth in community resilience, DRC strategically includes youth groups throughout the project cycle, ensuring their perceptions and insights are integrated into program design, implementation, and evaluation. This approach strengthens youth capacities and skills in protection, self-organization, and advocacy, equipping them to develop and implement self-protection strategies. Consequently, youth are empowered to act as effective community representatives, engaging with local authorities to advocate for their rights and addressing critical community needs.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

In alignment with Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 5 on gender equality, Danish Refugee Council (DRC) has structured its interventions in Cox's Bazar to empower women and girls, reduce inequalities, and promote inclusive development. Cox's Bazar faces complex social and environmental challenges, particularly in Ukhiya, where restrictive social norms, institutional barriers to service access, and the heightened vulnerability of marginalized areas to natural and human-induced hazards exacerbate socioeconomic difficulties. DRC's initiatives emphasize the inclusion of marginalized groups, gender-responsive programming, and youth skill development. These programs aim to foster active participation of all community members, allowing vulnerable individuals to assert their rights and access essential services. and prioritizes sustainable resource management that adapts to environmental and climate challenges.
