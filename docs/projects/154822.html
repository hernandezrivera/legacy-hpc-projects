Project Summary

The proposed project will provide emergency cash assistance to conflict-affected households in Baiji, Shirqat and Tikrit areas in Salah ad-Din (SAD) governorate through Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA). Human Appeal will concentrate its efforts on the most socio-economically vulnerable neighbourhoods. The priority will be given to areas with returnee and IDP households that are not covered by emergency livelihood programmes nor food security resilience programmes in order to maximise the number of people in need receiving cash assistance to meet their basic needs. MPCA offers people affected by crises the flexibility and dignity to choose how to cover their needs and could be seen as a temporary way for individuals to gain self-esteem and to feel more confident in coping with shocks and stress resulting from the post-conflict situation in the area. Injecting cash at a local level will also boost the economy and foster inhabitants recovery. Human Appeal intends to provide emergency one-off, two month and three month cash transfers to the targeted 10,000 HHs which will allow time for the households to be referred to social protection and safety nets scheme or to other humanitarian actors setting up longer term assistance.

Needs

A vast portion of the population in the target areas in SAD governorate are suffering from lack of resources required for the resumption of normal life, reporting that high levels of unemployment and a lack of job opportunities, combined with a lack of the access to basic food and the hygiene items, are the main causes of vulnerability in the IDP and returnee populations in the governorate. Areas of Ninawa, SAD and Anbar have been controlled by ISIL since 2014. Between late 2013 and 2017, intensification of conflict in north and central Iraq has resulted in large scale displacement, with 1.9 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) across Iraq as of August 2018. Of these, approximately 95,000 to 116,000 IDPs are estimated to be residing in informal sites. As the recent IOM’s DTM shows, SAD is hosting 158,346 IDPs and received 89,158 returnees until October 31st 2018. A major requirement in these conflict-affected areas is to support the affected populations via multi-purpose cash assistance in order to enable these families to meet their basic food and non-food needs, and to respond to the newly displaced IDPs and the returnees to provide immediate assistance. SAD reported returnees facing difficulties in meeting their basic needs, including a lack of livelihoods opportunities, lack of access to food, and lack of or lost savings and assets. Data from the Multi-cluster needs assessment (MCNA) shows that the need for MPCA has increased by 40 per cent, from 17 per cent of people in need estimated to be in need of MPCA in 2017 to 66% in 2018. These figures are based on the number of families which have an income of below 480,000 IQD per month, the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB). This is calculated to cover the basic cost of a monthly food basket; consumable NFIs; water; electricity; rent; and transport/communication.The proposed MPCA will ensure that highly vulnerable families receive direct financial support in the form of cash transfers to meet their basic needs (SMEB) in a manner that upholds their dignity and freedom of choice. The proposed MPCA will also result in cash injection into the economy and thus indirectly support the local markets’ revival. Finally, Human Appeal will try to identify available and efficient social safety net schemes, which will be part of HA’s exit strategy for emergency MPCA in SAD project area.The selection of the household beneficiaries and program set-up will be done over a period of two month, and this process will be guided and informed from contacts and lessons learnt from the UNHCR funded MPCA implemented by HA at Mosul, Ninawa and operational strategy. Security in SAD is still highly volatile and HA will adapt its implementation modalities, security policy and processes to context volatility.

Protection Risk Analysis

Safety and Dignity: - Theft and Extortion (Beneficiaries & MTA)- Creating high visibility by direct and heavy implementation and exposing people to further harm - Direct or indirect attack towards HA staff or beneficiaries Access and Targeting: - Exclusion or Inclusion errors. The number of cash transfers will be determined by the scoring obtained by each household; priority will be given to the most vulnerable households who score highest in the socio-economic vulnerability scoring model to ensure that the implementation approach is harmonized with the CWG guidelines. - Duplication of assistance Data Protection and Beneficiary Privacy: - Intimate partner violence and/or gender based violence, particularly if women are the direct recipients of the assistance and they do not typically control household resources; or if men are marginalized in aid delivery. - Inter group tensions e.g. IDP/refugees and host community or tensions between Human Appeal and target beneficiary or other community members because of targeting and exclusion. Fraud and Diversion with Protection Implications: - Cash diverted by MTA or cash transfer providers or extorted from beneficiaries upon receipt (link with access and safety) - Cash used for illegal or harmful purposes

Mitigation Measures

Safety and Dignity: - Complaints and Feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries: mailbox (wireless to be checked) and Mobile phone (Network to be checked) and if the most vulnerable have access to it- Involving individuals, households and communities in assessment and design - Emergency response/one off distribution & limited access- Clear information and Communication system with communities on targeting approach & operational implementation and strategy - Creating high visibility by direct and heavy implementation and exposing people to further harm - No grouping activity, Autonomous Cash transfer system - Cash in envelop or direct cash payment to be limited as much as possible (including beneficiaries and reimbursement of MTA) Access and Targeting: - Exclusion: HA will use the CWG beneficiary targeting and selection criteria and methodology for the exclusion to ensure that the implementation approach is harmonized with the CWG guidelines - Inclusion: The vulnerability criteria will be communicated to targeted communities to ensure awareness, acceptance and participation in the process. - Strong rational on the targeting and selection criteria: to be context driven and based on vulnerability analyzes thus; targeting should ensure that the most vulnerable people receive assistance first. - Complaint and Feedback mechanism - As much as possible, to have a common approach on registration process between partners to avoid duplication. Rely on PDS system for identification (still open question) Household and Community Dynamic and Relations:- Intimate partner violence and/or gender based violence, particularly if women are the direct recipients of the assistance and they do not typically control household resources; or if men are marginalized in aid delivery. - Well-designed eligibility criteria and targeting based on context, community inputs, evidence and objectives of transfers; may need to re consider targeting to ensure inclusion of different groups, host community, women, etc.- Have a clear exit strategy for MPCA and communicate on. Rationale on operational strategy to be clearly explained. Fraud and Diversion with Protection Implications: - Clear communication and Complaint and feedback mechanisms with beneficiaries’ cash transfer companies being reimbursed only once the money is in the hands of the intended recipients - Transparency criteria & clear implementation guidelines - Random monitoring by independent actor or Human Appeal monitoring team

How is your project accountable to affected population

HA takes the accountability to affected populations (AAP) as a fundamental principle of its programming. HA is committed to IASC’s commitments on AAP and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) both of which emphasise the participatory approach and establishing an effective beneficiary feedback mechanism. HA has a dedicated MEAL department which implements activities to strengthen the quality of our programming, increase the transparency and accountability to donors and POC we serve. HA’s MEAL team established a strong beneficiary feedback mechanism, which includes complaints and feedback boxes, the nation-wide hotline with the number displayed on all information dissemination materials operated by an Accountability Officer, and the IDP Call Center (nationwide, UNOPS established AAP mechanism). Through this mechanism, we gather views, feedback and complaints of the affected populations in addition to regular participatory assessments, needs assessments, feedback and complaints mechanism, FGDs, KIIs, etc. A sample of beneficiaries will be visited and interviewed, and MEAL team will regularly perform random satisfaction surveys by the dedicated Accountability Officer. The lessons learnt are utilized by programmes teams to improve the quality of the programming and promptly respond to community-needs making our interventions relevant, timely, and accurate. Projects and implementation will be based on the identified vulnerable persons of concern, with specific activities addressing various needs presented by children of various ages (0-17), women, the elderly, the disabled, and other vulnerable groups.Human Appeal will be extremely cautious on the community and targeting approach ensuring well appropriation by the community and not generating any socials tensions within the community or do no harm (CF. Risk and Protection Analyses). Beneficiaries' feedback will also be taken into account during the implementation phase to ensure appropriate adaption to needs for future implementation.
