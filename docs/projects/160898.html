Project Summary

DRC’s SNFI project will (i) provide emergency SNFI support to crisis-affected populations in hard to reach locations across South Sudan and (ii) provide life-saving and life- sustaining shelter support to the most vulnerable groups within Malakal PoC. Support will be provided through a mobile approach, using teams stationed in Juba and Malakal, and through DRC’s static presence in Malakal PoC. All responses will be based on comprehensive SNFI assessments incorporating protection risk analyses. DRC will aim to the cluster recommended 26% cash modalities and the remaining 74% with in-kind modalities depending on feasibility, appropriateness, and value-for-money determined through comprehensive protection risk assessments and market analysis.
DRC will aim to reach 45,000 individuals who are in need of life-saving SNFI assistance. Targeted individuals will be prioritized through a needs-based approach considering IDPs, host community, returnees, and other population groups. Within the target populations, individuals with particular protection vulnerabilities including pregnant and lactating women, unaccompanied children, elderly people in need of support, and people with disabilities, will be prioritized in accordance with SNFI cluster guidelines. Close coordination with the SNFI sector will be maintained to ensure DRC’s activities complement and feed into wider sectoral strategies for the wider South Sudan response.
DRC mobile response team is composed of Protection, S/NFI, WASH and CCCM sectors and DRC as a protection focus agency to mainstream Protection, Gender Based Violence (GBV) mitigation and prevention and AAP to enhance the overall quality of S-NFI response in 2020. According to shelter-NIF cluster strategy, DRC will participate in joint missions among others to avoid duplication and better coordination among the Shelter-NFI cluster partners and other clusters.
DRC Malakal Protection team is already implementing Housing, Land and Property (HLP) support in Upper Nile state and providing high-quality counseling on HLP legal rights and civil documentation/registration, freedom of movement and basic access to social services to community members in Malakal town and in the PoC. DRC consider HLP under the protection due to its complexity but Shelter/NFI team will access support from HLP team to enhance S-NFI qualitative response.
DRC will ﬂexibly draw on both its own funds to procure distribution items and the SNFI cluster pipeline as most appropriate to ensure an eﬃcient and timely response. In accordance with SNFI cluster priorities, there will be an ongoing focus on monitoring, evaluation, assessment, and learning (MEAL) activities through post-distribution monitoring activities and integrating quick monitoring exercises conducted for all distributions done by mobile teams and by our static oﬃces in Malakal and new ones that DRC may open in 2020.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is facing extreme levels of humanitarian need. According to the 2020 HNO- SNFI Sector Analysis report, about 2.4 people (1.3 million IDPs and 662,000 returnees) are in need of SNFI assistance in 2020. The SNFI Cluster response plan indicate people inneed and targeted are 1,989,228 and 1,015,331 respectively. The number of people in need by and large remain same as 2019. The needs remain high in hard to reach locations, particularly in Upper Nile (Fashoda, Melut, Longochuk, Malakal, Maiwut, Luakpiny/Nasir, Ulang, Jekow, Panyikang, Baliet, Manyo and Canal/Pigi), Jonglei (Fangak, Duk, Uror, Bor South, Akobo, Pibor, Ayod), Unity (Rubkona, Panyijiar, , Leer, Mayendit, Guit and Paring), , Warrap (Tonj North), NorthBarh el Ghazl (Aweil Centre), Western Equitoria(Mundri East), Lakes(Yirol West)and Eastern Equatoria (Torit) states. Amongst the IDP population, women and children will remain especially vulnerable due to both their roles in society and the protection risks inherent in being without shelter orsocial support networks. DRC’s protection-centered multi-sector assessments across South Sudan in 2019 (Upper Nile, Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria, Central Equatoria, Unity, Warrap, and Lakes) ﬁnd communities affected by multi-layered crisis, with sudden shocks and stresses caused by continued conﬂict, environmental hazards such as ﬂooding, and food insecurity compounding already-acute vulnerabilities compounded by decades of conﬂict and hunger. In 2020, the trend of increasing inter-communal violence due to competition over resources and as a continuation of the wider political conﬂict by other means, as well as continued climactic instability, is forecasted. Complex population movements encompassing continued displacement due to violence, lack of food, and climactic crisis such as ﬂooding, layered with some returns movements to areas without suﬃcient resources or services to support new arrivals, will also continue to increase humanitarian needs. The access of the worst-affected populations to humanitarian assistance will continue to be impeded by access barriers including poor infrastructure, ﬂooding, and conﬂict. The 2019 HRP ﬁnds almost 2.4 million people in need of SNFI support. Widespread poverty, access barriers, small or non-existent markets, and decades of displacement result in an extreme lack of basic NFIs in the worst-affected areas. In many displacement-affected areas, the host community shares NFIs with IDPs and other vulnerable households. However, this places increased pressure on host community household members who themselves have scarce resources and risks creating tension over competition between different populations over already stretched resources. Lack of access to certain items is strongly linked to speciﬁc risks for the most vulnerable; lack of mosquito nets is a major factor in the high incidence of malaria across South Sudan, causing the deaths of an unknown number of people, especially children, the elderly, and inﬁrm, every year. Lack of blankets promotes the spread of pneumonia, while lack of kitchen sets promotes inter-communal tensions as multiple families struggle to obtain the means to prepare food. Access to shelter is one of the most acute challenges faced in South Sudan; materials are often not available or affected communities are unable to access them due to safety concerns and vulnerabilities such as physical inﬁrmity. As a result, tukuls are often overcrowded with average occupancies of between 8 to 12 people regularly assessed in crisis-affected areas. This triggers major protection concerns where unrelated men/boys share tukuls with women/girls, encourages the spread of diseases such as trachoma and tuberculosis, and raises inter-communal tensions. In other locations, IDPs and returnees have little or no access to shelter and are instead compelled to live together in schools or other buildings, and in worse cases in the open.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Conduct 20 S/NFI analysis in crisis-affected and hard-to-reach areas newly affected areas, providing recommendations for responses, and any associated risks and mitigation measures. The mobile teams stationed in Juba and Malakal will be deployed in prioritized areas for assessment/analysis for a period of one week. The assessments/ analysis will use SNFI cluster tools revised in 2019, and will aim to identify the S/NFI needs and best mechanisms of delivering S/NFI assistance considering the protection risks associated with such assistance and a key consideration of DO NO HARM. Based on the findings, a response mission will be planned DRC during the project period aims at conducting 20 assessments/ analysis that guides the response. Activity 2: Provide access to emergency shelter (in-kind). Through a mobile response modality, DRC will distribute life-saving shelter materials to IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable members of host communities affected by sudden shocks/stresses and/or crisis. The S/NFI response missions will be conducted over a period of one to two weeks, and DRC will aim for the same team who conducted the analysis would be deployed to the location. Response interventions include distribution of emergency shelter kit, usually plastic sheet and rope, based on the cluster standard and recommendation. DRC will use pipeline supplies from the cluster for its interventions as well as its own stocks. Shelter upgrades to most vulnerable population groups through in-kind and/or cash support.Activity 3-Provide access to emergency shelter (Cash): DRC program based in Malakal, DRC will implement a shelter program targeting the most vulnerable households among returnees, IDPs, and host community. Based on the cluster recommendation, DRC will aim to ensure 26% or more SNFI intervention use cash modalities. Cash will be utilized in areas where markets are well-functioning (as determined by a thorough market analysis) and protection risk analysis determines that cash is a safe/appropriate modality for beneﬁciaries. Protection teams will be involved in the proﬁling of the most vulnerable populations with acute shelter needs and exposure to risk due to their current shelter conditions. DRC aims to reach 11,750 individuals (1,958 households (6 individuals in each household) through the emergency shelter provision. Activity 4: Provide access to life- saving NFIs (in-kind). Distribution of life- sustaining NFI items to most vulnerable IDP and returnee individuals/households in frontline areas based on the analysis report identifying the needs and the protection and do no harm considerations. The S/NFI response missions will be conducted over a period of one to two weeks, and the same team who conducted the analysis would be deployed to the location. Response interventions include distribution of emergency NFI kit based on the cluster standard and recommendation. DRC will use pipeline supplies from the cluster for its interventions. DRC aims to reach 33,250 Individuals (5,542 HHs) through the emergency NFI intervention. Activity 5: Monitoring of quality service provision (remote or field monitoring). For each mobile response mission DRC will deploy a quick monitoring tool to monitor the quality of service provision. Rapid response monitoring will be conducted in the days following distributions and will be factored into mission planning and daily activities. DRC will meet SNFI cluster standards by ensuring that a PDM is done for at least one in three distributions (four total). For the static program in Malakal, DRC will ensure PDM is conducted for each intervention. Activity 6: DRC will conduct training to build the capacity of new cluster partners on CBI programming and ES/NFI cluster response plan.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.1 DRC will carry out sensitisation among its own team members to raise their awareness about the need to engage members from all segments in the consultations/project implementation. DRC will also carry out home to home visits to ensure the inclusion of community members who may be unable to leave their household due to physical or cultural/social restrictions. 8.2 Low, responses designed in the project will be decided based on the integrated multi sector protection focused assessment conducted by the organization and the assessment tools have bene designed integrating gender dimensions into it. It ensures FGDs and KIIs are conducted specifically to ensure women and girls voices are incorporated in to the consultation and decision making process. 8.10 As was observed in Bentiu, Unity State, late last year, women and girls face an increased risk of sexual violence when accessing humanitarian assistance. Considering this, DRC will ensure protection mainstreaming across its programming and seek to reduce/mitigate protection risks that may be incurred during the course of the project. Examples, of steps that will be taken by DRC include engagement of beneficiary members around selection of the locations for distribution and screening of locations with a GBV risk assessment. 8.14 The provision of NFI and Shelter can serve as a pull for populations to move into the area, which risks creating conflict over resources. Considering this, DRC will carry out awareness raising with the community and safety audits prior to the distribution. All interventions will be implemented through a conflict sensitivity lens.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC adopts a community-based approach, where beneficiaries and local authorities are included in the prioritising, planning and implementation of activities as an important measure of rehabilitation and capacity building. DRC is a certified member of the Core humanitarian Standards (CHS) and as such fulfils the CHS benchmarks on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. DRC has deployed beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism and operates complaints desks to enable community members to flag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. DRC also has a feedback hotline Number (9686) that beneficiaries can call to register complaints, feedback or requests. Through its activities, DRC will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols. Complaints and feedback submitted to the F-CRM will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the project.
