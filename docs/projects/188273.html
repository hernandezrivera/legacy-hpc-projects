Project Summary

The intervention aims to respond to shelter and winterization needs for the most vulnerable population in NWS, where we plan to implement temporary dignified shelter units for sheltering 1,000 HHs from IDPs whose shelters are located in significant flood risk areas, besides implementing diester risk reduction activity targeting NWS camp, through providing the essential infrastructure rehabilitation for 30 km of roads and drainage, as well as provide tents insulation for 1200 HHs, in the vulnerable camps.
NFI assistance will be provided to approximately 5,000 vulnerable HHs in Atmeh Camp, and overall Idleb, while the winterization activity will be implemented in Atmeh Camp where we expect the population to reach 40,000 HHs of IDPs.

4 - Needs

Recent cross-data (REACH, HNAP, CCCM, and SNFI Cluster) indicate that among NWS, Dana subdistrict comes in the high level of severity and the highest number of PIN between NWS subdistricts. According to WoS SNFI Severity PiN 2022, Dana SD contains 667,038 PIN for shelter in the fourth level of severity (out of 5).Due to the continuous waves of displacement that the area has received, it is severely overcrowded. As HNO 2022 stated, 94% of the IDPs are living together with other IDPs, 33% of them in self-settled camps, and 12% in damaged buildings.Moreover, access to markets and non-food items (NFIs) is undermined by the widespread economic vulnerability that is driven further by COVID-19, the drop in the Syrian Pound, diminished income opportunities, rising prices, and economic restrictions, where 63% of the HHs in NWS reported increasing the Debt in the last year, and 1,175,225 PIN in NWS reported according to HNO 2022.For the Winterization in Atmeh, the most vulnerable population in the camp are either not heating their shelters, or burning the plastic and clothes for heating themselves and their families, moreover, Atmeh camp is considered one of the largest camps in NWS, and the population there is increasing every year and with each wave of displacement continuously, where the camp is located in the Dana SD where the 3rd severity level and a significant number of PIN 308,623 for NFI support, and 217,941 for winterization support.

5 - Implementation of activities

For temporary dignified shelter units, GC will work closely with the most in-need camps and communities which hosted a large number of IDP to identify the safe and proper locations for the activity implementation and examine the recommended guidelines in site selection and preparation standards for creating the planned camps, and considering HLP due diligence for the site land, topographic levels and flood considerations, enough farness from the front lines, nearness to exist settlements, main roads, and vital locations, and availability of the essential services in and around. We will identify the beneﬁciaries from those who are living in shelters that experienced a flood or the risk of flood is significant, and their camps are located in flood vulnerable locations. GC will rehabilitate critical infrastructure in the NWS camps to facilitate the delivery of life-saving essential WASH services and improve the overall shelter status of the Camp residents, particularly those affected by seasonal flooding. We will gravel roads, repair and/or redirect drainage, and provide tents insulation to be protected against the flood by raising the tent level. GC will follow the HLP guidance outlined in the SNFI Cluster’s due diligence guidelines, where we will implement the proposed repairs on the land with no HLP issues or problems.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

During the implementation of the planned activities, we expect the following risks:Through the temporary dignified shelter activity, there is a risk of Eviction due to lack of approval from the property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter. This can be mitigated through 1.Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster, 2.Do not proceed with the site preparation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP Due Diligence Guidelines. 3.Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed. 4.Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form. In this manner a high risk of exclusion from shelter assistance due to lack of civil status document/ID may arise in the field. This can be mitigated through Working closely with communities, community leaders and authorities to identify alternative way of identification.Providing CVA may arise risks such as putting vulnerable beneficiaries at risk of robbery or extorsion. Risk of cash and or voucher value diversion to non-humanitarian purposes. This can be mitigated through 1.Distribution methodology with clear protection risks analysed and mitigated. 2.Home delivered rather than at distribution location where beneficiaries could be targeted. 3.Verified beneficiary to prevent cash or voucher value being misused for other non-humanitarian purposes.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

In order to mitigate is a high risk of Abuse of Authority by actors (PSEA, GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). This can be mitigated through 1. Ensuring that GC’s and its implementing partners’ staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct. 2. developing a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly. 3. Ensuring active inclusion of complaint mechanism in place. Meanwhile, there is a high risk of Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation as well as communicable diseases. This can be mitigated through 1. Advocate for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites, 2. Provision of gender segregated/appropriate privacy in mass shelter in case of large displacement/lack of space to provide individual family shelter unit, 3. Provision of gender segregated sanitation and hygiene facilities sufficiently close to dwellings, 4. Provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities, 5. Work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them, 6. Extending consultations such as on siting of facilities with all groups of population including women, girls and PWD.
