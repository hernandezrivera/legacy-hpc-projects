Project Summary

This project will provide emergency assistance aimed at mainly improving immediate access to food. Unconditional (500) and Conditional (700) cash/voucher transfer to address food insecurity needs of populations in crisis and stressed food security situation. Livestock assets protection support (livestock treatment) through training of 30 Community Animal Health Workers and rehabilitation of water supply systems. Nutritional education (IEC messages) on balanced diet / dietary diversity in the local context.

Needs

A recent forecast issued by the Greater Horn of Africa Climate Out- look Forum (GHACOF50), issued on 28 August 2018, indicates a greater likelihood of normal to above normal 2018 Deyr (October- December) rains across Somalia. Despite recent improvement, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse through December according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018This intervention, target Afmadow and Badhadhe districts in Lower Juba, Somalia.The food security outlook update estimated population in need in Lower juba has placed 5,000 individuals under emergency (IPC 4), 77,000 individuals under crisis(IPC phase 3) and 80,000 individuals under stressed (IPC phase 2).Populations groups classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, reducing acute malnutrition, saving lives and protecting and saving livelihoods. This intervention proposes to target acutely food insecure populations in crisis and emergency phases in Afmadow and Badhadhe districts, lower Juba Somalia.Acute food insecurity remains prevalent among pastoral populations who lost most of their livestock and became destitute during the 2016/2017 drought and others experiencing large-scale, protracted displacement due to conflict and natural hazards. These groups are expected to face food consumption gaps or engage in unsustainable coping strategies to meet their food needs, and humanitarian assistance is required to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation- Food Security Outlook Update August 2018.Afmadow and Badhadhe Districts located at the southern end of Somalia has been affected by combination of man-made and natural disasters over the last three decades.This intervention seeks to support affected populations facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse in the 2 districts of Afmadow and Badhadhe.

Activities

Objective 1: To improve immediate access:Activity 1: WASDA proposes provision of unconditional cash transfer for 500 food insecure households in crisis situation and conditional cash transfers for 400 households (IPC phase 3)Objective 1: To improve immediate access:Activity 2: Provide nutritional education (IEC messages) on balanced diet / dietary diversity in the local context for 300 households through cash-for-nutrition education approach. This will target mothers from vulnerable households and female headed households. The Cash-for-Work (to produce assets-WASH) and cash-for-nutrition education (behavioral change communication) measures.The daily wage for CFW activities will be fixed at $5 for unskilled workers. Each CFW participant will work for 15 days per month for a period of 4 cycles earning US$ 75 per cycle. Supervisors will earn $6 dollars per day totaling $90 per cycle.Objective 2: To protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources:Activity 3: Provide refresher training for 30 Community Based Animal Health workers (CBAHWs) based in Afmadow and Badhadhe districts. The training will contribute to the continuous delivery of animal health services which is expected to limit/contain contagious livestock disease outbreaks and further protect and rebuild livelihood assets. Activity 4: Rehabilitation of water systems and water points through Cash for work approach. Cash for work labor will be used to rehabilitate public water pans, repair inlets and protect water sources. The project will target water points that support large population of livestock for rehabilitation. Wasda will employ a mobile payment transfer offered by a mobile network operator. Wasda will use its bulk payment service, which is a platform that enables organizations to send money to multiple recipients at the same time. Mobile transfer is cheaper, faster and safer option for sending money,and one that is considerably more accessible than other options.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

WASDA will put protection needs of project beneficiaries at the heart of its programming. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The proposed project is targeting areas and communities living in insecure and conflict sensitive environments where abuses and rights violations of women, youth, and children, the disabled and elderly often occur but sometimes unreported.Mitigation measures: WASDA will ensure that all its project activities are guided by the humanitarian principles of ensuring non-discrimination and the safety, dignity and integrity of assisted groups especially displaced populations.WASDA will seek to limit harm to vulnerable community members by putting systems in place that enable a close monitoring of the situation of target beneficiaries and communities, and the effective management of complaints. WASDA staff will regularly attend and contribute to the inter agency and cluster meetings including those called by the local administrations, project target communities and more so specifically the protection cluster and agencies to share and learn lessons and best practices as well as to share reported critical protection issues. WASDA will ensure protection monitoring and community complaints mechanisms (CCM) are established and include processes for effectively handling incidents of gender based violence, corruption and sexual exploitation and abuse and/or referring cases on to competent authorities or colleague agencies.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project will be implemented with strong consideration to gender mainstreaming to ensure it largely benefits women, men, boys and girls that are most vulnerable and have the highest humanitarian needs.Women, girls, men, boys and the elderly will be given representation at all levels to ensure participation and joint decicision making.WASDA will collect gender and age dissagregated data while carrying out monitoring of project activities.In order to ensure that the most vulnerable are reached, WASDA will employ community based targeting methodology. Selection criteria will be jointly established with the community leaders and local authorities.Key cross cutting issues such as gender, age and vulnerability will be considered during beneficiary selection.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The proposed project is targeting areas and communities living in insecure and conflict sensitive environments where abuses and rights violations of women, youth, and children, the disabled and elderly often occur but sometimes unreported. In order to mitigate the effeccts of conflict and violence on civilians, WASDA will:-• WASDA will map and analyze project stakeholders their interrelationships, roles, influence and their potential impact on the intervention. Set communications and participatory mechanisms to engage or not to engage these actors in the program stages. • WASDA will map the potential external and internal sources of violence, danger, coercion and take preventative and responsive measures to address, mitigate or reduce these threats to project beneficiaries. • WASDA will ensure that the protection risks and concerns underlined in the assessment are considered in project planning and implementation.• WASDA will ensure that the project design and will not cite the affected population, not only as beneficiaries but also as active contributors to the intervention. • WASDA will actively map the risks the project might expose to the beneficiaries (Host communities, IDPs, returnees etc) and take measures to mitigate these threats during implementation and reporting phases.• WASDA will ensure that the project implementation staff has signed the code of conduct and are oriented on humanitarian principles of humanity, independence and impartiality. WASDA will strongly cordinate with all humanitarian and state actors so as to ensure civilian protection remains a top priority.WASDA will also ensure that its project activities will not undermine the safety and security of project beneficiaries.
