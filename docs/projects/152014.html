Project Summary

The project aims at addressing food, income and nutrition insecurity by Support nutritious food production during the rainy and dry season farming through the provision of mixed emergency agricultural inputs to support the most food insecure and vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in the states of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. This will go hand in hand in hand with training on Good Agricultural Practices, nutrition and gender sensitive agriculture. FAO aims to increase the participation of women in these small scale production activities, especially through distribution of small ruminants and poultry, and women savings groups. In addition, FAO will also provide poultry inputs (chickens, housing, feed). These interventions will be aiming to rebuild the productive capacities of vulnerable women, who have lost large parts of their livestock/poultry assets due to the conflict. The project will target 190,000 households (approximately 1,330,000 individuals) with crop farming and livestock related support. The project will further target 20,000 households (approximately 140,000 individuals) address limited access to energy. Multiple challenges related to energy access directly result in food insecurity and malnutrition (e.g. insufficient fuel to cook food), deforestation (e.g. unsustainable cutting of wood resources for fuel), protection risks (e.g. harassment, assault, rape of women when collecting woodfuel) and health risks (smoke inhalation, respiratory illnesses). FAO will promote use of fuel-efficient cookstoves and support to sustainable management of woodfuel resources (tree nurseries, communal woodlots and agroforestry)

Needs

Food, income and nutrition insecurity: while food security indicators have improved in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states due to improvement in security and livelihood system, however population in Phase 3 emergency food and nutrition insecurity and Phase 4 food security crisis is projected to increase in the lean season from June to October 2018 compared to the same period in 2017 (Cadre Harmonisé March 2018). Humanitarian assistance complemented with recovery and building communities’ resilience to food security shock and hazard need to be continued.Vagaries of climate: cyclic drought characterized by long dry spells during cropping season and early cessation of rainfall and sometimes flooding, seriously affect crop performance and pasture development resulting in food insecurity. Irrigated farming is the way to go and has been demonstrated to be quite successful in dry season production of vegetables and rice production. Women are largely involved in the bulk of informal and small-scale food processes and participate in the production of food crops and vegetables (eg. Maize, cowpea, melon, pepper, cassava and vegetables). They participate in small scale food production and small scale small ruminant, poultry and aquaculture production. Moreover, women are less involved in the wholesale of agricultural produce and find it difficult to access markets due to limited education and funds to finance retail activities versus engagement in informal markets. It is worth to highlight also that when the agricultural machines are introduced or provided as part of the support for rural women, men use these machines and women are forced to engage in unmechanized farming in some cases.The conflict has created a protection crisis as women, men and children face grave human rights violations and sexual and gender-based violence. In the northeast, gender inequality, due to the interplay of patriarchal, cultural and socio-economic factors, seriously hinders economic and social progress. Low literacy rates and marginalization from decision-making processes means that many women in the region are unaware of their rights, potentials. Thus, women are often unable to access education, employment, and other economic opportunities. Such exclusion has been compounded as women continue to be victimized and had their rights grossly violated, which leads to the failure to adequately address women’s concerns such as sexual and gender based violence, women’s rights, post-conflict accountability and resilience.Livestock rearing plays an important role in the livelihood the population in the northeast states. The crisis has also severely affected people depending on livestock value chain, and the availability of high nutrient food such as meat, eggs and milk. Lack of domestic animal flock is a strong criterion of vulnerability. Wherever a household don’t own any animal, animal protein consumption dropped sharply for families with a very poor diet intake thus exposing households’ members to malnutrition particularly for youths under 5 years of age. Moreover, lack and or insufficient number of animals in a household sharply dropped vulnerable households’ resilience capacity in food crisis period. Limited access to energy. Multiple challenges related to energy access directly result in food insecurity and malnutrition (e.g. insufficient fuel to cook food), deforestation (e.g. unsustainable cutting of wood resources for fuel), protection risks (e.g. harassment, assault, rape of women when collecting woodfuel) and health risks (smoke inhalation, respiratory illnesses). Activities

Activity 1: Support nutritious food production during the rainy and dry season farming through the provision of mixed emergency agricultural inputs, backyard and micro gardening, livestock and poultry kits support to the most food insecure and vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities.The project will distribute diversified food based mix cropping/mix farming kits to 170,000 households beneficiaries (1, 190,000 individuals) for rain fed and dry season crop production, with the aim to enhance access to agricultural inputs so that they can resume their livelihoods and improved their food security and nutrition. 70,000 households (490,000 individuals) for dry season vegetable production and dry season rice cultivation for consumption and income generation. 15,000 households will also benefit from micro gardening. 20,000 households with 70% women headed households (140,000 individuals) will receive livestock and poultry kits to diversify their livelihood activities. FAO will also ensure that their staff and partners comply with their respective Code of Conduct. Sensitization for beneficiaries on the project purpose, their entitlements, the selection criteria, the risk of mines on GBV , PSEA and complaint mechanisms will be conducted. 5% of total budget will be used towards protection mainstreaming activities Activity 2: Capacity building on nutrition and gender sensitive agriculture. Apart from the direct intervention aiming at diversified food production. FAO will organize nutrition and gender sensitive agriculture targeting farmer field groups members and farmers in groups in village Savings and Loan groups (VSLG). The trainings will target the formed 50 (VSLG) members and 100 government ministries representatives. 50 Traditional leaders from targeted local government areas will be sensitized on the existing traditional and legal provisions guiding the practices in the agriculture sector and its negative impacts on women and the community. Activity 3 :Formation, Training and supporting Village Savings & Loans groups This activity will focus on the promotion of savings and credit that can be used to deliver sustainable financial services to the rural poor. FAO targets to support 50 groups of 20-30 members each who are part of FAO’s famer field schools initiative. The development of groups Village savings & loan groups(VSLG) will enable gradual accumulation of financial resources over a period of time and develops a culture of saving. Women farmers will be trained on how to identify their needs and influence decisions in the sector in line with their specific needs, including financial literacy. Activity 4 : Provision of food conservation and processing equipment and skills acquisition This activity will focus on Restarting of fish processing, aquaculture production and marketing enterprises to 100 women; Support agricultural production including provision of processing equipment such as maize miller, Thresher Pepper miller, Ground nut oil extractor, Rice miller to 50 groups of youth and women with 20 members each. Activity 5 : Provision of fuel efficient cookstoves and capacity building on Community- Based Climate Change Mitigation and Adaptation initiativesFAO will Promote use of fuel-efficient cookstoves and support to sustainable management of woodfuel resources through distribution of 10,000 fuel efficient cook stoves to 10,000 households and support two fuel efficient stoves production centers. FAO will further conduct Awareness raising campaign for beneficiaries on, gender issues and GBV, PSEA, Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) and climate change. FAO will ensure it has a functional M&E system through out the project implementation. FAO co-lead with WFP the Food Security Sector under the FSS, in order to share information, coordinate activities with other UN and NGOs. FAO will share the lessons learned which will emanate from the Project to the above mentioned Coordination systems
