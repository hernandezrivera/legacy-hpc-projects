Project Summary

A rapid assessment conducted by NCA in 18 selected schools revealed critical gaps in NCA and partners supported schools. About 80% of these schools are in rural and hard to reach areas and accommodate a significant number of IDP children. While numerous inequities exist within Somalia, the most socially excluded groups are rural children (particularly those from pastoralist communities), children from households with the lowest wealth quintiles (including ‘urban poor’), and children from IDP households. This is demonstrated by the primary school net attendance ration of 39% in urban areas compared to 11% in rural areas, while those with the lowest access to education are pastoralist and IDP children .
NCA assessment indicated that majority of the assessed schools (65%) had temporary learning spaces and needed major repairs to ensure a conducive learning environment. Majority of these schools (76%) do not have a fence hence which would enhance the safety of learners and the facilities within the schools. The assessment further indicated that the schools needed additional learning spaces to reduce overcrowding in some of the schools. 65% of the assessed schools have no handwashing facilities around the latrine area with only 41% of the latrines having internal locks to enhance safety and privacy for learners. 82% of the schools assessed reported that adolescent girls did not receive any menstrual hygiene kits during the year 2019 and a place where girls can dispose used menstrual hygiene materials. Additionally, none of the schools assessed had safe spaces for girls to access Psycho social support and participate in discussions and activities concerning them. Teachers in the assessed schools have not been trained on PSEA, child safeguarding, and psychological support and only one school has a Code of Conduct for teachers. Additionally, majority of these teachers lack a formal training with minimal pedagogical skills.
The project will provide life-saving education response for boys and girls in rural and IDP settings. It will seek to address the above-mentioned education needs and gaps in 18 schools across four districts namely Elwak, Luuq, Dollow and Belet-hawa recently assessed by NCA and which host a significant number of children from most vulnerable and hard to reach rural areas. School aged girls and boys from affected populations will be supported to access safe education opportunities; the capacity of teachers in supported learning institutions will be enhanced to deliver quality education; and education coordination platforms will be strengthened. The primary target group for the Education projects are boys and girls living in rural and marginalized areas as well as in IDP settings. In addition, teachers and CECs from the selected schools will be targeted through capacity building initiatives to empower them to effectively deliver on their roles. The project will be implemented in close coordination with local education actors and structures such as district education officers, education partners, CECs, and the Ministry of Education among others. It will ensure that the rights of girls, boys, men and women are upheld in all aspects of the project through supporting participation, accountability and non-discrimination of all relevant education stakeholders. Complementarity will be ensured through relevant cooperation and coordination with other humanitarian actors implementing EiE interventions in targeted locations. A total of 2,811 school aged children 49% girls; and 270Teachers and CEC 40% female from the targeted schools will benefit from the project.

Project Activities

1. Construction of 10 temporary learning spaces, 2. Provide teaching and learning materials for boys and girls in the target schools3. Establishment of lockable gender segregated latrines with hand washing facilities,4. Provide Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) and Kits to adolescent girls. 5. Support Child to Child protection activities in targeted schools6. Train 180 teachers (40% female) on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Child Safeguarding, Code of Conduct and Psychosocial Support in order to ensure that the safety and wellbeing of the targeted school children is enhanced.7. Train 90 CECs and MoEs (40% female) on DRR & school management and support them to Develop and implement School Development plans8. Provide monthly emergency incentive for 180 teachers (40% female)
