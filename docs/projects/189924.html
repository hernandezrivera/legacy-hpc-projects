Project Summary

Project Summary The proposed HRP 2022 interventions will target locations with IPC 4 and 5 with emergency food security and livelihoods assistance to the most vulnerable populations affected by shocks including floods, conflicts which has contributed to the elevation of food insecurity outcomes with 31 counties of extreme or severe concern and 36 very high or high concern. The convergence of shocks such as floods, conflicts, hyperinflation of prices for basic commodities and now compounded with social and economic impact of Covid 19 at glance have made the population vulnerable deepening the humanitarian situation. The proposed interventions are aligned to the HRP 2022 cluster objectives; CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations, CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support and CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. The project is planned to be implemented in Kajo Keji, Yirol east, Akobo, Pibor, Uror, Nyirol, Rumbek centre, Pochalla.
This HRP project will complement Oxfam ongoing resilient programming to provide immediate quick impact activities while protecting the gains of resilience programming. Protection and Gender will be mainstreamed throughout the project lifetime, a Do No Harm approach as enshrined in Oxfam safe programming approach will be mainstreamed by ensuring participation and accountability to affected population. This project will be implemented by Oxfam and its local partners.

1 - Needs

Plan Fields Needs South Sudan has suffered a fifth consecutive year of flooding primarily a result of climate change, most affected states include Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity state and some parts of Lake states. The flood compound already the cumulative consequences of the conflict and thus pushing the population to ever difficult to win humanitarian situation. On gradual basis since the conflict in 2013 sets in, there has been increase in the number of people falling off to worse food insecurity situation. In 2014 it is estimated that more than 2 million people faced severe food insecurity and an estimate of 2022 HRP now puts the population in need of humanitarian assistance to 8.4 million and a total of 31 counties of extreme or severe concern. With the impact of the interweaved of shocks, the population of South Sudan has been living in dire support of humanitarian assistance over time In 2014 it is estimated that more than 2 million people faced severe food insecurity and an estimate of 2022 HRP now puts the population in need of humanitarian assistance to 8.3 million and a total of 31 counties of extreme or severe concern this is a result of the war that ravaged the country and population resilience capacity to withstand shocks without humanitarian assistance has been depleted thus produced multidimensional outcomes which have aggravated the suffering of the most vulnerable households and communities, limited access to food due to reduced farming capacity due to displacement and fear of attack in the farm lands, prices of commodities have remained skyrocketed due to devaluation of local currency and limited or hindered flow of supplies partly due to insecurity but also physical access due to poor road networks. The economic situation continues to worsen over the years and monthly market price factsheet January 2022 a household in South Sudan on average basis requires 99 USD to meet SMEB moreover in Akobo as household require 178 USD monthly to meet basic needs and yet on average a household in Akobo earns only 88 USD per month, that mean a household purchasing power is limited to meet the basic needs. MSNA conducted in December 2021 by Oxfam, found out that in Pibor, 50% of the HH are falling under poor FCS score, while 38% are in boarder line and an average CSI of 76% falling into high coping with scale of (CSI ≥10) and medium (CSI = 4-9) respectively thus present poor food security outcomes. The interweaved of shocks continue to push more population into brink of catastrophe this is evidence by the fact that as of 2014, only more than 2 million requires humanitarian assistance and in 2022, 8.3 million people depicting an increase of 400% over time. The proposed HRP project is designed to ensure most vulnerable households access immediate food and livelihood support and skills transfer to improve production and productivity to avert the worsening food security outcomes in IPC phase 3, 4 and 5 and strengthen resilience of affected population

2 - Project activities

Project will provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity, and coping strategies for vulnerable populations by providing food through GFD- blanket/ seasonal/ targeted and GFD plus. Oxfam has signed already Field Level Agreement with WFP to implement this activity in Jonglei Final Distribution Points. It will continue conduct the planned rations distribution in accordance with WFP standard food basket ration and feeding days; 2) Provide of cash transfers to most vulnerable households. cash transfers will be provided to most vulnerable households to meet unmet food needs. Two modalities of cash transfers will be implemented that is unconditional cash (sector specific) for most vulnerable based on agreed criteria and conditional cash transfer activities to promote social skills and safety nets aim at improving social assets but also contributing to inoculating skills to the population to be able to meet their livelihood aspirations. Conditional cash activities will be conducted in accordance with the community based participatory Planning (CBPP) to ensure that social assets are selected in consultative process. This process will ensure participation and accountability in the process and instill ownership of programme for sustainability; 3) provide conditional food assistance, Oxfam has already signed FLA with WFP to implement conditional food assistance in Akobo, Pochalla and Pibor. This intervention will be implemented in accordance with WFP- Oxfam agreements for 2022. Standard food basket rations will be provided in accordance with WFP standards to meet the agreed feeding days. Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock support by providing emergency fishing kits, in accordance with FAO standards of providing emergency fishing kits which complies with inland marine fishing guidelines and policies and regulation of fishing activities in South Sudan. Fishing kits will be provided to locations with accessibility to fishing grounds to enable them to participate in fishing to meet their food needs and income during crisis times. Rapid awareness on proper use of fishing kits will be conducted to ensure that fishing is conducted in the way that does not deplete fishing capacity, reduce exposure to water pollution by responsible fishing and disposal and improvement of fishing handling methods and hygiene to improve nutrition of households; 2) provide crop and vegetable kits and tools. Vegetable and crops seeds and assorted tools will be provided as standard kits. 5 types of preferred vegetable seeds by the beneficiaries will be provided and a total 8 Kgs for crop seeds will be provided. A beneficiary preference of seeds will be conducted, and ecological and culturally adapted types of seeds will be prioritized. The distribution of seeds will be conducted both in Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism and static operations in locations with elevated IPC phase 4 and 5 and which Oxfam has established presence. Simple awareness creations on farming practices and methods as part of capacity building will be conducted on spot during the distribution to ensure the recipient households have the knowledge on the production practices and methods; 3) Distribution of fishing kits and assorted vegetable seeds as standard kit, Oxfam will distribute fishing and vegetable kits to the most vulnerable households to meet their livelihood assistance during the dry season. Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shock by 1) capacity building to beneficiaries receiving livelihood support such as GAPs and improved fishing and preservation. The project provision of seeds and tools such as fishing, vegetable and crop seeds will be complemented with capacity building to improve skills and knowledge towards improving production and productivity.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Markets are functional in the proposed project locations, Oxfam has been working with local markets in the delivery of humanitarian assistance and it is same approach of market based interventions that cash assistance will be implemented in locations which have the market functional and responsive to increase in demand. Use of cash based assistance has many benefits social and economic; it gives choice and dignity to affected population, easy to support large scale interventions rapidly compared to conventional in-kind assistance, cash revitalize economy and multiplier effects impacts on livelihoods and economic wellbeing of communities.

Markets are functional in the targeted locations, it also considers availability basic goods, pricing and household requirements to meet for MEB for the month. It also informed by the responsive capacity of market actors to increase in demand and preference for cash modality

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The findings from complaint and feedback and other sources of information such as exit interviews and post distribution monitoring will be used to inform the project activities and flexibly refined activities to address the concerns. Also, findings may be used for future programming, this is essential to ensure that future programme are designed with understanding of past understanding of needs and challenges

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Oxfam programming follows a non-discriminatory approach in which all people regardless of race, gender, affiliation, and disability status. The programme are designed to reach the most vulnerable groups within the population including PLWDs and others. The CFM mechanisms are robust including face to face interface with staff, words of mouth, written. The complaint and feedback mechanisms will be implemented accordingly with intention to reach all sections of affected population.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Oxfam has integrated complaint and feedback mechanism which includes complaint related to safeguarding. The process of handling safeguarding concerns such as sexual exploitation, harassment and abuse is different from the traditional complaints and feedback on project direct activities. Safeguarding is particularly concern about people and their interaction in their day to day activities with people we work with including beneficiaries, partners, volunteers, government etc. Oxfam has different mechanisms for reporting complaint related to safeguarding including international safeguarding desk, whistleblowing, web based anonymous reporting and trained dedicated safeguarding focal persons at various field locations

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The information will be provided during project inception or awareness stages, at distribution site and through community level meetings. Information on awareness on gender and protection pathways will also be made available to ensure beneficiaries can access such services in case of GBV and protection related concerns. Mechanisms such as help desk and exit and PDM interviews will be conducted to gather feedback from the different sources.
