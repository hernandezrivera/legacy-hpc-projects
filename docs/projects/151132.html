Project Summary

The project will increase access to safe learning spaces for for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and poor hosting children and adolescent youths of male and female of emergency-affected population in Daynile, Kaxda, Afgoye, Walanweine and Jowhar districts of Banadir, Lower and Middle Shabelle regions. SCC will deliver a comprehensive basic Education integrated with WASH package including gender-sensitive sanitation facilities, hygiene promotion, lifesaving and life skills development, essential education supplies, and teacher training which is aimed to alleviate the education gaps and produce positive education results for girls and boys and for women and men to better improvement in emergency education services in their IDP camps and villages.

Needs

According to Joint Multi Cluster Needs Assessment 2018," Out of 4.9 Million School aged children in Somalia, only 1.5 million children are enrolled in schools. The main barriers to access to education are lack of learning facilities and inability of families to pay the school fees. Without learning and protective environment that schools provide, more boys and girls in Somalia are vulnerable to and at risk of child labour and recruitment by armed groups. Girls face a higher risk of dropping out of school, or never access to enrol and become exposed to early marriage and domestic violence. Lack of education and livelihoods opportunities are among the underlying conditions that have driven youth to join radicalized groups in Somalia. Out of 4.63 million people who are food insecure in Somalia, 1.85 million are school aged children. Out of the 2.6 million internally displaced people approximately 1 million are school aged children. Currently, 1.83 million school aged children are in need of assistance to access and stay in school." Multi-faced conflicts in Lower and middle Shabelle regions continue to cause displacements to interrupt on education in the villages of Afgoye and Bal’d districts. School age children were among the displaced persons. In the first 8 months of 2018, almost 140,000 new displacements were monitored by PRMN within or from Lower/middle Shabelle regions, including 94,000 displaced due to conflict or insecurity. Conflict in Lower Shabelle/middle shabelles displaced more than 34,000 since august 2018 that ended up in the IDP camps in Banadir, Jowhar and Afgoye. In October 2018, about 2230 families have displaced from Basra and surrounding villages that majority of them moved to Jowhar and Afgoye. This affected about 1450 of school age children that lost education. Findings in SCC assessment in the IDP camps of Banadir, Jowhar and Afgoye an estimated 23,000 (12300 boys and 10700 girls) of school age children are among the newly arrived IDPs.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

SCC shall involve stakeholders’ participation in the programming stage to ensure that the target locations are not prone to conflict, insecurity and are easily accessible. The response is planned to involve the government administrations and the community established structures/systems to ensure ownership, sustainability and resilience. During the intervention, SCC shall train and create awareness in protection mainstreaming through the participation of the key stakeholders. SCC will ensure awareness and acceptance in both the government and target communities. The participation is expected to improve coordination at all levels of protection and prepare the stakeholders for handover of the project at the end of the interventions. SCC assumes that by the end of the project the stakeholders shall be well trained and prepared to take over and continue with the practices and maintenance on the awareness on GBV and protection at institutions (health centers, Ct Cs, schools,) and make sure vulnerable populations such as women, children and persons with disability is reached and attended to get rid of protection cases. By involving the government administrations it is to legalize the services and utilities by ensuring that the government representatives advise on all the legal requirements and facilitate the process in acquiring the same. Community participation shall create awareness of the ownership and the use of the utilities to the target beneficiaries. It is expected that by the end of the planned intervention, SCC shall ensure that education environment is sufficiently safe/stable to enable durable solutions/resilience.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Displaced Families experience a drastic change in lifestyle as they are forced to leave their comfort zone to the unknown. Community established structures are destroyed and households forced to relocate to IDP camps in fear of attacks. Due to the displacement, there is family separation resulting to children and female headed Households. Schools close leaving school going children out of school. Rape incidents, domestic violence,insecurity, lack of job opportunities and depletion of livelihoods, lack of or functioning police and law enforcement institutions, and denial of resource for women and girls. Lack of awareness for women and girls is a major challenge facing project target populations. The planned response shall target IDPs, Returnees and vulnerable host communities that are most affected and discriminated due to lack and limited access to basic services. Sharing the already inadequate resources with the host communities can trigger conflict and create insecurity amongst the IDPS, returnees and host communities. IDPs live in temporary shelters and these lead to protection risks. Single mothers and girls are exposed to GBV, lactating mothers, children under 5, the old and people living with disabilities cannot fight to access the limited basic services. The distances, locations, availability becomes a major challenge. It is due to this concerns that SCC proposes to carry out interventions that will ensure protection for beneficiaries at all level of intervention. SCC will discuss the issue with the local authorities to handle the case in hand to assist the vulnerable people out of risk of eviction. Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

IDPs live in temporary shelters and these lead to protection risks. Single mothers and girls are exposed to GBV, lactating mothers, children under 5, the old and people living with disabilities cannot fight to access the limited basic services. The distances, locations, availability becomes a major challenge. It is due to this concerns SCC proposes to carry out interventions that will ensure protection for beneficiaries at all level of intervention. SCC will look into the matter and discuss the issue with the local authorities to handle the case in hand to assist the vulnerable people out of risk of eviction. SCC will target the vulnerable population to protect them from persistent gender violence that most of the IDPs and the host communities are facing hence woman and girls remain the most vulnerable to gender based violence as they represent 88.6 per of the GBV cases registered every year. Women and children are vulnerable by nature and when disrupted by conflict, drought or evictions they become disoriented. The trend requires more robust preventive as well as response interventions in IDP sites which are the majority of cases are reported from. SCC shall Request the systematic participation of representatives of marginalized IDP communities in consultation meetings with local leaders, Condition the implementation of the project on equal and direct access with all communities and Establish contact directly with leaders of IDPs from marginalized communities, outside camps if necessary. The relevant local authorities will be properly engaged in activities design and implementation as well as informed of ongoing activities and developments through periodical meetings conducted by Project Manager. This will minimize the risk of misunderstandings and overlapping of activities with other partners and the government. A proper engagement also of village and community leaders will mitigate security risks.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Armed conflict is dimensional and can emerge as a result of social and political tensions that keep on fueling conflicts around Somalia. It is context specific with mixture of interconnected factors though ethnicity appears to play a prominent and almost ubiquitous role in many of them. In Somalia armed conflict has resulted to displacement of populations forcing them to leave their homes and relocate to other places where majority end up in already crowded IDP camps. Families are displaced to locations with no access to basic services. School going age children have no access to basic education due to limited learning spaces in the IDP camps and host communities. School going age children become vulnerable and prone to recruitment by the militia groups and girls early marriage. Other locations experience cut off NGOs services by the militia groups leaving them in dire need of provision basic services. To mitigate the risks, SCC plans to organize all the stakeholders meetings for dialogue to identify the possible risks and develop a mitigation strategy. SCC shall Request the systematic participation of representatives of marginalized IDP communities in consultation meetings with local leaders, Condition the implementation of the project on equal and direct access with all communities and Establish contact directly with leaders of IDPs from marginalized communities, outside camps if necessary. The relevant local authorities will be properly engaged in activities design and implementation as well as informed of ongoing activities and developments through periodical meetings conducted by Project Manager. This will minimize the risk of misunderstandings and overlapping of activities with other partners and the government. A proper engagement also of village and community leaders will mitigate security risks.
