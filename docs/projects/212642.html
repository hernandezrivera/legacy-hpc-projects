Project Summary

Regarding needs, MWL seeks to provide emergency and sustainable interventions to help the people in need in different locations in NS with coordination and collaboration with WASH, Health, Education and Protection clusters, and acting NGOs.
MWL intervention will target 150,622 BNFs (28,803 HHs, 27,574 Men, 37,066 Women, 39,542 Boys, 46,440 Girls) living in 35 camps and 9 communities in Idleb and Aleppo with WASH services and Hygiene promotion activities in camps and communities as well as in schools and health care units. MWL's strategy is to reduce the negative impacts of water scarcity on most affected populations in NWS in addition to covering families affected by earthquakes and decreasing the AWD/Cholera epidemic through conducted 2 strategies of approaches (Lifesaving and Sustainable solutions).
First Lifesaving approach to 26,700 BNFs (5.340 HHs):
MWLA will cover and support camps and IDP informal settlements with WASH lifesaving activities:
1. Water trucking between 3 to 6 months (35 L/Per/day) with distribution of communal and private water tanks.
2. Re-activate solid waste management through the distribution and installation of waste containers with a capacity of 100L to every 10 families and connecting waste daily.
3. Construction/rehabilitation of latrines with attention to people with special needs through ramps and special toilets, in addition to providing a lighting system to keep latrines lights on at night. Moreover, desludging the septic tanks regularly.
4. Distribute Hygiene items and conduct Hygiene promotion sessions.
Second Sustainable approach to 123,922 BNFs (23,463 HHs):
MWL will target communities, camps, and residential communities to create a dignified living environment, including:
1. Extension/construction of sewage networks and extension/construction of drinking water networks.
2. Rehabilitation of water stations and the provision of operating systems or necessary operating costs of Fuel, chlorine, and periodic maintenance, in addition in order to contribute to decreasing the climate change effects reduction and creating sustainable access to the provided service, and decreasing the operation costs to 95%.
3. To reduce the negative impacts of open and unsafe wastewater on the environment and society MWL will design and implement wastewater treatment interventions for re-using for agricultural purposes or safe disposal in public networks, in addition to providing rehabilitation to the solid waste landfills and to help combat water scarcity.
Furthermore, MWL will extend its WASH interventions to schools and healthcare facilities, aiming to combat the cholera epidemic and other diseases. This initiative is designed to cultivate a healthier and safer environment for children and the underserved sectors among the beneficiaries. Such efforts are critical in addressing public health concerns.
As part of the planning and execution process for these initiatives across the specified locations, MWL is committed to actively engaging with the community. This will involve organizing sessions with all groups to ensure their voices are heard and considered. Establishing WASH community representative committees is a key strategy in this engagement process. By doing so, MWL seeks to ensure that the design and implementation of its activities are both effective and reflective of the community's needs. This collaborative approach is essential to achieving sustainable improvements in WASH services and public health outcomes.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

During the implementation of the project intervention, MWL will conduct the following:-Enhance access to life-saving emergency WASH services for 35 camps within Idleb and Aleppo, targeting 5,340 households (26,700 individuals) through water trucking, distribution of water tanks, waste containers, managing wastewater disposal, deporting waste, cleaning latrines, and construction and rehabilitation of latrines with attention to persons with special needs. In line with this, establish WASH Community Representation Committees (CRC) comprising 3-5 members in each camp of men, women, and persons with disabilities. All the planned activities will be in consultation with disability and females in most need of lighting, ramps, and distance of WASH services in camps.MWL will empower women in committees through targeted training on hygiene promotion that includes clean water usage, sanitation, and hygiene management. Women will acquire practical skills to lead awareness campaigns and advocate for women's health. Also, the targeted camps will receive non-food items through the distribution of hygiene items and boost awareness of preventive behaviors for communicable diseases through hygiene promotion sessions.-MWL will construct and extend water networks in communities and camps benefiting 123,922 individuals, rehabilitate and upgrade water stations to decrease operation costs by 95%, and contribute to climate change mitigation. Based on WASH cluster recommendations, MWL will cover operational and maintenance costs for water systems.-MWL will enlarge the sewage system in Idleb and Aleppo, establish wastewater treatment interventions for re-use for agricultural purposes or safe disposal, and rehabilitate solid waste landfills in targeted sub-districts.-Implementing WASH initiatives in schools and healthcare facilities, including latrine rehabilitation, desludging services, and hygiene awareness for children and healthcare users to combat cholera and other diseases.-Ensuring WASH access in healthcare facilities, constructing latrines considering persons with disabilities, desludging services, and hygiene awareness sessions.-Regarding PSEAH, MWL will conduct awareness activities about safe, accessible, and child-sensitive reporting mechanisms for all project participants. -MWL will conduct meetings, one in each sub-district and camp, targeting local councils, key personnel, and camp management (FGDs) with project participants on inclusion and community engagement. Also, awareness sessions in targeted sub-districts on accountability modules targeting the most vulnerable categories such as women, girls, and PWDs. As for indirect project participants, MWL will also include students’ and teachers’ families, vendors, and the community.The MEAL plan, aligned with the project's log frame, involves on-site monitoring visits for all activities, a Baseline Assessment for WASH services, and an Endline assessment to evaluate impact.
