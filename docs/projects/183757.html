Project Summary

CARE in partnership with two local organizations, Women’s Affairs Center (WAC) and the Women's Center for Legal Aid and Counselling (WCLAC), will directly respond to the previously established objectives outlined in the HRP 2022 (Cluster 2 Protection Mainstreaming). The proposed set of intervention seeks to attend to the Protection needs of the most vulnerable community members in the West Bank/EJ & Gaza , with a focus on women, children, female youth and people with disabilities (PWDs), the specific target group in need of reporting and S/GBV and PSEA referral, as well as legal aid / representation in cases of reported violations, GBV emergency health services that include both MPHSS and SRH services, as well as emergency cash assistance (Cash Voucher Assistance / CVA) for GBV survivors, of whom the selected number will also receive income generating support through a holistic package of activities that will support their business ideation and development processes. In compliment to direct service provision on the community-level, CARE will also work with humanitarian staff, the 1st responders, activists and Women Leadership Councils (WLCs in 4 refugee camps, 2 Gaza, 1 West Bank (WB) and 1 East Jerusalem) who specifically work in GBV, to first increase their capacity in gender and PWD inclusion specifically with respect to emergency situations, and then to support and enhance their, as well as GBV survivors’ self-care and stress management skills.
CARE/partners have a long-standing experience and expertise in implementing said activities with the selected target groups, and here join together to address the existing needs and priorities synergistically. WCLAC, which already plays a prominent role in addressing GBV in Palestinian society in both the public and private spheres, under this intervention will provide legal aid, social counselling, protection and referral services to 100 GBV survivors, and will conduct capacity-building activities for 125 participants, WLC members, humanitarian staff and activists (i.e. journalists, etc) to support their inclusion of gender and PWD-focused services, and with WAC will jointly provide MHPSS services (1,050 total, 800 women, 200 girls, and 50 WWD/GWD). Also with WAC, it will work with 200 service providers who are front-liners working in response to GBV, MHPSS and CP to enhance their self-care and stress management capacities. WAC, who works to promote women’s rights and gender equality at the individual and community levels in Gaza, will provide SRH services to further strengthen response to GBV survivors, in coordination with MoH. CARE will provide CVA to help meet the most urgent needs of the GBV survivors, identified by partners in this project through referral pathways and the eligibility criteria, developed in cooperation with relevant actors like MoSD, and GBV sub-cluster. CARE will utilize unrestricted cash modality, supporting 500 GBV survivors with $100, once throughout the life of the project. CARE has global expertise in CVA and all modalities of assistance, as well as technical knowledge of GBV integration, and well developed CVA standards operating procedures, codes of conduct, protocols/trainings on PSEA with regards to GBV referrals so is able to monitor risks and service delivery in terms of ensuring safe access and delivery mechanisms, post-delivery Monitoring mechanism (PDM) and feedback mechanisms. To further their economic enablement, CARE will also bring in its well-established programming in women’s economic empowerment to provide a package of income generating services, including coaching, business plan, in-kind tools, as necessary, to 30 participants, focus on GBV survivors, who have the skills, business plans, and willingness to establish business ventures. Finally, CARE will also provide dignity kits to 200 GBV survivors (100 in WB, 100 Gaza).
The proposed intervention will be targeting 4,470 vulnerable community members in Gaza and the West Bank.

1 - Needs

The design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of target communities, based on a number of key sources, including the data led by the protection cluster needs analysis/HNO 2022, CARE’s discussions with partners and stakeholders working in protection against GBV who have articulated the existing gaps vis-à-vis protection mainstreaming that is both gender and PWD-inclusive, and the shortage of support for the most vulnerable community members who experience violence, and/or are survivors of GBV. CARE also has conducted a rapid needs analysis that included several meetings and discussions with targeted community members, as well as MoSD. The assessments took into considerations all new needs/implications of the COVID-19 crisis, as well as of the most recent conflict in Gaza, on the protection-focused services provided in the identiﬁed locations/communities and/or the capacity of service providers to provide life-saving protection to women, girls, men and boy in response to their experiences of GBV. In 2020-21, CARE conducted several gender assessments whose findings reiterated the crucial need for support to both service providers working in emergency response to GBV who could not answer the increasing demand for GBV services, which has led to experiences of stress and burnout, and to community members facing said violations. With the pandemic largely overburdening the healthcare system and infrastructure as well as the workload of healthcare providers, certain services that are preventative and life-saving, including the emergency SRH and MHPSS are not easily accessible to those in immediate need. According to the HNO-2022 “It is anticipated that the COVID-19 caseload will continue to burden the healthcare system and based on 2021 trends, (1) Therefore, it is likely that protection services will be further out of reach. Additionally, the ongoing exposure to violence across the oPt, and most recently to conflict in Gaza has had considerable repercussions on the mental health of community members., particularly children. The HNO-1022 indicates that there are “some 678,000 children (345,800 boys and 332,200 girls) across the oPt in need of child protection and MHPSS services, including 122,0007 in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services and 185,000 caregivers overall, including 137,000 caregivers in Gaza, who are also in need of various MHPSS services (specialized case management, especially for adolescents and caregivers, with a particular focus on males). CARE also looked at the oPt response plan for COVID-19, the COVID-19 Global HRP, COVID-19 related social stigma, IASC MHPSS Guide for COVID-19 responders, MHPSS IASC guidelines for COVID -19, and the UN Women’s study “The UN Women Gender Alert (4) (2021),” which further highlighted that the women’s mental health is disproportionately more affected by the situation than the men’s”. In Gaza particularly, even prior to the escalation, but subsequent to the COVID-19 pandemic, amid increased pressure on access to basic services, households violence and negative coping mechanisms, there was a reported quadrupling of suicide rates especially amongst adolescent girls, and according to some surveys 82% of adolescents in Gaza reported overall poor-very poor mental well-being (WHO/Juzoor 2021). The conflict has increased the risk of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), depression, anxiety, behavioral problems for all residents, with those displaced, PWDs and GBV survivors being identified as most vulnerable, given their exposure to increased threats. Moreover, adults and children with disabilities face additional barriers that limits their access to services that puts them in additional risk. In Gaza, 63% of persons with disability lack of accessibility to mental health and psychosocial support services (HNO-2022). Hence the need for MHPSS services that respond to distress, including psychological first aid (PFA), suicide prevention, specialized case management, and clinical mental health services. In one year, between May 2020 and May 2021, SAWA an emergency hotline reported a 135% increase in their caseload. According to the HNO-2022, the areas with the lowest % of households reporting availability of PSS services in case of GBV were East Jerusalem (8,9%), Gaza (31,6%) and Area C (38,8%). In Gaza, 58% of persons with disabilities lack of Information about GBV prevention and response and where to access said services. In communities such as Gaza, Area C and East Jerusalem, women and girls tend to suffer from higher incidents of GBV, and women and girls with disabilities (WWD/GWD), Bedouin women, as well as the internally displaced women and girls, as well as those residing in refugee camps face an amalgamation of other risks, including lack of access to services, including emergency SRH and legal aid, and the lack of livelihood opportunities, the latter of which keeps many in poverty, unable to meet their basic needs, and trapped in the cycle of dependency and abuse. Those working directly and indirectly with the affected populations of conflict and violence, and with survivors of S/GBV are likely to be secondarily exposed to traumatic events and to experience symptoms of secondary trauma. Therefore, it is crucial to provide them with skills and knowledge that support and advance their internal coping mechanisms. Moreover, based on recent discussions with partners and service providers, as well as CARE and OCHA’s 2020 synthesis report there is a significant need to further develop gender analysis throughout the HPC process, as well as PWD-inclusive programming and services, as to ensure inclusive response for all community members.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Service delivery and Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA) are the most suitable modalities to address the identified protection needs in the targeted communities. Direct provision of said services cover/ address most of the access barriers that were identified in the different assessments including MSNA, HNO-2022 and CARE’s assessments. All identified barriers, including physical access for most of the targeted communities due to either physical barriers (whether imposed by the existing geo-political separation and checkpoints across oPt, and/or potential risk of conflict and violence, or lack of infrastructure that would allow PWDs to access said services), or due to the lack of available services or cultural stigma vis-à-vis mental health needs, are addressed through the direct provision of protection services The CVA modality targets GBV survivors to help cover their urgent needs. As they may be vulnerable groups with additional risks or more marginalized (i.e. PWDs), and lacking access to livelihood opportunities, this modality provides economic support, as part of GBV prevention and response, enabling access to life-saving, and livelihood services for those at risk. CARE has a vast experience and technical expertise in GBV integration and to utilize CVA, and developed CVA SoP, CoC, protocols/trainings on PSEA with regards to GBV referrals so is able to monitor risks and service delivery in terms of ensuring safe access and delivery. CARE re-established connections with the CWG coordinator in Gaza, discussing further engagement and coordination with the GBV sub-cluster. WAC has long experience in CVA. CARE/Partners will monitor cash disbursement process over counter in bank Of Palestine to ensure that cash is disbursed ONLY to targeted women and they received correct amount. Beneficiaries will also be engaged pre and post distribution around risks and potential backlash to ensure there is no harm, and about priorities to meet their urgent needs.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

CARE is committed to assuring accountability to stakeholders in all aspects and phases of the project. At the inception stage, community members helped inform the design of project activities, the composition of the teams, frequency and type of the services, urgent health issues, etc. CARE/partners also took into consideration the different health needs of the different groups residing in the targeted communities. Monitoring has been carried out through the on sight monitoring of distributions and activities, and it includes segregation of data by sex, age and disability (SADD). CARE’s Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM) builds on our previous experience with humanitarian accountability, and it is in-formed by standards and guidance to ensure quality and accountability to our partners and stakeholders, and to support existing organizational and technical standards, with which to bring about sustainable change to communities in need. It was designed and piloted in other projects within CARE allowing targeted population to reach out to CARE for any feedback or complaint about the project activities, criteria, services, behavior by any of the project’s staff CARE will continue to raise community awareness regarding FAM, regularly reporting on feedback and complaints as part of CARE’s continuous learning agenda. CARE has started the distribution of the FAM cards in the targeted communities and started to receive feedback/complaints. Feedback/complaints are documented in a log sheet by the Focal Point / Accountability Officer responsible for monitoring the progress of each input until the feedback loop is closed. The data will be reported and used for management decisions and analyzing trends. CARE also is planning to conduct a community-based survey that will further assess accessibility of the mechanism, as well as preferred channels of communication; a patient satisfaction survey will be conducted to see the feedback to enable CARE to respond accordingly.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

CARE’s ‘Do No Harm’ principles are embedded in all approaches and interventions by CARE, from the ideation process of a project, throughout its duration and after the project is finished. CARE has identified risks, their potential impact and mitigation strategies, and all activities proposed are designed based on numerous discussions with community members, partners and other stakeholders, as well as on needs analyses (i.e. rapid / gender needs assessment) that are sensitive to the complexities of the local context to ensure that people are not exposed to additional risks through our action. CARE is committed to protection and ensuring safety and security of participants, dignity and accessibility of services; CARE’s activities are inclusive, and designed with the community members’ feedback, and the selection criteria for activities are transparent; the data collection processes are sensitive to the context, power dynamics in communities, and also the management of sensitive information is conducted with caution and care.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PWD/GBV survivors/women and other vulnerable groups are considered as main targeted vulnerable groups in the proposed project, as they were identiﬁed by as vulnerable groups who needs special consideration, for that the project team will work with the communities’ representatives and actors in the different communities to ensure equal access of PWD/women/others to all projects’ activities and will ensure the full inclusion of PWD/women/other in all project activities. PWD/women/others will have special attention when identifying/selecting the beneficiaries. The team will ensure that meetings locations in the different communities are accessible for PWD/women/other. Home visits will also be planned for those who can’t access the projects activities and have the potential to be part of the project, this to be coordinated with the local committees and local councils. All project activities will be implemented based on a clear criteria that ensure transparency, equal and impartial access to the aid
