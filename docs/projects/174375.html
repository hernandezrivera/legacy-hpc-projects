Project Summary

The project seeks to fill the gaps of CCCM Sector in line with the centrality of protection strategy and will target 120,000 women, men, boys and girls IDPs living in the camps, camp-like settings, IDPs out of camps and host community that received the IDPs and dwelling in their houses. The project will cover 4 LGAs in Borno (Ngala, Munguno, Banki and Damasak), 4 LGAs in Adamawa (Yola South, Yola North, Mubi and Madagali) and 2 LGAs in Yobe state (Damaturu and Gujba). The breakdown of the target for this CCCM project is 97,298 individuals (19,460HH) for IDPs and 22,702 individuals (4,540 HH) for returnees distributed as follow: 46% (male), 54% (female), 60% (children), 36% (adult) and 4% (elders). The CCCM Project will be addressing the gaps of tracking movement of population (new arrivals and returnees) particularly the most vulnerable including unaccompanied and separated children, assessing the needs of the women, men, boys, girls, adolescents and older displaced persons in a participatory manner and based on age, gender and diversity, (AGD) mainstreaming, supporting the coordination of response of service providers, capacitating the communities through their effective participation and ownership of activities and developing the resilience and self-reliance when they are still in displacement. The project will also focus on the sensitization of IDPs especially youth, boys, girls, adolescents and women who are facing different problem of protection. The CCCM project will improve the quality of management of the women, men, boys and girls IDPs within the camp settings, monitor and assist those out of camps and some vulnerable persons in host community. The project will support systematic follow up of the IDPs and returnees' new arrivals particularly vulnerable women and girls. The project seeks to provide quality services during the registration process and will ensure that reception facilities are equally accessible to women, men, boys and girls newly arrived. Camp managers will ensure proper relocation of new arrivals from the reception centers to the shelters constructed taking into account the needs of the most vulnerable. Through this project, CCCM interventions will also assess, analyze and report on all protection gaps and needs to be addressed by different sectors in order to improve the living conditions of women, men, girls and boys displaced, including the vulnerable populations in out of camp status. The CCCM project will promote the participation of the IDPs based on age, gender and diversity in the life cycle of camps particularly care and maintenance of infrastructures, small scale drainage, etc.

1 - Needs

There are total of 281 camps in BAY states with Borno having highest number of camps and IDPS. Based on the trends, 40% of the total IDP population live in camps. In addition to this, the returning refugees from the neighboring Niger, Chad and Cameroon who are unable to go back to their original homes due to the insurgency suffer secondary displacement and find themselves in already over-congested camps. Moreover, there are still 129 camps without site facilitation in place, while over 1.0m people with site facilitation needs are trapped in inaccessible and hard to reach areas across the region. Unfortunately, even in camps where CCCM activities take place, the demand for proper site facilitation which meets SPHERE standards is yet to be achieved irrespective of the remarkable progress made in the past few years. The presence of partners and support team to cope with the huge need is limited. The coordination mechanism is yet to pick up in many of the sites, hence the synergy between different actors remain challenging. There is a need for UNHCR in collaboration with NEMA, SEMA and the sector to advocate for more funding for CCCM to ensure there is dedicated and proper site facilitation to promote the reinforcement of camp committees, support beneficiaries in identifying needs and gaps, training of beneficiaries and launch innovative approaches to ensure that camp committees have necessary CCCM tools to identify gaps and refer them across sectors. The lack of effective feedback mechanisms in various camps and the lack of continuous training/coaching for site facilitators, camp managers and community members hamper continuous improvement of quality of governance structures in different camps. The absence of innovations and knowledge for IDPs to take their destiny into their hands when the humanitarian assistance phases out is a challenge. There is critical need to build their capacity in resilience and self-reliance and develop the ownership to manage the activities within the displacement sites and in the host communities; and to link where possible the camp activities with public services such as education, health and public works as this is one way in which CCCM activities can link with early recovery and development nexus. The level of congestion in most of the IDPs camps in Borno state remains a great concern. This is mainly due to the challenge in accessing secure land and the inability to provide functional security systems to large camps. The scenario requires a more structured and secured sites/settlements to ensure that internal security mechanism and various camp committee can function effectively in a more comprehensive way. The care and maintenance mechanisms interventions in the various camps also require review. The alarming rate at which new arrivals continue to access safer LGAs even in this period of COVID19, has significantly overwhelmed CCCM needs in camps and in out-of-camps setting. This results in over-congestion and a pile of unmet needs, as well as stretching the national authorities’ capacity to facilitate CCCM component of emergency response. As such, duplication of services, insufficient advocacy for services, lack of proper coordination among implementing and operating partners to provide appropriate and timely assistance, and lack of quality information to enable joint planning and evidence-based response. Additionally, poor site planning in many of the sites contribute to untimely response particularly during natural hazard occurrences such as fires, flooding and storms which leave the already vulnerable population more devastated. There are also unmaintained, abandoned, damaged or dilapidated camp structures and infrastructures that will continuously require urgent decommissioning (for example filled up latrines, blocked water drainage system or the lack of drainage channels).

2 - Activities

1. Site facilitation support in the camp to ensure all the partners across the camps have harmonized and coordinated approach to avoid duplication, overlapping and proper intervention and response in the camps 2. Support the tracking and the monitoring of displacement and information on needs gaps for follow up on displaced population and referral across sectors. 2. Camp management and camp coordination capacities reinforced 3. Support the maintenance, upgrade and care, decongestion of sites through site planning, improvements when required and coordinated assistance 4. Support relocation and return of IDPs 5. Community empowerment and Community mobilization for participation and ownership of activities within camps and camp-like settings 6. Community based capacity building on resilience and self-reliance 7. Provision of protection and physical protection measures in camps and camp- like settings 8. Regular monitoring and evaluation of activities carried out and response provided 9. Construction of reception centre in return areas. 10. UNHCR and the various stakeholders will therefore advocate where possible the provision of new secured plot with more standardized site planning or ensure the current site planning are reviewed. The community participation will be reinforced also through the coaching of the committee leaders. 11. UNHCR will support actors in tracking returns that will contribute to the development and implementation of a durable solutions strategy that will ensure that the humanitarian community address the return and the relocation process in more comprehensive and effective way. 12. UNHCR will implement the out-of-camp strategy to reach out to the vulnerable populations living within the host community, and through the area-based approach impact on the host community too.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection mainstreaming will be an important element of this project from the designed, implementation, monitoring ang reporting faces. This will be achieved through the following modalities; 1. The safety and dignity of our population of concern who are the right owners will be prioritized. All faces of the project must be analysed to prioritize and respect safety and dignity of persons of concern. 2. Also the project will provide ample access the rights owners through the project cycle. 3. Furthermore, there will be continues engagement with the right owners and setup of information centre at UNHCR protection desks to provide a channel for feedback to inform reassessment of needs to ensure the real needs of the people are addressed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR adheres to the humanitarian principles of accountability to affected populations by promoting and supporting community engagement in camp management and through implementing accountability systems such as complaint and feedback mechanisms. UNHCR and partners conduct protection and assistance monitoring, and collect information from partners, community members and Persons of Concern on regular basis, in order to have direct feedback on UNHCR’s interventions. During this project, Accountability to affected populations will be enhanced through strengthening of community-based feedback mechanisms; supporting partners to implement an all-inclusive, functional and accessible complaints and feedback mechanisms, convening frequent meetings with the community leaders, and beneficiaries through focus group discussions with women, girls, boys, and men, including adolescents to enable UNHCR and partners to gather accurate information on the specific needs and protection risks that different groups face, identify underlying causes, understand capacities, and hear proposed solutions. The process helps communities to take collective action to enhance their own protection and makes it possible to implement a right- and community-based approach.
