Project Summary

The number of people in need for general protection has increased by 26 per cent in comparison to the HNO 2021 PiN. The increase is primarily attributed to the increase in population by 26 per cent as well as increase in IDP population by 300,000 individuals. Over a quarter (26 per cent) of the people in need are located in Banadir because the area is densely populated with 2.7 million people, of which 904,000 are IDPs. The second and third districts with the highest people in need are Baidoa and Galkacyo, with almost 15 per cent of the people in these two districts. More than half (39) out of 74 of the total districts and also10 regions, including Banadir, Bakool, Bay, Gedo, Hiraan, Lower Juba, Lower Shabelle, Middle Juba, Middle Shabelle, and Sanaag have the most acute protection needs with ‘emergency level’ needs at severity score 4. While 29 districts, accounting for 39 per cent of the total district in Somalia, have ‘crises level’ needs of severity score 3. Conflict, Displacement and Protection of Civilians. This situation is exacerbated by the limited availability of basic services, weakened governance, negative social norms, and persistent marginalization.359 Protection of Civilians remains an important humanitarian issue across the country and deserves more consideration in humanitarian decision making and planning exercises.
Somalia humanitarian crises continues to remain protracted and recurrent crises, with devastating impact on affected populations especially for the internally displaced population across the country. South-west state of Somalia remains the epic centre of the humanitarian crises and currently has the highest caseload of IDPs in the country (400,000 IDPs). According to UNOCHA displacement Report 2019, there are huge unmet humanitarian needs in key urban locations hosting conflict and drought displaced IDP in Baidoa and Wajid districts in the south West state of Somalia. The triggers of the displacement are on-going conflicts against Al-Shabaab Insurgency as well as climatic shock in the country, Serious protection concerns, and violations persist which is putting civilian’s life at risk forcing many to displacement and heightened protections threats/risks. Abuse against civilians including Gender based violence (GBV), recruitment and use of children into armed forces and groups, forced early marriage, FGM and forced displacement remain a pervasive feature of the conflict in Somalia.
Displaced population in South West state of Somalia continues to face heighten protection risks and threats, Children and women are particularly impacted by the extreme difficult living conditions with inadequate social services which has resulted psychological distress compounded with physical and sexual violence against women and girls, abuse and exploitation of the IDP population and insecurity in the overcrowded IDP sites. According to UNHCR’s Protection and Return Monitoring Network, between November 2018 and May 2019, a total of 789,000 people has been displaced by the drought, the majority of drought-related displacement takes place from rural to urban areas and according to PRMN, more than 226,000 people arrived from (Jan-Aug 2019) in search of water and food, having travelled by foot, in donkey carts and trucks. Most of the newly displaced families (80% of them) come from villages in the Bay, Bakool and lower Shabelle region and have joined existing settlements for internally displaced in Bay and Bakool. Family separation remains a cause of concern as families move in search of livelihood. The project targets a total of 4700 children/ 2350 boys and 2350 girls including those with disabilities with protection services for a period of 12 months of the implementation

1. - Projects activities

1. Provision of the structured Psychosocial support through 3 CFS for 2400 children (1200 boys and 1200 girls) including those with disabilities in target locations. Those with disabilities and the most vulnerable ones will be included in to the project by comprehensive consultation with the primary caregivers and the alternate ones whenever applicable at all stages of the project right from the needs assessment stages as well as throughout the implementation stages including regular monitoring exercises to ensure all their concerns are covered by the project. 2. Provision of comprehensive case management services to 1800 children (900 Boys and 900 Girls) vulnerable or at-risk as well as those living with disabilities 3. Provision of IDTR service for 500 Unaccompanied and separated children/Boys and girls including those with disability in the targeted locations. 4. Conduct comprehensive safety audits for WASH, Health, Nutrition, Camp management and Shelter sectors in key IDP sites in Baidoa, and Wajid 5. Conduct a series of the community declaration events for the dissemination of the key messages to the community members and well as use of the local media outlet to disseminate the messages. 6. Facilitate a series of capacity Building workshops for the PSS Animators, Social workers, teacher and child protection officers on PSS, Case management, child rights and Gender based violence

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1. Ensuring inclusion of minority and marginalized groups such as minority clans/women, men, boys and girls, IDPs, the elderly and Persons with disabilities in the consultations on the project right from to incorporate their concerns in to the project and further ensure their concerns are are incorporated into the project. The mentioned groups will be involved in the project at all stages of implementation right from needs identifications, regular monitoring where their views, opinions and concern will form key part of decision regarding the agreed suggestions and recommendations made for specific actions for the project implementation.2. Protection of young girls from sexual violence by ensuring timely access to protection services for appropriate responses. In case of security concerns, the project will be implemented in close coordinaton with the community structures such as elders and local administration/ police units from the Federal Government States and where necessary involving the Somali National forces and AMISOM. The project implementation will further ensure strict adherence to PSEA policy and ensuring young girls and boys are fully aware of the project to mitigate cases of sexual exploitation and abuse during the project. The project will in addition ensure that complaints and feedback mechanisms are in place for easy access by the targeted beneficiaries and other community members.
