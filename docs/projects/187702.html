Project Summary

The objective of the project is to provide children adolescents, and adults with the tools and space to improve their mental and psychosocial wellbeing, identify and manage stress, and develop positive coping mechanisms in order for them to better cope with the stress and potential trauma that they are likely facing in their daily lives . Structured and sustained PSS activities targeting children/adolescents will be conducted in DCAs Community Safe Space (CSS) through art, music, drama, sports, storytelling, games and structured activities aiming to develop feelings of safety and trust. Moreover, provision of case management in the case basis and need basis will conducted. Adolescents who are unable to attend the CSS will be reached with awareness sessions focusing on stress management in their schools, sports clubs and/or youth centers. Additionally, parents/caregivers of children and adolescents receiving support will be provided with awareness sessions focusing specially on child protection issues. Individuals who require further assistance will be referred to specialized mental health services for follow up. Furthermore, DCA will facilitate a new intervention, support groups, where beneficiaries can help each other by discussing some of their problems and successful coping skills.

4 - Needs

Across the NES, almost 75.57% of households report having psychological disorder such as being nervous, irritable, worried or sadness, hopelessness, and other things. With continuous exposure to violence, deepening poverty and persistent lack of access to services there is an increased possibility of adopting harmful mechanisms. Children/ adolescents are especially susceptible as their development can be negatively impacted by prolonged/ protracted exposure to stress. An additional factor negatively impacting children and adolescents is caregivers’ limited capacity to adjust parenting techniques to handle difficult behavior because of stress. As per 2021 HNO, psychosocial distress among children is reported in 26 % of surveyed households, demonstrating the increasing cumulative toll on mental well-being with immediate and if not address, lifelong consequences. On the basis of sex, HHs reporting psychological stress among women 70%, men 67%, girls 42% and boys 39% in NES. Most of the respondent expressed being worried most of the time, having bad dreams, and feeling angry all the time. From the monthly data collected in the field by DCA, sleeping difficulties, persistent low mood, and constant anxiety were the most evident symptoms reported by beneficiaries.

5 - Implementation of activities

Structured and sustained PSS and CP activities targeting vulnerable boys and girls (aged 5-12) (following a 4- week curriculum). Activities will promote support through art, music, drama, sports, storytelling, and games. Provision of awareness sessions and parenting programmes to parents and caregivers focusing on topics such as CP,good parenting techniques and dealing with children who are displaying negative coping mechanisms. Knitting/handcrafts sessions will be offered within the CSS to some female adult participants as a wellbeing activity. This offers them a safe environment to engage in productive activities and receive support from other participants. Support groups will be provided for some of the follow up cases. Complex cases requiring follow up will be provided with more specialized PSS activities such as PFA, lay counselling and referral to specialized mental health services. Provision of case management to 590 beneficiaries in line with sector standards and guidelines will be conducted as need. Specialized mental health services will be provided by partner organizations that have the capacity to provide focused specialized services in medical facilities. DCA PSS teams will conduct regular M&E surveys to assess the impact of PSS activities. Children will be asked to participate in pre-post surveys, whereas adolescents attending awareness session in their school or sports club/ youth centers will be asked to participate in a post awareness satisfaction survey.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA Staff membersLikelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation Measures: DCA Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community and caregivers about provision of services that are available and who services are for. DCA staff will provide regular trainings to project staff on Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DCA with its established complaint box will allow staff, partners, or communities members to raise complaint confidentially. These complaints and grievances will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending upon the nature of complaint. Mitigation measures for children against abuse and harassment take place in the CFS through storytelling and developing a good rapport with providers. Children who show possible signs of harassment or abuse are spoken to by the PSS providers in the CFS in a safe reassuring environment. Children are encouraged to share their experiences in indirect ways through drawing and paintings as an alternative to the official complaint’s mechanism.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in locations impact project implementation causing delays and changes in operational areas and threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation measures: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project location, set up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and procedures and conducts regular security- related training for all staff. Management Team remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. DCA’s Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are upto date. If security and access situation deteriorate, the project will prioritize safety of staff and beneficiaries and will shift activities to other locations as and when possible.Risk: Closure of CFSs due to COVID-19 related lockdowns and curfews, causing delays and decreased access. Mitigation measures: DCA has developed an online system of delivery of activities to children and caregivers in case of closures. The system is dependent on platforms such as ZOOM and WhatsApp to allow access to beneficiaries. Risk: Lack of access to school and adolescents due to restrictions by the educational committee. Mitigation Measures: DCA continues to maintain good relationship with the educational committee and informs them about the possible timing and content of the activities. DCA will approached to community leaders and working groups in case of any disapprovals to mediate.
