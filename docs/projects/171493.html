Project Summary

GBV intensified in the OPT especially in the Gaza Strip (GS) due to the spread of Covid-19 pandemic & the associated procedures including home quarantine & restrictions on movement that led to increase the economic burdens & the loss of livelihoods which increases the psycho-social stress & domestic violence on women & girls (W&Gs). Where more than 1.26 million Palestinians need protection services in GS.
Lack of psychosocial & legal services have led to serious risk on W&G's mental health which makes them more vulnerable to anxiety, stress & depression, especially PWD.
Hence, the project idea is to bridge an important gap of GBV services in the GS & to respond to humanitarian needs of the surviving W&G or at risk of GBV including PWD. Whereas, the project will provide remotely & face-to-face individual PSS & legal consultations, adding to economic empowerment through cash assistance for W&G to mitigate the psychosocial & economic stresses facing them during Covid-19 pandemic. Case management & referral approach will also be followed to provide mental health services & representation in the courts.
The project will also raise the awareness of women, girls, men & boys in multiple psychosocial, legal & health topics, such as PSEA, prevention of Covid-19 infection, GBV, women's rights, self-care mechanisms & other topics to promote women & girls rights & enable them to cope with the current situation & face challenges. The awareness sessions will be conducted face-to-face or remotely through electronic platforms. This in addition to awareness in the above mentioned topics through social media pages targeting PWD by using sign language.
In addition, emergency health-care services will be provided to GBV W&Gs through the UHWC health centers in the project locations.
The project beneficiaries also will be provided with dignity kits to meet their special needs during home quarantine, the kits will include PPE to prevent them from Covid-19 infection.
To achieve the principle of protection, many of the services will be provided remotely to reduce the spread of the Covid-19 with ensuring that the project staff will have training on providing services remotely. They will also have training on PSEA to ensure the maximum protection of the beneficiaries.
From the other side, & to alleviate the pressure of the project staff, self-care training will be implemented.
However, concerning providing the services within the center, all prevention measures will be followed to ensure the safety of the staff & the beneficiaries.
To expand the protection for W&Gs, a number of non-specialized humanitarian workers & members of local committees will be trained on protection mainstreaming, detection & referral of GBV cases.
To achieve accountability & community participation principle, 50 beneficiaries will be contacted via phone at the midterm of the project to take feedback that contributes to the project’s monitoring & to modify the project activities if needed, furthermore, 100 E-questionnaires will be filled by the beneficiaries at the midterm & the end of the project in order to measure their satisfaction & take advantage of their suggestions for future interventions.
In information sharing with the community, the project will be announced on the UHWC website & facebook pages adding to radio advertisement.
In order to ensure the implementation of the project activities in the various circumstances related to the COVID19 pandemic, the UHWC has developed an emergency plan to GBV services to work in light of the crisis, it includes 3 scenarios (i) acute emergency (ii) medium emergency (iii) post-emergency & coexistence, therefore the project activities will be flexible to these scenarios.
It is worth to mention that, during the design of the project coordination has been done with the GBV-Sub Working Group Coordinator as well as with CFTA & Wefaq associations to avoid any duplication in the beneficiaries & locations.

1 - Needs

The HNO 2021 indicated that 1.26 million Palestinians are in need to protection services due to the spread of Covid-19 pandemic that has exacerbated the situation in the Gaza Strip (GS). Fear to get infected with Covid-19 creates emotional stress on the women & the procedures relating to the pandemic led to increased risks of MH problems, anxiety, tension & depression on girls & women especially PWD. A study conducted by AISHA Assosication in April 2020, indicated that there is a significant increase in the prevalence of GBV to 47%. The rate of economic violence was the highest rate of violence against women, reaching 61%, followed by psychological violence by 52%. The procedures relating Covid-19 pandemic have negatively affected the economic situation of the GS's families, particularly women-headed households which represent 10% of them. It should be noted that 54% of those families already suffering from poverty before the spread of Covid-19 pandemic.(PNGO August 2020) Psychological pressures on women have indeed been increasing as a result of increased work & household burdens during home quarantine. A study conducted by UN Women in April 2020 shows that 68% of the surveyed women reported that household burdens increased on them as 52% of them reported that the burdens related to childcare also increased. On the other hand, GBV rates increased as a result of stopping Sharia courts work which also led to psychological pressures & human suffering for women especially the custody of children cases, as many women were deprived of custody or watching their children. Due to the imposed restrictions on mobility & curfew since the start of Covid-19 crisis, MHPSS services are scaled down for GBV survivors in the GS, creating a response gap for both survivors & those at risk of GBV. Needs assessment conducted by UHWC in Oct. 2020, targeted 50 women from GS, indicates that 90% of the participating women emphasis that home quarantine cause them psychological pressures such as those resulting from stigma and not accepting the community of them due to their infection with Covid-19. The UHWC's protection program for women and girls has a significant element in UHWC’s strategic plan 2020-2024, the 7 community facilities deployed across the governorates of the Gaza Strip, strives to continue providing protection services to women and girls who are survivors/at risk of GBV. Whereas, psycho-social, legal services in addition to health-care services are provided to the women in need. UHWC targets, in its ongoing services, women and girls of different ages, especially those who suffer from marginalization and considered the most vulnerable. Moreover, UHWC has started from 28th September 2020 providing psychological support services to those quarantined in their homes in all Gaza Strip governorates, where services reached 934 people (47% females) until October 19, 2020. Moreover, a study conducted by UHWC over phone calls showed that women, during Covid-19 pandemic, have become weaker and more vulnerable in addition to their suffering from GBV. The study also showed that many women have not received GBV services recently, while most of the researched women called for the necessity to provide psychological, social and legal services remotely during Covid-19 crisis and the difficulty of accessing services.The women also emphasized the importance and necessity of obtaining dignity kits, which contain PPE. Furthermore, they demand for the importance of developing community awareness towards women's issues and rights. Therefore, the project’s idea responds to the needs of protection for W&G in the GS (including PWD), as through the project, MHPSS services, legal advice & economic empowerment will be provided to mitigate psychological, social & economic stresses facing women especially during Covid-19 pandemic. Moreover, the project will raise W&G's awareness in different health, psychological & social issues such as sexual exploitation & abuse (SEA), COVID19 prevention measures, self-care mechanisms & other topics that enhance the rights of women & girls & enable them to cope with the current situation & face challenges. This in addition to awareness in the above mentioned topics through social media pages targeting PWD by using sign language. Emergency health services will be also provided to the GBV cases in the UHWC primary health care centers. They also will be provided with dignity kits to meet their special needs during home quarantine & PPE to prevent them from Covid-19 infection. To achieve the principle of protection for the beneficiaries of the project, many of the services will be provided remotely to reduce the spread of the Covid-19 virus with ensuring that the working teams will have training on providing services remotely. However, concerning providing the services within the center, all protection & prevention measures will be followed to ensure the safety of the workers & the beneficiaries. Under the principle of protection, the working groups will have training on PSEA to ensure the maximum protection of the beneficiaries. To expand the protection for women & girls, a number of non-specialized humanitarian workers and local committees' members will be trained through the project on protection & the detection & referral of GBV cases. As well as, men & boys will be educated about women rights & GBV.

UHWC project department will coordinate with the donor for issues related to the implementation of the project. The UHWC project team will also document the project activities through photos and audio-visual report that will be published at the UHWC website, facebook and YouTube channel at the end of the project. The beneficiaries will also participate in the monitoring and evaluation of the project, as 50 phone calls will be conducted with the project beneficiaries to monitor the project services at the mid of the project where necessary modification will be made to the project activities if necessary. While 100 e-questionnaires will be filled to measure the beneficiaries' satisfaction about the provided services at the midterm and end of the project.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Within the policies and the principles of UHWC in involving the local community to participate in all stages of its programs and projects that are implemented, UHWC adopts complaints and feedback system, which collect the data, complaints and any suggestions from the beneficiaries and the community members through using the following tools: 1- The complaints boxes, which are located in all UHWC's health and community facilities. 2- Generic email 3- Hotline. 4- Paper-based or electronic questionnaires, which are distributed to measure the beneficiaries ’satisfaction about the provided services in the UHWC’s facilities. Under normal circumstances, UHWC relies on receiving complaints through complaint boxes distributed in all its health & community facilities.Due to COVID-19 pandemic & the restriction of movement, UHWC has developed complaints mechanisms to help the beneficiaries of its services to file complaints & suggestions remotely, through: 1- Generic email.2- Hotline.3- Electronic questionnaires.Complaints management mechanism: Complaints are managed through the following mechanism: - Enable receiving complaints from affected population. - Responding to complaints. - Accountability and lessons learned. In order to engage the local community and encouraging them to file complaints, UHWC announces complaints mechanisms and tools through social media. Through these methods, the beneficiaries' satisfaction can be measured in addition to the most important problems they face while receiving services from the UHWC’s facilities. However, the feedback is taken by the beneficiaries to determine their suggestions to improve the quality of the services in terms of both quantitative and qualitative. Whereas, beneficiaries can use e-mail to file more sensitive complaints about sexual exploitation while receiving services. Special committee from UHWC manage these complaints.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project idea, services and implementation mechanisms start from respecting the principles of protection stipulated in the humanitarian charter including the right to live with dignity, the right to receive assistance and the right to protection. Moreover, these mechanisms are part of the UHWC organizational values within its strategic plan 2020-2024 , in addition to ensuring the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries through: • In order to prevent SEA during the provision of the project services the project staff will attend PSEA training. As well as their contracts will include standard clause on PSEA in accordance with IASC principles and guidance. - In order not to expose the beneficiaries to more harm, they will be educated about SEA and the complaints mechanism. - Equal access and non-discrimination in the provision of health services. • Documentation are private and confidential. • Ensuring that beneficiaries are not harmed while receiving services on the basis of dignity & protection. • Gender needs will be taken into account through the presence of a female staff among the work teams to response the gender needs of women and girls. • Providing work accident insurance for the project staff. • Providing the project staff with health insurance at UHWC health facilities. • The project services will be provided to the beneficiaries regardless their color, race, religious or political affiliation. To achieve the protection during the provision of services in link with COVID19, prevention & safety measures will be followed This includes maintaining social distancing, providing the project staff and beneficiaries with Personal Protective Equipment PPE, in addition to measuring the temperature of the beneficiaries before entering the center, and in the case of suspected cases, they are referred to the health center to conduct IGG and IGM test, and if the test is positive, coordination is made with the Ministry of Health to refer the case to the central laboratory of the ministry.. This is in addition to the continuous disinfection and cleaning procedures inside the center.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The UHWC adopts the principle of equality and impartially in providing its services to beneficiaries, especially marginalized groups that need humanitarian interventions to improve their health, psychosocial and humanitarian status. UHWC provides its services without any discrimination on the basis of age, sex, religion, ethic, race or political affiliation. Knowing that through this project and to achieve the principle of impartiality in the provision of services UHWC will provide the services to the targeted people on the basis that each beneficiary has its individual characteristics and special needs, especially individuals with disabilities as the most vulnerable in the society and who need special attention and protection depend on Human dignity, confidentiality and non-discrimination. Access to the project services will be easy according to the following:Accessibility: The Union of Health Work Committees will provide the project services through 2 of its community facilities in Southern governorate of Gaza strip (Khanyounis and Rafa). The centers are accessible and are known to the public since their establishment. The project services will be provided to the target groups including PWD in a safety and dignity manner, and without any discrimination. Access to information: Based on the principle of transparency with the local community, the project will be announced through the UHWC website and social media. Availability: the services will be available face of face or remotely in link with COVID19 pandemic. Affordability: All the project services will be provided totally free of charge. Acceptability: All the services that the project will provide express an urgent need for the beneficiaries, making it more acceptable as a priority of the community and do not conflict with the values, principles, ethics and customs of the community.
