Project Summary

This project will provide support to the extremely vulnerable, conflict-affected girls, women, boys, and men in Ayod and Fangak who are exposed to gender-based violence and protection risks. CAO has a presence and filed offices in Ayod and Fangak. CAO access Fangak and Ayod through UNHASS, water transport, and during dry season Ayod can be accessed by road. This project programme will includes provision of comprehensive case management to the identified and registered GBV cases, provision of psychosocial support ( PSS), identifying and registering women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns and supported through case management and referral services, conducting referral services to women, men, girls and boys with protection concerns, provision of individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services at the women and girls friendly spaces, establishing women and girls friendly spaces (WFGs) ,Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience to the identified women men, girls and boys, Distribution of dignity kits to women and adolescent girls of the reproductive age as part GBV risk mitigation measure and addressing differential needs, Provision of awareness-raising to women, men, girls and boys on the GBV preventive measures, the project will target 15,000 (3,500 women, 3,500 men, 4,000 girls and 4,000 boys) with the total budget of $300,000.

1 - Needs

Women, Men Girls and boys in South Sudan are disproportionately affected by ongoing conflict and the natural disasters like floods. High levels of displacement of about around 1.8 million internally displaced, an estimated number of 400,000 people to have been killed, and 2.5 million fled to the neighboring countries especially Uganda. leaving Women, Men Girls and boys in psychological distress. Women, Men Girls and boys in are exposed to gender-based violence such as rape, sexual abuses, exploitation, early marriages sexual assault The 2022 HNO states that Protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in dire need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021.Women and children require lifesaving protection services. CAO aims at building more holistic approach to GBV survivors, integrating MHPSS with other sectors like Wash, education Nutrition, health and NFIs.CAO also aims at strengthen community-based support mechanisms where Women men boys and girls can report cases of sexual assault, rape and other abuses as they go to collect water, firewood and wild fruits. In the recent IRNA assessment that was carried out on 31st August to 2nd September 2022 following the conflicts that erupted in Tonga Upper Nile that later escalated to new Fangak that led to thousands of people displaced, the findings shows that there is an increase of Protection risks .The humanitarian situation of IDPs ,returnees and host community are in dire needs of humanitarian services, thousands of people, including persons with limited disability, reportedly left behind or hiding in bushes and river. Among the displaced, the majority are women, children and other vulnerable persons. Civilians fleeing are visibly traumatized and report killings, war injuries, abductions, extortion, looting and heavy damage of public and private infrastructures, burning of properties. Many have lost their lives, property and homes. Cases of GBV reported is growing day by day, this assessment was led by RRC, UNHCR and other partner on ground including CAO, it was noted that among reported GBV incidents 98% survivors were women and girls. Incidents included physical assault by an intimate partner (37 per cent), sexual violence (18 per cent), and emotional abuse (25
