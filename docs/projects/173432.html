Project Summary

IRC seeks to continue its implementation of a Protection and Rule of Law Project that encompasses, Systematic Protection Monitoring, Legal assistance, Community Based Protection and Protection Mainstreaming. The modality of implementation will be a hybrid of direct implementation and through its Partner BLAST.
IRC will identify community level protection risks and individual protection needs for direct intervention, referral, and specialized services in five camps (1E. 1W, 15, 16, 21). Using existing community structures and coordination with other humanitarian actors, the IRC will engage communities to identify the protection risks of greatest concern in the community exploring the causes, consequences and reflecting on existing prevention and response strategies. IRC/ Partner Legal Officers will conduct gender sensitive mediation services, provide mediation services to address non-criminal cases within the camps at IRC legal hubs/appropriate community structures in a manner consistent with fairness, equality and accessible to women.
IRC in Partnership with BLAST will engage in quality awareness raising on access to justice procedures, avenues for alternative dispute resolution and access informal/formal justice mechanisms. In addition, build capacity of key community members such as Maji, Justice Committees on conflict resolution. Within the Host community, IRC will implement through BLAST. IRC activities will be implemented through a protection mainstreaming lens, promoting safety, dignity, and meaningful access, participation of Women, girls and Elderly, and empowerment of beneficiaries.
IRC will implement a solid community feedback mechanism in both Host and refugee community. IRC will ensure the project is adopted to observe COVID-19 preventive measures. In it design and Implementation, the Project will consider DRR and environmental aspects in the design and programming such action would include Continuous assessments of risks associated with disaster and COVID-19. All staff involved in the project implementation will be periodically trained on PSEA and conform to the guidelines and Policy on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.

1 - Identified Needs

Refugees continue to get exposed to severe protection threats that undermine their safety, physical integrity and wellbeing. Their ability to access services including education, welfare, livelihood and justice system is limited by legal, economic and informational barriers this is further compound by the COVID 19 Situation. The impact of the Covid pandemic on the refugee has created threats and risks that have potential to adversely affect people’s rights. Characterized by, abuse, neglect, exploitation, violence and coercion has left the refugee population to adopt negative coping mechanisms like theft, illegal usage and sale of drugs and exacerbated trafficking with numerous reports of refugees seeking better conditions. Refugees face challenges in accessing legal assistance, resulting in risk of rights violation. Those at greatest risk according to IRC Protection monitoring are persons with disabilities, women and children often face challenges in accessing services at distribution points. Increased violence and potential deterioration of relations between the host communities and refugees heightens vulnerability of refugees. Misperception around access to humanitarian assistance will continue to deteriorate the fragile relationships between the affected communities. The evolving context makes the availability of accurate detailed protection data instrumental to providing effective services to meet protection needs. IRC’s Protection Analysis indicates gap in accessing justice system and legal documentation. For the Rohingya living in the camps the overwhelming preference is to seek justice within the camp through the informal process that is constituted in mediation, a process that follows a conciliation model where community leaders try and resolve conflicts. Despite this, the informal system often fails to provide adequate justice due to several factors. The quality of justice not aligning with basic human rights and principles, women’s interests not represented, lack of female representation in informal justice bodies, risk of corruption, bias and power abuse is a common place. The IRC in September 2020 developed a lesson’s learnt document whose findings revealed that there is need to Invest in capacity building of community structures as a long-term exit strategy and in preparation for events like the COVID 19 outbreak. Engaging community in protection activities had more impact thus a community based protection approach in such circumstances will generate more effective and sustainable protection outcomes by strengthening local resources and capacity and identifying protection which is crucial in ensuring full protection of the refugee community.

2 - Activities or outputs

Protection Monitoring.IRC will identify community level protection risks and individual protection needs for direct intervention, referral and specialized services in five camps (1E. 1W, 15, 16, 21). Implementation will be done through Focus group discussions (FGDs) and Key informant interviews (KIIs). Gender aspect will be considered to ensure targeted groups include at least 50% women.Through its Protection Monitoring activities, information generated will be used to develop and produce a number of protection information products such as protection monitoring reports and a Protection Analysis which will capture information related to protection trends and indicate recommendations for humanitarian stakeholders including the PWG, donors and camp level actors. Community Based Protection Engage communities to identify the protection risks of greatest concern in the community exploring the causes, consequences and reflecting on existing prevention and response strategies. Since this is a continuous process, a community based protection approach will be integrated into IRC's protection program that includes protection monitoring, alternative dispute resolution and referral activities.Appropriate and effective community-based protection will be operationalized and implemented by Building upon existing community capacities and structures to identify/establish self protection mechanisms from their own perception and needs via committees. Protection monitoring findings will be shared with the community and ensure that the community is involved in defining its priorities.Train and work with community outreach volunteers to ensure that information is widely disseminated and reaches those at heightened risk. A number of risk communication and community engagement channels will be used to inform the community on Disaster Risk reduction (DRR) and Covid-19 including prevention and response to both. To foster social cohesion positive information about social cohesion will be share Legal Assistance: IRC/ Partner Legal Officers will conduct gender sensitive mediation services, provide mediation services to address non-criminal cases within the camps at IRC legal hubs/appropriate community structures in a manner consistent with fairness, equality and accessible to women. Support will be provided on a case by case basis for the most at risk refugees facilitating access to formal justice mechanisms and linkages with the local authorities. IRC with its Partner will engage in quality awareness raising on access to justice procedures, avenues for alternative dispute resolution and access informal/formal justice mechanisms. IRC will build capacity of key community members (Mahjis, Imams e.t.c.) to resolve conflicts and disputes in line with international standards through awareness raising on issues like legal procedures and mediation. IRC will also engage camp level structures e.g Justice Committees and support in capacity building to its members on key protection considerations, justice mechanisms and international standards. IRC will provide protection mainstreaming trainings to internal and external teams of local Partners, using the Global Protection Cluster training Package. IRC will implement a solid community feedback mechanism in both Host and refugee community. Accountability activities will be established on all core dimensions and integrated into all sector activities, such will include Complaint and feedback mechanism, Help desks, To ensure Meaningful access and taking into account Gender consideration, The IRC will ensure that the structures in place are fair, inclusive and reflect the community's diversity such as Age, Gender and Diversity. Train staff and Partner periodically on PSEA and Do No Harm. DNH will be mainstreamed in the Design, Implementation and assessed periodically.
