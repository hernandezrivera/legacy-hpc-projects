Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains critical due to the ongoing impacts of drought, conflict, locust infestation, and the COVID 19 pandemic. An estimated 6.61 million people, about 42 per cent of the Somali population are in need of emergency life-saving WASH assistance of which 4.2 million will need safe drinking water, 3.2 million sanitation services, and 5.2 million hygiene supplies. According to FAO/SWALIM draught analysis from 2000 to July 2021, the trend shows that Somalia has been experiencing cyclic drought every five to six years: 2000/2001, 2004/2005, 2010/2011, 2016/2017, and currently in 2020/2021. These drought conditions were triggered by consecutive seasons of below-average rainfall including the below-average Gu rains (April to July 2021) and delay of short Dayr rains (October to December 2021). The Somalia Food Security Cluster as of 18th October 2021 confirms that half of Somalia is still experiencing serious drought conditions (moderate to severe drought with water shortages and livestock losses), with the worst impacts being felt in Jubaland, South West State, Galmudug, and areas of Puntland. The Deyr seasonal rainfall is expected to be below average, with depressed rains not going to be effective in mitigating drought conditions. A devastating drought is likely to continue into 2022 given the 1) La Nina conditions, 2) poor rainfall forecast for October and November and 3) existing drought conditions.
Access to water is mainly dependent on rainfall in the majority of the country. Barkads in nonriverine areas are the major water sources while others depend on shallow/deep groundwater sources. Thus, failure in rains triggers massive shortages as currently being reported from Jubaland, Part of Somaliland, Galmudug, and many other areas. 50 of the 74 districts in Somalia faced severe water shortages and water prices increased by 66 per cent7 in Gedo, Galgadud, Mudug, Sool, and Sanaag regions (JMCNA 2021). The Non-functionality of few strategic boreholes also increases as the operation hours increase. Many shallow-water sources are drying while others are having increased brackish water intrusions. Over 39% of Somalis don’t have access to sustained, improved and safe water. As result, over 84% are walking more than 15minuts to access water while over 92% don’t have water collection and storage facilities. Due to limited access and poor quality of existing water sources, over 1.3 million people in 24 drought-affected locations are dependent on water trucking – the majority of them are private water trucking. Oxfam PDMs and HNO results confirm the majority of Somalis use two jerry cans per day during drought conditions. 39% of people assessed confirm they wash their hands but have reported having no access to soap. 45% of girls assessed have reported having access to Menstruation hygiene management (MHM) materials at or can access from local markets.
Oxfam has been working in Somalia for over 40 years and will continue to provide life-saving humanitarian support and build the capacity of both private and public institutions. Oxfam is working with over 10 national NGOs in a different part of Somalia. In response to the continued emergency, Oxfam will continue supporting efforts to nation/local government in containment and prevention of COVID-19, will monitor and rehabilitate non-function strategic boreholes, will conduct expansion of existing water sources in order to reduce the walking time, will drill boreholes in chronically water insecure areas, will provide cash and voucher assistant for people to access live-saving water, hygiene and MHM kits from local markets or access point established by Oxfam, will work with community health volunteers to increase awareness and handwashing practice during critical times and will construct shared household latrines for communities practicing open defecations.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Oxfam’s Cash for Water is quite successful for emergency drought response where water committees or individual households are provided with cash from which they can buy water from local vendors, reducing the water trucking separately from Oxfam. Oxfam will use similar cash and/or voucher approaches in other aspects of public health response to boost the local economy and encourage the use of locally available materials. In-kind distribution has both good and bad consequences. It is good to reach immediately to the vulnerable communities if the local market is not functioning and doesn’t have enough quantity. However, the long-term impact of in-kind distribution to the local market is huge. In this response, Oxfam will pilot cash-based intervention to increase access to hygiene and MHM kits and sanitation marketing to enable communities to buy construction materials and slap from local vendors.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Oxfam will conduct a market assessment to identify market status, potential, and challenges. The outcome will be analyzed in reference to the caseload, items to be purchased, linkage with external/internal suppliers, and identifying priorities activities to support the market. Hygiene items like water containers, soap, and other items are expected to be purchased from the local markets. When purchased locally, it will help support the local WASH market providing more options for locals to purchase on their needs. The community will also get used to using the locally available materials rather than imported goods distributed. Women and girls in IDP camps and communities are slowly getting used to using sanitary pads for menstrual hygiene. As these items are distributed by NGOs, they are not available locally, making them dependent on organizations. Oxfam will encourage local vendors to supply the materials creating market and awareness at the same time. PHP team will work closely with FSL team to harmonize the market approach

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Oxfam in Somalia has a strong protection team and conducts continuous training on safe programming for both Oxfam and partner teams. During the assessment, key protection-related issues will be identified in relation to WASH services. A project-based risk assessment tool will be produced which include all protection-related risks including the right to access resources equally, land ownership, risk related to accessing water, sanitation and hygiene facilities and other security-related risks. This matrix will be used and continuously updated in order to reduce any potential risk that might be associated with our interventions. We will advocate and engage the government in case land ownership issues is found to be the biggest risk facing affected people. our community engagement, selection of different groups for focus group engagement and diverse staff will capture critical issues affecting people in relation to their clans or other related issues.
