Project Summary

Humanitarian needs in Sudan continue to grow, driven by rising food prices, the socio-economic impact of COVID-19 in 2020 and ongoing in 2021, localized conflict and disease outbreaks. 14.6 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance across Sudan in 2022. As per the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 (HNO), Needs are driven by an extended economic crisis characterized by sustained high inflation, weak public service provision and a volatile political context; with repeated shocks including intercommunal conflicts, mass displacement, flooding, and disease outbreaks including the COVID-19 pandemic.
The nutrition situation in Sudan has been characterized by persistent high levels of household food insecurity and under nutrition since the first S3M survey conducted in 2013. Based on available data (S3M II 2018), 36.6 per cent of children under five are stunted. The nutrition situation in Sudan in 2022 is projected to remain at elevated high levels with overall GAM levels of 13.6 percent. The situation is more critical in 64 localities that are classified with very high prevalence of acute malnutrition of 15% and above as per WHO emergency levels classification of which 9 localities fall into catastrophically levels i.e GAM 30% and above
Based on the sector analysis, a total of 3.9 million children under-five are estimated to be suffering from acute malnutrition including PLWs of whom over 611,000 have are severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and 2.4 million have moderate acute malnourished (MAM). Acute malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women is also very high with over 905,000 cases in need of treatment .
These children will be targeted through the scale up of access to quality lifesaving treatment
and preventive nutrition services (within multi-sectoral responses) to contribute to reduced morbidity and mortality among children under-five years old amongst crisis affected and vulnerable populations. the
project will focus on improving quality of nutrition services through capacity building, monitoring and reporting, strengthening referrals, implementation of integrated responses to address causes of malnutrition Furthermore, the project will improve understanding of the nutrition situation in prioritized localities with critical levels of acute malnutrition through evidence-generating assessments and analysis to support in planning. The project targets IDPs, returnees and vulnerable residents in the targeted localities with a focus on localities with a high severity ranking, and on specifically highly vulnerable groups.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy, and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programmer and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks. Save the Children will include environmental considerations into all interventions. In particular, the sourcing (local, national, international) of all supplies and materials, especially those used for construction and rehabilitation of infrastructure including fencing of nutrition facilities will be carefully considered to select the option with the least negative environmental impact, and where appropriate from locally available materials. Nutrition/IYCF messaging will encourage the use of locally made improved stoves to reduce charcoal consumption, and encourage growing of vegetables and trees which can contribute towards dietary diversity as well as an improved local environment. Sachets of the ready to use food (RUTF/RUSF) is regularly collected at the facilities and burned through the incinerator to avoid littering.The project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging those accessing nutrition services, children and their communities to implement better practices which support and better environmental protection and a safe local environment. SC Sudan will also communicate publicly about the impacts environmental degradation in Sudan.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Save the Children will ensure that girls and boys, women and men (of all ages, abilities and backgrounds) are consulted in a meaningful way in programming at all stages of the project lifecycle, and have regular opportunities to provide feedback and concerns. Gender, age and disability sensitive measures will be adopted, including through adapting meeting/service schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, considering ways to support the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries, and ensuring facilities are physically accessible to all, and adopting child-friendly methodologies. Save the Children will provide timely and accessible information (in local languages and culturally appropriate mediums) to affected communities on proposed interventions and deliverables, expectations of staff behavior especially with respect to our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct, and how to raise concerns or provide feedback. SC Sudan has a well-established Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanism, including a telephone hotline, and multiple further modalities which provide affected populations opportunities to give feedback anonymously and/or privately, and options for those literate and illiterate. Field level MEAL staff are responsible for receiving complaints, managing responses, and reporting them to Country Office MEAL staff who report to Senior Management. Data is anonymised, analyzed and utilized for project management and planning. There are clear processes for responding to PSEA and other safeguarding concerns raised through any feedback channel. Save the Children has safeguarding specialist staff at Country Office level, who will provide additional support and conduct monitoring visits.
