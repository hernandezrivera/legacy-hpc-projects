Project Summary

HI’s interventions aim to promote access to food and basic life skills opportunities for persons with disabilities in South Sudan. The South Sudan September 2018 integrated Food Security Phase classification (IPC) indicates that from October to December 2018, 4.4 million people are facing severe food insecurity. The IPC further indicates that about 5.2 million people (40% of the country’s projected population of 13m in 2018) will be facing severe food insecurity from January to March 2019. But it is important to emphasize that whereas a large population in the country is food insecure, persons with disabilities, who constitute approximately 7%-10% of the South Sudanese population are often left behind and forgotten from accessing humanitarian services including food. Failure to consider and support emergency and resilience needs of persons with disabilities will create a serious gap in the attainment of the strategic objectives of the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2019.
In 2019, Humanity and Inclusion will target at least 12,000 households (72,000 persons) including at least 1000HH with persons with disabilities in Yei and Juba in greater Central Equatoria and Yambio in greater Western Equatoria state. This number will include, 4000 households targeted through farming activities, 5,000 targeted through cash for work and 3,000 households targeted through livelihood skills training. Cash for work activities will entail quick impact activities like rehabilitation of dilapidated routes between townships and between townships and villages for farmers to access local markets and construction of temporary learning spaces with WASH facilities. Farming activities will entail the provision of main season agricultural seeds and farming implements to households, training of farmers including on proper crop farm cultivation, seed planting and crop husbandry as well as harvest and post-harvest handling of crops. In addition, 100 community mobilizers, 70 staff/volunteers of local NGOs/DPOs (Disabled Peoples Organizations), and 30 officials of ministries of Agriculture and Gender, will benefit from skills training in agricultural services extension, livelihood skills training and disability inclusion. HI will work with local disabled persons’ organisations to reach and target persons with disabilities (women and men, singling youth including those with disabilities) to engage them in project implementation. HI is currently implementing livelihood activities in Juba and Yei targeting 6100 households, and will be opening a field office in Yambio soon, funded from existing grants. Youth will be directly targeted and special attention will be accorded to households with persons with disabilities. HI will integrate FSL interventions with its protection, Education and Health intervention activities in these locations. Given the evolving peace initiatives in South Sudan, HI will preposition itself to target not only persons in POCs, IDP camps and host villages, but also returnees (IDPs displaced within SS and refugees from neighbouring countries).
These activities contribute towards the realisation of the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and Food security & Livelihood cluster 2019 objectives 2&4. In areas of high access constraints, HI will work through Local NGOs/DPOs to reach its beneficiaries, but where access constraints are less to medium, HI will implement directly, employing and training staff locally. Through a MHPSS project funded by DFID, HI is already setting up an operational base in Yambio from which operations in the counties in West Equatoria will be coordinated. In Yei and Juba, HI already has operational offices and structures. HI’s intervention in the 3 locations is estimated to cost USD 800,000 over a period of 12 months for 12,000 direct beneficiary households (USD 67 per beneficiary HH), with USD 700,000 being sought from SSHF, and USD 100,000 co-funding raised by HI.

Needs

Based on the recent survey conducted in Yei and Juba, and on the lessons learnt from phase 1 of the HI Mine Victim Livelihood Assistance project implemented in Yei and Juba, and as highlighted in the September 2018 Integrated Food security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis, the main gaps identified includes food insecurity where 4.4 million people (34% of the country’s projected population of 13 million in 2018) are facing severe food insecurity in October to December 2018 and 5.2 million people (40% of population) will be facing severe food insecurity from January to March 2019. Meanwhile, the REACH September 2018 assessment report in hard-to-reach areas indicates that 67% of the population in Yambio are having inadequate access to agricultural inputs and 69% in Yei having inadequate access to land for cultivation, This situation impacts worse on persons with disability as it further limits and undermines their ability to meet their basic needs. The baseline survey conducted by HI in Yei and Juba in July and August further indicates that 30% of persons with disability reported they go with 1 meal per day, more gaps includes the lack of access to services such as socio-economic opportunities (70%) which also limits the ability of persons with disability to meet the basic needs of their children such as education, this in turn increase the circle of poverty in the house hold. The lack of skills required to engage in food security and livelihood activities, lack of access to skills development trainings, education, loans and grants, microfinance products, understanding of market dynamics and private sector actors remain the major challenges for accessing market opportunity for people with disability. HI’s baseline survey further highlights that, livelihood service providers are not accessible for many persons with disabilities coupled with limited access to economic development initiatives preclude the persons with disability to fully pursue their goals and long-term development plans. South Sudan youth currently marooned in POCs, IDP camps, villages with very restricted movement are a ticking bomb, subjected to continuous social and psychosocial pressures and easy targets for enlistment into conflict and crime. In recent focus group discussions conducted in Juba and Yei, both communities reported that about 60% of youth are idle and despondent due to missed education opportunities, unemployment and lack of access to livelihood opportunities. For former child soldiers and persons with disabilities, stigma and discrimination is an added challenge which severely limits their participation in social and economic inclusion of their communities. Their families/caregivers and communities at large are insufficiently aware and incapable to deal with and manage the specific needs of their family members with disabilities and also survivors of gender violence and forced armed abuses. This leads to belated and inappropriate interventions with limited positive outcomes. Lack of rehabilitation services for persons with disabilities in health centres: The Ministry of Health has limited capacity to provide rehabilitation services and assistive devices to persons with disabilities at government health centers, health staff at government health facilities have limited or no training in rehabilitation work and there are few health staffs at health facilities, as a result, persons with disabilities face mobility challenges in the community, without the provision of basic rehabilitation services and assistive devices, persons with disabilities will have limitations to access services in the community and that also limits their participation in the social and economic opportunities available in the community.

Activities

Humanity and Inclusion’s activities are proposed to contribute towards meeting the stated needs and gaps, and are aligned to South Sudan Food Security and Livelihood Objectives and activity guidelines. HI will build a synergy between its FSL interventions and some of its project interventions including Education in Emergency, Protection and Health to expand opportunities for productive local labour. Activities will be implemented directly by HI through recruitment of local staff in each field location, utilization of existing ministry staff or where access constraints are high, working in partnership with Local NGOs/DPOs. Particular attention shall be paid to women and persons with disabilities to ensure they access equal opportunities in accessing agricultural and life skills services. At least one beneficiary each will be targeted from the 12,000 HHs, hence delivering gains to 72,000 persons. Targeting will be done in collaboration with other humanitarian agencies in the locations as well as local authorities and interest groups in order to avoid the risks of duplication and overlap of resources and services. Cluster Objective 2:Food production for the most vulnerable households: Increase vulnerable household food production to protect the erosion of livelihoods through agricultural seeds and fishing inputs, ensuring the needs of persons with disabilities are catered for.  Emergency food production: Procurement and distribution of good quality main season agricultural seeds (cereals, legumes, vegetables and grains) & farming implements to 4000HH, with special attention to households with persons living with disabilities. Training of at least 1500 farmers (women and men including youth) on the field on proper cultivation, planting and husbandry as well as harvest and post-harvest handling of rain fed crops. Training will be done through on farm demonstration, field schools and open days.  Procurement and distribution of farming implements (hoes, machetes, shovels, watering cans) Train farmers in seed selection and preservation for use in the next cropping season.  Train 250 Government, DPO officials/volunteers and community mobilizers in basic agricultural extension services. Cluster Objective 4: Dependency reduction on food and agricultural input support: Prevent and reverse the erosion of vulnerable rural & urban households and community assets through livelihood & conditional cash based support for more sustainable livelihoods.  Training & capacity building for 3000 persons, especially youth, women and at least 500 persons with disabilities in selected innovative income generation activities (small business and vocational skills as well as village saving & loan schemes & micro finance) Diversification & restoration of livelihoods; through payment of cash for work targeting 5000 persons especially youth, women including persons with disabilities for work done clearing routes, draining flood waters, casual labour for construction of emergency schools, clearing school compounds or installing WASH facilities like latrines in schools or constructing access foot bridges across small rivulets and streams as well as rehabilitation of feeder roads linking communities to markets and schools. Basic rehabilitation assessment of about 1000 persons with disabilities, and provision of basic assistive devices to promote access to and participation in productive services and activities.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

HI has a Code of Conduct complete with complaints and Feedback mechanism, a whistle blower protection policy and an investigation and determination mechanisms. Allegations are dealt with and determined very expeditiously. More so, Humanity and Inclusion’s feedback/Complain mechanism includes quarterly program of action review which involve partners, stakeholders and representatives of beneficiaries to gather feedback and complains related to the project implementation. Incidents reporting are done through our community workers.As part is its strategy to strengthen ME inn 2019, HI will roll out a revised complaints and feedback mechanism for this and all other projects, in line with the CHS –Core Humanitarian Standard and HI Code of conduct. This will entail creating training staff and outreach workers on the process, creating awareness to the beneficiaries on the process, establishment and publicising a charter of service stating timeframes within which a complaint should be addressed, and/or how complaints can be escalated to higher levels of management. HI will provide a desk login book at field level managed daily by responsible field officer and reviewed by the project manager on weekly basis and feedback provided at different level of decision making within HI on case by case basis. The project will also conduct monthly feedback focused group discussions with beneficiaries through which the beneficiaries can raise their issues. Issues will be recorded and addressed or answers sought from other levels of management/collaborating agencies Regular project satisfactory surveys with the beneficiaries will be conducted and reports shared with beneficiaries and key stakeholders. Complaints referral pathways will be disseminated to camp and interest group representatives and telephone contact of HI focal point for PSEA and beneficiary code of conduct shared.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

HI has recently (in October-September 2018) carried out a desk gender based analysis of the targeted locations. Recent studies and reports by various agencies operating in South Sudan do guide this preliminary GBA. Reports like Amnesty International’s (2017) Do Not Remain Silent provide horrific accounts of brutal rape, beatings, psychological terror, and even killings in greater Unity and Western Equatoria among other regions, perpetrated by armed groups during the renewed fighting of 2016-2017. These studies indicate a serious, and mostly deleterious, disruption of traditional gender norms and societal practices by conflict and displacement, with either gender assuming new roles. Traditionally, South Sudanese households are patrilineal, with men dominating leadership and decision-making roles, as well as having the say on land and other productive resources, while women hold reproductive and caregiving roles, including fetching firewood and water and cooking for the family. Among men and young male adolescents, engagement in conflict (either current or prior) and violence are linked and deeply rooted. In a society entirely unaccustomed to same-sex relations, boys and men have been sodomised and left with permanent psychological scars. The erosion of men’s capacity to economically support their families increases pressure (and consequently violence) on them. Besides cultural norms, families have cited extreme poverty as a driving factor in the decision to marry off young girls (for dowry) or for women to engage in sex for food. Recognizing the deep roots of such practices, HI will draw on its experience of community engagement to accompany and empower women to influence the adjudication of GBV and child-marriage cases in local customary courts. It will include working with and training traditional court officials and community leaders and initiating community-awareness and women leadership confidence building campaigns through local women’s groups.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will adopt a gender-equality and disability inclusion lens in all aspects and stages of the proposed work, seeking to identify, describe, and strengthen community capacity to address gender disparities and harmful social norms that contribute to sexual and gender-based violence. In line with HRP GAM guidelines, the project will strengthen and support greater access to comprehensive GBV services (including psychosocial and mental health) for all, with a particular emphasis on the needs of women and girls.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Activities will be designed and implemented aligned to a survivor-centered approach , with activities informed by the International Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines for integrating Gender-Based Violence intervention in Humanitarian Action. Do No Harm practices will be observed and beneficiaries sensitized and facilitated through dialogue on community-based mitigation measures on environmental hazards that accompany massive displacement and camp settlements. HI staff and staff of partners will be trained in Gender-Based Analysis and the mainstreaming of strategic gender rights in project design and implementation. Given the delicate security access issues in the two states, HI will mainstream Security Risk management in its project implementation
