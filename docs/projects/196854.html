Project Summary

Non-attendance is highest in Idleb at 15%, and in Deir-Ez-Zor at 19%. In addition, most schools are not flexible enough to accommodate children who work to support their families. Walking to school is high-risk for children, which can limit access to educational opportunities. Students travel about 25 minutes to get to school, with 49% in rural Damascus, 38% in Deir-Ez-Zor, and 27% in Idleb. The poor condition of education facilities, where about 15,481 classrooms are damaged, used as shelters, or inaccessible, and the limited availability of functioning ECE centers. 221,934 education workers need to be supported through capacity building, half of whom are female. In September the informal exchange rate of the Syrian pound weakened by 0.6 percent to reach SYP 5000/USD and severe winter conditions, with temperatures ranging from -2C to +10C, have led to a significant increase in the price of clothing in the markets and 80% of people are unable to cover their winter supplies. In addition, the income of most families is between 75-100 thousand SYP per month, compared to the minimum need of 600-800 thousand SYP without rent. One in three communities in Syria is believed to be contaminated and one in two people-about 11.5 million-are at risk from explosive hazards (HNO Protection 2023). The goal and mission of the project is to provide safe and equitable access to education services over 2023 to effect more profound change than would be possible with short-term projects for 153,469 (50% high displacement rates with severity 3-5) children in 9 sub-districts and adults of displaced persons, returnees, and overburdened hosts in the highly neglected sub-districts of:
Sabe Byar, Ghizlaniyyeh/Rural Damascus
Sanjar, Saraqab/Idlib
Deir Ezzor, Tabni, Ashara, Abu Kamal/Deir Ezzor.
Dar’a/Dar’a

Awareness sessions for 8250 students on Covid19-mine risk education. In addition to establish 27PTA committees to for active participation of local communities. 3 - Needs

According to the assessment conducted and the HNO2023, Deir Ezzor, rural Damascus, Idlib, Dar'a score3-5on the sectoral severity scale and an estimated6.8million children and9% children with disabilities have problems accessing education services, while43% do not attend classes. The figure is15% in Idleb and 19% in Deir-ez-Zor, 9% in Dara, due to the poor condition of educational facilities.15,481classrooms are damaged, used as shelters, or inaccessible, and there are few functioning ECE centers. Children work to support their families, and children must also walk an average of 20minutes to reach an accessible school. In addition, teacher displacement has led to a severe shortage of qualified teachers who are able to teach. Over 84,218 teachers are in need of pedagogical support. MEAL activities include daily site visits by monitoring team to collect informative, quantitative, and field media-supported information. KOBO used to document progress activities by weekly M&E report to ensure no delays. Ensure accessible, safe &documented CFMs for women,enrolled children,dropped children and CwD through help line and complaint & suggestion box. Commits to 4Ws and use CMS tracking tools to track children's academic level, continuous contact with parents to track the child's status, attendance data sheet on the average percentage of students in attendance. Pre&post surveys interactive teaching methods in the teaching process and conduct PDM.

4 - Implementation of activities

RSRP is implementing the project directly only and serve the northern and northeastern regions through its main office in DZ as a permanent presence and currently implementing ECW for 2023-24 with UNICEF. The activities are aligned 2023HNO at subdistrict level in Deir Ezzor, rural Damascus, Idlib, Dar'a and HRP2023 goals SO2&3 targeting 3 to 17 yrs old children and adults. Through community centers, field staff and mobile team, the activities to be implemented include conduct ECE for 1100, remedial and SLP classes for 7150, conduct BTL campaign for 88000 children, rehabilitate of damaged schools for 5210, provide gender and disability washing facilities for 2300 children, providing school with supplies to 8250 students, distribute 8250 winter kits, provide 699 teachers with MHPSS, 4000 students receive LSCE training. 3000 children of 9th and 12th grade receive transportation support to apply for official exam, conduct covid-19 awareness and mine risk education for 8250 students, establish 27 PTA committees to for active participation of local communities od parents and teachers in education. All activities will be carried out in coordination with the sectors, the MOE, and local authorities. In addition, RSRP will coordinate with the WASH sector to rehabilitate schools that will include WASH facilities, and Protection sector

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1:Learning facilities unable to put into place and maintain Covid-19 safety measures including positive behavioral changes. Likelihood:Medium Impact:High Mitigation Measures: RSRP will ensure that learning facilities should have Covid-19 safety protocols in place and protocols should include identification, prevention measures, awareness raising and a referral system between the learning facility and the relevant health center. These protocols include no-touch thermometers, hand washing and hand-sanitizer stations, face masks.2:Insuficient water and sanitation facilities result in an unwillingness or inability to use WASH facilities or a mixing of sexes and ages. Likelihood:High Impact:High.Mitigation Measures: 1Suficient water and sanitation facilities should be provided and maintained.2WASH facilities should be sex and age segregated and accessible to people with disabilities. 3:Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully students. Likelihood:Low Impact:High.Mitigation Measures:RSRP learning facilities to have an enforced code of conduct covering school; staff, aid workers or contractors 4:Learning facilities are structurally unsafe or protected due to damaged infrastructure. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures:Learning facilities improved to ensure that buildings are safe and protected including stairs, walls, roofs, electric, water,-RSRP to ensure Child Safeguarding measures are in place before work start


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PR1: Misconduct by humanitarian workers while realizing the activities. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation Measure: 1. Inform and train humanitarian workers on all aspects related to protection and develop strategies to address the risks of GBV and PSEA during all project-stages. 2. Selection of competent and professional hygiene volunteers and signing of a code of conduct by all humanitarian workers. 3. Establishment of an effective feedback and complaint mechanism. 4.Assigning PSEA focal point to deal with all PSEA cases that may occurred. PR2: Targeted communities including victims, do not report allegations of SEA, which results in RSRP being unable to refer the allegation to investigation, hold personnel to account and assist victims.Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation Measure:1. Train staff on PSEA policy and procedures. 2. RSRP will work on raise awareness of targeted communities about PSEA and how to report misconduct.
