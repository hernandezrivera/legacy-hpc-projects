Project Summary

Current and project levels of acute food insecurity and malnutrition in Somalia remain high. An estimated 849,900 children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden) face acute malnutrition over the next 12 months (Sep 2020 – Aug 2021), including 143,400 likely to be severely malnourished. Urgent nutrition and health support needed to address these needs. The nutrition situation among IDPs has shown no improvement. There are as many IDP population groups with Critical and Serious GAM as in the previous three seasons. SAM prevalence has increased in the most IDP population groups compared to the previous three seasons. These results underscore the underlying vulnerability of the IDP population. Morbidity rates were high (≥ 20%) among IDPs in Baidoa. The Crude Death Rate (CDR) and Under-Five Death Rate (U5DR) was high among IDPs in Baidoa which had serious level of CDR and/or U5DR – Baidoa IDPs (31.7%); Baidoa Urban (31.5%); Bay Agropastoral (30.5%). The nutrition situation is likely to deteriorate from October to December 2020 among Baidoa IDP due to seasonal factors as well as expected deterioration in food security conditions (access to milk, declining household cereal food stocks and likely in cereal prices). Vitamin A supplementation status and measles vaccination reported are low based on the last six months recall period, with most of these rates below 60%. In Bay Agropastoral they reported low access (<36%) to safe water and low access (40%) access to sanitation services.
Areas and Populations of Concern Population groups classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, eradicating acute malnutrition, saving lives, and protecting and saving livelihoods. The following are considered areas of concern and need urgent nutrition and health support interventions. They currently have or are projected to have a Critical GAM prevalence, indicated by a weight-for-height z-score of 15-29.9% or by a Mid-Upper Arm Circumference below 125 mm of ≥10%: Pastoral, Baidoa IDPs.
In response to this, READO aims to strengthen life-saving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency, micronutrient interventions and optimal maternal nutrition. This will be done through the treatment of severe acute malnutrition in children 0-59months, multiple micronutrients supplementation for pregnant and lactating women and children and infant and young child feeding promotion services among the IDPs in Baidoa Bay region in Somalia.

Project Activities

Provision of IMAM (Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition) with a combination of primary Health care service and out-patient (OTP) therapeutic feeding for the treatment of severe acute malnutrition in children aged 0-59 month in the Bay and Bakool Region; the nutrition service will be done through a network of mobile clinics, static OTPs. The supplies will be supplied by pipeline managers' projects as READO will only ensure the frontline service delivery. The system of OTPs is structured on 10 static sites, 10 mobile. (5 fixed OTPs in Bay region (1 in Baidoa relocation center, (2 in Baidoa Rural villages of Awdinle and Lowiile with the coverage Communities), 1 in Dinsor, 1 in Berdale) 5 Fixed OTP in Bakool region (2 in Huddur 1 in IDPs and 1 in the Hudur General Hospital with Integrate EPI services (immunization services delivery) in OTP services as part of the static and mobile nutrition program through health funding. • Provide and integrate Nutrition, health and hygiene education (NHHP) education in all static and mobile OTP sites. • Ensure that children have access to vitamin A supplementations and deworming. For PLW, as recommended by WHO, supplementation with iron-folic acid and using single-dose of albendazole (400 mg) • Promote breastfeeding practices among mothers providing IYCF counseling services. •Link SAM case-management to MAM treatment • Ensure a community screening of malnutrition, provide by CHWs and mother-led MUAC for both children 6-59 months and PLW. • Capacity building for the staff with refresher training on management and prevention of acute malnutrition and training on IYCF. Community health workers (CHWs) will also be given refresher training on the detection and referral of acute malnutrition, IYCF and NHHP promotion. Finally, mothers would be trained on Mother lead MUACScreening and provided with MUAC tapes for early detection and referral of malnourished children.

Mitigation Measures

Exclusion and marginalization: READO will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. READO will identify the power dynamics in the area which included who has access to nutrition care, who manages facilities, who is able to influence decisions on the provision of nutrition services. READO will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discriminationagainst particular groups. This information will be considered when determining locations for nutrition interventions. In regards to avoiding tensions between the host and displaced communities, READO will provide the local/host community with access to camp nutrition facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. In addition, READO will consult both displaced and host communities about nutrition needs and assess whether nutrition interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the twocommunities. READO will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the nutrition facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. This will be done through introducing special arrangements for persons unable to access nutrition facilities eg. Mobile nutrition services, facilitate accompaniment arrangements (e.g. youth groups assisting the elderly/persons with disability; community networks). In addition, READO will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. Furthermore, READO will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to nutrition needs. READO will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for nutrition center location, design and services. Moreover, READO will engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-makingprocesses that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build theirself-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. READO will also have have a proportionate number of female nutrition staff. READO will also establish complaints and response mechanism. READO will use information boards to display Complaint Response Mechanism phone number during training sessions activities/beneficiary selection and at nutrition centers. Beneficiaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaint. READO will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaints will be documented and reviewed regularly. In turn, the call center will be in charge of calling beneficiaries as part of the monitoring system. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence. READO will ensure, that the location and access routes to nutrition facilities and services are safe. This will be done by identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. READO will also advocate for install lights outside and on the access route to the facility with the government of South West State Somalia. In addition, READO will ensure that patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private. This will be done by establishing an information sharing protocol for survivors of abuse that avoids exposing her/him to further trauma and that all efforts are made to ensure her/his confidentiality and wishes concerning the care provided.
