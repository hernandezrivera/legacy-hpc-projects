Project Summary

This project seeks to address dire humanitarian needs in the IDPs in Banadir (Kahda, Daynile and Dharkaynley districts), Lower Shabelle (Afgoye and Walanwine districts) and Lower Jubba (Kismayo district), the activities will improve current WASH gaps to reduce incidents of AWD cholera spread, the project will reduce morbidity and mortality among emergency affected, vulnerable populations with high burden of active Internally displaced persons/Cholera outbreaks and with no access to basic health, WASH services, Nutrition and protection responses. Nutrition services including MUAC screening for children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), Treatment of MAM and SAM without medical complication, Referral of MAM and SAM cases with medical complication and failed appetite, Breast feeding promotion, Under WASH adoption of good hygiene and sanitation practices will be promoted in the affected communities, schools, health and nutrition promotion sessions, adequate WASH facilities (emergency latrines less than 50 miters to households, provision of WASH packages to schools and health facilities and Cholera Treatment Centers, hand washing and provision of clean drinking water to IDP settlements and poor host communities) assisting families with malnourished child with WASH package (hygiene promotion, hygiene kit, household water treatment) and support distribution of hygiene kits to discharged patients In heath centres. the project will provide primary health care services to 4300 in Banadir, Lower Shabelle and Guricel and Lower Jubba regions will be provided with lifesaving health services using IERT model, carrying out treatment of minor ailments, routine immunizations and campaigns and training of health workers on treatment and management of AWD/cholera and measles case management since early detection and effective management of measles case reduces case fatality rates. 48270 vulnerable persons of who 19791 are women, 12067 men and 16412 children will benefit the project. 80% of the beneficiaries are IDPs.

Needs

According to “ACAPS humanitarian crisis analysis,” in 2018 an estimated 4.3 million people are in need of WASH services with 70% lacking adequate safe water to meet basic needs.1.7 million people require urgent WASH assistance in drought-affected regions and 600,000 are in need in recently flooded districts. About 29% of the population gets water from unimproved water sources and another 12% from surface water. The risk of contracting waterborne diseases is high, owing to consumption of untreated and contaminated water, lack of latrines, poor hygiene and sanitation practices. SCC rapid need assessment in Banadir (Daynile, Kahda and Dharkaynley districts) Lower Shabelle (Afgoye and Walanwyne), Lower Juba (Kismayo) noted that access to WASH services is below Sphere and WASH cluster standards. More than half of the population lacks access to adequate sanitation facilities, especially in rural areas and drought/flood-affected locations. Absence of latrines and toilets lead to open defecation (39% of the population). Only 20% of people in IDP settlements access to sanitation facilities. Around 5.5 million people are in need of health assistance. The most common diseases are AWD/cholera, measles and malaria. Poor sanitation practices and limited health infrastructure during drought led to cholera outbreaks. Inaccessibility areas due to insecurity affect delivery of basic health services. According to FSNAU nutrition updates on July 2018, Critical levels of SAM prevalence (≥4-5.6) were recorded among IDPs in Mogadishu. Serious levels of SAM prevalence were also recorded in the IDPs of Kismayo. SCC assessment found out that serious and critical condition of SAM exist in both urban and IDPs in Mogadishu, Afgoye, Walanwyne and Kismayo. UNICEF’s Humanitarian situation report on 28th February 2018, more than 76% of recorded GBV survivors are from IDPs. In the need assessment, SCC met rape cases of rape whose survivors did not get proper care, treatment and counseling.

Activities

Nutrition:1: lifesaving curative nutrition services on identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases of 350 boys/girls (6-59 months).1.2: lifesaving curative nutrition services on systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases of 1,190 boys/girls (6-59 months). 1.3: lifesaving access to 210 pregnant/lactating women treated for moderate acute malnutrition in equitable manner.1.4: life-saving food-based therapeutic/preventive nutrition services for 830 vulnerable people, focusing on blanket supplementary feeding programs (BSFP).WASH:1: Deliver life-saving temporary safe water service to 9000 persons.2: 9000 people in the IDP settlements and communities have access to an emergency sanitation facility.3: construction of Emergency latrine separate male and female with hand wash facility and respect of Wash Standards guidelines. 4: Water treatment and chlorination of routine water quality testing, in the water point and household level Water treatment for distribution of Aqua tabs to targeted beneficiary. Water treatment monitoring, both at community and Public institution level, will be guaranteed via FRC monitoring control. 5: construction of WASH infrastructure at institutions (health centers, CTCs, schools, TLS, CFS) and make sure hygiene messages are coordinated with and support to other clusters. Health: 1: Improve access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for crisis affected people.2: 1,275 children under 1 year will receive Penta3 vaccine in the settlements.3: 1,400 births will be assisted by a skilled birth attendants.Protection: 1: 830 unaccompanied boys and girls are placed in foster care or reunified with families. 2: 250 of gender-based violence survivors receive clinical care, case management, psycho-social support, legal assistance, and safe house support.3: 310 of individuals will be supported with cash to cope with post-eviction stress.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

SCC shall involve stakeholders participation in the programming stage to ensure that the target locations are not prone to conflict, insecurity and are easily accessible. The response is planned to involve the government administrations and the community established structures/systems to ensure ownership, sustainability and resilience. During the intervention, SCC shall train and create awareness in protection mainstreaming in designing of the interventions through WASH, Health and Nutrition that will include screening for malnourished lactating mothers and under 5 children, referral pathways to health facilities and nutrition centers. Through the participation of the key stakeholders SCC will ensure awareness of the planned intervention and create acceptance in both the government and target communities. The participation is expected to improve coordination at all levels of the intervention and prepare the stakeholders for handover of the project at the end of the interventions. SCC assumes that by the end of the project the stakeholders shall be well trained, informed and prepared to take over and continue with the practices and maintenance of the established WASH infrastructures at institutions (health centers, CTCs, schools, TLS, CFS) and make sure hygiene messages are coordinated with support of other clusters. By involving the government administrations it is to legalize the services and utilities by ensuring that the government representatives advise on all the legal requirements and facilitate the process in acquiring the same. Community participation shall create awareness of the ownership and the use of the utilities to the target beneficiaries. It is expected that by the end of the planned intervention, SCC shall ensure that protection environment is sufficiently safe/stable to enable durable solutions/resilience, the project is linked to government/community systems, sustainable /handover component is built into the project and the entitlement of beneficiaries to service/utilities provided are legally recognized.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The planned response shall target IDPs, Returnees and vulnerable host communities that are most affected and discriminated due lack and limited access to basic services. Sharing the already inadequate resources with the host communities can trigger conflict and create insecurity amongst the IDPS, returnees and host communities. IDPs live in temporary shelters and these lead to protection risks. Single mothers and girls are exposed to GBV, lactating mothers, children under 5, the old and people living with disabilities cannot fight to access the limited basic services in WASH, nutrition and health. The distances, locations, availability becomes a major challenge. It is due to this concerns SCC proposes to carry out interventions that will ensure the following considerations are included in the project design. Water sources in visible, central locations and not more than 500 metres from settlement, Installation of lights near communal facilities. If lighting is not possible, provision of solar lamps / torches for each household where applicable. No facilities and distribution points near military installations or threats such as attacks stray bullets, crossfire or violence. No Weapon bearers present inside distribution points. Women and girls will be accompanied or walk in groups to reduce threats to the safety and dignity of women and girls when fetching water. Central locations for distribution points. SCC shall ensure that the distribution take place in daylight hours to give the beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. SCC will Ensure frequency of distribution to minimize exposure to safety threats that beneficiaries may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking their hygiene kits home. SCC will also design and construct WASH facilities that preserve the safety and dignity of its users.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Project's activities will guarantee appropriate engagement of all the population groups with priority to vulnerable people. Different population groups will be targeted (returnees, IDPs and host communities) in order to mitigate risks of exclusion and to promote successful reintegration and long term solutions. This approach is paramount in displacement affected communities in order to enhance social cohesion and peaceful coexistence. The relevant local authorities will be properly engaged in activities design and implementation as well as informed of ongoing activities and developments through periodical meetings conducted by Project Manager. This will minimize the risk of misunderstandings and overlapping of activities with other partners and the government. A proper engagement also of village and community leaders will mitigate security risks thus allowing SCC Staff to safely implement activities in all the targeted locations. SCC staff has so far mitigated the risk by discussing SCC staff purpose in advance with community leaders and putting into place a security protocol consisting in monitoring and evaluating the security situation before movement and check in calls to Field Managers. Risks of hoax and dishonesty will be minimized by Segregation of duties and sharing of responsibilities, as well as monitoring visits and quality control of activities implemented. Beneficiaries will be provided with hotline numbers to refer complaints.
