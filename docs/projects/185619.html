Project Summary

The goal of the project is to provide safe and dignified living conditions and contribute to environmental sustainability. This proposed project is targeting to address one of priority needs shelter, GUSS will construct a total of 1,000 MTS and distribute a total of 1,000 solar lamps for the RR and 90 shelters for the HC. Out of them, it is expected that, 5% of the targeted community will have PWD. These activities will be implemented at camp-1 (E&W) for the RR. In addition, GUSS will cover Nhilla and Palongkhali unions for the HCs.
Beneficiary selection will be done through a participatory consultation process that will involve local administration, community leaders and all age groups including PWD. To ensure gender and inclusion activities, 45 beneficiaries will be selected based on a need assessment and involved whereas adult men and women will be involved in site management, lightweight works, and bamboo mat production etc. In addition, elder people, PWD and children will engage to identify safe, accessible shelter location, map potential risks, environmental disasters. Men women ratio will be 50:50, payment will be equal and will have equal rights to express their opinion.
SADDD and AGDM will be helpful to ensure meaningful participation of all ages and backgrounds, using a participatory, rights and community-based approach, in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The level of engagement in gender and inclusion activities will be identified by age groups.
GUSS constructed about 400 MTS and distributed solar lamps in Camp 1 (E) and 1 (W) since 2020. No major safety and security concerns arises so far that could hamper the project implementation. However, COVID-19 issues, treated bamboo, frequent decision’s change regarding shelter design by Government may obstruct project interventions. To properly mainstream protection issues following actions will be taken-
- Shelter location will be secured from any threats or attack and not environmentally vulnerable
- Distance will be maintained between shelters to ensure privacy
- Sufficient lighting inside the shelters
- In addition, to make the shelters accessible for PWD, ramps and handrails will be constructed
- Child labor will be strictly prohibited during project implementation
To address PSEA issues, GUSS will follow the principles of IASC, UN regulations and its existing policy when and where required. Staffs will be trained on PSEA in coordination with PSEA networks and sectors. In addition, GUSS will coordinate with sector preparing 4W reports, attending meeting, training and workshop. Besides, required communication will be made with sector focal in the camp and other relevant officials.
J-MSNA reveals, CwC has always been denied for both RR and HC. To avoid misinformation, beneficiaries will be involved in implementing process, IEC materials will be produced addressing the assistance received, guidance of using assistance, branded tarpaulin and a functional CRM will be there. One feedback receiving phone number will be displayed and a responsible officer will be recording and updating all complaints and feedback into the register. Other activities include-
- feedback through written texts, phone call or SMS including face to face discussion.
- Identity of feedback senders will be kept confidential.
The project interventions will play a vital role to change the lifestyle of the beneficiaries in terms of safe and dignified living conditions. Capacity building of the beneficiaries will be tailored focusing on shelter and protection which will build a sense of ownership and empower community people. In addition, involvement of govt. stakeholders during the project will strengthen rapport with NGOs. The organization has a functional Environment Policy which enables us to comply high consumption of Day Light, recycling resources, discourages deforestation, reducing CO2 emissions and raising awareness of environmental issues.

1 - Identified Needs

"The Cox’s Bazar in south-east region of Bangladesh has been hosting Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (RR) for several decades since 1978 with the largest influx in 2017. The total population is as per UNHCR GoB Population data (as of 30 September) is 896,879 across the 34 camps. While this region is the most poverty stricken with very limited livelihood opportunities, hilly & uncultivable lands due to salinity and proneness to numerous natural disasters. Given the context, this massive influx also created extra pressure on the overall conditions of the host communities as well. The vast majority of the people both RR & Host communities have been living in extremely difficult conditions with limited access to basic lifesaving facilities as prioritized by the ISCGs (Inter-Sector Coordination Group) includes - Food Security, Health, WaSH, Shelter, Protection, Education and Nutrition. According to J-MSNA, the unmet needs for shelter is 74%, highest among the other sectors and it revealed, the refugee population in camps remains highly reliant on humanitarian assistance, and almost all households have unmet multi-sectoral needs, most commonly related to shelter/NFIs and food security/livelihoods, roughly three quarters of households reporting issues with their shelters, as well as roughly one in ten households reportedly having to make rent payments, and large proportions reporting having insufficient NFIs. In addition, it is also be noted that, specific types of households, such as female-headed households, households without access to self-reliance, 36% households reported not having made improvements to their shelter in the 6 months prior to data collection despite having reported issues and 12% of households reported having had to make rent payments in the 6 months prior to data collection. In case of HC, 54% of Populations of HC lives in kutcha house whereas 32% in semi pucca, 9% in jhuprie, and only 4% in pucca house. The people living in kutcha and jhuprie house are the most vulnerable. 71% of the households reported shelter issues and 30% did not notice any improvements to their shelter. Moreover, on the basis of J-MSNA, 53% of the respondents from the HC reported issues regarding upgradation of shelter materials which has made among their top 3 priority needs was notably higher this year compared to previous years (with 85% of the host community data collection having been completed prior to the flood event at the start of August). in case of HCs appear to have been no recovery from the impact of the COVID-19 outbreak and its secondary impacts on livelihoods, with a potential risk of a long-term deterioration of coping capacities and living standards. Besides, Large proportions of households were reportedly not able to access sufficient information on the types of assistance available to them, face challenges providing feedback or complaints, and do not feel consulted or heard by humanitarian actors. In response to CwC, J-MSNA shows 74% of households in RR reported that they were unable to access enough clear information on the types of assistance available to them, 9% of households reported having faced challenges when providing feedback or complaints in the 6 months prior to data collection whereas the percent is 99% and 36% respectively for the HC. In this regard, the targeted beneficiaries will have strong CRM to mitigate the misinformation, appropriate risk information messages in relation to support provided and organizational visibility. Although many of the organizations have been responding to the overall needs but a significant gap especially for Shelter and NFI are still persistent both in RR sites and host communities due to the limited availability of funds compared to the needs."
