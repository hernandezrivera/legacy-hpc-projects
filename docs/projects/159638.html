Project Summary

Severity of drought, flood disasters and prolonged conflicts have worsened the living conditions of the Somalia populations, particularly Internally displaced persons (IDPs) and extremely poor host community households in Kismayo and Afmadow Districts in Lower Juba region. Most of the children in the IDPs camps and poor host communities in this region are currently out of school. According to the Joint Multi-Cluster assessment report 2019, 59% of the school-aged children in the assessed households were reportedly not attending school at the time of assessment in Afmadow district of lower juba region and similar case in Kismayo. There are a myriad of challenges including funding gaps and insufficient humanitarian responses in these areas. The learning resources are in short supply, Classrooms are overcrowded, schools experience high teacher turnover because of lack of salaries or incentives and often teachers are not equipped with the skills to handle classroom management. Lack of school fees is the most common reported barriers to education in the households according to (HNO, 2019). Some IDP settlements such as Danwadag in Dhobley have no schools or even Temporary learning spaces. In instances where learning spaces exist, they are often private institutions that charge tuition fees that IDPs household and poor host communities of low income families cannot afford. According to the National Education Strategy plan 2018-2020, those suffering the greatest educational inequities are IDPs, females, nomadic and rural communities and those from the lower wealth quintiles. Child labour and domestic chores also serve as proxy indicators for the economic barriers to education according to HNO 2019. Poorer households often need help from household members, including children, to supplement income or to help around the house. Many parents in these Districts mainly the IDPs tend to send their children (Boys and Girls) to the market or street to do perilous jobs to earn a living for the poor households.
The proposed programme will strategically focus on the 2020 Education response plan on retention and increase access. Windle International Somalia will prioritize retention of crisis affected school children and ensure increased access to education for out of school children in the vulnerable displaced and host communities in the targeted areas. These are intended to contribute to the Education Cluster Strategic Objectives. WIS will give priority to children from among IDPs and poor households geared towards improving access to learning and retention in a safe and protected environment. The project proposes to support 8 schools (4 schools in each of the targeted Districts) this includes support to 2 IDPs learning center/School. The project will emphasis on four main components with the purpose of improving retention and access to protective, life –saving and inclusive quality education. These components include: 1.Establishing community dialogues to raise awareness on mitigating risks of child abuse. Ensuring schools are well fenced from attack and insecurity. WIS will undertake continuous community mobilization to Increase enrollment for vulnerable displaced out of school children (430 Boys and 400 Girls) and ensure retention in school children by construct/rehabilitating gender and disability responsive latrines with WASH facilities, Provision of safe drinking water and supplies of dignity kits for adolescent’s girls.2. Renovation/construction of classrooms with appropriate disability friendly facilities benefiting 2,289 Girls and 2,799 Boys. 3. Providing life-saving school feeding rations, Supplies and learning materials to 5,088 children (2,289 Girls and 2,799 Boys). 4. Improving quality of teaching and learning in Schools and increasing awareness on protection of children’s right. WIS will develop teachers proficiency, provide incentives and capacity improvement for 40 teachers (12 Female ), and 32 (20 Male) CEC's.

Project Activities

The project outcomes include; Outcome 1: 5,088 (2,289 girls and 2,799 boys) crises affected children have continued access to safe and protective learning environment for basic education. Activities: 1.1. Conduct enrollment campaigns in all target communities and schools to retain learners and encourage girls’ and boys’ enrollment. The project will conduct a series of community mobilizations aimed to promote inclusive education in all target areas. Communities will be sensitized on the importance of girls’ education and their right to access education.1.2 Community dialogue will be conducted to raise awareness on mitigating risks and all kinds of child abuse practices to enhance children’s access and retention in education 1.3. Construct 2 temporary learning protective and safe spaces in IDP settlements and rehabilitate 6 permanent schools.1.4 Furnish classrooms with Desks .1.5 Establish small libraries in 3 supported schools to improve the reading culture in schools. 1.6 Rehabilitate/construct latrines with WASH facilities that are gender sensitive and disability friendly. Outcome 2: 5,088 (2,289 girls and 2,799 boys) crises affected children have lifesaving support to ensure continued access to education that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion. Provision of incentives to 40 teachers (12 female and 28 male) and 8 head teachers (2 women and 6 men) in 8 target schools. 2.3. Construct storage water tank in 4 schools in Afmadow that have no water facilities and provide safe drinking water to the schools through water trucking to schools and procure and distribute hygiene kits to schools 2.4. Provision of emergency community based school feeding programs to 5,088 (2,289 girls and 2,799 boys) in the IDP settlements and host schools, this will be done through provision of food, utensils and training to mothers/cooks Outcome 3: Strengthened capacity to deliver effective and coordinated education in emergencies, preparedness and response within the education system 3.1. Conduct teachers training on basic pedagogical, life-saving learning skills and psycho-social support to 40 teachers (12 female and 28 male). 3.2 Provision of training for cluster partners and MoE staffs on emergencies preparedness and response target being 60 trainees (30 female and 30 male). 3.3 Conduct training of CEC members on DRR, Hygiene promotion, maintenance and management of learning spaces. The target CEC members are (20 male and 12 female). Mobilize the community to advocate and respond to school attacks from armed forces and other groups.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion and Marginalization as result of displacement The project targets the poor host communities, returnees with specific focus on the IDPs. The IDPs are more vulnerable to education inclusion since they are mostly affected by the environment they live in. Drought, Insecurity and conflict due to insurgency and clan conflict are now one of the main causes for displacement. People denied the rights of the IDPs as human being and they are facing evictions, rape and looting due to the fragility of the jubaland state government who was under their responsibility to protect them. In this project.WIS will address protection concerns and supporting education retention and access activities for children already in schools in following ways 1. Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and priorities the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. 2. Advocate for equal access to education for girls and sensitize the community about the value and benefits of girls’ education 3. Ensure equal participation of the communities in the project phases, that Windle will ensure IDPs will take greater chances in the project implementation and consultation process.b) Preventing risk of exclusion and Marginalization as a result of being Women, Girls and boy. The proposed objective of the project is to enable crisis effected school-aged children have immediate access of learning opportunities, support learners retention, improvement of learning environment and strengthening and improvement of quality of effective service delivery. A negative attitude of parents, especially mothers, toward girls’ education is one of the barriers to girl child education in Jubaland, Somalia that negatively impacts on access and retention of the girls in school.WIS will conduct awareness sensitization of parents especially female through parents forums or school clubs where they can be engaged on ways to retain girls in schools, provide gender sensitive learning environments for girls to avoid being risk of SGBV and will ensure female teachers are supported and considered. That women, boys , girls and men will be equally raise their voices and will be heard as project will trained CECs that are gender sensitive. c) Preventing Risk of violence and more Harm .Schools serve as protective spaces for both girls and boys, hence with the school dropout affecting both boys and girls, but girls are more exposed to risk of attacks as they move from one place to another in search for water and food for themselves and their families as well as boys being susceptible to be recruited into the Al-Shabab (AS) groups. Windle international will ensure that girls are provided with adequate friendly environment for learning and will ensure that school centers are safe are always protected while mobilizing the communities.The community will also be mobilized to safe guard the girl child from violence. Effective school committees composed of parents, teachers, school managers and religious leaders to be established. The committee should lead in administration of schools, discipline of students and prevention of violence in schools. This will ensure that schools are violence-free and that they provide a safe learning environment for girls. WIS will identify the risk of conflict in the project area and immediately avert this risk. WIS will closely engage the local leaders, government officials and the community to sort out any possible threat and the community prominent members as cross-cutting will be trained on Disaster Risk Reduction and conflict resolution mechanism measures. WIS is currently implementing Education and training programmes in both districts without any security problem, although sometimes there is security issues in the region. Safety and security for the project staff and beneficiaries are our top priority.

Please provide further explanation

Target locations of the project is safe and no major security concerns has been reported in the last one year though security dynamics of Somalia especially on clan conflict and attack from militia is not predictable. The project is aligned with National Government strategic objectives with a lasting solution in the education development. The education system to be used in the schools will be in line with the Ministry of Education strategic plan. The project proposes to work with the community in identifying lasting solution and management of the schools. Training of Community Education Committees will enable the communities manage the schools. Temporary learning facilities will be handed over to the community at the end of the project with the entire infrastructure used during the project. The community will be mobilized before the project and all the beneficiaries will be informed of their entitlement and services they will receive. Contract agreement with the donor as well as local administration engagement will outline all the services and entitlement the target beneficiaries will receive. WIS will hold at the inception of the project, community mobilization, and sensitization meetings with all stakeholders including the local authorities to officially launch the project. During the sensitization meetings, the project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement and roles and duties of each stakeholder including M&E roles will be discussed and agreed. WIS will develop elaborate beneficiary complain and feedback tool, project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be given 1 day workshop on how to use the complaint /feedback system to enhance transparency and accountability. Community ownership of the project and keeping the sustainability of the Education infrastructure after the phase- out of the project will be done through formation of structures such as Beneficiary Representatives, CEC Committees, and Community Hygiene Promoters with equal representation of men and women. In order to meet the Education needs of the affected community, WIS will involve the target community through its leaders and engendered committees in the first stage of planning of the project to ensure community participation. The organization will establish engendered committees (women participation will be strictly adhered) that will have hand-in-hand working relations with the project team and beneficiary communities; both sides will exchange any information related to the management and implementation of the project activities. Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will also be used to make sure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with services provided and complaints can be channeled to the right direction. WIS will adhere the principles of "do no Harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project beneficiaries.
