Project Summary

The context in Syria remains critical with the number of people in need increasing from 14.6 million in 2022 to 15.3 million in 2023 (HNO 2023). Over 27% of Syrian families are psychologically distressed due to conflicts, hostilities, displacement, or death. Conflict-related needs in Northern Syria persist, inadequate basic services and the destruction of basic infrastructure, leaving almost half of the population without access to healthcare and worsening economic conditions have become the most significant drivers of humanitarian needs in Northeast Syria.
The International Rescue Committee (IRC) and its partners – AMAL, DAN for Relief and Development (DAN), Ashti and Salam in Hassakeh, Deir ez-Zor and Raqqa governorates - will improve Protection outcomes for vulnerable, conflict-affected communities. The project aims to prevent and respond to violence through an integrated and comprehensive protection intervention via mobile and static locations in NES. IRC and its partners will provide life-saving support through Gender-Based Violence (GBV), Child Protection (CP), Psychosocial Support (PSS), legal and advocacy services, as well as set up new, and support existing community-based protection mechanisms, Case Management (CM) in Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) and Women and Girls’ Safe Spaces (WGSS). To enhance community resilience and improve knowledge, IRC will establish and strengthen Protection Committees that identify protection concerns, safely refer cases to specialized services and take community-led action to ensure that the rights of vulnerable groups are met.
Risk: Community stigmatization and discrimination of children, survivors receiving specialized services such as case management services. Mitigation: Use community acceptance strategy, including the delivery of non-specialized services as entry-point and trust-building with the community. Ensure specialized services are properly messaged to the community. Embed specialized services in broader service-provision to de-stigmatization, using confidential spaces. Risk: Community tensions resulting from expectations of service delivery following protection monitoring and by asking sensitive questions, also placing NGO staff at risk. Mitigation: Train staff on communication and protection monitoring. Ensure information from protection monitoring is disseminated with relevant stakeholders so information collected is adequately acted upon. Review protection monitoring questions and adapt to context.
IRC has dedicated staff to address and prevent sexual exploitation and abuse, including technical experts on child protection and safeguarding and dedicated staff in the field. IRC promotes a culture of zero-tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse and a safe reporting environment allowing anonymity and confidentiality. IRC will ensure staff and partners are well trained on PSEA and safeguarding and that beneficiaries are fully aware of their rights and reporting channels. Managers at all levels, and ultimately the Syria Country Director and NY Senior Management, are responsible to support systems which maintain this environment, in particular staff awareness of the Code of Conduct. Risk: Change of context and limited access to supported sites. Medium Impact: High Mitigation: Assess the situation, maintain a link with local stakeholders and facilitate negotiations through IRC Humanitarian Access team.
Risk: Security conditions, disruption in activities and limited client access to sites. Medium Impact: High Mitigation: Project activities allow for adaptive management in line with internal contingency plans related to each operational area. Ongoing security planning during the project will ensure the activities can continue to adapt to the security environment and access context, providing regular security assessments. During periodic access constraints, IRC will strengthen implementation through community-based focal points or remote service modalities.
