Project Summary

This project is designed to improve the wellbeing of people with disabilities through providing them with vital multidisciplinary services, including rehabilitation services, psychosocial support and community awareness on disabilities and inclusion, with focus on neglected people with disabilities. The project is in line with strategic objective 2 and is aligned with Cluster Objective 1.
Qatar Red Crescent, the National Society of Rehabilitation (NSR), and the Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS) will join efforts to improve the well-being and quality of life of people with disabilities through providing them with multidisciplinary rehabilitation for underserved people with disabilities and community awareness sessions for people of all ages and genders in all localities of Gaza Strip.
The proposed intervention will provide multidisciplinary services: rehabilitation, physiotherapy, occupational therapy and MHPSS services to 1000 people with disabilities (300 women, 400 men, 150 boys and 150 girls) in the five governorates of Gaza. Age distribution of beneficiaries is based on MoH 2020 report. In terms of geographical distribution, 245 PwD will come from North Gaza governorate, 307 from Gaza, 138 from the Middle Zone, 199 from Khanyounis, and 111 from Rafah governorate. Services will be provided through home visits by mobile teams, who will refer cases in need of further interventions for center-based follow up at PMRS and NSR facilities.
In addition, PwD will be provided with necessary assistive devices and medical supplies needed to improve the quality and functionality of their daily living activities. QRC will procure 400 assistive devices and medical supplies and will oversee their distribution. As COVID-19 remains a threat to public health where PwD are at higher risks of suffering, QRC will also be responsible for the procurement of hygiene kits for 1000 beneficiaries.
NSR and PMRS will conduct 60 workshops for 1200 people including family members and other community members with the goal of enhancing their awareness levels regarding PwD’s rights, their community participation, restricting the barriers against their engagement, and providing proper care for them. To maximize community engagement throughout the project duration, an awareness campaign will be organized by QRC, in cooperation with PMRS and NSR, and will be concluded with a public event on the International Day of disabled persons.
The intervention pays a close attention to equal and impartial access to aid, mainstreams gender and age, and protection and is specifically designed to benefit PwDs.

1 - Needs

For more than 15 years now, Gaza has been suffering from the direct effect of the internal Palestinian division and the blockade which has been described by the UN as an "illegal blockade". This combination of suffering has left Gaza in a suffocating economic situation with several public health services, including health, water, and sanitation, have been red-flagged, and the unemployment rate has been alarmingly rising. The consequences of this have affected the lives of the vulnerable groups devastatingly, mainly in the last year after the emergence of COVID-19 in the community. According to the annual report of the Ministry of Health 2020, there are 53,221 people with disabilities in the Gaza Strip, representing 6.8% of the total population. The MOH report indicated that among 50,206 households, there is at least one person with a disability. One-fifth of them are children under the age of 18, with 55.5% being males and 45.5% being females. The type of disability varies among them, as the physical disability presents 44.9% of the total disabilities, the visual disability presents 15.9%, and the hearing disability presents 11.7%. It was indicated in the annual report that the percentage of PwDs in Gaza governorate is the highest (30.7%), followed by North Gaza governorate (24.5%), Khanyounis governorate (19.9%), Middle Zone governorate (13.8%), and Rafah governorate (11.1%). As per the HNO 2022, there are 14,047 people with disabilities in need of health and protection interventions in the Gaza Strip. This vulnerable group suffers the hardest from the intensified restrictions that reinforce the inequalities in healthcare services, the limited accessibility, and the stigma among the population. The COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated the already dire situation of people with disabilities due to the limited operation of health and protection organizations during lockdown. Moreover, shifting the funding toward the COVID-19 pandemic and reallocation of the already scarce resources to respond to the crisis have undermined the PwDs access to healthcare services and affected their day-to-day life activities. Furthermore, the number of PwDs has increased since the last escalation in Gaza in May 2021, as 10% of the total injured (about 200 out of 2000) may suffer from long-term disability as per the flash appeal shared by OCHA. The political situation after the May escalation has been fluctuating, affecting the movement of goods across the borders. This was reflected on PwDs as there was a delay in importing the assistive devices they needed. As per the OCHA 2021, people with disabilities also face discrimination and social stigma, and they are perceived as being helpless, unable to care for themselves, or to make their own decisions. Specifically, women with disabilities face discrimination both as women and as people with disabilities, which subjects them to gender-based violence and maltreatment. The mental health of PwDs has been deteriorating dramatically as a result of accumulative compound and long-term stressors. The mental illness of these people is still under-reported, under-funded, and under-treated. Furthermore, there are no programs that aim at improving the mental health of PwDs in the Gaza Strip; all that is present are fragmented, short-term, and small-scale initiatives. The 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development includes people with disabilities and has thus opened doors for their participation and recognition as active, contributing members of society who must not face any discrimination or be left out or behind. People with disabilities should be recognized as equal partners, and be consulted by governments, the UN system, civil society and other stakeholders. Out of the 169 targets across the 17 Goals, seven targets have an explicit reference to persons with disabilities. Further, all goals and targets are applicable to people with disabilities by simple virtue of universality, which applies to all people, and the overarching principle of "leave no one behind”. In 2014, oPt has signed the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD). The purpose of it is to promote, protect and ensure the full and equal enjoyment of all human rights and fundamental freedoms by all persons with disabilities, and to promote respect for their inherent dignity. The CRPD marks a paradigm shift by addressing the human rights of persons with disabilities from a progressive social model approach to disability that depends on multidisciplinary rehabilitation and community engagement. The multidisciplinary rehabilitation approach depends, as described by the International Federation of Disability, Health and Functioning, on the integration of all health, social, and psychological management plans to attain and maintain maximum independence, full physical, mental, social, and vocational ability, and full inclusion and participation in all aspects of life. It includes physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and mental therapy. Qatar Red Crescent, the National Society of Rehabilitation, and the Palestinian Medical Relief Society will work to improve the well-being and quality of life of people with disabilities by providing multidisciplinary rehabilitation for underserved people with disabilities and community awareness sessions for people of all ages and genders. Striking a balance for PwDs in this extremely unstable context is crucial to ensure they do not further exacerbate their sufferings and worsen the humanitarian situation for which they are responsible.

2 - Activities

The project will include the following activities: - To provide multidisciplinary rehabilitation including physiotherapy, occupational therapy, MHPSS for PwDs. NSR and PMRS will provide a multidisciplinary rehabilitation for 1000 PwDs in the five governorates (300 women, 400 men, 150 boys, 150 girls); 245 from North Gaza governorate, 307 from Gaza governorate, 138 from the Middle Zone governorate, 199 from Khanyounis governorate, and 111 from Rafah governorate. The distribution of the target group according to age and gender was calculated based on the statistics of MOH 2020. This intervention will be conducted via regular home visits by mobile teams to PwDs to assure their accessibility. The mobile team includes a physiotherapist, an occupational therapist, a psychologist and a rehabilitation therapist. PwDs who need further intervention will be referred to NSR or PMRS centres to get the needed services using advanced devices. - To provide assistive devices and medical supplies for the neglected PwDs to improve the daily functioning. QRC will be responsible for the procurement of 400 assistive devices according to the needs of PwDs in the five governorates. The assistive devices include air mattress, wheel chairs, walking sticks, crutches…etc. In addition, QRC will be responsible for the procurement of medical supplies; 80 vaporizers, 50 oxygen cylinders, 50 vacuums, and diapers for 240 beneficiaries (2 packs per month). - To provide hygiene kits for the neglected PwDs to prevent the spread of COVID-19. PwDs are considered as a risk group for communicable diseases. Hence, QRC will be responsible to the procurement of hygiene kits for 1000 beneficiaries that include disinfectants, face masks, non-sterile gloves ...etc. - To enhance the awareness of the community, caregivers and families of PwDs about the rights and the proper care of the neglected PwDs. NSR and PMRS will conduct 60 awareness workshops for 1200 beneficiaries (20 persons per workshop). The workshops aim at enhancing the knowledge about the rights of PwDs, their community participation, restricting the barriers against their engagement, and the proper care of them. In the same context, QRC, in cooperation with NSR and PMRS will organise an awareness campaign that will be concluded with a public event on the international day of disabled persons. The National Society of Rehabilitation and the Palestinian Medical Relief Society have an extended experience in healthcare services provided to the neglected PwDs. Both organizations adopt the community-based rehabilitation approach that aims at maintaining the basic rights of human being such as health, education, social life, empowerment and livelihood. Both NSR and PMRS have the qualified human resources in physiotherapy, occupational therapy, MHPSS, and awareness activities. They follow internationally validated protocols to provide quality-based services that cover the five governorates of the Gaza Strip. NSR and PMRS provide their services to all PwDs regardless their age, gender or residency, and they implement special policies to assure the equity, humanity and inclusion of the neglected PwDs. QRC has its own procurement system that is intended to facilitate work and speed up the delivery of the supplies. QRC takes into consideration the quality of supplies, duration for delivery, and also prices. QRC collaborates and participates with NRS and PMRS in each step of the project from the design phase to implementation and also closing. The feedback of beneficiaries will be considered as a routine part of the project monitoring to improve the design and enhance the effectiveness. Community will be engaged in all stages of the project including design, implementation and M&E. during the design phase, QRC, PMRS and NSR arranged for focus groups with community and discussed possible and recommended methods of interventions, and decided together mechanisms of implementation and other needs and indicators. During implementation and for M&E, focus groups, field visits and surveys will be used to assess project progress and get feedback from the community. There is a clear mechanism of referral for people victims of GBV. All acting partners, including QRC, NSR and PMRS have clear referral mechanisms to local NGOs and CBOs including Aesha and Abd Alshafi Community Health Association.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

QRC and the implementing partners have developed an organizational policy- based on international and local protocols- to assure the maximum benefits and the minimum harm of the beneficiaries. Those policies emphasize on the safety of the beneficiaries as a top priority including the safe geographical location and minimum threats. The project will be conducted in the five governorates in order to decrease the possible transportation risk especially for PwDs and women. Moreover, the multidisciplinary rehabilitation will be conducted at home to reduce possible harm. The working staff will coordinate to get approval for home visits to protect the dignity of the beneficiaries. In addition, QRC and the implementing partners highly consider the adaptability and the cultural accessibility to respond to the cultural backgrounds of the beneficiaries. The working staff includes both women and men especially the mobile teams that will conduct the interventions via home visits. Furthermore, there are clear lines for the privacy and confidentiality that are shared between the working staff; there are private areas for examination and therapy, and there are clear rules for data collection that include; not collecting unneeded information nor sharing information with any except the working staff. Importantly, QRC and the implementing partners- in the context of COVID-19 pandemic- follow strict precautions to prevent the spread of COVID-19 and decrease the possible complication. Furthermore, they encourage beneficiaries and the working staff to take the vaccine to protect him/herself and the community. QRC and the implementing partners follow strict rules to ensure that the items procured are what is needed and reject items that don’t match the technical criteria and may cause any harm to the beneficiaries.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

QRC and the implementing partners are neutral and don’t lean toward any group over the other in Gaza context. They are impartial and provide the aid to those who need it regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc. QRC and the implementing partners are independent organizations and not affected by commercial nor political powers. They are humanitarian and provide aid to those vulnerable and suffering all the time. QRC and the implementing partners follow strict rules to maximize the geographical, financial, and cultural accessibility especially for PwDs. The working staff consider providing the beneficiaries with enough information about their rights is crucial in the service delivery. They use both verbal and written information to overcome intellectual differences and barriers. QRC monitor service delivery and procurement at the facility and beneficiary levels to ensure all items are made available. Due to the community spread of COVID-19, QRC and the implementing partners tend to utilize all alternative resources to implement the activities in a highly effective and efficient manner including mobile teams, distribution of hygiene kits, distancing during the awareness sessions, etc.
