Project Summary

The aim of the project is to facilitate the targeted community composed by: returnees, vulnerable host community, IDPs and breeders to have access on improved local seeds specifically drought-tolerant and flood-tolerant. The farmers groups and breeders will be organized into Saving groups after they have received improved agricultural items and have conducted at list 2 harvests according to the agricultural calendar in the project region.
In the actual context, the population of South Sudan particularly the vulnerable community groups are not getting sufficient food to cover the nutritional body need.
The project activities will focus on an integrated approach of food nutrition security and saving groups for the communal economic stability.
The local crop seeds production will be promoted via community seeds multiplication plot to guarantee the sustainable of the activities. For the environment protection, solar irrigation pumps will be promoted and trees nurseries, these under organized farmer groups and the school’s management.
In the process of project launching, Malteser International will conduct vulnerability mapping with the involvement of Payams representatives and local community leaders (Boma Chiefs and villages Chiefs).
The project target beneficiaries 3000 HH: 2400 HH with agricultural vocation and 600 breeders (in: Wau – Yei – Peri urban area of Juba). The beneficiary’s selection will be based on criteria such as: returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host community (widows; disable persons…) and small breeders at household level. Good Agriculture Technics will be provided to the community and to 12 pilots schools where school gardens activities will be implemented.
The project will, finally help local farmers to protect their crops from further damage; Promote SMART agriculture that does not make climate issues worse by using crops that require less water, less space and are more nutritious. It will also contribute in Holistic approaches to farming and health which take into consideration the interlinkages of climate, human health and animal health;

1 - Needs

Looking on the IPC – (Acute Food Insecurity: October 2022 – July 2023; Acute Malnutrition July 2022 – June 2023), about 6.6 million people, or over half of South Sudan’s population (54%), are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity, classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse between October and November. Of those, 2.2 million people are experiencing worse conditions in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and an estimated 61,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). In the post-harvest projection period of December 2022 to March 2023, an estimated 6.31 million people (51.0% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 33,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. Despite the counties in phase 4 – 5, a particular intention and urgent action should be considered for populations in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) to protect their livelihoods and reduce household-level food consumption gaps. Addition to this, the projection of acute food insecurity from December 2022 to March 2023 in Central Equatoria and Western Bhar El Ghazal will still be facing crisis – IPC / phase 3. The inequality of rainfall, the prolonged drought and floodings are currently observed in Wau – Yei – Juba (peri-urban areas); these different phenomena are due to climatic changes and are negatively affecting the agricultural calendar specifically in the pre- and post-harvesting stages which seriously reduce crop yields and seed production. Also the grazing spaces are reduced due to the prolonged drought and the persisting flooding. Among the effects of climate change, the disappearance of certain plant species will be observed. The above topics will be part of the communication to be spread within the community to prepare them on “coping strategic mechanism”. Malteser International will be monitoring the situation on the ground and consult the actors in the domain of Food security and Livelihood specifically FAO. A closer follow-up of the rainfall distribution and prevision will be done to ensure the planting seasons are observed.

2 - Project activities

-Distribution of agricultural items: farming tools and improved various seeds to 2400 households and pupils in 12 schools in Wau – Yei – Juba (peri-urban areas).-Organize the households with agricultural vocation into Farmers Groups.-Establishment of trees nurseries in all the organized farmer groups areas.-Installation of Local crop seeds multiplication fields and new agricultural technics demonstration plots.-Installation of School gardens in the selected 12 schools.-Distribution of portable solar irrigation pump to the schools for “school gardens activities”.-Trainings in Modern Agricultural practices.-Trainings in nutrition topics covering food diversification, meal nutritional value and culinary / cooking demonstrations. -Marketing skills trainings to 2400 hh small scale farmers and to 600 breeders,-Training to the 2400 HH farmers groups and 600 breeders in the “Saving Groups management”. -Training in basic veterinary skill to 600 breeders to improve their animal production.

3 - Project indicators

Outcome 1: Improving the access to local seeds and farming tools of returnees, IDPs and hostcommunities by increasing agricultural production, crop diversification and training inmodern agricultural practices and organize the farmer into saving groups with particular emphasis on vulnerable community groups and pupils at lower and upper secondary. Indicator 1.1: Improve the Food Consumption Score (FCS) of all participating households by 15% at the end of the project. This indicator is focused on a household's food security status, at this stage the project will not only considers the dietary diversity and food frequency but also the relative nutritional importance of different food groups. Indicator 1.2: 60% of the assisted households are applying a coping strategy index up to end of project.In the implementation phase of the project, monitoring and assessments will be conducted to check at what extent to which households use harmful coping strategies when they do not have enough food or enough money to buy food.The project will identify how "severe" each of these individual coping strategies is considered to be; this information will be collected from community-level focus group discussions, providing a "weight" for the perceived severity of each strategy (1 - low, 2 - medium, 3 - severe).Indicator 1.3: 90% of the organized farmer’s groups and breeders’ groups are implementing the Saving approaches in their common areas after trainings.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The Community Mobilizers will be doing home visits (moving house to house) and there they will be able to interact with the vulnerable groups of people who cannot be able to reach the selected distribution centers. The community Mobilizers, being local staffs will be able to discuss in local languages with the nonliterate persons. Strategical, the Community Mobilizers will be selected based on the gender balance to allow the women and girls to exchange their concerns in a free manner.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The feedback will be provided to the community beneficiaries through telephone numbers; feedback response from; feedback boxes, mails, home visit and direct face to face exchange.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The community will be having mobilization and sensibilization session targeting PSEA Topics every season of agricultural items distribution and veterinary software services (trainings). Particularly, the Supervisors and Community Mobilizers will be providing information to the community while doing monitoring of project activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Once feedbacks are received from the beneficiaries; they will be a team for analyzing the inputs from the community and the concerns will be shared with the hierarchy to allow the adjustment of project proposal or strategy by orienting the service delivery in the suggested way from the beneficiaries.
