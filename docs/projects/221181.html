Project Summary

A complex interplay of factors including ongoing flooding and localized violence such as raiding and looting has displaced families and reduced crop yields severely impacting food production. According to IPC AFI and Malnutrition Analysis Published on 18 November 2024, Ayod County of Jonglei State has been classified in Phase 4. RAO took part in an Inter-agency IRNA conducted between the 3rd and 6th of September 2024, the IRNA reported displacements with lack of food caused by inadequate milk products due to cattle death or migration and farms destruction. These factors have led to limited goods in the market, high prices and low purchasing power forcing community members to result in negative community coping mechanisms like depending on wild foods and begging from other community members. The County is sensitive to the negative nutritional impact of food insecurity, with 39,085 children under-5 (GAM 22.5%) and 14,624 Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women (GAM 24.8%) already suffering from forms of acute malnutrition and in need of treatment according to IPC AMN report.
In response to this, RAO is proposing a nutritional intervention with the overall objective of improving the nutritional and health status of children under-5 and pregnant and breast feeding women and girls covering the 7 Payams of; Ayod, Kuach-deng, Kurwai, Mogok, Pagil, Pajiek and Wau. RAO nutrition team will work with the nutrition cluster and the County Health Department to strengthen early active case finding and treatment of 18,500 under 5 children with MAM, 2,000 under 5 children with SAM and 9,500 Pregnant and breast feeding women and girls.
RAO is a static partner in Ayod County conducting SNFI and peacebuilding projects, to introduce the new nutrition project RAO will conduct a project inception meeting to further strengthen relationships with the community and begin a food and nutrition dialogue with the people.
In summary the RAO nutrition team will carry out the following activities: One on one Counselling: priority groups for each behavior will be counselled by MIYCN counsellors and CNVs. Formation of Mother to Mother Support groups who will have weekly sessions to interact and have group counselling, outreach visits house to house by CNVs to counsel and motivate mothers of children under 6 months for exclusive breastfeeding, trace defaulters and collect information on reasons for absenteeism and defaulting as well as conduct education on key hygiene messages using IEC materials for nutrition. Conduct early risk assessment and information sharing about breast related conditions while counselling mothers who have challenges introducing complementary feeding for their children, RAO will coordinate with the CHD to conduct behavior change trainings for health personnel to promote their critical role in ensuring babies are breastfed within the first one hour of life, skin to skin contact for baby and mother and ensure they are enabled with knowledge to provide advice and emotional support to new mothers. Conduct cooking demonstrations with the lead mothers from MTMSG. RAO will also identify and train 210 Community Nutrition Volunteers and Boma Health Workers from the 7 Payams who will play a vital role in helping identify and prevent malnutrition, they will be equipped with MUAC tapes to closely monitor the growth of children within their own community. Focusing on the Family MUAC approach a series of short training sessions (20-30 mins) will be held with 70 mother to mother support groups from targeted population to promote self-referral. RAO nutrition team will also conduct mass supplementation of Vitamin A and Albendazole administration to children aged between 6 months to 5 years.
The nutrition team will engage WART, PASS and CMD who are existing organizations implementing Health, FSL and Nutrition services respectively in the community to further enhance Multi-Sectoral Coordination and Collaboration and avoid the risk of duplicating services.

1. Needs

The population of Ayod is estimated at 200,228 with 160,182 people (80%) of the population currently estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance, this figure is expected to drop in the 1st projection to 100,114 people but will drastically increase to 85% (170,194) in the second projection. IPC Classification additionally records that the number of people in IPC crisis phase 3 will be 40% in the first projection and increase to 45% in the second projection while the number of people in Emergency IPC phase 4 will be 10% in the first projection and rise to 20% in the second projection. 39,085 children under the age of 59 months and 14,624 Pregnant and Breastfeeding women and Girls are already suffering from different forms of wasting. The key drivers for the high malnutrition prevalence are complex and include acute food shortage due to fragile security and recurrent floods, poor feeding practices, high morbidity of vaccine preventable diseases, and limited access to basic services. Deplorable WASH conditions has led to a sustained high prevalence of diarrheal diseases. An interagency needs assessment conducted by RAO and partners in Ayod County, Jonglei State on the 3rd to 6th September 2024 showed that the humanitarian situation has continuously worsened due to spillover effects of the Sudan crisis, seasonal rising of the waters in River Nile that subsequently led to the bursting of its banks leading to flooding most especially in the western part of the County caused heavy population displacements, destruction of property, farm lands, livestock and public institutions. The floods additionally led to suspension of operations in four nutrition sites. The vastness of the county, poor basic infrastructure and the migratory nature of the cattle keeping communities makes prepositioning of nutrition supplies a nightmare. Some of the densely populated Payams of Ayod have no river bank on their vicinity and this makes transportation of essential items especially during the floods to be only by air as roads become impassable. Vulnerable populations in Ayod due to these prevailing circumstances have been left exposed to extreme hunger surviving on wild food, deplorable shelter conditions high risk of diarrheal diseases outbreak, inadequate health care, poor menstrual hygiene management and sexual assault among women and girls. The situation has been worsened by the influx of returnees from the Gambella region and the Sudan. In light of this there exist a dire need for humanitarian assistance in form of food assistance (food aid and fishing kits), shelter/NFIs (plastic sheets, mats, mosquito nets and cooking utensils), health and nutrition care, WASH, protection (child protection and GBVmanagement and prevention) and education services.

2. Project activities

Reach Africa Organization a static partner in Ayod County envisions the following activities to contribute towards the achievement of the project’s goal and objectives: Contributing to the first strategic objective and preventing malnutrition RAO will; establish kitchen gardens within the vicinity of the nutrition sites where mothers will be trained to plant locally available nutrient rich vegetables, cooking demonstrations with the lead mothers from MTMSG applying the Positive Deviance/Hearth (PDH) Model to identify mothers who will demonstrate preparation of complementary food for children 6 months and above, using the locally available highly nutritious foods, mother to mother support groups will also be trained in all the nutrition sites on MUAC approach and provided with MUAC tapes to increase the self-referral of malnutrition cases, MIYCN will be promoted through individualized and group counselling sessions at the nutrition sites on breastfeeding and complementary feeding, The RAO nutrition team will liaise with the CHD and WART who is the health partner to train CNVs and the BHWs in the county to provide vitamin A supplementation and deworming to children between 6 months and 59 months. To contribute to the Nutrition sector strategic objective on early detection and treatment of wasting RAO will screen under 5 children and PBWGs through both outreach and static modalities, the children and women and girls diagnosed with malnutrition will be admitted in the program and assigned CNVs to follow up on their treatment. RAO will mainstream AAP by conducting broad consultations with the representative of the affected population this will be done through meetings and needs analysis, RAO as a static partner in Ayod already has established CRFBMs systems which allows for collection of complaints and provision of feedback to the affected population, the AAP committees already existing for SNFI and Peace building projects are already trained on HLP and PSEA. To Improve Nutrition Information Management System and contribute to CSO 3 RAO will share 5Ws, weekly, monthly and narrative reports with the cluster, CHD and the MOH both at state and national levels. RAO will also update their reports on the NIS monthly. Strengthening coordination & integrated programming RAO will work with WART and the CHD to test all children admitted with SAM for malaria and provide the antimalarial treatment accordingly, RAO will also liaise with the WASH partner to ensure that all children admitted in the TSFP and OTP programs are prioritized during registration of WASH item distribution projects, RAO will work with the education partner and UNICEF to engage children with locally made toys for stimulation and catchup growth, RAO protection specialist will train all the nutrition staff including the CNVs on RAO’s code of conduct and on GBV essentials including on how to appropriately respond and handle GBV disclosures.
