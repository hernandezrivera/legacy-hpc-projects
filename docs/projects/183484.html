Emergency in-kind food and hygiene support to vulnerable Palestine refugee households affected by COVID-19

Basic Info

Project Name

Emergency in-kind food and hygiene support to vulnerable Palestine refugee households affected by COVID-19

Start Date

31/12/2021

End Date

30/12/2022

Project Summary

To respond to increased food insecurity due to loss of livelihoods as a result of COVID-19, UNRWA will distribute food baskets and inclusive hygiene kits to 10,000 vulnerable Palestine refugee households, including 1,342 female headed households, reaching over 56,000 individuals unable to meet their basic needs because of obligatory quarantine or isolation due to exposure to or having contracted COVID-19. All distributions will include awareness raising on PSEA and information on complaints mechanisms. UNRWA's distribution is following strict safety and security measures responding to the changing environment around the COVID-19 emergency, while guaranteeing a fair, equal and consistent support to food-insecure Palestine refugees in the West Bank.

Implementing Partners

The United Nations Relief and Work Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East
(UNRWA) has one of the most significant humanitarian operational presences in the West
Bank both geographically and in terms of sectors of intervention. Next to the Palestinian
Authority, UNRWA is the biggest service provider in the West Bank. In total, the Agency
serves 1,078,772 Palestine refugees and other registered persons (UNRWA, Registered
Population Dashboard, January 2021), which amounts to one third of the population in
the West Bank, of whom more than half a million are women and girls. UNRWA was
established by the General Assembly in 1949 and has the mandate to assist and protect
some 5 million registered Palestine refugees in the region. As
the main service provider for Palestine refugees in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, UNRWA delivers basic
education, health care, relief and social services, infrastructure and camp improvement,
micro-finance, and emergency assistance. The Agency has extensive experience providing
emergency assistance to Palestine refugees during humanitarian crises to save lives,
preserve human dignity, and prevent and respond to the impacts of such crises. In the
West Bank, the Agency's programmes seek to reduce the impact of the protection crisis
stemming from over 50 years of Israeli occupation by addressing critical needs of the
most affected individuals. In terms of response, UNRWA provides critical services to those
exposed to protection threats including psycho-social counselling, medical assistance,
cash-based interventions, and the provision of dedicated programming to specific
vulnerable groups. UNRWA is the best-placed UN agency to engage with both Israeli and
Palestinian duty-bearers in the West Bank - it has wide-reaching access to all the relevant
actors and decision makers on the ground.

1 - Needs

The COVID-19 emergency is as much a public health crisis as it has come to be an economic crisis affecting the economy in the WB (including East Jerusalem) with impact on individual households, especially the most vulnerable. Regulations implemented by the Palestinian and Israeli authorities to contain the COVID-19 outbreak, have progressively limited movement into and within the WB and Israel, with areas often being closed off from each other. Public health measures to control transmission of the virus through restriction of movement and extended periods of home quarantine are having an impact in particular on populations that were already economically vulnerable prior to the crisis. These often are people that have relied on daily or informal labour or that have been income constrained altogether because of chronic illness, age, disability and gender restrictions to employment opportunities. Besides the obvious impact on incomes and livelihoods, these dynamics have particularly impacted women at home, e.g. in the form of home schooling and caring for vulnerable household members, tends to fall almost exclusively on women. Further, youth unemployment has increased disproportionately in the context of the COVID-19 emergency, particularly among women and those with advanced university degrees, illustrating the limited job opportunities and prospects for young Palestinian graduates. According to the results of the 2021 Multi Sectorial Needs Assessment (MSNA), 38.5% of refugee households in the West Bank reported members losing their job permanently or temporarily as a result of the COVID -19. The highest percentages were reported in East Jerusalem (50%), Area A&B (43%) and Area C (34%) and can be attributed to the restrictions in the WB. According to the same survey, 75.9% of refugee households in the WB reported that their monthly income has decreased as a result of COVID-19. Vulnerable households often do not have the required resources to purchase basic requirements for survival for a long time as they rely on constant flows of casual labour income or various sources of assistance over time. The near cessation of economic activity is reflected on the rates of unemployment, poverty and food insecurity that continues to to rise over time. Poor and near-poor households suffer disproportionally from higher food prices in the West Bank, and the number of food insecure refugees remains relatively high (11%, 113,000 individuals of which 46,500 are severely food insecure) compared to non-refugees (9 %). Furthermore, due to the deterioration in socioeconomic conditions of refugee families, the pandemic has posed additional challenges in terms of sanitation and personal hygiene. The reduced purchase power of households has undermined their ability to access hygiene items, which are critical in preventing the transmission of COVID-19. Beneficiaries for this intervention are identified by UNRWA social workers crosschecking the list of COVID-19 patients by the Ministry of Health on daily basis and then cross checking against the refugees register. Vulnerable households are then selected amongst refugees who were previously identified by UNRWA’s Social Safety Net Programme as poor, and those who were recommended and referred to by UNRWA’s social workers, Camp Services Officers, local committees, and hotlines. The project will prioritize the most vulnerable categories: female-headed households, elderly households, families with members having chronic diseases, families with multi-disability members, families who have lost their source of income due to COVID-19, and families who have reached out to the UNRWA psychosocial first aid hotline to request for assistance. As more populations are vaccinated, UNRWA aims to reduce the average number of food baskets and hygiene kits from an average of 30 food baskets/hygiene kits a day to less then 20 food baskets/hygiene kits per day.To strengthen inclusion of affected refugees, UNRWA Relief Services in the WB are planning to hold focus discussion with representatives of all vulnerable groups including female-headed households, elderlies, persons with disability, and women in the seven operation areas (Jerusalem 1 and Jerusalem 2, Hebron, Nablus, Jenin, Tulkarem and Bethlehem) to review this intervention and assess needs. During the focus group discussions, Relief Services will also explain accessibility to PSEA complaint mechanisms and provide material on the hotlines.As the situation of COVID-19 remains unpredictable, and continued or worsening surge could result in a substantial worsening situation. Food baskets and hygiene kits will also serve to enhance UNRWA's preparedness to acute situations of Palestine refugees.The vulnerability of the target population to protection risks following rights violations and the relative power imbalance between humanitarian workers and refugees requires UNRWA to ensure that safe complaints and feedback channels based on consultations with beneficiaries, are accessible and known to the target population, including feedback and complaints on goods and services and more serious complaints related to misconduct and sexual exploitation and abuse. Consultations undertaken by the PSEA Network for the oPt confirm the need for all organisations to strengthen SEA policies and processes, in addition to MSNA 2021 data which found that 23.8% of refugees (vs 11.1% of non-R) knew how to access complaint mechanisms. Information gaps remain on the needs, preferences, safety and confidentiality concerns of refugee women and adolescent girls, especially women with disabilities, single headed households, pregnant/lactating women and new mothers and elderly women. This information will be obtained through surveys, interviews and focus group discussions UNRWA plans to implement across the WB in 2022 to inform and strengthen UNRWA’s SEA channels and processes based on the consultation and participation of affected groups.

2 - Activities

Through this intervention, UNRWA, aims to mitigate food insecurity among the most vulnerable as a result of the COVID-19 outbreak. This will be addressed through the provision of in-kind food assistance reaching up to 10,000 vulnerable households. In addition, UNRWA will support vulnerable households in home quarantine or isolation with the provision of inclusive hygiene kits, designed and distributed according to hygiene promotion working group and WASH cluster guidance and requirements. The kits contain hygiene and sanitary items explicitly tailored to be inclusive of women, girls, infants, and the elderly’s needs such as sanitary napkins, hypoallergenic sanitary soap, infant pampers and incontinence pads. These items are intended to help maintain dignity, health, mobility, and security. This project provides a critical opportunity to support and sustain households facing acute economic shock. It aims to stop the most affected segment of the refugee population from using negative coping mechanisms to deal with reduced livelihood opportunities and the deterioration of their purchasing power. The project will serve the most vulnerable refugee households in the West Bank, both inside and outside camps including: 1. refugee families under obligatory quarantine due to infection or direct exposure to COVID-19; 2. poor families affected by the COVID-19 crisis that live remotely and do not have adequate access to banks and local markets; 3. vulnerable families with increasing needs due to the COVID-19. UNRWA's relief services teams will provide specialized hygiene kits adapted to the needed of female-headed households, families with persons with disabilities (PwD), families with chronic diseases; pregnant, postpartum and lactating women; children, adolescents and caregivers; elderly (+65) (especially those with cognitive decline or dementia, elderly with disabilities); PwD (especially those living in crowded or isolated conditions); single head of household with caring responsibilities; people with limited access to services (remote communities, Seam Zone, East Jerusalem, pre-identified communities by UNRWA's Protection team) SSNP families. As in 2021, regular coordination meetings between UNRWA and the MosD will take place, including between Area Relief Officers and MoSD directorates at the Governorate level. Assistance will be delivered through in-house distribution of food baskets and hygiene kits to vulnerable households in quarantine and/or in a situation of full lockdown. Food baskets include locally procured items such as flour, rice, beans and milk. The selection of the items has been reviewed after discussions with UNRWA's social workers, area representatives, MoSD, UN HCT Clusters and feedback from beneficiaries in 2020. Both food baskets and hygiene kits include awareness raising material on PSEA, pamphlets on COVID-19 and stickers with the PSEA hotline number .Beneficiaries will have meetings with UNRWA's area representatives and the social workers who follow their cases. Complaints can be received through the UNRWA hotline. As the number of COVID-19 cases decline, UNRWA social workers will restart post distribution monitoring by visiting the affected families, using tablets to gather and analyze the results. To strengthen M&E for this intervention and beneficiaries’ knowledge of beneficiaries on complaints mechanisms UNRWA's Relief Services (RSS) plans to expand the orientation and knowledge of refugees on access to hotlines for any other types of complaints such as sexual exploitation, beneficiaries’ protection issues.etc. RSS will equip and train social and relief workers, as well to all hotline workers on the PSEA policy and protection of beneficiaries’ principles as to advocate , provide awareness, respond and manage complaints on those sensitive issues. Consultations with beneficiaries on complaint channels (including specific consultations on SEA complaints) are planned through several health and relief interventions implemented in 2021/2022. Through its relief and social services programme, UNRWA has developed a survey for Q4 2021 to gather feedback and preferences from beneficiaries on complaints mechanisms. This survey will be triangulated with broader assessments including the 2021 MSNA for the oPt which found that 23.8% of refugees (vs 11.1% of non-R) knew how to access complaint mechanisms. Specifically on SEA complaint channels, consultations will take place in partnership with CBOs partners supporting GBV survivors through the RSSP programme which provides support to GBV and SEA survivors.In addition beneficiary inputs are provided in terms of their assessment of whether the assistance provided met their needs, and about additional needs that were outstanding. To ensure the protection of public health and safety of the population in the West Bank, UNRWA will continue home distribution while using enhanced measures according to COVID-19 health recommendations including strict use of PPEs by UNRWA's social workers. All UNRWA project-related documents contain a standard clause on PSEA in accordance with IASC principles and guidance, including all documents related to this project. Relief Services staff (prioritizing frontline staff) will complete at least one PSEA training during the project period. UNRWA plans to roll out a training-of-trainers on PSEA for staff building on the previous safe identification and referral training 80% of UNRWA WBFO staff had completed in 2020/21. This training has been developed for all UNRWA fields of operation and will be adapted to meet the needs of the West Bank staff from all departments and programmes, focusing on staff who interact with beneficiaries from vulnerable groups (school counsellors, principals, health staff). Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor centered approach in how the complaint is handled and support is offered to the survivor.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Relief Services has a complaint mechanism in place, in which free hotline numbers are provided to beneficiaries for submitting any type of complaints they have such as eligibility and other protection complaints. Also, the hotline runs a round of calls to a random sample of beneficiaries on quarterly basis for verifying their satisfaction on the service provided. The hotline regularly conducts also field visits to refugee camps and refugees communities outside camps to advocate for the hotline, encouraging them to call once they have issues or complaints. Feedback from beneficiaries will be reviewed by UNRWA's social workers to improve delivery of assistance as needed.Feedback and complaints relating to specific cases is followed up according tointernal SOPs used by health staff to adapt services, interventions and referralsfor support services to meet the needs of families and individuals. In addition,project staff collect and analyse feedback and recommendations from theaffected populations to inform decision making about the scope and modalityof service provision and overall design of the project. As part of the satisfactionsurvey, beneficiaries are provided with details about the feedback hotline as wellas specific information about UNRWA’s zero tolerance policy on sexualexploitation and abuse and how to safely report SEA cases and supportservices available. Feedback obtained through consultations on SEAcomplaints channels will also inform improvements to accessibility forvulnerable populations.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project adopts a do no harm and conflict sensitivity approach, including specificmeasures to ensure that identified risks are addressed within the action andunintended negative consequences are minimised. UNRWA has developed an internalguideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communicationsand others. UNRWA’s Case Management SOPs ensure that protection cases areidentified and referred to specialised support services. SOPs cover compulsoryreporting on SEA and support to survivors. A dedicated psychological consultationhotline is available to all refugees. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disabilityinclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in thisproject. In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignityis explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliverservices in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff.UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reducedprotection of beneficiaries, communities or staff, including risk mitigation for SEA. Ona biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreamingaudit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. Thisaudit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries,and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles,including the principle of Do No Harm. For the purposes of ensuring thatbriefings involving affected persons are carried out in a way that prioritises the safetyand dignity of these persons, UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethicalpractices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. Thisincludes a checklist to guide all staff working in this capacity. UNRWA has adapted itscase management in response to difficulty of physical access to concernedindividuals/households. UNRWA has established a dedicated psychologicalconsultation hotline available to all refugees. In addition, UNRWA has existingguidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which areall fully applied in this project.
