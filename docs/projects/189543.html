Project Summary

The aim of this project is to enhance and sustain emergency food production through provision of complementary and integrated agricultural crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools, fishing kits and agricultural extension support. The other objective of the project will be to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that builds the resilience of households. It seeks to enable vulnerable IDPs and host communities to recover from crises, with a focus on seeking more durable solutions and building the capacity of communities to cope and withstand recurrent shocks and strengthen resilience to future shocks. It will build on interventions that CTP and partners are currently delivering in the target communities. The communities have endured food insecurity caused by a complex interplay of climate variability induced cycles of droughts and food, below average crop productions and poor farming skills. These have been compounded by intensified inter-ethnic conflict, livestock raiding and worsening economic situation across the country.
This project will provide integrated streams of livelihood inputs including crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools including ox-ploughs as well as provision of fishing kits. The project will continue to support seed fairs to support local market where feasible, as informed by assessments that CAFOD and its partners continue to carry out. This will be accompanied by a comprehensive farmer training package on sustainable agroecological farming practices and climate smart agriculture including soil and water conservation techniques. The project will be implemented through a network of farmer extension services using recruited and well trained and equipped extension workers. The project will also strengthen capacity of lead farmers. Seed selection, preservation and storage will also be supported to strengthen seed security. The training will also include improved fishing practices, hygienic handling of fish and fish preservation for fisher folk. This project will be implemented using standard operating procedures that seek to prevent the spread and transmission of COVID19.
The project will reach 5,000 H/holds (30,000 people- IDPs and host communities) in
Tonj East, Tonj North and Tonj South of Warrap State. The community will be involved in targeting and a clear vulnerability criteria will be agreed. As part of beneficiary selection CAFOD will also deploy its SADI (Safety, Access, Dignity and Inclusivity) Framework to ensure inclusion of vulnerable and often hard to reach groups such as female headed households, disabled and elderly persons. To realize the inclusion of persons with disability, the project will ensure that at least, 10 percent of the benefiting households have a person/s living with disabilities.
The project will collaborate and work with other agencies working on Food Security and Livelihood intervention to achieve synergy and avoid duplication, utilize their expertise, work with health and nutrition actors such as World Vision, ACF, AMREF and Ministry of Health to support households with malnutrition cases with a basket of interventions.

1 - Needs

The Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) sectoral analysis estimates that 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) in 2022. This is an increase of 7 per cent above the 7.7 million people in need in 2021. In the Greater Tonj area, where CAFOD and its partners are working, the main drivers are floods and dry spells, inflation and high food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence. These are exacerbated by continued influx of IDPs fleeing violence and flooding from neighbouring counties, and poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services. These shocks have a disproportionate effect on children, persons with disability, older persons and women headed households. According to the FSL Cluster’s IPC-Compatible acute food insecurity analysis done for the HNO (Nov 2021-Dec 2022), 37 counties have experienced significant or minor deteriorations in their food security level, 31 counties are described as being of extreme or severe concern, 36 counties are described as of very high or high concern and 11 counties are of moderate concern. CAFOD is targeting the Greater Tonj region, which has been classified in the extreme severity class. The FSL Cluster analysis identified food security and livelihoods as the most needed forms of assistance for men in 55 per cent of assessed settlements; similarly, food and livelihoods were recognized as the number one priority need for women, in 52 per cent of assessed settlements and in the case of children, food and livelihoods were the second most frequently identified pressing need (as reported in 29 per cent of assessed settlements), behind education in 48 per cent. CAFOD’s annual post-harvest assessments in the target areas also revealed that over 50% of households harvested enough to last less than 6 months. This deterioration is driven by the seasonal depletion of food stocks, likelihood of high food prices, reduced market functionality and access challenges occasioned by the onset of rainfall that leads to the worsening of road conditions, as well as the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, economic shocks and currency devaluation that will continue to erode households’ purchasing power. Warrap State continues to bear the brunt of intercommunal violence which has displaced communities resulting in loss of lives and productive assets as they fled, making it difficult to pursue viable livelihoods activities. Despite the generosity of host communities in welcoming persons displaced by floods and fighting, they also experience poverty, and their coping capacity has been stretched to the limit. Climate variability has resulted in increasing frequency and intensity of cyclical droughts and floods

2 - Project activities

The project will assist crisis affected communities’, households, and individuals to produce enough food sufficient to meet their food requirement for a year or until next harvest season. This will be achieved through provision of locally adaptable, short maturing and high yield crops and vegetable seed varieties and fishing equipment. Seeds for traditional staple crops grown in the area like sorghum, cow peas, and ground nuts which are also major source of income and contributes immensely to food security will be provided. The quantity of seed will be based on the UNFAO crop and vegetable seed kit standard. Farmers will be provided with farm tools and implements such as ox ploughs. This support will be accompanied by contextually tailored extension support to increase uptake of sustainable and improved agroecological and climate smart agricultural practices. The project will support Agroecology training for extension workers, followed by model farmers who train farmer groups, building on traditional knowledge supporting regeneration of the environment, enhanced biodiversity, reduced erosion, improved groundwater infiltration, supporting increased productivity and production of diverse nutritious foods. Farmers will also be trained on seed selection and preservation techniques of local high yielding varieties, and will be supported to establish seed banks. The project will also support establishment of tree nurseries at schools and in gardens, to support farmer managed regeneration and reclamation of the environment. Training will also cover appropriate seeding levels, soil and water conservation, mulching, tie and ridge farming, pest management and post-harvest handling. The project will provide comprehensive and tailored agricultural extension services to the beneficiaries in order to improve productivity and production. The project will deploy agricultural extension staff and assistants from CAFOD, local partner and collaborate with state and county agriculture department to educate and train farmers targeted under the programme. Opportunities will be explored for collaboration with WFP and FAO through the voucher or cash for assets to support poor resourced farmers to plough, plant and weed farmlands. The intervention will be implemented in ways that support local markets through seed fairs based on seed market assessments. Given the high levels of malnutrition in most of the project areas CAFOD training package also includes training on preparation and consumption of nutritious foods given especially mothers and those with malnourished children. This will involve training on how to prepare various dishes using locally available foods and vegetables. Households affected by malnutrition will be prioritized for support with vegetable seeds and assorted hand tools to enhance food dietary diversity of that household. As a result, the project will provide training on fishing activities, along with this, will also provide recommended fishing kits according to UNFAO fishing kit standards. Among the training package, will be how to make and repair nets, hygienic handling of a catch of fish and preservation methods such as smoking, salting and fish storage. To ameliorate work burden of women collecting firewood’s, and health hazards caused by smoke inhalation when cooking such as respiratory and sight problems the project will promote adoption of locally adaptable fuel efficient cooking stoves. This will also minimize deforestation. The will incorporate COVID19 messaging for infection prevention and control (IPC) as well as adopt standard operating procedures to prevent and avoid infection. Conflict sensitive approaches will be used.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CAFOD implemented commodity voucher for food and non-food items in previous projects in Yirol East in 2020 and Gogrial in 2021. The venture into CVA was after comprehensive market assessment and feasibility analysis which informed the decision to undertake CVA in both Yirol East, and Gogrial East and West counties. The market assessment to ascertained the market potential to support the approach. Things considered in the assessment included functionality and access to the market by the affected population, availability and source of goods and services, and traders’ potential to supply the market. Cash transfer programming (CTP) refers to all programs where cash (or vouchers for goods or services) is directly provided to beneficiaries. It is an approach that builds upon linkages, capacities, incentives, and relationships to encourage effective market recovery. The logistical burdens and risks that are usually experienced by agencies in transporting in-kind supplies to end beneficiaries are thus transferred to the vendors or suppliers. The use of vouchers or cash reduces overall costs of the assistance and thus more people can be reached with limited resources. The engagement of local vendors in supplying items to the beneficiaries is intended to support the local market dynamics. Through this approach, the cash or vouchers given to beneficiaries and exchanged with local vendors for supplies would advance financial linkages in the local markets as well as boosting the purchasing power and local economy. Cash and vouchers assistance (CVA) is globally felt to be a more dignified form of assistance for humanitarian aid recipients because it promotes dignity of the participants. Individuals accustomed to supporting themselves and their families suddenly become dependent on aid and charity. While their sense of dignity cannot be easily and fully restored, cash and voucher transfers offer some choice and a small degree of control in a situation where many feel they have none. The affected populations preferred the flexibility that cash offered them and the choice it offered them to prioritise their own needs. We have realised that use of cash and vouchers empowers participants through ownership of their needs and the opportunity to purchase locally preferred supplies. Since CAFOD intends to provide seeds through vouchers or seed fairs this will further boost the agricultural productivity potential of local farmers and trigger market growth. Local purchase of seeds will save local farmers of transportation costs in search of better markets in Juba, The local participants will also benefit on acquiring seeds with adaptable to local ecological conditions.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Country programme undertook rigorous preparatory work including sending its cash transfer programming (CTP) focal staff for training CaLP in Kenya in 2019. Additional staff and partner staff were trained on carrying out market analysis for CTP. Additional staff and partner staff were trained on carrying out market analysis for CTP in the eld in 2019. This enhanced staff capacity on CTP. The skills in CTP were horned by carrying out an actual market analysis by CAFOD in Yirol East, Rumbek and Gogrial in 2020 in preparation for its food and NFIs distribution project which was successfully carried out involving traders in Wau and Yirol East. Consultations during the market analysis were held with communities, local chambers of commerce who mobilised the traders, other local vendors, consultations with other NGOs, and government officials. This was to dig out and unpack information to streamline processes before it rolled out its cash-based response. As mentioned above, a market assessment and cash feasibility analysis were undertaken which informed CAFOD to embark on the commodity voucher programme. Based on that experience CAFOD will build on lessons learnt from that experience in shaping the CVA approach for of this project. Currently CAFOD and partners are undertaking Market Systems Development (MSD) training to enhance staff capacity on market and value chain analysis. In 2021 CAFOD has already embarked on market analysis for supply of various commodities such as crop and vegetable seeds in which this project will bene t from this analysis. There is planned market and seed fairs feasibility assessment to gauge the venture into the use of local markets to support CVA and seed fairs. To ensure beneficiary choice and flexibility especially as regards the types of crops varieties and the mode of assistance there will be consultations with the participating households whose lives are affected. Over time, the fairs have shifted from offering seeds mainly from local farmers and local seed multipliers to including private sector partners and research centers to widen the types of seed and varieties available. An important secondary aim has been to stimulate local seed business and inject money into the local economy, as well as to build up private sector businesses (including agro-dealers) that might better orient their business to smallholder farmers on an ongoing basis. Seed fairs also enable disaster-affected farmers to access seeds of their choosing; typically focused on local sellers/farmers but may also include private sector and government, improve access to diverse seeds and agricultural products to enhance household nutrition and increase food security and resilience; includes household decision-making, private sector input dealers, community-based seed multipliers, and individual sellers, and protect and restore livelihood assets; often includes seeds, livestock, and other non-agricultural assets.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will make a deliberate effort to explore, understand and mitigate programme related risks leading to the development and implementation of strategy that minimize and address all ranges of threats and risks both external and internal. In practice, this means that all intervention responses will be guided by Core Humanitarian Standards to which CAFOD is certified and Do-No-Harm and conflict sensitivity approaches through analysis of risks to programme participants and environment. In relation to risk 8.1 on long distances to distribution centres CAFOD has proactively mitigated this risk in its projects and will do so for this project. In the past there were 7 distribution sites in Yirol, but because of protection risks related to long walking distances, an additional 4 distribution centers were created to reduce distances. In Gogrial distribution points were increased after consultations with traders who were asked to open temporary shops closer to participants. As part of its responsibility of mitigating risk 8.2 and 8.3 on SEA, CAFOD has various policies on PSEA policy, code of behaviour and safeguarding policies which are signed by every staff member, volunteers and contractors before any official engagement to ensure protection of project participants. Project participants are also made aware of these policies through various information sharing ways including setting up of information kiosks and agents in the project locations. CAFOD and its partners have a robust complaint and feedback handling mechanism in place, and hotline to receive complaints and feedback. CAFOD carries out regular community awareness on the existing channels of airing out their complaints and feedback as well as making them understand that it is their right to complain. In addition, there is a policy on whistleblowing which protects the whistleblower. CAFOD has clearly laid out procedures on handling and responding to feedback and complaints. Complaints are categorised and serious, sensitive complaints such as SEA are handled by senior management with clear investigation and disciplinary protocols it must be resolved within set time limits and feedback provided to affected persons. In relation to risk 8.4 on no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors CAFOD has robust protection response and prevention activities in most of its projects. CAFOD programmes provided community based GBV protection measures such as case management, psychosocial support, creation of women and girls friendly spaces, CAFOD and its partners have mapped out referral pathways trained service providers on their use. There is also training of various service providers (health workers, police, chiefs etc.) in Yirol East and Gogrial to strengthen their capacities on GBV case reporting, survivor centred handling and management of cases. As regards risk 8.8 where the risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs based selection of beneficiaries CAFOD has employed measures to restrict or limit this influence. CAFOD makes use of a targeting committee comprised mostly of women from the community. Targeting criteria are agreed with the community in inception meetings and their inputs incorporated to ensure no vulnerable families are left out. When targeting is completed a public meeting of community members is called for public verification and acclamation of selected persons. CAFOD also uses its SADI (Safety Access, Dignified and Inclusivity) framework to mitigate exclusion of marginalised and vulnerable groups in a project. The framework carries out a comprehensive assessment of at-risk groups and how they are affected differently by a disaster or conditions of their vulnerability using such diversity statuses such as sex, age, disability, being a refugee, or IDP, minority status and others. This leads to design of projects that meet the specific needs of groups of people.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The proposed intervention will contribute indirectly to this collective outcome by enhancing households own food production through provision of complementary and integrated agricultural crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools including ox ploughs, shing kits and agricultural extension support. The project will strengthen households’ ability to produce their own food and be self sufficient in ways that build the resilience of households and reduce dependence on relief aid. It will build the capacity of communities to cope and withstand recurrent shocks and strengthen resilience to future shocks. It will strengthen self-reliance in own food production by improving the agricultural productive capacity of farmers. In addition to crop inputs, vegetable seeds, farming implements the project will be accompanied by a comprehensive farmer training package to improve the farmers productivity and production. Through the increase in food production and self sufficiency in own food requirements this project will help to decrease the proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5
