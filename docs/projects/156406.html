Needs

The damage caused by the ongoing conflict in Syria has led to more destruction of homes, and displaced people trying to find alternative accommodation in a variety of shelter types, in inadequate conditions, lack of necessary facilities, inadequate space & lack of privacy.. A safe & sustainable solution has become urgent across Syria, with more than 4.7 million of people in need; 1,219,874 Syrians having returned to their place of origins, especially in Rural Damascus, Homs, Hama, Aleppo, and Deir ez Zor.The project will address through its activities the needs assessed within the assessment conducted by SSSD teams in the selected locations, indicating huge needs for permanent shelters in Damaged Houses for returnees, in areas of returning population; in Aleppo, Homs & Rural Damascus.

Implementation of activities

SSSD Project activities will focus on improving safe and decent housing conditions by sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing to vulnerable conflict-affected populations, selected based on vulnerability criteria (Guidelines of Rehabilitation of Damaged Houses) with priority to most vulnerable families of returnees, (owners/tenants/host families) & will be implemented by assessing the technical needs in the selected areas after obtaining the needed approvals, preparing BoQs for necessary works, announcing the tender for materials & labour, after the adjournment of the tender, & the preparation of contracts, SSSD staff monitor & evaluate the work performance before the handing over of the final work to beneficiaries.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. Likelihood: Medium / Impact: High / Mitigating measures:- Ensure that service is provided according to beneficiaries selection criteria & vulnerability scoringProtection risk: Tension in the community and risk of most vulnerable being left out if selection criteria is influence by local authorities Likelihood: medium/ Impact: medium / Mitigation measures: - Selection process for private shelter to follow sector guidelines ensuring all steps are followed in a transparent and compliant way. (Criteria, code of conduct..)Protection risk: Exposure to sexual harassment and assault, caused by overcrowding and lack of privacy, lack of doors and partitions. Likelihood: Medium / Impact: High / Mitigating measures: - Include women, girls and other at-risk groups to determine the needed safety & privacy measures.- Periodic community safety audits.Protection risk: Physical safety of beneficiaries/staff is at risk due to structural damages of the building. Likelihood: medium/ Impact: high / Mitigation measures: - Selection of structurally safe buildings. - Structural assessment of buildings to be done by qualified personnel. Protection risk: Exclusion of benefiting from shelter repair/rehabilitation works due to lack of tenure document Likelihood: high / Impact: medium / Mitigation measures: - Communication of alternative documents to prove ownership.- Engages local authorities to establish a mechanism to deal with such issues at local level.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Lack of building materials in the local market;Mitigation measure is to ensure the procurement of the total quantity to be stored in the project's warehouses ahead of implementation.2. Difficulties to access the location by the staff of the Syrian Society for Social Development due to security problems;Mitigation measure would be to recruit a part of the staff of local inhabitants of the selected location.3. Changes in prices of materials;Mitigation measure would be to ensure flexibility in the specifications of adequate materials.4. Delay in obtaining official approvals;Mitigation measure would be to apply for approvals ahead of the assessment stage of the project/ Change of the location.
