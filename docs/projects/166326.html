Provision of Integrated Comprehensive services in a COVID 19 Situation for survivors of Gender Based violence in the Northeast, Nigeria.

Basic Info

Project Name

Provision of Integrated Comprehensive services in a COVID 19 Situation for survivors of Gender Based violence in the Northeast, Nigeria.

Start Date

28/12/2019

End Date

27/12/2020

Project Summary

The proposed intervention will ensure IDPs, returnees and host populations in the northeast Nigeria are provided with immediate life-saving age and gender-responsive humanitarian assistance, through the provision of safe water, health (including trauma management), NFIs and shelter, emergency food assistance, detection and treatment of acute nutrition, clinical management of rape, psychosocial support and sanitary materials in a coordinated manner. These activities will ensure that social protection systems and measures are put in place to address gendered risks over the course of the outbreak, as they play a vital role in protecting women from poverty and insecurity, helping them and their families cope from the outbreak, and recover from other economic and social shocks and ultimately promote and protect their rights. Advocate for surveillance and response systems that include disaggregated health-related data by age, sex, pregnancy status and disability to ensure that the most at risk have equal access to resources and opportunities. Services provision will apply gender analysis when developing outreach messages to the general public on public health measures including tailored messaging directed to men and women, as specificity will be needed to resonate with the right intended audience.

1 - Needs

Nigeria as other countries in the world is facing a humanitarian crisis, the COVID-19 outbreak potentially further exacerbate tensions on fragile and weak social systems in the country especially the northeast region of the country. The COVID 19 pandemic compound existing gender inequalities, and increase risks of gender-based violence. During this COVID-19 pandemic, where movement is restricted, people are confined, and protection systems weaken, women and girls are at greater risk of experiencing gender-based violence, and the threat of harmful practices including female genital mutilation and child, early, and forced marriages, especially for girls in disadvantaged and hard-to-reach areas. The COVID-19 pandemic may increase women’s domestic burden, making their share of household responsibilities even heavier. Disease outbreaks affect women and men differently, and epidemics make existing inequalities for women and girls and discrimination of other marginalized groups such as persons with disabilities and those in extreme poverty, worse. Also women represent nearly 70 percent of the healthcare workforce globally and not enough attention is given to how their work environment may be discriminatory, as well as what their sexual and reproductive health and psychosocial needs are as frontline health workers. An additional layer of COVID-19 impacts will add to the challenges that those areas are already facing and is likely to further increase the number of vulnerable people. Due to access constraints and the limitations of the social worker capacity it could have the potential to spread more rapidly in hotspots and fragile settings and have more severe impacts that will be difficult to diagnose and contain. In addressing the COVID 19 Pandemic women can be less likely than men to have power in decision making around the outbreak, and consequently, their general needs and health, including sexual and reproductive health, may go largely unmet.

2 - Activities

1. Adapt services provision for GBV survivors and those at high risk using the women and girl safe spaces to decrease the risk of corona virus exposure to ensure that the Do No Harm principle is respected and scale-up remote modalities for service delivery, including for case management and psycho social support, with accompanying training, staff support and quality of care2. Provide technical support to ensure that GBV prevention and clinical management care and GBV referral systems are functioning according to national guidelines.3. Prioritize strengthening the response capacity of national hotlines; thus, increasing remote access to mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and safety planning opportunities with trained service providers4. Ensure that clinical management of GBV such as RH Kits, sutural of cervical and vaginal tears kit, post-rape treatment kits and dignity kits are available5. Promote and protect the ethical, safe collection and use of gender and GBV related data throughout UNFPA’s programmatic responses, which may include reviewing and improving the functionality of pre-existing data protection mechanisms for the COVID-19 response6. Prioritize strengthening the response capacity of national hotlines; thus, increasing remote access to mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and safety planning opportunities with trained service providers.7. Strengthen health services and provider competencies to render a comprehensive response to sexual violence and its consequences, including counselling, shelters, hotlines, prevention and treatment of sexually transmitted infections8. Ensure that health workers have the necessary skills and resources to deal with sensitive gender-based violence-related information, that any disclosure of gender-based violence be met with respect, sympathy and confidentiality and that services are provided with a survivor-centred approach.
