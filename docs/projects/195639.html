Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to provide integrated GBV ,child protection and general protection services to 70,000 most vulnerable conflict affected population including IDPs, resident communities and returnees in Darfur, South Kordofan, Jazirah , Port Sudan , Gadaref and Khartoum States. Under GBV sub-sector, UPO aims to reach 30,000 most vulnerable women and young girls affected by conflicts in Kass, Beliel, Alradom, Kirinik, Elgineina, Kadugli, Ede'en, Yassin, Umdukhun, Malliet,Zalinges, Sharq Alneel, Um durman, hasahisa, Um Algura, Ganab, Port Sudan localities with provision of dignity kits, awareness raising, gender sensitive consultation ( medical and mental )and capacity building , construction and rehabilitation of women multi use centers to help increase their protection, sharing and participation, the project will also focus on the provision of PSS, GBV community networks and safe channels, livelihood start-up kits for the most vulnerable, training of GBV front line services providers as well as GBV referral support system. UPO conducts its projects with an inclusive and responsible business-model that ensures two way communication pathways throughout the implementation and exit stages in addition to ensuring meaningful access and feedback to all stakeholders. UPO will coordinate all project activities with the GBV subsector team across the target states including sharing regularly the ¾ W. For child protection sub-sector, UPO aims to reach 20,000 children with CBCPNs PSS, CFS, capacity building, identification of
UAC, referral supports , distribution of dignity kits, alternative cares, case management ,
assessment and awareness raising to the public on child protection and development , disaster reduction and coping mechanisms. and 20,000 vulnerable people with general protection and identification, documentation and legal aid support, UPO commits to strengthen the coordination both at the field and Khartoum across the protection sector and the sub-sectors.
Over the ten per cent of the activities cost of the project will be implemented through cash
assistance approach for the PSN and extremely affected populations. This CASH will
directly be distributed to the target populations. Each family will be receiving cash support
for 12 months , $150 per month to help support essential needs of the families.
The majority of the activities are designed a durable, environmentally friendly and
sustainable way for long term solutions for the IDPs, returnees and host communities. i.e.
women and community centres will be constructed from permanent materials that can
last for over 10 years, Community structures/protection committees/ networks with
strengthened and supported to continue taking their roles beyond project lifetime. Women
capacity building and training to continue tacking, preventing and addressing any
protection concerns beyond the project duration. in the 2023 HNO the Protracted and new displacement induced by intercommunal localized conflict often driven by political agendas, increasing protection of civilians risks and threats due to the rise in criminality and insecurity in parts of Darfur and other conflict-affected areas. Humanitarian partners estimate that about 26 million people – about a half of the population – will need humanitarian assistance in 2023. UPO uses and focuses on gender sensitive programming and modalities by designing all activities through gender roles frameworks and gender matrix frameworks to ensure safe and inclusive participation and sustainability of the project outcomes by creating a change theory in the communities gender norms and harmful practices and promoting healthy habits and co-existing spheres.

1 - Needs

since April 15th the War between the SAF and RSF has a continuous affect of causing fatalities and displacement throwing Sudan in an unprecedented state of conflict and humanitarian risks, the inter-tribal conflicts have increased significantly this year than in 2022 and the situation has become worst than the previous years. The inter-tribal fighting also continues and significantly impose additional protection risks to the populations. displacement is expected to continue not only as a result of the fighting between the armed groups. There are still major concerns about intercommunal violence and general insecurity; as the situation remains volatile, more displacement is certain. This disproportionately affects women and children and restricts their access to basic services right to participation, development and their ability.to realize their potential. In conflict contexts, women and girls bear the brunt of theviolence and brutality as rape, sexual abuse, exploitation and other gender-based violenceis common. The forms of violence and abuse encountered by minors include childmarriage, child labor, child recruitment by armed groups, unaccompanied and separated.children, female genital mutilation, lack of birth certificates and child trafficking. several assessment reports revealed that very little aid has been directed to the IDPs, most of the support coming from the hostingcommunities. The needs include provision of shelter, PSS support for both adults and children, health services, especially to pregnant women, mitigating measures to reduce prevailing threats to women and adolescent girls they likely to encounter on their way to water collection points, fire wood collection and local markets. There is no active CBPN and the existing traditional community platforms are weak. there is many reported smuggling and human trafficking across the border with Ethiopia,Chad,Sudan and Eritrea andmany protection issues reported specially child protection in many states in Sudan with little response. There is an urgent need to respond to these identified needs as well as to continue the registration and family reunification of unaccompanied/separated (UASC) and missing children, and to reinforce and establish CFS, to prevent violence, exploitation, abuse and other protection risks. GBV continues to be a life-threatening concern for the newly displaced Jebel Marra and SK, women and girls continue to face risk of different types ofthe GBV, insecurity, economic challenges and lack of livelihood opportunities, as well as a lack of community awareness on women rights. S/GBV remain a big gap specially in Darfur, Khartoum due to the ongoing conflict and resulted to hundred of thousands of displacements. the protection /responses interventions are hampered by lack of accessibility to conflict affected areas, as well as the cultural acceptability of the issue, which affects the ability to provide a timelyresponse to the needs of vulnerable women and survivors. There is also lack of access tosocial services and legal redress in the worst impacted areas, which means that the mostvulnerable often do not receive the necessary support services due to cash liquidity andfunding issues.

2 - Activities/Outputs

the key activities will be implemented as follows. 1. Support 7 Community Based Child Protection Networks through training, building their capacity (local activists, community leaders, PTAs); 2. Develop and coordinate referral pathways at village level and conduct beneficiary satisfaction feedback on the use of the referral systems; 2. Establish 2 communitycenters to provide collective and individual PSS, emergency kits and conduct bimonthly workshops; 3. Train health workers on identification, referral and provision of PSS to malnourished children at risk and their caregivers; 4. establishing 3 women centers and supporting operation of 9women centers. 5. conducting 10 GBV assessment and distribution of dignity kits to8,000 women and girls 6. Conduct public community awareness-raising campaigns on GBV and gender sensitive programming at locality level targeting 20,000 (8,000 men, 9,000 women and 3,000 boys and girls). 7. Provide different services to vulnerable women & girls including GBV survivors targeting 4000 women and girls, 8. Conduct trainings on GBV for frontline GBV service providers targeting 200 GBV service providers (90 female and 110 male), 9. Conduct trainings for community-based staff members, paralegals targeting 200 person (80 female and 120 male). conducting focus group discussions, collection of information on the situation of physical safety, legal safety and material safety issues as well as freedom of movement and residence. supporting the existing and establishing new community-based protection networks, early warning systems.
