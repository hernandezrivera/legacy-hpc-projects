Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen disaster resilience among vulnerable Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar district through effective multi-hazard risk management. The project will target vulnerable Rohingya refugees and host community in Cox’s Bazar. The Project will work in close collaboration with ISCG Sectors, government, camp disaster management committees, and other relevant stakeholders to support ISCG's overall disaster risk management strategy through the following project activities:
(i) Capacity development of government, host & camp disaster management committees, and humanitarian stakeholders to manage disasters and extreme weather events (target 600).
(ii) Improving localized weather forecasting, landslide early warning, and rainfall monitoring systems (target 20000)
(iii) Supporting inclusive disaster risk assessment, disaster preparedness, and anticipatory actions (target 5000).
(iv) Facilitate and support multi-hazards/DRR analysis, plans, and weather forecast products for government, ISCG sectors (CCCM, EPRWG, EEN, FSS, Shelter), and humanitarian stakeholders (target 100).
(v) Capacity building (including the provision of DRM equipment and gears) of first response agencies/professionals (camp frontline volunteers, CPP, FSCD, etc.) for inclusive disaster preparedness and response (target 375).
(vi) Supporting DRR awareness raising in the Host community and Rohingya camps through information, education and communication in DRR (target 15000).
(vii) implementation of community-based disaster risk reduction interventions adopting nature-based solutions in the Rohingya camps (target 15,000).
(viii) Support vulnerable population with cash-for-work in implementing DRR interventions (target 2300)
(ix) Assessment study of the geophysical profile of the Cox’s Bazar district
(x) Installation of additional 10 optical rain gauges to landslide-vulnerable locations
The project will conduct disaster risk assessment (particularly landslides) and will contribute to implementing ISCG’s multi-hazard response plans. The project will train first response agencies/volunteers on DRM aspects and will provide DRM equipment and gear to support response and early action in Rohingya camps. The project, in collaboration with camp actors, will support the capacity building of camp disaster management committees and DRM planning at the camp level. The disaster risk reduction schemes adopting nature-based solutions will be implemented in coordination with the SCCCM sector, EEN, and CiCs in the camps.
The Project will develop strategic alliances and strengthen partnerships with Agencies implementing disaster risk management initiatives in the Rohingya camps, ensuring synergies, optimum use of resources, and effective programming.
Gender issues will be effectively mainstreamed across all disaster risk management (DRM) interventions and approaches. For instance, the project will ensure the active participation of women, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups in project DRM interventions. The project will support gender-sensitive and inclusive DRM training for local government and DMCs. The project will also train women volunteers/ representatives on DRM aspects. The project will ensure that local DRM plans include gender and disability-inclusive elements, such as the needs, risks, vulnerabilities, and capacities of women, girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. All assessment, monitoring, evaluation, and reporting systems will include sex, age, and disability disaggregated data. The project targets 50.8% of women as beneficiaries and prioritizes women and female-headed households for direct support. The project will implement community education and awareness-raising initiatives on DRM, particularly targeting women, girls, and persons with disabilities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Cox’s Bazar is one of the most disaster-prone districts in Bangladesh and is highly exposed to natural hazards and extreme weather impacts, including cyclones, landslides, flash floods, torrential rains, storm surges, and extreme temperatures. Cox’s Bazar is now home to around one million Rohingya refugees since 2017. The Rohingya crisis has significantly increased the size of the population at risk, as well as added pressures on the environment, which undermines resilience. The location, climate, topography, and human interference make the refugee camps and hosting areas more vulnerable to natural hazards and extreme weather impacts. According to the SMSD Daily Incidents Yearly Report, natural hazards and extreme weather events such as windstorms, heavy rains, slope failure, landslides, and flooding impacted 208,699 individuals in camps in 2021 and 139,687 individuals in camps in 2022. Similarly, natural hazards and extreme weather events adversely affected the camp and local communities in Cox's Bazar 2023 and 2024. This climatic context has a considerable impact on the refugee and host community response operation in Cox’s Bazar. Landslides remain one of the most substantial damaging and recurrent hazards in Cox’s Bazar, typically due to excessive and prolonged rainfall in a short period, high population density, unplanned land use, and rampant hill cutting. The Rohingya refugee crisis has further exacerbated the pre-existing landslide susceptibility due to the removal of vegetation and hill cutting for refugee shelters, other facilities, and new roads. The women, girls, children, elderly, persons with disabilities and other minorities were particularly affected by these natural hazards and extreme weather events in Rohingya camps. Female Rohingya refugees have a lower socio-economic status. This leads to unequal access to opportunities and risks, making them more vulnerable to natural hazards and extreme weather events. Despite the high vulnerability to disasters, women increasingly play a critical role in disaster risk management. Therefore, empowering women is the key to strengthening disaster resilience among Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar district. The vulnerable communities will be identified following consultation with the government, CiC, CCCM, and humanitarian stakeholders, with priority areas identified and verified against known hazard and risk maps. For the selection of participants from the communities, representation of both genders, youth, the elderly, people with disabilities, and other minorities will be actively sought. The major challenges for comprehensive disaster risk management in the district include: the lack of capacity of government, DMCs, and humanitarians actors to plan, manage and respond to disasters and extreme weather events; lack of effective weather forecasting, landslide early warning and dissemination system to trigger early action; lack of capacity of first response agencies/professionals to manage search, rescue and response operations in hilly terrain; inadequate nature-based solution initiative for DRR, especially for landslides; limited DRR awareness among excluded groups; limited gender-sensitive and disability-inclusive DRR projects/interventions; lack of capacity for mainstreaming DRR into sectoral planning and interventions. The project will closely work with local government, ISCG sectors and other relevant stakeholders to address these challenges and needs."

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project will contribute to age and disability inclusion and child safeguarding throughout the project. Despite the high vulnerability to disasters, the elderly, children, and persons with disabilities increasingly play a critical role in disaster risk management. So, the project targets the elderly (8.24%) children (11.02%), and disability (1.43%) groups to reduce their disaster risks and build their DRR capacities. The project will prepare a gender and inclusion plan and will ensure that project activity is tailored to different age and disability group needs. The project will ensure the active participation and inclusion of the elderly, children, youth, and persons with disabilities in the DRM interventions. The project particularly mainstream disability inclusion. The project will ensure the active participation of the elderly, children, persons with disabilities, and other diverse groups and will address their needs, risks, and capacities throughout the project. All assessment, monitoring, and reporting systems will include sex, age, and disability disaggregated data.

The project will consider and take action to promote ‘information, communication, and transparency;’ to ensure ‘participation, engagement, and inclusion;’ to place ‘complaint and feedback response mechanism;’ and to improve ‘learning and adaptation.’The project will set up appropriate mechanisms to ensure the active participation and inclusion of affected populations throughout the project cycle and will place measures to address concerns and complaints. The project will engage with communities through different meetings, consultations, and awareness-raising activities. 2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project solely focuses on strengthening disaster risk management, disaster risk reduction, and community resilience for Rohingya refugees. The project will support ISCG's overall disaster risk management strategy. The project will closely involve and assist ISCG in advancing DRR/DRM within the realm of the Rohingya crisis response and will facilitate coherent DRR efforts among the stakeholders working within the camps. 4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees. It aims to enhance disaster resilience for vulnerable Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar through multi-hazard risk management strategies. This includes capacity building for camp disaster management committees and first response agencies, improving weather forecasting and early warning systems, and implementing community-based disaster risk reduction (DRR) interventions with nature-based solutions. Additionally, the project offers cash-for-work programs to support DRR activities and provides gender-sensitive and inclusive DRM training to ensure active participation of women, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups. These efforts foster self-reliance, resilience, and preparedness within the refugee community.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project activities in the host community align with the development priorities of Cox’s Bazar district, specifically with the District Development and Growth Priorities (DDGP) from December 2022, where disaster management is the third priority sector. The project aims to build disaster resilience in the host community through multi-hazard risk management, capacity building for local disaster management committees, and improvement of early warning systems. The project also promotes community-based disaster risk reduction interventions, including nature-based solutions, and supports vulnerable populations through cash-for-work initiatives for disaster risk reduction (DRR) activities. These efforts not only address disaster resilience but also contribute to broader developmental needs in livelihoods, infrastructure, and community safety, thereby aligning with district-level planning and enhancing the community's capacity to manage recurring climate-related risks.
