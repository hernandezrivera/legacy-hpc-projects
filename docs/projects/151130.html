Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to contribute to the improvement of the access to adequate shelter solutions through small and medium scale rehabilitation works of the premises of the most vulnerable people affected by the conflict. The project will provide support to 3,150 vulnerable women, men, girls and boys among IDP, returnee and other conflict affected communities of Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts (NGCA).

Needs

Based on Shelter Cluster estimations 50,000 homes (20,000 are damaged in GCA and 30,000 in NGCA) incurred various forms of damage on both sides of the contact line during four years of the conflict. Around 10,000 vulnerable households located on NGCA require humanitarian shelter interventions. Because of the restricted humanitarian access that prevents from the systematic verifications the number of the damaged houses might be different. Shelter cluster database reveals that the following scale of repairs are required: 33 % light repairs, 27 % medium repairs, 33 % heavy repairs and 7 % reconstruction; According to the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA), October 2017, 17% of households reported living in damaged housing. In addition to increasing rates of physical damage to properties, many houses lack basic shelter infrastructure. Nineteen per cent (19%) of households lacked doors to their homes, seven per cent (7%) of households reported lacking windows and 3% reported lacking floors. Due to the cracks in the walls, old or damaged doors and windows, leaking pipes or lack of functioning heaters, during the winter the temperature in the entire building or in parts of it is lower than 18 degrees Celsius threatening the health and unfit for life of the household members. The half of respondents (50%) of the IOM’s PDM on coal distribution 2017/2018 noted the need for insulation of the houses as a type of assistance for winter 2018/2019;Recent surveys demonstrate increased trend for people returning to their permanent places of residents including to NGCA due to high living costs and lack of livelihood opportunities in the areas of displacement. The latest National Monitoring System Survey on the Situation of Internally Displaced Persons showed that the majority of the respondents (78%) indicated that the reason behind this return was the possession of private property and that they don’t need to pay for rent. The average profile of those who forced to return to the NGCA and frontline areas are women over 60 years of age. Up to 20% of those whose homes were damaged are unemployed making purchase of construction materials and payment for repair work difficult.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Vulnerable individuals in conflict-affected communities of Donetsk and Luhansk NGCA have been provided with shelter solutions; Output 1: Vulnerable households improved their access to adequate shelter through rehabilitation works of conflict-affected accommodation in light to medium categories - Activities for Output 1:1.1. Verification and selection of beneficiary households;1.2. Procurement of construction materials based on the results of the assessment visits;1.3. Select construction company to perform rehabilitation works in the selected households;1.4. Monitor small and medium rehabilitation works to be held by the selected construction company.

Indicators & targets

Outcome 1: Vulnerable individuals in conflict-affected communities of Donetsk and Luhansk NGCA have been provided with shelter solutions: % of households that reported that their living conditions are improved (Target: 75%); Output 1: Vulnerable households improved their access to adequate shelter through rehabilitation works of conflict-affected accommodation in light to medium categories: 1) # of households supported with light shelter assistance (Target: 500 households, equivalent to an estimated 1,050 individuals, of whom 630 female and 420 male – 105 children, 315 adults, 630 elderly); 2)# of households supported with medium shelter assistance (Target: 1,000 households, equivalent to an estimated 2,100 individuals, of whom 1,260 female and 840 male – 210 children, 630 adults, 1,260 elderly).
