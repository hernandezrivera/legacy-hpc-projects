Project Summary


Title: Dry Season Farming for Improved Livelihoods and Food Security in Adamawa State: A Focus on Jada, Ganye, and Toungo LGAs
This 12-month project aims to improve the livelihoods and food security of 5,000 smallholder farmers in Jada, Ganye, and Toungo LGAs of Adamawa State. The project will enhance dry season farming practices through the provision of irrigation facilities, technical training, and access to markets and credit facilities.
The project's purpose is to:
- Increase agricultural productivity and incomes among smallholder farmers
- Improve food security and nutrition among vulnerable households
- Enhance the resilience of smallholder farmers to climate change and environmental degradation
The project's target beneficiaries include:
- 5,000 smallholder farmers (3,000 men and 2,000 women)
- 15,000 household members
- Vulnerable households, including those headed by women, children, and the elderly

1 - Needs

The project will address the following assessed needs: 1. *Limited access to irrigation facilities*: Smallholder farmers in Jada, Ganye, and Toungo LGAs lack access to reliable irrigation facilities, making them vulnerable to crop failures and reduced yields.2. *Insufficient technical support and training*: Farmers require improved technical knowledge and skills to adopt climate-resilient agricultural practices, improve crop yields, and enhance their livelihoods.3. *Poor access to markets and credit facilities*: Smallholder farmers face challenges in accessing markets to sell their produce and credit facilities to finance their farming activities.4. *Vulnerability to climate change and environmental degradation*: The target communities are highly vulnerable to climate-related shocks, including droughts, floods, and temperature fluctuations, which exacerbate food insecurity and poverty.5. *Limited access to agricultural inputs and equipment*: Smallholder farmers lack access to quality agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizers, and equipment, which hinders their ability to improve crop yields and productivity. These assessed needs will be addressed through the project's activities, which aim to improve the livelihoods and food security of smallholder farmers in Jada, Ganye, and Toungo LGAs.

2 - Activities

Project Activities The project will undertake the following activities to address the assessed needs: - *Irrigation Infrastructure Development*: Construction and rehabilitation of irrigation canals, water harvesting structures, and distribution networks to provide reliable water supply for dry season farming.- *Capacity Building and Training*: Provision of technical training and capacity-building support to smallholder farmers on climate-resilient agricultural practices, irrigation management, and post-harvest handling.- *Provision of Agricultural Inputs and Equipment*: Distribution of quality agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizers, and equipment, to smallholder farmers to improve crop yields and productivity.- *Market Access and Linkages*: Establishment of market linkages and access to credit facilities for smallholder farmers to sell their produce and finance their farming activities.- *Climate Change Adaptation and Mitigation*: Promotion of climate-resilient agricultural practices, such as conservation agriculture, agroforestry, and integrated soil fertility management. These activities will be implemented using a combination of modalities, including: - Community-based approaches, working closely with smallholder farmers, community leaders, and local authorities.- Partnership with local NGOs, CBOs, and private sector entities to leverage resources, expertise, and networks.- Use of digital technologies, such as mobile apps, SMS platforms, and online marketplaces, to enhance access to information, markets, and financial services. By implementing these activities, the project aims to improve the livelihoods and food security of smallholder farmers in Jada, Ganye, and Toungo LGAs, while promoting climate resilience and environmental sustainability.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Our project recognizes the importance of collecting feedback from the most vulnerable community members to ensure their needs are addressed and their voices are heard. To achieve this: 1. *Inclusive Feedback Mechanisms*: We will establish multiple feedback channels, including: - Community meetings and focus group discussions - Mobile phone-based surveys and SMS feedback systems - Complaint and suggestion boxes in public areas - One-on-one interviews with vulnerable individuals2. *Targeted Outreach*: Our project team will conduct targeted outreach to vulnerable groups, including: - Women's groups and associations - Disability groups and organizations - Elderly and minority groups - Refugee and internally displaced persons (IDPs)3. *Trained Feedback Collectors*: Our project staff and volunteers will receive training on: - Effective listening and communication skills - Sensitivity to vulnerable groups' needs and concerns - Data collection and analysis techniques4. *Anonymity and Confidentiality*: We will ensure that feedback is collected anonymously and confidentially, with measures in place to protect respondents' identities and prevent retribution.5. *Feedback Analysis and Response*: Our project team will regularly analyze feedback, identify trends and patterns, and respond to concerns and suggestions in a timely and transparent manner. By implementing these strategies, our project will ensure that feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members, and their voices are heard and valued throughout the project cycle.
