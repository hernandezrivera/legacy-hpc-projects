Project Summary

Women and girls in Gedo region of Somalia are among the vulnerable groups most affected by heightened vulnerability from the negative consequences of the prevailing political and economic situation in Somalia. They face increased risks of SGBV, and their vulnerability is further exacerbated by dominant harmful traditional and cultural practices including FGM, early and forced marriages, child labour, and discrimination in accessing life-saving services and control of productive resources such as livelihood assets. A recent protection assessment conducted on December 2020 by Trócaire in Gedo pointed to the fact that there are very limited ways to seek justice for SGBV survivors, with traditional ways of resolving such cases being the most acceptable and common in the community. However, these traditional systems prioritize communal benefits over survivor’s rights. Of the women surveyed in the assessment, only 7% reported that they could access health services after experiencing violence. The fear of stigmatization in the community, a lack of information and physical and economical barriers to accessing health facilities deter survivors from seeking lifesaving medical care and legal aid. Furthermore, a recent completed two-year project by SEDHURO in Gedo region, funded by UNICEF revealed that the key impediments to pursuing and receiving justice for SGBV survivors included lack of knowledge of their rights, mistrust of the criminal justice system, coupled with gender biases of police officers and lack of financial resources by the survivors and their families.
The project targets about 10,000 people including 1000 boys/men. 6300 women, and 2700 girls from targeted communities living in Belet Hawa, Dollow, Luuq and Garbaharey districts in Gedo region by implementing actions against all forms of SGBV. The target beneficiaries will be empowered to become advocates of women and girls’ rights and supporters of law enforcement in achievement of girls and women’s rights.
As the coordinator of Gedo Sub-GBV working group. Trócaire has since coordinated with local and international stakeholders to strengthen protection and GBV services in Gedo and further seeks to engage with local organizations to strengthen inclusion in service delivery especially for people with disabilities.
Among the project target are 9,000 women and girls including those with disabilities (around 100 women and girls with disabilities will be targeted) who will be empowered through provision of livelihood skills that will help them attain financial independence. The project will also strengthen the already existing Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services by providing capacity building on CMR to clinical and non-clinical staff through refresher trainings to those who already acquired the training to enable them to provide the survivors with comprehensive care and medical services within the recommended time. It will also strengthen the referral system for protection cases among service providers and community actors. The project will Identify and train legal, health and protection service providers as well as the community gate keepers, including the police, District Health Boards, community outreach workers and organizational staff from other partners to strengthen their capacity in service delivery. Safety audit as well as barrier and facilitator assessments will also be conducted to identify the existing barriers for people with disabilities in accessing services and mitigation measures identified.
Trócaire will collaborate with Socio-Economic Development & Human Rights (SEDHURO) a national NGO with strong experience in implementing Protection projects in Gedo to implement this intervention in the four target districts.

1. - Projects activities

Trócaire aims to undertake the following protection activities:• Identify and build the capacity of community structures that support advocacy work on SGBV - 60 members of advocacy groups will be identified and trained, 15 from each of the 4 target districts (Luuq, Dollow, Belethawa and Garbaharey). • Identify and train legal, health and protection service providers, including the police, District Health Boards, community outreach workers and organizational staff from other partners on GBV- 60 service providers will be trained, 15 from each of the four target districts.• Provide life skills, leadership training and basic numeracy trainings to women and girls within the centres - 500 women and girls will be targeted across the 4 districts.• Provide Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism to ensure people receiving assistance share their concerns - CFRM desks will be set up at all Trócaire women centres in the 4 districts, that those receiving protection services can reach and share their concerns, ideas and feedbacks. The desks will be managed by center managers who will be trained on how to operate the desks. in addition, Trocaire’s other robust CFRM channels including the free hotline (2111) will and suggestion boxes at all the service points will allow two-way interaction between Trócaire and project beneficiaries.• Provision of psychosocial support to women and girls (survivors) - the project will support the ongoing psychosocial activities given to women and girls across the 4 districts. The project will target 1000 women and girls across the 4 districts.• Strengthen Referral Pathways and improve Case management for women and girls - the project will strengthen existing referral pathways and improve the case management services by providing f training to protection service providers. In addition, 30 service providers will be trained on case management across the 4 districts.• Running and maintenance of already existing girls and women safe spaces/ centres where they can learn livelihood skills to gain financial independence. The skills that will be provided at the centers will include, tailoring, baking and saloon management. The activity will target 40 women across the 4 districts.
