INTERSOS Action tackles identified problems and unmet needs through an EiE response targeting the most vulnerable population in areas with high severity conditions in Hama, Homs, Aleppo, Rural Damascus Governorate. In line with Sector 2021 Syria HRP, the Action aims to improve 1) safe and equitable access to formal and NFE services; 2) enhance the quality of teaching and learning; 3) enhance communities’ capacities and role in promoting the establishment of conducive learning environments to ensure boys and girls continuation of learning. INTERSOS aims at outreaching a total number of 8370 beneficiaries (B 4150; G4150; M35; W 35). The programme is starting in May 2021 and will be continuing throughout 2022. Despite being the first Action implemented in the EiE sector in Syria, INTERSOS is building on its extensive expertise in the delivery of NFE and supporting formal school systems in the Middle East region (Iraq, Lebanon, Jordan). INTERSOS is currently working under a bilateral MoU with SARC and negotiation for a bilateral MoU with MoE are in the final steps. To maximize the impact of the Action and enhance local sustainable response capacity, INTERSOS carries out integrated action where Protection and CP principles are mainstreamed along with GBV preventive messages. INTERSOS will work through multiple approaches promoting boys and girls ACCESS to formal and NFE through: SLP, remedial classes (after school for children at risk of drop out and in TLS/learning center for OOSC). INTERSOS promotes an inclusive environment enhancing access of CwDs (target 837) and providing the community with the skills to support CwDs inclusion in the learning environment. Educational kits are delivered to all the children enrolling in the classes (stationary and SLP). Classes are arranged based on a flexible schedule to accommodate the needs of children supporting the family with work/ house chores. Regular classes are alternated with PSS sessions aimed at promoting boys and girls’ wellbeing and retention. QUALITY of teaching and learning is promoted through training to formal and NFE personnel on Active Learning, Inclusive Education, Curriculum and Planning, Pedagogy, Teachers’ role and well-being, CP principles (last 4 subjects are already included in INEE standard TiCC training). INTERSOS has developed a response strategy based on a strong COMMUNITY-BASED APPROACH coupled with a robust capacity building programme to enhance the quality of services delivered to the affected individuals. PTAs will be established and/or supported and their members will be trained on: CP elements, PSEA, Safe identification and referral, education outreach and monitoring, gender mainstreaming in education. Once trained they become the first initiators of: i. education monitoring (outreaching activity) and ii. Back to Learning (B2L). Through consultations with the community and HH assessments, the education outreach volunteers identify Out of School Children and individual causes for being OOS. Such methodology promotes the definition of individual pathways for re-enrolment through NFE. The education outreach volunteers also contribute to identify boys and girls at risk of drop out in formal/NFE environment, along with main causes and mitigation measures. Finally with the starting of COVID-19 INTERSOS started integrating health and hygiene promotion and infection prevention and control in its action providing holistic knowledge and capacities to the targeted communities. INTERSOS will target the most vulnerable boys and girls at risk of drop out and OOSC privileging overage students, adolescents girls and boys, IDPs and CwDs.

4 - Needs

2021 HNO estimates 13.4M PiN of humanitarian assistance, where 95.1% is under severe, extreme and catastrophic conditions. It is estimated that about 6.9M of people (97% children and adolescents) are in need of education assistance (HNO Education Sector 2021). 1 out of 3 children in need is reported to be OOS. Under the effects of COVID-19 pandemic school aged children living in severe, extreme and catastrophic education conditions reached 94% (8% higher than 2020) entailing an exponential risks of children that spending prolonged time out of school will never re-enter the learning system. More than 10 years of conflict and displacement left schools destroyed and/or damaged, with consequent overcrowded classrooms, lacking essential supplies, equipment and adequate wash facilities. The lack of learning facilities in remote areas and last resort sites differently affects IDPs and residents access to education. Overaged students are at high risk of not re-entering education and this is also related to the scarcity of secondary schools. The quality of the learning system has been deeply impacted facing today an insufficient number of teaching personnel in need of professional development. Adults and children have been differently affected reporting signs of PTSD, depression and isolation, mainly girls, anger and violence behaviour usually among boys. Teachers themselves show signs of distress and are in need of PSS, peer support groups and of an improved referral system. The financial crisis is also pushing boys out of school to support their families and poses girls at higher risk of early marriage. CwDs are particularly affected by this complex crises and continue to be excluded because of lack of investment and awareness.

5 - Implementation of activities

In line with Sector 2021 Syria HRP, the Action aims to improve 1) safe and equitable ACCESS; 2) enhance the QUALITY; 3) enhance COMMUNITIES’ CAPACITIES and ROLE.INTERSOS works through SARC which guarantees access to the targeted communities following a community based approach strengthening PTAs role. Community resource persons are identified as education facilitators. B2L is launched to encourage the enrolment of children in formal/NFE and to inform parents considering B and G, IDPs and returnees, CwDs inclusive access to education. Remedial classes are provided after school hours in formal settings for children at risk of drop out and in Community Learning Centers targeting OOSC. CLCs are identified in reachable locations, capitalising on existing facilities where available or renting static centers. INTERSOS promotes blended teaching methodology adapted to COVID-19. Initial, progress and final tests based Syrian National Curriculum are submitted to children to evaluate the improvement of performances against the learning outcomes of each grade and to prepare the child for formal school placement test. Quality of the classes provided is monitored through: spot check,in class observation,Improvement of the Quality of the Learning Environment Checklist(IQLE),Teachers Learning Circles(TLCs),Children Friendly Feedback Mechanism,H Assessment. Teachers Capacity Development is supported through training on pedagogy, inclusive education, curriculum and planning, teachers’ role and well-being, active learning. Such training are complemented by Code of Conduct&Positive Discipline, CP, PSEA and Safe Identification&Referral training. TLCs are regularly facilitated to engage the education facilitator in a process of self-evaluation

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risks: Staff recruited has prior conviction for SEA or related offence; Implementing partner, staff member, suppliers, (etc.) do not comply/accept PSEA policy; SEA perpetrated by staff members/service providers; reinforced stereotype of beneficiaries through wrong communication and images. Mitigation: job opening state that INTERSOS has PSEA and CP Policies that all staff is expected to comply with; tests include the Attitude Scale; assessment about the partner’s commitment and capacity to prevent and respond to SEA is conducted; where possible, training are offered to partners to identify SEA risks and define related SEA mitigation measures throughout programming; SEA risk analyses and mitigation strategies are elaborated at the country level: categorization of interventions, implementation modalities by risk significance level, structure to report, investigation structure; “Do No Harm” principle guide the collection and use of images and information about beneficiaries; images and stories only with the full understanding and permission of the subject. The informed consent needs to be signed and filed in a secure location; all staff and affiliated workforce must read, understand and sign the INTERSOS PSEA/CP Policy Declaration prior to contract start date; all staff must receive the PSEA/CP Policy induction training; all staff must receive one PSEA/CP Refresher training per year; awareness raising sessions are to be conducted with communities and other actors by PSEA FPs.
