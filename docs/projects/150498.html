Project Summary

The proposed project relates to health cluster objective #1 and to activities #2 and #3. Medical Aid for Palestinians will work in partnership with Islah Charitable Society to run a mobile clinic serving Bedouin communities in the Jordan Valley. The clinic, run by a general physician, nurse and 3 community health workers from a vehicle equipped with basic equipment and medicines will visit 9 communities twice a month delivering basic primary healthcare, maternal and child healthcare, health awareness sessions, first aid classes and hygiene kits.
The communities served by the clinic are isolated, located in Area C of the West Bank where new buildings are prohibited by Israel and their shelters and buildings are often subject to demolition. The mobile clinic therefore will serve as a lifeline to these communities who otherwise would have little access to healthcare.

Needs

Bedouin communities in the Jordan Valley continue to be isolated from government services in part due to their need to live in rural areas within Area C to preserve lifestyle and cultural traditions. There are no permanent healthcare facilities as the Palestinian Authority is not allowed to build permanent structures and there is no regular public transport to take residents to access healthcare. UNRWA faces significant funding cuts and their support for mobile clinics in the WB will be compromised by MAP.The goal of the proposed project is to improve the health status of Bedouin communities in the Jordan Valley by providing equitable, high quality Primary Health Care and Maternal/Child Health services through a mobile health clinic. MAP’s mobile clinic has been running for over 10 years in partnership with Islah Charitable Society(ICS) and has developed trust with the communities. We maximize the impact and avoid duplication of services through close coordination with Health Cluster partners and the mobile clinics working group. Added value is provided by our continued advocacy on behalf of all Bedouin communities in Area C and planned legal support and our long-term support for the development of family practice in the WB in partnership with MoH and UNRWA. MAP will also be committed to serve all registered refugees in our targeted communities whom UNRWA will stop serving.Currently the mobile clinic visits 9 Bedouin communities (8710 Bedouins,95% refugees) at least twice a month with the most vulnerable visited weekly. Limited funding and a single vehicle have prevented weekly visits for all communities but this is the target following the recent purchase of a new vehicle and improved funding opportunities. During the past year focus group discussions and knowledge/satisfaction surveys were conducted with Bedouin women, men and children. These showed that services are effective and highly valued. A recent evaluation of the project validated these findings with the results informing the design of the new project phase.Women and children comprise 79% of the target population and are the most vulnerable. Research through the Lancet Palestinian Health Alliance showed significant maternal/child mental health needs and expected reproductive health/communicable disease needs. The Community Health Workers(CHWs) attached to the mobile clinic have unique access to women and children and can conduct screening mental health assessments referring on to the clinic doctor. They will also facilitate disease data collection and enhancing GBV detection and child protection linking these communities with the national referral system.Palestinian National Institute of Public Health research in 2016 also revealed a rise in levels of stunting in Bedouin children under the age of 5. Mobile clinic staff can detect early signs of malnutrition and prevent stunting which is irreversible leaving children unable to achieve their full social, academic and health potential. A new male CHW will improve access for men in the community; a particularly hard group to reach. People with disabilities, the most marginalized within these communities will also receive equitable access to health services. The health education and first aid training components of the project improve knowledge and behavior on a range of health issues building greater resilience within communities. ICS is the only mobile clinic providing these services and the demand for them is high.The project is urgently needed to support essential health care services, protect marginalized communities, promote greater resilience within these communities, preserve dignity and avoid causing harm to any Bedouin.Through our advocacy work and capacity building activities, MAP promotes the voices of members of these communities, encourages them to provide feedback and complain if needed without fear of adverse consequences strengthening their capacities and improving their sense of well-being.

Activities

MAP, working with ICS with close and direct supervision of MAP staff seeks to improve the health status of these communities through improving access to quality primary healthcare services as well as improving health resilience among the population. This is achieved through the provision of a newly staffed and well-equipped mobile clinic which travels twice a month to each of the 9 communities to provide PHC services, maternal and child health care for pregnant women, mothers and children up to the age of 5, treatment and monitoring of patients with chronic illnesses as well as hygiene kit distribution for everyone in the community. The project also seeks to increase awareness of community members and change attitudes and behavior through regular community educations sessions and first aid training which are only provided by ICS. These help to empower beneficiaries and increase communities’ resilience. Through the addition of a male CHW, we hope to see an improvement in male health seeking behaviour among the communities we target. In total, 3 CHWs with great knowledge of Area C and experience working with Bedouin communities, will be selected to build the capacity of the local communities who are isolated and vulnerable. The clinic staff also organise and run day trips for women to nearby cities, summer camps for children and celebrations to mark International Women’s Day and International Day of Persons with Disabilities. The project coordinator employed by our partner will have daily direct contact with MAP team to insure project is running as per action plan and budget. MAP staff will be directly involved in all procurement details and complete adherence of partner to MAP’s strict due diligence policies. The MAP team will also support ICS M&E follow up and progress of the project by improving current assessment tools to better measure impact on targeted population. Community participation in carrying out the project monitoring and evaluation is vital as it would highly develop the ownership of project amongst the targeted communities. Accordingly, MAP and ICS will encourage involvement of project beneficiaries to take part in the monitoring and evaluation process through on-going recording of case studies, conducting interviews and providing feedback.The activities include:Activity 1.1.
