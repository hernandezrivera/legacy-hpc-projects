Project Summary

The Darfur states area remains one of the areas worst-affected by the ongoing conflict which erupted in April 2023 between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), which is heavily impacting civilians and has triggered in Sudan the world's
largest displacement crisis. The overall objective of this project is to contribute to reducing the morbidity and mortality of vulnerable populations in the targeted localities in West Darfur, by improving access to quality primary healthcare, nutrition, and community-based PSS support, strengthening the health system and other community-based mechanisms.
PUI will provide assistance and basic quality lifesaving health services to recent IDPs and host communities with increasing needs, supporting five health facilities in West Darfur, 3 HFs in Geneina (Alwadi, Aljabbel,
and Shokri PHCs) and 2 HFs in Kereinek (Um Duwain PHC + one HF to be handed over by SMoH), + 2 Mobile Health teams, 1 in each of these two localities:
-provision of essential pharmaceutical commodities following standard purchase quality process, distribution and management, and non-medical equipment and supplies to health facilities + HFs rehabilitation, including necessary WASH rehabilitations, in and around the HFs. PUI will continue to coordinate with UNFPA and WHO for the provision of SRH and IEH kits.
-Provision of free life-saving primary health care services for the population in the targeted localities through support to health facilities and mobile clinics (OPD, SRH services, including ANC, PNC, family planning and deliveries, immunization, nursing care, investigations and drug dispensing, + medical care for SGBV), referrals to higher level of care to facilities supported by partners (MSF in Geneina and Kereinek), + provision of psychosocial support groups sessions. All services will be free of change to the beneficiaries. The second modality is the deployment of mobile health teams to reach locations where security and infrastructure do not allow for the provision of primary health care through fixed facilities)
-Staff training (outbreak preparedness, national/WHO standard protocols and guidelines, SRH.
Nutrition:
-support to community management of acute malnutrition: provision of quality nutrition services to IDPs and host communities in 3 HFs in Geneina, 2 HFs in Kereinek and 2 MHT, one in each location, through support to OTPs for children with SAM without complications and support to TSFPs for MAM children. Services provided will follow PUI’s Simplified Approach, as endorsed by the Federal MoH in case supplies are not available. PUI coordinating with UNICEF for nutrition commodities supplies, but will will purchase contingency stock to ensure access and continuity of the treatment for the newly displaced population or in newly accessible areas. + referrals of SAM with complications in Geneina and Kereinek hospitals, both supported by MSF.
-establish 5 Mother Support Groups, one per HF's catchment population areas, of 10 women each => provided with IYCF training, cooking demonstrations + Family MUAC. Network of trained community volunteers, in charge of active screening in community and community awareness
The project and activities will be directly implemented by PUI team on the field, and through our office in Geneina, West Darfur. PUI has a sub-base in neighboring city of Adre, Chad, where our central pharmacy is located. PUI is an active member of the Health and Nutrition Sectors, of the Protection Technical Working Group (TWG) and the MHPSS TWG, at the national and sub-national level in West Darfur., and of the Consortium Protection/Safeguarding TWG.
PUI plans to provide 78,976 OPD consultations, 2,288 deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants, and provide PSS support to 3,840 people; PUI will provide treatment to 1,182 children under 5 suffering from MAM or SAM without complications, and with complications through referrals.

Implementing Partners

PUI will work in partnership with a national NGO on this project, to ensure local acceptance, transferring of skills and sustainability. The NNGO will support community mobilization and engagement efforts, conducting community outreach activities, health education, including awareness raising sessions on the prevention of common illnesses, hygiene promotion and awareness raising sessions on GBV with local community groups, in close coordination with PUI and the MoH. In nutrition, they will select, train and support community nutrition mobilizers, support house-to-house active case search by CNMs; they will select, train and support Mother Support Groups, organize sessions on Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN), and cooking demonstrations, among other activities. The NNGO has already been identified: TDO, a non-governmental, non-political and non-profitable Sudanese organization. The Trust, Rehabilitation and Development Organization (TDO) has a long-standing experience and field presence in remote areas and volatile contexts. TDO's access to and experience with local communities, including traditional and religious leaders, will ensure the project’s and activities' anchorage and sustainability in the communities.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

PUI projects and activities are designed and implemented around an integrated approach, guaranteeing the centrality of Protection and gender issue as cross-cutting themes to ensure an intervention quality requirement. As per the guidelines of the Global Protection Cluster (GPC), protection principles are incorporated into each single project, and promote meaningful access and safety and dignity in all activities.The following pillars of Safe and Dignified programming are mainstreamed throughout all sectors and activities, and all PUI staff have or will be trained and will receive refreshment training on Safe programming principles and safeguarding policies:: 1. Prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing harm: prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks2. Meaningful Access: arrange for people’s access to assistance and services -in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who maybe particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 3.Accountability: set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints4.Participation and empowerment: support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to claim their rightsTo prevent causing harm and ensure the safety of the beneficiaries, PUI will conduct sessions on safety measures to adopt during the repairs and after the work. These will also be developed with the aim to reinforce the beneficiaries’ autonomy and capacity to protect themselves in case of endangerment. PUI will ensure creating a safe and dignified environment. PUI aims at identifying the most vulnerable individuals or groups when leading assessments and surveys, to be able to appropriately analyze their needs and feedbacks, and PUI will develop strong accountability mechanisms as well. PUI will develop efficient accountability mechanisms, with the local authorities and community leaders. PUI will apply the “Do No Harm” principle across all its activities. PUI will also ensure that the supported health facilities are safely accessible, particularly for the vulnerable groups. An entrance accessible for people with disability, and a safe waiting area will be dedicated to women. Across all its intervention at health care facility level, the health and nutrition staff will able to identify unattended cases in need of PSS and baseprotection assistance such as child protection related issues, GBV cases, and severely traumatic experiences. PUI will activate the identified and relevant referral system when necessary.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Premiere Urgence Internationale's commitment is to make sure that communities, beneficiaries, and among them the most vulnerable members, can provide feedback, make suggestions or express, file complaints if required. PUI has developed a community-based feedback system that consists of multiple entry points such as complaint/suggestion letter box, sometimes a free-of-charge helpline, a helpdesk, and community volunteers' networks. PUI also regularly conducts key informant interviews (KII) and FGDs to understand the evolving context of its intervention areas as part of the monitoring strategy. Besides, PUI works alongside community leaders and representatives to gather inputs that influence the program design and implementation, the targeting methodology, the gaps, and unaddressed needs analysis. These channels are reviewed yearly following beneficiary feedback to ensure the mechanism is inclusive, context-specific, and accessible safely and confidentially by women, girls, boys, and men. Accountability teams are composed of both male and female members who speak the most popular language of the area of intervention to allow beneficiaries to easily express their concerns. To ensure a consistent feedback management process across the intervention areas, SOPs emanating from the organizational accountability field guide are in place to track and respond to complaints and feedback. The SOPs describe steps for managing both non-sensitive and sensitive cases (e.g, fraud, corruption, PSEA, child protection).

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

PUI will ensure that the field staff share with the community PUI’s commitment to accountability to the affected population on issues such as staff recruitment and training, code of conduct, information sharing and transparency. All PUI staff receive the organization Code of conduct and all PUI ethical policies, including PSEA. PUI has a zero tolerance towards all forms of abuse, exploitation, mistreatment and moral or sexual harrassement. All persons entering in contact with Première Urgence Internationale collaborators, members, partners, suppliers, associates, and beneficiaries in relation with its activities must be protected to the greatest extent possible against deliberate or involuntary actions and failures which would expose them to risks of abuse, harassment or sexual exploitation and any other prejudice. PUI will ensure cases can be reported. All representatives and collaborators of Première Urgence Internationale undertake in all circumstances to respect the provisions, norms, and commitments to report any concerns, suspicions, or allegations of PSEA and any violations of this Policy in accordance with the wisthleblower mechanism and recommended timelines, as detailed in PUI Ethical Framework. The signature of the code of conduct includes a commitment to respect all policies and to maintain high ethical standards. PUI will set up a CFRM adapted to the local context to collect the views and complaints of a wide range of stakeholders (affected populations, communities, local civil society actors, international humanitarian actors, local authorities or national governments and donors) and take appropriate action. It will be designed in line with the protection principle of Do No Harm, in consultation with partners and affected communities, taking also into account available internal resources (financial and human) and with the objective of gradual scaling up. PUI thus aims at guaranteeing the availability of appropriate Feedback/Complaints channels,while ensuring effective complaints handling, in order to avoid creating frustration or mistrust with regard to the proposed mechanisms. Depending on the context, this mechanism consist of i) Community Mobilizers, who would be important messengersconveying feedbacks and complaints from the population, themselves being part of the community ii) Complaint boxes, especially in health facilities ii) Regular consultations with community focal points / community committees iv) Focus group discussions led by PUI teams. Whatever the mechanism(s) chosen, PUI will inform the population of the existence and functioning of the complaint and feedback mechanism. This information will be disseminated through several channels (i.e.through community relays, during surveys etc.) in order to ensure that the targeted community can make use of it as soon as the first activities are launched and Inform the population in the intervention area of the anti-fraud, anti-corruption and PSEA principles.
