Project Summary

"ACF intend to continue its important contribution in the reduction in morbidity and mortality associated with acute malnutrition in children under-5 through the provision of life saving activity in the Stabilization Centers (SC) in Kotupalong and Balokali Makeshift Camps. These 2 centers will cater for children identified as SAM with complications and/or infants with low birth weight from the 33 Integrated Nutrition Facilities (INF) run by other nutrition partners and those that are referred from the Health Facilities. Additionally, ACF will work with the INF teams to ensure that they are equipped with the knowledge and skills to conduct accurate identification of children needing referral to the SCs in a timely manner and on the follow-up care of the children when they will be sent back from the SC to continue with the undernutrition treatment.
At the SC, IYCF individual and group counselling as well as health and nutrition promotion will be organized for mothers and caregivers. The awareness promotion will focus on early initiation of breastfeeding, exclusive breast-feeding, age-appropriate complementary feeding, and continuation of breastfeeding up to two years of age. Nurses will support mothers with feeding difficulties and ensure appropriate linkages with other service providers such as health, Mental Health and care practices, and GBV for additional support. ACF team will provide caregivers information of the different services that are currently available in the camps and provide support to caregivers needing specific referrals.
The SC will operate 24/7 and will ensure systematic referral for children with complex medical issues and will work closely with the health team to continue the nutritional support while under treatment in the health facilities.
ACF aims to maintain its Community Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) in the host community in Cox Bazar. ACF will continue to support CMAM program implemented by the Ministry of Health (MoH) in primary health care units and community clinics through the local NGO SHED.
In order to maximize the impact of the CMAM program, ACF is working with different partners to improve the community component through several activities as IYCF and wash-nutrition (hygiene promotion focused on the 1000 days and support to nutritional treatment with hygiene kits). ACF proposed intervention also aims to contribute to the improvement of Sexual Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) services delivery. These services in the host community need a boost as this has been sidelined by the COVID-19 response and other competing health problems that the local health system is trying to respond to. SRHR needs among adolescents and women of reproductive age need to be better covered for improved health and decreased negative maternal outcomes.
ACF will conduct surveys in the refugee camp and the entire Cox’s Bazar District to assess the Malnutrition prevalence, project coverage and other relevant surveys. The surveys that will be implemented will be discussed and agreed at the Nutrition sector level and with approval from the IPHN/NSS. ACF will provide the survey expertise and services.
In all of ACF’s activities, mainstreaming of gender and protection aspects will be strongly embedded.
"

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"This project was designed to meet the ongoing nutritional needs within the Rohingya Refugee Camps and the host community. Refugee camp: According to the recent Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) survey (November/December 2021), the overall wasting prevalence (combines Global Acute Malnutrition - cGAM) remains below the emergency thresholds of very high level of 15% in all camps but still within the “Serious/High:10-14%” level of acute malnutrition. The combined GAM prevalence slightly deteriorated in Kutupalong camp [11.8% in 2020 vs 14.1% in 2021] while it improved in Nayapara RC from 14.7% in 2020 to 13.7% in 2021. Significantly higher prevalence of anemia (>60.0%) was observed among younger children (6-23 months) as per IYCF survey conducted in 2019. In the Host Community, the combined GAM rate was 9.9% in Teknaf in February 2021 and 13.0% in Pekua in June 2021 with average SAM prevalence of 0.5%, average stunting of 23.7% and underweight of 23.9% according to SMART surveys conducted in 2021 these areas. Therefore, it is important that the provision of life-saving services will continue to cater for SAM children with complications. Additionally, ACF will provide technical support through remote mentoring and coaching to health partners, managing undernourished children with COVID-19 complications if and when the need arises. The WHO Guidelines for the inpatient treatment of severely malnourished children include a recommendation to provide sensory stimulation or play therapy for children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM). That include psycho social well being, reducing maternal stress to ensure optimal breastfeeding and care for the child to recommended growth and development. During the implementation time frame, there is need to continue monitoring the nutrition situation of both the FDMN and Host Communities to ensure that the interventions impact are assessed and in case of deterioration of any indicator, timely actions are taken. Surveys and assessments are critical for the implementing partners to keep track of the situation, adapt response and for advocacy to donor partners."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project in both the Refugee and host community will be implemented in partnership with Unicef, WFP and other implementing partners. The Stabilization Centres will cater for referrals from the integrated facilities. No duplications of activities and full collaboration with the INF teams will be maintained. In the Host Communities, ACF will support the MoHWF in running the Stabilization Centres and work closely with them on the CMAM Program.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

ACF will continue to engage the affected people during the project cycle to be able to understand the different risks and threats and support them in coming us with the actions to protect themselves. ACF has worked in this particular humanitarian crisis for more than a decade and although not directly intervention activities, ACF has brought about the protection lens into its work. Staff were trained to prevent and mitigate potential and actual risks, being sensitive to people’s age, gender and consider diversity within the affected population. Part of ACF principle is to respect the dignity and rights with the consideration of the “Do No Harm Policy”. ACF will continue to assist people to claim their rights and access appropriate remedies with MHPSS and the protection referral system.

3.2 - Gender

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

ACF aims to integrate DRM into its undernutrition and emergency operations because: it is unethical to wait for the onset of suffering resulting from a disaster to trigger a reactive, emergency reaction: the dynamics of undernutrition and emergencies are increasingly known, and should be addressed proactively before hazards strike, it makes good operational sense to protect the gains made by humanitarian and development initiatives in communities and households and it makes good economic sense to spend proactively before an emergency strikes, thus protecting investments made by the community, governments and their aid partners. Both in the Host community and the Refugee camps, ACF team were already trained on Nutrition in Emergency and the same training were done in different parts of the country while concrete actions are integrated in ACF supported facilities and including keys messages during regular health and nutrition community forums and awareness sessions.
