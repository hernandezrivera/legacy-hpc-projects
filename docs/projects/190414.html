Project Summary

Please note the proposed action is partially funded and started for 2022.
Communities in Somali Region are facing the impacts of ongoing displacement and the effects of the worst drought in years – a drought that has built upon successive failed rains since late 2020. It is estimated that over two million people in Somali Region are in need of humanitarian assistance, while nearly 900,000 are displaced across the region. Interventions are particularly required in the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) sector, as well as in the agriculture sector to counteract devastating loss of livestock caused by a combination of drought and livestock disease.
In response to this situation, ACTED aims to respond across six IDP and host community sites in Dawa and Liben Zones with a multi-pronged WASH and Agriculture intervention that will benefit a total of 8,100 households (HHs) (48,600 individuals).
In the WASH sector, the project will achieve the following:
• Rehabilitation of two key boreholes and fit them with solar pump mechanisms;
• Rehabilitation of two earth pan water storage facilities;
• Establishment and/or strengthen of water management committees at the sites of each rehabilitated water facility;
• Construction of 164 double-unit toilet blocks to improve site sanitation;
• Provision of WASH NFI kits and dignity (menstrual hygiene management) kits to 1,600 HHs (at least 9,600 individuals)
• Hygiene promotion campaigns at all six target sites, benefitting all project beneficiaries;
• Three months of livestock fodder to 150 HHs (900 individuals);
• Provision of ad hoc logistical support to local authorities monitoring and responding to livestock disease outbreaks in Liben and Dawa Zones;
• Coordination with humanitarian structures and local authorities with regard to all activities undertaken over the course of the project.
The proposed interventions will respond to WASH and agriculture needs identified by ACTED, in coordination with local/regional authorities, clusters and UNOCHA. Specifically, the WASH response will provide life-saving access to water and WASH NFI items while also building communities’ resilience to future drought-related shocks by improving long-term water supplies. The agriculture response will support communities’ ability to bounce back at the end of the current crisis by allowing households to sustain key breeding stock and by supporting local authorities’ efforts to combat livestock disease outbreaks.
The proposed intervention will last 12 months.

1. - Projects activities

1.1.1 Rehabilitation and solarization of two boreholes (1,666 HHs; 10,000 individuals): Access to water is particularly crucial in sites affected by drought-induced displacement in southern Somali Region. ACTED will rehabilitate two key boreholes (one in Liben Zone and one in Dawa Zone), and both will be fitted with solar-powered pump mechanisms. 1.1.2 Rehabilitation of two earth pan water sources (500 HHs; 3,000 individuals): ACTED will rehabilitate two key earth pans to provide water for the purposes of washing, sanitation, and watering of livestock. In line with the fact that Liben is significantly more populous than Dawa, and also in line with the fact that the majority of the project’s targeted population is in Liben, both earth pans that have been identified for rehabilitation are located in Liben Zone. 1.1.3 Establishing/reinforcing water management committees (WMCs) (This activity will indirectly benefit all beneficiaries of the Water Supply Sub-sector): Alongside rehabilitation (and solarization) of water sources, ACTED will facilitate the establishment of WMCs at the sites of each rehabilitated source (for a total of 4 WMCs established/strengthened) to oversee continued and sustainable use of the rehabilitated water sources.1.1.4 household water trucking of 7.5L per individual per day to 1,920 HHs (11,520 individuals) across IDP and mixed IPD/host sites.1.2.1(a) Provision of WASH kits (1,600 HHs; 9,600 individuals): As part of ACTED’s multi-component, integrated intervention in the WASH sector, ACTED will provide wash NFI kits to 1,600 HHs (at least 9,600 individuals) across the six sites targeted through the overall project. 1.2.1(b) Provision of dignity kits (1,600 HHs; 4,800 women and girls): To enable IDP women and girls to manage their menstruation safely, ACTED will additionally provide 1,600 households (approx. 4,800 women and girls) with dignity kits suitable for use by up to three HH members, with contents informed by Wash Cluster Ethiopia recommendations.1.3.1 Construction of toilets (2,140 HHs; 12,840 individuals): As part of the overall package of work under the WASH sector, ACTED will construct 164 double-door toilet blocks (328 individual toilets). In line with Sphere standards of 50 individuals per latrine (for emergency and short-term situations), this number of toilets will serve 2,140 HHs (12,840 individuals) – a number representing 45 percent of the population of target sites with IDP or mixed IDP/host community populations.1.4.1 Hygiene Promotion (8,100 HHs; 48,600 individuals): To complement the project’s other WASH activities, ACTED will conduct hygiene promotion campaigns alongside other WASH activities conducted as part of the project. The hygiene promotion activities will achieve blanket coverage, benefitting all residents of target sites (8,100 HHs; 48,600 individuals). 2.1.1 Provision of fodder to pastoralist IDPs (1300 HH; 7,800 individuals): In response to the clear needs of pastoralists stemming from the ongoing drought and livestock loss on Dawa Zone, ACTED will support 1,300 HHs (representing 7,800 individuals) in Tawakal IDP Site, Hudet Woreda, with livestock fodder suitable for sustaining key breeding animals for three months. ACTED will provide each of the target HHs in Tawakal IDP Site with 24 x 15kg bales of hay per month over three months, meaning a total of 72 x 15kg bales will be distributed to each beneficiary HH over the course of the three-month livestock intervention. 2.1.2 Logistical support to local authorities for livestock disease monitoring and prevention (not possible to accurately estimate number of beneficiaries). Please note locations may vary as ACTED commenced drought relief activities in the target areas whereby the action is already partially funded for 2022.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Beneficiaries of the target locations including Woreda and Zonal authorities have been involved in the project design. Additionally, prior to the commencement of the activities, a detailed assessment will be undertaken in form of a KAP survey. The KAP survey will be done through mixed methods of questionnaires, KIIs and FGDs to ensure all the segments of communities are involved. Not only the survey provide a baseline, but also will gauge the knowledge of the communities to avoid repetitive messages which may lose interest of the communities and end up not addressing critical WASH knowledge gaps. Vulnerable groups will be consulted on their preference especially in terms of locations of water points and latrines to ensure safe distances are considered and to reduce protection risks. During implementation, communities will be informed of all the planned activities and their key roles at different stages. Community hygiene promoters ensuring gender balance will be selected from each target location and be the focal points for the delivery of the project. Preferred communication channels for each location will be agreed upon and communication means for the beneficiaries as to ensure no information gaps. Collected feedback will be analyzed and consultation undertaken, where necessary, leading to modifications within the course of the project.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

ACTED will establish/maintain Complaints, Response and Feedback Mechanisms (CRFM) in each target site. The CRFM will utilize a range of communication modalities determined in consultation with communities at project onset, and may include direct engagement with ACTED Accountability staff, dedicated 24/7 CRFM hotline, physical complaint boxes and text messages/WhatsApp. Consolidated CRFM reports will be developed to ensure a holistic overview of beneficiary feedback and consistency in response. The feedback will be classified on a monthly basis and categorized according to sensitivity. ACTED applies a five-tier scoring system for complaints received via the CRFM, enabling ranking of feedback, with each level from one to five having pre-determined procedures for due course of action. Complaints are also assessed with respect to ACTED’s Code of Conduct, which includes the aim of upholding ACTED’s accountability to those it serves and prevent any forms of abuse or exploitation. For the most urgent and/or serious complaints (sensitivity levels 4 and 5), the response pathway will be launched the same day in consultation with ACTED HQ investigation unit. This system ensures consistent handling of complaints in line with accountability standards and enables appropriate action. Additionally, the CRFM builds on a gender-sensitive framework and will detect any allegations of GBV, fraud, conflict of interest and diversion of aid. Where possible ACTED will support the referral of GBV cases or reported cases to ad-hoc agencies for handling of such complaints. Overall, the CRFM will ensure a direct, confidential and accessible feedback mechanism for all beneficiaries and wider stakeholders to register feedback whilst underpinning accountability to affected populations. As such, the CRFM emphasizes the inclusive participation of the communities, building trust and enabling them to influence decisions for themselves.
