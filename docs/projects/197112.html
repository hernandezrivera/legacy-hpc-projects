Project Summary

The intervention aims to provide Humanitarian actors in Sudan with logistics services, coordination and information management support to enable the humanitarian logistics response in Sudan. The intervention is oriented to enable the humanitarian actors' movement of staff and cargo to and within Sudan

1 - Needs

The conflict has worsened the already supply chain accessibility issues in Sudan, leading to broken supply routes and price increases in storage and transport services and fuel. Humanitarian agencies halted most of their operations in country and evacuated or relocated most of their staff, affecting also their capacity to resume and to scale up their activities.In addition to the supply chain disruption, damage in key infrastructure, such as roads, airports, etc., is expected as a result of the conflict and due to the ongoing fighting, this damage is still unknown. Although the Government of Sudan issued a press statement on 12 May of 2023 allocating Port Sudan, Dongola and Wadi Sayedna military airports to receive aid, the airspace remains closed and necessary permissions are needed to dispatch humanitarian cargo by air. Some of the possible consequences of the limited options to move humanitarian cargo into the country (currently only Port Sudan airport and port) include competition among assets and/or congestion and bottlenecks at the main entry points. As the situation evolves and if entry points for overland transport are identified and secured, the possible set-up of humanitarian corridors from other countries will require increased coordination to ensure smooth transit of cargo to the delivery locations. Additionally, due to the current access restrictions and a highly volatile scenario, there is an increased need for logistics information to allow for operational and contingency planning, including the status of border crossing points, customs information, and the physical status of logistics infrastructure.On top of the effects the conflict is causing, the rainy season has also started in Sudan. The greatest concentration of rainfall during the rainy season occurs between June and September, which will add additional challenges for road transport, with several roads expected to be blocked during that period.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The World Food Programme, as the Logistics Cluster lead agency, will address the identified needs by providing coordination and information management as well as logistics services to fill the current and future gaps. A coordination cell will be established in Nairobi to coordinate the regional response as, due to the logistics complexity and size of the country, access to affected areas should be considered from different countries (Ethiopia, Chad, Egypt, Libya, South Sudan and Central African Republic) to identify the best supply route according to the access restrictions (borders closed, security, congestion of entry points and customs offices activities). Due to the volatile scenario and the current access restrictions to enter the country carrying humanitarian cargo, logistics services will be provided to humanitarian actors to enable the preposition of humanitarian cargo in surrounding countries (Saudi Arabia and Chad). A logistics hub and staging area will be established in Jeddah (Saudi Arabia) with storage capacity and sea transport services connecting Jeddah with Port Sudan. This hub will facilitate cargo preposition and the smooth flow of cargo in Port Sudan, avoiding congestion. The storage provided will also include temperature-controlled equipment and assets to support the health response and its specific needs. Regarding Chad, a hub will be established in Abeche, close to the Sudanese border, where WFP already has a presence. The storage capacity that will be provided to humanitarian partners will also support the humanitarian response once reentry to Sudan from Chad is possible. Securing the Chad route will be key to enabling access to Darfur areas in Sudan rapidly. Regarding in-country activities, the World Food Programme will support the humanitarian response with logistics services across different areas of the country. The initial logistics hub will be established in Port Sudan, securing storage capacity for the incoming cargo by air and sea as Port Sudan airport and seaport are currently the main entry points. The hub will serve as temporary storage for cargo that is ready to be distributed. The Logistics Cluster through WFP will also facilitate transport services, initially from Port Sudan to accessible areas in the East and Southeast areas of the country. Once new locations become accessible, the Logistics Cluster will also provide transport services when required to fulfil an existent gap. In terms of coordination, WFP will also provide coordination for the organisation of humanitarian convoys coordinating with OCHA Access WG and UN-CMCoord. The UN Humanitarian Airs service will support the organisation of international flights in and out of Sudan and, once conditions are secured, with flights inside Sudan, enabling humanitarian staff to access hard-to-reach areas.
