Project Summary

This project will ensure that the newly displaced and most vulnerable populations affected by conflict or disasters related to natural hazards with specific needs have timely access to Emergency Shelter (ES) and Non-Food Items (NFIs), Communal Shelter, Tents, and Cash or Cash for Rent assistance. This project contributes to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) Strategic Objectives 1 (provide timely multi-cluster lifesaving assistance to crisis affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity). IOM proposes to purchase, preposition, and distribute 140,000 NFI kits to reach 700,000 individuals, and 40,000 ES to reach 200,000 individuals. IOM will also provide 10m x 24m communal shelters for 2,000 households (HH) reaching approximately 10,000 individuals, distribute 20,000 tents reaching 100,000 individuals, and provide cash support or cash for rent to 2,000 HH reaching approximately 10,000 individuals as a preparedness and response measure for addressing the needs of newly displaced and vulnerable people with special needs and from protracted displaced populations in 14 different states. ES/NFI, needs and market and feasibility assessment missions will utilize data from IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), ES/NFI cluster, and the cash working group to select the most vulnerable households to be assisted. IOM will transport the ES/NFIs, communal shelter materials, and tents to the distribution locations and also make arrangements for cash support or cash for rent to be distributed to the most vulnerable populations in selected states. The distributions will be conducted in close coordination with ES/NFI sector lead, cash working group and partners and relevant stakeholders to ensure consistency in provision of assistance and to reduce possible duplication of assistance. As a final stage, IOM will undertake post-distribution monitoring (PDM) missions to monitor usage and relevance of the assistance two to four weeks after the distributions.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023 identified 2.9 million people in Sudan as having emergency shelter (ES) and non-food item (NFI) needs (52% female, 48% male), including 1.5 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), 412,000 returnees, and 671,000 vulnerable residents. However, these figures have increased due to the conflict that broke out on 15 April 2023 between the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and the Sudanese Armed Force (SAF) causing devastating consequences for millions of civilians and exacerbating humanitarian needs in Sudan. IOM DTM reported more than 1.8 million people are newly displaced, this is in addition to the 3.8 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) already displaced primarily concentrated in Darfur, prior to the new conflict. Approximately, 68 per cent of the internally displaced population are from Khartoum state, with some fleeing to neighboring states and others seeking refuge within the state. The highest numbers of internally displaced people are in West Darfur, White Nile and Northern states. More than 700 people have reportedly been killed and 5,500 injured so far, with an expectation that these numbers will increase as the violence continues, with nowhere to get medical support as the health system’s capacity has been weakened severely.The conflict has also involved heavy mass looting, destruction of water, communication, electricity supply, and other community facilities particularly in Khartoum, Darfur states, and other affected states across Sudan. Sources of livelihoods for the majority of the populations have been destroyed, coupled with scarcity and price increase of fuel and other basic commodities such as medicines and further inflation/ devaluation of the Sudanese pound. The insecurity, displacement, and economic conditions have also limited the availability of safe and affordable shelter solutions for the most vulnerable increasing their exposure during the conflict. This has significantly increased the need for urgent humanitarian assistance for affected populations. Given the current context, the lack of critical services and an increase in urgent humanitarian needs, will force populations to continue to flee their homes in search of assistance and are likely to require a greater response. Priority needs as identified at the moment include Shelter, NFIs, and cash assistance as people are seeking for safety in the eastern and northern parts of the country which are currently still accessible. In response, out of 5.7 million people in need the ES/NFI cluster will target 1.9 million people with needs assessed as severe. IOM plans to address the critical humanitarian needs through delivering timely emergency assistance to newly displaced persons including in a protracted displacement situation, refugees, returnees, and host communities. Focus will be on severe affected locations reported from the needs assessments within the country and along border points with Egypt, Chad, South Sudan, the Central African Republic, and Ethiopia where the displaced are facing harsh conditions, enduring extended waiting periods due to border crossing complications, with scant or non-existent infrastructure and services, as host populations were already overstretched due to climate change and food scarcity. The ES/NFIs will include certain items - plastic sheeting, plastic mats, jerry cans, solar lamps, blankets, and cooking sets in the kits provided to help restore a minimal sense of dignity and protection against exposure to the elements, mitigate health risks, and provide some privacy and security for those affected. In addition, IOM will establish communal shelters and distribute tents and provide cash support or cash for rent to the vulnerable affected populations. This will be critical to ensure privacy and safety, mitigate GBV risks, safeguard displaced populations from harsh environmental conditions to preserve security in displacement and their health amid the upcoming rainy season.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1.Purchase, preposition, and distribute 40,000 ES for approximately 200,000 most vulnerable newly displaced persons, protracted IDPs, and returnees based on assessed needs, and PSN criteria utilizing environmentally friendly material and/or locally acceptable shelters in line with the ES/NFI sector standards. 2. Purchase, preposition, and distribute 140,000 NFI kits, in line with ES/NFI sector standards and consisting of (plastic sheets, sleeping mats, jerry cans, blankets, kitchen sets, solar lamp and mosquito nets) to benefit approximately 700,000 crisis and disaster affected IDPs and other vulnerable persons, prioritizing women, children, and the elderly. 3. Establish 2,000 communal shelters (10m x 24m) for newly displaced populations primarily at border points in coordination with the ES/NFI cluster to accommodate approximately 10,000 individuals 4. Distribute 20,000 tents covering approximately 100,000 most vulnerable newly displaced persons primarily at border points within the country and other affected locations 5. I) Provide cash for NFIs/ Shelter materials to affected population to continue to respect their dignity and reduce risk of GBV. IOM will conduct a market assessment on its feasibility through service providers in coordination with the Logistics cluster. II) Provide cash support or cash for rent to 2,000 newly displaced HH covering 10,000 individuals for six months, informed by needs and market and feasibility assessments to be conducted by IOM in coordination with the Logistics cluster and the cash working group. Additionally, IOM aims to include gender and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) considerations related to the participation of men and women in the distribution process. 6. Prepare and share progress reports and conduct PDM missions in locations and border points where ES/NFIs, communal shelters, tents and cash have been distributed (two to four weeks after the distributions). The PDM will include household interviews on the usage of distributed materials, beneficiary satisfaction, impact of the assistance and complaints on the quality and quantity of received materials and overall response.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

IOM will integrate environmental considerations into all programmes, and it will follow applicable international requirements. IOM will ensure that the proposed project will have a positive environmental impact by encouraging the beneficiaries to be involved in hazard mitigation activities such as awareness raising sessions on materials used and safe construction of ES, communal shelter, and tents as well as environmental impacts related to flood and fire hazards in addition to protecting their shelters and best handling of NFIs for longer term use. The training sessions will also include promoting alternatives to firewood, such as fuel-efficient cooking and livelihood options, to preserve the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM will adhere to its gender mainstreaming, PSEA and accountability to affected populations (AAP) guidelines, as they are aligned with the “do no harm” principle and IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing Gender-Based Violence in Crises. The needs of individuals in extremely vulnerable situations will be particularly reflected in the needs assessments, programme design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, the sector will ensure the following elements are considered: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2) equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms, with information collected based on on-site and PDM. This project will prioritize female, child, and elderly headed households to receive ES/NFI assistance, communal shelter, tents and cash assistance. All members of the community including people with additional vulnerabilities as well as women and girls will be separately consulted and encouraged to be fully involved in the consultation, distribution, and post-distribution processes. Beneficiaries will also be involved in assessing their access and safety around accessing services, e.g., identifying the locations of distributions points, and identifying appropriate risk mitigation measures that ensure adherence to “do no harm”. Staff will be trained on protection topics including how to treat GBV disclosures ethically and appropriately, as well as using the referral pathways and provide essential support to community members to access these pathways. IOM will work with staff and suppliers to strengthen the implementation of the PSEA through awareness raising and delivery of PSEA face to face or virtual trainings in line with IOM PSEA guidelines.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

All IOM staff will be carefully trained on codes of conduct and PSEA, prior to any engagement with targeted communities. The PSEA topics will include on the standards of conduct and what is SEA, how to anticipate or identify SEA risks, what action to take in response to risk, how to safely and ethically respond to beneficiaries who discloses a SEA incident and how to report. Trainings will be refreshed on a frequent basis throughout the year. The project will apply principles ensuring accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase - registration of newly displaced persons and other affected populations, immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected populations to the implementation of the interventions. IOM will apply PDM to track the implementation of project activities and conduct interviews with beneficiaries to understand their views on ES/NFIs, communal shelter, tents, and cash support or cash for rent quality, appropriateness, and utilization as well as any complaints including alleged SEA, which will be reported using the appropriate pathways. PDM will also assess the short-term effects of ES/NFIs, communal shelters, tents and cash support or cash for rent in improving living conditions of IDPs/returnees. Information generated will be shared with the ES/NFI working group, and cash working group at the state level while policy or programmatic issues will be taken up in Khartoum ES/NFI Advisory Panel Meetings. A PDM plan will outline data collection methods (household interviews, focus group discussions, and observations by key stakeholders), data sources, time frame, responsibilities, and dissemination procedures. Methodologies used will include a desk review prior to the field exercise being conducted.
