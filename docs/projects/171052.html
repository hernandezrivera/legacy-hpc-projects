Project Summary

The number of people in IPC Phases2, 3 & 4 has increased in the passed few months with predictions that this number will further increase early 2021 (FSNAU, 2020).Although the country has been cushioned by mitigating factors like above average rains in Deyr 2019 and Gu 2020 in most parts of the country and successful mitigation of the desert locusts, several shocked experienced have contributed to this increased numbers. Shocks include flloding, COVID 19 pandemic,conflict and insecurity and increased poverty levels especially among the urban poor. FSNAU and food security cluster predict that effect of these shocks might prevail to early 2021 and might even increase if aggrevating factors occur.
For hard to reach rural populations food security status remains poor due to the residual
impact of shocks and the fact that assistance to mitigate these shocks remain limited which continues to undermine opportunities for improvement in food security status. Further, some residents from rural area who lost their livelihood , opted to migrate ending up in already overcrowded IDP camps in towns, some hosted by relatives who already have limited access to food.
Despite this, the district has access to a great resource; the ocean. This readily provides fish which is a food resource that has not been fully explored due to cultural beliefs and norms. Most of the residents of this areas do not consume fish, neither do they work in the opportune fish industry (OCHA, 2016) which could have provided job which in turn would improve food
access. The proposed plan seeks to assist residents explore the livelihoods in the lucrative
fish industry, and initiate behavior change to fish consumption through activities as
enumerated in the plan. If behavior change is achieved, fish consumption will increase diet diversification and hopefully household food and nutrition security.
Kismayo district has a huge IDP population which will be a key target of proposed interventions.
The response plan seeks also assist the residents of this district access and create livelihood opportunities in the fish industry. This will be done through training of youth and men to help them acquire fishing skills, and provision of fishing equipment. Women will not be left behind but a selected group will be equipped with knowledge and enabling factors in food processing and preservation also in a bid to improve their livelihood. The response plan acknowledges that those disabled in the population are mostly stigmatized and a result do not see themselves as contributors to the workforce and the country’s economy. The plan therefore will seek to include this group by equipping them with skills and enabling factors for them to earn a living and
hopefully improve the food security status of their families. The district have a significant number of disabled, most of who are among the urban poor (REACH , 2018).
Acute malnutrition in the district is at critical levels.The response plan seeks to contribute to reduction of prevalence through addressing the underlying cause of acute malnutrition through improvement of household food security. The proposed interventions will contribute to improvement of food availability and food access by creating livelihood opportunities through expansion of job opportunities.
Government officials will take lead in most key decisions and will be involved through the
cycle for sustainability. The programs will adhere to set government strategies and
policies related to interventions, and will also contribute to achievement of cluster 2021
HRP working closely with government entities and the cluster.

Mitigation Measures

For IDP the main risk is displacement. For this the organisation hasmaintained a database for all beneficiaries with telephone contacts and that ofrelatives. Victims of GBV will also be provided with an organisational GBV focal pointwho will link survivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensure IDP haveaccess to basic services in an equitable and dignified manner with equality alsoobserved in job allocation in projects serving IDP population. Among IDP the risk ofexclusion based on tribe is real. Most IDPs in Kismayo are from a certain communitywho found it hard to access services in the recent past based on their tribe and skincolor. The organisations' policy does not allow exclusion and actually reaches out toall community members to receive services. Recruitment of staff is not based on tribebut professional competence. This has produced staff from a hybrid of cultures whohave learned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved to the targetcommunities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs and women sothat equity is achieved. All gender will be awarded opportunity to benefit in equitably.Women and youth who are mostly left out in recruitment will be considered in allprojects and positions will be awarded equitably. Needs assessment will include allcommunity groups so that an all conclusive, agreeable and fair assessment isreached. Both men and women leaders with an additional youth representative will bepart of community health board as per organisational policy. This will ensuredecisions made are all inclusive. SAF UK policies have sought to put men at the helmof needs affecting their wives, daughters and female relatives. The organisation's AAPframework seeks to ensure men participate through affirmative action for menensuring that men are not excluded from interventions targeting women beneficiaries.Community diagnosis, community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will seek toinclude all community groups to ensure all are reached. Dialogues, evaluations andconversations will include women, children and youth with their own forum so as toensure their views are considered and used to programing.
