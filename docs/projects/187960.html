Project Summary

This project is part of an overall program through which OHCHR will strengthen its support to the humanitarian leadership and partners in implementing a robust response based on expanded factual data and legal analysis for the protection of human rights in Syria. This builds on OHCHR’s innovative model of embedding human rights advisors in the humanitarian operation since 2016 enhanced by monitoring and reporting, legal advice and civil society support.
This project builds on work implemented over the last five years by OHCHR since the deployment of Human Rights Advisors to each HC office in the three hubs. It is grounded in the Office's value-added of impartial reporting on the human rights context, robust and responsive legal analysis, and its position as a trusted interlocutor on sensitive issues with key national and international partners, including Humanitarian Coordinators, UN humanitarian agencies and partners across the hubs. The Office also considers work being done by other actors to ensure that its programming contributes to responding to gaps and avoids duplication.
This project is based on the intersection across all areas of OHCHR's programming, whereby solid monitoring/reporting, legal analysis, support to civil society and technical advice on IHL/IHRL to humanitarian partners mutually reinforce one another. OHCHR shares data reflecting the work in these areas with partners, such as Member States seeking to develop response policies, humanitarian actors focused on addressing urgent concerns, political actors who are working to establish a more stable future for the country, and civil society organisations campaigning on these issues.
The OHCHR Monitoring and Reporting Unit (MRU) monitors, analyses and reports on the human rights situation in the Syrian Arab Republic through the contribution to the Secretary-General's monthly reporting to the Security Council pursuant to Security Council resolutions 2139 (2014), 2165 (2014) and 2191 (2014). MRU continues to be a key source for OHCHR advocacy and data collection. Analysis provided by the Syria Office contributes to numerous speeches and press statements by the High Commissioner, the Deputy High Commissioner and other senior United Nations officials. The information and reports regularly shared by MRU with the Humanitarian leadership and partners support a better protection analysis and informs early warning efforts across the hubs.
This is complemented by the work of the Rule of Law/Transitional Justice Unit. A series of legal notes on topics including Indiscriminate Attacks and Indiscriminate Weapons in International Humanitarian Law, Attacks on Medical Units in International Humanitarian Law and Human Rights Law, Transfer of the Civilian Population in International Law, International Humanitarian Law and Human Rights Law Relevant to Siege Warfare, Child Recruitment & Use in Hostilities in International Law, Belligerent Occupation: Duties and Obligations of Occupying Powers, Position paper on Syrian Law 10/2018 on HLP rights and a Q&A on Suspected ISIL Fighters & their Families Held in Detention Centres & IDP Camps in Northeast Syria were drafted to support the UN analysis and response in Syria. Legal advice and analysis to humanitarian partners is also provided on an ad hoc basis in response to developments on the ground.
Since 2015, the HRAS work to enhance the integration and mainstreaming of human rights into the work of the UN Country Team and in the region. To this end, HRAs have provided inputs to the Humanitarian Needs Overview and the Humanitarian Response Plans produced by OCHA and humanitarian partners since 2016.
OHCHR will use HRP funding to expand its reporting, analysis, capacity-building and advocacy on the rights of groups who are most at risk of being discriminated against, including women, girls, young people, adolescents, and persons with disabilities with a particular focus to inform a better protection analysis and advocacy efforts.

4 - Needs

In Syria, preventing and responding to violations of IHL and IHRL remains one of the most challenging aspects of the humanitarian agenda. The broad scope of human rights expertise within the OHCHR Syria Office creates opportunities for a holistic approach to protection (human rights protection, humanitarian protection and protection of civilians). OHCHR’s proactive role in engaging and working closely with humanitarian actors is clearly recognised in the SSG protection strategy.OHCHR Syria’s HRAs seek to strengthen the protection analysis and protection advocacy efforts of the humanitarian leadership and humanitarian partners as well as their effectiveness and capacity to implement a robust humanitarian response based on IHRL/IHL – which was identified as a gap in the response back in 2015. OHCHR’s work on protection in the Syria response seeks to address two identified 1) supporting a consistent, effective and robust implementation of the SSG protection strategy, through direct engagement with the humanitarian leadership and sectors/clusters at Whole of Syria and hub levels. 2) conducting monitoring/reporting and analysis on core protection/HR issues as a means to provide humanitarians/UN senior leadership/other actors with a solid factual & legal basis on which to inform their advocacy and operational response. Embedded in the humanitarian operations, HRAs identify key areas where humanitarians need deeper analysis & information to support their programming & advocacy.

5 - Implementation of activities

OHCHR will implement this project by: - Strengthening its monitoring, documentation and legal analysis of the human rights situation and patterns of violations committed by various parties on the ground to support UN protection and advocacy activities. - Increasing the public and internal reporting for stakeholders, the international community and civil society reflecting an impartial and substantive analysis of the human rights situation throughout Syria, outlining legal obligations of the multiplicity of actors involved, and promoting measures to improve the protection of human rights across the country.- Expanding the protection advocacy of the humanitarian leadership and partners at the Whole of Syria and hub levels as well as the implementation of a robust humanitarian response based on IHRL/IHL. - Providing technical advice and strategic utilization of the results of its monitoring and analysis – particularly relating to the rights to health, food, water and sanitation, adequate housing, and education. - Based on data from its monitoring and engagement with Syrian CSO partners, support multi-stakeholder interventions on sexual and reproductive health and rights to improve survivor/client-centered processes, heightened awareness of human rights obligations and good practices for enhanced protection, and improved health outcomes. - Disseminating early warning analysis/reporting to alert on risk factors

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The protection of humanitarian partners involved in OHCHR-led IHL/IHRL activities is essential and risk is mitigated through the delivery of neutral and impartial IHL/IHRL training and capacity building activities as well as through dialogue with the host State and parties to the conflict.The main perceived risks related to this project include: (1) Increased exposure by humanitarian partners, in particular Syrian CSOs and individuals involved in IHL/IHRL-related activities; (2) Sources provide inaccurate information or instrumentalize data as situation in Syria evolves. Steps taken to mitigate these risks include: i) Crafting support to the humanitarian leadership/partners in the Syria response carefully to respond to a need identified by the SSG and IASC. The HCs were involved in drafting/approving the ToRs for each HRA and work closely with them. ii) Maintaining strict procedures to ensure the safety of its partners and sources, such as undertaking formal and/or informal risk assessment in all areas of its work. The Office takes serious regard of the confidential/personal information provided by individual victims, witnesses, and partners. It does not publicly use information provided unless informed consent is obtained, and then only if its disclosure poses no security risk. OHCHR's work is grounded in a foundation of 'do-no-harm' and regular assessment of sources/information.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

OHCHR does not anticipate any PSEA-related risk for the implementation of this project, as it involves technical advice at the humanitarian leadership and partners level rather than direct assistance provision to beneficiaries and humanitarian workers. As PSEA is a priority for OHCHR, particular attention will be paid throughout the implementation phase including by regular risk assessments and mitigation to ensure that this remains the case. OHCHR Syria is guided by OHCHR SEA action plan and internal procedure.
