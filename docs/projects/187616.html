Project Summary

The school meals programme aims to restore and stabilize access to education and to enhance health and nutrition status of children in targeted areas particularly areas hosting large number of affected populations, IDPs and returnees with low food security and education indicators. By encouraging regular attendance and enrollment, the programme contributes to providing protection to children from reverting to negative coping mechanism such as child labor and child marriage in addition to the education, health, and nutrition benefits. The programme meets the first objective of the education sector “Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth”
The School meals activity includes:
- In kind food distribution including daily distribution of fresh meals or fortified snacks to children in formal education pre and primary schools during school days and to children enrolled in non-formal education, including children in Self learning centers in North East Syria camps. The fresh meals project will expand to new areas fund permitting
- Provision of electronic vouchers to students who were out of school and have returned to education and are currently enrolled in curriculum B or non-formal education programmes. The provision of the electronic voucher is conditional to student’s attendance rate of 80%.
School meals activities will target boys and girls aged 5-18 years in formal and non-formal settings, including children with disability, who are attending the targeted schools and learning centers. The Programme is implemented in coordination with the Ministry of Education and other partners of the education sector in Syria including UN agencies and National NGOs. Considering the high needs and limited resources, the project will focus on areas with acute and immediate needs of humanitarian assistance. Targeted sub-districts will be prioritized based on food and education severity of 3 and above. In case of lower funding, priority will be given to locations where severity of food security and education of more than 4 and to newly accessible areas where education sector is picking up its activities.
The Activities are aligned with the timeframe of the school year and are expended over two calendar years.

4 - Needs

The education system continues to be impacted by compounded factors including the protracted conflict, COVID 19 and overall economic situation in the country. The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 shows that almost 6.4 million are in need of education humanitarian support, most of them living in areas with severe and catastrophic education conditions. HNO 2022 showed that the percentage of children who have never attended school is about 18% (self-reported) , while this number does not represent the number of out of school children it indicates the size of the problem, especially that more than third of the families are not sending their children to school due to economic situation (because they can't afford expenses or the child is working to support the family). The attendance rate drops between adolescent boys and girls.Data from 2021 HNO showed that about 6.4 million children are affected by the increased levels of food insecurity in the county. Insufficient food at home has negative impact on children health and growth and on their attentiveness and cognitive abilities. The provision of school meals to children in both formal and non-formal education will address the child’s short term hunger and provide the required nutrients and energy which children will need to learn and grow. In addition, the provision of the snack and the voucher will alleviate part of the economic burden on the family, by allowing it to shift its limited resources to address other needs.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The existing WFP Helpline has witnessed a significant expansion in 2021 and further strengthening of the mechanism will be supported in 2022 with the development of new ones, i.e. complaint boxes and partners’ helpdesks. The line is open to all the communities that are reached through the different WFP activities and an informative flyer is contained in all the WPF food baskets that are distributed. A specific mechanism for SEA related cases has also been established so that allegations of SEA are immediately referred to trained specialized staffAll the Field Offices have an active PSEA focal point who has been trained on how to safely identify and report cases. Focal Points also raise awareness among staff about SEA related issuesAll WFP partners have been assessed within the PSEA Interagency Network on capacities around PSEA. The partners with low capacities and without clear policies to prevent and respond to SEA cases, will be trained in 2022 and supported in the development of strategies and policies.
