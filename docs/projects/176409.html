Project Summary

SAADO's proposed project will provide access to holistic emergency GBV prevention and response services for vulnerable flood and conflict affected populations in Duk and Ayod Counties. The proposal will target 10,957 beneficiaries (2259 boys, 2436 girls,2852 men and 3410 women) in Ayod and Duk Counties of Jonglei, South Sudan. SAADO proposes to implement GBV prevention, response and risk mitigation activities by providing quality and timely, age appropriate, case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors. This project is designed to improve responses to cases of GBV and enhance the ability of survivors to recover from GBV incidences. SAADO will provide individual psychosocial support for women and girls in 4 established women and girls' safe spaces (4-WGFS) supporting 800 women and girls. The project's proposed activities will also include case management for survivors, dialogue sessions, psychosocial support through individual counseling sessions targeting 600 survivors, 200 vulnerable women and girls will be engaged in skills building and IGA's activities such as embroided bedsheets, table-cloths and beading, information sharing sessions on pertinent issues and topics identified by the women. The activities in the established WGFS will be conducted using the guidelines from the ministry of health and World Health Organization(WHO) on COVID-19 prevention and will include maintaining social distance, wearing of masks and washing hands with soap. SAADO also intends to strengthen the gender-based violence community-based protection mechanisms (CBPN’s) available to reduce GBV risks on men, women, boys and girls.
Currently, SAADO is running two (2) WGFS in Duk Pajut and Duk Pagak established through SSHF 2019 second allocations and four (4) WGFS established through SSHF 2020 Reserve allocation in Duk Poktap and Padiet. In Ayod County, SAADO is managing two (2) WGFS in Jiech and Mogok payams supported by UNFPA through CERF funds. The proposed project will build upon and complement SAADO's currently ongoing GBV prevention and response project supported by SSHF Reserve Allocation in Duk County and Increasing access to GBV services in Jiech and Mogok payams, Ayod County supported by UNFPA.
SAADO will provide integration with the 3-year Education Cannot Wait project in the targeted location of Duk County which focuses on GBV prevention and response for adolescent girls in 11 schools. SAADO will coordinate this proposed response with the GBV sub cluster to ensure that the proposed activities are not duplicated and there is no overlap of activities and will work with health partners such as John Dau Foundation to refer survivors for Post Rape services.

1 - Needs

According to the humanitarian needs overview January 2021 report, armed conflict, sub-national organized violence, COVID-19 restrictions and flooding have exacerbated the already extreme levels of GBV in Jonglei state. The report reiterates that over 6,000 GBV incidents were recorded between January and September 2020 with 97 per cent of the reported GBV incident survivors being female. To buttress this, the FSMNS+ assessment reports that the risk of GBV is categorized as high as 49 per cent of surveyed households expressed concern on GBV and 34 per cent of households travel more than 30 minutes to fetch water or firewood. Similarly, 49 per cent of affected women and girls in need of services have no access to GBV services and only 12 per cent of the communities in Duk and Ayod have awareness on available GBV services according to the 2020 GBV IMS data analysis. The increased risk of GBV cases in the communities coupled with gaps in response services necessitates an urgent need for strengthening primary GBV prevention, integration of GBV risk mitigation in other sectors, and scaling up GBV response services according to the HNO report January 2021. The report further reiterates that a fifth of the survivors were less than 18 years old with intimate partner violence accounting for more than half of the alleged perpetrators. To compound this dire situation, an estimated 1,066,000 people have been affected by flooding with nearly 90 per cent of those affected in Jonglei state including Duk and Ayod Counties. A combination of factors such as abnormally heavy rainfall from July to October 2020 which led to the overflow of the Nile, Pibor, Sobat, Lol and other rivers, topography with flat land and minimum gradient and the collapse of the main dyke along the White Nile has led to massive flooding in Duk and Ayod Counties displacing thousands according to South Sudan OCHA situational report December 2020. In addition to this complex situation, according to the Inter-Cluster Initial Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) conducted on the 17th October 2020, Jonglei state has been experiencing intensified sub- national violence which has displaced thousands of people and resulted in hundreds of casualties as violent clashes and reprisals have played out between Murle, Dinka Bor and Lou Nuer communities. This desperate situation has increased GBV needs in Duk and Ayod Counties which remain severe as violence and violations persist as a reality of daily life for girls, women and children. The increased presence of mixed populations such as returnees and IDPs has exerted pressure on the few services available in these locations thereby exacerbating protection risks and increasing the need for GBV prevention and response interventions and . The increasing protection needs far outweigh the available and limited resources for GBV programming as the women, girls, the disabled and the elderly reported being beaten, looted, raped, abducted and tortured when moving out to seek safety.

2 - Project activities

Dignity kit programming that involves distribution of dignity kits accompanied by discussions, information-sharing, awareness-raising on GBV and SRH, available services to survivors (disaggregated by age) as part GBV risk mitigation measure and addressing differential needs. Awareness raising activities to reach women, men, girls and boys. Conducting of GBV prevention initiatives including SASA and EMAP targeting women, men, girls and boys. Training on moulding of Fuel-Efficient Stoves (disaggregated by age) as part of GBV risk mitigation measure. Strengthening the capacity of community mobilisers on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning. Training of staff/frontline workers trained on GBV minimum Standards (Case management and PSS). Establishment of functional GBV referral pathways in targeted locations of Duk and Ayod. Ensuring availability of Women and Girls Friendly Space programming (WGFS). Provision of individual specialized PSS and other related services such as recreational activities, livelihood services, parenting skills. Supporting women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns through case management and referral services referral services. Support to COVID-19 prevention activities such as social distancing, provision of face masks, soaps and buckets for hand washing.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure that beneficiaries are provided with information about PSEA through tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; In addition, consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), will be undertaken as well as consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and will include confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) which will be used.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members through having tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions made by the project; In addition, consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard),as well as consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) which will be used.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

A core component of SAADO’s beneficiary accountability and communication in this proposed intervention will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To adapt beneficiaries feedback into the project assistance, SAADO's proposed project will encourage communities to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.
