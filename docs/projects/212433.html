Project Summary

The project aims to enhance the financial stability of the most vulnerable families in northwest Syria by providing life-saving humanitarian assistance to 2156 vulnerable families, totaling 10780 individuals, including 2430 men, 2700 women, 2760 boys, 2890 girls. This will be accomplished through various measures:
- Distribution of cash assistance to 2156 vulnerable families, totaling 10780 individuals, including 2430 men, 2700 women, 2760 boys, 2890 girls) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, Each family will receive monthly e-vouchers with a balance of 100 USD for a continuous period of 8 months. The e-voucher (NFC) modality allows beneficiaries to access cash from multiple distribution points, as NFC will provide cash at the distribution points.
- BINAA will facilitate the process by preparing a vendor map and equipping selected vendors with devices while providing training on their usage. Moreover, orientation sessions on using e-voucher cards will be conducted for the beneficiaries. All project phases will strictly adhere to COVID-19 precautions outlined in BINAA's guidance.
- The project inclusively targets individuals of all ages, including the elderly and people with disabilities.
The output of the project involves supporting 2156 households (10780 individuals) through MPC using the E-voucher modality (NFC) for a duration of 8 months. The activities include community engagement through consultation sessions and FGDs, preparing a cash provider map via a competitive and transparent vendors' selection process, contracting with an E-voucher service provider, identifying targeted beneficiaries, distributing voucher cards (NFC), providing training on their usage, distributing POS devices to selected providers, and supervising the voucher redemption process.
One significant risk is the potential for gender-based violence (GBV) due to overcrowding at distribution points or while returning home after distribution. This may particularly affect women and could lead to domestic violence. The likelihood of such incidents is moderate, but the impact is high. Mitigation strategies include avoiding late distribution times, selecting distribution points close to residential areas and distributing in separate sections for men and women. Additionally, accessible complaint mechanisms for reporting GBV incidents will be established, and focal points will be trained to handle complaints effectively.
