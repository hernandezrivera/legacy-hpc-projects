Project Summary

Overall, there are 6,596,342 persons in need of protection support across the country. This
includes 3,036,593 IDPs, 969397 returnees, 1,098,272 refugees and 1,492,080 vulnerable
Sudanese. New and protracted displacement, lack of basic services and the deteriorating
economic situation, have all had profound consequences on conflict-affected persons’
immediate physical and mental well-being, living standards, and their long-term resilience.
Rule of law, access to justice and government social protection remains weak in conflictaffected areas of Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile including the two areas controlled
by nonstate actors. The natural disasters, including disease outbreak and scarcity of
resources have further impacted public service delivery and heightened the socioeconomic vulnerability of displaced families.
General protection needs are most severe in the hotspot localities, where inter-communal
conflict and factional fighting have been reported throughout 2021 and people remained
deprive of access to government services in the two areas. Such chaotic situation is
exacerbated by the proliferation of firearms, competition over land and water resources,
unregulated migratory routes, and weak systems for land administration. As a result of
reported incidents: hundreds of civilians were killed or wounded; HLP damaged, looted,
destroyed or occupied; thousands of families displaced; and public facilities destroyed
occupied. Furthermore, UNAMID withdrawal at the end of 2020 created a gap in the
civilian protection framework in Darfur. This left humanitarian protection mechanisms
and services, and durable solutions programming by protection and peacebuilding actors,
as the mainstay across Sudan.
Vulnerable groups - such as women, children, elderly, persons with disabilities and chronic
illnesses - face a range of challenges unique to their individual circumstances,
compounded by their displacement where familial/community support networks not
functioning or are overstretched. These and other groups of people suffer
disproportionately from various protection risks, including violence, sexual and other
forms of exploitation and abuse, and human-rights violations. For women and girls, risks
of GBV remain significant, particularly when they are on the move, working in fields,
collecting water or firewood. Boys and men remain especially at risk of physical violence,
injury and death and arbitrary arrests, as they are often suspected of supporting armed
opposition groups. Child marriage and child labor are on the rise. Access to birth

1 - Needs

The overall situation in Sudan has drastically deteriorated since the conflict broke out last15 April 2023. The war that erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and theRapid Support Forces (RSF) has further aggravated the overall humanitarian situation,causing massive (re)new displacements within the country as well as across borders.Prior to the crisis, Sudan was indeed already home to 3.1 million IDPs. The InternationalOrganization for Migration (IOM) reports that over 843,000 persons have been newlydisplaced internally, and almost 250,000 persons (of various status) have crossed intoneighboring countries. Egypt is hosting the largest number of new arrivals, includingrefugees. The security situation inside the country as well as along certain borders isinsecure and volatile, particularly in Khartoum, Darfur States, North Kordofan State, whereclashes and airstrikes have occurred unabated since 15 April 2023. Several attempts forceasefires have failed. Civilians have consequently been exposed to the impacts of activefighting including gunfire, shelling, airstrikes, and unexploded ordnance. Urban warfare inthe capital as well as in other main cities has led to killings of civilians and widespreaddestruction of civilian infrastructures, including markets and hospitals, as well as lootingand occupation of homes, in gross violation of international humanitarian and humanrights laws. For West Darfur State, the current conflict has taken a tribal dimension andled to further intercommunal division, aggravating drastically the humanitarian situation.Essential services have been severely impacted by the ongoing war, affecting civilians’ability to access emergency medical services, water, electricity and connectivity. Prices offood, fuel and other basic goods have dramatically increased, rendering access toessential goods unaffordable for many people. Access to cash is heavily compromised asthe banks have generally remained closed or been looted/destroyed since the beginningof the conflict. Acute shortage of food, water, medical services, cash and fuel alsoheightens the risks of exploitation and abuse. Despite the above-mentioned severe socioeconomic situation, many humanitarian programs have been suspended due to insecurityand lack of access. Due to the conflict, access to services for GBV survivors has beenseverely impacted. Where conflict is active, there are high risks that persons of concernwill not have any safe access to services. Clinical Management of Rape has stopped inconflict areas, and the supply of PEP Kits is limited and getting depleted whereveravailable. The rising insecurity as a result of the ongoing crisis in Sudan has alsonegatively impacted children’s access to basic needs and essential services. Familyseparation and loss of contact have been reported in various communities of concern dueto conflict and network disruptions. Access to education has been disrupted in areaswhere conflict is ongoing, while space in schools and fees for exam placement havehindered refugee children’s access to school even in safer locations. Concerns remain onthe impact of conflict on the risk of child recruitment and engagement in the violence. Theescalation of conflict has forced people to flee their homes seeking safety, protection andassistance in other parts of Sudan or in neighboring countries. The provision of servicesin the states of White Nile, Sannar, and Gezira which have witnessed recent influx of IDPsand refugees poses particular challenges as there were very few humanitarian actorsactive there prior to the conflict.

2 - Activities/Outputs

In line with HRP objectives, UNHCR will address the immediate protection needs of highly vulnerable people among the IDPs and returnees by identifying, assisting and referring them to service providers including for the provision of legal assistance for access to justice, civil documentation, cash-based intervention, follow-up with relevant authorities and communities for implementation of community support projects. UNHCR will incorporate community-based and area-based approaches to its response. UNHCR will also engage in protection monitoring, which will allow the organization to monitor the protection environment to inform advocacy and evidence-based response. The main protection objective will be to strengthen and maintain the centrality of protection with a particular focus on the most vulnerable categories -- namely children, women, families with three and more children, the elderly, chronically and mentally ill and persons with disabilities. This will entail increasing social cohesion, strengthening community self-management and capacity, empowerment, and strengthening community-based protection mechanisms. UNHCR will strengthen national and local capacities investing more in communities through Community-Based Protection Networks as part of the focus in UNHCR’s key global strategic direction. UNHCR through its partners will support the strengthening of existing community-based structures and systems. Community groups comprising of IDPs and host communities will be mobilized and empowered with resources and skills to improve livelihoods and be more self-reliant. Community-based responses to promote peace and social cohesion, while considering those with specific needs, will be implemented. UNHCR will also contribute to ensure participation and information sharing with displaced and returnee communities in the planning and implementation of durable solutions, including through continued IDP and assessments, while encouraging the participation of IDPs and IDP returnees throughout the UNHCR programme cycle. Strategically, and in reflection of the growing need to monetize humanitarian delivery services, UNHCR in Darfur will seek to scale up its financial and human resources capacity to institutionalize CBI as a service delivery modality. Moreover, as the protection lead agency and considering limited protection services, UNHCR will identify other avenues of Protection services that could be achieved through unconditional cash grants. The intent is to positively impact the self-reliance and resilience of the affected communities. Lastly, if the doors remain open for dialogue with the government on law and policy, UNHCR will also advocate with GoS on ensuring that laws and policies conform with international standards such as the 2009 Kampala Convention. UNHCR will also support implementation of GoS’ National Plan on Protection of Civilians, at national and state-levels

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNHCR will integrate environmental considerations ensuring that the proposed projectwill have a positive and sustainable environmental impact by encouraging partners towork with beneficiaries on disaster risk activities such as awareness sessions, genderbalance, environmental impacts, and safe construction of shelter and disposal of NFIs.The training sessions will also promote alternatives to firewood, such as fuel-efficientcooking and livelihood options to preserve environment. Furthermore, UNHCR willpromote shelter solutions considering the geographical context, climate, culturalpractice and habits of the communities serve, local availability of skills and access toadequate and locally available construction materials that ensure minimum impact inthe local environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UNHCR aims to strengthen protection, gender mainstreaming and accountability toaffected populations as they link with the principle of “do no harm”. The particularneeds of such individuals will be reflected in NFI assessments, programme design,implementation and monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, the sector will ensurethe following elements are taken into account: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2)equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms,with information collected based on on-site and post-distribution monitoring that willfeed into the sector’s program cycle; and (4) sharing information, consultation,participation and community empowerment. The sector will work with partners tostrengthen the implementation of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abusethrough awareness-raising for both partners and suppliers. UNHCR within its capacityas Shelter/NFI Sector lead will advocate to draw attention to shelter issues such ashousing, land and property rights, particularly for returnees and/or integrated IDPs.HLP due diligence tools will be set in place with cooperation of the HLP WG toharmonize the shelter responses among partners ensuring that before building durableshelter partners will ensure in cooperation with the communities that beneficiaries’land if free of claim and refer those conflictive cases to protection partners providingICLA support through a referral pathway. This is to reduce the risk of further harm andmainstream protection on the shelter construction programs.
