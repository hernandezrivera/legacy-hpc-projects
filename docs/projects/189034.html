Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to enhance the protection of civilians and resilience of communities in South Sudan. Civilians throughout the country have been severely affected by the existing humanitarian crisis resulting from recurrent violence, insecurity, and natural disasters. To alleviate suffering and increase the protection and security of affected and vulnerable communities, NP will directly implement activities utilizing a participatory model that seeks to further build on local protection capacities, social cohesion, and resilience as well as prevent and respond to pertinent protection concerns. All project activities will be designed to best respond to the immediate security needs of beneficiaries and support the resilience, adaptive capacity, and recovery of communities from violent conflict, insecurity, and environment shocks utilizing NP’s Unarmed Civilian Protection (UCP) methodology. The project’s three programming components which will center upon: 1) General Protection 2) GBV and 3) Child protection.
Nonviolent Peaceforce (NP) will utilize a participatory approach in order to prevent and respond to protection needs, including gender-based violence (GBV) and Child Protection (CP) concerns. All project activities will be designed based on NP’s methodology of Unarmed Civilian Protection (UCP) to respond to the immediate protection needs of affected populations as well as support the resilience, adaptive capacity and recovery of communities from violent conflict and insecurity.
Project activities are implemented in both static locations, as well as through mobile response missions. NP also recognises the specific and unique needs of women, men, boys, girls, elderly and Persons with Disability (PwD). As such, NP has made use of the community feedback/information in the design of the proposed project. During project implementation, concerns of all community members will be captured and disaggregated by age, sex and PwD, and will be constantly reviewed to adapt the project.

1 - Needs

Despite the implementation of the R-ARCSS in 2018 and the formation of the TGoNU in 2020, intra/inter-communal violence and clashes between armed forces continue to pose a serious threat to the protection of civilians in South Sudan. Furthermore, deadly violence after the SPLM-IO in 2021, clashes in Tambura between armed forces, and incidents of ethnic violence indicate a lack of national unity and the fragility of the peace agreement. In February 2021, the UN acknowledged a “massive escalation” in localized violence, with the scope of the violence “far exceeding” that between 2013 and 2019. Further, community-level violence, including cyclical revenge killings, ethnic conflict, cattle-related violence, and resources conflict remain major concerns. In 2021, South Sudan was struck by the worst flooding in 60 years, displacing over 800,000 individuals, exacerbating the levels of food insecurity, lack of access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation and causing disease outbreaks in affected areas. Against this background, the 2020 IPC report stated that South Sudan now faces the highest levels of food insecurity since 2011, with 7.2 million people estimated to be facing acute food insecurity. Protection needs are high, and according to HNO 2022 5.6 million people require protection services, particularly conflict and flood-affected populations, children, women, women- and child-headed households, elderly, and people with disabilities. Further, 2.6 million people needs GBV support and prevention and 3.5 million people, “including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation and need critical child protection services”. Closure of schools and economic decline related to the COVID-19 pandemic and extensive flooding have contributed to increased intimate partner violence, family separations, violence against children, physical/emotional maltreatment, and exploitation. Further, in areas where flood-displaced populations are not able to return to their areas of origin due to ongoing conflict in their communities, civilians face exacerbated vulnerability to protection concerns. In addition, perceived disenfranchisement of youth in humanitarian assistance compounded by high levels of unemployment and lack of livelihood opportunities, has spurred violent strikes/protests by youth against humanitarians, hindering access and indicating the risk for further violence perpetrated by youth in the absence of holistic and inclusive approaches to safety, security, peace, and development. Gender inequalities in South Sudan contributes to the lack of participation of women and girls and exacerbates vulnerabilities. At the household and community levels, inequalities have a mutually reinforcing relationship to levels of structural violence, preventing females from accessing opportunities and exposing them to violence. Inequalities also assist in the normalization of violence against women/girls. Further, gender inequalities contribute to women/girls being used as commodities, with CEFM being used a coping mechanism to multidimensional poverty (Support Peace Initiative Development Organization reported that between April and December 2020, approximately 1,500 girls were married off or impregnated in the Equatorias alone). GBV is also perpetrated against males with militarization of masculine behaviors and rigid norms on men’s roles reinforcing hegemonic masculinities that contribute to normalization and cycles of violence. In the context of an increment in localized violence in the country, women and girls continued to be deliberately targeted. Gender inequalities sit within the wider divisive social structure exacerbated by political and ethnic dimensions of conflict, leading to targeted SGBV according to, for example, perceived affiliation across divergent lines Finally, stigmatization and lack of staff trained in survivor-centered approaches hinders safe access to response services.

Under CO3 (General Protection), Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas: 1) Facilitate peacebuilding awareness raising activities targeting people of concern and 2) Conduct trainings on conflict resolution, mitigation mediation and peacebuilding. Under CO 4 (General Protection), Implement protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response: 1) Produce and disseminate protection monitoring reports to the protection cluster. Under CO5 (General Protection), Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience: 1) Facilitate general protection awareness raising activities to women, men, boys, and girls, with a focus on DRR, EWER, and community resilience. Under CO6 (General Protection), Enhance protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response: 1) Conduct protection monitoring activities in hotspot and at-risk areas to identify and respond to pertinent protection risks facing women, men, girls and boys. Under CO7 (Child Protection), Vulnerable and at-risk girls, boys, men and women including children with disabilities have improved access to quality lifesaving child protection: 1) Provide PSS first aid, recreational, developmental, and education support through child safe spaces to girls and boys, with a specific focus on the inclusion of children with disabilities. Under CO8 (Child Protection), Reduce the suffering of boys, girls, women and men including children with disabilities who are at risk or experience violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect including GBV through provision of specialized/lifesaving child protection and multi sectoral services: 1) Facilitate awareness raising on child protection with a focus on reducing the suffering of children at risk or experiencing violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect and 2) Provide child protection in emergencies trainings to women and men. Under CO9 (GBV), Reduce the suffering of GBV survivors including women, men, girls, boys and ability-challenged persons through the provision of multisectoral specialized GBV response services: 1) Provide trainings on GBV guiding principles and response services to NGO staff and frontline workers to enable to effective provision of immediate response support to survivors through a survivor-centered approach and 2) Refer women, men, girls, and boys to GBV response services. For protection and child protection activities, NP calculated its target number of beneficiaries with the assumption that average budget per beneficiary is 30 USD. This results in 30,000 people targeted under GP, and 23,355 targeted under CP. Guidance and operation support from Juba office will also ensure timely and effective implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

NP shall design, monitor, and evaluate the goals and objectives of programs with the involvement and feedback of affected populations. This information will feed back into planning process and implementation for the project period, thus ensuring that suggested activities of the projects will be evidence-based and community-driven. Principles of Accountability to Affected Populations shall be integrated into relevant organizational processes and documentation, such as: assessments, country strategies, program and project proposals, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings and performance management, partnership agreements, and reporting in a manner that is participatory to the community members. For project design and implementation, beneficiaries’ feedback is constantly collected through engagement with project participants via different channels such as trainings, workshops, community protection/security meetings, patrols, key informant interviews and meetings. When NP engages in peacebuilding and social cohesion activities – such as the implementation of shuttle diplomacy – NP also consults local actors beforehand to solicit their views on how best conduct such activities. Feedback is also gathered as part of any monitoring and evaluation exercises, which in turn provide important information for the design of future projects.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NP will ensure most vulnerable members of the community have adequate access to feedback/complaint mechanisms. In the case of formal feedback and complaint mechanisms, NP will make the effort of including most vulnerable community as part of the aforementioned community feedback/complaint committees. Various communication channels will also be established (NP reception desks, suggestion boxes, telephone hotline etc.) depending on the need and appropriateness of the context. Similarly, and in more informal settings, NP will ensure adequate representation in more informal settings, either by ensuring participation of most vulnerable people in community protection/security meetings, training sessions, focus group discussion during assessment. Recognizing the issue of underreporting due to social stigma, NP will ensure the use of these feedback/complaint mechanisms by sensitizing communities on protection concerns, women and children’s rights; informing them of the ways to access these feedback/complaints mechanism and procedures. In line with NP’s emphasis on community empowerment, the organization will also work with the community in developing and adopting action plans for addressing the protection concerns. When receiving community feedback and/or complaints, NP ensures the confidentiality and safety the persons reporting. This is essential for preventing retaliation and encouraging community members to continuously submit their inputs. As such, NP will protect the identity of the individual/groups who are involved in an complaint or feedback mechanism process unless there is express consent from the reporter to waive the confidentiality procedure and when it is in the critical interest for protection of human lives and dignity (e.g. when there is a risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, or when human lives are endangered).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

NP staff have the responsibility of raising awareness of the community members on sexual exploitation and abuse, so that they know that no NP Staff or any other humanitarian staff can sexually or otherwise exploit project participants. All project participants and affected persons will be encouraged to use complaints mechanisms and a trustworthy focal point (from NP or any other community structure) in case an incident occurs. Additionally, NP is committed to protecting whistleblowers. Such information will be provided as part introductory sessions and will be incorporated as standard topic for each activity. In accordance with the IASC's minimum operating standards, NP continually implement its policy on sexual exploitation and abuse through eight (8) areas: (1) Disseminate its policy on PSEA and implement related action; (2) Engage in cooperative arrangements that requires NP to expand its PSEA accountability obligations to its partners and contractors; (3) Have dedicated focal persons who are committed to PSEA at head office and field offices; (4) Put in place an effective and comprehensive communication channel from headquarters to the field offices for purposes of raising awareness on PSEA in all NP Target Communities; (5) Establish effective community-based complaints mechanisms jointly with other humanitarian actors that includes survivor's assistance (where possible); (6) Mainstream PSEA into NP’s programs, human resource, and operational management in a manner that responds adequately to PSEA requirements; (7) Have an effective and comprehensive mechanisms that ensure awareness-raising on PSEA amongst NP Staff and partners; (8) Have internal and functional complaints and investigation procedures in place for accountability purposes within NP.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Risk 8.1 Medium Likelihood, High Impact Before the registration/distribution event, NP will work closely with the concerned humanitarian partner to identify the appropriateness of the distribution site including the distance between the location of targeted population and planned distribution site, and space of distribution site. In addition, NP will help identify potential protection risk during this distribution exercise and will respond accordingly, for example through protective presence and/or patrolling that would inter alia help facilitate safe access to distribution/registration sites. Technical assistance – for example in the form of training and partner agreements – will also be provided. Risk 8.4 Medium Likelihood, High Impact In its project locations, NP established that survivors – especially those living in remote communities – and challenges in accessing protection/referral services due to insufficient coverage of the said services, social stigma, and/or not being able to afford transportation cost. Similarly, survivors have little to no awareness of the aforementioned services due to insufficient information on available services. To counter this, NP will engage with available service providers and communicate their functionalities to concerning communities. This will be done through direct awareness raising and empowering multiple entry points – community chiefs, local protection structures such as WPTs and YPTs – to equip them for further awareness raising, post-incident support and referral. Furthermore, NP will disseminate information in at least one other local language to reach the majority of the population. It will also engage influential figures, men and boys to secure their support in addressing the socio-economic barriers for survivors to access post-incident care services. Furthermore, NP will work with referral pathway and protection actors to review and upgrade the accessibility and quality relevant services. Finally, when there is a protection case where survivors either face direct threats or require support to access post-incident services, NP will facilitate access and deter threats through protective accompaniment and referral. Risk 8.5 Medium Likelihood, High Impact As part of its community engagement effort, NP will make a conscious effort of including marginalized groups, PwD and Older persons, women, boys and girls into its consultations, decision-making processes and/or supported community protection structures. Whenever engaging with a community, NP will identify most at-risk persons such as PwD, Older persons, single mothers, widows etc., and by passing this information to humanitarian partners NP could ensure access to humanitarian assistance. Besides, NP will required provide support and/or protective accompaniment to certain humanitarian assistance, for example distributions. Specific and appropriate measures – such as the use of visual/hearing aid – will be used to ensure feedback is collected from at-risks persons. Once feedback is collected, NP will disaggregate data by age, sex, and PwD, thus ensuring age, gender and disability specific concerns are captured and can be used in project cycle. Risk 8.9 Medium Likelihood, Medium Impact To mitigate the risk of land dispute and tenure insecurity, NP will facilitate dialogues between host and returnee communities. At project start, NP will engage host community to set a peaceful ground for returnees by conducting sessions on peaceful co-existence and social cohesion. In cases of actual disputes, meetings will be facilitated as soon as tension arise or violence erupts. Regular communication channels, engagement of local authorities and community protection/peace structures, such as WPT and YPT, as well as training on violence prevention and social cohesion will act as additional measures for preventing tensions and mitigating conflict.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Project activities will contribute towards the related indicators: # of women, men, girls and boys reached through awareness-raising activities (GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting); # of community mobilisers trained/strengthened on GBV risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning; # of staff/frontline workers trained on GBV guiding principles and response services(Case mgt, PSS etc); and # of women, men, girls, and boys referred for GBV response services. Given that NP has been providing GBV prevention and response programming in South Sudan since 2011, in 2022, NP will maintain a focus on GBV prevention and response activities while increasing its emphasis on engaging men and boys as well as strengthening and expanding inclusive community mechanisms that are able to independently support prevention, response, advocacy, and behavioral change. GBV response activities seek to reduce the harmful impact of GBV on individuals and their communities by addressing barriers to survivors accessing post-incident support services through a coordinated response with partner agencies. Dissemination of information will contribute to community knowledge on the availability of different post-incident support services and decrease survivor stigmatization. In addition, issues related to security, the confidence of survivors, economic costs and transportation issues will be overcome through confidential protective accompaniment and referral activities. Further, awareness raising activities and trainings inform community members about GBV, promote positive social change to reduce stigmatization of survivors, and continue to transform harmful norms and hegemonic masculinities that perpetuate GBV in South Sudan. Building upon these foundational activities, NP then facilitates the development and implementation of community-based action plans as well as supports independent GBV prevention and response interventions by community-based mechanisms. By doing so, NP hopes to enable more sustainable and community-driven approaches to GBV prevention and response. NP activities aimed at women, men, girls and boys highlight key issues such as women’s rights, positive masculinities, transformation of gender roles, and change in health and support services seeking attitudes. Mixed gender activities seek to provoke discussion surrounding the stigmatization of GBV survivors and its harmful effects to promote change. Through regular workshops, meetings and trainings with communities, the project will seek to promote non-violent means of communication and dispute resolution and catalyze positive behavioral change. Building the capacity of community-based protection mechanisms such as WPTs is one of the primary vehicles for NP’s work in GBV prevention and response and a major pillar of sustainability and transformative change.
