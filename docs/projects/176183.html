Project Summary

In Jonglei and Unity state, the risk of GBV is continuously exacerbated by a myriad of factors including prevailing cultural norms, traditional gender roles, regional conflict, a highly militarized environment, and the frequent cattle raiding and revenge killings among the different clans.
Protection concerns such as generalized violence and insecurity, limited access to food, water and firewood, expose women to a high risk of GBV, specially in county with high severity needs such as Pibor, Akobo, Nyriol, Canal/Pigi and Ayod. There is, therefore, an urgent need to enhance GBV prevention efforts with additional strategies to mitigate risks, such as fuel strategies and protective accompaniments. Furthermore, lack of recognition of GBV as violation of fundamental human rights continues a culture of impunity throughout the region. Therefore, notwithstanding the advancing of the peace process, it is important to continue to raise awareness, reduce prevalent stigmas in the communities and increase identification and documentation of GBV cases as well as providing survivors access to psychosocial support and other services for their recovery and healing.
Through this project INTERSOS aims to strengthen the prevention and response to GBV risks in some of the counties with the GBV severity index of 5 (Akobo and Pibor), 4 (Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol) and 3 (Mayendit). The intervention will seek to adress the immediate needs of GBV survivors through the provision of safe spaces, Case management services, PSS and the enhancement of access to services and opportunities, empowering women across locations through skill acquisition trainings. To promote durable solutions a strong component of GBV prevention has also been foreseen in the form of mass awareness through a community approach and the engagement of men on accountable practice training.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan Gender-based violence is pervasive, having been nurtured in structural gender inequality and unequal gender power relationships. At least 9,500 incidents of GBV were reported in 2020. Physical assault (37%) remains to be the most reported type of GBV incident followed by sexual violence 24% ( rape - (19%) and sexual assault -(5%) and emotional violence ( 23%)Jonglei state in general has become one of the areas more severely affected by generalized GBV. A Protection monitoring report carried by INTERSOS throughout 2020 in Lankien, Akobo and Ayod revealed that physical violence is the most common form of GBV, followed by sexual abuse as well as denial or resources. This normalization of GBV has been exacerbated by a phenomenon of mass displacement where at least 37% of IDP have been displaced by conflict alone followed by the direct threat at 11% and another 11 % by natural disasters such as floods. In Akobo, inter-community violence has increased throughout 2020, claiming the lives of at least 25 people in revenge feuds, and water points becoming dangerous places for women where they face constant conflict and abuse from rival clans. The danger of fetching firewood as a means of livelihood has increased as constant ambushes from Murle community persist and sexual abuses in the outskirts of villages spread. Additionally, the protective environment of women has been reduced by the closure of Women and girls’ spaces due to COVID19 restrictions, forcing women to spend more time at home where an increase in domestic violence cases has been visible. In Pibor and Akobo, besides the inter-community violence the situation is also exacerbated by the severity of food insecurity in the area, showing a significant increase in negative coping strategies including early marriage and some cases of survival sex since particularly in women headed households. The GBV protection partners lack sufficient funding to respond sufficiently to the S/GBV needs that are worsened by the lack of basic items and amenities. Boma on the other side currently suffers the same fate and has remained without GBV focused protection actors allowing for lack of awareness and response to issues of S/GBV.In Ayod and Mayendit the increased need of GBV services are explained by the flooding .The locations most affected include Ayod, Pajiek, Kuachdeng, Padek, Wau, Mogok, Gorwai and Pagil, some of them still underwater in January 2021. As a result of perennial floods, the risk associated with movement and travelling outside of the residential areas for firewood collection, fishing, and hunting has heightened as they are exposed to snake bites and Murle guerilla attacks. Collapsed or flooded latrines at the community and institutions expose communities to increased sexual harassment. In Mayendit, adolescent girls reported the risk and incidences of sexual abuse in cattle camps, in the community, (traditional dances) ,being overworked at home, rape on roads and bushes, physical assault (at the market) as well as early and forced marriage. The lack of functional health facilities equipped with PFA and CMR professionals only serve to exacerbate the situation. In Lankien and Canal/Pigi, the situation is no different. The flood water is reported to hamper access to vital services such as transportation of people for medical treatment causing the risk of under reported cases of sexual abuse and in attendance of pre-natal sessions. Incidents of inter-communal conflict and fighting between clans have led to an influx of IDPs in the area (e.g. Mayom and Pieri). Cases of abduction of women; including pregnant women and children have been reported. In Pigi, All respondents to the needs assessment performed in January 2021, recognized that it was culturally appropriated to use of violence at domestic level with frequent beating. S/GBV is therefore prevalent with little consequence from local administration, causing the need for the intervention.

2 - Project activities

1. Provision of GBV case management INTERSOS will maintain confidential Listening Centers operated by qualified and trained Case Workers for survivors and women at risk of GBV. INTERSOS regularly update referral Pathways through services mapping. The referral pathway will be strengthened in two ways. One will be community-based mechanisms where CBPN have the roles of identifying and referring cases to local authority or caseworkers for assistance or further action. The second pathway is inter-sectoral where INTERSOS will ensure all protection cases can access all relevant services aiming at reducing their vulnerability and including health, FSL, among others. 2. Provision of cash for protection Individuals identified through case management facing protection risks will be provided with cash support according to predefined criteria. This support will aim at mitigating a protection risk or support victims to access services. This could be for example transport support for accessing referral centers, medical costs, or ensuring access menstrual hygiene products or female headed household in need of support to send children to school. 3. Provision of survivor centered PSS The project will support women and girls attending WGFS through focused and unfocused PSS. Peer to peer sessions will be conducted among women groups will be established where they can share their experiences, ideas and solutions to personal challenges. Participation in dramas, songs and dances that support and advocate for women rights, S/GBV prevention and response will also be promoted. INTERSOS will refurbish structures and strengthen activities in the existing WGFS across all locations. Activities including, counselling rooms, safe rooms, peer groups sessions, drama and dancing sessions among others will be conducted there-in. Women and girls will be able to gather at the WGFSs run by INTERSOS and participate in peer-to-peer social cohesion activities that connect them to address GBV issues and related harmful traditional practices. INTERSOS will further support the women attending the WGFS to organize themselves as a Women’s Association and mobilize community members in all locations to recognize them as such. This will give them a stronger voice in their community and representation in the community leadership.4. Provision of vocational training and livelihood support INTERSOS will seek to promote durable livelihood solutions to GBV survivors by proposing vocational training. Through a participatory approach INTERSOS will identify activities of their interest and skills at the beginning of the project. A selected group of women with entrepreneurial interest will furthermore participate in regular basic business skills training and be supported and assisted in making a business plan and setting up their business to run income generating activities. 5. Awareness raising INTERSOS will conduct community mobilization activities to raise awareness on various GBV topics and mobilize community members to join hands in the efforts to prevent and eventually eliminate GBV in their community. INTERSOS will include peace and conflict mitigation workshops for the POC with a focus on men and youth. 6. Engage men and boys in accountable practices (EMAP) INTERSOS will engage men and boys as change agents for shifting gender norms through regular workshops using the EMAP curriculum with the aim of achieving social cohesion and peaceful coexistence between men and women from IDP and host communities. The focus will be on key community actors in each area. 7. Provide capacity building trainings on various topics of GBV to frontline service providers. INTERSOS will provide training for S/GBV frontline actors such as project staff, community leaders and local authorities in Prevention and Response of for S/GBV including MH/PSS, Conflict mitigation, Communication with survivors, referral pathways and effective mobilization skills.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

INTERSOS is committed to prevent acts of sexual exploitation and abuse by INTERSOS employees and related personal. INTERSOS PSEA policy commits INTERSOS to achieve full implementation of the six core principles related to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse adopted in 2002. All INTERSOS staff and volunteers sign a Prevention of SEA Code of Conduct and undergo a PSEA training as part of the onboarding process. In addition, recurrent trainings are also conducted to refresh staff on their obligations to prevent SEA by timely reporting cases. INTERSOS will also ensure a confidential reporting and mechanisms of any cases regarding SEA. For complaints related to PSEA and CP policy issues, two members of each CRM committee (one men and one women) will be selected by the community as PSEA / CP focal points. They will receive specific sensitivity training and direct communication line (phone and airtime) with the PSEA focal point in mission in Juba. The focal point will then follow INTERSOS PSEA protocols and liaise directly with the internal audit

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will use several channels to ensure proper communication with beneficiaries. First, the project will include a strong awareness and mobilization component where community mobilizers will be in charge of reaching all the community using door to door methodologies to comply with COVID19 regulations and FGD in small groups to present the project, the selection criteria and the activities in general. Additionally, the project will work closely with local authorities to ensure they also promote the project within the community. To ensure beneficiary perception of the project is gathered on a regular basis, satisfaction quality surveys will be done on a quarterly basis and at the closure of every case under case management. The project will also include a final satisfaction survey at the end of the project, aimed at determining the impact of the project in the overall protection situation of beneficiaries. INTERSOS will ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place throughout the implementation period, aiming at safeguarding proper accountability and program quality, For complaint and feedback on the implementation of activities (including for example delays of implementation, complaints about selection of beneficiaries, etc.), a CRM committee in each location will be established by ensuring all groups of the society - and particularly women, children and people with disabilities - have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained about standard procedure for treating complaints and feedbacks. Additionally, complaint box will be established at every distribution point and training venue, and the CRM will also be in charge of collecting feedback provided by this means.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The projects will be designed in accordance and in response to needs assessments and will rely on the feedback from beneficiaries to ensure appropriate material for project activities, adaptation of activities to the needs and wants of the community and to recommendations collected from FGDs , change is context such as influx of IDPs etc.
