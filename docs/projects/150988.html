Project Summary

The most prevalent causes of morbidity and mortality have been ranked to the maternal and neonatal ailments, with mental health having been looked down up on for decades. Juba Foundation with the implementing partner WRRS proposes to implement integrated project through multispectral approach in an effort to reduce morbidity and mortality rate of Host community and Internally Displaced People (IDP) in Sanguni, Barka, Jiihey and Werkooy areas within Jamame District. The project actions focus on children under 5, pregnant and lactating women conflict and flood affected children through the provision of life saving emergency health, and nutrition services by establishing supported by a network of Community Volunteers (CVs). The Jamame has been inaccessible for a very long time as result of protracted conflict and recurrent drought. Jamame is in severe need of emergency assistance and have been identified as extremely vulnerable by all indicators. Through this project, a total of 25,000 vulnerable and poor host communities will have access to a package of Emergency Primary Health Care (EPHC), provision of nutritional support, school feeding for drought affected children in the IDP schools, nutrition and WASH services. The Integrated Health, Nutrition Package includes vitamin A supplementation, screening for malnutrition for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) cases, health education, provision safe water, sanitation and good hygiene practices, distribution of hygiene kits. JF (Juba Foundation) will integrate components of health education when mapping and screening of MAM, SAM case and refer them to treatment centers.
Hygiene messages and public health awareness will be passed to curb cases of Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD)/Cholera and SAM cases and would also be prepositioned at health and nutrition centers for distribution to beneficiaries.
While the proposal is a multi-sector programme (Health, and Nutrition), the project ensures attention for protection and gender mainstreaming, aimed at ensure sufficient awareness among health staff on protection and gender/SGBV related risks and referral pathways available.
JF will implement both the interventions in partnership with WRRS. JF have access to the region and will be working with WRRS since they have more capacity to build on the JF.

Needs

Humanitarian situation in Somalia remains fragile. Recently experienced floods still remains a challenge in that the residence are yet to recover from the effects of floods that was experienced in the country. Jamame which has been hard to reach area has encountered a series of health issues. Exclusion and discrimination of socially marginalized groups are contributing to high levels of acute humanitarian need and lack of protection among some of the most vulnerable. At the same time, disease outbreaks such as cholera, other waterborne diseases and measles continue to lead to preventable deaths across the country. FSNAU post Gu report has majorly been contributed by displacement due to clashes between local community and Al Shabaab and also the floods. Latest food security outlook in these areas indicate that both locally produced and imported commodities will be less available and prices are likely to remain higher as long as the flood effects and inaccessibility impact are felt.despite this improvement, nearly 954,007 children are expected to be acutely malnourished, including 173,594 who are expected to be severely malnourished in the coming months. The surveys indicate Critical levels of GAM (15-29.9%) in 12 out of 33 populations surveyed (7 IDP and 5 Rural) while Critical levels of SAM prevalence (≥4-5.6) were only seen among IDPs Guban pastoral. Similarly, the Crude Death Rate (CDR) rates are Critical (1 to<2) in 3/33 population groups surveyed while CDR rates are Serious (0.5 to <1) in 11/32 and U5DR rates (1 to 1.9) in 6/32 population groups surveyed, respectively. Furthermore, Morbidity rates are high (>20 %) in many parts of the country but highest among populationOverall, the median GAM is 14%an improvement from Critical in 2017 Gu (median GAM of 17.4 per cent). Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project will work in a close coordination with donors, clusters and other agencies implementing other projects and communities in Lower Juba. Furthermore, the project will coordinate with health and nutrition cluster at the field level and Nairobi level by regular update of 4W matrix. In addition, the project will work closely with local community/ beneficiaries and administration as well as elders during project monitoring to ensure maximum impacts of the project to the vulnerable communities and IDPs (beneficiaries)JF has been implementing several WASH nutrition, health, education and protection based projects in Kismayu, Afmadow and Dhobley. The organization has a very good working relationship with the local communities, elders, women heads of households and religious leaders that has always been key to successful implementation of past implemented projects hence they will ensure effective implementation

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Exclusion of female personnel from planned training/services Mitigation; Ensure sufficient representation of both female and male included in all planned training and services.The minors may be deprived of the ration provided to ensure that they are kept in the program to still sustain the entire family with the project intervention. JF with the help of the CHW will be doing HH to HH visits to ensure that the services rendered are provided to the real beneficiary in the intervention.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Juba Foundation in partnership with WRRS through the project will work towards integrating Health and nutrition in order to provide easy access to both interventions The project will increase access to nutrition and primary health care to the community so as to address the high rates of morbidity and mortatlity particullarly to mathers, children and the disabled with different challenges. This will also be delivered amongst the IDPs and vulnerable host / crises affected communities and further protect them from the dangers of contacting AWD/Cholera and other water borne diseases. Women and girls that have been at risk of Sexual gender based violence that might have affected them psychologically and mentally. Mental health services will be address and public health concerns.
