Project Summary

Through the proposed response, Save the Children International will provide integrated lifesaving nutrition service to the population in Akobo West, Budi , Bor, Kapoeta, Lopa Lafon, Magwi, Nyirol, Torit Counties, and Abyei administrative area. The aim of this is to reduce suffering, morbidity, and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable populations, SCI's proposed response will focus both treatment and preventative approaches, using community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and infant and young child feeding (IYCF) approaches. SCI Emergency nutrition programs will include. i) Community mobilization to increase access and coverage of CMAM services, ii) Treatment of children and PWLs with acute malnutrition through inpatient and Outpatient therapeutic programs. iii) blanket Supplementary feeding program. iv) Institutional feeding program (IFP), v) Maternal dietary supplementation, vi) Promotion of optimal IYCF through mother-to-mother support groups. vii) Improving Dietary Diversity through household kitchen gardening, viii) Strengthen nutrition information systems and operations research to ensure an evidence-based response, ix) Building capacity for frontline nutrition workers and institutions at all levels. SCI will work through communities to promote, protect and support recommended IYCF practices through one-on-one counselling and group message and counselling. Besides the CMAM and behaviour change interventions, SCI will implement cash transfer intervention in selected areas targeting families of severely malnourished children and SAM PLW to maximize the program's nutrition outcomes and reduce the relapse of acute malnutrition among under-five children and PLW. Through this response, SCI will support 104 static OTP/TSFPs and eight stabilization centres integrated with health facilities. The service will be delivered through static and outreach/mobile facilities for remote and hard-to-reach areas in the targeted project locations. SCI will work with MOH, UNICEF and WFP to secure timely provision of Nutrition supplies necessary for Nutrition Service Delivery. SCI will reach 73,683 under-five Children and 66,992 PLW.
SCI will make deliberate efforts to enhance coordination and integration with the Health, WASH, FSL, Protection and Education clusters to address the causes of malnutrition through a multi-sectoral approach that aims to reduce vulnerabilities, prevent malnutrition and build resilience among the vulnerable people. Specific multi-sectoral activities are to increase access to safe water and sanitation, promote critical WASH messages, and integrate early childhood development strategies in nutrition treatment sites. Kitchen gardening, screening and treating of malaria, managing diarrhea, and mainstreaming child protection and gender-based violence risk mitigation will also be promoted at the nutrition sites.y. SCI will also support hygiene interventions to improve nutrition outcomes and reduce risk of hygiene-related morbidities
SCI will monitor the nutrition situation and the needs of the affected people through KAP survey, SMART surveys, monthly nutrition programme data collection and analysis, and joint field monitoring visits. Spot checks, supportive supervision and monitoring visits will monitor service delivery and engage with the community and other stakeholders to gauge evolving needs. Community nutrition volunteers will be supported to undertake household visits, conduct regular screening of malnourished children and women to ensure timely detection and treatment, and report on a monthly basis. Data collection tools and analysis will be strengthened to enhance monitoring of integrated services

1 - Needs

Malnutrition remains a persistent problem in South Sudan that continues to adversely affect the well-being of women and children. In 2022, an estimated two million children and women will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will need urgent treatment. The main causes of malnutrition are directly related to inadequate dietary intake as well as high prevalence of diseases, and indirectly to multiple factors including food insecurity, sub-optimal childcare and feeding practices, inadequate sanitation conditions and hygiene practices, poor to access health and nutrition services. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 percent was reported in twenty-six counties in six states (Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and Eastern Equatoria). According to the FSNMS+ round 27(October 2021) results, a Very High Concern (>15%) of malnutrition rate continued to be reported in SCI project locations, GAM prevalence exceeded the WHO emergency threshold in 6 SCI Operational counties namely Akobo(19%), Bor(14.8%), Lafon (13.9%), Kapoeta(16.5%), Nyrol (19.%) and Abyei (16.5%) respectively. Despite the improved quality of nutrition care and robust coverage of CMAM services, a very High Concern of nutritional situation is due to food insecurity, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease, inadequate sanitation conditions, and hygiene practices, hence the need to focus on context-based multi-sectoral nutrition programming or response is paramount to have faster and more significant improvements on nutrition outcomes and impact.

3 - Project indicators

SCI will measure the progress towards programme and results using various nutrition information data sources, including nutrition surveys, nutrition programme data/reports, the Food Security and Nutrition Management System (FSNMS),. Programme data is one of the major sources of nutrition information. Routine Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) reports are collected through the nutrition cluster reporting mechanism, using the nutrition information system (NIS) application. The NIS captures various programme components including OTP, Stabilization Centers (SC), TSFPs, MIYCN and Family Led MUAC approach. SCI Country Office’s nutrition information team will analyses the reports on a monthly basis and produce knowledge products including trend analysis, to guide the nutrition cluster to better understand the monthly evolving programme performance and compare with previous years for decision making.In addition, SCI will conduct post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to monitor and assess the progress of distributed cash/vouchers, the distribution process and measure the beneficiaries’ satisfaction. To improve access to diversified foods in the target areas, save the children will promote vegetable gardening through the MtMSGs by establishing demonstration gardens in the nutrition sites.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

SCI using its Food security and livelihood team will conduct Market assessment to determine the actual of the Cash/Voucher of the local market. Cash/voucher delivery mechanisms will also be determined based on the market assessment.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

SCI will support families of severely malnourished children with cash/vouchers for three months while the child is discharged cured of the feeding centers. This improves the child’s access to nutritious foods and prevents the child from relapse from acute malnutrition. A cash/voucher transfer value (on average $80 per household per month) is provided to cover the minimum energy food basket (MEB) of the household. The actual value for MEB will be determined based on the market assessment of the local market. Cash/voucher delivery mechanisms will also be determined based on the market assessment. SC will conduct post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to monitor and assess distributed (cash/voucher), the distribution process, and measure the beneficiaries’ satisfaction. To improve access to diversified foods in the target areas, save the children will promote vegetable gardening through the MtMSGs by establishing demonstration gardens in the nutrition sites link the mothers with food security partner for technical support and learning on techniques of growing vegetables in their backyard.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SCI will ensure the feedback and complaint mechanism design and implementation is appropriate for all members of the community including those who are not literate, the elderly, women and girls and individuals living with disabilities; that the most vulnerable have equal access to the feedback and complaint mechanisms;SCI will ensure that there is sufficient time, resources and flexibility to implement a feedback mechanism and respond to feedback once the mechanism is in place and to actively solicit feedback from individuals representing marginalized groups; SCI will ensure Conducting a thorough context analysis to ensure that all the targeted groups/ vulnerable groups are considered before deciding on a particular feedback mechanism and ensuring the mechanism has different ways to report into it, including methods which are without cost and which are suitable for persons who are not literate;

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SCI will ensure to create meaningful opportunities for communities, including children, to participate in program decisions and provide SC with feedback on nutrition interventions. SC strongly relies on community participation to improve the quality of SCI programming and ensure interventions continue to meet the most urgent needs of vulnerable populations. Beneficiaries will be informed about project activities, planned duration, their entitlements, and expected staff behavior, hotline and other communication channels they can use to communicate with Save the Children field office and the Country office in Juba. The affected communities will be reached out through ongoing community mobilization and awareness raising sessions by our community mobilization team. The team will engage with the various project stakeholders, such as the government authorities, NGOs, INGOs and the community representatives on an ongoing basis Key methods of communication are, Word of mouth, Flipcharts, Videos for small groups of health workers on tablets, Megaphone, 1 to 1 meetings with key stakeholders, Extensive IEC materials.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Most of the work in protection from sexual exploitation and abuse will be done at community, payam and county level. Engaging local populations and having support locally is a crucial element in succeeding in this work. This will involve, raising awareness in local communities of what PSEA is, that there is zero tolerance for PSEA, the options for reporting suspected PSEA cases, how the reporting mechanism works and where they go access further support and referrals. SCI will conduct regular awareness on the beneficiaries/affected population on PSEA in the nutrition sites

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Save the Children review the feedback with program teams if these are related to the request for information, request for assistance, minor dissatisfaction. If these are related to categories of major programmatic complaints or breach of code of conduct then these are referred to Safeguarding and Child Protection focal persons. ,SCI will engage the affected population to participate fully in the project design and implementation and will use collect the beneficiaries’ feedback and draw an action plan on areas to improve program implementation.
