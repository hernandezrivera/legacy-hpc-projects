Project Summary

The interventions planned with this project will ensure timely access to essential live saving and comprehensive sexual and reproductive health services to Rohingya refugees and a host community in ukhiya and Teknaf Sub districts of Cox’s bazar district. The project aims at addressing the priority sexual and reproductive Health include ensuring the minimum initial service package (MISP) for reproductive health in crisis, and now expanding to comprehensive SRH for the affected population. Although some partners are providing the minimum initial service package of sexual and reproductive health, access to essential comprehensive reproductive, maternal and new-born health services remain a major concern.
Safe access to 24-hour rapid transfer of all life-threatening emergencies is in need of expansion. This is of particular concern with obstetric and sexual and reproductive health (SRH) conditions of women and adolescent girls to prevent maternal and gender-based violence morbidity and mortality the project will support the emergency referral hubs. The project will also ensure 24/7 lifesaving services (EmONC and support CEmONC services in selected referral facilities) and strengthen referral pathways as well as maintaining continues supply of life saving SRH commodities including family planning services.
Based on available data an estimated less than 32% of deliveries among the refugees occurred in facilities which may reflect a combination of both demand and access issues. So the project will employ several approaches to increase service uptake which includes:
• Strengthening community based SRH services and behavioural change approaches by ensuring that quality SRH information and services are available near the communities.
• Scale up quality midwifery led care across the camps by providing mentorship support to the service providers.
• Attract women to deliver in health facilities using evidence based financial and non-financial incentives, which the project will cover
• Address issues such as obstetric fistulas and the comprehensive SRH services
The project will also address SRH needs for high-risk and special need populations (e.g. sex workers, Adolescent Sexual Reproductive Health and disabled) which are currently yet to be addressed systematically and need to be integrated into the overall reproductive health care response.
The project will also ensure SRH and GBV services are provided in an integrated manner.
Due to the temporary nature of facility infrastructure which poses a challenge to longer-term continuity of services, as the infrastructures degrades the project will reinforce, repair, and maintain vital sexual and reproductive health structures.
Continued support to the SRH Sub Sector coordination to provide high quality technical assistance to ensure comprehensive SRH information and services are of international standards and on par with nationals.
The project will directly target 406,108 population including women of reproductive age, men,boys and girls and high risk and special need (Key) populations.

Needs

The priority sexual and reproductive Health include ensuring the minimum initial service package (MISP) for reproductive health in crisis, and now expanding to comprehensive SRH for the affected population. Although some partners are providing the minimum initial service package of sexual and reproductive health, access to essential comprehensive reproductive, maternal and new-born health services remain a major concern. Based on available data an estimated less than 32% of deliveries among the refugees occurred in facilities which may reflect a combination of both demand and access issues.It is critical to ensure 24/7 lifesaving services and strengthen referral pathways to ensure timely access. Family planning needs remain unaddressed for these very vulnerable populations. More support is required to strengthen community based SRH services and behavioural change approaches by ensuring that quality SRH information and services are available near the communities.The SRH needs for high-risk and special need populations (e.g. sex workers, Adolescent Sexual Reproductive Health and disabled) are yet to be addressed systematically and need to be integrated into the overall reproductive health care response. Given the high home deliveries and low skilled birth attendance, issues such as obstetric fistula need to be addressed urgently. There is also an urgent need to scale up quality midwifery led care across the camps by providing mentorship support to the service providers. More efforts are also needed to attract women to deliver in health facilities using evidence based financial and non-financial incentives. The temporary nature of facility infrastructure poses a challenge to longer-term continuity of services, as the infrastructures degrade. Additional support is needed to strengthen host community facilities and a mechanism is needed to cover referral costs to avoid burden on Government facilities and more support is required to strenghten the referral facilties to provide 24/7 management of emergency obstertric care and gynaecological complications. Care provision for pregnant mothers with other complications, which may increase the risk of obstetric emergencies, need to be strengthened. Continued support to the SRH Sub Sector coordination will be required to provide high quality technical assistance to ensure comprehensive SRH information and services are of international standards and on par with nationals.

Activities or outputs

1. Expand the services from MISP to comprehensive SRH by strengthening existing health facilities to provide 24/7 emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC)• Continue to support the deployment of midwives at health facilities/tent clinics in the affected areas to ensure safe deliveries for pregnant women and provide essential RH services, including Clinical Management of Rape and Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EmONC). • Provide high quality technical assistance to ensure comprehensive SRH information and services provided are of international standards and on par with nationals • Support upazila health complexes of Ukhia,field hospital, and Cox's Bazar Sadar Hospital to provide 24/7 CEmONC services• Implement conditional cash voucher and non-cash incentive program to improve service uptake, focusing on ANC & SBA• Establish 24/7 SRH facilities at the sites where no SRH services are provided• Procure and distribute Emergency Reproductive Health kits/commodities• Provide comprehensive sexual and reproductive health services in all facilities including FP, management and prevention of STI, HIV/AIDS.• Provide comprehensive adolescent sexual and reproductive health services• Provide screening services and care for RH morbidity i.e cervical cancer and obstetric fistula• Provide SRH services for special groups i.e sex workers and TG by establishing service access points (DIC) 2. Strengthen the referral pathway• Procure three-wheel ambulances, stretchers to ensure safe and timely transport of pregnant women to health facilities especially during labor/complications.• Reinforce, repair, and maintain vital sexual reproductive health structures• Establishing and supporting the emergency referral hubs in the camps 3. Ensure GBV-SRH integration i.e ensuring basic SRH services are provided in all UNFPA supported safe spaces for women and girls (deployment of midwives in WFS)4. Continue to strengthen SRH coordination and ensure that SRH and GBV services are provided in an integrated manner and provide technical assistance to partners through SRH resource centre, i.e. workshops, training, capacity building activities, 5. Improve data management systems and assessments to better understand the SRH needs and gaps 6. Strengthen community based SRH services to improve service uptake
