Needs

An estimated 2.6 million people are displaced in Somalia. The displaced population’s coping mechanisms have been affected over years of repeated drought and conflict, leaving them vulnerable to shocks and preventing sustainable return to places of origin. The proposed project will primarily target Mogadishu, Baidoa, Kismayo, and Dolow, regions that have been particularly affected by the drought where the needs remain particularly high. Protracted conflict-driven displacement, aggravated by new drought-related displacements, lack of self-reliance, and unclear plans for a durable solution, is resulting in an increase in overall displaced populations and deterioration of living conditions in IDP settlements and host communities. IDPs primarily live in communal sites with limited access to basic services and exposure to protection risks. Lack of coordination on service provision among partners makes it difficult for services to be delivered adequately and, in some cases, has led to duplication of aid. Strengthening of coordination continues to be crucial in ensuring assistance is provided to those most in need. Forced evictions of IDPs and urban poor continue to be a major problem in Somalia's urban areas. Mogadishu has the highest number of IDPs per urban centre with 892 sites being noted across the city housing approximately 485,731 IDPs. The CCCM cluster partners active in Mogadishu are only able to cover 10% of these IDP sites with CCCM support and thus a great majority of these sites remain un-supported. Following Mogadishu, the CCCM cluster Detailed Site Assessment which was first undertaken in October 2017 shows that both Baidoa (371) and Kismayo (134 sites) are in the top three areas with the most IDP sites. In addition, Dolow has two of the largest single sites in Kabasa and Qansaxley which together total an approximate 50,400 individuals and thus all four areas of the projects focus therefore remain in the top priority for CCCM interventions.

Activities

Objective 1: Strengthen the predictability and effectiveness of multi sectorial services at site level through improved site management and coordination • Represent CCCM in relevant coordination forums • Conduct site verifications on every threemonth-basis• Establish/update service mapping of partners operating in sites• Monitor service delivery in sites Objective 2: Improve living conditions of displaced people through site development, care and maintenance• Provide training on site maintenance and site planning to camp committees• Support community led site maintenance activities to ensure upkeep of sites (cash for work, site maintenance committees, distribution of tools)• Conduct safety audits to identify areas of high protection risk• Implement emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites • Support relocations to public land when appropriate Objective 3: Strengthen community self-management and access to information for displaced populations• Mapping of existing community structures• Identify and support governance structures, encouraging participation of all different segments of the displaced population• Provide capacity building to existing governance structures/focal points• Provide training on CCCM principles to community governance structures• Provide community spaces for management committees • Establish/reinforce mechanisms for communication of multi-sector services available • Ensure all members of the displaced community have the opportunity to submit feedback and complaint through harmonized feedback mechanisms relevant for sites across all clusters Objective 4: Support opportunities for displaced people to achieve durable solutions• Implement community based activities to promote community cohesion and recovery• Conduct intention surveys where needed with relevant partners/clusters to ensure the intentions of populations are understood and supported

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Community participation is a key pillar of CCCM and supporting communities to find longer term solutions from displacement is also a key focus for CCCM. Community lead initiatives will be encouraged with communities being encouraged to take over responsibilities of the care and maintenance of their IDP sites in order to support local integration as a longer term solution where appropriate. The IOM CCCM team will also work closely with relevant authorities and community governance structures to ensure that affected populations are informed about options for durable solutions and receive support to attain their plans. Finally,IOM CCCM will also continue to work closely with the Durable solutions IOM Programme team to implement joined up programming internally that can help foster these longer term solutions and support for affected populations.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

IOM CCCM will work closely with the protection cluster at both field level and National level to develop strategies to mitigate exclusion in service delivery. IOM CCCM will continue to ensure that monitoring and complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place, are active and are well known and used by the affected communities in order to mitigate exclusion based on ethnicity, age, disability or gender.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The CCCM teams will also work with existing community governance systems and support them to ensure there is gender and age diversity in their representation as well as ethnic diversity to avoid exclusion. Where no governance structure is presence IOM CCCM will support communities to come together and agree on the type of representation they would require that allows the participation of all groups in decision making. Where possible the CCCM teams will reach out beyond the sites to extend support to the host communities and encourage peaceful coexistence through joint projects.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

By supporting a more effective and well targeted humanitarian response through improved coordination and information sharing in sites – CCCM helps to mitigate the effects of armed conflict and violence by supporting IDPs to find a safe and secure settlement option with access to basic services in a quicker manner, that is catered to their needs; reducing the stress of displacement.
