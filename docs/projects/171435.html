Project Summary

This project comes to address the urgent needs of the health sector in Gaza as a result of COVID-19. While the incidence rates for COVID-19 were very low in Gaza due to the strict lockdown that has been imposed by MoI and MoH on returnees; the pandemic broke in the community like it was anticipated by health professionals since February/March when the pandemic was announced. Not to dwell much on figures (as these will be explained further in the needs assessment section) but Gaza is now suffering from a COVID-19 community outbreak. Taking the characteristics of the place, the socio-economic status of the people and the political map in the region; the crisis hit Gaza hard. This all adds up to the fact that the health system has been deteriorating since more than 13 years now.
The MoH has identified many needs to respond to the COVID-19 pandemic and has activated the highest level of its emergency response (level D – code Red). As such, the needs to respond effectively are growing as of the minute of writing this concept paper. The health and nutrition cluster (HNC) is anticipating that there are needs of more than 31.3 million USD to respond to the COVID-19 pandemic in oPt. The HNC also anticipates that the case load for COVID-19 in Gaza is 35,000 people. This project will be part of the overall coordinated response with the MoH, HNC and other organisations to respond to the most urgent health needs that are currently surfacing in relation to COVID-19 such as the provision of lab materials for testing, drugs, disposables for case-management and PPEs for health workers. This will also be coupled with awareness messages through several platforms and printed materials about COVID-19 awareness through a community partner that MAP has had a good experience within this field.
This project will fall under HRP Strategic Objective #3: The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with, and overcome, a protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
Will also fall under Cluster Objective: 2 Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises.

1 - Needs

On 24 August, at 11 pm, the MoH in Gaza held a press conference in which they announced the detection of the first cases of positive COVID-19 patients from within the community, indigenous cases. The cases were detected inside Al Maghazi refugees’ camp in the middle area, and a full lock-down was announced on that geographical location. However, the government’s plan was at level 3 (code amber) of the response as this was a case of only few cases detected. Exactly the next morning, everybody in Gaza woke up to an activation of level 4 of the response plan (code red) which led to more stricter measures: full closure of all governorates, curfews, shutdown of all public and private spaces, closing streets with cement blocks, etc. Since then, and as per the most recent statistics from the MoH, 4285 cases of COVID-19 have been reported: 1695 are still active cases 2522 cases recovered and 26 deaths were reported since late August. Of those infected, there is 165 health workers including 59 nurses, and 21 doctors (MAP was able to get this figure through direct communication with a MoH senior figure). More general statistics indicate that almost 80% of the infected are adults while 20% are children while 55% are male and 45% are female. Currently 5 patients are in severe conditions inside the EGH’s ICU, 16 are moderate but symptomatic. A KII with a senior figure at the ICD of the MoH indicated that needs are increasing day-by-day and that, just an example, a need to supply the MoH with testing materials for 50 days to cover 100,000 cases with a net worth of 2.5 million USD, and this was just the tip of the iceberg. Since before the detection of the indigenous cases, a need to urgently fill gaps related to lab reagents needed for COVID-19 testing, drugs related to the ICU and respiratory system to treat the symptomatic patients, medical disposables, personal protective equipment (PPEs) for the health workers, and materials for Infection Prevention Control (IPC) has persistent. The most recent updates from the MoH’s Central Drug Stores (CDS) indicate that there are shortages in drugs and disposables equal to 47% and 30% respectively including a shortage of 51% in epidemiological drugs(CDS, September 2020) While the laboratory unit reported that there are shortages of around 59% (as per an interview that MAP got did with CDS). More importantly, MAP was also informed that the testing capacity of the MoH has recently dropped from 2,000 tests per day to 1,500 due to the shortages of disposables needed for one of the extraction machines at the central laboratory. The MoH has been urgently calling for help to try and react in a speedy manner to the needs on the ground, however, the needs are bigger than any single organisation. Thus, it is extremely important to try and alleviate the suffering of the people and Gaza and help the health system respond rapidly.MAP is also planning to implement an awareness campaign through our partner Social Development Forum (SDF) to reach the public and increase their awareness about COVID-19. MAP has implemented a similar campaign with this partner this year and we were able to influence 1.5 million reach via more than 100 contributors to the campaign. The campaign will include a mixture of social media messages via Facebook, Instagram and other famous platform while also there will be posters and billboards printed in addition to radio sports and SMS.

2 - Activities

The activities that MAP will implement are :Procure and deliver urgently needed PPEs to the front-line health workers. The PPEs will be used by 500 health workers at any given time of the delivery of the items as per the official statistics of MoH for health workers at the EGH and also the Turkish hospital where the COVID-19 cases are being treated now. Procure and deliver urgently needed: drugs, disposables, and lab reagents for case management of COVID 19 of 7,300 patients and for testing 25,000 people. Please note that there is no place in the indicators section to express those numbers separately, so we combined both together and put in the # of people with access to testing to detect communicable diseases e.g. COVID-19.The technical committees formed will evaluate the items based on technical data, quality, delivery time and then MAP does the financial analysis to award suppliers. Preference will be given to local suppliers who are able to deliver fast and who have a good experience with MAP. Produce 3 radio spots about COVID-19Print 20,000 posters and 5 billboardsProduce 50 social media messages via famous platformsSend 25,000 awareness messages via SMS Please note that for the above community activities, MAP was able to reach (in a similar campaign that we implemented with our community partner) 1.5 million hits/reaches and these are the combination of social media hits + radio listeners + SMS reaches + viewers and readers of other publications of hard and soft nature. MAP has a unique and adaptive procurement that enables us to procure in the fastest fashion possible while also completing the needed and required paper work. During emergencies, MAP sends RFQs to all suppliers we have in the data base of vendors and we avoid tenders in newspapers to save time. Quotations are all collected by e-mail and technical sheets for tender analysis are filled then sent to the technical committee members via e-mails. We get responses by e-mails from the technical members and then we finalize the selection analysis based on: delivery time, price and reputation of the vendor and the history they have delivering items on time without delays. Please note that MAP will take extra care to ensure that gender mainstreaming is taken into consideration in this project. Gender will be considered during the planning, delivery, monitoring and evaluation of all project activities. Such consideration will include, but not necessarily be limited to, the accessibility, acceptability, staffing, reporting and impact of those activities. This will be done via the community component with our partner via the different activities mentioned above. For example, women at reproductive age could be vulnerable to the disease and that this vulnerable category should be addressed uniquely in all the activities such as the SMS, radio spots, social media posts and other printed materials.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

MAP’s own slogan is “working for the health and dignity of Palestinians under occupation and in refugee camps.” This slogan implies how MAP works closely to prevent harm and ensure the safety and dignity of Palestinians. This project is created to prevent further harm in the community that may be caused by COVID-19, so in its core, it serves the Do No Harm principle. As for safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults: MAP has developed an organisational algorithm for responding to Safeguarding concerns and region-specific algorithms for the initial investigation of such concerns. These documents were developed in conjunction with safeguarding experts and are considered fit for purpose and consistent with current sector guidance and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS).They contain clear lines of responsibility and reporting and, where appropriate, confirm the timelines to be followed when responding to safeguarding concerns. All these documents are reviewed at least every two years. An incident report template is used to report any suspected breaches in MAP’s Code of Conduct or Safeguarding policy to MAP’s Safeguarding focal point.MAP has trained all its staff on the safeguarding code of conduct and also trained all its partners (including SDF, the potential partner for this project) on this policy. Yearly, we refresh the training course for partners, not only to staff working directly on the project, but also the senior level management at the organisation.In addition, MAP applies strict rules to ensure that the items procured are what is needed by MoH. We provide testing as part of the contracts and we reject any items that do not match the technical criteria ensuring that the medical items are of the most benefit to the beneficiaries.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MAP abides by the four humanitarian principles:We are Humanitarian meaning that we provide help whenever and wherever it is needed in order to protect lives.We are Impartial and we do deliver our help based on the needs regardless of any discriminatory facts such as politics, gender, religion, or class.We are Neutral and do not take sides with any party over the other. The most important thing is to alleviate the suffering of people.We are Independent and we are not controlled or affected by any external powers by those political or commercial.The above principles are engraved deeply into MAP’s foundations and are guiding all the work we do.For this project specifically, we will deliver directly to the CDS which in turn distributes to all MoH facilities in need ensuring access to all services by all vulnerable groups according to their needs. MAP monitor the delivery at the facilities level to ensure that items are made available for beneficiaries.In addition, collaborating with SDF (local partner) will ensure the large widespread of the COVID-19 messaging and guidance to reach as wide as possible of an audience. The usage of several media platform makes sure that the messaging is reaching a maximum number of beneficiaries regardless of the educational background (for example, radio spots can be very useful to elderlies at home and illiterate people while publications will be helpful to literate people). All the above will play part in the equal and impartial access to aid.
