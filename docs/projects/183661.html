Project Summary

This proposal responds to the identified needs from Alianza por la Solidaridad (ApS) and its partners, that have solid experience in GBV programming (both jointly and separately). We aim at improving the
GBV protection response in Gaza and West Bank. Our intervention will be articulated in 3 main results with specific activities allocated to each one of them:

R1: Increased access of women and girls survivors of gender and sexual violence with and without disabilities to inclusive quality multisectoral response services (Gaza and West Bank):
ApS and its partners will work to improve the quality of, and access to, comprehensive and inclusive services for GBV survivors. Through One Stop center and remote assistance, women, girls men and boys with and without disabilities will have access to Sexual and Reproductive Health Services, psychological, social and legal services. Home visits and mobile clinics will be mobilized to ensure access for the population living in remote areas, or under mobility restrictions. ApS will provide support and monitoring to ensure compliance with quality standards in the services provided and all the staff will receive capacity building in SOPs, PSEA, disabilities and Sexual violence with the aim of provide inclusive and discrimination-free services.

R2: Improved the coverage of multisectoral needs for women and girls survivors of GBV with and without disabilities in the mitigation of protection risks and the strengthening of positive coping strategies (Gaza).
ApS will provide multipurpose conditional cash to women survivors of GBV to meet their multi-sectoral needs and as a protection tool to strengthen their positive coping strategies and mitigate the risks associated with GBV. Cash assistance to women is considered an effective tool to support their empowerment and increase their capacity to be part of the decision-making process in their households, provided that protection needs and risks are taken into account. Food and hygiene vouchers are also foreseen as a response to more immediate and urgent needs of women in emergency situations and when multipurpose cash assistance can increase the risk of GBV especially so for women living in the same building/together with the husband’s family. In addition, since conflicts and humanitarian crises damage livelihoods, GBV survivors will receive support through vocational training and materials to help them to restore access to productive resources and self-sustenance. All these activities will be implemented as a part of the multisectoral services and in a close monitoring and evaluation of the impact of CVA intervention.

R3: Strengthened Community prevention and protection mechanisms against GBV (Gaza and West Bank):
ApS will work towards strengthening community protection mechanisms against GBV and SEA. A methodology focused on safe spaces for women and girls, community participation, rights promotion, activation of community mechanisms and accountability will be used. In addition, a strong component is included for the inclusion of PSEA policies and their effective implementation within the program, as well as the participatory design of a community protection plan in case of emergency.

1 - Needs

The situation of the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) it is marked by a protracted humanitarian crisis driven by the Israeli blockade and overlapping crisis. According to the HNO 2022 draft around 1.8 million Palestinians across the oPt, 64% in the Gaza Strip and 36 % in the West Bank are in need. COVID19 and the last military offensive in May 2021 in Gaza has exacerbated this protracted protection crisis, with the most critical problems related to protection of civilians, forced displacement, access to essential services, and erosion of resilience. Understanding that crisis are not uniformly distributed across the territory and needs differ according to their location, gender, age and disability, some appear to be perpetually more vulnerable and systematically at a greater disadvantage. The deterioration of the living conditions and its impact on gender and family dynamics, has exacerbated GBV in all its forms. Women and girls with disabilities, bedouin women, internally displaced women and women and girls living in refugee camps are particularly at risk of violence. The impact of the conflict in Gaza has increased the need of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) specially in children. Accordingly, COVID19 increased pressure on access to basic services, households’ violence and negative coping mechanisms as early marriage for the girls under 18. In Gaza, (37.5%) of married or previously married women (18 - 64 years) have been exposed to some form of GBV mainly within their homes (PCBS, 2019). According to the data collected, violence manifests itself in various forms during quarantine, with 49% of women reporting physical violence, 61% psychological violence, 25% sexual violence and 41% violence (AISHA, 2021). In addition, with COVID19, cyber gender-based violence has increased significantly and it is needed to increase prevention and response measures. Men also declare an increased of psychological stress due to feelings of being unable to protect the family and meet their basic needs, this feeling is exacerbated in men with disability. As the crisis continues there is a need to scale up emergency GBV services including emergency health care; build capacity of services providers in safe remote assistance, disabilities and sexual violence; access to remote areas, strengthen referral pathways specially for children and people with disabilities, include men in prevention and response of GBV and masculinities and support frontline workers with stress management and psychological support. The context in oPt has a detrimental impact on employment, income and education, in many cases forcing women and girls, including those with disabilities, to adopt negative coping strategies that expose them to further exploitation and abuse. These negative coping strategies are often related to economic vulnerability and access to resources. Women and men frequently linked the lack or absence of employment with increase of tensions in the household. Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA) has the potential to alleviate economic hardship and to become an effective tool to respond to protection concerns and GBV risks, contributing to the resilience of women. However, without proper gender considerations, there is a concern that cash transfers may fail to reach those left furthest behind. The way resources are distributed and controlled between and male and female recipients inside the household might also affect protection risks negatively. it is important to integrated into multi-sectoral services, ensure monitoring and support during and after assistance and to link short-term cash support to women and female headed households to economic empowerment and income generating programs. There is also a need to coordination with community-based initiatives and strengthen culture GBV- sensitive including affected population, men, religious leaders and other groups and to promote monitoring and transparency complaint mechanisms, specially from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). There is a shared responsibility of international organization and local partner to strengthen community protection mechanism and PSEA.

2 - Activities

R1: Increased access of women and girls survivors of gender and sexual violence with and without disabilities to inclusive quality multisectoral response services (Gaza and West Bank): Provision of Sexual and Reproductive Health services; Provision of Mental health and psychosocial services for GBV survivors (MHPSS) and men and children when needed; Provision of Legal aid (legal information, counselling, representation); Support referral and cover transportation of beneficiaries; Adaptation of multi-sectoral services to people with disabilities through interpretation services, information and communication materials; Provision of Emergency health care; Capacity building for GBV service providers in SOPs and the provision of safe and remote services; Specialized training in disability inclusion and GBV (intersectionality); GBV: Capacity building for humanitarian actors in identification, referral and disability mainstreaming (non-GBV specialist); GBV: Awareness raising for persons at risk of GBV (Including cybercrime); Provision of Self-care and stress management for service providers; Monitoring and documentation of GBV case ; Provision of MHPSS and SRHSS trough mobile clinics in ARAs; Provision of MHPSS and Legal counselling through home visits; Training of personnel in the accountability policies and protection against Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (PSEA) policies and their application. R2: Improved the coverage of multisectoral needs for women and girls survivors of GBV with and without disabilities in the mitigation of protection risks and the strengthening of positive coping strategies (Gaza): Capacity Building of GBV service providers on the implementation of the SOPs, integrating the cash delivery programs into the GBV response programs; Delivery of multi-purpose cash and CVA to women survivors of gender violence and their families; Provision of MHPSS and social support to mitigate the risks associated to CVA to women survivors of GBV and their families; Provision and support of Economic empowerment and protection of livelihoods initiatives to GBV survivors (also in West Bank); Develop monitoring tool during and after delivery to measure the impact of cash assistance and tailored the support according to their needs. R3: Strengthened Community prevention and protection mechanisms against GBV (Gaza and West Bank): Awareness raising and consultations on GBV, and protective mechanisms; Advocacy initiatives that aim to address protection concerns, prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations; Create and support Safe Space for Women and Girls in person and remotely; Training of women for the management of the Safe Space (time bank); Distribution of dignity kits (the dignity includes information about PSEA mechanisms, channels and information); Accountability workshops with the beneficiary population; Identify and map community agents in the accountability policies and protection against Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (PSEA); Establish safe complaint channel(s) for beneficiaries based on consultations with the beneficiaries and accessible to different groups; Ensure a clear process for receipt and referral of complaints of PSEA, in accordance with the oPt PSEA SOPs on Inter Agency Referrals; Training to community agents in the accountability policies and protection against Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (PSEA) policies and their application; Awareness on the prevention of SEA and access to services; Needs assessment and mapping of community resources to include in the Community Protection Plan in emergencies (CPPE); Participative design and capacity building of the Community Protection Plan in emergencies; Implementation of a simulation of the CPPE.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Health providers have the potential to serve as an important entry point for identifying sexual and gender-based violence. The project will strength the capacity of health and service providers to ensure access to quality multisectoral services, including Sexual and Reproductive Health Services (SRHS); psychological, legal and social services. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) can support to GBV and other sexual and reproductive health services, and contribute to the safety, dignity and resilience of women and girls in humanitarian contexts. It can offer discretion and flexibility compared to in-kind assistance, which can be particularly helpful for those who may be more at risk of GBV. CVA can also strengthen protection outcomes for women and girls: women at risk who can use CVA for housing or other basic needs are less likely to resort to negative or harmful practices such as forced marriage and may be less vulnerable to sexual exploitation and abuse. However, in order to maximize effectiveness of CVA, GBV and protection risk prevention and mitigation measures must be established to ensure CVA delivery is safe and appropriate to the context, and that the CVA modality does not create or exacerbate tensions within the family or the community.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

There are different ways in the project to collect feedback from the affected population. There is a complaint box in the premises where multisectoral services are provided. The boxes are easily accessible to the users and anonymous. Each implementing partner has its own system for complaints management and feedback, but all of them are survivor centered and protect the rights of affected people. On the other hand, satisfaction questionnaires are distributed in the face-to-face sessions and for people receiving services or participating in different activities, as well as on online platforms and groups. The surveys are standardized with a set of closed questions that allow assess the level of satisfaction in different dimensions, at the same time that include some open questions to open the space for recommendations. According to the project a hotline it is also available. The information collected through these tools is systematized by the programme coordinator and shared with ApS with the internal monitoring reports. The received feedback will be used for: i) programmatic purposes. Identifying any needed corrective measure to improve access, quality or impact. ii) identification of emergency or nonidentified needs. For planning future interventions or for advocacy purposes. ApS will directly engage in the PSEA network, participating as requested and engaging in the capacity program to build a robust complaint mechanism to allow the affected groups to safely report any misconduct or abuse at the same time that to allow the partner organizations to investigate and take immediate action if needed.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

APS has a strong track record in gender-sensitive capacity development interventions and the intervention has been designed, planned and implemented in a gender sensitive manner, avoiding further damage and suffering as a result of our actions. Our staff recognize the potential negative effects of interventions; taking into consideration not only the target group but also the wider environment; taking a step back from and even questioning interventions; APS always make sure and is aware: of the power-relations and conflict dynamics in the contexts in which projects are implemented, are not creating conflict any harm to the local population and target group, that conflict analysis conducted and updated regularly, that we plan, implement, monitor and evaluate our intervention in a no harm-sensitive fashion (including reformulation where necessary) Community participation and self-assessment of the needs of local community is always a part of the intervention, since quality relationships with beneficiaries is integral to do no harm principle. APS consults and involves communities from the identification phase of a project. APS project teams have a wide range of participatory tools at their disposal, which are available in several documents. They allow for meaningful consultation with communities at the project identification stage. Methods we use include: Needs assessment; Desk research; Key informant interviews; Surveys; Expert interviews; Community consultations/focus groups; and Workshops with staff, partners and other relevant actors. Using these methods improves accountability towards beneficiaries, relations with beneficiaries, understanding the context and community dynamics in which projects take place, and acceptance in an intervention area. ApS is willing to integrate the Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) in GBV programming as a resource for service providers to support women exiting the cycle of violence and meet their basic protection needs. ApS will start by conducting an in-depth assessment to determine the contextual and personal requirements and potential challenges for each modality, as well as identify the needs in capacities for the GBV staff to apply CVA in their case management. During the implementation, several practices are implemented as part of project management, referring directly to the “Do no harm” principle: Measures to mitigate pre-identified negative effects, before, during and after implementation of activities. The measures and actions will consider the target beneficiaries and people that may be indirect affected as its family and social networks; Implementation of a complaints management system; Communication and information shared with beneficiaries; Implementation of a mechanism to manage and protect data; Disaggregated data collection according to the most vulnerable categories. In terms of COVID-related prevention and no harm: ApS is always ensuring that facilities and office arrangements are safe for the target group and staff, and adhere to all minimum precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and hygiene promotion, as defined by health authorities. All ApS staff has received comprehensive information on prevention and mitigation and are fully updated and compliant with the imposed restrictions and requirements. ApS Head of Mission is the designated member to follow up and monitor the implementation of hygiene and safety measures within the organization, to establish clear policies and procedures for what must be done when COVID19 symptoms are suspected or there is a potential exposure to a positive case, including immediate reporting, monitoring, isolating staff and activate home-working when necessary. - All activities are subjected to modification to adapt to COVID19 restrictions at any time. Remote services to survivors will be activated as soon as face to face is not advisable, and regular need assessments will be conducted to identify emerging needs.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The observance of humanitarian principles is a matter of ApS professional duty. Considering the definition of impartiality, ApS staff ascertained that aid provided will be allocated in proportion to need and without discrimination. ApS chooses the area of intervention solely on the basis of its own assessment of humanitarian needs, and no distinction is made among the victims on any criteria other than their vulnerability. To ensure that we target those most in need, we continuously build and deepen cooperation with our partners and other local organizations, who share our values. Their contribution is fed into the need assessment done before the project is conceptualized. The needs analysis consists of: • the analysis of the actors, their positions, challenges and power relations, • the understanding of local culture and power relations in communities (including all stakeholders, and considering discriminated groups, especially women), • the training of the institutional capacity of local actors, including their understanding of humanitarian principles and standards. Our staff always makes sure that representatives of all social, ethnic and religious groups in the area of intervention participate in needs assessments. Furthermore, in order to avoid any possible misunderstandings, APS explains to the population that humanitarian aid has to be provided to the most vulnerable as a priority. Here the nondiscrimination principle, which implies equal distribution of aid among beneficiaries with similar needs, complements the principle of the equity, so that an assistance has to be given in priority to those who need it most. If the humanitarian needs of one community are clearly more important than those of another, ApS may still consider rolling out activities for each of them to prevent social fragmentation, conflict and reprisals. Another measure to comply with the principle of equality and impartiality involve the APS attempt to build the response on local capacities. We always strive to involve programme beneficiaries in the management of aid delivery and reflect the composition of local society. Ensuring that staff composition reflects the diversity of the operating environment help us to reinforce the local perception of ApS as principled, transparent and accountable. A gender equality perspective will be included to ensure the program is designed and implemented with a “do no harm” approach that it does not set off conflict, exacerbate tensions between populations or subject individuals to danger and/or abuse including GBV. The program has been designed upon the joint experience of ApS and its partners, with a strong knowledge of the sector, the communities and the needs of the people we are assisting. Gender is mainstreamed in all actions and all activities are planned to ensure meaningful access of most vulnerable groups. GBV services, awareness, self-care sessions, and many other actions are conducted taking into consideration the unpaid and care responsibilities of women that are socially assigned, to ensure they can take part in them. Furthermore, special considerations are taken to mitigate any potential reputational or stigmatization risk, that may prevent women to engage in the activities. ApS and its partners are all committed to enhance the participation of people with disabilities, either physical or other type of impairments. When preparing for each activity, the assessment will be conducted to ensure that any corrective measure can be done in time to promote their participation.
