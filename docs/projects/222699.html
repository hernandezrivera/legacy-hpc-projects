Project Summary

The project titled “Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response in Conflict-Affected Communities of Borno and Adamawa State” aims to strengthen the capacity of health systems to detect, report, and respond to disease outbreaks like cholera, measles, diphtheria, and viral hemorrhagic fevers in communities affected by conflict. Due to the ongoing conflict in northeast Nigeria, these communities are highly vulnerable to disease outbreaks, exacerbated by displacement, overcrowded camps, poor sanitation, and a weakened healthcare system. This project will focus on building the capacity of health workers and community health volunteers by training them on early warning systems and disease reporting mechanisms. Additionally, it will establish sentinel surveillance sites, promote real-time reporting through mobile technology, and improve community engagement in disease prevention through sensitization campaigns. The project will enhance coordination between local health authorities, humanitarian actors, and community stakeholders to ensure a timely and effective response to outbreaks. The ultimate goal is to reduce morbidity and mortality from preventable diseases in these conflict-affected regions by creating a more resilient and responsive health system.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2024 analysis, more than 5.3 million people across the BAY states were identified as requiring health assistance. The affected people are primarily concentrated in rural and hard-to-reach, security-compromised areas. Around 1.5 million returnees are in areas of relocation in the three states, and about 1.8 million IDPs are living in camps and host communities.It is anticipated that in 2024, affected people will remain at significant risk of epidemic-prone diseases, like cholera, measles, diphtheria, and viral hemorrhagic fevers, due to low access to essential healthcare, seasonal patterns, lack of access to potable water and sanitation infrastructure, and cumulative socio-economic vulnerabilities because of continuous displacement. Women and children will continue to be the most vulnerable to disease outbreaks, especially cholera and diphtheria. In addition, limited access to basic quality health care, especially in hard to reach and compromised security areas, prolonged stay in IDP camps in unhygienic conditions, limited access to food, rising inflation, an increasing number of cross-border returnees, including new arrivals from inaccessible areas will increase the vulnerability of the population to diseases outbreaks and other health threats. HNO 2024As a result, disease outbreaks often go undetected or are detected too late, leading to preventable deaths and further strain on the already overburdened health system.In addition to these infrastructure challenges, the local capacity for disease surveillance and response is critically low. Health workers and community health volunteers lack adequate training on Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) protocols, limiting their ability to detect, report, and respond to outbreaks in a timely manner. This gap in capacity is further exacerbated by poor communication and coordination between healthcare providers, local authorities, and humanitarian agencies, which delays response efforts during outbreaks.Community awareness of disease prevention and control is also limited. Many people in conflict-affected areas lack access to basic health information, contributing to the rapid spread of preventable diseases. Sensitization campaigns and health education are needed to promote proper hygiene practices, disease prevention measures, and the importance of seeking timely medical care.This project seeks to address these pressing needs by improving disease surveillance systems, strengthening the capacity of healthcare workers, enhancing community awareness, and fostering better coordination among key stakeholders. Through these interventions, the project aims to reduce morbidity and mortality from disease outbreaks in these vulnerable communities.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1: Capacity-Building Workshops for Health Educators, Health Workers, and Community Health Volunteers• Organize training sessions for at least 200 health workers and 150 community health volunteers on Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) protocols across Borno and Adamawa States.• Focus on early warning systems, disease surveillance tools, data collection, and reporting mechanisms to equip at least 352 participants with the necessary skills for effective disease monitoring.• Provide training on infection prevention and control (IPC) practices specific to conflict-affected settings, ensuring that at least 90% of trained health workers demonstrate improved knowledge and understanding of IPC.Activity 1.2: Identification of Sentinel Surveillance Sites• Identify and establish at least 30 sentinel surveillance sites in high-risk communities within Borno and Adamawa States.• Equip all identified sites with necessary tools and infrastructure, including disease monitoring tools, ensuring 100% of the sites are operational and ready to monitor disease trends effectively.• Ensure real-time data collection and analysis systems are in place, with all 30 sites using proper record-keeping and reporting mechanisms..Activity 1.3: Conduct Community Awareness Campaigns on Disease Prevention• Launch sensitization campaigns that reach at least 100,000 community members across both states, focusing on hygiene, sanitation, vaccination, and disease prevention.• Utilize radio programs, posters, and community meetings to disseminate health messages to at least 75% of households in the high-risk areas.• Engage 50 local leaders and 30 religious/community groups to actively participate in awareness efforts, ensuring 60% of the target population demonstrates improved knowledge of disease prevention.Activity 1.4: Supply of Disease Surveillance Equipment and Tools• Provide essential surveillance equipment to 50 health facilities, including thermometers, personal protective equipment (PPE), and disease reporting forms, ensuring all facilities are fully equipped.• Procure and preposition medications, supplies, and other consumables at 80% of target facilities to ensure preparedness.• Support the monthly distribution of health commodities to 50 health facilities and 10 response points, aiming for 100% distribution on time.• Distribute health education materials (pamphlets, posters) to 150 health facilities and 100 communities, reaching 70,000 individuals directly.Activity 1.5: Support for Mobile Health Clinics and Rapid Response Teams• Deploy mobile health clinics to reach 20 hard-to-reach communities, serving an estimated 50,000 conflict-affected individuals with essential medical services.• Establish 10 rapid response teams for immediate deployment to suspected outbreak sites, aiming for 100% response within 24-48 hours of outbreak alerts.Activity 1.6: Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Activities• Conduct monthly monitoring visits to at least 40 health facilities and 15 communities, ensuring that 90% of surveillance activities are on track.• Collect feedback from all trained health workers and community volunteers, targeting at least 70% satisfaction with the training and response efforts.• Analyze data from disease surveillance systems and field reports, leading to quarterly program adjustments based on the findings.Activity 1.7: Reporting and Documentation• Prepare periodic reports (monthly and quarterly) documenting progress across all target communities and facilities, including any outbreaks detected, with 100% compliance in report submission deadlines.• Submit reports to relevant authorities and stakeholders, ensuring 90% of challenges identified are addressed in subsequent action plans.• Promoting safe water sanitation and hygiene practices for healthier communities in Borno and Adamawa State.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

For a project like managing disease outbreaks in conflict-affected regions (such as Borno and Adamawa), implementing robust mechanisms to prevent and address sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) is critical to ensure that the project maintains a zero-tolerance policy towards abuse, protects vulnerable populations, and creates an environment of safety, trust, and accountability.A well-designed communication strategy ensures that all stakeholders are aware of the PSEA policy, understand the risks of exploitation and abuse, and know how to report violations if they occur. The communication strategy should include: Clear Messaging on Zero-Tolerance for SEA: A central message throughout the project will emphasize the project's zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse. This message will be communicated to all project staff, partners, and community members through various platforms, such as community meetings, workshops, printed materials (e.g., posters, leaflets), and public service announcements. Awareness-Raising Campaigns: PSEA-focused awareness campaigns will be implemented to educate both staff and the community about what constitutes sexual exploitation and abuse, the risks of SEA in emergencies, and the consequences for perpetrators. These campaigns will use community radio, local language materials, and visual aids to reach illiterate populations.Specific messaging will be tailored for women, children, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups to ensure they understand how to recognize and report SEA, as well as the protections available to them. For example, posters with pictorial depictions of abuse and how to report it may be used in communities where literacy is low.
