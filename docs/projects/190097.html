Project Summary

FHI 360's response to the emergency in Cabo Delgado is through an integrated approach for (WASH, Health and Protection). The project areas are Metuge and Ancuabe Districts. The IRAMP project is supporting IDPs and host communities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

• The IRAMP project targets IDPs and host communities and especially the most vulnerable persons. FHI 360 shall use the vulnerability assessment tools,report,vulnerability selection criterion to identify project participants. FHI 360 shall involve the CCCM governing structures in making decisions on who should benefit from the project and who shouldn’t. • FHI 360 shall use the existing CFM mechanisms setup by IOM CCCM to address complaints and shall conduct PDM incase of distributions of WASH NFI Kits.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

• The current humanitarian response for affected people in Metuge and Ancuabe has a gap in addressing the needs for IDPs and host communities that have been affected by the crisis. FHI 360 intends to scale up emergency water sanitation and hygiene program for IDPs and host communities in Metuge and Ancubae to bridge the wash gaps between the IDPs and host communities because of the over used WASH infrastructures especially water points, sanitation facilities. The IRAMP project is focusing on the the following emergency wash activities for Metuge and Ancuabe resettlement camps.• Procurement and distribution of 900 wash kits to the IDPs.• Construction of 9 household latrines in Nantove and Cujupane resettlement camps.• Hygiene promotion and distribution of IEC materials• Rehabilitation and upgrades of water system• Installation of hand washing facilities.• Solid waste collection and management in Ancuabe and Metuge Camps.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

FHI 360 follows the IASC’s commitments on Accountability to Affected Population. In that, FHI 360 will establish the following AAPs mechanisms; • Establishments of leadership and Governance of the project:• FHI 360 will ensure feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into project strategies,monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings and performance management.• Participation: FHI 360 will enable affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately. • Ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence.• Transparency: FHI 36o0 will conduct its project activities in Metuge and Ancuabe with utmost transparency by Providing accessible and timely information to affected populations on organisational procedures, structures and processesthat affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organization and its affected populations over information provision.• Feedback and Complaints: FHI360 will actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robustenough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respondto and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy andstakeholder dissatisfaction. Specific issues raised by affectedindividuals regarding violations and/or physical abuse that mayhave human rights and legal, psychological or other implicationsshould have the same entry point as programme-type complaints,but procedures for handling these should be adapted accordingly.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

• FHI 360 will adapt a holistic progamming approach to mainstream protection into this emergency WASH project. FHI 360 will pay special attention to the following protection areas: • Equitable access to WASH services and information: Emergency WASH services, whether provided in IDP sheltering sites or to the public in target areas, will be provided equitably. WASH services delivery points will be chosen within sheltering sites and in host communities so that all inhabitants have access to the services. FHI 360 will closely engage with vulnerable groups (women of reproductive age with children under the age of five) through female activistas to ensure that water access points meet their needs. This provides the opportunity to integrate key messages effectively in a culturally appropriate way and for FHI 360 to obtain a clear understanding of motivations and barriers to the practice of positive hygiene behaviors. • Addressing Safety, Dignity and Unique Needs: To ensure physical safety, FHI 360 shall consult with IDPs to identify safe places for implementing community-based activities. FHI 360 will also ensure that delivery of services is provided during the day and earlier than the current government-mandated curfew, so individuals will not be put in harm's way to obtain WASH services. FHI 360 will work with UNFPA and other relevant agencies to ensure that the unique needs of men, women, children, and other vulnerable groups are addressed in a dignified manner. This involves the inclusion of dignity kits into the NFI distribution, the creation of awareness on menstrual hygiene management, and providing convenient and gender-segregated sanitation services. • Prioritize Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm: To minimize unintended impacts of proposed WASH interventions, FHI 360 will conduct at the outset of program a Do No Harm assessment in each IDP sheltering sites that will be embedded into the baseline survey. To that end, FGDs will be conducted with different demographic and socio-economic groups. Additionally, further analysis of stakeholders and how they relate to specific activities will be conducted. In terms of ensuring safety, dignity, and privacy, FHI 360 will adopt service standards that will fit the local context. For instance, the inclusion of toilets that will enable women and other vulnerable groups the ability to make use of services safely, with privacy, and without shame. • Meaningful Access: The proposed interventions are delivered at multiple service delivery platforms, including at the health institution, village, and household levels. Where feasible and appropriate, FHI 360 will use inclusive designs to improve access to services for the disabled. The WASH component will also address other aspects of access, such as designating gender-separated access to sanitation, particularly at the IDP sites. At the community level, communities getting trucked water will be made to have a voice in deciding where the offloading points should be. • Participation and Empowerment: FHI 360 will promote empowerment and participation through organizing IDP sheltering site community-based institutions and community-based management. FHI 360 will conduct a series of community consultations on where and how their water delivery points and latrines should be located and oriented.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

• FHI 360 clearly understands that disability and poverty inevitably reinforce each other. Poor nutrition, and living conditions, limited access to health care, poor hygiene, bad sanitation, inadequate information about the causes of impairment, war conflict are some of the main causes of disability. FHI 360 will strive to ensure that: • Inclusion of the specific needs of people with disabilities in terms of access and use of WASH facilities and services.• Advocacy and support to policy development and capacity building of WASH staff in recognizing and responding to the specific needs of people with disabilities.• Pay specific attention to disability in WASH in school programmes: Effective WASH in Schools programmes seek to remove barriers by promoting inclusive design. Toilets and washstands, for example, need to be customized to meet the wide range of needs of children with disabilities.• Appropriate consultation with people with disabilities in developing/designing WASH interventions at household level, for institutions/schools/hospitals, camps of internally displaced people (IDP).• Addressing issues of self-reliance and dignity of people with disabilities in access to suitable WASH facilities and services.• Monitoring, evaluating and reporting of WASH programmes with a specific attention to people with disabilities so that appropriate steps can be taken to fulfil the rights of people with disabilities in WASH programmes.
