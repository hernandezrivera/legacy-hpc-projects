Project Summary

CINA will work in the proposed states to provide protection services to children and their families affected by conflict, including those fleeing violence from Sudan. In Lakes State (including Yirol East & Awerial counties), IDP children and families continue to be exposed to different forms of violence as a result of incidents of inter-communal violence and competition over scarce water resources in the camps and other outlying areas. IDP concentration remains high in Mingkaman (Awerial) with many conflict affected children facing psychosocial problems. CINA’s active caseload for Awerial is 145 UASC (76 boys and 69 girls). Therefore, CINA is expected to respond to increasing needs of UASC due to population movement from Jonglei. In Upper Nile, the population of returnee children from Sudan (estimated 440,000 persons) is more than the number of residents. Schools and residential areas are overstretched. CINA will meet the needs of children and families through psychosocial support and case management interventions. Similarly, support will be tailed to reception and resettlement and reintegration of returnees through support at Transit centers in Malakal, Renk and Juba. In Jonglei, CINA will provide support to children and families affected by communal violence in Fangak, Pigi, Pibor, Akobo, Uror, Ayod and Duk counties. Through community negotiated mechanisms, women and children returned from abduction requires reception and reintegration services to enable them reintegrate into their communities. In Eastern Equatoria, the Lafon and Kapoeta East are amongst the under-served, marginalized and hard-to-reach communities with immense child protection issues such as child marriage, violence and lack of education. CINA will work in these communities to support resilience and reintegration into education, livelihood and youth empowerment.

1. Needs

CINA will address critical needs of children and their families, as well as other caregivers within the selected project locations based on priorities and needs identified through the Humanitarian Needs Overview by the Protection Cluster and Child Protection Area of Responsibility. The following interventions shall be implemented during the project period: 1. In line with objective 1 and protection cluster needs analysis for 2024, CINA will provide case management services to unaccompanied and separated children and other highly vulnerable children. Using the interagency case management SOPs, CINA proposes to provide comprehensive sets of protection packages and services to children separated from their families and other highly vulnerable children such as orphans, children living/working on the streets, children with disabilities, children and youth gangs, children formerly associated with armed groups, etc. The services, to be provided by trained case workers that will be drawn from the affected communities, and who must be youth and adolescents themselves, will include family tracing and reunification, reintegration packages, enrolment in schools and referral to other services in nutrition, livelihood and economic empowerment. Throughout the four prioritized states, CINA will reach at least 2,500 children with case management services. This represents at least 4 percent of the overall target by the CP AOR. This number includes those children to be reached with family tracing and reunification services as well as placement and support in family and institutional (interim care center) based care arrangements. Children in need of specialized services and complex cases shall be supported through the inter-agency case management process and referral system. In Jonglei and Upper Nile states, CINA has existing projects (funded until April 2024) where at least 70 case workers have been trained and deployed. This technical capacity will boost project implementation in those locations. 2. In order to address the psychological needs of conflict affected families, CINA will provide psychosocial support services through the community-based systems as well as through static child friendly spaces and mobile interventions programming. In locations where displacements due to communal violence is persistent, CINA will set-up and operate mobile teams to provide integrated package of child protection assistance including psychosocial support, psychological first aid, case management and capacity building of local actors. CINA will deploy existing pool of trained social workers/child protection community volunteers to run activities in remote and hard-to-reach locations. In Duk and Malakal, for example, CINA is operating child and adolescent friendly spaces where children – including returnees in the case of Malakal – are benefiting from ‘modelled’ child protection services being offered by trained community workers and youth inspirators. In collaboration with UNICEF and State Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Worker, CINA will build capacity of social welfare workforce in the prioritized states. 3. Through community networks that will be strengthened by social workers and child protection workers, CINA will create awareness on the child protection situation at community levels. A total of 2500 sessions (representing 5% of the CP AOR target) in the selected priority counties. Similarly, CINA will participate and facilitate capacity building efforts of community-based networks, social workers, teachers, and caregivers and non-child protection workers in various sectors to help in the preventing and addressing child protection needs in their communities.

2. Project activities

1. Provision of case management services through family tracing and reunification services, placements in foster and or institutional care arrangements, case management packages for complex cases and referral to other services for specialized support; 2. Provision of mental health and psychosocial support services through community-based systems, child and adolescent friendly spaces and mobile interventions in hard-to-reach locations; 3. Creating awareness among conflict affected communities to prevent and address child rights violations; 4. Facilitating capacity building efforts for community-based networks, social workers, youth and adolescent networks, teachers, religious leaders and non-traditional child protection workers in health, nutrition, WASH and education to foster enabling environment for protection of children in their communities; 5. Provision of live-saving integrated child protection services to children and families fleeing the violence in Sudan, at the northern counties of Renk, Fashoda, Malakal Panyikang and Baliet.
