Project Summary

Under this proposed action, ACTED aims to assist 297 households (1,455 individuals) with emergency shelter interventions. This action is in line with the Shelter Cluster’s Objectives (2) " Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacities," through the shelter rehabilitation and winterization for the most vulnerable HHs in Area C and East Jerusalem (EJ). Shelter rehabilitation for 235 vulnerable HHs in Area C, will focus on the following 15 communities in 2 governorates:
Hebron: Halaweh, Jinba, Beit Ummar, Khirbet al Fakheit
Jerusalem: Wadi Sneysel, Abu Shusheh Bedouins, Ma'azi Jaba', Wa’ar Al-Baik, Wadi Al-Hindi, Abu Nuwar, Abu George Road Bedouins, Dahiyat al Aqbat Bedouins, Anata, Al Khalayleh Bedouins, Jaba'.
In addition, 62 HHs will be supported with the rehabilitation of their sub-standard shelters in the most vulnerable communities of EJ. The Palestinian Housing Council (PHC) will implement this activity in the following 15 communities: Beit Hanina, Shofat, Sheikh Jarrah, Wadi Al-Joz, Al-Swaneh, Al-Tur, the Old city, Ras Al-Amoud, Silwan, Al-thouri, Jbal Al-Mokaber, Sourbaher, Beit Safafa, Um-Tuba, Issawieh.
As technical lead for natural and man-made disasters response within the West Bank Protection Consortium and multiple projects previously implemented in the shelter sector, ACTED will benefit from the community knowledge and experience on the most sustainable and quality shelter solutions and rehabilitation methodologies. The long-standing experience of the PHC in the complex context of EJ, will ensure tailored implementation approach for the most vulnerable HHs. Based on its coordination with local Community Based Organisations (CBOs), notably in EJ through the Child Protection Coordination Committee, 10 CBOs in target areas will receive capacity-building trainings, to ensure protection mainstreaming within communities, communications with communities, and accountability to affected populations. CBOs in EJ will coordinate directly with the PHC, with the support of ACTED, while CBOs in Area C will coordinate directly with ACTED.
The project will focus on the following activities:
1. Shelter needs assessment in Area C and EJ targeted communities
2. Shelter rehabilitation in 15 communities in the 2 governorates Area C
3. Shelter rehabilitation in 15 communities of EJ
4. Strengthening of 10 CBOs in EJ and Area C
The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with specific focus on vulnerable groups (women, girls, boys, PWDs, older people). Gender and age principles will also be mainstreamed, adapting each step of the implementation to the needs of specific categories. During the need assessment, special focus will be provided to assess needs of older people, women and PwD, in order to plan a tailored shelter rehabilitation response plan. ACTED will ensure that those categories have the possibility to express their needs during the assessment thus ensuring that during the planning and rehabilitation phases a protected and adapted place and time where the field team can reach them. As PwD are sometimes prevented from expressing their needs, the project team will conduct HH-level visits to assess their specific needs and explain the purpose of the project. PwDs and their caregivers will be consulted to better understand their safety and dignity concerns. AAP will be core to the proposed Action by giving account to affected populations.
Across all activities, BNFs will be made aware of ACTED’s complaint response mechanism (CRM) and PSEA awareness leaflets (designed in collaboration with the PSEA network) will be distributed. Any outstanding needs detected all along the different phases of the project, especially protection-related ones (PSEA, MHPSS, GBV) will be referred to sectoral partners.

1 - Needs

In the West Bank, Israel’s prolonged military occupation, illegal settlement expansion and settler violence continue, as well as the demolition of residential, livelihood and service infrastructure (Protection cluster, August 2022). As highlighted in the 2023 HNO, the restrictive and discriminatory planning regime applied by Israel in Area C and East Jerusalem makes it virtually impossible for Palestinians to obtain building permits, impeding the development of adequate housing and infrastructure. It is also estimated in the shelter sector HNO that 72,000 people in the West Bank will need humanitarian assistance in 2023 to access adequate shelter (including basic households supplies), which is an essential component of providing people with a minimum level of privacy, protection and dignity. According to the Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted by REACH and OCHA in 2022, 23.7% of households in the West Bank in fact reported having some kind of shelter damage, defects, or issues at the time of data collection (June 2022), in fact 18.9% of the HHs are in Area C. More specifically, 10.4% of HH surveyed in the WB reported poorly ventilated/ bad smells; 9.2% of HH reported some cracks in the walls; 8.2% of HH reported opening or cracks in the roof. 138 HHs located in Area C interviewed for the MSNA in the West Bank, reported having a standing demolition order against their shelter at the time of the data collection. Furthermore, Israeli settlers’ violence against Palestinians has been on the rise since the beginning of 2022, where 55% of the settler-related incidents resulted in damage, defects, or issues in their shelters (Premiere Urgence Internationale, 2022). In addition, in June 2022, at least 40,476 individuals in Palestinian Bedouin and herding communities in Area C living in extreme and severe conditions in basic shelters like tents, metal shacks and caves, as being substandard and in need of urgent rehabilitation and upgrading (HNO 2023). This proposed project will thus prioritise HHs in need of humanitarian assistance in both Area C and East Jerusalem. Furthermore, the last MSNA particularly emphasized the need to target HHs with PwDs. In fact, HHs assessed to have a member of the HH with a disability were more likely to report their shelter having any kind of damage, defects, or issues (42.1%) than those households without a PwD (21.8%). The most frequently reported types of shelter damage, defects, or issues by households with a member with a disability were some cracks in the walls (20.7%), opening or cracks in the roof (14.9%), and poorly ventilated/bad smells (13.7%). Lack of privacy was reported by 3.7% of HHs with a household member with a disability, compared to 1.2% of HHs with no household member with a disability Based on these assessed needs, ACTED and PHC will prioritise households with a member with disability in their beneficiary selection. In addition, 12,508 individuals living in a female-headed household are experiencing stress, severe or extreme need of assistance in Area C, including 1,694 individuals in extreme need of assistance, while in East Jerusalem 19,869 individuals living in a female-headed household are experiencing stress, severe or extreme need of assistance. As such, this project will also prioritise female-headed households in the beneficiary selection. The proposed intervention was thus designed based on the findings of the MSNA and further informed by PHC’s needs assessments, and requests for assistance received through ACTED’s CRM. In 2022, ACTED carried out consultations with key informants to complement MSNA data and provide the necessary granularity (community members). This was complemented by data collected as part of ACTED’s ongoing humanitarian interventions in the West Bank which highlighted the need for additional shelter assistance, in communities already affected by shelter demolition and natural hazard in which ACTED has been recently providing assistance. PHC regularly conducts needs assessments for its PHC Housing Rehabilitation Program. These assessments depend on: I) technical assessment in sight of minimum shelter standards and II) socio-economic assessment in sight of the needs of women, men, boys, and girls, and the needs of different age groups. In June 2022, PHC conducted two assessments: the first done jointly with the Shelter Cluster vulnerability assessment team and lists from the MoSD, and the second was based on PHC’s waiting lists and request for assistance. The assessments found 220 vulnerable families in urgent need for rehabilitation of their shelters, 180 of them in EJ, and 40 in Area C of Jerusalem Governorate. This assessment notably highlighted that some families lack the safety, health, and comfort requirements due to overcrowded shelters and poor living conditions that affect family members’ privacy and dignity and children’s welfare resulting in the intergenerational transmission of social inequality. This proposed action will thus target the needs of 62 of these pre-identified vulnerable households in EJ. Furthermore, ACTED identified vulnerable communities through the Shelter Cluster vulnerability mapping, the requests for assistance (under its on-going WBPC activities) based on its AMEU’s verification of needs, and finally avoided the duplication of assistance in target communities by removing communities already identified by other NGOs in the shelter pre- submission coordination sheet for the HRP 2023. The proposed action thus responds to the needs identified in the 2023 HNO and considers Shelter priorities, as highlighted for the HRP 2023. In particular, through self-repair approach, the action will ensure that the most vulnerable HHs can meet the minimum shelter standards, to cope with harsh weather and protection concerns, including winterization, summerization, shelters upgrade and rehabilitation, and damaged shelter repairs.

2 - Activities

A1. Shelter needs assessment in area C and EJ targeted communities ACTED will identify 235 vulnerable HHs in area C, by transparent application processes and consultation with village councils and the community, to ensure all community members have access to the application, especially socially and geographically isolated HHs. ACTED technical staff will conduct a socioeconomic/protection vulnerability assessment, with particular emphasis on vulnerability factors and shelter rehabilitation needs. PHC will form an Advisory Committee, which will consist of female and male representatives who acquaint with the areas of the target group and are familiar with their needs and challenges, as well ACTED, Shelter Cluster, and the Ministry of Jerusalem Affairs. The committee aims to support program implementation and participate in BNF selection. PHC has its internal Project Committee that is assigned to determine applicants’ eligibility criteria, tracking and monitoring the project progress. For the EJ BNF selection, PHC will conduct an eligibility check of HH pre-identified in its latest assessment and applications submitted by HHs through transparent application processes. This will be followed by a visit to each HH to conduct a technical assessment and a socio-economic survey. Accordingly, PHC will prepare social and technical reports, with a preliminary BOQ and cost estimate for each case. The Project Committee will study and raise the most vulnerable cases to the Advisory Committee (AC) for the final selection. The AC will establish the final BNFs’ list. Selection processes will focus on female-headed HHs, older people, families with large HH size, and PwDs, and will ensure their needs are assessed during HH visits. Key PSEA messages and ACTED’S CRM will also be shared with communities and HHs during the needs assessments. Act 2. Shelter rehabilitation in Area CFollowing information sessions at community level, to ensure the engagement of local authorities, ACTED will conduct PwD and gender-and age-sensitive focus group discussions, to ensure differentiated needs are considered in activity design. The project team will develop BoQs and workplan with each HH (at least 1 male and 1 female), to ensure gender balance in needs prioritization. Following the signature of agreements, with related BoQ and workplan, and the distribution of a technical manual for shelter rehabilitation including a leaflet on environmental awareness, the 235 selected HHs will be provided with restricted cash assistance (average amount of 16,500 ILS/HH) in 3 instalments (40%, 40%, 20%) to conduct the rehabilitation works through a self-implementation approach. The third instalment (20%) will be a reimbursement of material purchased by HHs, in order to increase HH-level commitment and control over cash usage. Mixed implementation approaches will be available to BNFs: BNFs lacking the capacities and/or technical expertise to conduct their self-implement the rehabilitation works will have the possibility to request assistance from contractors selected following a transparent procurement process. It is anticipated that HHs headed by women, older people, and PWDs will request this technical support. The implementation of the workplan will be monitored through visits to each HH by staff to review progress, provide technical support and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel subsequent instalments. Act 3. Shelter rehabilitation in EJ Based on findings from the verification exercise under A1, a response plan will be drafted for each HH (BoQ and workplan) to ensure selected BNFs’ shelter meet minimum shelter standards. Following validation by committees of the BNF’s final list, orientation meetings will be held and agreements signed with each HH, including their individual response plan. The rehabilitation strategy will focus on adapted solution to not harm HHs, and reduce their exposure to further risks related to legal restrictions from Israeli authorities. The 62 selected HHs in EJ will be provided with conditional cash assistance in 3 to 4 installments. The precise amount will be based on the tailored BoQ but it is estimated to be 24,800 ILS/HH on average. PHC will conduct workshops for BNF, in order to clarify the action methodology, guidelines, CRM, rights, protection risks, and safeguarding measures. Through these workshops, PHC will train BNFs on technical practices for basic repairs, resulting in more energy-efficient environments. PHC will also ensure access to legal aid through proper linkage between BNF and the Information Counselling Legal Assistance, which works both in Area C and EJ. PHC legal advisor will also be available to guide BNF and provide them and the project team with necessary legal support.Activity 4. Strengthening of CBOsA community-based protection approach, established in 2016 by ACTED, will be followed throughout the whole project to ensure solid engagement at community level and to guarantee better respect of the safety and privacy of each individual, especially most vulnerable groups (female-headed HHs, PwD, older people). ACTED and PHC will support 10 CBOs of 5 to 7 members in targeted areas, including at least 30% female members. The capacity of committee members will be strengthened, based on project needs, notably on the main protection-related topics (protection, PSEA, case referrals), climate change awareness/greening mainstreaming, disaster-risk reduction, legal framework of intervention, and project-related topics. Throughout the implementation, ACTED and PHC will prioritize most vulnerable categories, such as PwD, older people, and girls/women, with a particular focus on BNF affected by protection-related concerns. ACTED will ensure that a standard close on PSEA is included in all agreements in accordance with the IASC (Inter-Agency Standing Committee) principles and guidelines.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

As noted in the 2022 MSNA findings, cash assistance is beneficiaries’ preferred method, as 64.2% of households surveyed in the WB reported that they would like to receive aid in the form of cash and voucher assistance. This is particularly accurate for women and PwD, as cash provides them with more choice and flexibility, while safeguarding their dignity. In addition to being a cost-efficient method for humanitarian actors, the multiplier effect associated with injecting cash within the local economy will contribute towards restoring the Palestinian economy. Service provider solution will also be an alternative available to beneficiaries for these activities, notably the rehabilitation work, which require specific technical skills. In this way ACTED/PHC will ensure the quality and effectiveness of the results and ensure a do-no-harm approach for beneficiaries. For this methodology, ACTED/PHC will identify experienced and professional contractors who will be selected through a transparent procurement process thereby ensuring the completion of activities on time whilst fulfilling all technical requirements.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and PHC’s work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that ACTED teams learn from the feedback provided through this process

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address, complaints boxes and directly through ACTED staff during field visits. This plurality of means aims to ensure CRM accessibility to all, including vulnerable groups with specific needs. At the first stage of the project, during the assessment, field officers will provide a CRM card to each BNF, including all communication channels mentioned above, and explanations on the CRM, to ensure that all BNF feel comfortable and in measure to reach it if needed. Female staff, part of the team to which BNF will be able to refer to, will notably ensure female BNF feel safe to complain. AME staff will dedicate specific time to vulnerable groups during field visits to re-explain the CRM and emphasise that BNF can directly complain to them during visits - if one person would not have the capacity to use/access a phone/email address or the complaint box (notably children, PwD, and the elderly). ACTED’s commitment to accountability to affected populations is fully integrated into its recruitment process, staff inductions, trainings and performance management and partnership agreements. All ACTED staff working for the ACTED oPt mission are trained on the Code of Conduct, on the Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Protection Policies. Trainings on PSEA aim to ensure that all staff understand the Code of Conduct and the PSEA policy, their obligation to report any allegation or suspicion and the sanction measures taken for any breach of the Code of Conduct. In early March 2022, all ACTED staff also participated in a refresher session on PSEA organized by the PSEA network, to strengthen their capacities to recognize exploitation and abuse situations, as well as adopt a good behaviour to report. This session also provided an overview of the Interagency Community Complaint Mechanism and its SOPs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). In order to take account of affected populations, the action will also take strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly in regard to designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. In particular, ACTED and PHC will undertake regular visits to the communities and beneficiary HHs to ensure that protection concerns are incorporated throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for women, children and other vulnerable groups. During the assessment, ACTED’s field officer will inform BNF of potential risks and to ensure risks associated with the project are minimized, the project team will conduct assessments tailored to each BNF’s specific needs. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do-No-Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do-No-Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’s Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by all staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; awareness amongst beneficiaries of what is unacceptable behavior and how to report it; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have specific needs and assistance will therefore be adapted accordingly. To this end, ACTED will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and family and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project. Throughout the project, targeted communities and participants will be involved through community awareness sessions and focus groups. This will help to assess the needs of the BNFs in the targeted communities. ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting various verification exercises. For example, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process and all applicants will be notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process through ACTED’s CRM. ACTED will put particular emphasis on informing beneficiaries on the functioning and use of the CRM in the WB, since the last MSNA conducted in June 2022, revealed that only 4.2% of HHs which benefitted from humanitarian aid, reported awareness of how to access a complaint or reporting mechanism. Similarly, to ensure the agency of beneficiaries and further local ownership of project outcomes, the affected population will be actively consulted during the assessments to ensure BoQs and workplans are responsive to their self-identified most pressing needs. Where applicable, at least one male and one female per HH will be consulted to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are considered, as well as those of boys, girls, PwDs, older people and other marginalized persons to ensure assistance reflects their unique vulnerabilities. In addition, CBO members and ACTED staff will be present to ensure the integration of HH concerns into BoQ and workplan design. In the event beneficiaries are dissatisfied with their respective BoQ and/or workplan an appeal mechanism will be offered through the CBOs or ACTED’s CRM. If the CBOs or ACTED/PHC staff consider the request justified, a new BoQ and/or workplan will be produced by a different engineer. To mitigate this, all staff involved in the assessment process will undergo extensive refresher training in protection standards prior to the assessments to ensure equal and impartial access to assistance. Finally, the project will deliberately target the most vulnerable groups for inclusion into the program. For instance, women will be highly encouraged to participate in the CBOs to ensure that membership remains at 30% female at a minimum. Similar efforts will be made to ensure that vulnerabilities do not act as a barrier to accessing aid. For instance, CBOs will conduct HH visits to assist in the completion and collection of applications for the vulnerable (including older people, PwDs) that might be unable to reach the registration site.
