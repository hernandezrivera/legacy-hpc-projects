Project Summary

Under JRP 2022, BRAC’s DRR sector intends to support the development of the institutional and technical capacity in camp-based 46 Disaster Management Committees across 10 Rohingya camps (1E,1W, 2E, 2W, 3,5,6,7, 17, & KRC ) which include membership of Majhees, religious leaders, and Rohingya volunteers as well as representation from vulnerable groups such as elederly, women, and persons with disabilities. In the host community, BRAC will work with 67 Disaster Management Committees (DMCs). The DMCs include Ward Disaster Management Committees (WDMCs), Union Disaster Management Committees (UDMCs), Upazila Disaster Management Committees (UzDMCs) and Pouroshova Disaster Management Committees (PDMCs) from Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila.
The main interventions will emphasize collective approach to strengthen community-based disaster preparedness, response, and recovery model by which disaster management authorities can establish their joint effort before, during and after the disaster event.
This project will embolden awareness and good practices in disaster risk reduction through community wide simulation exercises, training on Skill based SAR (Search and Rescue) and EWS (Early Warning System). These training will incorporate considerations for environmental stability and restoration (Ecosystem based DRR – ECO-DRR) in the local humanitarian and development planning process.
Sustainability will be ensured through capacity building for the implementation of planned preparedness and risk reduction activities at the community level. Engaging youths and ensure participation from gender diversity groups in each project interventions will add to safeguard the protection dynamics of the targeted population considering DRM cycle. Communities will benefit from improved capacity of DMCs through parallel technical assistance (TA) activities (Conduction of Community Risk Assessment, Risk Reduction Action Plan, Disaster Management (Contingency) Plan, Emergency Stockpiling etc.) which ensures effectiveness in disaster preparedness and response including Covid-19 situations. Moreover, in this course of risk and vulnerability assessment climate variables will be added for future mapping to address anticipatory action in both scenarios of disaster impact for long term HDN programming.
BRAC plans to pilot ECO-system based DRR capacity building on disaster management response systems (DMCs) and mitigation interventions (both infrastructural and non-infrastructural) to support livelihood opportunities for vulnerable populations. Also establishing skill based proper networking within the camps and host communities to manage intense disaster response and boost up environmental restoration of resources regarding increased peaceful co-existence to establish protection rights. The project will create provision of Emergency Preparedness and Response activities in Rohingya Camps in Bangladesh addressing environmental, social, economic, and physical vulnerability of different hazards and Covid-19 situations. The project will ensure continuous mobilization of resources and capacities to ensure that both infrastructure and the communities are well prepared. In line with BRAC’s ‘leave no-one behind’. BRAC will ensure that people of both refugee and host communities are consulted and include in all aspect of project- design, planning, implementation and monitoring. BRAC will ensure especially that vulnerable groups of the women, girls, elderly and people with special needs are represented and participate in these activities.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar is prone to different natural disasters due to its geography and topology, frequently hit by cyclones, flash floods, storm surges and landslides and high risks of earthquakes. Since 2017, the presence of a large population of displaced Rohingyas in Cox’s Bazar has transformed the disaster management response system into a complex scenario with respect to aspects of disaster response, recovery and preparedness as rapid changes in geophysical & environmental settings including increased socio-economic vulnerability of both Rohingya and host population . To accommodate the refugees, nearly 6000 acres of reserved hilly woodland were cut down across the two upazilas of Ukhiya and Teknaf in Cox’s Bazar (Change of Vegetation Cover at Rohingya Refugee Occupied Areas in Cox’s Bazar District of Bangladesh: Evidence from Remotely Sensed Data: Rahman et al. September 2019) . Significant changes in land usage by the Rohingya camps and its adjacent areas also degraded the compaction of soil of these hilly areas which has increased landslide risk for people living on the hilly areas, especially; urbanized Bengali communities, indigenous tribal population and the Rohingya refugees. The region is experiencing climate change impacts more than before, especially after the refugee influx. Climate change seems to have prolonged the annual monsoon and cyclone season with heavier than usual rainfall events and landslide incidents during monsoon. In 2021, more than 542 monsoon-related incidents – largely windstorms, slope failures and flooding (ISCG Flash Update #6 Monsoon Response, September 2021), which took the lives of more than 21 people (15 Bangladeshis and 06 Rohingyas)-. More than 200,000 local people were stranded by flooding in Cox’s Bazar and also affected more than 21,000 people and damaged more than 4,000 infrastructure ( Al-Jazeera- 30 July 2021).Due to huge pressure on supply of drinking water, aquifers become shortened and impacted by intrusion of saline water during winter that are the nearest to the sea (UNDP & UNWOMEN March 2018). The extreme climatic conditions have also threatened livelihoods, ecosystem services, food security, public health, human security, and socio-economic development, often in the most vulnerable communities, and disproportionately impacting on those displaced populations. In addition, in the densely populated camps, flammable construction materials (tarpaulin, bamboo) of the shelters result in increased frequency of fire incidents, especially in the dry season. Devastating fire in March 2021 completely or partially damaged over 17,000 infrastructure including houses, community centers, schools and camp management offices and affected over 60,000 people (Massive Fire at Rohingya Camp in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh-ReliefWeb, 23 Mar 2021).Besides, the breakdown of COVID-19 and the following consequences have changed the concept of preparing for response to emergency situations. To counter the multiple hazards Cox’s Bazar is facing, there is a need for an integrated, holistic and gender equitable approach in disaster management response systems (DMCs) and mitigation interventions (both infrastructural and non-infrastructural) to support livelihood opportunities for vulnerable populations. Critically important is the environmental restoration of resources to also ensure increased peaceful co-existence among both communities to establish better protection.
