Project Summary

In line with the South Sudan HRP and cluster objectives 1 and 2, the project will save lives by addressing the acute severe food insecurity of 57,000 conflict and natural disaster affected people in IPC3+ in Jonglei (Pigi/Canal, Bor) and Unity State (Rubkona and Guit) through distribution of food vouchers to 2,500 HH and enhanced and sustained food production through distribution of seeds, crop and fishing kits, training and cash for work to 9,500 HH. In support of nutrition centres, kitchen gardens will be established to improve the nutritional status of people. This will allow severe and acute food insecure people in IPC3+ to meet their immediate food needs whilst allowing them to rebuild their assets. In addition, by improving people's food security, acute malnutrition will be reduced. All the selected locations are affected by different natural and man-made disaster, showing high and multiple vulnerabilities and are priority areas as they are in IPC3 or worse, confirmed by Cordaid assessments. Assessments also showed that women (incl. PLW), children (incl. children under 5), PWD, elderly and chronically ill are impacted differently and are bearing the brunt of conflict, droughts/ floods and COVID-19. The project will therefore target these most vulnerable groups through inclusive assistance, and will ensure that protection, gender and COVID-19 is mainstreamed in the proposed activities.
Protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle to ensure beneficiary safety & dignity, avoiding doing harm as result of the intervention, providing meaningful access to all sets of beneficiaries and ensuring all groups are actively involved and participate in the project in an inclusive manner. Additionally, the project will ensure all necessary Covid-19 measures and protocols will be adhered to; for example, during distributions, beneficiaries will be encouraged to maintain safe social distance, always wash their hands and wear face masks.
Cordaid has been responding to the increasing humanitarian needs in all 4 areas providing WASH, FSL and ES&NFI assistance and is fully operational in the 4 areas with offices in Malakal, Bor and Bentiu.

1 - Needs

The 2021 South Sudan HNO estimates that 8.3 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance this year 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. In addition, 60% of the country’s population will be facing Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3+) levels of food insecurity from April–July 2021 (IPC results). Pigi/Canal, Bor South, Rubkona and Guit Counties are all classified in Emergency (IPC phase 4) acute food insecurity. According to the IPC Acute malnutrition projection analysis, seasonal deterioration of acute malnutrition is expected during the lean season and Rubkona, Bor South & Canal/Pigi are classified in critical phase and Guit county in serious phase (IPC AMN April – July 2021). Additionally, the four locations all lie in WASH vulnerability severity mapping classification 3/4/5 which justifies the underlying situations in the location. On this background, in mid-December, Cordaid conducted a rapid HH level needs and markets assessment in Pigi/Canal, Rubkona and Guit and post distribution monitoring (PDM) in Bor South following its emergency flood response intervention and the findings are in line/conformity with the HNO, IPC results and the WASH severity classification mapping. The assessment found out that all the four proposed areas are showing high levels and multiple vulnerabilities caused by the ongoing conflict/ inter-communal violence and natural disasters such as droughts and floods. Cordaid assessments and PDM showed that HH are demonstrating multiple and high levels of vulnerabilities increasing their protection risks: more than three-quarters of the HH is headed by a female, who is in charge of an average HH size of 10+ people. The majority of the HH includes an elderly person and most of the HH have a PLW and children under the age of 2. In Rubkona 5% of the HH also includes a disabled person and in Guit and Pigi this is much higher, 12% and 15% respectively. On average there are more females than males (52% female, 48% males and more than 60% are children under 18yrs. In Rubkona and Guit most of the assessed population is host community and most of them (85%) had not received any support in the past months. In Pigi 54% are IDPs and most of the respondents to the PDM in Bor were IDPs. On average around 30% - 40% of the respondents indicated that their shelter was damaged.

2 - Project activities

In line with the cluster strategy the project will save lives by providing emergency food assistance (cluster objective 1) which is complemented by livelihood support (cluster objective 2) contributing directly towards the first strategic objective of the HRP. Protection, gender and COVID-19 will be mainstreamed in the activities:1. Distribution of food vouchers or cash for food to 2,500 HHsCordaid will distribute unconditional/restricted cash or food voucher to vulnerable HH in areas in IPC3+ in Pigi/Canal, Bor (Jonglei) and Guit, Robkona (Unity). The approach may vary depending on context and market structure/functionality. In Rubkona, Guit and Pigi markets will not cope with an increase in demand and thus food vouchers will be distributed. Cordaid will select and contract local vendors through a tendering system who will procure and distribute food to selected HH. These HH will redeem their vouchers with these vendors. In Bor Cordaid will distribute cash for food, as the market will be able to meet demands. Beneficiaries will receive USD 100 per HH per month for a period of 3 months. Training of the beneficiaries on food utilisation, including hygiene promotion, use of safe water, appropriate feeding practices, efficient and renewable energy sources and technology for adequate processing, cooking and conservation of food will be included. Selection criteria are based on FSL cluster. Within 4 weeks after distribution a PDM will be conducted.2. Distribution of emergency livelihood inputs (Vegetable, crop seeds and fishing kits) and training for 9,500 HHs in IPC3+Emergency livelihood input will be distributed including distribution of fishing and seeds/ crop kits. Fishing kits: Potential fishing groups will be identified and supported with fishing kits distribution and training on fishing best practices, and on proper fish preparation and storage. Fishing kits will be distributed, each kit consisting of twine (2 spools), monofilament (1 coil) and hooks (2 boxes), following FAO guidelines. Fishing is one of the key sources of income for households in South Sudan and a source of important nutrients.Distribution of vegetable & crop seeds and tool: In close coordination with the ministry of agriculture, we will conduct a seed needs assessment. A Seed germination test will be carried out to determine the viability of the seed before it reaches in the hands of the farmers, to ensure high quality. Following FAO guidelines, each seed kit will include one tool (hoe/maloda), and a variety of crop seeds and vegetable seeds:Crop Kits (10-13 kg) existing of Maize (5kg), Sorghum (5kg) and Cowpea (3kg)Vegetable Kits (7 to 9 varieties including Amarant (20G) , Onion(20G) , Tomato (20G), Eggplant(20G) , Collard (20G) , Pumpkin (30G), Watermelon (30G), Kudra (20G), Carrot (20G) and Okra (50G).Group formation will be facilitated through awareness and guidance from Cordaid and provide training on vegetable growing/production and agronomic best practices for harvesting and marketing. This will improve technique, increase production and income. This activity will also include key nutrition messages to improve the food security situation and the nutrition status of beneficiaries. One month after distribution a PDM will be conducted to obtain feedback from beneficiaries on the items distributed and the trainings conducted.3. Cash for work activities and distribution of conditional unrestricted cash.Cash for work Cordaid will target 700 households mostly areas much affected by the flooding basically in Jonglei (Pigi Canal and Bor) to support repair their Community assets. In agreement with the locally community roads, market drainage systems, dykes and bridges will be repaired. Cordaid will provide tools and following CVWG and FSL guidelines, number of working days and day rate will be determined. A PMD is conducted at the end. 4. Establish kitchen gardens in nutrition centre in Rubkona (Bentiu).

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Food vouchers: Cordaid is proposing to issue food vouchers (USD 100/ hh per month for 3 months period) to complement WFP’s food distribution efforts. Local vendors will be selected through tendering (incl but not limited to visits and quality checks). These vendors will procure and supply the food to selected beneficiaries. This way market functionality will not be jearpordized, and markets will not suffer from the food distribution and will benefit from the assistance as well.Cash for food: In Bor, the market will cope with an increased demand and as such, people will be provided with the same amount in cash. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to use the money for food. Overall, local traders will benefit from the cash assistance. Cash for work: the amounts for cash for work will be small and are complementary to the food assistance. Based upon markets assessments, markets in the proposed areas will be able to cope with small cash injections that are unrestricted and can be used based on people’s needs. Markets in Pigi and Bor will be able to cope with this.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In addition to the HH assessments, Cordaid also conducted market assessments to measure market functionality, prices and feasibility to meet increased demand. The market assessments show that although markets are moderately functional, they are not able to cope with large or increase in demands in Guit, Rubkona and Pigi/Canal. This is why Cordaid is proposing to use food vouchers in combination with local vendors who procure and distribute the food. In Bor, the market will be able to cope with increased demands in food and as such, Cordaid will distribute cash for food.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The Cordaid community communication and engagement plan has a separate section on communication concerning PSEA. PSEA is based on communities preferred ways of communications, assessed by Cordaid. In line with Cordaid's PSEA policy, communities will be informed verbally and in writing concerning expected behaviour of Cordaid staff. For this a specific plan is drawn up - Specific Communications Plan on PSEA:1) Stakeholders – by name if known. (In the department, organisation, partner organisations, wider community, service users?) 2) Purpose of communication3) Key messages to be communicated (To do this, what do they need to know? Which questions should be answered?) 4) Timing of communication (Stages of project, specific times, frequency) 5) How are you going to communicate? (eg routine report, flyer, newsletter, poster, case study, social media, video, blog) 6) Who is responsible? (Who will do the communicating? What do they need to know?) Through the communication plan:- Communities understand their rights, the role of Cordaid, expected behaviour of Cordaid staff and how to make complaints - Ensure appropriate investigations are made and actions are taken, and closing the loop with the complainant. - Cordaid staff, community and other stakeholders understand the Cordaid core principles on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse- Community engagement efforts complement and support protection strategies.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Cordaid will ensure an inclusive feedback system, which will ensure that all have access to it. This is done through face to face meetings, visiting HHs at home, FGDs for PSN and complaints can be filed verbally and in writing through a Cordaid help & complaints desk, community leader or local authority.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Cordaid will design a community communication and engagement plan based on the communities' preferred ways of communication, which was already assessed during the rapid needs assessment. This plan outlines what and how different topics are communicated and how Cordaid is engaging with communities. Based upon information obtained during the assessment Cordaid will provide information at the different stages of the project to the community/ beneficiaries through face-to-face meetings (One-on-one or FGDs), helpdesks and through community leaders/ local authorities. Because of limited phone/ internet possibilities and illiteracy, most of the information is provided verbally:1) At the start project information is provided verbally and on paper, outlining the project purpose, activities, information on the donor, illegibility, rights of beneficiaries and obligations of Cordaid. People will also be informed about Cordaid complaints feedback and response mechanism (CRM), its purpose and how to access it. Expected behaviour of Cordaid staff is also communicated2) During HH assessments and beneficiary selection designated Cordaid staff will again inform communities about issues mentioned under point 1 and a help and complaint desk is established 3) During distribution a help and complaints desk is again put in place. In addition, Cordaid will also follow up with community leaders and local authorities to obtain any feedbank and or complaints. Questions will also be asked during distribution whether people understand the project, eligibility and how to access CRM.4) At last, a PDM will be conducted 2-4 weeks after distribution of food vouchers/ emergency livelihood inputs. In the PDM questions related to the project and CRM are included, to measure to what extend people understood the project and whether the CRM was communicated sufficiently to beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All feedback, collected regularly throughout the project during FGDs, HH interviews, individual meeting and KIIs will be logged and analysed by Cordaid MEL department. Analysis will be shared with the Programme staff and community and discussions held to what extend there is a need, and how the assistance needs to be adapted. Any adaptations are done in consultation of Cordaid and community. Information collected after the distribution through the PDM is used to inform and improve future activities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

As part of Cordaid procedures, at proposal stage a project risk matrix will be completed identifying operational, political, programmatic, reputational, financial and organizational risks. At the start and during the implementation the risk matrix will be reviewed and risks closely monitored. The below identified risks are part of Cordaid programmatic risks:8.1 Risk of beneficiary exposure to violence during registration/ distribution: Likelihood is medium – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid will ensure that all registrations and distribution sites are safe, nearby people’s dwellings and easy accessible for all. People will be hired for crowd control and when desirable, separate male and female distribution sites will be organised. Sites will be selection jointly with the community. In addition, Cordaid is using the PMWG checklist for safe distribution to identify potential risks.8.2 Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid will inform communities about policies and procedures in place such as code of conduct and PSEA. All Cordaid staff are trained in these procedures and policies. This will explain expected behaviour of Cordaid staff and how to report misconduct. To prevent sexual violence by community members distribution sites will be safe and nearby people’s dwellings. Distribution times will also be convenient to make sure that people are home during day time. Community leaders and communities will be asked to protect their women, girls and boys as well. 8.3 Risk of insufficient internal mechanism to combat and prevent child safeguarding: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid as part of the Caritas network has child safeguarding policies in place and staff are trained on these procedures. In addition, in the event children are on their own, Cordaid will make sure they are connected with local leaders and make sure communities look after them.8.4 Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral service: Likelihood is medium – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid will map other and protection actors in the area and establish a referral with these organisations. This information will also be shared with communities and community leaders, raising awareness on available services and referrals.8.5 Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: As part of the assessments, Cordaid organized FGDs about and with people with specific needs but also different age groups, to ensure that their needs were raised and incorporated in the assistance. Cordaid will continue to do this during the project preparations, implementation and also PDM8.6 Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: Likelihood is low – Impact is medium. Mitigation measures: Through the project starting at the first community visits and consultations until the PDM, Cordaid will share its complaints and feedback mechanism and how to access it with communities. Information on the CRM and its importance will be shared verbally and in writing and is based on communities’ preferred ways of communication (informed by assessments).8.7 Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries: Likelihood is low – Impact is medium. 8.8 Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: Likelihood is medium – Impact is high. 8.9 Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity: Likelihood is low – Impact is medium. 8.10 Risk of explosive hazard: Likelihood is low, impact high
