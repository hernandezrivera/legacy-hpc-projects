Project Summary

The proposed intervention is to provide access to immediate basic health and nutrition services to populations in need prioritizing maternal and child health services including immunization, integrated management of childhood illnesses, integrated management of moderate acute malnutrition,skilled delivery and focused antenatal care services. The intended interventions will be delivered through static health centres, mobile health units and at the community level through trained Community Health Workers. Surveillance, early detection and response to outbreaks will be an integral part of the intervention. Services will be provided through fifteen fixed health facilities and six mobile health/nutrition outreaches.

Needs

The absence of a strong central government in Somalia in the last three decades has made health sector dysfunctional, unable to provide basic, long-term quality health services to a population already faced with multitude of challenges including sporadic and often deadly conflicts leading to death and large-scale displacements and recurrent disasters such as floods, cyclones and droughts. The health sector is acutely underfunded and service delivery entirely in the hands of none governmental organizations with the federal government providing minimal support in coordinating interventions and ensuring standards and patient safety are maintained. As a result, Somalia has one of the worst health outcomes in the continent. According to UNICEF Somalia (Situation analysis of children in Somalia 2016), children and women face more health challenges in Somalia than in almost any other of the world’s countries. The under-five mortality rate (U5MR) is 137 per 1,000 live births and the maternal mortality ratio (MMR) at 732 per 100,000 live births.UNOCHA estimates that about 5.4 million people need immediate assistance. 2.7 million of those of are in need urgent life-saving assistance. Malnutrition is at a critically high level with over 1.2 million children are projected to be malnourished. FSNAU-FEWS NET 2018 post Gu technical release, notes general even improvement in nutrition situation, high levels of acute malnutrition persist in several population groups due to underlying/structural causes with GAM rate estimated at 14.0% and SAM prevalence at Critical (≥4-5.6) among Mogadishu Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) settlement and in Guban pastoral livelihood. In the target regions of Hiran and Lower Shebelle, access restriction due to insecurity and limited humanitarian support puts the population at a more vulnerable position. According to reports from Facilities operated by WARDI, pneumonia, diarrhea and malaria are the leading causes of death in children U5 years.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The target areas for the project are safe and accessible currently to WARDI staff. WARDI will liaise with the local authorities and community leaders from project design, inception, implementation and evaluation to promote ownership and enhance sustainability. the facilities targeted for service provision are majority government-owned while health workers recruitment will be done by the ministry of health at regional levels. District and village level health committees will be formed and strengthened where in existence for the purpose of them being part of the entire health system and playing a critical role in enhancing community ownership of the project.WARDI will conduct health systems strengthening activities which will strive to build the capacity of the government line ministries with the ultimate goal of handing over the health facilities to continue the provision of health services once the project ends.Staff of the project will be recruited from the target communities and community level health workers such as CHWs incorporated into the project implementation enhancing sustainability of the project.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The overall project goal is to reduce the disease burden for the vulnerable members of the society. The project aims at bringing services closer to the community so that the distances in search for health and nutrition services is reduced which by extension reduces the risks for the community. The project will focus more on internally displaced persons who are disadvantaged having displaced from their original homes. The health and nutrition services will be available to the IDPs within their camps free of charge.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women in Somalia are mostly disadvantaged in terms of decision making on issues that impact their health and nutrition services. The project is geared towards gender specific with the focus mainly on women, boys and girls as well as the general population. Even though services will be provided to all community members, women of reproductive age, boys and girls will be targeted for both health and nutrition services. To ensure their voices are heard and that they are not disadvantaged, WARDI will organize community meetings, focused group discussions where women will be met differently to articulate the issues affecting them and how to discuss it.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Security in Somalia is dynamic and fluid at best. One of the main security risks facing the targeted population is the risk of injury following unintended explosions while in other areas such as Hiraan and Lower Shebelle, the risk of clan conflicts exists which flares up once in a while. With this in mind, WARDI has been working in these target areas for a long time and has developed a cordial working relationship with the communities. WARDI will liaise with the local authorities on a daily basis to get updates so that no harm comes to the beneficiaries and staffs working in the project areas. WARDI will also ensure that health and nutrition services are available in areas that are close to the target beneficiaries to prevent them from walking long distances in search of these services and hence reduce the risks of violence. Staffs and volunteers will be trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) so that those with direct contact with patients and beneficiaries ensure no sexual exploitation and abuse doesn't occur.
