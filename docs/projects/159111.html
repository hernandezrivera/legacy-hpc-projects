Project Summary

Despite massive humanitarian assistance and resilient intervention carried out through the Somalia, food security and nutrition expected be worsened in the second half of 2019. Pastoral and agro-pastoral populations in Sanaag and Togdheer has experienced food insecurity gaps resulted by dry conditions as a result of the below average Deyr season in 2018 combined with harsh dry season (Jilaal) followed by a delayed and erratic GU rains of 2019. According to FSNAU-FEWS NET report (2 Sept 2019) in total, 6.3 million Somalis will face acute food insecurity through the end of 2019. An estimated 2.1 million out of 6.3 classified (IPC Phases 3 & 4) and 4.2 million Somalis are in IPC 2 (Stressed). Moreover, an estimated 1million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through mid-2020.
Due to multiple reasons many rural families in Sanaag and Togdheer regions are at a threshold point, which is beyond their ability to recover from shocks is becoming severely contentious. According to FSNAU/FEWS NET (2019) the population classified crisis or worse in Sanaag and Togdheer regions are estimated 198,000, and 58,000 people are in Emergency while 462,000 people are in stressed situation. Those large number of population require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, eradicating acute malnutrition, saving lives and protecting and saving livelihood assets.
To take immediate response the proposed project will provide immediate life-saving relief assistance through unconditional in-kind/ cash-based transfer to 700 vulnerable households in crisis and emergency (IPC 3 and 4) to access essential food items for four months. Likewise, the project provide restocking for 250 vulnerable HHs for 11 herds per households that makes 2,750 herds. Water trucking for additional 42,000 animals benefited 600 pastoral households in target areas will be supported by the project. Complementary cash programming (e.g. “Cash+”) will enable households to cover food consumption gaps while avoiding the distress sale of livestock to afford food and non-food items.
Furthermore, the project will provide emergency agricultural support to 500 agro-pastoral households to secure harvest in the upcoming cropping seasons (Gu and Deyr rainy seasons in 2020). This includes provision of quality drought-tolerant seed varieties to replenish depleted seed stocks, farm tools, land preparation, rehabilitation of irrigation canals and extension services. Complementary cash programming will cover immediate food needs during the lean season (i.e. before the harvest), while contributing to prevent further displacement. Cash transfer is highly crucial in order people to access food and non-food items immediately without disturbing market functionality. There are facilities for goods availability in some locations of project target areas. ADO will have effective coordination with CWG in Somaliland. 70,000 Lm of contour bunds will be constructed through conditional cash transfer in pasture and agriculture degraded land to harvest rain water and 333 Hh will be get employment for 3 months. 4 earth dams will be rehabilitated by cash for work approach. In total the project will create 120 temporary jobs for 90 days that will enhance household’s resilience and income opportunity. In total the project will support 2,503 HHs (Est. 15,018 individuals of which, 3,454 are Men, 3,755 women, 4,055 girls and 3,754 boys). The project is line with food security cluster target regions and aims to reduce food insecurity gaps for people in need both IDPs, and host communities including people with disabilities.

Project Activities

Provision of food/cash voucher for 700 vulnerable households through voucher value of USD 105 for the period of 4 months. Food items will consist 50 kgs of rice, 25 kgs of flour, 5 kgs of cooking oil, 12.5 kgs of sugar, 2 kgs of dates, 1 tin of milk powder. 2,268 will be female while 1,932 will be male beneficiaries. Priority will be provided disable people, women and elder people. 2. Supply for restocking for 250 poor pastoral Households. 2,750 healthy and young goats will be purchased and distributed for 250 target pastoral households who lose their livestock herds. 130 will be female pastoral HH’s while 120 will be male. Priority will be provided disable people, women and elder people. 3. Provision of Cash voucher for 250 pastoral households to enable to access food and nonfood items for restocking families. 4. Supply 3,560 water voucher system to 53,400 domestic livestock population owned by 600 HHs (average 70 heads/HH) in Ceelafweine and Burao districts of Sanaag and Togdheer regions. Priority will be provided disable people, women and elder people. 6. Distribution of Agricultural input packages consisting land preparation through provision of tractor hours, certified seeds, rehabilitation of flood irrigation system for 500 HH’s in Marawade, Ceeldhaliile, and Jiidali of Erigavo district and Ximan and Qoyta of Burao district. 280 are male while 220 are women headed households beneficiaries. Priority will be provided disable people, women and elder people. 7. Construction of 70,000 linear meters of soil contour bunds in agricultural Land through cash for work approach in Marawade and Qoyta and Ximan of Erigavo and Burao Districts. 333 casual labors will be get employment opportunity for period of 3 months. 190 workers will be female while the 143 workers will be male. Priority will be provided disable people, women and elder people. 8. Rehabilitation of 4 earth dams by removing deposited sands in the dams through cash for work approach engaged 120 casual labors for the period of 90 days. 52 casual labour will be male while 68 will be female workers. Priority will be provided disable people, women and elder people. 9. Conduct 4 farmer training workshops attending by 65 farmer trainees in project target areas of Sanaag and Togdheer regions. Priority will be provided disable people, women and elder people.

Mitigation Measures

2a> 1. Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to FSL needs. Different criteria may affect the power dynamics. 2. Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalised persons to collect accurate information about their specific food and nutritional requirements and preferences for food distribution points and livelihood services.3. Reinforce the capacity of the community - encourage collective livelihood projects over those that benefit individuals. Collective practices contribute to community protection and resilience, particularly in rural community economies which are closely linked with social networks, community structures and cultural practices. 4d. 1. Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organisational procedures and processes that affect them.2. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. place feedback surveys in food baskets, feedback box at distribution site, phone lines.3. FSL staff and relief committees should be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members should receive protection mainstreaming training. 4b> Protection mainstreaming 1. Collect and use data disaggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming.2. Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups (refer to Vulnerable Categories guidance note).3. Apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries – otherwise vulnerable persons, such as older people, may easily be excluded as other more mobile and vocal population groups are registered.
