Project Summary

Gender Based Violence (GBV) remains a serious concern particularly for women and girls in Somalia which in most cases is escalated by state of insecurity. Due to the prolonged protracted conflict, protective environment is weakened for the internally (IDPs) and civilians. (UNOCHA Report on IDP Assessment 2019). Forced eviction coupled with unrelenting pervasive GBV trends subjects the most vulnerable in the community especially women and children and persons living with disability to further violation of rights. During these displacements, the civilians are fearful of the explosives on the conventional routes therefore use non-conventional routes that expose women and children to sexual violations including rape. Key risks for children are family separation, child recruitment to armed groups and arbitrary arrest which then exposes them to forms of GBV. Elevated exposure to GBV including early marriage, trafficking, psychosocial distress, a lack of access to education, and hazardous child labour also affect children (OCHA, 2020)
Shocks experienced this year have increased protection needs. Some of those mostly affected are : the disabled, adolescent girls and boys, pregnant and lactating women and girls, female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children, persons from minority clans, older persons without caregivers, persons missing documentation, persons facing eviction, persons living in areas with armed conflict and violence.
Inequality between groups persisted in 2020 which aggravated most contributing factors further increasing protection needs. COVID 19 containment measures, especially among IDP, caused loss of livelihoods which in turn heightened the protection environment with an increase in gender based violence cases in families.
Gender based violence affecting women and girls remains underreported but widespread, with IDPs remaining more vulnerable. Data indicates that GBV occurs while in transit to IDP settlements or to receive humanitarian assistance, while exposure is especially high in IDP sites. IDPs from minority clans are reported to be more frequently affected allegedly due to outsiders intruding into IDP sites taking advantage of their status and instilling fear causing under or no reporting.
The proposed project will be geared towards addressing gender inequalities in protection through identification, reporting and response of scenarios of sexual exploitation and abuse against women and children.
The proposed project will be achieved the following: To improve access to timely basic services to survivors of sexual and gender based violence with special focus to health and psycho social support to women and children and elderly persons , to strengthen community based structures to mitigate and respond to women and children safety issues, with focus to sexual and gender based violence, to integrate protection in projects from other clusters, to train all staff on GBV identification and referral and PSEA and to strengthen resilience and coping mechanism for women and children against sexual and gender based violence.
Proposed interventions will also include all gender groups, marginalized groups and the disabled who are often left out or given unequal treatment. Disabled will participate at all levels of the project cycle. Those who qualify will be employed in projects. Through sensitization, community will be encouraged to report GBV against the disabled and report incidences.Staff will be trained on disability inclusion as to to eliminate discrimination. All data will be disaggregated according to gender, age and disability.
SAF UK is currently running health and nutrition program in Kismayo, covering IDP,protection will be integrated with health providing post rape clinical management in all health facilities. In Kahda SAF UK’s health project ended but we hope to renew soon and integrate protection activities in all health facilities and project activities.

Project Activities

-Conduct trainings on basic psychosocial support first aid to women and men on a ratioof 2:1, with special focus to sexual and gender based violence and post rape caremanagement -Provide basic training on post rape care management skills to healthworkers in the health facilities and including clinical management of rape cases targetingthe vulnerable members of the family. -Provide training to the health record workers/registrar on rape case data management and entry to the GBVIMS ( gender ratio of 2:1 female to male) -Conduct a gender sensitive community driven mapping exercise to identify critical referral points to aid in development of reporting and referral pathway in the IDP settlement areas -Hold stakeholders forums including the women safety and child protection team, the community psycho social support team, the health officers, local authorities, GBV and protection clusters IDPs representative and other referral points representative to disseminate and validate the reporting and referral pathway. -Hold women and children safety audit in the two locations in order to identify women andchildren safety issues and protection concerns, trends and hot spot points for sexual andgender based violence and other human rights violations against women and childrenwithin the IDP settlements -Conduct mass media campaigns through local radio stations on the need to protect women and children against sexual and gender based violence - Provide psychosocial support, including counselling for victims of sexual violations including rape -Clinically manage post rape victims in 6 health facilities

-6 basic psychosocial support first aid trainings conducted -4 post rape care managementtrainings conducted to health workers in the health facilities -4 trainings on rape case data management and entry to the GBVIMS for health record workers/ registrar -3 gender sensitive community driven mapping exercises conducted -3 stakeholders forums conducted -2 women and children safety audits conducted -300 victims of sexual violations provided with psychosocial support -175 rape survivors clinically managed for various condition in health facilities

Mitigation Measures

For IDP in Mogadisho the main risk is displacement. for this the organisation hasmaintained a database for all beneficiaries with telephone contacts and that ofrelatives. Programs will also ensure IDP have access to basic services in an equitable and dignified manner with equality also observed in job allocation in projects serving IDP population. Among IDP the risk of exclusion based on tribe is real. Most IDPs in Kismayo are from a certain community who found it hard to access services in the recent past based on their tribe and skin color. The organisations' policy does not allow exclusion and actuallyreaches out to all community members to receive services. Recruitment of staff is notbased on tribe but professional competence. This has produced staff from a hybrid ofcultures who have learned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved tothe target communities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs andwomen so that equity is achieved. All gender will be awarded opportunity to benefit inequitably. Women and youth who are mostly left out in recruitment will be consideredin all projects and positions will be awarded equitably. Needs assessment will includeall community groups so that an all conclusive, agreeable and fair assessment isreached. both men and women leaders with an additional youth representative will bepart of community health board as per organisational policy. This will ensuredecisions made are all inclusive. SAF UK policies have sought to put men at the helmof needs affecting their wives, daughters and female relatives. The organisation's APPframework seeks to ensure men participate through affirmative action for menensuring that men are not excluded from interventions targeting women beneficiaries.Children who are often left out will not only be seen as beneficiaries but their views onservice delivery ad need will occasionally be sought by a professional child specialistso that the whole community participates in program design and implementation.Community diagnosis, community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will seek toinclude all community groups to ensure all are reached. Dialogues, evaluations andconversations will include women, children and youth with their own forum so as toensure their views are considered and used to programing. 2020 programs will seek toimprove access to women and girls , who are key beneficiaries of proposedinterventions by scaling up services to be within a few kilometers walk.
