Project Summary

The proposed intervention is envisioned to improve GBV, shelter and NFIs(In Kind) response through increased access to temporary shelter, NFIs ( In kind), GBV services and provision of immediate and comprehensive lifesaving services to survivors, and GBV risk mitigation for vulnerable women and girls through the following interventions: Clinical management of Rape (CMR), case management (CM); psychosocial support services (PSS) including psychosocial counselling, psychosocial first aid (PFA), social networking and access to information on available GBV services, through women and girls friendly spaces/safe spaces, strengthening of GBV referral pathways (to enable access to comprehensive GBV services including legal, medical and livelihood services) and provision of emergency shelters, NFIs to most vulnerable and displaced female headed and people living with disability special need in the target communities, including IDP, returnees and Host communities in the target locations. Alight will also strengthen GBV risk reduction through strengthening and engaging community protection groups, distribution of dignity kits, whistles and solar lanterns to vulnerable women, girls and mobility reaching Aids and Grabbers for PLWD, provide capacity building and trainings to community volunteers/workers and GBV service providers to enhance service provision and risk reduction for women and girls, and conduct safety audits for GBV risk assessments to inform the project implementation and decisions, among other interventions. Proposed project will therefore increase accessibility, quality and coordination of GBV, shelter, NFIs and Disability services for conflict and drought affected communities in Banadir, Middle Shabelle Jubbaland, Puntland and Somaliland regions. Alight will work with various stakeholders to ensure women and girls are protected from GBV and receive support when they experience harm. Some of the stakeholders include line ministries at state government level, the local administration, the police, and women leadership at community level, community and religious leaders and will establish protection networks for women and girls. Community action plans and protection mechanisms will be developed and implemented to reduce protection risks for women and girls including those in IDP settings.
The proposed intervention address Shelter and NFIs ( In Kind, because cash may not help to purchase all needed items from the market) through the immediate material needs of newly displaced populations in Banadir, Middle Shabelle, Jubbaland and Somaliland including, the most vulnerable by conducting timely protection and shelter assessments and distributing the essential emergency kits (emergency shelter/NFI) as well as, referrals of protection and other needs and some limited direct provision of protection services to affected communities, particularly persons with specific needs. The project will collect information in assessments on general access to services issues and also general protection issues, as well as provide information on the protection concerns in other areas such as CP, GBV and HLP in order to guide the AoRs to address those issues and also other general services clusters to respond to access to food or wash issues. Also address the as well as HLP rights of the beneficiaries by providing HLP information services and capacitating committees and government line authorities and other humanitarian actors on HLP issues. The project will assist in capacity building local authorities, IDP committees etc. on IDP rights/obligations. In addition, the project will also aim to mainstream protection across sectors through advice on effective implementation of protection-sensitive and rights - based approach into the relief assistance for other humanitarian actors/ clusters – PWG/All sectors, training and sensitizing duty bearers and other key stakeholder on protection concerns including GBV, and freedom and safety of movement.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

ARC will administer cash based intervention for the survivors, displaced, at risk persons and persons with specific needs through unrestricted cash transfer via commercial agencies modality to cover specific necessities such as food, NFIs, dignity kits, transportation and to cover the needs of their children including school fees and daily livings and medical costs associated with serious illness, human rights violation and physical disabilities, hence, an average of $70 for 300 persons with above category will be supported for two months. The cash will provide the vulnerable persons above with flexibility and total decision for their specific priorities. ARC participates both the sub and National Cash Working Groups (SNCWG) for all the proposed areas and will support the working group in market assessments and evaluation of the cash impact. The NFI and Shelter will be provided as in-kind due to the volume of the supplies and contents of the (Kits) obtained by the POC in the package of in kind is more helpful then the cash, where the cash my be administered to use other purposes or may be controlled by dominant male counterparts who use other then the decided purpose, causing Intimate violence. Therefore, the community will mapped through set vulnerability criteria, The ENFI and Shelter kits will support alleviate the worsened situation of the displaced population affectively and efficiently preventing them from fierce Cold, rains and wild winds. The In-kind support will be accompanied by information dissemination and awareness on how to utilize the kits supported. Alight have worked in this sector in both Somaliland and Jubbaland improving the life of the weak and vulnerable shock effected communities. This proposed Emergency shelter and distribution of Non-Food items (NFIs) HRP plan is based on the operational context and is in line with shelter results framework, which has prioritized the key thematic sectors and strategic objectives to ensure that IDP, returnees’ populations with insufficient basic and domestic items are supported with emergency Shelter and NFIs through rapid protection assessments. The HRP pan addresses the immediate material needs of the displaced populations including, the most vulnerable groups by conducting timely rapid protection assessments and distributing the core relief items which is essential for the emergency effected households. The proposed HRP will collect information in assessments on general access to services issues and also general protection issues, as well as provide information on the protection concerns in the AOR of protection sector; CP, GBV and HLP in order to guide the AORs to address those issues and inform other relevant clusters on the service gaps in the assessed sites.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Women and men experience market systems differently and tend to have unique opportunities and constraints based on gender. The gender roles assigned to men and women have an impact on their respective access to resources and the power to decide over the benefits of using those resources. They have an impact on women and men’s differentiated roles and participation in value chain and their respective control over decisions that affect the management of the chain as well as the use of benefits generated by the activity. For instance, women often face unique barriers such as lower ownership of assets and are disproportionately responsible for unpaid household work. They also make decisions based on a different set of motivations and priorities. ARC will carry out a gender and market analysis which will aim to fully understand differences in participation, constraints and opportunities between women and men, as well as critical intervention points to create gender-equitable outcomes. The analysis will focus on examining gender roles, responsibilities and relationships; gender-differentiated consumer needs and the ability of service providers to meet these demands; and formal and informal rules that can affect women’s and men’s market participation differently. The analysis will facilitate the collection of sex-disaggregated quantitative and qualitative data at the macro, meso and micro levels using both a gender and a market chain development perspective. Through this, gaps, discrimination and key gender issues will be identified and discussions facilitated in light of the multiple dimensions on which gender inequalities and opportunities operate: economic, psychological, social, political and at different levels: individual, household, community, market, institutional nationalARC will conduct an in-depth Solutions analysis for the targeted region using Solutions based framework which will work as an analytical basis for a broad variety of stakeholders to see in detail and in concrete terms the key gaps and opportunities to inform cash distribution modalities, self-reliance and integration strategies. The process will entail a thorough review of existing secondary data and apply a participatory and consensus building approach including consultative workshops with regional authorities and partners to collectively agree on gaps and priorities. The analysis will also provide cash distribution analysis and specific recommendations on the environment needed to support inclusive access to cash, services and self-reliance building initiatives. ARC will facilitate learning platforms to support the uptake of recommendations and learning from the solutions analysis with a wide range of stakeholders.During this period, ARC will carry out a clear, coordinated targeting, ensuring beneficiaries/communities are selected on the basis of need, resource availability, accessibility and strategic advantage from the point of view of building a resilience. The ARC understands the risk of targeting creating conflict and division within communities, and efforts will be made to make all targeting and selection processes as conflict-sensitive as possible. For this reason, the initiation phase will include a conflict assessment and, within communities, engagement with leadership and all sub-sections of the population will be taken to ensure equitable access. The ARC will actively promote the inclusion of minorities, survivors, returnees, refugees and disables in the action. Particular care will be taken to ensure the inclusion of women and girls. To achieve this, participatory community decision-making will be central feature of the selection process of households and beneficiaries for the cash activities.
