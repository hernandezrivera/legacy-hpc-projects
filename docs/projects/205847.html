Project Summary

Save the Children proposes an emergency food and livelihood assistance intervention in response to the protracted humanitarian crisis resulting from inter communal violence, effects of climate change, the ongoing Sudan crisis and economic shocks that have exacerbated acute food insecurity and access to basic services. The targeted states include Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei, and Upper Nile including Abyei administrative area. These locations are severely food insecure, have high malnutrition rates, continue to receive returnees and internal displaced people worsening the humanitarian situation. The proposed interventions seek to save lives by addressing the immediate needs, meet the food security needs, restore and protect livelihoods to enhance capacities of the communities to absorb shocks. The project targets the conflict and flood affected households including children and youths of the food insecure displaced (IDPs), returnees and host communities.
In response to the escalating food insecurity and limited access to other basic needs, Save the Children proposes an emergency food security and livelihood based project to improve food security and access to livelihood opportunities. The proposed intervention will strengthen the household’s capacities to access food, income including agricultural inputs and productive assets to improve emergency food production and livelihoods. In addition, key messaging on nutrition, early warning systems will be done to enable communities to anticipate and respond to shocks.
The project geographical targets include the conflict and flood affected food insecure locations that are categorized under the IPC 3, 4 and 5 locations. The targeted households will be inclusive of internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities' households meeting the defined vulnerable criteria. The most vulnerable youths at risks, female headed households, widows, elderly, people with disability, low-income households, food insecure households, households taking care of chronical ill members, orphans, separated children, households with high dependency ratio will be considered. The interventions proposed include, unconditional cash assistance (MPCA), conditional cash assistance, emergency agricultural and fishing inputs provisions, emergency livelihood support coupled with post-harvest handling, business management, agronomic, nutrition sensitive and climate smart agricultural trainings. Key messaging on gender, protection, early warning systems, environmental management, climate change, conflict sensitivity, hygiene, nutrition and disaster risk management will be cascaded, and efforts done to ensure they are incorporated into project activities working with the community structures. The accountability to the affected population mechanisms will be strengthened to ensure beneficiaries are aware of the project activities, aware of complaint feedback mechanisms, can safely access distribution sites with dignity to enable safe programming.
The design of the proposed project is informed by findings from the need assessments, multi-sectoral assessments, IPC analysis, other partner reports, humanitarian updates and project evaluations.
The proposed response is in line with the Food Cluster Objectives (CO1, CO2 and CO3) that contributes to the HRP 2024 Strategic Objectives (SO1, SO2 and SO3). The project will reach out to 136,236 people (at least 60% female, 15% people with disability), which fall under IPC 5, 4 and 3 in three States of South Sudan, Jonglei (Akobo), and Eastern Equatoria (Budi, Torit and Magwi), Upper Nile (Renk) and Abyei Administrative Area. Save the children will coordinate with other actors in the areas of location to avoid duplication and ensure harmonization of cash transfer values, targeting and strengthen complementary.

1. Needs

South Sudan humanitarian crisis continues to escalate compounded by complex and multifaceted shocks; the prolonged inter communal violence, consecutive years of flooding, economic shocks has resulted in massive displacement of households, high levels of food insecurity, malnutrition, lack of access to basic services, and increase in protection concerns. In most food insecure areas household engage in negative coping mechanisms to survive, and this expose them to protection risks; like gender-based violence, sexual abuses in women and girls. During the acute food insecurity periods, normally women and adolescent girls, being primary caregivers are deprioritized during internal allocation of meals within their households. The women, girls, youths at risks, boys and people with disabilities are the most vulnerable in fragile context as they lack resources, skills and not included in decision making processes. The influx of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia to the high priority concern areas like Fangak and Akobo, due to armed conflict and reduction of aid has worsened the situation as people in need of humanitarian assistance keeps increasing. The country has experienced a reduction of agricultural outputs, and economic downturn that has significantly decreased the availability and access to nutritious food for the vulnerable households. Hyperinflation coupled with limited livelihood and employment opportunities has eroded the household purchasing power and exacerbated vulnerability. These conditions continue to undermine households’ capacity to cope with shocks and rebuild their livelihood. According to the IPC report, 2024; high levels of acute food security persist in South Sudan with an estimated 5.83mlllion people (46% of the population) in the period of September to November 2023 likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 35,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. In the lean season period -April - August 2024. The deteriorating food security and nutrition situation in the country is aggravated by conflict, the economic crisis, poor maternal and child health, poor dietary options, limited food production capacities and income to access adequate, safe and nutritious food. SMART surveys conducted in Jonglei have also revealed Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates of 25.9 per cent which is above the WHO emergency threshold. Food insecurity and high malnutrition rates in the proposed locations remain critical. A total of 1.6 million of children and women are facing acute malnutrition and communities have large food consumption gaps.In the HRP 2024, Save the Children proposes to support six counties, in different geographical areas. The target areas include Jonglei (Akobo), Upper Nile (Renk), Eastern Equatoria (Budi, Magwi, Torit) and (Abyei). In the high priority areas (IPC4+), an emergency food and livelihood response will be implemented and in IPC3 the nexus approach will be adapted to transition the beneficiaries to recovery. The proposed counties are selected based on the FSL and multi sectorial need assessments and have high population of people falling under the food insecure category. Save the children has operational presence in these locations implementing multisectoral programmes; the proposed project will be integrated to other ongoing projects to enable maximization of impacts. The proposed emergency response is developed in line with Cluster Objectives (CO1, CO2 and CO3) of FSL Cluster and Strategic Objectives (SO1, SO 2 and SO3) of HRP 2024 and will be implemented in accordance with the FSL cluster standards. The proposed action will be implemented in six counties by targeting a total of 46,179 food insecure people in IPC 5, 4 and 3 locations.

2. Project activities

Save the Children proposes the following emergency food and livelihood-based interventions to support 46,179 vulnerable population to contribute to the achievement of the three FSL cluster objectives. Cluster Objective 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5). This will be achieved through improving access to food and incomes to reduce vulnerability to food insecurity and malnutrition for children and their families through provision of cash transfers coupled with key messaging on cross cutting themes; gender, protection, nutrition, hygiene, sustainable management of natural resources including strengthening of the early warning systems to meet the immediate needs and enable them to be resilient to shocks.Activities1.1 Distribute multipurpose cash (MPC) to the most vulnerable households in IPC4+ locations where markets are functional.1.2 Sensitization of beneficiaries on MPCA and other cross cutting themes.1.3 Distribute conditional cash to the labor endowed households (IPC3+) as per the agreed work norms.1.4 Create or rehabilitate the community infrastructure or household productive assets. Cluster Objective 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. This will be achieved through provision of agricultural and agricultural and livelihood kits coupled with training on climate smart agriculture, production, post-harvest handling including nutrition sensitive. This will enable households to meet their food needs and other basic needs.Activities2.1 Distribute the main and dry season agricultural kits (Crop kits, Vegetable kits, fishing kits, tools) using the in-kind, cash/voucher modalities.2.3 Establish kitchen and nutrition gardens in the target locations.2.4 Train targeted household on agronomic practices and sensitization on nutrition and other cross cutting themes, improved farming and fishing practices and use of newly adapted technologies in a sustainable way.2.6 Training of community nutrition volunteers and mother to mother support groups on nutrition education and SBCC.Cluster Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. The household will be supported with business startup grants or assets, agricultural inputs, climate adapted technologies and trainings to enhance and diversify climate resilient livelihoods for improved incomes and resilience to shocks. Activities3.1 Distribute of climate smart seed varieties, technologies (procured from local markets) and provide training.3.2 Training on climate and disaster resilient agricultural practices (good agronomic practices, post-harvest handling adapting the farmer field school approach) to the targeted beneficiaries.3.3 Provision of post-harvest storage materials.3.5 Training on agricultural value addition and chains including marketing.3.6 Training on vocational including enterprise access to financial services support.3.7 Establish farmer organization and cooperatives. 3.8 Strengthen community based early warning systems and dissemination of climate information to the communities. The indicators will be disaggregated by gender, age and disability including status.
