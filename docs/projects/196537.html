Project Summary

The planned intervention contributes to the reduction of morbidity and mortality and improves the overall access to quality health services for conflict-affected Syrians and IDPs. It will focus mainly on the provision and improvement of primary health care services, secondary health care, referral system, physical rehabilitation, and capacity building of healthcare professionals.
The core of QC response for 2023 in Northern Syria include supporting 4 key hospitals in Northern Aleppo (1 pediatric hospital in Afrin, 1 general hospital in Afrin, 1 general hospital in Raie, 1 general hospital in AlBab) and 1 key hospital in Nabe’ Alsalam Area (Tal Abyad).
QC plan for 2023 is to support the core health services represented by the primary health care services. The plan is to support 10 PHCs following the standard of the essential health package. The targeted PHC centers are distributed between Idlib, Northern Aleppo, and Raqqa. 9 of the targeted PHC centers are supported and 1 PHC center is planned to be added starting from the beginning of 2023. Totally 4 PHC centers in Idleb, 5 PHC centers in Northern Aleppo, and 1 PHC center in the rural of Al-Raqqa governorate.
To ensure smooth referring between the targeted health facilities, QC will run 18 ambulances (owned by QC) in the same targeted areas.
All projects are planned through self-implementation by QC field teams in targeted areas; partners also involved in certain circumstances and the joint implementation modality is adopted.
This fund of will be utilized for supporting staff salaries, procurement of drugs and consumables, and providing other running expenses for one year.
In order to ensure project quality, resources management, and accountability towards beneficiaries, QC MEAL department will follow-up regularly on project activities to ensure activities are implemented in line with the planned scope of implementation, linked appropriately to the needs, and going in line with the time frame. At the early beginning, QC MEAL staff will set an M&E plan as per to project log-frame. All the means of verifications and the frequency of MEAL visits will be scheduled with clear roles and responsibilities for each activity.
M&E Tools:
- Health staff attendance to facilities will be monitored by the admins and Health supervisors with attendance sheet as per the -Registration Form of the beneficiaries.
- Patient satisfaction surveys will be conducted to get feedback on the quality and need-based distributed items as well as the other health services provided.
- Staff satisfaction surveys will also be conducted to better understand obstacles and difficulties if any, and to keep the staff motivated and up to the task. Moreover, QC will conduct lessons learned and quality review workshops, this will be Monitored through field visit and documented in a detailed report.
QC and its partners maintain a strong professional relationship with the local health authorities and Local Councils in areas of operation in Syria which currently serve as vital resources for health information and verification of the identified needs. QC continues to coordinate with humanitarian actors in the targeted areas in addition to close collaboration with OCHA, WHO and Health Cluster, NGOs (HIH, IDA, UOSSM, SHAM, SR..) and INGOs (WVI, IMC, IRC, HAI) to ensure adequate access to updated information that is critical for delivery of efficient health services.
The proposed intervention will also complement the interventions of other humanitarian health actors delivering assistance in the same areas through constant communication with health directorates and other coordination networks.
QC has already approached its key donors to advocate and secure the needed fund for the proposed interventions, prioritizing the continuity of services for 2023.
QC response is holistic since it provided support for PHC centers, referral system that links them with the secondary health level, and key hospitals that receive referrals.

3 - Needs

The fragile health system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies and chronic challenges which affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population. With more than 164,000 confirmed cases and close to 5,800 deaths, COVID-19 continues to disrupt the already-fragile health services and systems due to low levels of COVID-19 vaccination, lack of adherence to preventive measures, and emerging variants which, together, strainattempts to stabilize and restart services affected by the pandemic, including gaps in routine childhood immunization services. Security incidents in Northeast Syria persist alongside renewed hostilities and displacement in Dar’a. and have been accompanied by an uptick in attacks on healthcare, particularly in southern Syria, which are on track to reach or exceed the total number of reported attacks in 2020.The water crisis in northern Syria has triggered significant increases in waterborne diseases and disease vectors, particularly in parts of Northeast Syria, has strained existing surveillance and response systems, and may also drive increases in malnutrition. Theongoing crises continue to impact the health system and patients alike – resulting in increased needs such as mental health support, threatening those who cannot afford treatment, and disrupting basic supply chains of life-saving medicines and supplies.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The Whole of Syria (WoS) Health Cluster conducted a review to identify the most pressing health-related protection risks facing health actors and the affected population and suggested a set of mitigating measures.Risk: Attacks on health facilities and health care staff. Mitigating measures: While neither the health cluster nor humanitarian actors can guarantee protection from attacks on health care, health actors are encouraged to avail themselves of mechanisms for visibility, advocacy and accountability as follows: Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities. Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System for Attacks.Risk of Gender Based Violence GBV. Mitigating measures: Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Specialized health staff will be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services.Risk of lack of Duty of care. Mitigating measures: implement staff care programs which provide psycho-social support services and resources to staff. Measures ensuring regular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logistical imperative to go further than basic labor rights. - QC team is aware of and follow Covid-19 rules.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

QC has a clear strategy to mitigate the risks of PSEA, the strategy includes direct/indirect beneficiaries of our programming and adults in wider communities in which we work in. It also includes QC’s staff, partners, suppliers, sub-contractors, and any other individual engaged under QC's programs. Particular attention is given for vulnerable groups, including women, children, and those most vulnerable for discrimination based on disability, gender identity, sexual orientation, age, and religion. Our approach to PSEA is through the following: 1- Ensure that all staff, representatives and third parties are aware of the high standards of behavior and conduct expected of them to protect individuals of SEA. 2- Having an organizational culture based on accountability where there is zero tolerance for PSEA. 3- Having an endorsed reporting mechanism for staff, beneficiaries, and partners to feel safe to report violations 4- ensure that reports are handled in a manner that respects due process, and swift and credible investigation for any violations. And actively promote fair investigation and sanctions. 5- Setting efficient risks mitigation measures in programming by identifying the risks and designing the program accordingly. Starting from needs assessments through project design (health facilities, kits components) to the implementation to closing phases. 6- Comprehensive operational, HR and mgmt. measures are also required to PSEA, by conducting vetting of contractors.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Through all project locations, environmental risks may influence the implementation of the activities in the case that a major disaster took place in one of the areas of implementation. The likelihood of this risk to happen is low, while the impact on project activities will be very high. QC through its staff, will have a preparedness plan and mitigation mechanisms in all the activities and this to be shared with the respective staff. In this intervention, QC assume that the implementation of the activities will go smoothly in all targeted locations. Some risks might be beyond QC control. In case major risks took place, QC has measures in place to deal with the prospected risks.*These can be mitigated by: -putting in place task-shifting guidelines as part of care protocols -providing Training and adequate follow- up of competency-decentralizing services, clear messaging to communities around risks associated with health care facilities and underground care.Risk of Increased morbidity and mortality rate due to increasing the opportunity of diseases and medical conditions that needs professional intervention.*Mitigating measure: - Hire a qualified medical staff - Distribute medical supplies and consumables.QC assumes northwest Syria areas will be stable for the next year. The risk of increasing pressure on health services cause by increased influx of IDPs is very high. Program could be negatively impacted when the number of IDPs increased.
