Project Summary

According to the Health Cluster, more than 1.45 million Palestinians across the oPt have been identified as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2021, two-thirds of them in the Gaza Strip and one third in the West Bank. This year health humanitarian interventions in the oPt will be dominated by the operational response to the COVID-19 pandemic and ensuring that the most vulnerable affected population has uninterrupted access to life-saving health interventions in a safe and dignified manner. More specifically, WHO will focus on scaling up COVID-19 laboratory testing, case management, including MHPSS, infection prevention and control and risk communication, targeting an estimated 350,000 Palestinians across the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. WHO will also support the MoH, health authorities and partners in the provision of mental health and psychosocial support, trauma and emergency care and early essential newborn care to an estimated 226,000 people. WHO will also aim to strengthen respect, protection and fulfillment of the right to health for all Palestinians in the occupied Palestinian territory through enhanced monitoring and documentation of barriers, capacity building with the Ministry of Health and partners, and enhanced advocacy. An estimated 122,000 people will benefit. Finally, WHO will support the MoH in preparedness for any other future outbreaks through the provision and prepositioning of medical supplies and capacity building of staff, to benefit up to 7,000 Palestinians in the first 3 months in case of an outbreak. WHO's project has been coordinated at various levels with all counterparts involved in the activities:
(1) COVID-19 response with the MoH and through the inter-agency response plan,
(2) MHPSS activities - with the MoH and through tri-cluster MHPSS technical advisory body,
(3) EENC activities - with the MoH and UNICEF, WHO, UNFPA coordination mechanism
(4) Trauma activities - with the MoH and Health cluster Trauma WG,
(5) Right to Health - with the MoH, counterparts, and partners of the Advocacy WG.

1 - Needs

According to the Health Cluster, more than 1.45 million Palestinians across the oPt have been identified as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2021, two-thirds of them in the Gaza Strip and one third in the West Bank.Since March 2020, the already challenged healthcare system in the oPt experience added strain from the COVID-19 outbreak. The reallocation of scarce resources, along with the contagion of healthcare workers, has undermined people’s access to health. Direct mortality from the virus and indirect mortality from preventable diseases is of major concern. Overall, the pandemic has rendered an estimated 115,000 men and women in need of COVID-19 health humanitarian assistance, including people 65+ years. The demand to scale-up laboratory testing, surveillance, contact tracing, isolation, and case management is increasing rapidly, as the number of confirmed COVID-19 cases raised to 57,400, including 1,019 health staff as of 15 October. The MoH estimates that at least 5,000 tests needed daily across oPt. Also, COVID-19 awareness is inadequate despite the significant progress achieved (KAP Survey, Aug'20). To contain the pandemic, resources were redirected from essential healthcare services. In Gaza, 32 out of 50 PHC are converted into quarantine and isolation centers. Some 400 doctors, nurses, and administrative staff were mobilized to support the COVID-19 response. In the West Bank too, most PHC and hospitals have suspended services. Outpatient admissions at hospitals have declined by over 50%, as a result of a policy decision on the reallocation of resources, as well as due to the access restrictions and fear of contagion by the population. The financial situation of East Jerusalem hospitals has been severely undermined, due to a 30% decline in revenues since the beginning of the COVID-19 crisis, driven by the decline in referrals from the remainder of the oPt. In oPt, vulnerable people are not getting the appropriate MHPSS services, referral, and support due to a massive gap in MHPSS integration into essential healthcare service delivery. Shortages of specialized personnel and drugs also add to the challenges. Even though half of all mental illnesses begin by the age of 14 and three quarters by mid-20s, continuity of mental healthcare for children through primary-, secondary-, and tertiary-levels of intervention does not exist. The mental strain generated by the pandemic and its related measures has also increased the need for mental healthcare services. COVID-19 itself can lead to neurological and mental complications, such as delirium, agitation, and stroke. Recently with the COVID-19 pandemic, an increase in youth risky behaviors has been observed. Palestinian patients and companions needed over 250,000 permits to reach essential health services in 2019, most for services within the oPt. Around 60,000 were delayed or denied: over 21,500 in Gaza and over 38,000 in the West Bank. In 2020, COVID-19 and the end of coordination between the PA and Israel have exacerbated barriers to health access and the need for effective monitoring and advocacy. Protection gaps adversely affect healthcare, with 266 attacks against healthcare, including the killing of a health worker, in the oPt in 2019 and 41 in the first three quarters of 2020. While the number has reduced structural needs for protection and prevention of impunity remain.Health inequities have also been exacerbated by COVID-19: in Gaza, referrals remain half of the pre-COVID-19 levels, essential medicines are disproportionately depleted, and health staff receives lower salaries. Strengthening equity monitoring and advocacy is needed to reduce humanitarian needs.While resources in Gaza shifted to address the pandemic, 3,500 elective surgeries per month are being postponed. Among them, at least 540 patients injured during the ‘Great March of Return’ are still in need of limb reconstruction surgeries, and more than 1,000 need rehabilitation, according to Health Cluster. To facilitate the needs, WHO plans to expand the Limb Reconstruction Center (LRC) into an advanced Multidisciplinary LRC with an educational capacity for other levels of services. Besides, no formal triage exists in prehospital settings and in the Emergency Departments of the hospitals. The absence of a standardized triage method leads to inefficient use of resources and delays in handling urgent cases. The adoption of the triage system on a daily basis will increase the resilience and provide sustainable support to the health system that is not only a protracted crisis but also now imposed with additional burden by the pandemic. This intervention will directly benefit 80,000 most vulnerable people.Redirection of resources from the provision of sexual and reproductive healthcare, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services affected an additional 80,000 vulnerable women and 210,000 children, including 26,000 neonates born through C-section, with LBW and prematurely. In oPt, where the neonatal mortality rate is 11 deaths per 1000 live births, neonatal deaths account for 50% of deaths in children under the age of five. One-half of neonatal deaths occur in the first day of life, mainly from complications related to prematurity, birth asphyxia, and infection, as well as birth defects. Most newborns die of preventable causes: sepsis, asphyxia, and complication of prematurity and were not provided with potentially life-saving interventions during labor, delivery, and the immediate postpartum period, as international evidence recommends. What’s even more troubling is that health workers often use outdated and harmful clinical practices during and after delivery which increases the risk for newborn morbidity and mortality. Consequently, the needs for the delivering and sustaining of essential healthcare services remain acute, and WHO plans to address the needs to maintain the services as a part of the integrated COVID-19 response.

2 - Activities

This project is fully aligned with the Health Cluster HNO and HRP for 2021 and will focus on three interlinked areas. WHO aims to: (1) Support the PA MoH in the containment of the COVID-19 outbreak; (2) Support maintaining the availability, accessibility, acceptability, and quality of essential lifesaving health services to vulnerable communities across oPt that were affected by the COVID-19 pandemic to achieve the universal health coverage and ensure the right of all Palestinian to health. The activities in this project are in line with the Health cluster interventions under Strategic Cluster Objectives 1, 2 and 3.To support the MoH’s efforts in containing the COVID-19 outbreak, WHO will support the life-saving interventions in the following areas of the COVID-19 response: 1. Strengthen coordination, Information, and Monitoring, 2. Scale-up laboratory capacity to test for COVID-19, 3. Scale-up capacity of the MoH in case management, 4. Scale-up case finding, contact tracing, and isolation, 5. Strengthen surveillance, 6. Accelerate Risk Communication and Community Engagement, 7. Infection Prevention & Control (IPC), 8. Operation support and logistics, and 9. Support maintaining the essential health system. Specifically, WHO and the Health cluster estimate that 350,000 Palestinians will benefit from the following interventions:1) Provide MoH with the best available technical advice and coordination on strategies to contain the COVID-19 outbreak in the oPt.2) Procure and deliver medical supplies and equipment to scale-up laboratory testing to up to 350,000 COVID-19 tests, ensure quality case management for up to 115,000 male and female cases in a safe environment for up to 40,000 frontline health workers, including for East Jerusalem hospitals. 3) Support capacity building of health and non-health workers across the oPt in laboratory techniques, improved case management, including intensive care and MHPSS, infection prevention and control, epidemiological surveillance and contact tracing. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

WHO is committed to ensuring a people-centered humanitarian response that is accountable to the affected population. For this project, the affected people will participate throughout the project, in the following ways:• A final list of procurement for supplies and equipment will be drafted and discussed at the Health Cluster COVID-19 task force and endorsed by the MoH to ensure that there is no duplication and procurement responds to the most acute needs in MoH laboratories.• Where possible, WHO will continue to conduct regular visits/meetings to collect feedback, including using the complaint book, on the quality and availability of services from patients and health staff. WHO will consider the feedback and identify/implement remedy solutions collectively to improve the performance in consultation with affected people and counterparts. This includes any SEA complaints. WHO will continue to advocate through the Health cluster for strengthening the feedback mechanism for the entire response. * For advocacy, feedback will be used to strengthen processes and activities, while complaints will be fully investigated, with the involvement of impartial bodies where required. All complaints related to serious ethical concerns will be raised through WHO’s ethics mechanisms.* Through Risk Communication/Community Engagement (RCCE) Task Force, WHO will seek feedback from the affected people on the materials produced. Affected people are also part of the RCCE task force and will participate in the design/implementation stage. Feedback will be used to modify the materials.• For trauma activities, feedback will be used to adapt the training according to the needs of the direct beneficiaries, making sure that health personnel will be receiving training tailored to their needs.• For MHPSS activities, a report with patients' feedback will be submitted to policymakers for decisions and modification of policies and interventions.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The centrality of protection is at the heart of WHO emergency response as mandated by the IASC. WHO works to ensure the prevention of harm to all persons it engages with through activities, including through upholding due processes of consent, the protection of confidentiality and the proper collection, communication, storage, dissemination and disposal of information on affected persons. Additionally, activities supported by WHO will contribute to ensuring that beneficiaries are not exposed to further contamination risks through adherence to IPC. WHO will also ensure that all interventions are context-appropriate and culturally sensitive. Affected people will be consulted on this where and when appropriate. The prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse has also been integrated across the WHO-supported projects through mandatory training of all staff on PSEA and seeking feedback from the affected people, which will be acted upon. The Right to Health programme has been specifically designed to promote protection.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project activities will clearly focus on the provision of life-saving health interventions to the most vulnerable, including women, the elderly and people with disabilities (PWD) based on the latest available impartial assessments. PWD will be considered as one of the main targeted vulnerable groups in the proposed project. All indicators will be gender-age disaggregated where possible. WHO will continue to work to strengthen the empowerment of affected persons, including through the provision of platforms to promote patient voices as well as capacity building with health service providers to promote participatory mechanisms within health services design and delivery. WHO also works with the Ministry of Health and partners to strengthen monitoring and analysis of equity and barriers to health access, focused on vulnerable groups and protected characteristics.
