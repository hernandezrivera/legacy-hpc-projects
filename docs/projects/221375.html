1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The North West and South West Regions of Cameroon have been in an armed conflict since 2016. There are multitudes of Non State Armed Groups (NSAGs) fighting with state security officers within the North West Regions of Cameroon. Within the armed conflict state security officers separated from their families have been assigned to confront the growing influence of NSAGs in localities without prior military presence. It is unclear how many armed men (both NSAGs and state security) are actively engaged in hostilities within the North West Region, but they can be estimated in their thousands. There have been multiple cases of 1) members of non-state armed groups holding girls/young women in captivity for domestic servitude and sexual exploitation; 2) state security agents using legal control operations to engage children, girls and young women in coercive sexual exploitation; and 3) belligerent of the conflict victimizing any girl/woman suspected of being in a sexual relationship with a rival combatant, considering her as the enemy whereas she is the victim. The armed conflict has engendered a growing wave of conflict-related forced pregnancies resulting in an equal growing number of children fathered by belligerents of the conflict. These survivors of conflict related sexual violence who have become single mothers are a result live in constant fear of further violence and sexual exploitation, slavery, and abuse and suffer from neglect and stigma associated with the circumstances of their births. Without a source of income and lacking social/emotional support from their surroundings, some survivors regard the children as dream killers. A situation which seriously jeopardize the mother-to-child bondIn 2022, GLOWA started a program to support the healing and empowerment of survivors single mothers through providing vocational training in sewing, traditional embroidery and enhancing the mother to child bond. Besides other challenges faced by program participants in their daily survival, the healing distraction associated by children hanging on their backs and laps during individual and/or group empowerment activities has equally been enormous. The movement of children around the vocational training environment equally possesses safety and security risk for the toddlers. In respond, GLOWA is proposing a closed child safe space within the premises open only to the children born of conflict related sexual violence whose mothers participate in our healing and empowerment program. The closed child safe space project shall facilitate the recovery and livelihood development of the participants at GLOWAHaus by reducing the distractions and anxieties created by their crying babies. It shall equally facilitate the development of vital pre-school skills for children 0-4 years as they prepare for school. It is estimated that the children shall stay in the program during the time their mothers is actively participating in the program. The presence of the child safe space shall serve as additional incentive to program participants to enrol and participant in the program to its logical end. This will address the current rate of absenteeism that is related to fending for themselves and childcare at home
