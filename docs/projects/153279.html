Project Summary

Impact Health Organization (IHO) proposes the Provision of Integrated Emergency WASH Service project to be implemented in Torit County. The project will deliver a package of integrated WASH services (WASH-NUT, WASH-Protection and WASH Health) to meet the needs of the vulnerable populations (IDPs, host community and returnees) in Torit County, Eastern Equatoria and Fashod County, Upper Nile state.
Impact Health Organization (IHO) needs assessment conducted in Torit County, and Fashoda in October 2018 howed that Most Households had no access to latrines, drink and use water from the river with limited treatment, limited access to water collection containers and as well as limited hygiene awareness. In both locations WASH infrastructure at the nutrition centers, health centres, schools and at the community-level were limited hence there is a need for an urgent response
The IPC analysis September 2018-March 2019 classified malnutrition level in Torit County at IPC 2 classification (Stressed) for Fashoda IPC 4 emergency Phase and the smart survey conducted in 2018 reported GAM rate for Torit County at 12.2% and Fashoda 16.1%. This project will integrate health, nutrition and education WASH related interventions into the response. The project will target communities with poor access to safe water supply, unsafe sanitation and hygiene practices, areas with poor sanitation coverage and support health, education and nutrition centers with rehabilitation of WASH facilities.
In this project, IHO will provide lifesaving quality WASH service to 25000 host community, IDP and returnees in Torit County 49% Male (12250) and 51% female (12750 Female) of which 15% shall be children below 18 years and 75% adults 18-59 years and 10% elderly 59 and above years. In Fashoda County IHO will reach 21312 Individuals (10741 Male and 10571 Female of which 15% shall be children below 18 years and 75% adults 18-59 years and 10% elderly 59 and above years. This Integral package of activities will decrease the prevalence of diseases and malnutrition and increase protection of especially women and girls. In the process of implementation, IHO will work closely with communities to identify the problem affecting them and the community will greatly participate in the development and implementation of activities and provide feedback on the progress of the interventions. The project will build capacities of the communities through trainings and awareness campaigns designed to halt the spread of WASH related disease. IHO will address unique WASH needs of women and girls, particularly respond to GB. Finally, IHO will work closely with WHO, WASH, health, Nutrition, Protection clusters and relevant government ministries in ensuring provision of timely live saving WASH services.

Needs

Following the December 2013 crisis another major crisis erupted in July 2016 and intensified in the Greater Equatoria region which further aggravated the humanitarian situation, producing secondary or third displacement with populations under stress lacking access to basic services and constraining humanitarian access. Among the most affected Counties in Eastern Equatoria is Torit County and Fasgoda County Upper Nile state having figures way off thresholds in the thematic areas of WASH, nutrition, health and food security. The situation has been deteriorating with ongoing conflict in the region. As a result, water point infrastructure such as boreholes, water systems, and latrines and other facilities were destroyed. Impact Health organization assessment in Kudo, Ifwotu, Imurok, Lowoi, Hiyala, Himodonge, Bur and Torit Payam Torit County in October 2018 showed that most household 71.3% do not meet the basic minimum of water requirement of 15ltrs per person per day,88.6% household do not have a single latrine practicing open defecation (OD) and there is limited access to hygiene promotion messaging or training. IHO needs assessment October 2018 conducted 3 Payams of Lul, Kodok and Kodok Town, Fashoda County, showed most households 85.3% had no access to latrines. All the households assessed 100% reported drink and use water from the river and only 12.1% of the households reported to treat drinking water and mostly through the use of aqua tabs and boiling. Most households 97.5% reported to consume less than 15 Liters of water per day per person due to lack of containers and because the water source was too far. In both locations WASH infrastructure at the nutrition centers, health centres, schools and at the community-level were limited hence there is a need for an urgent response. The IPC analysis September 2018-March 2019 classified malnutrition level in Torit County at IPC 2 classification (Stressed) for Fashoda IPC 4 emergency Phase and the smart survey conducted in 2018 reported GAM rate for Torit County at 12.2% and Fashoda 16.1%. In response to the above indicators, IHO plans to undertake Integrated WASH interventions (WASH-NUT, WASH-GBV and WASH-Health so as to meet the needs of the Vulnerable Communities.

Activities

To meet the community WASH needs in the targeted locations,1. Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations: IHO will rehabilitate non-functional boreholes, distribution of water purification supplies such as PUR, Aqua tab hence promoting timely and equitable access to safe water, 2. Enable vulnerable and affected populations sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate. sanitation services to practice safe: IHO will facilitate construction emergency sanitation facilities and hygiene awareness among IDPs/host communities and returnees. The process will involve working closely with communities to identify their WASH needs in terms of availability to safe water, access to sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion. 3. Undertake hygiene promotion to Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases: IHO undertake, hygiene promotion by training community hygiene promoters who shall conduction household awareness and hygiene campaigns. Hygiene awareness shall be complimented by distribution of WASH NFI to vulnerable households and groups. 4. Mitigate WASH related GBV issues: IHO will also mitigate WASH-related GBV issues outside PoC/settlement most specially among women and girls who are the primary direct users of WASH services, by assessing GBV gaps through safety audits both at water sources and Sanitation facilities and finding will guide interventions to promote safety of women while accessing water and Sanitation infrastructure. GBV awareness will be integral part of the project intervention in IHO will also map GBV actors and create a referral pathway for GBV cases. 5. Reduce Malnutrion among SAM/MAM cases: IHO will undertake WASH/Nutrition interventions both at household and OTP/SC/TSFP sites. These shall include increasing access to safe drinking water through borehole rehabilitation, reduce open defection through increased access to latrines and promote infant young child feed practices to reduce diarrhea diseases through hygiene awareness. In addition, Boreholes rehabilitated shall consist of water traps for animals and irrigation purpose and communities shall be education on use traps to support household farming. 6. Avert and controlling disease outbreaks such as cholera, etc. IHO will promote access to WASH services (access to safe water, and build capacities by training of health workers, WASH focal points, school children etc to appropriate hygiene practices both in institutions and household level.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IHO has a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IHO staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IHO Contractors. All IHO staff members are trained on PSEA Awareness-Raising. The organization staff or contractors or suppliers are prohibited from exploiting beneficiaries for exchange of services for sex or any form of abuse. All IHO staff members have a duty to report in good faith any SEA allegations, concerns or suspicions, or retaliation related to SEA and SEA of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and can result in disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution, where appropriate.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IHO mainstreams protection into WASH programming by assessing gender specific needs and identifying appropriate responses to address the particular concerns of women, men, girls and boys. IHO prioritizes women and girls as the primary user of WASH services, for example they play a major role in water collection in household and as well could be at risk of abuse and rape in process of water collection and during access of sanitation facilities, therefore IHO ensures that before WASH intervention are implemented, the protection needs of women and girls should well understood. IHO undertake safe audits before rehabilitation and construction of water point and before construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facility in addition to holding a series of key informant interviews, focus group discussions (FGDs) with the intervention communities. Through mapping and working closely with protection cluster, IHO will establish a referral pathway to victims of gender based violence. The result is a strategy that incorporates the view, opinions and needs of all men, women, boys and girls and seeks to address existing protection gaps. It is through this process that IHO identifies the WASH priorities for girls, boys, men and women in terms of needs.
