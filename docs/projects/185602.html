Project Summary

To improve equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive sexual reproductive health (SRHR) services for Rohingya refugees and host communities, UNFPA will continue to strengthen its implementation of integrated services through health systems strengthening and community engagement, through coordination and partnership within a multi-sectoral approach, with the overarching guidance of the Health Sector and key players. Family planning will be the center of UNFPA’s strategic, technical and programmatic guidance related to provision and delivery of a continuum of care package for essential SRHR services to the last mile. This includes strategic collaboration with the Gender based violence [GBV] Sub-Sector, Community health workers [CHW] Working Group and other key sectors/actors.
UNFPA will ensure continuous availability and facilitate accessibility of family planning and other related SRH commodities, supplies and equipment through improved supply chain management, to meet the unmet needs of refugees and surrounding host communities. The project will centralise facilities and community based family planning, improve and maintain the quality of services provided through strengthening of referrals and transfer of obstetric emergency cases, availability and accessibility of life saving SRHR commodities, client centered quality and respectful midwifery led antenatal and postnatal care, safe birth, menstrual regulation, cervical cancer and obstetric fistula screening and treatment, adolescent SRHR services, and quality assurance through mentorship and support to service providers. Health providers' and community health workers’ capacity will be strengthened to provide integrated client centered quality services, including family planning. In addition, these integrated services will strengthen and expand existing behavioural change approaches.
UNFPA will strengthen readiness to meet SRHR needs during disasters through capacity building, stock prepositioning, and SRHR policy formulation. Furthermore, the COVID-19 pandemic presented an unprecedented burden on health systems around the world, which Bangladesh is not an exception. It exacerbated existing gaps in availability of comprehensive SRHR services provision and threatened the continuation of these essential services. Like other health emergencies, as restrictions and lockdowns were put in place to prevent and curtail the spread of the disease increased women and girls’ vulnerabilities. To ensure continuity of comprehensive integrated SRHR services provision during this protracted pandemic, UNFPA will continue its preventative and response interventions, through the operationalisation of maternity red zones and Isolation Treatment Centres [ITC] and provision of integrated SRHR/GBV services in health facilities, communities and women and girls protected spaces.

1 - Identified Needs

The Joint Multi Sector Needs Assessment (October 2021) estimates 1.36 million people to be in need and targeted with humanitarian assistance in Cox’s Bazar district, of which, an estimated 334,502 (Rohingya refugees 221,222 : Host communities 113,280) are women of reproductive age (WRA) in need of SRHR services. SRHR services are however, not available 24/7 across facilities and capacity to provide emergency obstetric care to ensure safe deliveries is insufficient. This contributes to the higher levels of maternal morbidity and mortality for the country. The proportion of facility-based deliveries has improved over a period of time from 32% at the start of 2019 to 60% in October 2020 and reaching 71% in October 2021. This is attributable to appropriate rights based SRHR interventions, including facility and community based service provision by partners, even during the peak of the COVID-19 pandemic. To sustain these gains the need to reinforce the system and support to all SRHR partners to ensure provision of quality services cannot be overemphasised. Furthermore, in view of the morbidity related to SRHR, there is an urgent need to scale up midwifery led intensified cervical cancer and obstetric fistula screening and treatment across the refugee and host communities, including targeting elderly women. Progress continued to be made in the delivery of modern methods of Family Planning. The 2021 JRP annual target of 141,476 women of reproductive age using any method of contraception is already nearly achieved, with 130,838 (92.5%) reached as at September 2021. Whilst Long-acting reversible contraceptive (LARC) methods (intrauterine devices and implants) accounted for 4.6% of all methods distributed by the SRH Working Group in quarter three 2021, there is room for expansion. The Government of Bangladesh has made the expansion of access to Long-acting reversible contraceptive methods in the refugee community a key priority. This is further demonstrated by the RRRC’s approval and signing of the Family Planning Strategy in October 2021. There is therefore, need for skills development of mid-level service providers to improve provision and uptake of LARC. Community engagement is required to address social and cultural barriers to contraceptive use. Adolescents constitute approximately 19% of the Rohingya refugee population. However, access to adolescent friendly SRH services in existing health facilities is limited. Hence the need to build capacity of service providers to enhance provision of adolescent friendly services at existing facilities as well as in integrated women protected spaces. In the spirit of leaving no one behind, strengthening interventions to address the SRHR needs of other vulnerable populations, including people with disabilities, is critical. The 2021 Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment highlighted the heightened risk of persons with disabilities and older persons, and estimates the prevalence of persons with disabilities to be 12%. COVID-19 pandemic continues to pose a burden on health systems, increasing health care related infections, creating a major public health problem. There is an urgent need to continue strengthening Infection prevention and control (IPC) measures at service delivery points, including ensuring access to Personal protective equipment (PPE) and capacity building for health workers. There is also need to continue engaging community members, including through gatekeepers, on continuation of services, to regain lost confidence in utilizing SRHR and related services due to fears of contracting COVID-19. Interventions such as establishment of Maternity Red Zones in SRH facilities, COVID-19 isolation and treatment centres with maternity care, provision of emergency transportation, strengthening of the referral system and community based surveillance remain key in preventing excess maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Provision of comprehensive SRHR services in all supported facilities to meet international standards and national guidelines, including during COVID-19, through strengthening existing health facilities to provide 24/7 CEmONC, obstetric fistula and cervical cancer, as well as ensuring midwifery-led care and improving coordination of referral pathways 2. Scale up the provision of family planning services including implementation of SRH WG’s Family Planning Strategy at facility-based and community levels, as well as advocacy and coordination 3. Support the deployment of midwives and other health care providers at health facilities, maternity red zones and COVID-19 Isolation and Treatment Centres with maternity care units to provide safe deliveries and EmONC, including Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) for women and girls during COVID-19 4. Mentoring and training of midwives to increase their knowledge and capacity to provide basic SRHR services, including EmONC services, Family Planning, CMR, Treatment of STI, Menstrual Regulation, Post-abortion care, Cervical Cancer and Fistula Screening 5. Supporting the emergency referral transportation system and ambulance services in the camps to facilitate transfers of pregnant women during labour, obstetric emergencies and other patients requiring other emergency medical care 6. Coordinate and strengthen the Maternal Perinatal Mortality Surveillance and Response (MPMSR) system to provide information that effectively guides actions to avert preventable maternal mortality at health facilities and in the community. This involves the effective MPMSR committee & sub-committee coordination, institutionalisation of facility based maternal death audits and conducting verbal autopsies for the women of reproductive age deaths at the community 7. Continue to support GBV-SRHR-GBV integration i.e. ensuring basic sexual reproductive health services with specialised adolescent SRHR services, including CMR care, are provided in all women and girls protected spaces 8. Ensure accountability to affected populations through maintaining and establishing communication, community participation and feedback support mechanism as well as strengthening the PSEA network 9. Procure and distribute SRHR commodities and equipment - including family planning to support UNFPA, Government and SRH Working Group partner facilities to ensure uninterrupted supply 10. Strengthen Contingency planning and preparedness to provide lifesaving SRHR services during natural disasters i.e. cyclone and monsoon 11. Expand the Community Health Volunteers Network to strengthen community engagement programmes on SRHR, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic, to improve the uptake of services 12. Engaging the religious leaders and community leaders as well as the involvement of men and boys as SRHR partners to raise awareness on key SRHR topics 13. Provide comprehensive adolescent sexual and reproductive health services through facility and community level interventions especially targeting Young Mothers 14. Continue to strengthen SRHR coordination through the provision of support and leadership to the SRH Working Group and ensure that SRHR and GBV services are delivered in an integrated and standardised manner to improve the delivery of quality services - including standardisation of SRHR services provided in protection spaces 15. Improve Information Management Systems through timely data collection, reporting and dissemination of SRHR information products, interoperability and streamlining with other reporting systems in coordination with the Health Sector, to promote evidence-based decision making
