Project Summary

Relief International (RI) will increase awareness on integrated messages related to health, COVID-19, immunization, sexual and reproductive health and mental health through outreach activities. RI will also conduct critical distributions to enhance hygiene practices and ensure dignity of women in the reproductive age. Due to the continually worsening economic situation in Lebanon, households (HHs)containing Lebanese and migrants across the country have been pushed into severe economic difficulty. With limited financial capabilities, HHs are unable to seek or obtain the medical services required, have limited access to health care services, are limited in movement due to major fuel shortages. All these factors contribute to HHs and individuals being unable to seek needed health care, or prioritizing other needs.
Aligned to the health cluster strategic objectives, RI will address the health needs of 37,320 Lebanese and migrant communities in Beirut, Mount Lebanon, Bekaa, Baalback- Hermel, North and Akkar. With existing extensive outreach and experience, RI will bolster healthcare efforts to the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant families. This will be done through essential preventative measures for maternal healthcare, focused community outreach and awareness raising, accompanied with the distribution of essential hygiene and feminine kits. Individuals in need of health care services including mental health and access to medications will be referred by RI’s community health team either internally to RI’s mental health team or externally to supported PHCs where they can get their essential treatment.
Such intervention is required given the growing needs across Lebanon. Eighty per cent of Lebanese are now classified as poor, which directly impacts health outcomes and the likelihood to seek medical assistance when needed. It also directly impacts diet and self-care practices, which in turn can impact health and nutrition of all HH members. According to the World Bank, GDP declined by 20.3% alone in 2020, and economists at Bloomberg reported a 400% increase in food prices. This directly impacts on the capacity of HHs to be able to respond to health needs. This is further illustrated in the Needs and Vulnerability Assessment of Migrants in Lebanon report by IOM (May 2021), in which 20% of respondents reported having health problems (physical and mental) that required continuous treatment. However, with 50% of the respondents reporting unemployment, debt, and an inability to meet food needs, health expenditure was substituted for more pressing basic needs.
RI's proposed intervention will support 37,320 men, women, boys and girls across Lebanon from Lebanese and migrant HHs, contributing to stem the growing humanitarian. With ongoing coordination with MoPH and UNICEF, RI will provide critical assistance and contribute towards a unified response. Integration of migrants will be achieved with continued coordination with IOM who will support RI in identifying community Health workers from the migrants’ community with community mobilization skills to raise awareness in their community on health topics and refer them to services when needed. Furthermore, IEC materials will be received form IOM in different migrant languages, to ensure accessibility and understanding by all groups.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Throughout the project duration, RI will: - Support 191 CHWs providing awareness sessions on different health topics including COVID-19 and mental health through door to door activities in Bekaa, Baalback-Hermel, Beirut Mt Lebanon, North, and Akkar. CHWs will also be trained on SGBV core concepts and safe identification and referral in the above mentioned areas. - Provide critical distribution of dignity kits and personal hygiene kits to women and girls of reproductive age in Bekaa, Baalback-Hermel, Beirut Mt Lebanon, North, and Akkar. RI will create contact with UNFPA, and coordinate appropriately so this activity will avoid overlapping and contribute to a harmonized approach.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Main protection risks of this project for Lebanese and migrants are related to barriers (physical, cultural, language) in accessing health and nutrition services. RI will build on existing networks and community engagement across all areas to reach the affected population. As the deteriorating security situation poses high protection risks (conflicts, robbery, roadblocks) for staff and beneficiaries, RI will coordinate movement and activity through its Security Focal Point, LAF and municipalities to minimize harm. For COVID-19, RI will follow WHO standards to reduce risk of transmission. All staff members trained on PSEA policies and beneficiaries will receive awareness of the strong referral and feedback mechanisms displayed to ensure feedback and complaints are received and followed-up. RI is part of the PSEA network and signed the PSEA SOPs. RI’s staff care policies will ensure concerns/challenges are addressed as possible and access to specialized MH services is available.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

RI will ensure AAP is implemented across all project activities through: - Consulting project participants throughout the project lifecycle to enhance and reinforce leadership and ownership of the project. This will take place through one-on-one consultations, FGDs, surveys;- Sharing information with project participants about RI’s and other services available to them based on articulated needs. RI will ensure that this is achieved during the door-to-door monthly awareness sessions and other project activities;- Ensuring gender and protection mainstreaming through close consultation with women, girls, boys and men at all project levels and project evaluation to assess whether their needs were met;- Ensuring RI’s FCM is accessible to all project participants by establishing at least two channels of reporting (hotline, in-person, complaint boxes). In addition, RI will continue to use the RIMS for referrals to external service providers and report referrals’ status on Activity Info.
