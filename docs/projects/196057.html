Project Summary

ATAA is planning to target children and youth including ECCD in locations as follows: Idleb governorate: Dana, Harim, Maaret Tamsrin, Teftnaz, Badama, Idleb sub-districts. Aleppo governorate: A'zaz, Al Bab, Jarablus, Aghtrin, Atareb, Daret Azze Jandairis, Afrin sub-districts. The 41 schools located in Aleppo & Idleb The proposed project will target children and youth from the age of 3 -24 who are in the ECCD, Basic, Secondary Vocational Education (Pre-school, 1st&12th grade) including 5-7% CwDs. These communities prioritized based on severity 5,4 &3. ATAA’s concern is towards the dropped-out children, those at the risk of being dropped out and newly displaced as a result of frequent displacement and difficult living conditions. The project will provide FE, NFE, ECCD, & TVET for 19897 (F 9746, M; 10151, CWD F; 127, CWD M; 189) in addition to 862 educational personnel (F339, M523), students (49% females and 51% males), staff (45% F & 55% M).
The project targets children with FE & NFE and provides incentives to education personnel and minor rehabilitation of schools, TLCs based on a rehabilitation need assessment and school furniture to be friendly environments to achieve safe, equitable and protective education considering COVID-19 and Cholera measures. The project is cost-efficient as some of the targeted schools are equipped with furniture, fuel tanks, heaters, book corners, solar panels, textbooks, etc. FE (SIG curriculum) will be provided & NFE (ALP, Curriculum B, Remedial, Catch-up &SLP) for OOSC more than one academic year based on age & period of dropping out, TVET will be given to the dropped out and OOSC who are between 16 and 24 years who are at risk of being recruited to armed groups, early marriage & child labor. There will be an MoU signed with ED to recognize the students who got NFE in order to be referred to FE. CWDs will be provided with tailored activities and facilities to come back to education (recreational, rehab of WASH facilities & classrooms). School safety plans include fire extinguishers, first aid kits, evacuation maps, drills, and sharing information (banner, brochures, roll-up). Teacher recruitment and incentives payment will be strictly applied by ATAA, Regular teachers’ observation, PTA and TLC will be conducted on monthly basis, and training packages to teachers (TICC, BLN, CSG, PSS, SLP, Distance Education, SEL, Safe learning).
Monthly running cost to schools, including PPE provision to each school to take precautions against a cholera epidemic.
Education process (physically, remotely, blended), apply distance education modality via a digital platform, WhatsApp groups "based on the situations". The summer program will include Catch-up-UP and remedial classes, SEL to 1st&4th grades, Base & end-line ASER, one BTL campaign per school to decrease the rate of dropping out. ATAA will invest in strengthening the community by PTA, Providing ECCD, school-in-box materials, and recreational, teachers & students kits in addition to coordination with UNICEF. Winterization (fuel & heaters) and individual support activities will be there also. Visual aids, textbooks printing, recreational, ECCD and school in-box materials will be provided. Recreational and CP activities will be given and tailored in the targeted locations considering the INEE minimum standards along with teacher students’ ratio will be there. In the schools, maintain water tanks & taps to be gender-separated, provide drinkable water on a daily basis (ratio 10 liters/student/day). Re-rehabilitate latrine units and septic tanks, deslodge them regularly for schools not linked to sewer systems and connect some schools with the main sewer system. All activities mentioned will be reflected through both standard and custom indicators which in turn will be monitored and reflected using MEAL activities and tools classified in MEAL plan which also contains all of MoVs will be collected, target, timing, frequency and roles and responsibilities

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, an estimated 7.6 M people (83% children) need humanitarian education assistance where many children became OOSC or at risk of dropping out due to effect of the conflict and/or lack of support. Based on 2023 Syria HNO-Edu, education needs (FE, NFE, ECCD and TVET) will be further compounded by the economic crisis, insecurity, displacement, water shortage and ongoing cholera outbreak. These factors continue to limit the ability to build on past investments, leading to an increase in out of school children and dropouts, exposing children to a myriad of protection concerns. Learning environments continue to be unconducive for children, further contributing to low learning outcomes and less usage of services. Without sufficient investments, schools will continue to unduly put students, teachers, school personnel and the communities at risk of communicable diseases due to overcrowding, lack of regulated ventilation (with regulated heat in the winter) and insufficient WASH facilities.Learning continues to be constrained/disrupted by insufficient support to attract and retain skilled teachers and education personnel limiting the ability of teaching to take place.Finally, lack of access to secondary education in many areas limits the ability of adolescents to continue their education in addition to that the lack of vocational education prevents adolescents who will not return to school to learn skills needed for future economic opportunities.

4 - Implementation of activities

ATAA will carry out direct implementation through coordinating with all stakeholders (Education Cluster & ED, local communities, &parents). Different curricula: FE & NFE (SIG, Curriculum B, catch up, ECCD & TVET). Rehabilitation will be very effective considering the schools including CWDs in which ramps, and latrines will be equipped with special tools considering CWDs requirements & school disinfection & Cholera protective measures. Enrollment of students along with BTLs, recreational, ECCD & teacher kits, winterization, individual support, and transportation will be provided. Baseline & end-line Placement test. Provide needed training as TICC, BLN, CSG, PSS, SLP, Distance Education, SEL, and Safe learning. Hygiene promotion to increase the awareness of students &communities. CP activities & awareness raising sessions will be integrated such as PSS, SEL, and referral mechanisms in each school. TLCs & PTAs will be activated. CRM in schools using suitable methodologies will be activated, in textbooks. MEAL: Checklist to monitor the construction of TECs, WASH facilities & rehabilitation of the existing ones & to verify their quality & suitability for CWDs, school safety will be measured by school safety checklist, training will be measured by both the KAP survey & training evaluation & PDM will be conducted after each distribution, Physical & distance education modality will be monitored. Students will be paid monthly incentives (40$) to continue their vocational education.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ATAA PSEA policy developed in June 2022 and with official approvals from PSEA network and UNICEF PSEA focal point and delivered to all staff to be signed, on the other hand ATAA became low risk partner in SEA with UNICEF for 5 years after conducting PSEA assessment.ATAA adopted a core concept in all intervention to make PSEA and CSG as mandatory in all projects and activities, in this regard, all ATAA staff, volunteers and contractors will get orientation sessions about PSEA and CSG policies and sign both PSEA and CSG code of conducts.All project locations will include banners and complaint boxes with delivering messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels in addition to stand alone system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case starting from receiving the report, to investigation process, and provide aid and support to the survivor till closure of the case after achieving the objectives of investigation.ATAA is PSEA network member participate in all regular network meetings, in addition to submit monthly reports to the network, in addition to conduct training for all staff about PSEA and target humanitarian workers in the intervention location to support the role of PSEA network.ATAA is operating PSEA stand-alone project focusing on raising the awareness of targeted communities (78 community) in NW Syria about PSEA and enhance the ability of BNFs to reach reporting channels of ATAA and PSEA network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

One of the biggest risks is the intervention of military factions or civilian entities in humanitarian work. To mitigate this, ATAA SSD depends on achieving community acceptance during all phases of projects and building a trust relationship with the local authorities by clarifying for the local authorities the impact of the project on the region. The SSD is committed to all humanitarian principles, especially neutrality and impartiality.•In a complex conflict environment, all changes in conflict are expected. Thus, the SSD follows any developments in the project area, conducts a daily risk assessment, and informs the crisis cell of possible scenarios and mitigations, including updating the evacuation and contingency plans (attached contingency plan)•In ATAA, the safety of field staff comes first, especially arrests, kidnappings, or even direct targeting. Therefore, the staff is trained on humanitarian principles, neutrality, movement clearance, as well as the provision of S&S equipment. ATAA is committed to the duty of care towards staff in accordance with the HR policy.•In terms of COVID-19 and Cholera, As a part of risk management, ATAA trains all staff on personal hygiene and continuously measures staff temperatures in addition to providing hand sanitizers. ATAA will distribute brochures electronically through WhatsApp groups to the local authorities and community on Cholera as possible.
