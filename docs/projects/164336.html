Project Summary

Sadad has been providing protection services for 3 years in south and north of Idleb , but due to the latest security developments, Sadad was obliged to relocate its centers and field staff to Azaz city in North of Aleppo After coordinating with the sub-cluster of GBV, where the majority of the beneficiaries displaced to.
This new project is planning to enhance the Life-saving protection services of Sadad centers to be more comprehensive and reachable for more numbers of new IDPs arrivals , in addition to the neglected and vulnerable women and girls, and marginalized persons (especially PWDs), through:
- The relocation to the areas of new displacement.
Sadad will work on tracking IDPs from Idlib countryside and other locations. It is estimated that nearly over 75% of population have been fled or planning to flee from the Idlib countryside to safer locations to seek refuge.
Sadad is planning to establish a new WGSS center in Azaz city as it is considered a safer and under-served location with high numbers of IDPs and residents.
Sadad will provide capacity building training activities through internal resource and will establish a committee to consult with all beneficiaries through activities implementation to ensure survivors participation in selecting.
According to 2019 Voices report, various forms of GBV are experienced primarily by women and girls including restriction of freedoms, denial of services, domestic violence, early marriage. Men are generally perceived in Syria as the head of the family with great power over the freedoms and the movement of the female members of their families.
Sadad aims to conduct protection project for 12 months and will take place in WGSS in Azaz Sub-district. Activities will be:-Provide life-saving specialized quality GBV services، including case management، psychosocial support and reinforce referral pathways.
-The most vulnerable and crisis-affected women and girls are empowered with the skills required which include vocational training and life skills courses.
-GBV prevention activities and outreach activities will be conducted in WGSS located in Azaz sub-district. The activities will include recreational activities such as development of peer-support-networks, development of communication skills, positive thinking, time management, self-confidence, self-esteem and future-planning. The life skills activities will entail literarcy course, computer skills, English language course, Psychological First Aid (PFA) and non-specialized individual as well as group counselling. For vocational education sewing courses will be offered. Recreational activities such as sport will be offered for boys where they will also receive messages on gender equality and GBV-sessions. Throughout all these activities GBV survivors will be identified.
-Enhance inclusiveness of WGSS for women and girls with disabilities through the rollout of available training package and technical guidance beside to improve access to the facility for persons with disabilities such as installing slopes, lateral side ladders and other required modifications.
- Protection Monitoring and cash assistance. The team will map available services targeting IDPs, host communities in the wider area, and establish referral pathways with them. Finally, mobile protection monitoring teams will conduct household and community level assessments and provide information to beneficiaries about services and facilitate referrals based on initial service mapping and beneficiary assistance criteria, in the meantime (within the household visits) PM team will be working on determining cases that require the cash assistance and refer them to the cash intervention that Sadad is already implementing in the area of intervention.
-Sadad will train SOP staff in prevention and response to SEA Life-saving specialized quality SGBV services are being provided to girls, boys and women.SOP new staff will be trained on case management psycho-social support and CMR.

4 - Needs

Over the area of Northwest Syria, women and children continue to undergo multiple protection risks and rights violations as a crisis influence. GBV and child rights violations sits as stringent apprehension. This environment leaves the psychosocial needs of many girls and boys unmet, leading to experiences of profound distress. Recruitment and use of children increased sharply in some areas; where children, particularly adolescent boys, are used in both combat roles as well as in support roles. UNFPA’s 2020 Whole of Syrian Voices report also noted child marriage as a major protection concern, placing girls at a particular risk to domestic violence, divorce, early pregnancy, curtailed educational opportunities and psychological harm.Furthermore, both returnees and IDP communities across NW Syria face mounting difficulties in meeting their protection needs due to the fact that protection services are stretched, and the provision of humanitarian assistance is primarily focused on delivering shelter and food supplies to the most vulnerable. Due to limited protection capacity/presence on the ground it remains a challenge to ensure that vulnerable populations (including host communities) have meaningful access to sustained protection interventions and have the means to build and maintain accountability systems for these interventions. Needs of affected populations are further exacerbated by lack of effective legal support, which has been identified as a major gap in both Idlib and Aleppo Governorate. According to the OCHA 7th, Situation report for 29th, Jan.2020 more than 390,000 IDPs, where displaced starting from Dec2019 from Marret alnouman and surrounding and the Northern and western countryside of Aleppo continues to receive huge influxes of IDPs every day, those people are in basic needs of everything and displacement is negatively affecting local communities economic situation.This displacement has resulted in many needs for women and girls.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Population in the targeted locations are familiar with Sadad’s WGSSs and trust the staff which will positively reflect on the number of people reached with accessible GBV and services through this project.Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas. Changes could increase the risks faced, displace or restrict movements of the population, local partners and others relevant to the project. Depending on how these changes manifest, they could make the projects activities less appropriate to the new situation, make it difficult, unsafe or not possible for beneficiaries to access services or staff/consultants to provide services.Planned risk response: Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners, civil authorities and relevant security and access stakeholders.Services duplication as many organizations that also working on providing GBV activities. Sadad will do high coordination with GBV Sub cluster depending on the services map to make sure that there is a GBV need with no sufficient responders in the targeted areas. in addition to establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place. 4- Difficulties in coordination with other sectors regarding to the Referral path way during case management services, as each organization has its criteria in selecting beneficiaries especially Livelihood Programs which can be a restriction of doing an effective referral path way. Sadad will work on more coordination with other sectors by setting service mapping for all services that exist in addition to be aware of the other sectors criteria trying to do more advocacy and mainstreaming for GBV concerns and needs .Sadad will ensure continuous updating of the GBV exit plan as needed.
