Project Summary

The main goal of the proposed project is to deliver immediate humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable IDP people in camps and member of host communities (HC) in Afrin complemented with a livelihoods activity to improve the resilience of 50 households through small grants. The project intends to integrate livelihood, food security and wash using cash as a modality. In North-West Syria (NWS), the price of basic food commodities remains volatile and is subject to speculation. Disruptive events, specifically displacement and COVID-19 have harmful and immediate consequences on the households’ economy andmay result in reduced consumption and exclusion, requiring the adoption of a framework of priorities and actions that are focused on livelihoods. Proposed intervention has 2 components:(1) distribution of cash to internally displaced persons(IDPs) in camps and vulnerable HC members to cover their urgent lifesaving needs (2) Small grants for 50 women to improve their resilience towards external shocks through a sustainable livelihood.
Regarding the Early Recovery cluster section, WHH aims at improving the resilience of 50 women through the provision of small business grants. Each grant will be worth 500 USD and enable the beneficiaries to start her own small business. Potential areas of business are textile, handcrafts, livestock, agriculture, and others. The women will receive a training that educates the beneficiaries in (a) basic profit/loss accounting and (b) marketing. WHH will further assist with market analysis. To avoid that the grant is used for ineligible own-consumption purposes, the grants will be provided in two tranches. After completing the first phase of the activity, the grantees will receive the second tranche. Moreover, the women will be assisted for eight months with cash vouchers in line with the activity introduced above.

4 - Needs

Economic situation in NWS further exacerbates humanitarian needs as prices of basic items further increased. COVID-19 (C19) has added another layer of complexity to a what is already a humanitarian disaster becoming a complete catastrophe. According to REACH (01/2021), a regular daily worker must work for 64 days to earn the income necessary for the SMEB. This matches WHH data concerning HH economic profiles (04/2020), that states that people in Afrin only have around 50% of the financial needs per month to buy needed items. During last 6 months, prices for SMEB increased in Afrin by 28% further hindering access for vulnerable people where food part of SMEB rose in Afrin by 30%, while hygiene related share rose in Afrin by 3%. Humanitarian needs are severe across all sectors, in many cases due to the lack of livelihood opportunities, caused by movement restrictions in/off camps. According to REACH almost 57% of assessed residents and 51% of IDPs in NWS report livelihoods as a priority need. These findings match with WHH MSNA from 04/2021 in A’zaz & Afrin where 53% of respondents mentioned employment opportunities as their main priority. HNAP (03/2021) records livelihood opportunities as a priority need for 57% in Afrin. The lack of livelihood opportunities combined with increase in prices for food and other SMEB related items leads to a further increase in humanitarian needs and dependency onhumanitarian assistance & the use of negative coping mechanism; reducing number of meals, borrowing food, relying on debt, child labor and early marriage andothers consumption as well as livelihoods based negative coping mechanisms (WHH MSNA 04/2021).These coping mechanisms create a severe risk ofmalnutrition as well protection risk.
