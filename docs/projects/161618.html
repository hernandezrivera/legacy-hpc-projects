Project Summary

The insurgency in Nigeria continues to affect the population living in the North-East. According to the Needs Overview by the Early Recovery & Livelihoods sector(August 2019) an estimated 5.5 million people are in need of early recovery assistance, 1 million are IDPs in host communities, 1.5 million IDPs and returnees, and 2.9 million people in host communities. This includes 1.5 million girls, 1.3 million boys and 0.9 mullion women in the most affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe State.
Several LGAs have been prioritized based on the levels of damages and service delivery burden associated with high numbers of returnees and IDPs in host communities: Damboa, Konduga, Monguno (Borno State); Madagali, Mubi North, Hong (Adamawa State); and Gujba, Potiskum and Fika (Yobe State).
The 27th round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM - May 2019), estimated the number of IDPs and Returnees to be 3,602,943 (1,980,036 IDPS and 1,622,908 Returnees), 92.6% of whom have been displaced due to the Boko Haram insurgency in the North East. The number of returnees went up by 64,850 to take the total number to 1,622,908 (or 265,649 households) from 1,558,058 returnees that were assessed in the last round of assessment conducted in January 2019. Out of the total number of displaced people, 133,003 IDPs and 1,622,908 Returnees are currently in Yobe State (The 27th round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM - May 2019) Ongoing crisis has worsen the situation including disruption of market supplies and livelihoods, and significantly limiting the populations having access to livelihoods. IOM estimates that 60% of the IDPs reside with host communities.
As situation in Yobe state is stabilizing, COOPI proposes livelihood activities in Yusufari,Geidam, Potiskum, Fika and Gulani LGAs. The project will target 5,865 households

(approximatively 24,173 beneficiaries) in the project. Beneficiaries will be supported in order to foster the transition from emergency to resilience as COOPI is committed to apply the humanitarian-development nexus approach and ensure coherence between the actors. COOPI intends to protect and rehabilitate livelihoods of most vulnerable groups, pre-identified through COOPI’s ongoing food assistance and CBT activities funded by other donors, through technical trainings on adapted livelihood practices where possible in coordination and complementarity with ongoing line ministries and sector. COOPI will complement proposed activities with EU funded early recovery programs in NE. COOPI is implementing EU supported Yobe package and will supplement all livelihood activities and link to the humanitarian development nexus.
COOPI forecast strong potential for humanitarian development nexus through its presence in Yobe and particularly in proposed LGA. COOPI has already developed liaison and MOUs with state government’s line departments as well as providing capacity building opportunities to government institutes. COOPI will also consider the livelihood action to be linked with other sectors which are planned to be executed as multiyear response mainly to ensure transition from emergency to recovery and rehabilitation including conflict management. COOPI will consider to work through local NGOs. Implementation of current activities in the area have revealed a deterioration in livelihoods sources primarily due to frequent conflicts and inherent hardships in the state as well as climactic conditions and increased market demands. COOPI, following Early Recovery and Livelihood sector's objectives, intends to protect and rehabilitate livelihoods of most vulnerable groups, identified through technical trainings on adapted practices and formation and training of livelihood beneficiaries focusing on women groups who are already engaged with various small enterprises. COOPI will ensure that composition of sectors is defined based on COOPI Community Based Participatory Planning sessions.

1 - Needs

In April 2019, COOPI conducted Livelihood Market Assessment in Yobe State to assess retail market system, wholesale market system, consumable products market system, agricultural produce market system, construction market system, service market or vocational skills market system and financial market system. In 2018, COOPI conducted rapid assessment in Northern Yobe to examine the impact of the conflict on livelihoods issue including the effect of any shock at the household level. Finding from the KIIs and FGDs show their livelihood base is deteriorating from time to time and most vulnerable coping mechanism (like borrowing, social security) are affected. Most of the population in Yobe State relies on daily labour and livestock production for subsistence and income, with more than 80% of citizens reportedly engaged in small-scale subsistence farming. Markets status varies from town to town and across LGAs: the main urban towns such as Damaturu, Potiskum, Nguru, Gashua and Geidam currently have functional markets, and Potiskum Local Government Area (LGA) is home to one of the largest cattle markets in West Africa. The insurgency hit market infrastructure, with fuel stations, water pumps, and storage facilities destroyed, damaged, or closed. Half of all agricultural households in Yobe do not have access to agricultural storage infrastructure, increasing the risk of post-harvest losses. Deforestation, due to burning by insurgents and displaced populations’ need for cooking fuel, will have lasting economic and environmental impacts if not effectively managed in the short-term. Crop pests such as Fall Armyworm have also impacted yields in Northeast Nigeria. The supply of fertilizer, pesticide, and herbicide dropped to about half of pre-conflict levels, and have not fully recovered. Input suppliers lack rural outreach and marketing strategies to distribute their produces ‘over the last mile’ and into the hands of rural farmers, and sometimes only sell one product, forcing farmers to visit multiple input dealers to access all demanded inputs. COOPI is proposing a project that is aligned with ER sector’s Strategic Objective 1“Improve access to infrastructure and basic social services through rehabilitation and reconstruction of public service” and SO 2 “Support livelihood stabilization and income recovery” will roll out livelihoods recovery and promotion.,5865 households will be targeted, for approximatively 24,173 beneficiaries in the project. Proposed Livelihoods interventions reflects the recommendations of the Yobe State Socio-Economic Reform Agenda (YOSERA), produced by the State Ministry of Budget and Economic Planning. The WFP livelihoods assessment in Damaturu, Bade and Jakusko (Rapid Livelihood Assessment, April 2018) and result from COOPI’s recent community consultation, FGDs and KIIs reveal the needs of livelihood support to households and recommends the implementation of livelihoods and other conditional assistance through engaging communities. Based on COOPI’s past experience through its presence in proposed LGAs and implementing FS and IGA since 2015, will carry out Cash for Work, Income Generation Activities and conditional cash transfers as well provide capacity building to beneficiaries and government line department staff. COOPIs field observation is showed Yobe is appropriate for cash transfers and voucher modality. Beneficiaries will be selected among current in order to foster the transition from emergency to resilience as COOPI is committed to apply the humanitarian-development nexus approach and ensure coherence between the actors. CBT and food assistance beneficiaries from COOPI’s existing programs, will be selected as per their skills and links to various livelihood activities finalised through community consultation, CBPP and will be registered as livelihood beneficiaries. All asset creation or restoration initiatives will be designed in a way to maximize the potential for cash for work opportunities.

2 - Activities

Strategic Objective 1: Improve access to infrastructure and basic social services through rehabilitation and reconstruction of public service facilities Activity 1.1: Vulnerable households receive conditional cash transfers – cash for work, providing CFW to 1000 households over the course of 9 months. Activity 1.2: Rehabilitation/construction of agri farms, infrastructure, water access points in markets, schools, and/or health centres through Cash for Work. During COOPI’s focus groups and KIIs, all FGDs and a majority of KIIs indicated that access to water is a key constraint to productive livelihoods, health, and is a potential driver of conflict. Activity 1.3 male and female youth aged 18-24 receive skills-training for in-demand sectors and/or are referred to market-aligned vocational training programs Activity 1.4: Support environment clean-up, and rehabilitation of local infrastructure 1.5: Restoration of critical local infrastructure at community level which can support generating employment Strategic Objective 2: Support livelihood stabilization and income recovery Activity 2.1: Conduction of baseline and end-line Activity 2.2: Beneficiaries identification/ CBPP verification Activity 2.3: Community mobilisation and sensitisation Activity 2.4: Defining modalities of cash transfers Activity 2.5: Identify improved seed vendors in target LGAs Activity 2.6: 1,000 male and female farmers (50% female, 20% youth aged 18 to 24) have skills in climate-resilient agricultural techniques and effective post-harvest storage. COOPI will adapt the approach to the specific agro-ecosystems of Yobe State Strategic Objective 3: Support Restoration of Local Governance Administration and Services. Activity 3.1: Consultation meetings and sensitization sessions with line ministry staff Activity 3.2: Conduction of capacity building training for local authorities staff Identify farmer and livestock associations/cooperatives for strengthening; or create new associations/cooperatives Activity 3.3: 20 Farmer and livestock associations created or strengthened. Farmer and livestock cooperatives and associations are able to negotiate a number of benefits for members, such as access to inputs and training, market information, higher prices for goods and services through bulk purchasing, and reduced transaction and storage costs Activity 3.4 30 Establishment of Community resilience groups, with diverse representation, to develop community resilience plans and conflict prevention forums. Activity 3.5: Conduct conflict prevention and conflict management dialogue sessions

COOPI will also put into place systems/mechanisms by which beneficiaries can channel or express their grievances and seek redress. COOPI will institute two methods for complaints and feed backs: A. Complaint Desk: COOPI will set up a complaint desk in the community during crucial activities as well as during payment of beneficiaries, to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries and concerned community members.
