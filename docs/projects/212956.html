Project Summary

Through sustainable, adequate, and stable food availability, accessibility to all food items that are needed, utilized, and affordable by all people in a fair and decent way, and preservation of people's dignity through facilitating accessibility for those foods and enhancing their continued availability in the market, Silk Road seeks to support the affected people in Aleppo and Idleb in the districts of Idleb, Harim, Afrin, and Azaz. The project will benefit about 12,000 households, or roughly 72,000 individuals, mostly made up of low-income IDP women and households led by them. The monthly distribution voucher is valued at US$50. This kit is designed to guarantee that the FSL cluster's recommendation of 1500–2000 Kcal/person/day is met. The implementation plan is predicated on the monthly vouchers that are disbursed to eligible families. This ensures that each family receives benefits from the voucher program for eight consecutive rounds, or a total of 12,000 families for a period of seven months. attaining eight distribution rounds, since the final two rounds will take place in the final month. whereby the final distribution round will be carried out by the end of the final month to be consumed during the upcoming month. enhance safety and dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation.All project staff and stakeholders will share an ethical responsibility for mainstreaming protection across the humanitarian response, including general and sector staff, programming staff, advocacy staff, design, monitoring, and evaluation staff, and their managers.Priority will be given to the following categories:IDP families, with a focus on IDP families headed by women.PwD will be given priority within this program.women/boys-headed households.Families with a family size above six members do not have a breadwinner.Silk Road will take into consideration girls, separated or unaccompanied girls and boys, orphans, women heads of household, and people with disabilities. As well, the criteria will focus on GBV survivors and women and girls at risk of GBV:Women and girls head households, Women and girls with physical or mental disabilities, Widows, divorcees, and pregnantpriority:New IDP families. Families with low income.Criteria for selecting sellers:Seller's ability to re-supply goods The diversity of goods in the marketThe accessibility of beneficiaries to the market Number of days and hours worked The reputation of the seller in the regionFees or commissions payable by the organization, if anyCooperation with the organization and the seller's willingness to accept the terms and conditions of the payments1. Also, within the first month of the project, we will conduct the seller's capacity assessment, which will include the frequency of re-supply of goods focusing on the goods we plan to distribute, the availability of a warehouse close to the shop, notes by the team on the seller's skills in dealing with customers, as well as the number of customers who came during the evaluation period, reputation and the seller's interest in participating in the program, and finally, the financial capacity of the seller. Furthermore, we will update the assessment of prices, the current situation of the local market, and the most requested goods by the local population before identifying the seller.2. In this project, we will use unconditional vouchers (food for a fixed monetary value) (value-based vouchers) in the selected shop that allow sufficient flexibility for the beneficiaries. In this case, the beneficiaries will receive 8 rounds of distribution to give the vulnerable people the ability to continue depending on their own resources. as we will work to achieve minimum standards for food consumption.
