Project Summary

This project aims at building an active social and learning environment that permits children and adolescent to enroll, learn and complete the school cycle in 4 schools in 2 subdivisions. The project also will enable teachers to conduct research and self-training as part of capacity building to improve their performance and enhance the quality of teaching.
UNICEF goal is to connect 02 subdivisions in the Zest and Littoral regions with Internet services precisely Menoua (West region) and Wouri (Littoral).
Presently the Spanish Natcom for UNICEF is financing the connection for 8 schools in East Region with Internet services. A field visit was conducted to visualize how the children are using the tablets with both online and offline content. The children showed a mastery of the usage and this will go a long way to improve on learning and ICT capacity of the underprivileged children in these schools. The tablets with both online and offline content are improving the learning of children. This project will provide teachers and learners not only with learning ICT, but also learning through ICT, it will also aid actors of the educational supervision chain to ensure a better supervision by relying on tools existing through ICTs. This will contribute in improving the quality of education. It will then be a question of revitalizing the internal and external participatory management structures in the schools so that they can mobilize for children in general and most especially girls and other vulnerable children. Thus, it will consequently improve the school retention in the locality of the project.
ICT (Information and communication technology) tools will be installed, these tools will be used to provide children and teachers with learning platforms that gives opportunity not only to learn ICTs, but also to learn using ICTs and through ICTs as prescribed by the National policy of teaching ICTs in the primary cycle.
The ICT equipment will also support the implementation of the pedagogical supervision model and monitor the attendance of pupils and teachers thereby making information available to the authorities quicker for a quick decision making. This sustainable and attractive method in making changes in the access and retention of pupil in school will be supported by the entire educational community. From this, the project will focus on activities to strengthen the technical and logistical capacities of the internal participatory Management structures (Parent Teachers Association, Association of Pupils’ mothers, School Councils, Management committees, Children's Government) and external (community-based organizations) at the school for the enrolment, monitoring attendance and retention of children in school, especially girls.
The project will consist of constructing e-containers in the 04 selected schools that will serve as multimedia center for the sites and the communities. These schools will be connected with internet using solar power and connectivity system to provide electricity. Materials such as desktops, tablets will be handed to the selected schools. The school administrators in collaboration with the community will make sure the materials are well kept and managed. The teachers will be trained on the use of ICT in schools, on the usage of the tools to improve learning. They will also be trained on how to manage these materials and on the sustainability side of the project.
The project will be monitored by UNICEF in collaboration with the Government, computer start-ups and the community.
At the end of the project, it is expected that in each school: students will demonstrate learning improvements, learning ICT based platforms will be ameliorated, teachers will use ICT to improve their teaching, school enrolment and retention for learners and teachers will be improving.

1 - Besoins / Needs

According to the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan, Cameroon has approximately 4.4 million people in humanitarian need, including 1.9 million children in need of education and training services. The multiple displacements following the different crises exacerbated by the Covid-19 health pandemic have created enormous and growing needs in the Education and Protection Sector in general, as a result of school dropout, risks of sexual violence and exploitation, as well as juvenile delinquency and psychological trauma caused by population movements. The PPR takes into account the specific needs related to this group of children in crisis-affected areas, particularly in areas with high pressure on limited school resources and infrastructure.Many girls and boys in Cameroon have been out of the school system for years. The project will provide a gender-sensitive alternative educational offer adapted to the needs of boys and girls who are in school, as well as those who have been out of school and cannot re-enter for a variety of reasons, including their age. The project will be coupled with community sensitization and mobilization activities to send all children to school with a focus on girls and children living with disabilities.
