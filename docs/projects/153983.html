Project Summary

Medair will support the 2019 HRP Strategic Objectives through implementing activities aligned with the SHE/NFI cluster objectives. Medair will provide war-damaged repairs and basic SOKs and NFI kits where appropriate, only to those who are at risk of being secondarily displaced due to their vulnerability. Medair will focus on peri-urban and smaller village communities in multi-sectoral responses (SHE/NFI, Health, WASH, MPCA), focusing on returnees. However, Medair will retain the capacity to respond to protracted IDP needs in camp and non-camp settings, newly displaced IDPs, and vulnerable host/stayee communities to uphold their safety and well-being.
In accordance with the cluster standards, Medair will also support returnees, stayees, protracted IDPs and those unreached by social protection services to have access to basic shelter and NFI needs. Medair will also be positioned to respond to emergency displacements in and around disputed areas with SHE/NFI support.
In Ninewa governorate, areas of interventions include Sinjar, Telafar, Tilkaif and Mosul districts. In Kirkuk governorate, the main focus will be on providing support in Hawiga district and surrounding villages to reach returnee, stayee and other vulnerable households without access to basic shelter and NFI needs. Assessments are being conducted before the start of 2019 in Salah al-Din governorate (particularly Tikrit and Tooz districts) to prepare for an expected response.

Needs

Since the successful operations to retake previously occupied areas in 2017, there has been a reduction in displacements and impact on conflict affected populations. However, there has been an increase in instability, with isolated attacks on local authorities, checkpoints, and key infrastructure. Additionally, IDPs have not returned to their areas of origin at the rate previously anticipated by the government and humanitarian community (IOM DTM); therefore there remain high needs among protracted IDPs in and out of camps, in addition to the needs of returnees, host communities and stayees. These issues require a continued capacity to respond to emergency needs which arise as a result of these attacks or other potential shocks, in addition to continuous programming targeting highly vulnerable populations. Medair will target those IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable groups living in camps, collective centres, unfinished/abandoned buildings; war damaged houses and looted homes, with difficulty accessing assistance and services with shelter and NFI support following the SHE/NFI technical guidelines. Conflict affected populations remain vulnerable with little to no access to social protection services, and vulnerabilities to protection violations remain high. Those highly vulnerable men, women, boys, girls, older persons, persons with disabilities or chronic conditions, and those facing human rights violations will need priority support in order to bridge a period from their displacement to a more sustenance solution. 45% to 59% of people report not intending to return to their places of origin due to the need of reconstruction activities (REACH - IDP Intentions Survey, October 2018).The proposed locations are those that Medair has an existing presence in Health, WASH, Shelter/NFI and MPCA sectors. Though the proposed project will target multiple locations throughout Ninewa, Kirkuk and Salah al Din governorates, many of the anticipated SHE/NFI needs will likely be similar across locations. Medair will undertake community-specific needs assessments throughout the upcoming year in order to precisely identify needs and inform targeted project design. Medair plans to be responsive to changing needs and priority locations as the context develops. Providing SHE/NFI assistance will not be a standalone activity. Medair will integrate these activities with ongoing Health, WASH and MPCA programming in order to provide a more comprehensive approach to maximize the impact of its activities, and provide more cohesive multi-sectoral needs assessments to all appropriate clusters. SHE/NFI activities will link directly to WASH and Health activities in certain contexts. Medair will seek links with protection actors, particularly HLP actors to respond appropriately. Though it is anticipated that returns will continue to areas of origin, there are also IDPs, many in protracted displacement in camps and non-camps, that may not be able to return due to safety and security reasons, and that are in need of continued support and assistance with SHE/NFIs. In Kirkuk and Salah al-Din governorates, returnees are continuing to occur; however one of the main reasons for not returning is damage to homes and buildings. There remain IDPs in camp and non-camp settings with restricted movements due to insecurity and ethnic tensions. In Ninewa governorate, in Sinjar, Mosul and Telafar districts returnees continue to be slow, and those that have returned to their areas of origin are met with high level damage to their homes. Throughout affected areas in Ninewa the reported level of damage to homes is 82%, ranging from completely destroyed to partially destroyed (REACH Informal sites IDPs intention survey, Aug 2018). In Sinjar district, some families are returning to their areas of origin in the villages by the Syrian border or in the north of Sinjar. The need of assistance remains high for the returnee groups with lack of basic services and destruction to home in areas of origin (Medair RNA in Ain Hessan, Fao, Bir Jary, and Ayadyea on September- November 2018, REACH Intention survey August 2018). Returns the south of Sinjar mountain are slower due to the ongoing decontamination activities, high levels of destruction and ongoing insecurity and ethnic tensions.In the upcoming year, Medair’s response plan will focus on post-emergency phase interventions and will implement a targeted approach including the usage of the socio-economic vulnerablity tools that are familiar to Medair staff (CWG tool) and other tools under development (New taskforce CWG tool, CwC and guidelines such as HLP, WDS and NFI/SOKs). Medair will focus on war damage shelter and NFI activities for returnees mainly in Kirkuk and Salah al Din, as significant gaps exist in Tikrit and Hawija districts in particular. Shelter and NFI support for IDPs out of camp will focus on identified areas in Ninewa as well as Tooz and Tikrit districts in Salah al Din due to to current gaps.

Protection Risk Analysis

In areas of intervention, protection risks will be analyzed and mitigation measures will be developed to incorporate into project design and implementation. Overall protection issues may include unequal access to benefits and services due to gender, age, ethnicity, and religion (among others) and other associated discrimination by authorities and armed groups. Beneficiaries may have difficulties with identification documents, forced evictions and returns, camp closures, lack of services, restricted movements and insecurity. Shelter/NFI intervention-specific issues may include the risk of increasing social tensions between beneficiaries and non-recipients of services and distributions. Additionally, Shelter/NFI distributions are not sustainable, and are typically delivered via single distribution in times of need. There may also be discrepancies between the targeted beneficiaries and the most vulnerable in the target community, with some of the most vulnerable remaining unreached. Additionally, there may be risks associated with staff delivery and distribution of kits, construction and repairs of shelters.

Mitigation Measures

All activities will take into consideration protection concerns ensuring a “Do No Harm” approach. Protection considerations surrounding employment of female staff include ability to travel (cultural impediments) safely. Staff will be supported as necessary to ensure safety at work. Beneficiary vulnerability criteria and selection will be coordinated with other actors and will be monitored to mitigate against abuse, including the potential use of the vulnerability assessment tool being developed by the MPCA CWG. To mitigate against unequal access to services and to ensure that the most vulnerable are targeted through the intervention, Medair will work closely with local authorities to identify those most in need while cross-checking against surveys and focus group discussions. Construction site health and safety rules will be signed on by contractors to ensure safety to workforce where possible. Construction site risk assessments will identify risks and strive to mitigate them through careful planning in awareness. Where applicable, female target groups will be consulted prior to intervention and attention given to location of meetings and scheduling to ensure equal opportunities for participation. Background checks will be undertaken to screen staff for acceptability for household visits, and monitoring activities will measure household sentiments of safety during any distributions and interactions with project staff. Where possible field teams will consist of female and male members, to ensure participation and leadership opportunities for women, also to have the ability to reach all beneficiaries (overcoming cultural barriers such as the limitation in women and men interaction). Partner field staff, suppliers, and contractors working on the project sign on Social Accountability Minimum Standards. Infrastructure will be designed according cluster minimum standards with target groups consulted prior to the intervention as to appropriateness of the rehabilitation / installation. To mitigate against unsustainability, Medair will conduct trainings to ensure that beneficiaries know how to use all items included in SOKs and other NFI kits. Issues of HLP are understood by management, and further investment into field teams and beneficiary knowledge will be conducted throughout the project cycle. Additionally, Medair adheres to PSEA guidelines and regulations, and all staff receive this information on induction. Medair has a zero tolerance policy on sexual abuse and related issues, also communicated to all staff.

How is your project accountable to affected population

During the assessment and beneficiary selection phases, Medair will work closely with local community leaders to communicate effectively regarding project activities and vulnerability criteria, aiming to find solutions to any possible tensions arising between social groups as a result of project activities. Where applicable, vulnerable target groups (such as women and girls, elderly and PWDs) will be consulted prior to intervention. Where possible field teams will consist of female and male members, to ensure participation and leadership opportunities for women, also enabling the ability to reach all target group beneficiaries. Households will be consulted prior to intervention as to their specific needs to ensure a tailored response. Focus group discussions will ensure community participation prior to community type interventions. Randomised household post-intervention evaluations will be conducted to evaluate project satisfaction and gain feedback on outcomes. Where possible local casual labour will be used to gain community buy-in to construction projects. Where applicable local councils and community leaders will be consulted for their advice/approval prior to project start. From initial assessments to beneficiary selection stages and evaluation, beneficiaries will be provided information on how to contact Medair, provide feedback, and issue complaints. Medair’s internal logging system for this feedback allows follow up by senior managers if issue are not tracked or dealt with in a timely manner. Please see the Medair Accountability to Affected Populations document attached for additional information.
