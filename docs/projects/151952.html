Project Summary

This project aims at strengthening safe and timely access to Gender-Based Violence (GBV) services among the conflict affected populations as well as migrants and refugees throughout Libya in order to promote their healing, safety and well-being.
Recognizing that women and girls are disproportionately affected by GBV, the target population of this project is mostly female, with a specific focus on adolescent and young girls, and elderly. This project will work to ensure that vulnerable minorities have access to services, namely the Tawergha community, and refugees and migrants (in communities and detention centers). Persons with disabilities will be prioritized across the targeted populations to ensure that all services are accessible to them. Men and boys are also subjected to GBV in Libya, especially conflict-related sexual violence. Anecdotal evidence reports ruthless stories of men – especially vulnerable minorities and Tawerghans – coerced into raping other men and women in an attempt to cover up a perfect crime of political revenge that will leave an unhealable scar on a generation of Libyans, refugees and migrants. Men and boys will thus be targeted by the services in an attempt to help them overcome the profound wounds and involve them in the fight against gender-based violence.
In total, this project is expected to reach 100,000 direct beneficiaries. It is worth noting that some of these beneficiaries will have access to more than one service, like dignity kits procured by UNFPA and distributed by local INGOS. Additionally, UNFPA is operating in collaboration with several implementing partners as explained further, and is looking at scaling up the interventions in an effort to cover the existing gaps and as provider of last resort.
Static and outreach activities will be started in women's centers, one in each of the locations of Al Kufra and Murzuq, based on the findings of the protection sector on severity. Additionally, current activities carried out by UNFPA and implementing partners in Tripoli (3 centers with CESVI, Al Safa and Al Bayan), Benghazi (1 center with PSS Team and Amazonet), Sebha (1 center with Women's Union) and Misrata ( 1 center with IRC) will be sustained. The project will be carried out in synergy with other sectors.
Close coordination with IOM and UNHCR is envisaged, in particular, for the implementation of the GBV prevention and response services in selected detention centers as well as in the to be established Gathering and Departure Facility once operational under UNHCR management. These activities will specifically aim at strengthening the detection of GBV survivors and migrant women at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, providing GBV and Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services and improving the referral systems for cases of survivors among migrants, refugees and vulnerable Libyans. Using innovative approaches, UNFPA will target migrant and refugee women and people-at-risk with messages raising awareness on their basic rights and informing on the availability of SRH and GBV response services, while engaging young community leaders to be agents of behavioral change in host communities and reduce the stigma on displaced people, particularly women and GBV survivors. Outreach activities carried out by volunteers from the Scouts and Y-Peer network will ensure a participatory approach for boys, girls, adolescents and adults (In 2017 and 2018, UNFPA conducted several focus group discussions and consultations with youth and adults). Given the sensitivity of GBV in conflict in a conservative society and the nature of the activities to be carried out, the need for highly specialized human resources is anticipated.
The approach to GBV case management requires the provision of a multi-sectoral package of services, therefore case managers will be providing psychosocial support and referring survivors to other services such as Clinical Management of Rape (CMR). SRH services will also be provided by UNFPA teams in static and mobile service delivery points, and in collaboration with PUI in some locations. Specialized mental health care will be offered by other organizations such as IMC. UNFPA is also scaling up the capacity of specialized nurses to provide PSS support and mental health services in Tripoli, Misrata and Benghazi. Access to legal and judicial support remains a challenge for GBV survivors in Libya. Efforts will be put on advocacy for the availability of these services. Local and INGO partners in each area will help in designing and implementing the activities in a way to ensure community participation and access to areas which are hard to reach, namely El Safa, El Bayan, PSS Team, Women Union, The Scouts, Libyan Red Crescent, Amazonet, Cesvi, and IRC.
In addition UNFPA seeks to strengthen the overall coordination of GBV in order to upscale GBV prevention, response and risk mitigation in Libya. Currently, UNFPA has deployed a GBV sub-sector coordinator. Key achievements so far include; completion of a GBV service mapping and gap analysis; finalization of the GBV referral pathway for Tripoli; and completion of the Interagency GBV action plan. The sub-sector is currently working on the interagency GBV prevention and response in standards operating procedures. The process of establishing functional GBV working groups in different Mantikas has been initiated. UNFPA envisages that in 2019, at least 3 GBV working groups will be functional inside Libya. The roll out of the Gender Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS) has also been initiated under the leadership of UNFPA. It is envisaged that the process will be achieved and an information sharing protocol will be developed in the last quarter of 2019 to ensure interagency data sharing.

Needs

The security situation in Libya remains volatile. 193,000 Libyans have been internally displaced, in addition to 1,051,401 migrants, refugees and returnees now enduring the consequences of another crisis. According to a recent study conducted by UNFPA (UNFPA, 2017. GBV situational analysis in Libya), women, girls and boys are the most at risk of GBV. The same study further reports 40% of survey respondents in Libya perceiving GBV as common or very common. The study also reports close male relatives, such as spouses, fathers, and brothers as the most common perpetrators. Of major concern is the culture of silence which is fueled by fear of reprisal, gossip and lack of trust about confidentiality among service providers. Furthermore, women face movements restrictions and often have to ask permission from their spouses or close male relatives to access health facilities. Given that most violence occurs at home, women could face constraints seeking GBV related services. Conflict affected populations in Sebha, Murzuq and Al Kufra are in need of urgent protection. A recent multi-sectoral needs assessment in Libya indicate that 46% of IDPs in Alkufra and 19% in Sebha have experienced multiple displacements due to the recent armed conflicts. The IDPs continue to face risks of violence, including GBV. The recent armed clashes in Tripoli also led to multiple displacements especially among the Tawergha community. There is need to continue advocating for safe and dignified return. Heavy damages on shelters have also been reported in Al Kufra and Murzuq which further exacerbates the situation of IDPs and exposes them to risk of GBV. Furthermore, the assessment reports that 33.6% of IDPs in Al Kufra and 24.9% in Sebha engage in negative coping mechanisms like asking strangers for money or food. This could potentially expose women and girls to heightened risks of sexual exploitation and abuse. According to the UNFPA GBV study in Sebha, FGD respondents were largely unaware of any GBV related service provided by any health facilities or organizations in their community. In addition, 43% of health facilities lack medical supplies. According to the UNFPA GBV study, young women aged between 16-22 years in Benghazi are exposed to high risk of GBV. The same study indicates that 40% of survey respondents perceive sexual violence to be common or very common. However only a mere 4% of the respondents are aware of any association, institution or person offering psychological support to women and girls in their community. According to the same study, other barriers to GBV help seeking behaviors in Benghazi include distance of services, lack of confidentiality and risk of stigma for GBV survivors. Among the migrants and refugees and asylum seekers, smugglers, traffickers, criminal gangs and individuals are implicated as perpetrators of rape and other forms of sexual violence. Furthermore, migrants and refugees including women and children continue to be held in unsafe environments where they face harsh treatment. They have reported being taken from their cells and repeatedly raped by officials managing the cells. The detention centres are not separated by gender and often women are subjected to strip searches in front of children. Furthermore, latrines and bathrooms in the detention centres are not also separated for gender further heightening the risks of GBV for women and girls locked in cells. The risks of sexual violence during transit by the migrants seem to be well known even by the smugglers. Women have reported being advised by smugglers and traffickers to carry three months emergency contraceptives while transiting through Libya. Furthermore, boys and men have reported being subjected to rape as a form of torture necessitating the need to ensure specialized GBV services are tailored to the needs of men and boys including GBV risk mitigation. A strong collaboration with IOM and UNHCR will help in ensuring access to these populations.

Activities

1) Setup 2 new women's centers in Alkufra and Murzuq and sustain 6 already functional centers (3 centers with CESVI, Al Safa and Al Bayan), Benghazi (1 center with PSS Team and Amazonet), Sebha (1 center with Women's Union) and Misrata (1 center with IRC) . These centers provide conducive and safe environment for women to meet on a regular basis to seek information about services, rebuild their social networks, engage in life skills sessions and access both individual and group psychosocial support services. 2) Provide services to survivors and individuals at risk of GBV for IDPs, returnees, migrants in detention centers, Gathering and Departure Facility, and communities.3) Distribute dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age following the distribution sector guidelines.4) Provide Psychological First Aid and information on available services through an emergency hotline functional on a 24/7 basis.5) Conduct information dissemination sessions on GBV including existing/established GBV referral pathways.6) Support weekly peer education sessions on GBV and gender concepts as well as the provision of youth friendly services exclusively done by and with young women in the established centers.7) Rollout and maintain the GBV Information Management System8) Strengthen the capacity of frontline workers and community members in the provision of a holistic package of services to survivors and individuals at risk of GBV including referral to services.9) Conduct safety audits and implement safety plans accordingly10) Initiate GBV working groups at Mantika level to ensure the coordination of humanitarian activities on GBV in aforementioned regions, including at least 2 representatives from Y-PEER. UNFPA is confident about achieving the ambitious target set in this project. Over the last two years, UNFPA has strengthened its internal human resources capacity. UNFPA has now a fully-fledged office established with four times the effective of human resources and three times funding available compared to December 2017, and several grants in the pipeline to expand the current pilot programme building on the lessons learnt. UNFPA has already identified a number of new partners, which would allow effective and speedy implementation in the locations where GBV services are currently lacking. The proposed locations are deemed accessible and safe for humanitarian response. UNFPA therefore seeks to strength current GBV services in 2 locations and expand to 5 other locations where needs are particularly high. The proposed project is in line with UNFPA mandate as the provider of last resort (concept is key to the cluster approach. It means that the global cluster lead agencies commit their utmost to ensure that the response to an emergency is adequate and appropriate and, when assuming cluster leadership at the field level, to make every effort to address any gaps themselves if cluster partners are unable to).

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

Beneficiaries are consulted within the areas of residence and in locations they have chosen, where they feel safe and comfortable to speak. In addition, same-sex interviews are organized in private and confidential spaces to ensure privacy. Furthermore, the consultations are conducted in line with the WHO standards and guidelines. UNFPA has a zero tolerance policy on PSEA. Its mandatory for newly recruited staff to receive the PSEA training prior to deployment. In addition, UNFPA ensures that implementing partners staff are trained on PSEA and supported to establish and sustain internal procedures for reporting and investigations. At the interagency level, UNFPA is leading the roll out of SEA prevention in Libya including the establishment of the in-country PSEA network and community based complaints mechanism.
