Project Summary

Harare with a population of 2 123 132 million people, has been facing chronic water supply and quality problems over recent years due to aging water and sanitation infrastructure, eroded institutional and community water management capacity, drought, water bodies pollution and increased population causing a strain on current reticulation facilities. The current drought has negatively affected the provision of safe water thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases. According to the Rural WASH Information Management System (RWIMS) of July 2019, 28.8 per cent of the rural water points nationally are seasonal and only 12.74 per cent are good yielding and perennial. The same RWIMS report points out that only 49.4 per cent of water points (mainly hand pumps) are currently fully functional. As of the June 2019 ZIMVAC Assessment report, showed that 16 per cent of households travelled more than 1km to fetch water from the nearest main water source and 32 per cent over 500m, above the Sphere standards. The risk of drought will further increase the burden (especially for children and women) of fetching water who sometimes have to fetch water at night putting them at a high risk of sexual exploitation with cases of rape/sexual/physical/verbal abuse of women and girls at water points in Harare on the increase as a result of continued water supply challenges. The last Cholera Outbreak experienced in September 2018 had Harare western suburbs namely Glen view and Budiriro suburbs as epicenters. Harare province alone contributed 97% of these cases as at 20 October 2018 (Cholera sitrep 2018-10-20 ZWE). The Western Suburbs namely Glenview, Budiriro, Glennorah have in addition remained at risk of typhoid. The cumulative figures for typhoid are 6 031 suspected cases, 165 confirmed cases and 12 deaths according to the MOHCC Weekly surveillance report of 3 November 2019. Over the past 2 months, the city has recorded a surge in diarrheal cases. Oxfam as the Provincial Focal agency for the Harare Metro Province for WASH issues recognizes a need for an emergency response to the situation currently obtaining in some suburbs of the city to avert a looming humanitarian crisis. The response priorities include provision of safe water through the drilling of 60 boreholes, rehabilitation of 100 borehole rehabilitation, installation of 80 inline chlorinators for point of use water treatment, construction of 30 piped water schemes, hygiene promotion to reach out to 214000 community members in the targeted suburb. The intervention will also conduct rapid contact tracing and response to reported WASH related diarrhoeral diseases and distribute hygiene kit to the affected households and neighbors for promoting safe water chain and handwashing. Safeguarding issues including gender and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and abuse (PSEA) in the community and at water-points will be addressed through awareness raising, strengthening reporting channels and construction of piped water scheme which tends to reduce the time spend in fetching water. Oxfam have piloted a bush-pump inline chlorinator which serve to guarantee water quality is improved through chlorination. This technology shall be installed on all boreholes to be drilled and repaired to ensure water quality is guaranteed especially given that some boreholes have lately been reported contaminated by sewage seepage as a result of the dilapidated reticulation system. The project shall also promote environmental protection through awareness raising and will advocate for improved WASH service delivery through a review of current WASH related by-laws, operational system and development of a policy briefing paper on WASH for local authority advocacy. In order to capacitate municipality staff on emergency response and other WASH technical skills, Oxfam technical staff namely Public Health Engineers and Public Health Promoters shall be seconded to the respective municipality departments.
