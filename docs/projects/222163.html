Project Summary

This project is grounded in the reality of Aweil East County, where systemic change in the education sector will be needed to drive results for all children in the state, with a focus on girls and children with disabilities, while also supporting recovery and the return of refugees and IDPs. Additionally, given the impact of displacement, returnees, conflicts and crises, and extreme levels of poverty, learners and teachers will require well rounded and holistic support to ensure that they can learn and teach and that children can progress in education. Our theory of Change will focus on conflict sensitive interventions, that move communities from emergency to opportunities, that support their transition into further levels of education and that support their holistic well-being and development. A large portion of the out of school children, especially IDPs, refugees and returnees face multiple intersecting exclusionary factors, for example, young mothers, returnees, children recruited into armed conflict, survivors of conflict related sexual violence and children living with disabilities require targeted responses to ensure their access. A holist package of support that enables access, quality, inclusive practices, wellbeing, safety and a wider range of support will be implemented for steps towards all children’s equal participation.

1. Needs

According to OCHA’s HUMANITARIAN NEEDS AND RESPONSE PLAN for 2024, Approximately 2 million children and adolescents between 6 and 17 years of age (53 per cent are girls), along with 1 million children between 3 and 5 years of age, encounter significant obstacles in accessing education,including early marriage and GBV, especially for girls. The primary focus for 2024 is to enhance access to safe and conducive learning environments for vulnerable groups, particularly IDPs and returnees. Providing a secure and enabling learning environment and facilities with a particular focus on gender-sensitive settings remains a pressing concern. Notably, children with disabilities, especially those residing in crisis-affected areas, continue to receive inadequate support for quality education.According to OCHA's Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2023, over 259,000 people are in need in Aweil East County (up from 235,500 in 2021). This is one of the largest numbers in South Sudan and is approximately 75% of the estimated population for the county reported in the HNO. The county is said to have acute levels of Education needs, including GBV and children protection needs, with over 50% of the population in need of these services according to the 2020 HNO. Aweil East is home to one hundred and eighty-five (185) primary schools and eleven (11) secondary schools located across the county. And according to Reach SS Situation overview Aweil East report October 2024, Nearly eighteen months since the outbreak of fighting in Khartoum in April 2023, the war in Sudan has displaced more than 800,000 people into South Sudan. The project will adhere to safe programming and implement specific protection interventions and gender issues. Boys, girls, men, women have experienced the consequences of conflict and displacements differently. We will Increase girls and 'boy's participation in the project activities to identify and report persisting issues to child help desks and other forums for PSS will be strengthened. The project will strive to increase the enrolment of girls in supported schools. One strategy is mobilizing trained female teachers to serve as mentors for girl and foster safer environments for girls in classrooms. Protection shall be mainstreamed to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse and exploitation faced by the target population. We shall carry out consultation with stakeholders to determine the locations of service implementation, taking into consideration safe spaces that are accessible to all benefi ciaries. RDAA in consultation and in coordination with the County Education Department, state Ministry of Education will identify the location of the schools, mobilize teachers and PTA/SMCs to be trained and with local authorities identify out of school youth and adolescents for livelihood training. The main target of this action are 1,964 beneficiaries. Out this we shall have 1,778 learners (889 boys, 889 girls from 6 primary schools) 54 teachers (40 male,14 female), 78 PTAs members (50 male and 28 female),54 SMC members (44 male, 8 female). The benefi ciaries are composed of host community, IDPs and returnee populations. We shall also have 40 (20girls and 20 boys) out of school for livelihood training. Beneficiary messaging will take into consideration those with less access to information due to social barriers and discrimination, we shall use a combination of existing networks of male and female groups/network leaders, Religious Leaders Networks, schools, community-based disability groups, women leaders, community complaint box in safe places, Hotlines and other humanitarian networks. During community based close participatory planning, marginalized members of the community shall be given a special audience to air out their views and these views to be captured in the project implementation. RDAA in partnership with Education Cluster and HRP will increase access to education in emergency/crisis-affected children and youths.
