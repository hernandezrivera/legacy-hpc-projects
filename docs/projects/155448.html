Needs

The occurrence of the El Nino induced drought in the 2018/19 agriculture season will likely have devastating effects on the lives of children, adolescents, families and communities. The El Nino induced drought will occur when the country is facing a myriad of economic and social challenges that have eroded the ability of families and communities to cope and reduced the number of coping mechanisms available for families to use. The current economic situation is characterized by high pricing of basic commodities against the backdrop of dwindling family income. Communities are also currently recovering from the 2015/16 El Nino induced drought that negatively affected household assets and incomes. Prior to this 2018/19 El Nino induced drought, Plan International in Zimbabwe had undertaken a number of initiatives to reduce the occurrence of child and early marriages in communities and increase girls agency using the Champions of Change Model. However the onset of this drought is expected to increase the numbers of children (both boys and girls) who drop out of school in order to assist their households source food and water for consumption. For girls this will include walking long distances to look for water for household consumption as well as increased work loads. The long distances from home will also increase their vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse. This 2018/19 El Nino induced drought will intensify the risk of sexual and gender based violence among adolescent girls. Adolescent girls will engage in transactional sex with older partners in exchange for food or money to meet their own and household consumption. However, when girls engage in transactional sex they are unlikely to be able to negotiate for safer sex increasing their risk of unplanned pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections. In the last El Nino induced drought, girls as young as 12 years were engaging in transactional sex warranting urgent and immediate interventions among this group. Plan International Zimbabwe will use a peer led approach through its Champions of Child Protection Mentors to support adolescent girls and provide information on risks, knowledge on referral and reporting structures and increase their agency to reduce risk behaviour. For families, reduced family resources, household food insecurity have negative impacts on the family structure leading to breakdown of the family structure, conflicts, increased reports of physical violence and family separation. Parents and caregivers also report increased stress which reduces their ability to cope with a wide range of stressful situations. Families may not have adequate knowledge and skills to provide adequate support for their children and this increases the number of children experiencing trauma and emotional distress. Plan will use the parenting clubs to provide support to parents and caregivers to provide knowledge and skills that will enable them to protect children in targeted areas. The participation in parenting clubs will increase the social support available to parents and caregivers reducing burnout among this group.

Activities

• Community awareness raising sessions on child safeguarding• Partner capacity strengthening on child safeguarding• Provision of psycho social support to manage the traumatizing effects of family separation, death• Family reunification and tracing• Case Management Strengthening (identification of children in need, referral pathway)• Information sharing on good nutrition practices for child survival with young mothers using the Lead mother approach and referrals for infants/children in need of further management• Influencing Government for better preparedness
