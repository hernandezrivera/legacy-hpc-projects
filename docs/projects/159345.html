Project Summary

TThis project is targeting 15,000 persons from 2500 households including vulnerable children, women, and men from returnees and resident communities in Raga County (Ringi and Uyujuku payams) who are facing WASH challenges and are risk of gender-based violence (GBV).
The project activities and targets will include; training of 50 hygiene promoters (30 females and 20 males) and conducting 8 hygiene campaigns across the two payams (benefiting 15,000 people; 3,750 men, 3,750 women, 3,750 boys, and 3,750 girls) through participatory hygiene promotion. Training of 10 water management committees (15 members from each community, 50% female). Distribution of WASH NFIs to 2500 HHs (15,000 people; 3,750 men, 3,750 women, 3,750 boys, and 3,750 girls). Distribution of menstrual hygiene products benefiting 1500 girls and women (500 women, 1000 girls). The project will further promote sanitation through construction or installation of 12 emergency latrine structures with 4 stances each (amounting to 48 stances) aimed at promoting proper human excreta disposal in public places at Boma level. The project intends to benefit 15,000 people, majority of whom are women and children. The project will provide GBV services to people of the area.

1 - Needs

The conflict in South Sudan continues to affect civil population in many ways including denying them access to basic WASH services provided by humanitarian organizations/agencies. South Sudan is still very low at 41% coverage with basic water supply, 11% coverage with basic sanitation, and with 63% of the population still practicing open defecation according to the UNICEF-WHO 2019 Joint Monitoring Program (August 2019 report). There have been gaps in WASH response in Western Bahr el Ghazal (WBeG) due to insecurity which blocked humanitarian access to the local population in Bagari, Jur River, and Raga counties. Since the signing of peace agreement in 2018, access to Ringi and Uyujuku payams of Raga county has been possible and displaced persons who fled to Sudan and other neighboring counties have returned to their villages. Charity and Empowerment Foundation (CEF) team conducted assessment in the 2 payams in September 2019 and found that the basic WASH facilities which were present in the area were either damaged or malfunctioned. This lack of basic WASH facilities compounded by lack of safe drinking water sources are threats to safety of returnees and their resident communities in Ringi and Uyujuku. These returnees with their resident communities constantly face diarrheal diseases, typhoid, and cholera. There is an urgent need for provision of clean water by repairing malfunctioned handpumps, form and train water management committees, promote good hygiene practices, support returnees to construct pit latrines in their homes, train hygiene promoters, and train handpump mechanics to maintain the functionality of the handpumps.

2 - Activities

CEF intends to do the following: 1) repair 10 malfunctioned handpumps (6 in Ringi and 4 in Uyujuku). 2) Establish and train 10 Water Management Committees (WMCs) for community management of the handpumps. 3) Train 10 hand pump mechanics to maintain the functionality of the handpumps. 4) conduct 6 hygiene promotion campaigns on proper disposal of human excreta, importance of hand washing, and proper ways of handling food. 5) Train 50 hygiene promoters (30 female and 20 males). 6) Distribute WASH NFIs. 7) Distribute menstrual hygiene management kits to 2000 adolescent girls and women (500 returnees and 1500 members of resident communities). 8) Distribute construction materials to 50 households for constructing their pit latrines. 9) Conduct community led debris clean up campaigns to ensure appropriate waste management. 10) Train 8 WASH staff on protection and GBV guidelines. 11) Conduct 4 community consultative meetings. 11) Provide PSS and legal advice to facilitate access to justice. 12) Refer GBV victims for medical, access to justice, livelihood and other support services. 13) Prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV through enhanced preparedness and resilience through Women & Girl Friendly Spaces programming (information, basic PSS, referrals, literacy & skills training incl income generating activities/alternative livelihoods). 14) Strengthen community based GBV/Protection structures and systems. 15) Conduct safety audit & safety perception mapping for prevention/mitigation of GBV risks. 16) Community awareness raising, attitude and behavior change with communities, faith leaders - Advocacy, engagement with police & legal/justice actors (statutory, traditional). 17) Engage male & female youth in local advocacy as change agents, promotion of gender equality. 18) Support local women’s associations & opportunities for women’s leadership opportunities & empowerment.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project consultation meeting with beneficiaries will consider the attendance to have 50% female, 50% youth, and 15% persons with disability during consultation and decision-making processes. Beneficiaries will be informed about this criterion to ensure voices of female, youth, and persons with disability are heard in relation to project design and implementation. The beneficiaries will be provided with information about program activities before the commencement of the project to involve them in project management, create awareness and sense of ownership, and provide accountability mechanism. CEF will ensure staff are trained on protection of persons of all age including child protection and GBV risks. CEF recognizes the community engagement and therefore will ensure participation of community members in activities at community level hence championing community protection mechanisms. CEF will train community existing protection structures such as church leaders, school administrations, and local authorities on protection principles including GBV risk and referral pathway existing in the community.CEF will work closely with local leaders and authorities to create community awareness on the targeting and selection criteria. CEF will engage some resident community and returnees’ members during the identification of project beneficiaries in order to build a strong relationship between returnees and resident community members. CEF will consider all age group and gender in the project implementation.The confidential complain mechanism will be establish and all the issues that may arise will be addressed without discrimination. CEF will position a complain desk and box in a visible area and place accountability to affected population officer to receive complains. Complaints received will go through the case management process to generate appropriate solutions and feedback.
