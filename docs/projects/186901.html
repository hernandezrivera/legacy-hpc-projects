Project Summary

To address the high morbidity, mortality and malnutrition rates caused by water borne disease transmission, Mercy Corps will continue to implement a comprehensive WASH intervention in LGAs of operations in Borno state, Nigeria – covering five sub sectors (Water Supply, Sanitation, Hygiene promotion, WASH NFIs and Environmental Health) and COVID-19 mitigation measures/risk reduction. Under the sanitation component, Mercy Corps aims to ensure that the affected communities in new displacement sites and host communities have full access to improved sanitation facilities that are secure, sanitary, user friendly, and gender-appropriate, Mercy Corps will ensure providing Safe clean water, Promotion of sustainable Hygiene best practices and behavior change, Environmental management, vector control, capacity building and good governance. Furthermore, to mitigate the depletion of the water table in the areas of operation, Mercy Corps will build on the existing groundwater monitoring in other camps and other LGAs and strengthen the local capacity to monitor the groundwater level through the installation of data loggers in hybrid water supply systems.
The Primary purpose of Mercy Corps WASH intervention is to reduce morbidity, mortality and malnutrition from Water- borne diseases through safe and appropriate access to clean drinking water, hygiene promotion, Waste management, distribution WASH NFI’s and appropriate access to sanitation infrastructure and facilities and. Mercy Corps WASH intervention primarily targets the affected population of Internally Displaces Persons (IDP’s), returnee and Host communities

1 - Needs

In Northeast Nigeria, extreme protracted conflict and crisis has created persistent and acute WASH service needs particularly in Borno state. Almost all Local Government Area (LGA) has been severely affected. From the WASH Sector Northeast Nigeria response Monitoring Dashboard, 2.88 million people in the Northeast need reliable, affordable, equitable and safe access to improved WASH services. Out of the 2.52 million affected people targeted with Humanitarian response to access reliable, affordable, equitable and safe WASH services, only 1.71 million have access to WASH services leaving out 810,000 (nearly 1 million) affected people without access to reliable, affordable, equitable and safe access to WASH services. From the lesson learned from current ADAPT I and from the internal and external assessment report findings, 23% Households in Ngala LGA, and 12% Households in Dikwa LGA take more than 30 minutes (on average) to access the main water source, fetch water, and return, including time spent queuing at the water source. In IDP camps and host communities, the average consumption in liters of water per person per day ranges between 8 to 13 liters, below the sphere standard for emergency settings. The current influx of new arrivals from Maiduguri camps to informal settlements in garrison towns increases population pressure on limited water points at LGA levels. Lack of access to improved sanitation facilities and open defecation also puts affected populations at a greater risk of water related disease outbreaks like acute water diarrhea/cholera through fecal-oral transmission. According to the REACH MSNA 2021 assessment report, access to improved sanitation facilities was one of the major problems facing respondents across several locations in Borno state. Sustainable adoption of key hygiene best practices and behavior change continues to be of challenge. Poor hygiene practices coupled with the residual threat of COVID-19 has created another dimension of acute humanitarian needs that require extensive hygiene. The recent Nutrition and Food Security Survey (NFSS) round 10 Nutrition assessment conducted indicates that in Borno state, the general acute malnutrition (GAM) level is at 11. 8% requires improvement in WASH services to address malnutrition. In addition, because of WASH service gaps at LGAs, there was an outbreak of cholera that claimed a measurable number of lives of vulnerable people.

2 - Activities

Mercy Corps WASH activities shall be addressing five key thematic areas of: 1) Supply of Safe clean water, 2) Provision of improved sanitation facilities and 3) Hygiene promotion&COVID-19 mitigation and risk reduction 4) Environmental Health/ Solid waste management and Ground water monitoring 1) Supply of safe clean water: i) Rehabilitation and construction of water points., Mercy Corps will rehabilitate or construct water points to address the alarming, prevailing, and emerging acute humanitarian needs of IDPs and other vulnerable populations in Ngala, Damboa, Gwoza,Bama and Dikwa LGAs. These activities will take place in both IDP camps and host communities through contract driven approach. ii) Formations and training of WASHCOMs for sustainability. Mercy Corps, will ensure the sustainability of the intervention by reviving and/or selecting and training members of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene Committees (WASHCOMs), pump mechanics and pump attendants from within the affected communities. iii) Water Quality Measures: Mercy Corps will ensure that the water provided for communities meets acceptable WHO drinking water standards of zero E.Coli contamination per 100 ml of water sample. Mercy Corps’ WASH technical team will take water samples from various water sources, household water containers, water vendors (donkey carts and water trucks) for water quality testing. The key parameters to be monitored are (E-coli form, PH, Turbidity, Free residual chlorine, Total chlorine, temperature and other dissolved chemical parameters. iv) Safe clean Water trucking for new arrivals and IDP’s Mercy Corps will conduct water trucking for the new arrivals to support the already stressed existing water points to try meet the high demand of safe water for the new arrivals. 2) Provision of improved sanitation facilities. To address identified sanitation gaps in IDP Camps in Dikwa, Ngala, Bama, Damboa and Gwoza LGAs, Mercy Corps will ensure that the affected communities have full access to improved sanitation facilities that are secure, sanitary, user friendly, and gender appropriate in all the targeted IDP camps and host communities by supporting the construction &rehabilitation of latrines. 3) Hygiene promotion: To deliver the hygiene messaging, MC will make use of hygiene promoters conducting hygiene campaigns in both IDP camps and host communities in the targeted LGAs of Gwoza, Dikwa, Ngala, Bama and Damboa. The CHP's will be selected in the target locations and receive a thorough five-day training at the beginning of the program. The trained CHP will conduct door-to-door hygiene sensitization in both IDP camps and host communities. ii) Cholera preparedness response plan, prevention intervention and awareness: Mercy Corps intervention will be cholera preparedness, respond plan; community awareness and prevention intervention that will contribute to Health and WASH cluster objectives by including activities related to surveillance, case management, capacity building, infection prevention and control, and public awareness in all the targeted areas/ LGA’s. iii) Distribution of Hygiene kits to the new arrivals. 4) To promote environmental Health, Mercy Corps will form Sanitation Teams to clear the drainage channels from debris and accumulated waste-materials blocking them, thus eliminating both potential and actual persistent standing water sites. To carry out the rehabilitation of the drainage channels, Mercy Corps will engage communities through the Cash for Work modality. The Sanitation Teams will be linked up with the WASHCOMS, who will facilitate and lead their work, under the close supervision and support of Mercy Corps’ qualified WASH engineers.5) Ground water monitoring: To mitigate the depletion of the water table in the areas of operation, Mercy Corps will also continue ground water monitoring with the existing ground water monitoring systems in our areas of intervention.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure Gender and Protection Mainstreaming in all our WASH facilities, Mercy Corps will provide ramps at the blocks latrines and taps to help facilitate the movement of people in wheelchairs/hand-pedaled bicycles. As hand-pedaled bicycles are often wider than a regular door, Mercy Corps is also prepared to change the width of the latrine entrance. Mercy Corps will construct raised toilet seats/sitting pans in the latrine for those disable individuals using wheelchairs. Women and girls often have to walk long distances to fetch water, or wait a long time in queues to receive water, increasing their time poverty and exposure to sexual violence and abuse. To minimize risks, Mercy Corps will involve women, girls, boys and men in all consultations and assessments to identify safety and security risks, and to identify locations for water points that are accessible and low risk of harassment and abuse while taking into consideration the safest pathways. Furthermore, Mercy Corps will ensure that basic protection standards are respected, such as doors with interior locks and the separation of male and female latrines that are clearly marked.
