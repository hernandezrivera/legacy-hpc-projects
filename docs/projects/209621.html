Project Summary

The principal objective of this intervention is to improve protection of civilians through engagement and advocacy among armed actors, de facto authorities and civil society to increase knowledge and respect of international humanitarian norms. This project will work directly with the duty-bearers for international humanitarian law (IHL), both as armed groups and local authorities. The organisation will focus on addressing the most urgent protection risks faced by civilians as result of the behaviour, policies and actions (or failure to act or pass policy) of armed groups and de facto authorities. These risks include child recruitment, Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights violations, forced displacement, arrest and detention, gender based violence etc. Moreover, the organisation will also advocate for the protection of healthcare and the vacation of civilian infrastructure. Geneva Call will also advocate directly with AGDAs to address their conduct of hostilities during inter- and intra-armed group escalations to minimise harm to civilians. To this end, the NGO will actively engage all armed groups and de facto authorities in the Northwest. In addition, the NGO will work with civil society organisations (CSOs), and community-led groups, such as Women-led and ethnic-minority led CSOs, tribal leaders, and religious leaders to bolster communities’ resilience and their capacity to safely engage duty bearers on their protection concerns.
The organisation will engage in public and private humanitarian advocacy (on IHL, IHRL, humanitarian principled action) with armed groups and de facto authorities across Northern Syria to enhance the prevention and mitigation of the most severe protection risks. This advocacy will take a wide variety of forms, employing a combination of approaches for each group and protection risk, including: closed-doors advocacy with groups to address particular instances of violations; rigorously monitor compliance of signatory armed groups to multi-year action plans for the implementation of existing commitments; organise engagement meetings with non-signatories to promote the signing of long-term commitments; conduct awareness-raising and technical training sessions on humanitarian norms for commanders, officers, technical departments, and governing bodies; lobby duty bearers for the creation of accountability and grievance mechanisms; facilitate regular dialogue within and among armed groups, de facto authorities and communities; utilize public communication and advocacy materials, including collaborations with influential religious and tribal leaders.
In terms of key protection risks, this project aims to prevent, mitigate and address violations committed by duty bearers towards the most vulnerable community members at weighted protection risks including, most notably, minorities, those households affected by multiple protection crisis, youth and confined groups.
Key risks and assumptions are that the security situation remains permissiveness enough to enable regular field and monitoring visits by national and international staff to target areas and that AGDAs remain willing to engage in discussion around IHL.
