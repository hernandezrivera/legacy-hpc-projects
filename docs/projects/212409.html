Project Summary

Approximately 5.7 million people in Syria are malnourished, including 2.8 million critically targeted individuals. This includes over 275,000 people in Latakia and 300,000 in Homs. Over 650,000 children in Syria nationwide are stunted due to malnutrition, and 500,000 require life-saving treatment to deal with acute malnutrition – a 40 percent increase from 2019. Some governorates report breastfeeding rates as low as 30 percent, posing challenges for the health of infants who thus become more susceptible to a host of complications. In 2023, only 1 in 10 in Syria consumed a minimum acceptable diet, meaning that the vast majority of people are at risk for being at least moderately deficient in important micronutrients.
This project will address these nutrition needs in two ways:
1) Provide nutrition grants (in agriculture or livestock) for highly vulnerable families – those pregnant or breastfeeding women, a high number of young children, etc. in earthquake-affected Latakia / Homs, to improve their access to food security
2) Host nutrition information sessions at pediatric clinics in Latakia and Homs in IYCF and hygiene. ADRA will target areas currently underserved by other humanitarian organizations in Latakia – Jableh, Qrdaha, and Stamo. In Homs, ADRA has experience serving families that are most likely to have new mothers or young children by targeting clinics they are already likely to visit. For the grants portion, beneficiaries will be able to select from approved vendors items that help them provide nutrition for their families – livestock, agricultural tools and inputs, etc. Manual verification in the form of house visits will be conducted for 100% of beneficiaries, concurrent with the verification for ADRA’s food security voucher program in Latakia, allowing for reduced operating expenses.
Given that grant recipients will be a subset of those receiving vouchers in the food security program, there is a risk that consolidation narrows the scope of people receiving support by too much, leading to potentially highly vulnerable individuals being left out. This will be mitigated by an in-depth needs analysis on both projects and active communication with local leaders in order to find those most in need of assistance. There is likewise a risk of conflicts should people who were not beneficiaries of either program be upset about how beneficiaries were selected. This will be mitigated by transparently explaining criteria and communicating our actions proactively with local leaders, and providing information on how to contact our office for any complaints regarding program implementation or abuse.
In order to prevent sexual abuse and exploitation, grants will be distributed by staff which are trained in PSEA. Moreover, half of the staff members doing the distribution will be women themselves, and the location of distribution will be selected based on ease of access and security for women. Likewise, the location of nutrition awareness sessions with be places accustomed to accommodating women – pediatric clinics. The channels for reporting abuse will be well advertised and explained, and these will be monitored regularly to ensure that any problems which might arise are quickly dealt with.
The successful implementation of this project assumes that we will maintain access to all target areas, be able to procure all required equipment and materials, and obtain any necessary approvals from local authorities.
