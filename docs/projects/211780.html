Project Summary

Need: Northwest Syria (NWS) continues to grapple with the protracted consequences of conflict, leaving an estimated 4.1 million people requiring humanitarian assistance. NWS is plagued by persistent challenges of poverty, unemployment, and compromised infrastructure, forcing families into cycles of vulnerability. The most vulnerable population in NWS are IDPs, of whom there are currently 2.5 million. IDPs in NWS also face additional challenges due to limited job opportunities and difficulties obtaining sustainable income.
Women, particularly female heads of households (HHs), constitute another highly vulnerable group within NWS. They typically face gender bias, resulting in lower wages and limited access to assets and productive resources. Additionally, women often shoulder the burden of HH chores, leading to time and mobility constraints compared to male heads of HHs. Further compounding vulnerability, female heads of HHs generally have more dependents.
Intervention, Implementation of Activities: NGO X will continue and expand the implementation of a graduation approach (GA) initiative tailored to lift households out of extreme poverty, marking a transformative step towards sustainable economic and social well-being targeting 500 HHs. NGO X will also provide small and medium enterprise (SME) grants to 514 vendors providing food commodities, and conduct rehabilitation of 3 access roads between communities and markets, reaching a total of 513,100 individuals.
1. Graduation Approach: The GA is an initiative centered on empowerment and sustainable progress. This model shifts from providing immediate relief to equipping families with the skills and resources to generate sustainable income, fostering independence and dignity in the face of adversity. The GA utilizes a multi-layered selection process that prioritizes the most vulnerable HHs, including female-headed families and IDPs. The GA program adopts a multifaceted intervention strategy encompassing five key pillars: (1) Immediate food security through NGO X's CVA programming, (2) Skills development for market integration, (3) Financial inclusion and microenterprise support via business start-ups, and VSLAs, (4) Continuous coaching for personal and professional growth, with an emphasis on serving women, youth, older persons, and those with disabilities, and (5) Facilitating access to essential social services. The program measures success through the Self-Reliance Index (SRI), evaluating progress in food security, employment, debt management, and social capital, ensuring participants gain not only financial independence but also the social and community support needed for long-term self-reliance.
2. Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSME ): The MSME support program aims to strengthen food markets in NWS by providing cash grants, business development training, and coaching sessions to food vendors. The goal is to help businesses stabilize revenue, cope with market shocks, and improve market functionality. NGO X uses the Market Functionality Index developed by WFP to identify poorly functioning markets, prioritizing women, persons with disabilities, the elderly, and vendors operating in poorly functioning markets. The process involves a registration process, baseline assessment and a two-day training, leading to the submission of business plans. Eligible vendors receive up to $1100 in two installments, alongside individual business capacity-building support and mentorship. The program's impact is measured through baseline and endline assessments.

4. Roads Rehabilitation : This activity aims to enhance local infrastructure by rehabilitating roads, improving market access, reducing transportation issues, and supporting the sustainability of essential businesses. road repair conducted through contractor-led modality focuses on safety and standard compliance, it facilitates economic recovery by improving the flow of goods and services. Managed by NGO X's engineers to ensure quality, safe, and improved access to vital resources and services. It involves collaboration with local entities to ensure long-term success and integration with the region's wider economic recovery efforts. NGO X emphasizes the safety and well-being of program participants with robust measures integrating comprehensive PSEAH (Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment) strategies into all activities. This includes prevention, awareness, responsive reporting, and risk mitigation, reinforced by training for staff, partners, and leaders to enhance SEAH incident management.Assumptions, Risks and Mitigation Measures: Assumptions: Community cooperation and a stable security environment for project success. Operational Risks:• Implementation Delays: Mitigated by improved coordination, streamlined logistics, and staff training.• Quality Control: Ensured through regular monitoring, skilled engineers, and beneficiary feedback. Financial Risks:• Mismanagement of Funds: Addressed with strict financial controls, audits, and transparency. Security Risks:• Conflict and Instability: Managed with security monitoring, safety protocols, and local coordination.• Resource Theft or Diversion: Prevented through secure storage and local community and security collaboration. Protection risks:• Harassment and SEA (Sexual Exploitation and Abuse): Risks of harassment and SEA are mitigated through robust policies, trainings, and awareness programs, ensuring adherence to GOAL’s policies. • Exclusion of Marginalized Groups: Older people, persons with disabilities, and participants with lower literacy levels may face challenges in participation and could be subjected to discriminatory practices. Mitigation includes additional support, consultative training timings, and prioritizing accessibility.• Disputes and Social Tension: Risks of disputes between host communities and IDPs are managed through inclusive program design, active complaint mechanisms, and community involvement in decision-making.• Child Labor: Coordination with the Protection Team and conducting protection sessions with vendors mitigate the risk of child labor.
