Project Summary

4 years of the signing of revitalized peace agreement &11 years after independence, South Sudan continues to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions throughout 2022 worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as effects of COVID-19,Ebola & climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, access to basic needs, health services and water, sanitation and hygiene, negatively impacting the nutrition status with more children acutely malnourished than the previous 4 years due to water borne diseases & food insecurity due to distraction of crops & loss of livelihood, leading to families adopting to negative practices as the coping mechanisms are weakened by the cumulative shocks. According to 2023 HNO (draft) using FSNMS R28 & 2022 SMART survey show no improvement as its estimated 1,469,919 in the host community, 315,503 returnees, & 390,125 IDPs, will suffer from acute malnutrition which include 1,437,735 million children U5 (733,245 girls, 704,490 boys) of which 353,606 will have SAM (180,339 girls and 173,267 boys), 1,084,129 (552,906 girls and 531,223 boys) will have MAM, and 737,812 PLW. Sadly, more children and pregnant and lactating women in SS will suffer from acute malnutrition in 2023 compared to 2022 (increased by 158, 201).Further, 2022 WHO classification noted, 5 out of 9 counties of UNITY manifested with GAM between 19.2%-15.2% above emergency threshold with Leer, Panyijar & Mayendit at 19.2%, 18.7% and 14.6% respectively with FSNMS 28 noting the 3 counties with GAM levels of 20% or above. Malnutrition situation intensified due to prolonged flooding, sporadic armed fighting/cattle raiding, severe food insecurity, poor access to health nutrition services, extremely poor diets, poor sanitation, hygiene & COVID- 19 effects in the 3 counties Through 2023 HRP, UNIDOR seeks continue providing timely, qualitative and quantitative lifesaving services to children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women & people with disabilities through provision of integrated nutrition services reaching at least 80% of SAM, 75% MAM and 60% PLW in line with the set cluster objectives in the counties of Leer ,Mayendit and Panyijar, UNITY targeting total of 50,929 beneficiaries. Through management and treatment of 4097 boys and girls with SAM (Mayendit 2,013, Leer 980, Panyijar 1104) 6,742 boys &girls with MAM (Mayendit 4,012, Leer 1080, Panyijar 1651), PLW 3570(Mayendit 1610, Leer 840, Panyijar 1120) through OPT/TSFP static sites and outreaches per the cluster objective two. In line with the cluster objective one, 14471 will be reached with MIYCN counselling integrated with COVD-19 messaging, 22,049 & 19,425 boys & girls will receive Vitamin A and Deworming respectively in prevention of malnutrition in the three counties with an estimated budget of $1,104,847. UNIDOR will adopt combination of strategies for this nutrition response such as establishment of static services and outreaches in the flood affected areas in the 3counties and continued treatment through the static OTP/TSFP sites, capacity building, and integration of nutrition services with Health (malaria screening, EPI &GMP distribution of LLITN),WASH (provision of safe water, soap, education on hygiene and sanitation), FSL on kitchen gardening & cooking demonstration, Education though provision of age appropriate toys and child friendly spaces. Protection through referral pathways and GBV safety audits and feedback mechanism through establishment of complaint feedback mechanism and complaint desks, and community dialogues meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback in line cluster objective 4.There will be strong component of surveillance through continuous program monitoring that will include Mass MUAC screening, SMART surveys & supporting in FNSMS rounds in line with cluster objective 3

1 - Needs

South Sudan remains a concern for malnutrition & food insecurity due to compounding shocks. According to 2023 HNO, 2 million people at risk of acute malnutrition, including 353,606 children (180,339 girls & 173,267 boys), SAM, 1,084,129 children (552,906 girls & 531,223 boys) MAM, & 737,812 PLW with MAM. 2022 IPC analysis, estimates 6.6 million people (53% ) face Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity,of which 2.2 million people face Emergency (IPC 4) & 61000 in catastrophic (IPC5) & projected to surge in 2023 to an estimated 7.76 million (62.7 % ).UNITY continuous face deteriorating humanitarian conditions worsened by conflict, access constraints & operational interference & extraordinary flooding which have a severe negative impact on the nutrition status of children due to water borne diseases & food insecurity due to distraction of crops & loss of livelihood.2022 IPC cites 7 out of the 9 counties were classified in Emergency (IPC 4) acute food insecurity with 2 counties at IPC 5 catastrophe while Feb–July 2022 IPC notes 67.6% of their populations are facing Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity with 5 out of 9 counties with GAM between 19.2%-15.2% above emergency (WHO 2022). Mayendit,Leer and Panyijar situation continues to worsen due to the endemic violence related to cattle raiding & revenge killings, attack on aid workers, looting of nutrition supplies, access hitches, which led to displacement of people thus resulting to intensified malnutrition status of the most vulnerable. Feb 2022 IRNA conducted in Leer,cites6,498 HH were displaced to Leer Town from other Leer Payams &Mayendit due to continued clashes between the armed youth. Climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding for the 4th year consecutively which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods further compounded the malnutrition situation due to displacement, destruction of crops & loss of livelihood thus resulting to severe food insecurity & continued to compound the humanitarian needs in 3 counties. According HNO 2022 &2023, floods destroyed critical in fractures and limited Humanitarian accesswhile simultaneously limiting ability to migrate to access services. August 2021 IRNA in Panyijar noted over, 5,791 HH were affected coupled with the slow recession of floods from 2021, and the affected households increased spontaneously with an estimated 31,245 people (16,017 female, 15,228 male) affected in 2021 with Sept 2022 IRNA noting 30,125 people were affected & the situation is expected to worsen in 2023. Mayendit September 2022 IRNA 17,000 individuals (2,833 HH) were newly displaced adding to the August 2021 IRNA where 5,018 HH displaced with additional 820 returnees from panyijar,Juba and Bentiu POC newly registered bringing to 5,838 with the screened U5 for acute malnutrition proxy GAM of 26.59% (SAM 3.47%/MAM 23.12%).2022 HRP WHO cites Panyijar GAM 18.7% SAM 3.2% ,MAMU5 15.2% & MAM PLW 26.7%,Mayendit GAM 14.6% SAM 4.0% ,MAMU5 10.6% & MAM PLW 19.9% & Leer GAM 19.2% SAM 6.1% ,MAMU5 13.2% & MAM PLW 11.3% which is above the WHO emergency threshold. Further,2023 HNO PIN cites the 4 counties with GAM prevalence of 24.6% at severity 4 (high concern). October 2022- July 2023 IPC analysis projection of April - July 2023, 7.8 million people (63% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 43,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Leer and Mayendit. Dec 2021 SMART survey by IMC in Panyijar, cited GAM 15.70% (12.5-19.5) SAM 3 %( 2.0-5.6).UNIDOR supports 19 OTP/TSFP sites (Mayendit 11,Leer 3 & Nya 5),1 SC in (Rubkuay) & 10 outreaches supporting the integration of Health in management of SAM/MAM, micronutrient supplementation, MIYCN awareness, kitchen gardening & cooking demonstrations GBV safety audits & AAP. UNIDOR is Education, FSL, WASH, CP/GBV, Peace and Health partner in the three counties hence the advantage of integration.

2 - Project activities

In order to ensure that the intended purpose of this project is effectively archived in providing lifesaving project intervention, the project intend to address the following needs; conducting community nutritional assessments for acute malnutrition through community screening and referral to the respective programs. Strengthening Family MUAC to caregivers and mothers in HH with children U5. Treatment of SAM U5 without medical complications in OTP. • Treatment of SAM with medical complications in SC • Treatment of MAM U5 children in the TSFP sites • Treatment of MAM pregnant and lactating women in TSFP sites. •Malaria testing and treatment for all SAM children U5 at OTP and MAM U5 symptomatic at the TSFP sites. MIYCN/GMP/ECD and CMAM training to nutrition and health workers • Training of nutrition workers on GBV referral pathways. Conduct GBV safety audits at the nutrition sites • World breastfeeding week celebrations • Conducting cooking demonstrations. • Vitamin A supplementation to children 6-59 months boys and girls during NIDs • Deworming to children 12-59 months boys and girls during NIDs • Rehabilitation of 11 nutrition sites and construction of 6 nutrition sites affected by flooding. •Provision of preventive packages ( Soaps, water purifiers & treated mosquito nets) • Integration of ECD in the OTP/TSFP sites for stimulation by ensuring availability of Child friendly spaces with age appropriate toys• Implementation of minimum package of integration on Nutrition,WASH,FSL,Education, health and CP.Provison of Zinc and ORS to children with diarrhea at nutrition sites • Community awareness on Growth monitoring and promotion with the GMP charts. MIYCN counselling and behaviors change messaging to mothers/caregivers of children 0-59 months and pregnant women through mother support groups, individual and group counselling at the community and facility • Developing kitchen demonstration gardens at the nutrition sites • Community mobilization and sensitization with integrated health, hygiene and COVID -19 messaging • Provision of PPEs to nutrition front-line workers • Conduct Mass MUAC screening ,SMART surveys and and supporting in FSNMS

3 - Project indicators

UNIDOR is committed to regular monitoring and evaluation (M&E) using indicators and targets as priority one to effectively track the progress of project to ensure implementation is guided by a results orientation and continuously informed by lessons learnt and beneficiary feedback. UNIDOR will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan and indicator tracking table to measure progress towards the expected results. The Indicators on the tracking table will include those in the log frame corresponding to on-going and planned activities. The Monitoring tools (indicator tracking template) will include the Departmental Questionnaires, online reporting tool, nutrition cluster NIS tool,Family MUAC excel and DHIS 2, Programme Tally sheets, and Pictorial evidence especially during health facilities visits and trainings , will also use FGDs with the Nutrition/Health Workers and the local Authority to collect views on how the project is impacting on their lives. Data will be collected by the relevant Programme Managers and weekly/monthly/quarterly reports will be produced for internal monitoring. UNIDOR will submit quantitative data, disaggregated by sex, age and IDP/host when relevant both progress and final programmatic and financial reports online in line with donor reporting templates like FTS and Nutrition Cluster donor requirements within the stipulated period as will be evidenced in the contractual documents . A joint quarterly supervision visits to all health and nutrition facilities will be conducted together with our CHD counterparts using QoC/QSC tools in order to see the HFs compliance as per South Sudan BPHNS

The compounding factor was destruction of nutrition facilities, loss of nutrition supplies due to flooding. Therefore, UNIDOR will ensure nutrition facilities are relocated to the higher grounds to mitigate damages on infrastructures, assets and supplies. Also, through the synergy with UNIDOR FSL-FFA project, the communities will be supported in construction of dykes and trenches to prevent/control the flooding waters in the villages

With the synergy of UNIDOR CP/GBV department, nutrition staff will be trained on GBV and PSEA, this will ensure referral pathways are strengthened. Also, GBV safety audits will be conducted on quarterly basis at all the nutrition sites and the hotlines displayed at the nutrition sites
