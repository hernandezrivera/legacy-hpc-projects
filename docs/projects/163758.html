Project Summary

The Logistics Sector will aim to provide Information sharing and Coordination platform to the humanitarian community operating in Libya where needed. The Logistics Sector will facilitate humanitarian community access to common logistics services identified as critical to implement humanitarian response plan in Libya, such services will be made available where the most needed across Libya – with a focus on main hubs such as Tripoli, Benghazi and Sehba.

Associated response plan

Libya Humanitarian Response Plan 2020

Plan fields

1 - Needs

Based on consultations with humanitarian organisations, the major gaps and needs identified are:1. a Logistics Information Management and Coordination platform that can support the collection, analysis and sharing of timely and reliable logistics information in order to coordinate organisations interventions, avoid duplication of efforts and ensure that available resources in the country are optimally utilized among the different humanitarian actors.2. A logistics hub and staging area close to Libya to act as a buffer stock for contingencies for the different humanitarian actors.

2 - Activities

The Logistics Sector activities will include providing information sharing and coordination platform -where and when needed – to allow humanitarian community (i) to have access to up to date and relevant logistics information, (ii) make sure available in-country logistics resources are used, and (iii) avoid duplication of efforts. The logistics sector will support the implementation and access of common logistics services – where and when access to sufficient logistics services on the local market has been identified as a common gap or bottleneck by the humanitarian community in order to maintain an uninterrupted supply chain of relief aid. In case of identified common logistics gaps, the Logistics Sector Lead Agency, WFP, will act as “provider of last resort”. The Logistics Sector will provide mapping and assessment of key logistics infrastructures, including but not limited to main entry points into Libya. The Logistics Sector will also support the identification of customs related bottlenecks/constraints and will identify/advocate for ways of improvement. These activities will support a multisectoral response approach. The Logistics sector will also conduct a Logistics Capacity Assessment (LCA) in Malta to identify potential staging area at the free port to serve as a storage area prior to shunting to Libyan ports.

5 - Mitigation measures

The Logistics Sector will comply with rules on UN staff movement in terms of filling proper trip documentation of UNDSS website. The staff to get the security clearance to move prior to any movement. Additionally, the Logistics Sector adheres to rules of movement within Libya and avoids areas which are not cleared from a security perspective. Prior to any cargo movement, The Logistics Sector sensitizes market intelligence such as transporters to obtain advise on the security of the route. Moreover, seeks information on routes from several sources such as agencies security, WFP Security, UNDSS and other humanitarian players.
