Project Summary

The BRIDGE GIRLS project, meaning Building Resilience, Integration, Development, and Growth in Education for Girls’ Success, is a strategic educational initiative focused on empowering adolescent girls from the Rohingya refugee community and host communities. By fostering resilience and promoting inclusive education, the project aims to bridge cultural, social, and educational divides, enabling girls to achieve holistic development and meaningful growth. Creation of safe, community-based educational spaces (CBLFs) equipped with relevant teaching materials, focusing on both formal and non-formal education curricula.
Since 2021, PRANTIC has successfully implemented an adolescent-focused project in Camp-4, supporting 80 students and building a model of impactful, community-centered education. Drawing on this valuable experience, PRANTIC now seeks to expand its reach by establishing 40 Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs) across both Rohingya camps and host community areas. This initiative is rooted in inclusive decision-making and continuous, open dialogue with stakeholders, ensuring that the voices of female teachers, community representatives, and parents are central to fostering trust and accountability. Through these partnerships, PRANTIC strengthens a collaborative approach to education, promoting a supportive and responsive environment for adolescent girls and their communities.
The BRIDGE GIRLS project aims to provide access to education for 1120 students including 880 Adolescent girls in Rohingya Community (RC) & 240 (24X10) Adolescent girls students in Host Community (HC). Students in the 11-18 age group will follow the Myanmar curriculum approved by the Education Sector and Non-formal for Host Community. Among the total number of students, 5% CWD students (56) will be included. By implementing this initiative, PRANTIC aims to foster an environment that promotes learning, empowering Rohingya and Host community adolescent girls with knowledge and skills that will have a profound impact on their overall well-being, both in the present and in the future.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The BRIDGE GIRLS initiative by PRANTIC seeks to address the pressing educational needs of adolescent girls and support the expansion of Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs) for enhanced access to education. The Inter-Sector Needs Analysis (ISNA)- 2024 published that only 57% of girls aged 3-18 enrolled and a stark drop to 8% for those aged 15-18, there is a critical demand for targeted programs. Education remains a critical need for adolescents in both the Rohingya and host communities, particularly for girls aged 11–18. Due to cultural barriers, safety concerns, and limited resources, many adolescent girls lack access to quality education, leaving them vulnerable to child marriage, gender-based violence, and economic dependency. The BRIDGE GIRLS project seeks to address these challenges by providing accessible, inclusive, and gender-sensitive learning opportunities.  Gender-Specific NeedsAccess to Safe Learning Spaces: Adolescent girls face significant mobility and safety challenges. Establishing Learning Centers (LCs) and Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs) close to their homes ensures a secure environment conducive to learning.Female Teachers and Management: To address cultural sensitivities, the project prioritizes female teachers and management staff to encourage girls’ participation and create a comfortable and supportive learning environment.Empowerment through Education: Equipping adolescent girls with education fosters self-confidence and empowers them to actively participate in decision-making within their families and communities. Age and Disability ConsiderationsChildren with Disabilities (CWD): The inclusion of 5% of children with disabilities (56 students) ensures the project addresses the diverse needs of learners and promotes an inclusive educational environment. These students will benefit from specialized support tailored to their needs.Age-Appropriate Curriculums: Students aged 11–18 will follow the Myanmar curriculum approved by the Education Sector in the camps, while those in the host community will have access to a non-formal curriculum designed to meet their unique needs. Community NeedsCapacity Gaps: Camps 3, 4, and 10 in the Rohingya community, as well as Ukhiya in the host community, have significant gaps in education services for adolescent girls, leaving them without structured learning opportunities.Community Engagement: The involvement of community stakeholders is crucial for sustaining the project and ensuring its acceptance and success. Awareness campaigns and consultations will help promote the value of girls’ education and reduce cultural barriers. Infrastructure and Facility NeedsRepair and Renovation: Many of the proposed CBLFs require repairs and improvements to create safe and functional spaces for learning. This includes addressing ventilation, lighting, and accessibility issues, particularly for CWDs.Learning Materials: Adequate provision of teaching aids, textbooks, and other resources is essential to delivering quality education. Strategic LocationCamps 3, 4, and 10 were strategically selected due to their proximity to each other and the identified gaps in educational access. Ukhiya in the host community also represents an underserved population, particularly for adolescent girls. By addressing these identified needs, the BRIDGE GIRLS project aims to empower adolescent girls in both communities, providing them with the knowledge and skills necessary to improve their well-being and contribute meaningfully to society. This initiative supports sustainable development by bridging the education gap for marginalized groups and promoting gender equality.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The BRIDGE GIRLS project aims to provide access to education for 1,120 students, including 880 adolescent girls in the Rohingya community through 2 Learning Centers (2 × 80 = 160) and 30 Community-Based Learning Facilities (30 × 24 = 720). Additionally, 240 adolescent girls from the host community will receive education through community-based modalities (10 centers × 24 students each). Students in the 11–18 age group will follow the Myanmar curriculum, as approved by the Education Sector, while a non-formal curriculum will be used for the host community Notably, the project prioritizes the inclusion of children with disabilities (CWD), with 5% (56) of enrolled students identified as CWD. By situating CBLFs in camps 3, 4 & 10 and the Ukhiya region, and employing female teachers, the initiative aims to create a supportive, accessible, and gender-sensitive learning environment that caters to the diverse needs of students, promoting age-appropriate education and empowering girls of all abilities.By actively including PWD and CWD in the project, PRANTIC aims to provide them with equal opportunities and access to the project's activities, services, and benefits. This inclusive approach recognizes the rights and unique needs of individuals with disabilities and seeks to create a supportive and empowering environment that promotes their well-being and development. PRANTIC will ensure that appropriate measures and accommodations are in place to facilitate the participation and engagement of PWD and CWD. This may include providing accessible infrastructure, adaptive learning materials, specialized support, and targeted interventions to meet their specific requirements.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The BRIDGE GIRLS project by PRANTIC is committed to ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) through transparent, responsive, and participatory practices. This initiative prioritizes the voices and needs of adolescent girls and their communities, establishing channels for meaningful feedback and engagement. PRANTIC has successfully run an adolescent project in Camp-4 with 80 students and now aims to replicate this model, utilizing its learned experiences to establish 40 Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs) in Rohingya camps and host community areas. PRANTIC emphasizes inclusive decision-making and continuous dialogue with stakeholders, with female teachers, community representatives, and parents playing integral roles in fostering trust and accountability. The project incorporates mechanisms for monitoring, addressing concerns, and adapting interventions to ensure educational services are culturally relevant, accessible, and aligned with the rights and aspirations of the affected populations.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

PRANTIC also trained on the PSEA, Code of Conduct to all employees, as well as other safety precautions, and reminds them of their responsibilities. Through the project outcomes framework, the project focus is tasked with keeping an eye on AAP-related outputs and indicators. In order to ensure active engagement and a successful community feedback system by the facility, PRANTIC also includes affected populations in the monitoring process to ensure community feedback through facility based CESG & Parents’ meeting. PRANTIC also collects feedback in the ‘Complaint & Suggestion box’ in each of the LCs and CBLFs. The BRIDGE GIRLS project of PRANTIC integrates Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming across all facets of its operation to uphold the safety and dignity of adolescent girls and their communities. Staff, volunteers, implementing partners, vendors, and third-party associates are provided with comprehensive PSEA training to ensure they understand and comply with ethical standards. Beneficiaries also participate in awareness sessions, empowering them with knowledge of their rights and reporting mechanisms to address any misconduct. Additionally, the project develops and distributes Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials focused on PSEA, which are accessible and culturally sensitive. This multi-tiered approach strengthens a culture of respect, accountability, and zero tolerance for exploitation and abuse, ensuring a secure educational environment within the LCs and Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs).

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The BRIDGE GIRLS project by PRANTIC actively incorporates climate action to enhance resilience and promote environmental sustainability among adolescent girls in the Rohingya camps and host community. By integrating Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) principles, the project prepares students, teachers, and communities to anticipate and respond to climate-related risks. Climate change adaptation is woven into the curriculum and activities, teaching students sustainable practices and raising awareness about environmental stewardship. As an active participant in DRM efforts in Cox’s Bazar, PRANTIC both provides and receives community support for disaster risk reduction during critical moments. The project also mitigates its environmental impact by utilizing eco-friendly materials in LCs and Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs) and promoting waste reduction. To support environmental sustainability, PRANTIC observes international environmental days and engages in tree planting activities. Through these anticipatory actions and environmental initiatives, BRIDGE GIRLS contributes to a more climate-resilient future for both the learning communities and the broader environment.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The BRIDGE GIRLS project by PRANTIC incorporates robust Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures to ensure the safety and continuity of educational services in the face of crises. The project includes a business continuity plan and standardized emergency response procedures that guide staff, teachers, and students in responding effectively to emergencies. These protocols cover a range of scenarios, including natural disasters and other disruptions common to the Rohingya camps and host community areas. Additionally, PRANTIC has established contingency funds to enable rapid response, ensuring resources are available for immediate action when emergencies arise. By embedding EPR measures, the BRIDGE GIRLS project not only safeguards educational access for adolescent girls but also strengthens resilience within the community, equipping them with critical skills to navigate future challenges.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the BRIDGE GIRLS project actively engages youth, particularly girls aged 15-18, in its educational and life skills initiatives. Through LCs and Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs), the project provides tailored learning opportunities aligned with both the Myanmar and Bangladeshi curriculums, fostering academic growth as well as practical life skills. By engaging this age group, the project addresses the unique challenges older adolescents face, such as high dropout rates and limited access to education. Additionally, the project encourages leadership and peer mentoring, empowering youth to support one another and take active roles in their communities. This approach strengthens both individual and community resilience and prepares youth for future opportunities.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the BRIDGE GIRLS project promotes self-reliance and resilience among refugee adolescents in the camps by providing education and essential life skills that empower them to navigate their future with greater independence. Through Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs), the project enhances academic learning while focusing on practical skills such as problem-solving, health awareness, and environmental stewardship skills essential for resilient living. By equipping pre-adolescent girls and raising awareness among their parents about the importance of girls' education and protection from unjust social norms, the project helps prepare them with these vital skills. This strengthens their capacity to adapt, make informed decisions, and contribute positively to their communities, fostering a foundation for self-reliance despite the challenging circumstances of camp life.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the BRIDGE GIRLS project activities in the host community align with key development priorities for the Cox’s Bazar district, as outlined in the District Development and Growth Priorities. By focusing on education and skill-building for adolescent girls, the project supports local goals of enhancing educational access, improving gender equity, and building human capital. The initiative’s life skills training, environmental awareness, and community engagement components contribute to broader district priorities, such as promoting sustainable development, fostering resilience, and advancing economic empowerment. Through these efforts, the BRIDGE GIRLS project strengthens the capacity of young women in the host community, linking educational advancement with long-term development outcomes in Cox's Bazar.
