Project Summary

The Purpose of this humanitarian response plan will be to increase access to lifesaving nutrition interventions by establishing 13 new mobile and static nutrition facilities in hard-to-reach areas and also scale-up response and build the capacities of nutrition/health workers to provide emergency lifesaving integrated nutritional services to 41,558 vulnerable Under-five and PLWs living in Pochalla, Pibor, Yirol East and Yirol West counties. LiveWell would like to establish 3 static and 3 mobile nutrition facilities in Lakes State and 3 static, 4 mobile nutrition facilities in Jonglei State.
LiveWell is currently providing primary and secondary healthcare and nutrition services in 3 health facilities with static nutrition services integrated in the health facilities in Eastern Lakes State (Yangtikyom, Machuor & Aruopnyiel OTPs/TSFP) and 19 health facilities in Boma State (especially Pibor, Pochalla, Boma) without nutrition services (2 CHs, 5 PHCCs, 9 PHCUs, 4 Mobile Clinics) with support from World Bank and SSHF SA2 for Health & Nutrition. 40% of these facilities were non-functional before and had recently been open with no current nutritional service in the OPD/IDP. This response plan is also aim at filling gap with integrated nutritional responses with GBV, Health, WASH services and people with special needs.
The overall HRP Nutrition budget for HRP 2020 is at US$ 779,213 which will aim at supporting the following level of Nutrition interventions in current Boma and Eastern lakes States to:
o Set up 13 OTP site to provide treatment for 4,430 severe acute malnutrition (SAM) among children 6-59 months
o Set up 13 TSFP sites to treat moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) among 17,717 children 6-59 months and 7,203 PLWs
o Build the capacity of 150 health and nutrition care providers on management of acute malnutrition using current CMAM guidelines.
o Set up 13 BSFP locations for under-five year and 8,859 PLWs
o Conduct individual counseling on quality MIYCN/IYCF to 15,539 parents/people
o Conduct Vit A supplementation (VAS) to 12,202 SAM and MAM under-five year children in nutrition sites and community level.

1 - Needs

• Access to clean water and improved Nutrition services is a major challenge in several parts of Central, Western and Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei & Lakes States and it has caused health insecurities to worsen over the second quarter of 2019 leading to measles outbreaks. This is largely attributed to ongoing conflict and impassibility of road network by humanitarian actors (REACH, April-June 2019), hence increased morbidity and mortality in USR children. • Inadequate provision of essential healthcare services due to inadequate funding led to inadequate access to improved services such as RH and MPSS services. Development partners like World Bank support 27% of the existing health facilities in Jonglei State, 63% of Health Facilities are non-functional due to lack of funding (IRNA report, 2016/2017), resulting into increased morbidity/mortality of common diseases. • IPC report (July, 2019), 10,000 people in Cueibet in Lakes State are likely in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), Jonglei State populations estimated to be food insecure and 73% of its population facing crisis (IPC 3). All the proposed counties in Lakes are classified in Emergency phase (IPC 4); Awerial, Cueibet, Rumbek North, Yirol East, Yirol West etc. This is caused by a lean season, delayed rainfall, persistence inflation, displacement, assets depletion and destruction of livelihood as a result of ongoing conflicts in WES, CES, EES, Jonglei, WBS, and Lakes. • Of the 22 SMART surveys conducted between September 2018 and January 2019, eight of them showed GAM rates above the 15% WHO emergency threshold. However, five counties in Jonglei (Ayod, Pibor, Pochalla, and Twic East), Kapoeta East in Eastern Equatoria and Jur River in Western Bahr-al-Ghazal State show Critical levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM 15.0%-29.9%) (IPC Reports 2019). • LiveWell’s PHSA assessment (March, 2019) reported that inadequate Nutrition services, GAM rate of 19.9%, EPI coverage, inadequate provision of health, nutrition and WASH services are major causes of measles outbreaks in greater Pibor (IDSR, Week 8 of 2019), therefore, HC supported IOM/LiveWell, and MEDAIR/LiveWell to conduct measles vaccination campaign in 5 payams in March and September, 2019. Inadequate provision of primary health care and mental health services to be specific (9%) as a result of inaccessibility, insecurity, attack on health etc. Flooding Assessment in Pibor (Oct, 2019), 13,000 host populations are displaced as a result of flooding, and health services are severely disrupted. • Increased risks of Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) in borders of Yei & Magwi, and measles outbreaks since the beginning of the year have been reported in 15 counties including Pibor, Yirol East, Jur River and including Juba and Wau. 308 suspected measles cases in Jur River have been reported of which 4 tested positive for measles. It was recommended that WHO and humanitarian partners should carry out a reactive campaign in the areas (WHO EVD bulletin & IDSR report of weeks 36 & 37, September, 2019). • Low EPI coverage in Pibor, Jur River, Yirol East, Yirol West (EWARS Week 34, 2019), coupled with flooding in major counties in Jonglei State is anticipated to likely precipitate the outbreaks, malaria outbreaks, acute watery diarrhea (AWDs) and resultant severe acute malnutrition (flooding effect on health report in Pibor, Oct 2019) in under 5 year children and other vulnerable group like disabled, and pregnant women. LiveWell is therefore seeking your approval to recommend our concept note “Increase Access to Emergency Lifesaving Nutrition Services to vulnerable Under-five year and PLWs in Jonglei and Lakes, South Sudan” for the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Nutrition Cluster in the coming Year 2020.
