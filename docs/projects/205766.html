Project Summary

This project aims to increase access to potable water, sanitation and hygiene for households in in Cueibet County in order to reduce the risk of water-borne, hygiene and sanitation related diseases and ensure the right and dignity of the population. This will be achieved through the promotion of good hygiene practices for food preparation, stressing the five critical times of hand washing, promoting on more hygiene conducive environment for food intake, provision of WASH NFIs, construction and maintenance of WASH facilities. The project will also restore immediate access to basic and permanent WASH services within the communities of Cueibet counties. Furthermore, availing WASH facilities/services within the community will help in positive behavioural change and ensure quality access to WASH within the community. The capacity of WASH promoters and community mobilisers and Health Workers shall be built to promote WASH key messages within the community. WASH related IEC material shall be provided at all displayable positions in the community and near the water-points. Weekly sessions with Community Resource Persons and Water User Committees shall also strengthen and reinforce key messages earlier provided to communities including conflict sensitivity and resolution techniques at the household level. This will also help to mitigate WASH-related GBV and protection risks that particularly women and children face when spending long hours at the boreholes.
The project will significantly contribute to gender equality, including across age groups. This will be achieved through involving women in all decisions as pertains the direction of the project (e.g., priority areas of intervention); and having them occupy at least 50% of leadership positions in Water User Committees so that they can improve on their leadership skills. In total this project will benefit 11,111 boys, girls, men and women in the three targeted counties.

1. Needs

The right to receive humanitarian assistance is a necessary element of the right to life with dignity. This encompasses the right to an adequate standard of living, including adequate food, water, clothing, shelter and the requirements for good health, which are expressly guaranteed in international law (WASH Cluster South Sudan, Strategic Operational Framework, April 2023). In 2023, the standard of living and well-being of an estimated 6.1 million people will be affected as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Forty-two (42) percent of the population do not have access to an improved water source and 45 per cent need to walk for more than 30 minutes to access its main water source. This is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities, as well as for women and girls who are increasingly exposed to GBV. Limited access to water is also compounded by only 16 per cent of households reporting to having at least one jerrycan and/ or bucket to safely store water. Protection concerns are also present, with 16 per cent of both rural and urban households reporting that they felt unsafe while collecting water at the water points. Furthermore, South Sudan has historically displayed one of the lowest sanitation coverages globally due to long-lasting development challenge. Access to improved sanitation is below 10 per cent in 56 counties. Recurrent flooding regularly damages the existing sanitation infrastructure and with frequent displacement, there is great hesitation by the communities to invest in, reconstruct or repair sanitation facilities (HNO South Sudan 2023). Open Defecation rate at national level ranges from 80 to 90 per cent! Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. High open defecation rates also expose women, children and people living with disabilities at higher risk of violence and discrimination, particularly in IDP sites and other crowded settlements. The absence, presence or placement of WASH facilities is a driver of population movement and conflict over land and property in some areas and requires careful management to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach that strengthens social cohesion (WASH Cluster South Sudan, Strategic Operational Framework, April 2023). Similarly, Plan International conducted a rapid multisectoral need assessment in September 2023 and confirmed that there are few partners doing WASH intervention and at a very limited capacity. Furthermore, the assessment identified that there is: limited access to a clean water source for drinking; several spoiled water points; a lack of access to latrines; a lack of awareness on hygiene and sanitation; wide spread open defecation, a high prevalence of WASH-related diseases; and a shortage of WASH NFIs. Given the above facts, WASH response in the priority county of Cuibeit of Lakes is crucial to reduce morbidity and mortality related to hygiene and sanitation and to ensure the right and dignity of the affected community.

2. Project activities

The key project activities are as follows.- Construction of flood –resistant disability inclusive Boreholes and Gender segregated and disability inclusive toilets - Rehabilitation of spoiled boreholes and upgrading of existing boreholes to make them accessible to the disabled. - Formation/Reactivation & Graduation of Water User Committee. - Training/Mentorship of Water User Committee (Each committee having 5 members) on WASH Management, Revenue Collection, Gender and Protection and Conflict Sensitivity - Training/Reactivation of hand pump mechanics in some Payams. - Procurement of toolkits for hand pump mechanics (Standard and Special toolkits).- Training of Project staffs (Officers, Hygiene Promoters) on WASH, Gender and Child Protection and conflict sensitivity.- Procurement and distribution of WASH NFIs to children under five exiting nutrition sites and vulnerable community members- Printing of IEC visibility items (100T Shirts, 200 WASH IEC materials on WASH Key Messages) for Community Resource Persons. - Conducting Hygiene Sessions with Community Resource Persons by Hygiene Promoters per week for 4 weeks a month. - Quarterly Review Meetings with project staff and stakeholders. - Monthly monitoring visits by government staff and stakeholders.
