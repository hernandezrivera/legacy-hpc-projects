Project Summary

Protracted Syrian conflict, economic deterioration, ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, coupled with predated gender inequality rooted in harmful traditional norms and patriarchal culture, significantly exacerbate GBV risks throughout the country. Evidence show that women and girls are disproportionately affected by GBV, with certain groups being particularly exposed to risks of GBV, e.g. adolescent or separated and unaccompanied girls, women and girls with disability, divorced or widowed women, displaced women and girls. Various forms of GBV were mapped as prevailing, from sexual violence, IPV and family violence, physical and emotional violence, denial to resources, opportunities, and services, early and forced marriage, SEA, and many others. Covid 19 pandemic poses additional challenges: both with gender roles in care provision, further isolation of girls and women, increasing the rates of IPV and family violence, inaccessibility of GBV services, but also increase of reported online violence , particularly among younger generations.
As demand for GBV related services grows, the existing GBV services are insufficient to respond to all of the people in need. Aside of their availability, their accessibility for particular groups of vulnerable people can be challenging, while applying intersectional lenses when providing GBV services is often not practiced, resulting in lack of appropriate tailored made, case-by-case, and adapted services. Among the services mapped, where increased number of interventions is needed, are woman and girls safe spaces (WGSS) in various modalities, including quality GBV case management, CVA for protection outcomes, PSS activities, adapted programming for adolescent girls, etc.
Within such context, NCA will implement the project, building on its institutional knowledge on GBV programming (extended to GBV mainstreaming and mitigation as well). In order to respond to the mapped needs, and in alignment with 2022-2023 WoS GBV Framework, the project proposes a series of interventions aiming at improving access to quality life-saving GBV response services to GBV survivors, and women and girls at risk, and putting measures to prevent and mitigate GBV.
Under Activity 1, Provision of quality and life-saving GBV response services, the project will provide quality GBV case management and referrals, Age-sensitive PSS activities (for adolescent girls and adult women), CVA within GBV case management, and Capacity building of GBV actors to respond to GBV. Under Activity 2, Enhancing strategies to empower women and girls, the project will include Awareness raising activities to address GBV and harmful social norms , available services, and COVID-19, and Distribution of dignity kits. Finally, Activity 3, Promotion of GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response, will focus on engaging and training non-GBV (WASH) practitioners on reducing GBV risks and referring GBV survivors or women and girls at risk to specialized services. The project will implement all activities through static WGSS, and complimentary mobile teams to extend the outreach and include remote locations.
The selection of locations for the 2022 is informed by (1) HNO severity scale of GBV PiN, targeting Rural Damascus (with in Babella and Hajar Aswad with index 5) and Aleppo (with Jebel Saman, and As Safira with index 3), and (2) NCA’s institutional knowledge and experience in working with these governates through its partners, as well as presence of its WASH projects where GBV mainstreaming activities can be directed. Expansion towards Idleb in the districts with severity scale 4 in the 2023 is considered (Sanjar, Abul Thohur, Khan Shaykun).

4 - Needs

the 2022 HNO, GBV AoR, where 7.3 million people are identified as being in need of GBV services, 89% of them being girls and women, and 96,1% in severity category 3 or higher. The assessments further illustrate the realities of the most vulnerable populations: HNO’s shows that 71% of communities sees child marriage as an issue for adolescent girls and 57% mentioned that widows are at risk of exclusion from receiving humanitarian aid; “Voices from Syria” report on particularly heightened risk of violence, including SEA, and barriers in accessing services and opportunities women and girls with disabilities face, etc. In the governorates this project is targeting, Aleppo and Rural Damascus, GBV PiN are 1.306.840 and 1.299.012, respectively, with the severity scale in selected locations in Aleppo being 3 (Jebel Saman and As Safira), and in Rural Damascus 5 (Babella and Hajar Aswad).On the other hand, “Voices from Syria” map various gaps in GBV Service provision, highlighting (1) insufficient number of accessible static and mobile quality GBV specialised services (particularly to adolescent girls); (2) lack of sufficient support to women’s and girl’s resilience through the economic crisis, including the CVA assistance; (3) high risk of SEA and need for GBV mitigation (including within humanitarian response), amongst other challenges. Furthermore, NCA’s recent GBV assessment recognizes the countrywide need for building capacities in GBV response, particularly case management.

5 - Implementation of activities

In the first quarter of 2022, NCA will focus on identifying location of project implementation in targeted subdistricts – both through (sub)cluster coordination, and NCA’s assessment, including consultation with the affected populations. Acquiring relevant national administrative permissions and setting up the safe spaces will take place in the second quarter of 2022 (with the beginning of the project). In Rural Damascus, a WGSSs will be established in Babella, with mobile team targeting Hajar Aswad; in Aleppo, a WGSS will be in Jabel Saman, with mobile team targeting As Safira. In the second quarter service provision will begin, starting with outreach and dialogues with community leaders to ensure the project acceptance in the targeted communities, building on lessons learned through previous partner implemented projects in the selected governorates. Key procurement processes, and contracting services will take place in this period as well.Capacity building and mentoring of GBV staff will start from the onset of the project and continue throughout its life, ensuring GBV technical expertise, but also gender, age and disability sensitive programming in general, with adapted service provision and access for different beneficiaries. Focus would be put on the specific needs of adolescent girls (dedicated PSS groups, separate safe spaces, adapted case management, etc.), as well as persons with disability (PwD) (access, communication, case management and risk mapping).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

(1) Stigma and safety concerns prevent women and girls to access GBV services. Mitigation: The selected WGSS locations will ensure the conditions necessary for respecting privacy, while safety audits and consultation with girls and women will ensure protection for participants. The language used in outreach will avoid stigmatizing, or singling out GBV survivors. All staff will be trained on key GBV principles and concepts. (2) Lack of support from family members to allow women and girls to travel to WGSS and access services. Mitigation: WGSSs will be in proximity of targeted populations. To ensure acceptance, communities (including community and religious leaders) will be engaged in awareness raising as allies in outreach, promotion of services, and specific needs of girls and women. Parents of adolescent girls will be targeted as well when reaching out and identifying e.g. PSS participants. (3) PwD meet barriers in accessing GBV services. Mitigation: PwD will be included in outreach and IEC materials, while physical access to WGSSs will be adapted; women and girls with disabilities will participate assessments; staff will be trained to communicate with, identify risks and provide GBV protection services for PwD. (4) Beneficiaries are at risk of SEA and harm from staff. Mitigation measures: Accountability will be ensured trough community participation and set up feedback and complaint mechanisms; PSEA measures will be taken (please see PSEA Strategy).


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

NCA will implement a series of activities to roll out its global "2021 Norwegian Church Aid’s Protection from Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (PSHEA), and Child Safeguarding (CS) policy" in order to ensure ethical, safe and accountable service provision.The capacity building on the Policy will take place to inform all project staff on the code of conduct (including UNSG bulletin on PSEA), reporting, and responding to cases of SEA, including the training on Survivors assistance strategy (with particular focus on GBV case management teams, and relevant referral pathways); complaint report mechanisms will be set up with multiple entry points; PSHEA focal points for each project location will be appointed; awareness raising session and distribution of PSHEA related IEC will take place among affected communities (sensitive to age, gender, and cultural context). PSEA messaging and entry points for incident reporting will also be included in all other outreach and IEC materials as well.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1. Delay in receiving permits and access approvals from relevant authorities 2. Banking transactions are delayed as sending banks are reluctant to transmit funds to Syria, due to overcompliance to international sanctions 3. Access to target locations is restricted due to security or other external issues 4. Fraud and/or diversion of aid 5. Continuation and worsening of COVID-19 pandemic 6. Breach of data privacy by contracted service providers, e.g. cash Mitigating measures: a)Selection of project locations based on needs as prioritised by the HNO, and agreed with local authorities, selection criteria and red lines clearly communicated and agreed with authorities b) Identify alternative banking routes, and participate in advocating efforts to stress the humanitarian nature of the funds (exempt from sanctions)c) Continuous monitoring of the security situation d) Procurement of relevant services through market assessments and supply chain analysis e) Financial and procurement monitoring and checking procedures in place f) Remote service provision in the event of COVID-19peaks and lock downs g) Assessment of service providers, capacity building, diversifying of delivery methods.
