Project Summary

Through this project, CENTER FOR COMMUNITY SERVICE -SOUTH SUDAN is planning to distributed combined vegetable, crop and fishing kits to 12500HHs, 75000 individuals where 2500HHs, 15000 individuals will be for Pigi/Cana, 2500HHs 15000 individuals for Fangak , 2500HHs 15000 individuals for Uror, 2500HHs15000 individuals for Nyirol and 2500HHs 15000 individuals for Panyikang Counties of Jonglei and Upper Nile States respectively. The project wills also 3000HHs 18000 individuals in Fangak and Pigi for Cash Assistance. Fangak will have 2000HHs 12000 individuals and 1000HHs ,6000 individuals will be for Canal/Pigi Counties. The cash program project is set run for the period of six (6) Months only.
This will be followed by provision of training on improved agronomic practices as well as fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics to 600 fishermen in Fangak South, Old Fangak, Pauer and other 700 Fisher Men in Tonga of Panyikang County.
Within the onset of the project, CENTER FOR COMMUNITY SERVICE -SOUTH SUDAN is setting to trains the selected 120 per each location with the best agriculture practices and other management’s.

The trained farmers/fishermen will be expected to pass on their knowledge to the remaining farmers/fishermen. The knowledge gained from these trainings will be of importance in building the community resilience to future shocks eg floods and famine. These activities are aimed at improving production of vegetables, crop and fish hence contributing towards reducing malnutrition rates. The vegetable, crop and fish products will go a long way in restoring the nutritional status of the affected population, through increased food consumption and better dietary diversity score.
The project will reach the most vulnerable affected by the ongoing flood and existing Host Communities and the returnees who are already too vulnerable because of different shocks including conflicts.
We are planning to support the small group of farmers especially the returnees with vegetable seed and other tools for cultivation especially in the area where the space is available. Women play an integral part in agricultural production, as subsistence farmers, cash crop growers, food processors, and livestock owners, among other roles. It follows that empowering women will impact the agricultural markets overall. Recently, many development organizations have begun to integrate gender into their agricultural development projects. While this new generation of projects are too recent to provide evidence on the long-term impacts of targeting women in agriculture. CENTER FOR COMMUNITY SERVICE –SOUTH SUDAN seeks to identify strategies that are most effective in targeting women and that have the potential to economically empower women in the agricultural sector.

1. Needs

Between September and November 2024, 6.3 million people (47% percent of the population analyses)are in IPC Phase 3 or above (crisis or worse). Of this total,1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity –classified in IPC 4(Emergency and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic level of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (catastrophe).The most food insecure populations across South Sudan have been significantly affected by frequent climate-related shock(flooding, dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation ,high food prices), conflict and insecurity ,low agricultural production,anf the effects of the ongoing conflict in Sudan . Between September and November 2024, 11 Counties across the country and estimated 126,000(20 percent ) returnees are in phase 4, 62 counties are in phase 3 and 6 counties are in IPC Phase 2 (stressed). While some of the counties populations will experiences or likely experiencing IPC Phase 5( catastrophes)With the confirmed different assessments conducted by the UN Partners and food security NGOs, ( IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity are Uror County of Jonglei State ,and Pibor County in Greater Pibor Administrative Area and Malakal county of Upper Nile State.. In Fangak according the IRNA assessment conducted by the partners and the RRC concluded that 44,915 are confirmed to be under food insecure due to the floods impact while in Pigi, the IRNA report shown that 33,912 individuals facing food insecurity and as well in Nyirol County with the figures of 28300 individuals and Panyikang with 39820 individuals. The main causes of food insecurity are climate related shocks, manly floods which have destroyed farms, livelihoods and resulted in displaced populations. Over five consecutive years of flooding have been experienced in greater Upper Nile, Crops were destroyed before harvesting and in storage, this scenario has happened in Pigi, Uror ,Nyirol and Akobo Counties. While in Fangak there was no harvest at all due to the fact that the flood onset was start early of June.. Livestock died through drowning and starvation as grazing grounds were flooded mostly in Fangak, Akobo ,Pigi and part of Uror Counties. Conflicts witnessed at both sub-national levels and at regional level including spillover effects of conflict in Sudan that has increased pressure on humanitarian needs. Coupled with flooding, are intensified sub-national violence arising from political instability, cattle raids and revenge killings. Violence causes instability limiting access to land for cultivation but also results in deaths of productive members of the House Holds. Agricultural productivity has been persistently low and coping capacity of these populations has been eroded. At the Macro-economic level, depreciation of the local currency and effects of international Wars in Ukraine and Gaza have made food more expensive and scarcer. Coping strategies of affected households have been exhausted and household assets eroded. Without timely humanitarian intervention, this situation could worsen and plunge populations into famine situation. The goal of this intervention is to support the vulnerable populations in these selected counties with livelihood kits and training so as to help them restore and sustain their livelihoods. This intervention will be guided by 3 cluster objectives ,CO1: Food Assistance: Providing unconditional or conditional food assistance to people facing food insecurity CO2: Emergency Livelihoods: Protecting and rehabilitating the livelihoods of people at risk of hunger and malnutrition CO3: Resilience: Building resilience and access to housing, land, and propertyThe FSL Cluster targets different groups of people based on their needs and vulnerabilities. 2. Project activities

Project activities will be structured around the three cluster objectives. Objective one: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5& 4). Under this objective, the main activity will be construction of community assets through conditional food assistance and as well cash assistance to the most vulnerable families.. Intended activities will include construction of dykes around farms to prevent perennial flooding, excavation of drainage channels, construction/rehabilitation of access roads, de-silting water pans to collect water during the rainy season, excavation of shallow wells. FFA will help communities establish communal assets, mitigate effects of flooding on food production and enhance preparedness and response to flooding which has become a yearly occurrence in the target counties. Cluster Objective two: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. Under this objective, main activity will Emergency food production focusing on main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits. Dry season activities will include Emergency food & livelihood support through provision of fishing kits/ vegetable kits; and at least three variety of fast maturing vegetable seeds. Dry season farming will focus on water efficient farming methods to produce sufficient and nutritious vegetables for consumption and possibly sell for some cash. This is to ensure increased availability of food even during the dry season where availability of vegetables becomes a challenge and therefore complicates the food insecurity and malnutrition situation. Main season activities will concentrate on crop production specifically maize, sorghum and legumes. Fisheries activities will cut across the main season and dry season as there are sufficient fishing grounds for both seasons. Cluster Objective three: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. The main activity will be Training and capacity building on both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production targeting, farmers through field schools and on demonstration plots as well as the agro pastoralists. All activities will be implemented so as to establish a nexus between Humanitarian response, Peace building (conflict sensitivity) and Resilience programming. A strong emphasis will be laid on accountability to affected population to ensure the do-no-harm principle is observed. There will be a strong coordination at county level with other partners to reduce duplication and ensure efficiency and wider coverage. The project will emphasis integration with other sectors to achieve greater impact in meeting household needs. For instance, the project will support establishment of kitchen garden within Nutrition centers and target mothers and caregivers of children enrolled in nutrition program with training and input support. This is to ensure that those children discharged from nutrition program do not relapse but have sufficient and nutritious food at home. The project will also incorporate WASH messaging at home, especially with reference to preparation and utilization of food, human waste disposal and provision of WASH items including water containers and purification tablets, cooking utensils and food preservation containers from other partners. To improve access to milk and household income, the project will provide small ruminants to caregivers of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) children within the Nutrition centers. This will be implemented through the mother-to-mother strategy and Care groups for both distributions and training.
