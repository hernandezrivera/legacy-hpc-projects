Project Summary

The overarching objective is to provide time-critical and quick impact support to the vulnerable farming and herding families affected by man-made and natural shocks, including the COVID-19 pandemic. Special focus will be placed on women and youth, and
the integration of women in the decision-making process throughout the project. The project aims at ensuring the resilience and protection of the targeted communities and reviving their livelihoods, affected by the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic and the May
2021 Gaza escalations. This project falls under the Food Security Sector Strategic (FSS) Objective 1: “The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced" of the Humanitarian Response Plan 2022, as it improves the coping abilities and reduces shocks and environmental hazards of farmers and herders, with special focus on women and youth in those communities.
This project ensures accessibility and inclusion of all stakeholders irrelevant of their background, geographical location, or social status. A clear Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) confidential reporting process will be distributed and explained to all stakeholders, including beneficiaries and partners.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity in the West Bank and Gaza Strip (WBGS) is chronic as it directly relates to the political dimensions affecting most drivers of economic growth, including access to land, water, markets and jobs. More than 27,500 herders, half of which are Bedouins are living in Area C. Most of the Bedouin in the Jordan Valley are refugees. Originating from five tribes in the Negev desert, they were expelled from or fled from their lands during and shortly following the creation of the state of Israel.The Bedouin communities are traditionally either herders that roam the area to graze livestock, or farmers that need land to domesticate animals. Since Israel’s military occupation of the West Bank in 1967, the livelihoods of these communities have been strangled in different forms; lack of access to land and resources, illegal settlements and outposts, closed military zones and checkpoints obstructing their freedom of movement, in addition to harassment and violence from settlers is frequent. Bedouin families face constant threat of demolitions to their homes and other buildings such as schools, and water tanks. The Israeli authority states that these structures are demolished as they were erected without an Israeli building permit, however, a restrictive and discriminatory planning regime makes it virtually impossible for Palestinians to obtain Israeli building permits. Hence, empowering herding and farming communities retains a significant economic, dietary, and cultural importance. Their production is an important source for food, livelihood and income generation in the West Bank and Gaza Strip (WBGS). The importance of small ruminants (sheep and goats) is indicated by their various productive contributions (meat, milk, etc.), socio-economic relevance, and stability to farming systems. Almost all regions of the WBGS have holdings of sheep and goats, including around 200 Bedouin and herder communities that spread over the West Bank and Gaza Strip (WBGS). Traditional breeds predominate the herds. Small producers (1-35 heads) account for some 60-65 percent of the total number of holders. Producers that have less than five animals accounted for 15 percent of total goat holdings, with those with less than ten animals accounting for 26 percent of all sheep holdings. These herds are typically managed by family members, especially women. Milk and dairy products are major contributors to breeders’ household diets, income and are also important to the overall family livelihood and food security. The period between 10 and 21 May 2021 saw the gravest escalation in hostilities and eruption of violence in the Gaza Strip since 2014. This has worsened the already poor humanitarian situation, including the destruction or damage of homes, infrastructure and livelihoods; the severe disruption in the supply of basic services; and the internal displacement of people. Since 10 May 2021, at least 242 Palestinians have been killed, including 66 children, and more than 106 000 have been internally displaced due to the Israeli military attacks, which have halted following a ceasefire agreement reached on Friday, 21 May. The daily agricultural reports issued by the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) highlighted the below: • Fodder stock is depleting. The risk of disruption of the ompletely animal production sector (requiring approximately 600 tons of fodder per day) remains imminent, with dire consequences for the livelihoods of 17 000 households. • One of the largest agricultural inputs warehouses was destroyed in the north of the Gaza Strip, resulting in the destruction of 50 percent of overall agricultural inputs. • Due to the risk of being caught in the middle of a military operation, farmers cannot access their farms and markets. • There is limited supply of electricity to power essential infrastructure such as wells. • All of the above are expected to create a lasting detrimental effect on the whole agricultural sector. Additionally, the bombing of the largest agricultural inputs warehouse, which secured 50 to 60 percent of agricultural inputs needs n Gaza, and the destruction of the inventory of inputs and chemical fertilizers and pesticides put the agricultural season in the Gaza Strip at risk. In the aftermath of the escalation, exports of fresh agricultural products were suspended and did not resume to re-May levels. Moreover, the COVID-19 pandemic has affected both herders and farmers immensely. During 2021, women and men farmers in the Gaza Strip continued to face limited availability of inputs and higher prices for fertilizers, pesticides, seeds, plastic sheets and steel. Women and men Farmers and herders reported that fodder was available, but the prices were higher than pre-May escalation levels. Farmers reported that credit was extended selectively, favoring large scale producers, while small holders reported continued constrains due to liquidity shortages and/or limited access to credit. Additionally, poultry farmers in the Gaza Strip have stable consumer demand, sustained by the requests of school canteens; however, the high cost of fodder continued to constrain production capacity. Likewise, herders in the West Bank and Gaza Strip (WBGS) have faced obstacles of production and marketing all throughout 2020 and 2021. Hence, this intervention aims at protecting the endangered livelihood of those women and men farmers and herders in both the WBGS. Women play a vital role in maintaining and increasing the food security of rural households in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. In herding communities, women’s roles vary according to the number of livestock. In households with small numbers of livestock, women do most of the activities starting with wood collection for heating and cooking, taking care of the livestock including herding the sheep, feeding them, fetching them water, maintaining a clean shelter, milking them, processing the dairy products and marketing the products.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention will systematically consider Accountability to Affected Population in general and PSEA standards in particular. As part of the project activities, farming and herding communities will be informed about the project objectives, intended support, targeted group and selection criteria through information sheets that will be made available to all community members including elderly, women and children. The information sheets will include information regarding FAO grievance mechanism and PSEA.Output 1: Coping capacities enhanced and resilience against environmental shocks improved for vulnerable families (with focus on women and youth) in the targeted herders’ communities in the WBGS1.1 Distribution of drought tolerant seeds, in coordination with the MoA for 2100 HH (to plant around 25,000 dunums) in the West Bank.1.2 Rehabilitation and/or construction of 80 water cisterns (around 9,600 cubic meter), which would particularly lessen the burden of women in fetching water, from locations far from their house, and on youth in the WB.1.3 Rehabilitation of 1,500 (1000 in the WB and 500 in GS) animal sheds which allows easier cleaning of animal barns in WB and GS.1.4 Small-scale poultry farms supported with water sprinkler network and small water pump for mitigation of climate change in summer for 600 farm in GS 1.5 Distribution of small-scale farm management inputs (disinfection preventive material, salt blocks, wool and hoof secateurs). This helps to prevent animal disease and support animals’ wellbeing. Accordingly, the toolkit will support this purpose of animal disease prevention which takes the burden off women of caring for sick animals 200 Hh in WB and 200 Hh in GS.1.6 Distribution of dairy processing equipment (milk jars, milk shakers and cheese tanks): This intervention will save time and efforts on women as they are the main contributors for producing of dairy products for 600 Hh in the WB1.7 as part of controlling animal diseases and enhance part of the livestock value chain in addition to enhancing and enabling the market accessibility for women and youth through upscaling the capacity of the livestock market in Al Dahrieh and promoting their participation in local exhibitions and providing them with spaces to sell their products. FAO has previously implemented two phases in Al Dahrieh market under two different projects and this will complete the third stage of the market as recommended by the MoA. Output 2: Restored production capacities through immediate rehabilitation/repair of damaged water systems and improved management of water resources.Activity 2.1 restoring pumping efficiency of three underground wells through rehabilitation and the provision of three solar energy units for 150 farming households cultivating at least 450 dunums in the Gaza Strip (Biet Hanoun, Jabalia, and Diwer Al-Balah).Activity 2.2. Rationalizing water use in irrigation through the rehabilitation of five damaged water storing towers (each of 100 m3) for 100 farming households cultivating at least 300 dunums in Gaza Strip (East Rafah).Activity 2.3 Improving water accessibility and availability through the rehabilitation of 100 rainwater-harvesting cisterns for the benefit of at least 300 farming households cultivating at least 300 dunums in the West Bank (Salfeet: Dier Balout and Azzawyeh) via Conditional Cash Assistance approach (each cistern has a volume not less than 70 m³).Activity 2.4 Improving water use optimization in irrigation through the rehabilitation of ten irrigation water ponds in the Jordan Valley (Jericho: Jiftlik and Fasayel) of the West Bank via Conditional Cash Assistance approach, with total storage capacity up to 30 000 m³ for the benefit of at least 250 farming households cultivating at least 750 dunums. The Conditional Cash Assistance approach was noticed by FAO, through implementing several projects of similar nature, to be effective in addressing not only the needs of households and improve accessibility and availability of water used in irrigation, but also to inject cash (about 10 percent out of the total project cost) within targeted communities though contracting local labors and suppliers of the same targeted communities in order to perform the works. Consequently, this will capitalize on benefits of the rehabilitation work through enabling and creating job opportunities in the targeted localities from one hand, and maximize the ownership of the rehabilitated assets.Activity 2.5 Reduce water losses and restore water distribution efficiency through repairing/ replacing 90 km of damaged conveyance pipes at farm-level for the benefit of at least 360 farming households cultivating at least 1 080 dunums in Qalqilia (Izbit Salaman, Hableh, and Azzoun Atmeh).Activity 2.6 Technical support to water users through the creation of 40 water users committees for at least 40 rehabilitated water systems (eight in the Gaza Strip and 32 in the West Bank).Output 3: ARA farmers supported through immediate rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs.Activity 3.1 - 500 farming households supported with organic and basic chemical fertilizers for two dunums for each in the ARA.Activity 3.2 - 200 dunums of small-scale farmer’s greenhouses rehabilitated with plastic sheeting, insect-proof agricultural nets and required wood paneling for one dunum per households.Activity 3.3 Sixty plastic water harvesting ponds (each of 100 m3) rehabilitated with plastic sheeting, shadow net, safety fence and water pump.Activity 3.4 - Thirty-five concrete water harvesting ponds (each of 100 m3) supported with layer of concrete, shadow net, safety fence and water pumps.Activity 3.5 Training and coaching for beneficiaries of greenhouses and water harvesting ponds on irrigation water management conducted by the MoA.

3 - Indicators

Throughout the selection process, FAO will target the most vulnerable and most at risk households due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Women headed households, women’s cooperatives and households whose female members are actively engaged in agriculture related activities in support of household income will be prioritized during the community targeting and beneficiary selection processes. Similarly, beneficiaries with disabilities and older people will be given priority during the selection process.FAO will be working with local partners, who will be selected at a later stage during the inception phase. The local partners will play an important role in the following activities:1- Support in the beneficiary selection process,2- Support in distributing the inputs to beneficiaries, 3- Support in providing training.Each indicator will be gender disaggregated. Project activities and achievements will be monitored consistently to ensure continued alignment with the FSS response plan and the FAO Country Programme Framework 2018-2022. Sex- disaggregated data will be collected on all activities. Participation of women will be closely monitored and tailored adjustments will be made to ensure effective participation of women. Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted by interviewing a sample of female and male beneficiaries in order to monitor use of the inputs distributed, prospects for sustainability of the intervention as well as overall satisfaction of beneficiaries as part of FAO’s accountability to beneficiaries. A Grievance Redress Procedure on the basis of FAO guidelines and taking into account local best practices has been established and is currently functional. It includes clearly defined responsibilities and follow-up channels, starting with the officer in charge of receiving the feedback/complaint, and the proceeding processing procedures, up until final decision and the subsequent follow-up. The procedure includes several well-defend steps tied to specific timeframes, including preliminary review for eligibility, verification process and resolution, and will be publicized among targeted communities in the context of beneficiary selection processes, post- distribution monitoring as well as in the context of partnerships agreements if and when relevant. All project activities and results will be coordinated with the FSS to avoid duplication of emergency support efforts. Relevant results, analysis and lessons learned shall be shared with all FSS stakeholders. In addition, FAO will maintain coordination with the protection cluster and the Humanitarian Country Team in order to ensure alignment with and support of advocacy initiatives. The indicators are specified in the indicators tab at the end of the profile. All monitoring, reviews and reporting will be based on SMART and gender, and age sensitive indicators and targets ensuring accountability and coherence with gender mainstreaming throughout the project. Mainstreaming gender, age and environment within FAO’s programming is based on socio-economic/environmental assessments and analysis, utilizing a range of tools and analytical modules that are tailored to the WBGS context. FAO’s gender focal point, supported by FAO’s gender committee will play a key role in assuring that women and men associated with the project stakeholders have equal opportunities to participate in and benefit from the project’s activities at all levels.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

FAO will use in-kind modality as herders depend on seasonal products, for that they usually wait for the season to generate money and for that they can't buy the needed items in time. Hence, FAO will provide in-kind support through inputs distribution in order to have the material in time to improve their productivity. Cash modality: FAO will use the cash modality only in the cistern rehabilitation intervention. There is a high need for water in distant areas and contractors do not easily reach these herders communities. Hence, FAO will be using the cash modality to rehabilitate the cisterns FAO together with a representative of the MoA and the local partner will visit cistern locations and estimate the required support needed to have the cisterns functional. The herders will be given the cash amount in installments linked to achieving specific milestones. Once a milestone in the rehabilitation is achieved, a field visit is organized to ensure the cistern rehabilitation is on track and following that, part of the payment linked to this specific milestone will be released. FAO, MoA and the local partner will be involved throughout the rehabilitation process and will estimate the needed cost for rehabilitation and monitor the work achieved.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable farmers and herder's whose livelihoods are endangered. The selection of farmers and herder's is solely based on needs and the most food-insecure and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory factors. The interventions proposed under this project do not put beneficiaries at risk, however, FAO aims to ensure that all efforts will be made to prevent and minimize as much as possible unintended negative effects of the interventions. As part of the project inception phase, FAO will develop a risk log identifying all potential risks, their probability and ways of mitigation. The assessment and analysis of risks will be followed by a coordinated application of resources to monitor, minimize and control the probability and/or impact of risks. External risk factors will continuously be considered in order to ensure an adequate understanding of the context of FAO operations during the implementation of the project. FAO will also ensure the implementation of PSEA principles and will have a built-in process for reporting such incidents. The proposed intervention is meant to protect endangered livelihoods of vulnerable female and male farmers and herders in the Gaza Strip through securing the availability of time critical indispensable inputs and services, hence the core of the intervention aims at protecting beneficiaries rather than doing any harm. Priority whenever possible, will be given to vulnerable marginalized groups including women, people with disabilities, and older people. FAO also abides by the principle of Do No Harm; and respects a low profile during implementation of activities to avoid any harm coming to beneficiaries. Through FAO’s participation in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the development of the political situation in the WBGS will regularly be monitored and reviewed in line with project objectives. FAO contributes to policy-level initiatives to protect Palestinians from further reduction in access to land, sea and livelihood resources (e.g. water and agricultural inputs) owing to the political situation, through its participation in the UNCT Commendation Group and HCT Advocacy Working Group. All advocacy efforts will be conducted under the banner of the UN in the WBGS to ensure that FAO’s mandate is not compromised.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

FAO’s approach provides a framework for addressing and integrating issues such as gender equality, protection, the inclusion of the aged and people living with disability, and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse by FAO and partner staff. Throughout the process of beneficiary selection, disability and age will be a factor taken into consideration Beneficiaries with vulnerabilities will be prioritized.This is done through the following:• Formulation of local committee: a committee presenting all the CBO’s (women, men, youth), MoA and village councils, and municipalities; to ensure the social and technical criteria.• Announcement at community level for beneficiaries to apply for the project: for two weeks announcements are made at community events (such as after Friday prayers) and notices published in widely visible sources (posters in the market, local shops, women association center, health center and village council building etc). These announcements contain information about the project, including its location, methods and the selection criteria for beneficiaries, who are divided into two types: social and technical. The former includes family income, the number of family members (which must be more than seven) and that the family is female-headed: in our experience, women who are the sole providers for their families stand to benefit more from the skills and income which an FAO project will provide than women in male-led households, even if such women meet (and even exceed) the other requirements. • Sites visit to assess potential beneficiaries The project also project seeks to engage with targeted stakeholders and mainstream FAO’s commitments in each phase of the project cycle. FAO maintains its commitments through regular stakeholder engagement and participation, two-way communication, transparent disclosure and a grievance mechanism.
