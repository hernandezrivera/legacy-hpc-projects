Project Summary

The project targeting 30 000 resource-poor smallholder farming households and 6 schools in Cyclone Idai and El Nino affected areas aims to enhance resilience to extreme climate events such as flooding and drought by establishment of solar irrigated nutrition gardens founded on diversified agro-ecosystems through agroecology within the schools and communities.
The project is targetting Manicaland province, Mutare and Chimanimani districts. Practical Action has been working in various districts of Manicaland province over the past two decades and recently there has been evident need for support for households affected by the El Nino in Mutare Rural District. Chimanimani has also been hardly hit by the recent Cyclone Idai and community seed banks have been detroyed and need rehabilitation. According to the ZimVAC 2018 Rural Livelihoods Assessment, the number of severely food insecure people are expected to increase from about 567,000 people between April and June 2018 to 2.4 million between July 2018 and March 2019, translating to 28% of the rural population. Approximately 400 000 people are estimated to be food insecure in Manicaland based on the VAC results 2018 (this is second highest among all provinces). Manicaland is also among the top 3 provinces nationally with the lowest proportion of people who have an acceptable Food Consumption Score.
Practical Action applies the general guidelines on categorization of smallholder farmers in Zimbabwe that categorizes smallholder farmers into 4 main categories, i.e.:
o Category A farmers: Poor rural families with no land and/or no labour resources; these households need to be supported through social safety nets rather than agricultural interventions.
o Category B1 farmers: These households have access to land and labour and gain food security through cereal production and/or improved gardening or livestock interventions. However, these rely on other coping mechanisms to supplement on production and usually do not have adequate draught power.
o Category B2 farmers: These are emerging small holder farmers who, produce enough or consumption and sometimes surplus for sale. With some support, they can increase agricultural productivity to achieve food security and improve livelihoods though increased cereal production and/or sale of crops and/or livestock. They also often have adequate draught power.
o Category C farmers: Farmers in this category have the potential to enter into market linkage arrangements with the private sector and produce surplus and/or cash crops.
This specific project will target category B1 and B2 farmers
Intervention summary:
At least 5 000 farmers and 100 schools affected by Cyclone Idai and El Nino will receive season-sensitive emergency crop and livestock input assistance specifically in the form tools, locally adapted seeds, agricultural production and irrigation equipment.
A minimum of 24 critical community assets for crop and livestock production, specifically 12 seed banks and 12 nutrition gardens will be rehabilitated and built.
A total of 30 000 vulnerable smallholder farmers will receive extension and advisory services to improve yields, manage crop pests and livestock diseases, specifically in the form of agroecology training.

1 - Activities

1. Strengthening farmer managed seed systems through working with public (e.g. AGRITEX, CBI, Gene Bank) and private partners (including research and academia) in:a. Training and demonstrations on seed production and participatory plant breeding (through on-farm variety selection and trials)b. Facilitating seed exchange through seed fairsc. Distributing relevant seed starter packs (that include ocally adaptable cereal and legume crops for dryland farming and horticultural crops for the gardens)d. Restoring/rehabilitating 12 household and community seed banks (in a participatory and engaging manner to ensure ownership and sustainability)2. Provision of tools, seed packs and equipment for agricultural production to at least 5 000 farmers and 100 schools affected by Cyclone Idai3. Provide agroecology training to at least 30 000 farmers and setting up 12 agroecology demonstration sites in target communities through:a. Working with the community to map challenges and tailor-make agroecology solutions that respond to the context and needsb. Pluralistic training approaches that include training sessions, look and learn and practical demonstrationc. Working with communities to identify suitable sites to establish/rehabilitate school and/or community solar-powered nutrition gardens in target areas to demonstrate innovative approaches to strengthening food, nutrition security and climate resilienced. Working with the community to identify and set up easily accessible agroecology beacons of hope/centres of excellence for community learning that also demonstrate the use of renewable energy in agriculture and climate resilience building. Existing/dilapidated community gardens/irrigation schemes are primary targets for this. e. Promoting integration of crop and livestock enterprises to enhance diversityf. Roll out a Participatory Market Systems Development (PMSD) process in the target areas. Focus will also be on working with the community to identify value addition opportunities and promote relevant technologies to ensure a value chain approach towards promoting agroecology as well as widening the basket of livelihood opportunities for households and communities affected by Cyclone Idai4. Implement community-led natural resource management activities by:a. Facilitating community-based natural resource mapping to identify the challenges and possible actionsb. Supporting the integration of NRM and livelihood centred DRR in community initiatives and/or planning

2 - Indicators

1. Outcome indicators: a. Proportion of smallholder farming households in the target areas with improved resilience to climate shocks and stressesi. A locally adapted framework for resilience measurement/analysis will be applied based on learnings from existing frameworks such as FAO’s Self-evaluation and Holistic Assessment of climate Resilience of farmers and Pastoralists (SHARP) and Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis (RIMA)b. Reduction in the prevalence of hunger/undernourishment in the target areasi. Hunger will be assessed based on the WFP’s Food Consumption Score (FCS) framework and movement in the score from baseline levels will be assessed.c. Number of smallholder farming households with improved agricultural productivity through adoption of agroecology and sustainable NRMi. Farmers’ outputs per unit area (yield) will be assessed from baseline throughout the life of the project, but more importantly focus will also be on the association of yield changes with farmers’ practice. 2. Key output indicators (these focus on the key deliverables of the project):a. Number of smallholder farming households in target areas adopting environmentally sustainable agricultural production/farming practicesb. Number of seed varieties suited for the target areas that are identified and bred through participatory plant breeding methods c. Quantity of locally adapted seed stored/retained by smallholder farming households (and in community seed banks) per season d. Number of Cyclone-Idai-affected smallholder farming households with improved access to seeds, farm tools and equipment for agricultural productione. Number of community-led Natural Resource Management actions implemented in the target areasf. Number of men, women and children with improved access to technical knowledge and services through restoration or rehabilitation of community infrastructure, such as seed banks
