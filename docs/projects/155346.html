Project Summary

Goal: Contribute to enhance resilience of vulnerable Earthquake affected
households in Nord-Ouest.
Objective: Reduce shelter related vulnerability of the Earthquake affected population in the
municipalities of Port-de-Paix and Saint Louis du Nord through access to safe, healthy, and
appropriate living spaces, and safe building skills.
Results: R1: Vulnerable families from the rural areas in Nord-ouest have increased access to adequate and disaster resilient housing.
Under this result, the project will respond to housing needs of most vulnerable families through the construction of 250 new chore houses for the families whose house were destroyed. Under this result HFHH will also work with families to increase their understand about safe construction method and therefore increase their and community resilience to future shocks such as Hurricane, earthquake and flooding’s. The selection criteria to identify the most vulnerable ones will be guided by (5) majors’ phases: 1. Mapping of most affected localities (number of houses damaged or destroyed) – 2. Census of households -3. Preselection – 4. Community validation – 5. Final verification 6. Publication. Based on the data collected on the households related to their socio-demographic status, assets and income, HFHH will rank the most vulnerable families affected by combining, Technical assessment, qualitative and socio-economic criteria. Key selection criteria are as follow: Head of family/member Handicap; Woman headed family; Minor headed family; Large family more than 5 people; Breastfeeding family leader; Pregnant women Elderly people without support; Family income. The assessment will be guided by the approved Template by UCLBP
The draft list will be submitted to both Mayor office and community representative for validation. After local validation, Habitat for Humanity Haiti will submit the full beneficiary’s dossier including the assessment, BOQs and other relevant documents for UCLBP approval
To ensure transparency in the project implementation and ensure active participation of community in the monitoring, a complaint Mechanism will be established where beneficiaries and other community member can report their concern in regard to the project implementation. . Cross-cutting issues: Protection / (do no harm): HFHH will place emphasis on community engagement and participation to minimize potential tensions.
Activities: Result 2: knowledge and competences of community members and Construction professionals from Nord-Ouest on safe and disaster resilient constructions is Increased:
Under this result, HFHH will implement action to improve the skills and competencies of construction professional on safe and resilient construction and therefore promote safer construction culture in the project intervention areas. Theoretical and practical training will be giving to Masons and carpenters. Trainings will be facilitated by HFHH engineers. The trained construction professionals who successful complete the training program and pass the final test, will be used in the construction and repairs of household under this project. the list of trained masons and carpenters will also be shared with Mayor offices and community representatives who can refer them to community members in case of house construction/ repair need. Additionally, the list will be shared with UCLBP and shelter working group

Needs

On October 6, 2018, at approximately 8:11 p.m, a magnitude 5.9 earthquake struck 19 kilometers northwest of Port-de-Paix, Haiti. Following the earthquake Habitat for Humanity assessment team was dispatched to Port-du-paix and joined other humanitarian actor’s assessment teams under the coordination of DPC.Several municipalities in the Nord department are affected by the earthquake, namely Port-de-Paix, Saint Louis du Nord, Chansolme, Basin bleu, Annse-a-Foleur, La tortue, Jean rabel, Bambordopolis, Mole Saint Nicolas and Baie de Henne. However, the two most affect Municipalities are Port-de-Paix and Saint Louis du Nord. The number of affected families in need of urgent housingn assistance in these two municipalities can be estimated at 6611, according to the final assessment report from DPC released on 13 October 2018. 1014 out of 6611 houses were destroyed. DPC report also highlighted the protection risk for affected family’s protection and further exacerbated the likelihood of exposure to weather conditions such as cold temperatures, rain and storms. The risks of flooding, landslides and landslides remain very high throughout the department. Preliminary assessment data from Habitat for Humanity indicates that damage caused by the Earthquake damages were exacerbated by poor quality of constructions, especially for the most vulnerable whose construction did not meet the minimum requirement for safe and resilient construction that can resist to shock such as earthquake or hurricane. This is particularly due to low awareness on linkages between safe and resilient construction and shelter related disaster impact mitigation. Adding to this is also the limited availability of construction professional trained in disaster resilient construction.

Activities or outputs

Activities: R1Land assessment: to mitigate the risk of future housing problems related to flooding, land erosion and hurricane, Habitat for humanity Haiti will conduct a thorough analysis of land appropriateness for safe and disaster resilient construction. Construction of a Core House: Homeowner-driven construction approach will be used for constructions following TCLA model, the project will support 250 Households from Nord-Ouest to build new Core house of 27.88 m2, comprised of a bed room, a multi-functional room, a veranda, rain harvesting system and a toilet. Home owners will be involved and take the lead in the construction process to define the locations for construction, design and technical characteristics, selection of the local trained masons, negotiations of the labor cost, supervision of the work of the masons etc. each core house will cost as follow: Construction Materials funded by the project 3,048.88 and Beneficiary contribution = 384,26 USD a total of 3,469.14 USD. Labor funded by the project 912.43 USD and beneficiary contribution 35.74. total house cost to be funded by the project is 3,997.03 USD. The total cost of the house with beneficiary contribution is 4,417.31 USD (91% funded by the project and 9% from Beneficiary contribution in form of local available and accessible materials. Supervision of houses’ Constructions: To ensure strict application of technical guidelines, HFHH engineers will be based in the community to guide the households and trained local masons in safe construction process to ensure houses fully meet safety and security standards and they are disaster resilient activities: R2Safe construction Training: to fill the existing skills gap on safe construction, the project will support to increase local technicians’ construction skills and knowledge on construction techniques, incorporating disaster risk reduction practices, while respecting local/traditional norms (and culture). Technical Construction training for masons will be conducted through a mix of theoretical and practical sessions, facilitated by HFHH civil engineers. Safe and resilient construction awareness campaign: Building culture and resources represent the starting point to develop vulnerability reduction strategies strongly rooted into local conditions and practices. Housing construction in rural areas of Haiti is by default owner-driven combined with the use of local masons. Target: the project will target 250 most vulnerable households affected by 2018 earthquake in Nordouest Nord-Ouest. The beneficiaries will be selected based on transparent and participatory process guided by Humanitarian standards. Women headed household and Handicaps will be prioritizing. Other prioritization criteria include: Minor headed family; Large family (more than 5 people); Breastfeeding family leader; Pregnant women; Elderly people without support and family income. Out of the 6 eligibility criteria, 3 are gender related.Coordination and Collaboration with local authorities and Shelter working group: In Nord-Ouest the collaboration started with DPC will be continued throughout the implementation of the project. HFHH will continue attending the shelter working group meetings and shared lessons learned with other shelter focused actors. The project will also work in close collaboration with local authorities who in Coordination with Habitat for humanity will provide the list of potential beneficiary communities and households based upon which Habitat will conduct the need assessment to identify the final project beneficiaries validated by community-based structures and local authorities. Habitat for Humanity Haiti will closely coordinate with UCLBP. To ensure UCLBP oversight of the project, a full package with all beneficiaries’ information, including assessments, BOQs and other relevant data will be shared with UCLBP for approval. Joint visit between Habitat and UCLBP team be organized to the project sites
