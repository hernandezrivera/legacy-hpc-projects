Project Summary

In line with HRP SO2, Syria Relief project for 2023 aims to strengthen access to lifesaving and life-sustaining essential primary health services, provide routine immunization services to all children under 2 years old, provide antenatal, and Increase access to quality health services by establishing functional health facilities and mobile medical units with supporting referral, care in addition to Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services
The total estimation target will be 131600 individual beneficiaries in 2023 for as following;
• 2 fixed PHC with a target of 65000 BNFs, including GP, gynecology, pediatric, and pharmacy
• 3 medical mobile clinics with a target of 24000 BNFs (establishment of mobile clinic units will be in the most needed areas like Tal Abyad and Ras-Alain, SR MEAL team conducted MSNA with key informative there and the lack of health services were one of the top five concerns in addition to long distances and difficulties in access to health centers, so the mobile clinics will be a good solution to this issue.
• 5 immunization fixed centers, and 1 immunization mobile team with a target of 41600 of children (DPT3 2800 under 1 year of age, MMR2 13600 under 2 years of age)
These centers are located in Der Hassan Hospital- Kafr Takhareem Hospital-Jennie PHC-and Kelly PHC ,and Sarmada PHC.
• Antenatal care 1000 visits to be conducted in the RH clinics.
There will be increasing focus on outreach services, including health promotion activities and homebased specialized medical care focused on people with chronic conditions, people living with disabilities (PwD), and at-risk pregnancies.
The project has been developed to respond to needs identified by MSNA multi sectoral need assessment that conducted by Syria Relief team in Sep 2022, and other secondary reports like (HNO 2023), to ensure that project will be implemented in high quality manner M&E department will support in various types of monitoring activities , and conducting, the final project evaluation at the final stage of the PCM. addition to the evaluation provides information that is credible and useful, enabling the incorporation of lessons learned into the decision-making process

3 - Needs

More than 15.3 million people are in dire need of life-saving health assistance in 2023, a 3.2 million increase from 2022. This includes 5.3 million displaced persons, 2.2 million children under 5 years – including 502,795 live births expected, 4.2 million women of reproductive age (15-49 years), and more than 700,000 elderly people who will demand integrated and lifesaving health services in Syria. The increase in 2023 PIN is attributed to the recurring disease outbreaks, , the change in the methodology to be more proactive rather reactive and focus more on services availability, deteriorating socio-economic status of the communities, which resulted in increased displacement and worsened determinants of health thereby negatively impacting health outcomes . Both the quantity and quality of healthcare workers are major gaps. They need to be trained and equipped to implement integrated and essential health service packages and provide gender-sensitive and comprehensive services (Draft HNO 2023) SR conducted MSNA in Tell Abyad and Ras-Alain where health services and needs were one of the top five concerns according to KIs in those two districts, no mobile clinic, lack of primary and secondary health facilities in addition to spread Leishmania, Cholera outbreak, and difficulties in access to health facilities. SR developed this project based on needs that mentioned in HNO 2023 report (secondary data) and SR MSNA report (primary data)

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

SR will do many actions to mainstream protection and gender across all project stages as following: • SR conducted a rapid needs assessment by using Focus Group Discussions (FGD) with males and females separately as well as the most vulnerable groups such as disabled and PLW to ensure the program design meets the exact need for beneficiaries to avoid doing harm in the implementation stages.• Female staff will be hired for health team, to recognize and address gender and power dynamics and to ensure that people do not experience coercion, discrimination, or violence/mistreatment/disrespect/abuse in receiving health services.• Ensuring that services and supplies are affordable or free, accessible to all without any discrimination.• The health facilities should be designed in a way that ensures the confidentiality and privacy of each beneficiary especially for women and girls.• Safeguarding environment should be considered to prevent harm and reduce the risk of abuse or neglect to adults and children. • Confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system will be designed and beneficiaries will be clearly informed where to refer or report incidents or violations.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual exploitation and abuse risk against beneficiaries especially women and girls.2-Gender-Based Violence Risk against beneficiaries.Mitigating Measures:• All staff will be trained, acknowledge, and sign a code of conduct for sexual exploitation and abuse, moreover, SR has zero-tolerance against SEA.• PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (PSEA posters will be distributed to beneficiaries, PSEA banners will be placed at the location of the project, PSEA messaging/videos and hotline information will be shared with beneficiaries through SMS/WhatsApp).• All project staff will be trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies, to ensure full adherence with the Do No Harm principle and to stress that PSEA and GBV issues will be dealt with firmly and expeditiously.• Ensuring that services and supplies are affordable, free, accessible to all of beneficiaries without any discrimination or exploitation. • Establishing internal SEA reporting and investigation mechanism, with ensuring that all protection cases including GBV and PSEA will be referred to the respective focal point in the respective locations and sensitive data will be handled and kept confidentially with the PSEA field FP.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Risk: Safety and security is the major and high risk across Syria. The security situation remains unstable in NWS. There might be a high risk of closure of borders due to recent political developments.likelihood: High, İmpact: high. Mitigation: SR owns a warehouse in its Idleb to store life-saving drugs and other essentials for use as a backup in case of any security incident. Disruption in medical supplies: Due to the situation in Syria supplies of essentials, particularly drugs can be affected due to closure of borders. It poses a medium risk.2.Risk: spread of epidemic diseases such as Covid-19 disease and Cholera.Likelihood: high, Impact: medium.Mitigation measures: SR will apply precautionary measures about COVID-19 as well as WHO recommendations for staff and contractors in terms of hygiene practices, using PPE, and maintain physical distance for safety. In addition to raising awareness towards Cholera, and updating the staff about all the measures according to cholera taskforce.
