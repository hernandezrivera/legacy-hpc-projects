Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to ensure safe quality education to 30, 000 children affected by natural disasters in Haiti including 4, 000 children deported from the Dominican Republic . To reach this goal different interventions will be carried out during the implementation phase of this project: establishment of Temporary Learning Spaces or light rehabilitation for the schools that have been damaged or totally destroyed by the earthquake of October 6th, 2018 or by Hurricane Matthew on October 5th, 2016; distribution of school kits and school material, psycho-social support support to children and teachers affected by those natural disasters, direct cash transfers to the most vulnerable families, capacity building of the Ministry of Education in Disaster Risk Reduction and Management.

Needs

The most vulnerable children in Haiti continue facing many challenges regarding their right to quality education due to several structural factors. According to the latest study around 320,000 children are still out of school because of multidimensional barriers such as prohibitive costs of schooling, child work, lack of public schools and poor quality of teaching and learning. This situation is exacerbated by frequent natural disasters that regularly hit this Caribbean Country. Just in two years (2016-2018), according to the latest figures from the education sector’s assessments, around 1800 schools have been either damaged or destroyed by Hurricane Matthew in Grande-Anse, South, Nippes departments and by the earthquake of October 6th in North, North-West and Artibonite Departments. Despite the efforts of many partners, around 300 schools remain badly affected or have not been rebuilt and more than 500, 000 children have been exposed to the risk of exclusion due to natural disasters. In addition, because of the Haitian-Dominican migration situation, around 10, 000 families are expelled from the Dominican Republic every month. UNICEF along with other partners have supported the Ministry of Education in restoring education facilities to get the students back to schools. However, despite all efforts and commitments, around 180, 000 vulnerable children are still in need of safe education services.

Activities or outputs

Major activities will include: 1) Establishment of Temporary Learning Spaces and/or Rehabilitation / refurbishment of schools and classrooms equipment with WASH facilities in cooperation with WASH Sector. 2) Provision of adequate learning materials for children and teachers. Essential materials such as pens, pencils, chalk, notebooks and textbooks are needed to ensure learning and teaching can take place in classrooms. 3) Ensuring psycho-social support and promotion of the normalization of children’s routines through child friendly approaches to learning during emergencies;4) Support to the MoE in the preparation and response activities (coordination, assessment and information management activities);5) Support to most vulnerable families through CASH Transfers to ensure education for children and avoid child labor and child separation. Outputs are the following: 1) 6,000 affected children 5 14 years old access education through establishment of Temporary Learning Spaces and/or rehabilitation and/or equipping of 20 schools 2) 30,000 affected children 5 14 years old receive learning materials to support their access and learning3) 4, 000 returnee children have access to education through Direct Cash Transfers to their families4) 5,000 affected children 5 14 and 450 school teachers, directors and inspectors benefit from psycho-social activities and training 5) South, Grand Anse, North, North West, North-East, South-East and Artibonite departments have an updated education contingency plan Indicators and targets # of affected 5 18 years old children accessing education through establishment of Temporary Learning Spaces and/or rehabilitation and /or equipment of schools # of affected 5 18 years old children receiving learning materials to access education # of affected children and school teachers, directors and inspectors receiving psychosocial support # of departmental directorates having an updated contingency plan
