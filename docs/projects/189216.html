Project Summary

The proposed 12 months project is intended to enhance provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services, through integrated approach, to address the priority needs of vulnerable women, men, girls and boys among IDPS and returnees in Rubkona, Magwi, Bor South & Juba, which is linked to the intersectoral strategic objectives 2.4, 2.5 7 2.7 ie. reduce the suffering of GBV survivors including women, men, girls, boys and ability-challenged persons through the provision of multisectoral specialized GBV response services; vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys including ability-challenged persons have improved access to GBV prevention and risk mitigation interventions; and host communities, IDPs and Returnees, including women and persons with disabilities, have increased knowledge of and ability to exercise and enjoy housing, land and property (HLP) rights, respectively.
The project will cost $692,065 i.e ($311,427 for GP; $256,888 for HLP & $123,750 for GBV) and will be implemented in Rubkona with severity level 5; Juba & Bor South with severity level 4 and Magwi with severity level 3. The multisectoral services i.e. medical, PSS, legal support & livelihood assistance for GBV survivors will be provided in line with the GBV sub cluster SOPs focusing on the guiding principles, survivor centered approaches and referral pathways. Overall, 156,366 people are targeted in the 4 project locations, however STW will target 23,309 where 6340 will benefit from GP, 4520 from HLP and 12,449 from GBV, comprising of 9729 women, 4238 men, 7477 girls and 1915 boys and 2300 persons with disabilities.
STW has presence in Rubkona, Juba, Bor South and Magwi with available WGFs i.e 1 in Rubkona, 1 in Juba, 4 in Bor South and 1 in Magwi with available case workers i.e 2 per WGFs. Through these spaces, STW will provide women, men, girls and boys with individual, group, specialised PSS and other related general protection services; we shall also conduct case management through legal information, medical treatment, PSS, livelihood and referral support to women and girls attending the WGFs in order to provide holistic multisectoral GBV response. The utilization of the WGFs will be in line with South Sudan WGFs guidelines which ensures that women and girls are protected, empowered and feel physically and emotionally safe, comfortable and able to express themselves. In Juba, STW has entered MoU with a medical facility where rape survivors are referred for timely treatment. So far 48 survivors have been treated with fistula and uterus tear surgery, STDs etc as a result of rape. In this project, STW will provide medical treatment to survivors through similar arrangements in the other locations as a way of strengthening GBV case management.
STW will also provide women, men, girls and boys including PWDs and the sick with legal advice, counseling and awareness raising sessions through neighborhood assemblies awareness raising sessions, general protection awareness raising; and peace building awareness raising. These sessions are meant to encourage survivors to seek timely help at the WGFs where specialized individual and group PSS sessions are provided to survivors’ to facilitate healing process from traumatic events. 80 women and girls (60 GBV survivors & 20 HLP cases) who may wish to pursue legal services will be supported through initiating legal proceedings.
STW will also provide trainings to male and female staff, traditional chiefs, women leaders, local authorities on GBV legal frameworks on GBV guiding principles, referral pathway, PSEA and legal aid in in Rubkona, Bor and Juba. STW will train men and women on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building. We shall also hold consultations with communities throughout the project to ensure their concerns are included in the project implementation. All activities will take a “Do No Harm and conflict sensitive approach”. IDPs, returnees and HC will be targeted.

1 - Needs

The effects of years of conflict continue to be felt throughout South Sudan, with 8.9 million people estimated to be in dire humanitarian needs. Access to essential protection assistance remains a huge challenge to IDPs and returnees in Bor-Jonglei, Rubkona, Magwi & Juba. The humanitarian situation in these areas is expected to worsen through 2022 and 2023 due to multiple shocks, including recurring violence, impacts of flooding & the COVID-19 pandemic [Humanitarian Needs Overview Report, Jan 2022]. By Dec. 2021, the population of Rubkona IDP site increased to 118,000, with 82% female headed HHs due to floods. Physical access challenge caused by floods continue to limit protection assistance: many adolescent girls cannot go to school because their parents cannot pay school fees, which increases the risk of child marriage, thereby worsening the already dire protection situation [Inter-agency GBV risk assessment, Bentiu, Dec. 2021]. The absence of static formal courts, which allows informal courts to continue handling GBV cases including rape, remains a major challenge to protection. Traditional chiefs continue to rule cases against women due to patriarchal bias. There are no permanent judges in Bentiu. The only permanent state prosecutor lives in Juba. The UNMISS supported mobile court is meant for Bentiu IDP site and was last organized in 2020 [Field Monitoring Report, Rubkona, Dec. 2021, Steward Women].In Bor South, more than 7000 people were displaced in February 2022 alone; food, animals and HHI was also looted. The IDPs rely on wild fruits and sale of firewood as alternative source of livelihoods, which increases the protection risk of women & girls [Baidit IDPs Joint Inter-cluster assessment mission report, Bor, Feb. 2022]; and worsens the already precarious humanitarian situation. Access to justice and rule of law remains limited to GBV survivors, with many violations going under reported. In Magwi, the fighting between cattle herders and the host community in Feb, 2022 resulted into the death of more than 20 people, with several houses burnt down and properties destroyed, further causing displacement. There are no safe places for the displaced women and girls in Magwi town- only one toilet is shared by all- male, female and children, thus posing potential risk to GBV. Lack of clear referral pathway has limited GBV reporting and protection assistance to survivors [Inter-agency rapid needs assessment report, Magwi, 4th March 2022]. With crop cycles destroyed by herders, Magwi will face a massive shortage of food. With the IDP population increasing, the humanitarian situation is expected to worsen in the coming days [https://radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/7-people-killed-hundreds-flee-attacks-in-magwi]. Our interaction with IDPs and returnees in Juba demonstrate increased cases of land grabbing and illegal evictions [with women falling the worst victims], which prevents people from returning home in large numbers. Juba continues to witness horrendous acts of rape on women and girls. The conflict, coupled with triggers has increasingly weakened survivors' protection pathways. Generally, women, girls, men and boys protection vulnerabilities have increased since the transition of the POCs into IDPs in Juba, Rubkona and Bor, thereby creating further risks related to HLP and the established IDP camps. Over 82,000 returnees to Magwi are also faced with multiple issues connected to both HLP rights and wider security. By Oct 2021, only 64% of the overall critical protection needs, including medical assistance to survivors were addressed & less than 50% of the cases were prosecuted, thus the need for this project [https://reliefweb.int/sites/reliefweb.int/files/resources/January-2022-Update-Sexual-Violence-in-South-Sudan.pdf].

2 - Project activities

1. STW will provide case referrals to 800 women and girls (200 per location); PSS to 1000 women, men, girls and boys (250 per location); assorted games for 2000 adolescent girls in 4 WGFs (500 adolescent girls to benefit per location); and business start-up kits for 1000 women and girls (250 per location). These are aimed at ensuring the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas in line with SO2.4(GP).2. STW will conduct awareness sessions on HLP topics such as Land Act, 2009, Transitional Constitution and local government Act through the neighborhood assemblies reaching 4000 people (1000 per location); conduct individual, group, specialised PSS and other related general protection services (Psychosocial support and access to documentation to 1200 women, men, girls & boys (150 per location); conduct general protection awareness raising activities to 2000 women, men, girls & boys (500 per location); conduct peace building awareness raising activities to 2000 women, men, girls & boys (500 per location); STW will also conduct legal advise and legal counseling on HLP to 500 women, men, girls & boys (125 per location); train 140 community leaders/members (35 per location) including women & men on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building. These activities will enable durable solutions for IDPs and other populations under (HLP) SO2.7.3. STW will also provide services to 1800 women and girls in the 6 WGFs (300 per WGFs) (Juba=1, Magwi=1, Bor South=3 & Rubkona=1); conduct GBV awareness sessions through the neighbourhood assemblies reaching 4000 people (1000 per location); legal advise and legal counseling to 600 (150 per location) women, men, girls & boys; litigation support to 80 GBV survivors including HLP cases through the statutory court (20 per location); pay medical bills of 45 GBV survivors of rape and child marriages in Juba, Bor South and Magwi. These services are aimed to ensuring the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas in line with SO2.4 &2.5 (GP & GBV).4. STW will conduct GBV prevention initiatives in Bor South outside the former POC through EMAP aimed at understanding the participants’ perception on social norms, gender roles etc; support the updating of GBV referral pathways with 60 partners Juba, Rubkona & Bor South; provide 2000 girls with dignity kits (500 girls per location); refresher training for 6 community paralegals on GBV legal frameworks (2 in each location of Magwi, Bor South, Rubkona); conduct refresher training for 6 caseworkers on GBV minimum Standards in , Rubkona, Magwi and Bor South (2 per location); train and support 355 women and girls with fuel Efficient Stoves as part of GBV risk mitigation measure (74 in Juba, 64 in Magwi, 76 in Bor South, 121 in Rubkona); train 6 women leaders and 14 traditional male chiefs on GBV guiding principles and GBV legal frameworks in Magwi-5, Bor South 7 and Rubkoba 8; aimed at preventing and mitigating protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience under (GBV) SO2.5]5. STW will provide livelihood support to 470 women & girls ((111 in Juba, 95 in Magwi, 114 in Bor South, 150 in Rubkona); train 252 men and women on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning as community mobilisers (56 in Juba, 48 in Magwi, 57 in Bor South and 91 in Rubkona).

3 - Project indicators

The provision of case referrals will be measured through the number of women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns supported through case management and referral services. The provision of PSS to the community, engaging adolescent girls in recreational activities and the procurement and distribution of business start-up kits will be measured through the number of women, men, girls and boys provided with individual, group, specialised PSS and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services,). Legal awareness sessions including on HLP through the neighbourhood assemblies will be measured by the number of women, men, girls and boys reached through awareness raising activities. Training for women & men on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building will be measured by the number of women and men trained in conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building. Provision of services to women and girls at the WGFs will be measured by the number of women and girls who accessed services in WGFS (disaggregated by age and type of service. Provision of legal advise and legal counseling including on HLP to women, men, girls & boys and litigation of GBV cases including HLP cases through the statutory court will be measured by the number of women, men, girls and boys supported with legal counselling/legal Aid. Payment of medical bills of GBV survivors of rape and child marriages will be measured by the number of women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns supported through case management. Implementation of EMAP activities will be measured by the number of women, men, girls and boys reached through GBV prevention initiatives (disaggregated by type e.g. SASA, EMAP etc.) Supporting the updating of (3) GBV referral pathways Juba, Rubkona & Bor South will be measured by the number of GBV referral pathways established /updated in targeted locations. Provision of dignity kits will be measured by the number of women and adolescent girls who received dignity kits (disaggregated by age) as part GBV risk mitigation measure and addressing differential needs and GBV risk mitigation measure. Trainings for staff and frontline workers will be measured by the number of staff/frontline workers trained on GBV minimum Standards ( Case management , PSS etc.).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback through suggestion boxes, SMS messages, focus group discussions, public forums, mobile voice calls and individual one-to-one outreach will be used to address implementation challenges and improve on achieving project goals. The feedback will also be used to address specific needs of the target groups or to fit precisely within the local contexts. We shall also use the feedback to improve the quality of project implementation, design of new projects and evaluate our project and be accountable to beneficiaries before, during and after implementation of projects.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

An all inclusive and gender sensitive complaint and feedback mechanisms comprising women and men, youth, people with disabilities, the elderly and community and local government leaders will be set up to monitor project implementation and ensure the voices of all beneficiaries are heard. Project accountability groups will also be formed based on community-defined vulnerability criteria and consulted regularly to provide feedback on the project and influence our programming on an ongoing basis. We shall continue to support the established survivor support groups through the organization's communicate with communities strategy to reach out to women survivors with key messages on human rights, access to justice and referral pathway to amplify their voices. Special community based awareness raising events on HLP and GBV will be designed targeting people with disabilities, the elderly and lactating mothers. In this case, the neighborhood assembly will be an integral strategy of the awareness-raising events, aimed at enabling women and children, the elderly and women taking care of little children to benefit from the project. Designing relevant, appropriate and updated audiovisual materials for feedback on the project from illiterate beneficiaries is also integral to our communication strategy.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

STEWARDWOMEN has PSEA policy that recognizes the fact that PSEA must be integral to its humanitarian and resilience work and that the policy is disseminated to all project stakeholders, vendors, project staff etc. We shall ensure that electronic copies of the PSEA policy are shared with the project staff during project staff induction, as has been the case the last couple of years. Besides, all staffs will continually be briefed and reminded of the need for adherence to the PSEA policy, similar to other organizations’ policy during staff meetings, project orientations and other key organization events. We shall brief beneficiaries on the PSEA policy during the joint-stake holders' project inception workshops, including the role of project stake-holders in monitoring and reporting sexual abuse and exploitation of beneficiaries by project staff. Key aspects of the PSEA policy will also be printed and distributed to representatives of key project beneficiaries. We shall also set-up community based project accountability groups for monitoring and reporting complaints related to project implementation, including sexual abuse and exploitation by the project staff. We shall undertake thorough background check of all project employees during recruitment and award of vendors' contracts, to minimize the potential risk of PSEA to project beneficiaries. Information on the PSEA will also be disseminated to beneficiaries during activity implementation, thereby making PSEA an integral part of our strategy. Besides, we shall mainstream information dissemination on the PSEA into all our development and humanitarian work, as well as promote standalone information sharing sessions with beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

STW will hold joint stakeholders’ project inception workshop in month one of project implementation, to inform target communities about the project, solicit local expertise and clarify stake-holders roles. The M&E officer will conduct quarterly field monitoring visits and share the reports with beneficiaries during key community based project accountability events. We shall set-up a gender-sensitive community feedback mechanism (for consultation, feedback and complaints) to inform and change programming, including the establishment of accountability groups, based on community-defined vulnerability criteria. STW will also establish GBV survivor support groups through the organization’s communicate-with-communities strategy to reach out to women survivors with key messages on human rights, access to justice and referral pathway. Also, documentation of activities is a critical component of our communication strategy in the project. Stories of change and best practices will be documented in form of videos and newsletters and distributed to beneficiaries. The team plans to use Excel and SPSS software for community based project monitoring, which can link beneficiary feedback and track responses over time. This will provide valuable lessons and accurate credible data for multiple actors on trends, the quality of current programming, and the potential for scaling up impact.
