Project Summary

The food security situation in East and West Hararge zones of Oromia region continues to deteriorate following the poor performance of the 2020 meher season, the complete failure of the 2021 belg crop, elevated livestock deaths, and increasing cases of malnutrition. The Zonal Disaster Risk Management Office (DRMO) estimates that there are over 2.8 million food insecure people across both zones (nearly 40% of the total population). In September 2021, OCHA, quoting East Hararge Zonal Disaster Risk Management Office (DRMO), reported that ongoing ethnic conflicts between Jarso/Oromo and Geri/Somali has caused recurring mass displacements, destruction of property, and occupation of land.
The security situation along the border of Chinaksen woreda, Oromia Region and Tuliguled woreda, Somali Region has been volatile since 2017. The conflict which occurred in July 2021 in these border areas resulted in over 8,370 new displacements. Overall, due to the recurring conflict, there are an estimated 148,000 IDPs and over 201,000 returnees in need of assistance in East Hararge.
On top of the conflict-induced displacement and looting of livestock, during the months of July-August 2021 over 4,628 weather-related livestock deaths were reported from eight (8) woredas of East Hararge, severely affecting the livelihood of over 25,5000 individuals. The conflict coupled with drought severely affected and deteriorated the livelihood of pastoral and agro-pastoral IDPs, returnees and host communities. About 56% of Chinaksen woreda is categorized as lowland or pastoralist whose main source of livelihood is livestock rearing.
In 2021, both the rainy seasons failed to provide sufficient rain to the region,
East Bale zone is among the food insecure area vulnerable to most recurrent man-made and natural hazards. The amount of rain received in the last belg and meher was below average. On October 2021, drought was declared in South Eastern part of Ethiopia (in Somali region and East Bale zone of Oromia region). It caused crops to dry at juvenile stage, animals facing shortage of feed and water, and humans in need of support. In the zone, 277,533 people or 38.6% of the zone population were aid dependent in continuous manner in 2021. There were also 118,390 people registered food emergency beneficiaries. More than 61,437 HHs demand seed support for ‘belg’ and ‘meher’ seasons.
Because of the current drought, feed shortage and disease outbreak in many woredas of the two regions, animals’ loss their body weight and thousands. Furthermore, an increase in pastoralists migrating in search of water and pasture for their livestock. The woredas worst affected by the drought in East Bale zone are Dawe Ketchen, Dawe Serer, Rayitu and Seweyna.
The proposed project aims to provide critical life-saving livelihood assistance in line with the HRP 2021 and 2021 EHF Second Round Standard Allocation Strategy Paper. The project will provide livestock feed and animal health service and agricultural input support to conflict and drought affected most vulnerable people in six woredas of Oromia zone. The project will prioritize IDPs, returnees and host communities who lost their livestock and livelihoods due to conflict.
The project targets a total of 3,280 HHs (2,745 MHH and 535 FHH) or 16,810 (7,709 M and 9,101 F) from the IDPs and non-displaced population in the six woredas of Oromia region.

1. - Projects activities

The intervention targets 6,150 core breading animals of the 2,050 beneficiary households (10,660 individuals) with provision of animal feed to enable them survive the drought situation in all woredas. The feeding will be undertaken in feed distribution centers where the core breading animals will be continuously provided with feed for 90 days. The animals will be provided with hay (3.5 kg/day /head) and concentrate (2.5 kg /day/head) together. The feeding monitors who will be recruited from the community will manage the feed distribution centers in collaboration with the community mobilizers and kebele administration offices. The project will procure and transport 19,372.5 quintals or 138,375 bales of hay and 13,837.5 quintals concentrated feed to the six woredas, namely Dawe Ketchen, Dawe Serer, Rayitu and Seweyna in East Bale zone and Chinaksen and Gursum woredas in East Hararghe zone of Oromia region. A total of 6,150 core breeding animals will be provided with hay feed at a rate of 3.5 kg/head/day combined with concentrated feed at 2.50 kg per day for a total of 90 days. The feed will be procured from recognized feed suppliers /factories following the LWF procurement guideline. The feed will first be transported by trucks to each of the woreda towns and use secondary transport (Isuzu or FSR trucks) to transport to the selected kebeles and stored at safe locations jointly selected with the woreda sector offices. The feed will be distributed to 2,050 households (338 FHH) in the six woredas on assumption that each household owns 3 heads of animals and feed for 90 days. Awareness on feed utilization will be provided to the beneficiary before the distribution. The project will also procure vet-drugs and support the woredas for treatment of livestock against common diseases. Albendazole 2500mg, Albendazole 300mg, Wound spray Oxy tetracycline 20%, 100ml, Oxy tetra cycline10%100ml, Melti- vitamins, 100ml Pinstripes 100ml, bottled Ivermecktine/ accarisides, Vaccine needles HD, reusable Vaccine syringes10/30ml, Treatment needles, IM reusable Treatment syringes10ml, nylon Treatment syringes 20ml, Normal gloves, Arm length gloves, Glass Barrels, for vaccine syringes, Bags (Gown(white tetron cloth), Alcohol(70% conc.), Cotton, Thermometer, Forceps medium size , Hoof cutter stainless steel, Bulldoze small stainless steel, Burdinzo large stainless steel, Trocur and canola stainless steel etc. drugs and equipment will be provided to the woreda livestock offices. Perdiem will be paid to animal health extension agents for support on animal feed utilization and treatment campaigns in the six woredas. The project will purchase different seeds varieties and farming tools and distribute to 1,230 HHs (197 FHH and 1,033 MHH) or 6,150 people in the six woredas. The seeds will be identified by the woreda agriculture offices for planting in the next rainy season. Development Agents in the woredas together with the project facilitators will provide orientation to the farmers on improved farming practices before the planting season.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

LWF has been consulting regional Food Cluster (led by WFP) and Agriculture cluster (led by FAO) to ensure the selection of project location is well informed and avoids duplication. The targeted locations are selected from the priority locations in the Oromia region affected by drought and conflicts. LWF directly implements humanitarian interventions, coordinating closely with government authorities at the kebele, woreda, zonal, regional and federal levels. Following the signing of operational agreements with the concerned government offices in the Oromia region, LWF coordinates its interventions with the Administration, Agriculture, Finance and Women & Children Affair Offices at each level by involving them in implementation, monitoring and performance. LWF is actively participating in different cluster coordination meetings at the national and regional levels. As cluster meetings are formed at different levels and with the scale up of LWF humanitarian response in the Oromia region, LWF will further strengthen its engagement with different coordination platforms at regional, zonal and woreda levels. LWF is a regular participant of the Humanitarian International NGO (HINGO) and Canadian NGO (CANGO) platform at the Addis Ababa level. LWF serves in the management committee of CANGO. These forums are used to exchange information among government and non-governmental actors in order to improve coordination and facilitate complementarity among actors.LWF is committed to Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) and to the principles of accountability to its stakeholders including the affected population. LWF has trained all its staff in the core principles it entails to further strengthen the well-established accountability and feedback mechanism aimed at enhancing the participation of persons of concern and, thus improving the quality of service delivery. There will be engagement with the beneficiaries from the beginning of the project all the way to the end. The planned activities will be shared with the targeted beneficiaries to allow them to share their views and contribute to planning. Before project commencement, LWF will sensitize the targeted beneficiaries on the planned activities, project period, the type and quantity of supplementary feed per beneficiary. Beneficiaries will participate in the project in terms of selection criteria, identification of beneficiaries, their entitlements and responsibilities.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

LWF has a Complaints Handling Policy and a well-established Complaints and Response Mechanism with several different avenues for persons to raise concerns at field, regional, National (Addis Ababa) and/or international (Geneva HQ) levels. The CRM system is based on rights holders being able and willing to actively report their concerns. Their ability to report is enhanced through making sure they have a variety of accessible reporting systems, and by ensuring right holders are aware of their rights and how to raise concern. Their willingness to report cases is enhanced through making these systems safe, dignified and trusted. For example, LWF ensures that reports are followed up and investigated, feedback is provided and actions based on findings are taken based on fair and due process. Accountability will be enhanced through complaints handling that will be channeled through various means, including in person, by telephone (toll-free hotline), by confidential email, by complaints box and through community based Complaints Handling Committee composed of two men and three women – with at least one being able to read and write. The project team will continually engage with the CRM committee to be aware and build their capacity to resolve operational complaints (ex. timeliness or quality concerns) in collaboration with LWF field staff, and to report serious complaints (ex. rape or harassment) directly to LWF’s national office in Addis Ababa, including the Country Director, for processing and investigation. LWF has also gone a long way in adopting the Core Humanitarian Standards and Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Response, Humanitarian AccountabilityLWF will monitor the working context and also the possibility of GBV for example if girls and women are exposed to such situations due to their actions and unaddressed needs. LWF will respond to GBV cases based on the organizational principles and guidelines. The project will ensure women and girls are participating and their voices are heard from the project inception throughout the project implementation, Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), project evaluation and project close out. Women and girls will have a say on important aspects of the project such as during selection of beneficiaries (identifying beneficiaries’ selection criteria), verification, distribution site selection, distribution day/timing selection, and who (from the HH member) should receive the assistance.
