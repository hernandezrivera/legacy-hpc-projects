Project Summary

The proposed interventions would strengthen the knowledge on child rights at community level and will empower the returnees and children in identifying child protection issues and using referral pathways set up in the community for protection concerns. The purpose is to build a system at community level that would sustain on its own and is used for dealing with child protection issues in the community. The ultimate goal is to reduce the incidence of protection issues in return areas.
PADO will make efforts to bring the affected population back to their normal life through the establishment of CPCs (child protection centers) for women, boys and girls and establishment of community-based protection committees in North Waziristan; PADO will further conduct psychosocial activities within protective spaces and will deliver appropriate messages and information specifically related to the protection concerns of girls, boys and women, building the capacity of children, parents, communities on identification, prevention and reporting of child protection issues. Similarly PADO will also be monitoring, preventing, reporting and responding to child rights violation in return areas.

Needs

As per UNHCR (dated 31 October, 2018) 91,003 displaced families (86.09% of total displacement) successfully returned to their area of origin in Tribal District North Waziristan when their area was de-notified by FATA Secretariat and after being cleared from the miscreants. The prolonged displacement of more than 6 years have left devastating effects on mental and physical health of returning families specifically women and children. PADO has been already implementing WASH project in return areas of North Waziristan, during its WASH projects the team witnessed that returnee children face child protection issues; this specific population is not educated on child protection issues. Children has been exposed to violence and are experiencing psychological and social distress. Children in most cases and are not able to protect themselves; returnee children encounter additional challenges to continue their education especially for girls. There is a need to enhance monitoring of protection concerns of women and children as well as older persons and persons with disabilities in the area of return. Child rights violation in return area is a serious health, development, and human rights problem affecting the majority of returnee children. Based on all the studies and observations, children are not only being affected by the conflict, but there are increased risks to children of child rights violation. The strengthening of community protection systems are immediately required. There is a need to devise a system through the formation of child protection committees with a strong mechanism for referring child protection cases.

Activities

Activity 1.1 Establish community based child protection centres and outreach services for 2,500 women, 3,000 girls and 3,000 boys. Activity 1.2 Establish community based child protection committees (50% female) Activity 1.3 Deliver appropriate messages and information specifically related to the protection concerns of girls, boys, women and men. Activity 1.4 Conduct psychosocial activities within community based child protection centres with children (50% girls) and women. Activity 1.5 Strengthen capacities of staff (40% female) operating community based child protection centres to ensure application of guiding principles and minimum standards, particularly related to CP and GBV Activity 1.6 Establish and strengthen referral pathways through monitoring, capacity building with refresher orientations and support with resources
