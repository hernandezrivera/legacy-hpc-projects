Project Summary

This project supports children in Idleb and Aleppo from the IDPs which are the most vulnerable groups to provide equitable access to quality learning opportunities for girls and boys in non-formal settings. The implementation approach will focus on supporting WASH activities through the establishment and maintenance of the targeted temporary learning spaces (TLSs), providing Hygiene kits for children, implementing a school improvement plan in the TLSs that includes WASH activities, and Water supply for the TLSs. Delivering quality education in these non-formal learning spaces in the targeted sites based on the result of assessing children’s educational needs conducted by Violet's team in those locations. The project design complies with INEE minimum standards and safe-programming principles for children to ensure that children have access to inclusive and equitable quality education that provides opportunities for lifelong learning. The education component is complemented by child protection intervention in child-friendly spaces (CFS), to provide psychosocial support (PSS) group-based activities for girls, boys, and their parents, including the distribution of individual PSS kits to girls and boys, providing individually specialized child protection services, including referrals, case management, and individual in-kind assistant for girls and boys. Empowering community participation and resilience by supporting child protection committees and networks and building the front-line staff and committees' members in child protection. The goal is to strengthen children’s self-confidence and positive coping mechanisms while increasing parents` positive parenting skills.
The project's targeted locations (Aleppo: Jebel Saman, Idelb: Harim, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Idleb) with pre-identified needs (high numbers of IDPs or out-of-school children according to HNO and HRP 2022-2023). 100% of benefices under education severity 4.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if the education service is provided to a specific group, e.g. IDP Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Before implementation, Violet conducts assessments and selects most vulnerable communities based on severity; selection criteria (eligibility/vulnerability) transparently communicated through the AAP team. AAP team has access to service mapping to communicate informed with communities about alternative services. 2) Children with special needs might be excluded from education and PSS. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Ensure facilities and services are suitable for special needs, e.g. mobility or learning materials, conduct community events that help understanding the importance of participating in education/PSS, consultation with local stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training teachers on conflict sensitive teaching. 3) Child labor, early marriage, recruitment into armed groups. Likelihood: High; Impact: High Conduct awareness to parents (PTA) on importance of education, complementary with other assistance (cash, food baskets, NFI) to offer support to the families as positive coping mechanism. 4) Overcrowded physical infrastructure as a risk to children’s safety including unexploded ordinances (UXOs) Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium Conduct necessary rehabilitation (incl. gender sensitive WASH facilities and mobility) for a safe learning environment and mine action awareness and safety training.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Potential risks: 1) Misconduct of education staff against child safeguarding principles Mitigation: initial and refresher training on CSG responsibilities, behavior training for teachers, assign focal point for CSG, child-friendly AAP sessions including CSG information and how to raise complaints, zero tolerance on misconducts and rapid action after investigation. 2) SEA misconduct from education staff to parents or children Mitigation: provide information sessions to parents (sex-segregated if preferred) on expected behavior from teachers and humanitarian workers, explanation of complaints mechanism, availability of AAP staff face-to-face for sensitive complaints. 3) Request of SEA for selecting BNFs to the services Mitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasise on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative.
