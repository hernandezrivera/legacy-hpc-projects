Project Summary

Education services remain highly prioritized and are a critical bridge between relief and early recovery. Supporting education services under a protracted crisis necessitates simultaneously addressing immediate needs and fostering coherent service delivery especially for the most vulnerable people. Under SO1: Availably & Access according to "Sub-district Severity, Aleppo –Jabal Saman severity score/4/ and Dayr Hafir& Rasm Harma Al –Imam and Eastren Kwairse severity score/3/.
ONG Rescate as part of its responsibility towards the community, submits this proposal to rehabilitate and provide supplies, remedial classes in cooperation with ST, in addition to teachers capacity building: plan in 2022 to rehabilitate and equip
- One Girls vocational school in Aleppo,
- This intervention is a response to NLG the data reveals that 18% are out of school and half of the children who are not in school never enrolled. children face potential risks when traveling to school which may limit access to education services. Over a 1/4 households with school aged children indicated that their children are not in school because there is no school to send their child to.
Hence two elementary schools in Rural Aleppo, (Yousef al Azmah and Um Al Mara) to facilitate attendance for dropout students due to unsafe road especially for children, and overcrowded classrooms.
Rescate will support 11 schools in (Dayr Hafir-Rasm Harma Al-Imam-Eastren Kwairse). The project focus is students who are attending in the near over-crowded schools 80-90 students/classroom, without functional wash facilities. So, students’ dropout from schooling.
Rescate intervention is an area based approach as Rescate just opened a public Market in Dayr Hafir and the schools rehabilitation is in response to student's families request, the schools are in the surrounding of this facility.
On another urgent need based on this situation analysis Youth and adolescents (10 to 24 years old), representing over 30 per cent of the entire population, struggled to access learning opportunities, vocations or to have meaningful engagement in their communities. Many adolescents had no access to schooling during the height of the crisis and are now unable to return to school due to over age and are in desperate need of alternative pathways. Vocations Institutions are the solution for them since they abandoned school to work, therefore; vocation institutes will support their livelihood.
• In some situations, girls and boys, including recruits and those with disabilities, were also at risk of
additional and multiple forms of violence due to their economic vulnerability. Life Skills and Citizenship Education (LSCE) will be provided to these young people (52% girls) to enhance critical thinking, communication, collaboration and creativity skills to propose solutions to local social challenges.
Rescate plan to rehabilitate and include renewable energy at two community centers in cooperation with the Syria Trust for Development (ST) subject to fund availability to conduct vocational trainings at the centers for students who are out of schooling since years and they are between 14-24 years. Curricula will include remedial classes in addition to vocational trainings mainly on 8 vocations which are needed for rehabilitation of houses, souks, and schools. including furniture production and historical sites restoration handicrafts. Vocational trainings will cover Stone work, Masonry, Carpentry, Smith work, WASH, Electrical works, Glass Paint and Wood works. Tool start up kits will be distributed.
These will be vocational institutes in community centers to fill the gap for youth boys and girls to be self reliant, productive professional persons who will cover the gap of handicrafts workers. This is for 2023 plans in coordination with the education sector, MoE and ST. entrepreneurship plans will be arranged in coordination with the chambers of industry and trade, and with private sector.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

In terms of inadequately safe learning facilities, the assumption after repair works is that the targeted population will accept the idea to return their children to school and prevent early marriage for female. There is a mid-risk that this will not be the case, and the impact of this would be grave. The mitigation measures will be taken across the project identification were: 1. Learning facilities will be improved to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected. This include stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, solar power systems, water, etc. School grounds will have fencing with all necessary work in schools rehabilitation standards. 2. Sufficient water and sanitation facilities will be provided and maintained. WASH facilities will be sex and age segregated and accessible to PWDs. 3. Learning facilities and their grounds will have good lighting, 4. Rescate will have remedial class for students in schools to encourage them for learning and Back to school next year 2023 after rehabilitation and provision of equipment and supplies to guarantee intact services to out of schools children.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of PSEA: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: All Rescate staff, partners, and contractors receive awareness session includes the definition of what is SEA and channels of reporting/whistleblowing are. Hence, they are required to sign the Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of (PSEA). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, Rescate is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its project to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on Rescate work, as well as the contractors’ work, and to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA issues, will be addressed first by the Project coordinator, who will share the concern with the Head of Mission based in Damascus, in order to establish a solution or way to proceed to enhance the protection of the PSEA survivor. When needed, the HoM will scale the issue to Rescate HQ in Spain, where the Director, in consultation with the gender specialists’ team, will look for potential solutions and/or an appropriate way to proceed.Rescate have PSEA and Code of Conduct in place, it is under annual review at HQ, and will be adopted when finalized in all projects, Staff, contractors and partners sign the PSEA and Code of Conduct after an awareness session at the launch of any project.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption is that Covid-19 will not disrupt the project. It is difficult to assess beforehand the probability for this to happen and its consequences. Rescate will take prevention measures since the beginning to avoid contagion: PPE will be distributed for the activities, staff, workers and beneficiaries will be informed about recommendations. In terms of support, the assumption is that the MOE, the local authorities maintain a strong collaboration with ONG Rescate. There is a low risk this would not be the case (as Rescate received full support from Stakeholders. • The devaluation of the Syrian pound due to the inflation may cause differences between the prices, in this context, Rescate will reflect the difference in exchange rate on the target of the project. • Regarding the security issues that may occur and access during the implementation of the M&E phases, RESCATE staff will commit to the safe access procedures that will be announced by UNDSS and other concerned partners in this context.
