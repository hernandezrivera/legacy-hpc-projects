Project Summary

ZIMVAC report projects 7.7 million people to be food insecure out of which 2.2 million in urban areas.
The recent Crop and Livestock Report stresses the 2019/2020 season was marked by a delayed onset
and a false start throughout the country. Average dam capacity for the big dams stands at 47.5% (lowest
being 23% capacity). This resulted in reduced area planted and subsequent expected yield. El-Nino induced
drought and the destruction caused by Cyclone Idai induced drought resulted in lost casual labour
opportunities for the rural population due to the late start of the 2019/20 rainfall season due to which
Zimbabwe's economy contracted in 2019 by an estimated 6.5%, weighed down by exogenous shocks.
The recent government imposed lockdown to contain the spread of COVID-19 created obstacles for
farmers, farm labourers, farm service providers, extension officers, input suppliers, processors and other
actors in the food system to perform their tasks. A rapid assessment conducted for the markets revealed that most businesses were not fully operational because of COVID-19 measures put in place. Petit traders, especially women groups, who have been affected by the prolonged lockdown require livelihood stimulus package to boost their small businesses. Cash shortages continue to have negative impact on livelihoods in both rural and urban areas. The use of the 3-tier pricing system (using local currency, USD and other foreign currencies of neighbouring countries) contributed to distorted prices of goods and services. Poverty continues to be one of the major underlying causes of vulnerability to food and nutrition insecurity as well as precarious livelihoods in Zimbabwe. According to the ZIMSTAT Poverty, Income, Consumption and Expenditure Survey 2017 Report, 70.5% of the population were poor whilst 29.3% were deemed extremely poor. Prices of basic food commodities increased due to prolonged lockdown. Some food commodities like mealie meal are being repackaged from ordinary 50kg into smaller units as sign of reduced buying power.
The action will support 25.000 people in Matebeleland South, Manicaland and Masvingo Provinces with food assistance, including through cash/voucher and GIK distributions. It will aim for the beneficiaries to meet basic food needs, meant by the action assistance combined with beneficiaries' own resources, to meet the standard of 2100 Kcals per person per day for the period of the action. Oxfam will use a community-based, participatory approach, taking into consideration factors such as the following: vulnerability status of the head of HH (women, disabled, elderly, etc.), families including pregnant and lactating women, asset ownership (reflecting on level of poverty/wealth status, etc.), HH size and dependency ratio. The selection criteria will be fine-tuned with communities at the onset of the program and the post-distribution monitoring will be an integral part of the distribution process. The action will ensure that the needs of women and girls that are exposed to COVID-19 related domestic gender based violence will be met by provision of informational campaign on relevant referral pathways and services available at distribution points.

1 - Activities

1. COVID-19 related provision of monthly food baskets through either in-kind or voucher transfers to acutely food insecure populations in rural and peri urban areas. Modality to be informed by market assessments. The transfer is $10 dollars per person per month which has been informed by the food security cluster and meets 80% of the minimum expenditure basket. Altogether, 25.000 individuals will be reached through the vouchers of $10 distributed over the period of 6 months. Beneficiaries receiving multipurpose cash transfers via electronic vouchers will redeem them at shops and markets that will be approved by Oxfam. Oxfam will support these stores to roll-out COVID-reduction measures. For those beneficiaries without mobile phones that will not be able to take the opportunity of the mobile electronic vouchers, Oxfam will provide analogous smart-card vouchers. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted to evaluate the process. This amount is in line with the Survival Minimum expenditure basket that has been defined in coordination with other humanitarian actors who are members of the Cash and Voucher Working Group including the World Food Program (WFP). The proposed entitlement will cover an estimated 62% of a household’s Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB), considering an average family size of 5 persons. Oxfam will ensure that the distribution process is orderly and timely. To ensure that money is used essentially for basic food items, nutrition awareness messages will be delivered to project beneficiaries. When identifying vulnerable HHs, Oxfam will use a community-based, participatory approach, taking into consideration factors such as the following: vulnerability status of the head of HH(women, disabled, elderly, etc.), families including pregnant and lactating women, asset ownership (reflecting on level of poverty/wealth status, etc.), HH size and dependency ratio, etc. The selection criteria will be fine-tuned with communities at the onset of the program. Community mobilisation and beneficiary registration will be conducted in the districts. Each beneficiary will be registered, and a serial number generated for each beneficiary that is computer generated. Beneficiaries will receive their vouchers through direct distribution and sign off to acknowledge receipt of the vouchers. Due diligence for selected retailers will be done and communities will be educated on the voucher process and how to redeem, guardagainst theft and as complains mechanisms in the event of any challenges. 2. Provide COVID-19 related gender and protection awareness raising sessions to the targeted groups.Beneficiaries will be targeted by awareness raising on GBV and referral pathways during COVID-19. Oxfam will prioritize vulnerable women, female headed households, disadvantaged minority groups, girls, boys to have equal and safe access to essential services including food, water and protection. Oxfam will deliberately target the disabled people to benefit throughout the project and will promote their meaningful participation in project implementation. The indicator will measure the number of people reached through gender-based violence education and awareness. The project will raise awareness on gender-based violence through IEC material, SMSs blasts ,jingles and radio talk shows. Gender champions will be strengthened to facilitate door to door GBV prevention messaging in the target areas. Educative materials on gender and food security, gender and COVID-19, gender-based violence in COVID-19, women’s access to resources as well as other relevant themes will be developed and placed in strategic points in the communities including at food distribution points.
