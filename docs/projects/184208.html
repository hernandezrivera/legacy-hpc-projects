Project Summary

World Vision Somalia will implement education programs in the four regions of Somalia like Southwest state Jubaland, Puntland and Somaliland through the various approaches to ensure Education retention of crisis-affected schoolchildren, increased access to education for vulnerable displaced children and children in host communities including, children living with disabilities and minority clans mainly in the rural areas of project location. The project is premised on both secondary desk review and an assessment conducted by world Vision Somalia that indicates that Out of 6 million school aged children in Somali only an estimated 1.8 million (45% girls are enrolled in schools. While upholding the right to education promotes normalcy, hope for the future for children, their families and communities, majority of children in Somalia face a myriad of challenges and rarely able to benefit fully from education. More often than not, classrooms are overcrowded, water and sanitation facilities are inadequate, trained teachers and schoolbooks are hard to come by. Somalia has one of the lowest primary school enrollment rates in the world. Just 30% of all school-age children have access to learning opportunities, with over 3 million children remaining out of school. Those in South and Central Somalia are affected the worst. Among rural and IDP children the situation is even worse, with only 17% enrolled in primary schools Puntland ESSP implementation guide indicates numerous factors contribute to high dropout and low enrolment rates in education. Lack of schools, teachers, low quality of education, and limited outreach to rural areas. In addition, poverty forces many children to drop out of education, where the parents cannot afford to pay the fees for schools, or not enroll at all. Girls in particular are vulnerable as their situation is exacerbated by socio-cultural factors and cultural constraints underpinned by a general under-valuing of education for girls, compounded by economic factors . Somalia’s pastoralist communities which account for nearly 60% of Somalia’s population face further impediments to education as communities move with their children and livestock in search of water and pasture, making education in normal, static schools impractical and often impossible. Low availability of sanitation facilities for girls, lack of female teachers, safety concerns and social norms that favor the education of boys inhibit parents from enrolling their daughters in school. Vulnerable children boys and girls living with disabilities, those from IDPs face more challenges and adolescent girls are seldom able to complete secondary education. Ongoing conflict and natural disasters continue to displace children and families, making it difficult for them to continue schooling Children with disabilities have very limited access to any educational opportunities and face widespread discrimination in the education sector both from teachers and from other student’s. Compounded by Limited resources, limited teacher skills and capacity, insufficient funding, and environmental and attitudinal barriers make inclusive and special needs education a challenge. A summary of the recent education assessment by WVS agrees with the above desk review which identifies long distances to schools; large class sizes which impact a teacher’s ability to effectively manage a class and student’s ability to learn; lack of suitable and adequate WASH facilities and menstrual hygiene management materials on site; and teachers not being paid on time or being grossly underpaid as pressing issues in education. Security issues also prevail along the way to school, which can result in rape, recruitment into armed or rebel forces, and/or abduction. Underlying poverty and societal norms in Somalia often push parents to force their daughters into Child Early Forced Marriage, which requires expensive bride prices.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA) in education in emergencies will increase enrollment and attendance for the most vulnerable children whose families are unable to meet immediate house hold needs including education needs due to varied reasons. In addition, impact and cost-effectiveness of cash programming is better than actual item vouchers and NFI/food transfers. This is because, the provision of and support to the communities with cash addresses the immediate child’s scholastic needs and as well as food and nutrition and livelihood insecurity at household level. This is so because assistance in the form of cash generates the largest welfare gains because it allowsbeneficiaries the freedom to determine how they want to use the cash to meet different household needs, for example some families will be able to buy food as well as restock their livestock and also join VSLAs to be able to generate more income for themselves. While in the case of fooddistributions - getting a lot of kilograms of maize floor that may go to waste dueto expiration. Mobile cash transfer is also identified as a better modality as itsignificantly improves the quantity and quality of food consumed by thebeneficiaries since they get food that leads to significantly larger increase incalories consumed because of choice. This may also result in increased dietarydiversity thereby improving food security outcomes.There is freedom enjoyed when spending earnings from unrestricted and unconditional cash transfers as beneficiaries have autonomy by choosing to spend on things that best fits theirneeds. By allowing the beneficiaries to have purchasing power this modalityprovides the communities with greater choice and dignity through the ability toprioritized family and individual needs while at the same time stimulating localmarkets and improving the market cash flow. It’s argued that there is lessstigma attached to cash transfers when compared with in-kind or vouchers orfood stamps. Cash transfers are perceived to be less costly to administer thanin-kind transfers associated with a large dead weight loss due to substantiallylower benefits relative to the cost of providing it. Inline with centrality of protection and in alignment with Do No Harm principles this project will apply a beneficiary centered approach for example Cash and voucher assistance directly to beneficiaries via mobile will protect and prevent beneficiaries from contracting COVID-19 because there is no physical exchange of cash and mobile phones will allow them to provide feedback that would be used for program accountability and improvement in service delivery. Vulnerable beneficiaries expectant mothers, people living with disability will prioritized through consultation on which means of Cash and voucher they prefer. To ensure meaningful access to CVA Gender and protection mainstreaming measures through setting-up community-based protection mechanisms to assist persons with specific needs to access and spend CVA will be applied. In addition during the design and feasibility studies, the community will be included and facilitated to conduct safety audits to identify self protection mechanisms. For purposes of risk management associated with distribution of cash, WV will identify a reliable service provider to deliver humanitarian cash assistance while ensuring accountability to beneficiaries.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The nature of the context is such that there is likely hood of certain risks occurring. However, the project shall safeguard the rights of vulnerable groups especially women and children by giving them first preference in response activities. In addition, the project will ensure that nobody is left out, harmed, neglected, abused, discriminated or exploited. Further to this, WVS has in place a safeguarding policy framework on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) with a zero-tolerance approach towards abuse and exploitation and ensures that staff, service providers as well as Protection and Education Committees get oriented on the policy to ensure they are aware and informed. More specifically as a stringent measure on SEA violations World Vision employees found to violet the policy go through disciplinary action as per the policy. WVS will actively involve and allow the community to participate in program implementation and maintain their sense of ownership and responsibility. WVS shall adopt a Do No Harm (DNH) approach whilst striving to ensure that the proposed interventions do not generate disagreement or conflict and that staff adhere to the humanitarian principles. WVS will ensure equality/meaningful access to the project interventions while ensuring beneficiaries access to impartial assistance and services proportionate to need and without any barriers or discrimination whatsoever. WVS shall provide equal chance, inclusiveness, and participation of minority and majority community members as well as disabled groups in the district/s to be able to be transparent enough in terms of employment, capacity building, empowerment and resource sharing hence strive to foster comprehensive community engagement in the target locations. Of concern are those from IDPs and minority clans and other marginalized tribes at risk of being neglected, boys and girls, men and women living with mental illness and physical disability. Community-based workers selection and training will also consider gender sensitivity including both men and women. All this will ensure gender issues and equity are addressed. WVS will put accountability mechanisms in place to allow all groups with suitable channels to voice their concerns. Their feedback will influence the program so that it can continuously adapts to cater for the well-being. WV enjoys strong and positive working relationships in the target geographies. To ensure that there is no interference from external stakeholders who may bring about partiality in selection of beneficiaries, WV will work to ensure regular community consultation in all the targeted regions, particularly in the South Central, where insecurity can interrupt daily project site access and create confusion in target communities. To ensure that all voices are being heard in the course of humanitarian assistance, a deliberate efforts will be made in order to get feedback from the elderly, women and the illiterate through face to face dialogue sessions on monthly basis so that their pressing issues including those of gender are responded to in time.
