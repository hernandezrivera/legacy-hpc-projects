1 - Needs

According to UNHCR, as of 6 November 2019, the total number of new CAR refugees in Um Dafoug Locality in South Darfur, has surpassed the 10,000 mark. The majorities of which are women and children and more people are expected to arrive as conflict in CAR continues. The 2020 Refugee Response Plan state that children of CAR refugees in South Darfur, face specific challenges, including access to basic services and assistance due to limited capacities of partners and language barriers. Among new arrivals, language barriers and cultural differences are key drivers of service access challenges in public facilities. For CAR refugees in Nyala, Dimsu, Um Dafug, and El Radom face lack of livelihood opportunities together with dropout of school, child labour, early marriage and other protection issues for families aggravate the vulnerability situation of the families and their children. The HNO 2020 also highlighted that CAR refugees face high malnutrition across settlements due to cumulative effects of food insecurity and gaps in community care and WASH. New arrivals arrive in Sudan in poor health status, with low immunization coverage. Refugees access primary health services through public health facilities. Language barriers, fee coverage remain key challenges with gab in WASH facilities, shelter and NFIs and livelihood opportunities. East Darfur State is home to registered and unregistered refugees. According to UNHCR, the number of South Sudanese Refugees is 844,262 (which is the sum of UNHCR/COR registered population and IPP registered & unregistered figures). Additional sources estimate a total of 1.3 million South Sudanese refugees in Sudan; with total arrivals in 2019 estimated at 6,253, and 3,142 of them arrived only in Feb 2019 however, note that data requires verification. The CAR refugees are estimated at 8,194 (Sudan: Population Dashboard - Refugees and Asylum-seekers, Feb 2019). The majority of the CAR refugees are located at South Darfur and few are at Central Darfur. South Darfur only has 34,655 refugees and asylum-seekers, 26,610 of them are south Sudanese part of them are UNHCR/COR registered, and part of them IPP Registered & unregistered. Apart from basic needs such as health and food, refugee children are in need of protection in their families, communities and when interacting with humanitarian services. Children make up over 56% of the refugee population. As refugees struggle to meet their basic needs in an increasingly difficult economic environment, children are at heightened risk of harassment, exploitation, neglect and abuse. Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) are particularly vulnerable in this regard, as they lack the support structure and protection of their parents. Additionally, refugee youth suffer from lack of education opportunities or vocational skills training, making it difficult for them to find a livelihood when they become adults. In addition there is a limited amount of recreational or social activities targeting adolescents and youth, having a serious negative their development and psycho social well being. These refugees are coming States with already high health needs. Men and women play different roles in the assessment of the health and nutrition, Education needs, water use in a community, etc, it is important to consult both men and women, seeing that they have different roles in the society. For example Men might have a better knowledge of the use of water for livestock and women often have better knowledge of the amount of water used on a household level. The combined information will give more accurate information on the total need for water for a community. Equally, assessing the need for wood women often have better information on cooking practices, whilst men might have better knowledge of wood used for construction.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project is going to address the environmental issues by considering the local environmental context of the targeted area, and a continuous assessment for potential environmental impacts will be carried throughout the project life. The project is also going to do mitigation measures by compensating for negative impacts (if any), and enhance environmental benefits in the project by practicing some measurements, the followings are part of it: • Replace the use of fired bricks with Soil-Stabilized-Blocks (SSB) or Cement-Stabilized-Blocks (CSB). • Locally available material is recommended provided that it is not impacting negatively on the environment. • Include environmental awareness sessions in projects, and support solid waste management schemes • Reduce packaging material and discourage use of plastic bags to reduce the amount of waste generated. • Promote alternatives such as Fuel-Efficient Stoves to reduce use of wood, solar panels for lighting and pumps. Include fuel efficient cooking techniques (e.g. pre-soaking beans, sheltering cooking fires, etc.) in training. • Raise awareness on environmental protection and conservation • Reduce wood consumption by supplying iron pipes and using environmentally friendly material for constructions and rehabilitation; and promoting alternatives such as Fuel Efficient Stoves, and fuel efficient cooking techniques. Consider also alternative energy sources such as solar panels for lighting and pumps.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will both be mainstreamed into sectoral activities and also implemented as a sector program.As mainstreaming issue, the project will ensure that each activity will not expose most vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community consultation will be conducted throughout all the project cycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be ensured protection to prevent exposure from further harm. To possible extent, community discussions will be held separately between women and men to create safe space for everybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones for adults.Project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. Selection of villages will be done in consultation with line ministries, HAC and other relevant agencies such as UNFPA, department of welfare in the Ministry of social welfare to ensure needs of people with special needs are taken care of. The safety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and WV staffs is not negotiable and project will ensure implementation activities will not put beneficiaries, WV staffs or project stakeholders on security risks. Activities for children will be done during the day with the present of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While project will prioritize female and child-headed families to participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion. Discussion will be held to inform communities on beneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion will involve different groups within the communities including women and children.To adhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staff will use their current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staffs will include Code of Conduct and safeguarding principle to prevent exploitation of the beneficiaries.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to beneficiaries will be two-fold; to direct beneficiary groups such as refugee groups and accountability to host community leadership. Accountability to direct beneficiaries will similarly be based on MoU with group members and regular joint review of progress. Accountability to host community leadership will be through MoUs that WVI represented by ‘Village Focal Points’ - will sign with community representatives. The MoUs will spell out the roles of the organization as well as the role and contribution of the communities. In addition to being accountability tools; MoUs enable WVI to communicate decisions such as criteria for selection of beneficiaries and project locations. MoU are reference points for issues that arise during implementation. MoUs have enabled WVI hold communities accountable for commitments they make at the onset of projects. Participation: This programme will be designed in consultation with refugee and host communities leadership and government entities. In addition to regular consultation as part of ongoing interventions, refugee and host communities and government leaders were informed of this project and involved in selecting beneficiaries and prioritizing interventions. At the project onset, kickoff meetings at the village level will be facilitated project staff to further publicize the programme and communicate opportunities for participation. Feedback mechanisms: The program will institute feedback mechanisms such as ‘suggestion boxes’; quarterly feedback forums and ‘hotlines’ where feasible. The ‘hotline’ approach has been piloted successfully in Darfur. The project staff will be tasked with mobilizing communities – particularly women – to participate and will be responsible for investigating factors that hamper participation. These factors will have come through normal feedback mechanisms and / or through or discovered through interactions. The project staff will work towards ensuring that beneficiaries are aware of their rights and entitlements, ensuring beneficiaries have timely access to relevant and clear information and they are satisfied with the opportunities they have to influence the response. The program staff will consult with beneficiary communities’ on the design, implementation and monitoring of complaints-handling processes. Program staff Welcome and accept complaints, and communicate how the mechanism can be accessed and the scope of issues it can address. The complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages.
