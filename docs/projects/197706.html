Project Summary

Based on the ERL Cluster, HNAP, HNO, and IMMAP reports, and BINAA Need Assessment (all in 2022), the overall development challenge of the target area is represented in increased poverty, absence of community services, the tension between hosts and IDPs, less access to markets, damaged roads, minimal employment opportunities, and widespread negative coping mechanisms.
Recurrent floods in NW Syria in winter severely impact physical access to IDP sites by rendering roads completely or partially impassable. Heavy rains trigger flooding, primarily affecting self-settled IDP sites, which do not have the appropriate infrastructure to withstand this elemental onslaught and negatively impact the physical access for IDPs as well as the safety and dignity of residents. This further limits access to markets, livelihood opportunities, health, and other critical services.
The project will provide critical improvements to road and associated flood drainage infrastructure through:
1. Rehabilitating critical flood-affected roads and drainage systems that connect IDP sites to host communities in Harem.
2. Creating short-term work opportunities to involve local community members to directly contribute to and benefit from preventative rehabilitation works.
3. Training the local communities on Asset Management aspects to improve the sustainability of infrastructure and increase social cohesion.
The interlinked ERL activities have been developed in coordination with the ERL, SNFI, CCCM, and Logistics Clusters, where:
*First, the project will rehabilitate 40 km of roads and 5 km of drainage systems in immediate proximity to 35 IDP sites flood-affected in Idleb.
*Second, the project will engage 400 people in CFW activities, including rehabilitating closed canals of road drainage systems, road paving for road shoulder support, road painting and marking, and installing road signboards.
*Third, the project will train 250 key stakeholders on asset management to ensure that the infrastructure will be maintained beyond the project.
The project aims to provide road infrastructure restoration for flood-affected IDP communities, simultaneously creating short-term livelihood opportunities.
The project will directly benefit 2,000 individuals (400 HHs) under the CFW, and specifically, ERL activities will directly benefit 200,000 individuals in Harem (40,000 HHs) from the rehabilitated community infrastructures and ensured access to 35 IDP sites.
Transfer Modalities going to be used in the project are identified based on Need Assessment and Market Assessment, in addition to secondary data collected from HRP, HNAP, and Clusters Reports.
Designing the CFW component has been done following the Do No Harm approach, where consulting community/local stakeholders at the area level, especially for the daily wage rate, the number of working hours per day, and project duration, in addition, to ensuring the CFW daily wage rates do not distort local labor market and draw workers away from local employment opportunities, not harming local producers, and not arbitrarily introducing non-sustainable practices.
Each beneficiary must be paid the wages for four months, 132 USD for each month, totaling 528 USD, divided into four payments, “one a month” to help the beneficiaries to secure their households’ needs for living.
Payment is according to his/her timesheet, paid money in his/her village by the contracted Hawala agency to ensure protection-mainstreaming and safety, where each worker is provided with the Payment Slip showing the # of working days and due amount.
BINAA has a proven track record in the rehabilitation of roads and drainage systems, as well as in the creation of livelihood opportunities. This will strengthen overall risk management and ensure timely implementation of the project along with the high quality of activities and deliverables. The added value of BINAA is anchored around their extensive experience in ERL Programming in Idleb go.

3 - Needs

Over 50,500 households were affected by floods in Idlib, hosting 72 percent of flood-affected households as per SNFI Cluster assessment. Moreover, Idlib is the primary district for flood-affected campsites.To evaluate the feasibility of the intervention in the proposed project areas, BINAA conducted a need assessment and covered 120 IDP sites to assess physical access issues and livelihoods, then zoomed in on 35 prioritized sites where technical assessments were conducted.The assessment reported that IDPs face limited access to the nearest markets due to poor road conditions to the nearest communities, which are exacerbated by heavy rains and flooding. KIs reported that 40 percent of assessed roads are graveled in bad condition, 32 percent of dirt roads require rehabilitation, 24 percent are asphalted and require heavy rehabilitation, and 19 percent are asphalted and require light rehabilitation. The most required maintenance is asphalting, which is a costly task. Regarding the livelihood condition, more than 85 % of IDPs face poor economic conditions (their monthly income may not reach even 20 USD/360 TL )). In turn, 98% of respondents report livelihood support as a top priority.In addition, the assessment identified the flood risk and traffic usage, including the presence of trucks over 20 tons for the transshipment of humanitarian aid.
