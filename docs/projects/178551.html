Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen permanent protection assistance, child protection, and GBV prevention and response services in IDP sites by establishing facilities inside and/or close to IDP sites.
This project comes in accordance with protection cluster recommendations and the HRP 2021 that aims to target the IPDs and vulnerable people through the protection program, including providing protection services; PSS, case management, Ambassador's program to increase PwD participation in humanitarian action, mobile teams… etc.)
IHR plans to target [11,900] IDPs and alleviate their suffering due to the multiple displacements. In close coordination with protection cluster & sub-clusters, IHR will seek to unite the efforts to reach many vulnerable people. Implementing this intervention is a priority for IHR to respond to IDPs' needs, and according to the HNO 2021 and IHR's need assessment made in March 2021.
The current population in the proposed locations of interventions in Idleb governorate
(2,363,035) of whom (1,164,580 IDPs).
IHR protection team has a good capacity and experience in emergency, especially responding for IDPs through implementing protection projects supported by UNOCHA in both Dana and Afrin. Besides, IHR has access to all the mentioned locations since IHR is implementing projects in different clusters since 2016 in the proposed sites.
The main objective of the project is to enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs through:
• Provision of quality and integrated protection services focusing on community-based approaches, including awareness-raising, psychosocial assistance.
• Individual Protection Assistance targeting people with General protection, CP, GBV concerns
• Support other community initiatives through community centers and outreach mechanisms to address particular protection issues/concerns identified through protection monitoring.
• Provision of PFA support and structures PSS support, including case management.
• Expand the availability and quality of protection" CP and GBV services" in targeted location accordance with strategic priorities of the 2021 GP, CP, and GBV. As well to ensure the protection of the population affected by the crisis is improved through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and advocacy with duty bearers, Increased access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the child protection minimum standards in Humanitarian Action " by providing Specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys. Provide quality specialized GBV services for survivors to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV, e.g., case management, Psychosocial
Support and establish referral pathways.
IHR will provide GBV awareness-raising activities, conduct GBV training, including the GBV Awareness Raising toolkit for male employees. All the previously mentioned will be under the GBV subcluster shared toolkits and GBV SOPs
It is worth mentioning that all protection services designed to be accessible to PWDs and adapted
to a COVID-19 situation

4 - Needs

According to HNO (March 2021): 42% of the assessed communities reported the presence of at least one of the assessed eight protection issues as very commonly occurring and in 53% of the communities, respondents have reported the presence of five or more out of the assessed eight protection issues, and Lack or loss of civil documentation was reported as an issue by 61% of assessed communities. The disproportionate impact of negative coping mechanisms on women in children is evident when interfaced with the fact that early marriage is prevalent in 62% of assessed communities and child labor prevents school attendance in 67% of assessed communities. Protection risks, especially GBV and CP continue their disproportionate impact on women and children. Multiple protection risks continue to threaten the ability of people with disabilities to engage in basic activities of daily living in addition to being identified by humanitarian actors and supported to access vital services. 25% Out of the 13.4 million people in need have disabilities, experiencing one or more limitations in functioning, As for the in-camp population, 51% of females experience disabilities compared to 29% of males. Out of the 13.4 million people in need, approximately ten million (75%) are women and children; including approximately 2.3 million females and males with disabilities who are 12 years and over. Children with and without disabilities comprise 44% or 6,1 million persons of the people in need across Syria.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy. In multi-family tents and multi-household dwellings, lack of doors and partitions for sleeping and changing clothes can increase exposure to sexual harassment and assault. Tensions linked to overcrowding may lead to an escalation of intimate partner violence and other forms of domestic violence. Where situational and risk analyses are not systematically conducted, these risks might not be identified and rectified.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigating measures: a) advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided. We will include women, girls, and other at-risk groups to determine such things as if systems/criteria are in place to determine how shelters are being allocated; if they are built for safety and privacy; and/or if shelter materials and shelter-related NFIs are being distributed in areas that are safe. B) Consultations with women, girls, boys, and men should be organized c) community safety audits.Protection risk: There is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed; 2. Ensure consultation with communities related to the location of WASH services to understand relevant community perspectives; 3. Carry out safety audits for beneficiaries’ access to site locations.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

IHR has PSEA, Code of Conduct, Protection and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms to ensure every effort is made to protect all program participants. IHR s PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising (for staff regarding power dynamics, risk and vulnerabilities etc.), Prevention (awareness regarding the existing controls which are in place and how PSEA is prevented), Reporting (how participants can report and what are the specific steps which need to be taken regarding SEA cases) and Response (how and what IHR will do when cases of SEA are reported). A similar mechanism is available for CP where staff is familiar with approaching and dealing with cases, or suspected cases, of child abuse, exploitation, or violence.
