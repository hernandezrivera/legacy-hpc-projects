Project Summary

For the past 3 years, Cameroon has seen a sociopolitical crisis emerge in the North West and South West Regions of the country known as the Anglophone crisis. The unrest which started in 2016 has progressively escalated into an armed conflict between secessionists and government security forces, resulting in many civilian deaths, destruction of villages and livelihood activities, disruption of basic services and public infrastructure, and subsequent large displacement of population. A DTM conducted by IOM in December 2019 indicated that over 465,000 persons have been displaced by the conflict. This report, together with UN inter-agency and NGO assessments including LUKMEF conducted in 2018 highlighted that among other needs, the following are some of the urgent needs of these internally displaced persons; Food, Shelter, Nutrition, WASH, Health . An assessment conducted by LUKMEF in July 2019 with the first beneficiaries of the WFP GFD in the North West (Mezam division) revealed the following.
- the Division is relatively calm as compared to other divisions of the region, has a number of functional hospitals , markets, schools and other services the IDP population is in need of. this has greatly favored the movement of people in the area, as its characterized with highly populated houses and less income for immediate needs especially food. The worry of most of the participants of this assessment was what happens after the food they received runs out.
The rapid Emergency Food Security and nutrition Assessment (EFSA) and mobile Vulnerability Assessment and Mapping (MVAM) conducted by WFP in 2019 revealed that 1.5 million people in SW and NW (37 percent) are food insecure (900,000 in NW and 600,000 in SW) with 370,000 (258,000 NW and 112,000 in the SW) severely food insecure.
According to the situation report number 12 released by OCHA, October 2019, 8 out of 13 divisions of the North West and South West regions as compared to 6 out of these 13 divisions from previous assessments fall under the crisis phase of food insecurity.
Between April and July 2019, LUKMEF through WFP has been able to support 45000+ beneficiaries with general food distributions. This intervention has been of great support to beneficiaries but is not sustainable as there exist many other vulnerable persons who need to benefit from the assistance , thus the same people cannot always receive all the time.
Before the coming of the General food distribution intervention by WFP in the North West a good number of population especially women and girls were exposed to various forms of violence as a means to an end.
- Women and girls became sex workers to be able to provide fr the food needs of theie families or the households in which they found themselves.
- A constant increase in crime by men and stealing to live looked like the only option to some of them who lost their documentation and had no capital to start over anything.
- Children and the old were exposed to high nutrition needs as they got malnourished on a daily basis.
These people face the risk of being pray to the above situations again if some sustainable strategy is not identified to maintain the flow of their supply of food and other basic needs.
Based on this Backdrop, LUKMEF will be carrying out an intervention in Mezam, boyo and Ngo-ketungia divisions of the North West region to support 20,000 individuals through 3000 household in a period of 12 months with Economic kits (Agriculture and livestock inputs inclined e.g farm seedling, tools, chicks, piglets, fish etc) so as to provide them with a more sustainable source livelihood. which will in turn help meet their food need and other basic needs.

Besoins / Needs

LIVELIHOOD: A total of 315000 people in extreme food insecurity and 8 out of 13 divisions of the North west and South West regions are categorized as food unsecured. this is moving the vulnerable population which is highly dominated by women and children towards high nutrition needs. The limited access to food in these areas is directly linked to missed out planting seasons which has led to a rise in prices. IDPs and other vulnerable host communities have limited financial resources and therefore find it very difficult accessing food. WFP has intervened in this region with General food distribution to help relieve the burden. through LUKMEF, between the months of April and June 2019, WFP was able to support 45000+ beneficiaries with food. this was a beautiful intervention which was welcomed by many at the start but most beneficiaries were worried about a sustainable source of food and other of their identified needs. As a strategy towards meeting this need, LUKMEF is going to be providing vulnerable host communities and IDPs with Economic kits (Agriculture Oriented), to improve on their liveihood. This project will be implemented through the following activities. - Identification and training of community mobilisers on humanitarian principles, PSEA, mobilisation, targeting and proper reporting. - Identification of potential beneficiaries of the economic kits- Training of selected beneficiaries on book keeping and basic accounting - Sensitization of the general population on gender and participation. (this is to improve on the participation of both women and men on decision making within the framework of the project)- Distribution of economic kits to beneficiaries.- Monitoring and Evaluation of Activities.
