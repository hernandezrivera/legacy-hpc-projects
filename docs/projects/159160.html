Project Summary

Somalia having been in a state of emergency for a long period of time, with protection concerns being the most raised. WRRS through this response will offer support to the recent conflict affected beneficiaries who have been displaced with most settled in Afmadow, Kismayu and some of the targeted districts including Baidoa with an aim of reducing on the cases that have rendered most of the vulnerable not to get any protection services. The project will seek to strengthen and scale up lifesaving protection services in the targeted location to prevent and respond to protection risks and concerns experienced by women, men and children especially in Internally Displaced Peoples camps but also in the host populations. Survivors ranging from rape, sexual assaults, forced marriages to earn a living for the family among other challenges that are being faced in the targeted location. WRRS through the identified gaps will integrate other interventions with protection interventions to have a whole rounded service delivery. Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities.
The key focus will be sustaining and scaling up prevention and response to separation of children from their families and GBV survivors. The project will conduct regular analysis of the situation of children regarding causes and patterns of separation and respond accordingly through prevention and strengthening of appropriate protection systems and services, build capacity of protection service providers and stakeholders. This project also strengthens service mapping to facilitate appropriate referrals and coordination and linkage in service delivery to ensure quality comprehensive services to unaccompanied and separated children children at risk of their rights being violated with the current rates being reported on gender based violence. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. In Baarwako, WRRS will be targeting the newly displace and settled IDPs that are in urgent need of protection services and psychological support. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data.

Mitigation Measures

WRRS will ensure that services are offered to those in need to prevent further exposure to risks and vulnerability.The beneficiaries will be involved in targeting and WRRS will use local knowledge to identify needy households and/or review selection, beneficiary liaison of both elected and un elected members of the community, beneficiary representative, community communication forums, monitoring of program implementation to ensure transparency, and management and/or monitoring of supply and quality of services. The participation of beneficiaries also allows the project interventions to strengthen community based institutions that organize the poor & build their capabilities to act collectively in their own interest which in turn increases the sustainability of intervention through fostering ownership of program objectives and processes. In the Somali context the beneficiaries will be consulted at local level, through community, clan and religious leaders regarding project location sites and the quality of services to ensure that all the vulnerable people can actively participate in the project. Due to the complexity of Somalia, SPHERE standards may not be fully met, but, women have not been allowed by religious and cultural norms to take a lead in community development activities; until recently when the local leadership structures began to promote women’s participation in community based activities. Women do participate actively but are frequently side-lined when it comes to decision making processes despite playing a vital role in household and community dynamics. The project will seek to encourage the increased participation of women in project meetings so that they can provide input and feedback regarding the quality of services they will be receiving from the interventions. Encouraging increased participation by women and girls in the project structures does not in any way diminish the role to be played by their male counterparts.WRRS has over 10 years of experience in Somalia, with high technical capacity, institutional knowledge especially in South Central Somalia, with strong knowledge of the local context and developed a good relationship with the administration. Since there are greater protection concerns in IDP camps, WRRS will target the districts with the proposed intervention that have previous experience on the same.

Please provide further explanation

Sustainability of the project will be reached with a number of efforts put in place by all the actors starting with the local authorities and other actor who are working in the project area that involve the local authorities to the national level with an aim of ensuring that the human rights are not violated ranging from GBV, CP, early forced marriages as well as reunion of the un accompanied children with their families. Serious protection concerns persist in Somalia, putting IDPs and the most vulnerable host community’ lives at risk, forcing many to exit, exposing them to multiple risks while displaced, and impeding durable solutions. Compounding the conflict and its inherent risks to civilians, is a massive increase in drought related displacement and further exposed civilians to serious protection risks. Women, children and marginalized communities are especially at risk and face specific protection concerns. The protection crisis is also characterized by entrenched but societal divisions, resulting in competition for resources across social groups. severe drought and the ongoing conflict continue to cause displacement. It is estimated that the number of IDPs have surpassing two million. Displacement dynamics have consistently been directed mainly towards urban centers and merging with urbanization dynamics of increasing pressure on services in larger towns and other urban cities. IDP settlements, many reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security provision. Where the basic needs of IDP are not met due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion from accessing services, or from the sheer absence of services due to poor targeting of settlements. Of specific concern is the large number of drought-affected IDP communities from conflict areas, who are often made up of separated families with men and youth remaining behind, increasing the exposure of women and girls to protection risks related to hazardous livelihood activities and reduced protection to GBV. WRRS through the proposed intervention will ensure accessibility of services and specifically for the GBV victims, proper referral mechanisms will be put in place to reduce the risk of further vulnerability for women and Girls. WRRS will also coordinate with other organizations to ensure that services are well coordinated and that the beneficiaries are well informed of the available services.
