Project Summary

The project will sustain water supply, sanitation and hygiene services being provided in response to protracted displacement, loss of dignity and human suffering of population affected by armed conflict in north-east Nigeria and expand services to reach newly displaced persons from hard to reach areas and populations living in areas affected by recurrent incidents of disease outbreaks such as cholera, acute watery diarrhoea, hepatitis E and other emergency disasters. The response will be integrated with protection, health, nutrition and education in emergency response projects supported by UNICEF and other humanitarian agencies. The purpose of the project is to provide immediate life saving WASH services in hard to reach areas, sustain and improve services for populations living in areas of protracted emergencies and support evolving links between emergency programming and early recovery and development in areas of return. The following interventions and targets are planned:
i) 65,000 people (new arrivals or people living in hot spot areas of disease outbreak) access temporary services for safe water supply (15 lppd for 60 days)
ii) 136,000 people newly affected by conflict access safe water
iii) 1,550,000 people affected by conflict and living in IDP camps and host communities access risk-informed hygiene promotion and behaviour change messages
iv) 75 institution (health/nutrition centre, public places, Learning spaces, CFS) provided water supply source or connection, serving population affected by conflict or disease outbreaks
v) 75 Institutional latrines (2-drop hole VIP latrine with hand washing facilities) provided in areas of return and host communities; or in IDP camps
vi) 5,000 households affected by conflict receive cash transfers for WASH commodities and supplies
vii) 400,000 people affected by conflict gain new access to improved sanitation in IDP camps
viii) 480,000 people affected by conflict or disease outbreaks receive initial WASH response kits or replenishment kits.
ix) 200,000 women of child bearing age receive reusable sanitary kits distributed (pack of 6 per person)
x) 77,500 Households with SAM affected children receive a SAM NFI package
vi) 4,000 latrines per months are desludged to sustain use of improved latrines in IDP camps; 16,000 cu m of sludge removed per month, benefitting 500,000 internally displaced persons.

Needs

The WASH sector estimate 800,000 people in hard to reach areas are in need of humanitarian assistance. In 2018, more than 240,000 people arrived from hard-to-reach areas in camps and informal settlements in 43 LGAs across Borno and Adamawa states, further overcrowding existing WASH facilities in these sites. In 73 IDP camps across 11 LGAs in Borno State, the average allocation of water is 14 liters per person per day, below minimum standard of 15 liters, while IPDs are sharing a drop-hole latrine with an average of 72 people, below minimum standard of 50 people per drop-hole latrine. Some of the latrine facilities provided more than two years ago are now dilapidated and need reconstruction or major rehabilitation. Cholera is endemic in Nigeria and in 2018, cholera outbreak across the BAY states has been particularly deadly, affecting at least 18 LGAs. By mid-October, reported cases exceeded 5,000 with case fatality rate exceeding 1 percent. Women and children are disproportionally affected. Borno State recorded 33 per cent of cases with 24 per cent associated deaths; Adamawa State recorded 48 per cent of the cases with 28 per cent associated deaths, and the remaining were recorded in Yobe State. The outbreaks, especially in Adamawa and Yobe states, reveal the lack of structural WASH investments: early recovery and development partners need to scale up their programming to address those structural needs and prevent the spiral of cholera outbreaks. Systematic blanket chlorination in all areas at risks, including urban and rural locations, displacement sites, host communities, and return areas, is required along with the promotion of good hygiene practices, and the replenishment of emergency supplies (water containers, aqua tabs for household, water treatments, and soap). The emergency nutrition situation faced by newly-arrived children from hard-to-reach areas is of critical concern for the WASH sector, with an average GAM prevalence of 15 per cent and above in 2 LGAs in Yobe State, and 11 per cent and above in 9 LGAs in Borno State . This includes at least 28,723 children screened in 9 LGAs in Borno State, between October 2017 and August 2018, with GAM rates ranging from 11.4 to 50.1 per cent. Those proxy indicators reveal a poor nutrition status and an urgent need to improve WASH conditions which are key to cutting malnutrition rates, as exemplified in malnutrition-diarrheal diseases cycle. Lack of access to safe water and other WASH facilities poses specific protection risks including sexual violence and harassment. Only 38 per cent of latrines are segregated by sex (39 per cent in Borno State; 44 per cent in Yobe State) and 31 per cent of latrines do not have lock inside, which make latrines unsafe or potentially inaccessible for women and girls . For example, in the Farm Centre IDP Camp in Borno State, sexual abuse of girls were reported in latrines, that were not sex-disaggregated, did not have locks and were not well-lit.

Activities

Social mobilization, risk-informed hygiene promotion by C4D and RUWASSAs will anchor integrated services, targeting new affected population or sustaining health seeking behaviours, and upholding the dignity and protection of targeted population in protracted crisis areas. Private contractors will provide periodic emptying of latrine (camps and host communities). RUWASSAs will provide technical support for community organization (by supervisors) and in-kind incentives (cleaning tools, protective clothing, other materials and disinfectants) to user community groups such as adolescent or Youth Groups, Women Groups or other Groups that will mobilize users to clean and disinfect their latrines, ensure proper use by their communities and facilitate proper hand washing with soap after use of latrines. C4D will regularly monitor receive user feedback on protection through Focus Group Discussions to be held with women, girls, boys and men in separate groups. The Camp Management including State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA), IOM and Traditional Community Leaders will be engaged to ensure implementation of camp community based actions and to follow up WASH partner agency-related actions. The same structure will be used to provide feedback to the community on any issues raised in the FGDs. Construction and rehabilitation of new facilities to reach new arrivals or expand services will be by private contractors. NFIs will be distributed to new arrivals and children under SAM treatment. Replenishment NFIs will be distributed to household in protracted emergency or disease outbreak areas. To sustain safe water supply services in IDP camps in areas of protracted conflict, this project supports daily chlorination of water supply, routine maintenance of water facilities through Project Cooperation Agreements with INGO partners DRC and TdH; LNGO partners CIDAR; State RUWASSAs. Private sector providers will be contracted for drilling of boreholes and expansion of services either directly through Long Term Agreements or through State RUWASSAs. Evolving capacities of LGA WASH Units will be strengthened to engage them in long term responsibility for water supply services in the LGA capital towns. The LGA WASH Units will also be gradually engaged to participate with SEMA and Camp Management in longer term planning for the management of the operation and maintenance of large water supply facilities that will eventually be used for town water supply systems when the IDPs return. In this way, the investment made in water supplies for the humanitarian response will be factored in LGAs' long term planning for water services in the capital towns. The Camp Management will also benefit from the responsible government agency's support for the management of WASH services in Camps and host communities. In this way, the gradual strengthening of the LGA WASH Units will contribute to the humanitarian - development nexus. New arrivals will be provided initial WASH response kits while long term IDPs living in camps and communities will be provided replenishment WASH kits to build their resilience in safe water management from collection to transportation and storage at household level. Households with SAM affected children will receive targeted assistance with safe water management. a critical nutrition sensitive intervention which the nutrition section has been shown is reducing recovery time for SAM affected children and increasing the cure rates. Similarly, WASH will integrate with health, education and protection in responding to protection issues of segregation and protection of women and girls from gender based violence, as well as sexual exploitation and abuse connected with use of WASH facilities. Targeted focus of giving priority of locating new water and sanitation facilities at or near social service institutions like schools, health facilities, child friendly spaces and markets will contribute to integrated social services.
