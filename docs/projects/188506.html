Project Summary

This project is designed to contribute to helping Syrian returnee families, especially those who are already living in their apartments in bad weather circumstances without any security and privacy.
Rural Damascus governorate has a high number of PiN (2022), more than 615,000 people, and is ranked between severity levels 3-4 (Shelter HNO 2022). Other areas, such as Moaadamieh Harasta and Daraya in high need of rehabilitation of damaged houses.
In Deir Ezzor governorate, more than 340,000 are in shelter need at severity level 3-4 (Shelter HNO 2022), ADRA identiﬁed a high need for rehabilitation of damaged houses in the neighbourhoods of Al Omal (wasat and est) where there is an increased number of returning families because the governorate and INGOs are working to provide main services (electricity, Water, Sanitation….). Most of the buildings are structurally safe.
In Aleppo, as per ADRA’s assessments, the needs in Southern Rural Aleppo (such as Al Hajeb and Khanaser) are highest due to the increased number of returnees and lack of presence of humanitarian partners. Eastern Rural Aleppo is also in growing need of shelter response. Deir Hafer (in Al Bab) and Al Bab lack adequate living space for the returnees from Aleppo city and camps in Turkey and Lebanon.
ADRA scoop of work will be under the following activities:
Activity 1.1.1.2: Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs.
Activity 2.1.1.1: Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished housing.
Activity 2.1.1.4: Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished building common areas.
Activity 1.2.1.3: Distribution/ installation of emergency shelter kits/ materials
ADRA is planning to respond to 16890 individuals including(male, female, boys and girls) from the most vulnerable families (IDPs, Returnees and host communities)through several shelter activities such as the rehabilitation of damaged houses, Shelter kits distribution and Winterization vouchers distribution in the governorates of Deir Ezzor, Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Idleb and Homs. A total of 170 apartments will be targeted under the long-term shelter solutions.

4 - Needs

55% of returnees live in substandard, damaged, or inadequate shelter, with nearly 80% stating inability to meet their basic needs, leading to secondary displacement. 25% of IDPs continue to reside in last resort sites, primarily in the northwest and northeast, which lack crucial infrastructure. Besides, nearly 30% of the host community households live in damaged and/ or inadequate shelters where repair/rehabilitation is needed alongside a multisectoral response to ensure access and availability to essential services. (Sheler HNO 2022)The IDPs situation inevitably creates a severe strain as the hosting period becomes prolonged, leading to overcrowding, additional intra/inter-family tensions, and additional costs, pushing even more IDPs towards collective shelters or other emergency alternatives. The needs assessment for the NFI project was collected through field visits with SARC to HHs in targeted areas by ADRA female and male staff and information from previous interventions in the targeted areas during 2020 and 2021.In Idleb and Homs, Young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults and older adults without appropriate shelter and lack electricity and fuel. The winter in these locations is cold, windy, rainy and snowy. During the coldest months (from Dec to Feb). ADRA met potential beneficiaries individually and in focus groups who expressed the need for blankets, children’s clothes, clothes for men and women, and rugs for all.

5 - Implementation of activities

ADRA Syria will identify the most vulnerable and in-need persons regarding the winterisation voucher project. The initial list of potential households will be obtained from SARC ADRA will do a comprehensive market assessment to identify vendors for vouchers to be redeemed. Vouchers will be distributed through multiple distribution points, with easy access for all targeted groups. ADRA staff and volunteers will be available on the site to give the project's information and receive feedback and complaints. ADRA will be available at distribution points and in vendors stores to monitor and ensure equal access for beneficiaries.ADRA will coordinate with the relevant governorates, MoLAE and the local municipalities to obtain a technical assessment and implementation approvals for the shelter rehab project. ADRA will target the most vulnerable families by conducting social assessment and following the selection criteria. ADRA follow the implementation process starts with selecting the potential areas then conducting the social and technical assessment. The BoQs will be prepared and shared with the relevant stakeholders. Then, ADRA will conduct the tender process and select the most successful bidder based on the submitted samples, experience in similar projects, technical and ﬁnancial offers and timeline. ADRA will regularly monitor the implementation progress. The MEAL team will assess the process of projects and the quality and satisfaction of beneficiaries.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

ADRA ensures mainstream protection and adherence to humanitarian standards of ‘Do No Harm’. Close attention will be paid to the protection risks that may occur during all stages of the project such as:• increase of community tension due to different assistance packages.• unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to a feeling of discrimination. • GBV, exploitation and abuse may rise due to the increased privacy in houses after rehabilitation. mitigating measures:• ADRA will ensure to adhere to the standardised shelter response as per the shelter sector guidelines.• all stakeholders will be informed of the selection criteria. Leaders of the community where ADRA will intervene will be involved, and the phone number where beneficiaries and other partners call will be shared to allow the raising of any complaints. • ADRA will ensure each rehabilitated apartment has lockable doors and windows and can provide protection.• The common areas in buildings will also be targeted to provide lighting.• ADRA will work closely with the protection and other sectors. • ADRA will ensure that male and female staff are available during the life of the project.• The MEAL team will conduct field visits and distribute the complaint cards with a hotline number allowing beneficiaries to raise complaints anonymously. All complaints will be followed up on the sensitive issues will be referred to the ADRA Protection policy, which will then contact appropriate institutions.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1. Inflation in the local market causes the withdrawal of the contractors from the projects during implementation. 2. COVID-19 pandemic and the preventative measures that can result in a delay of implementation of the project. 3. Lack fuel due to the imposed sanctions, causing long lines to purchase fuel, may result in the inability of the ADRA team to conduct field visits 4. Delay in obtaining necessary approvals from the MoLAE, which may delay implementation of the project.5. Delayed fund transfer resulting in delayed implementation. Mitigation measures:1. ADRA will try to shorten the procurement process duration in order to allow them to commit to their prices.2. ADRA has a COVID-19 response policy that ensures the staff's protection during site visits ( Masks, Sanitizers, social distancing). In case preventing measurements were taken, ADRA will consult with donors to explain the current situation and find possible solutions.3. ADRA will ensure to provide several cars to the project to use during the project cycle and may use taxis for field trips.4. ADRA has a good relation with MoLAE and the relevant governorates. ADRA will conduct regular visits to follow up on the approval process.5. ADRA will provide more than one transfer pathway, will closely monitor the situation inside and outside Syria, and keep close coordination with donors.
