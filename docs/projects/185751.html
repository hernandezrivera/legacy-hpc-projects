Project Summary

Lifesaving services are being addressed since June, 2018 to till date and planning to continue for the coming year of 2022 in camp-24, 25 & 26 at sub-block-D,E &F; A & D,E,G,H &I respectively for FDMNs and host communities at word no-8 & 9 in Nhilla Union in Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar. Considering the camp context, field experience and assessing the needs of beneficiaries regarding WASH services, we found that maximum facilities has already been installed or constructed but still have some gap to install or construct Pit Latrine & Bathing Cubical due to internal relocation within the camp at camp area as per sector ratio. Now need to ensure the proper use, operation & maintenance with continuous repair of those facilities as well as quality, regular, sufficient, equitable, and dignified access to WASH facilities for all women, men, girls and boys, inclusion of all age groups and persons with disabilities in the Rohingya camps and targeted host communities and need to address the new challenges or demands. Bearing in mind the above issues we are planning to address the currents need of targeted beneficiaries Just as: supplying sufficient safe water to targeted Rohingya and host Beneficiaries from Advance Reverse Osmosis (The first ever Sea water treatment plant for Rohingya and host Beneficiaries) apart from this we will also have plan to supply sufficient safe water to targeted Rohingya beneficiaries through Borehole water network, sanitation and hygiene activities through regular cleaning & maintenance of every single WASH facilities Just as: Twin-pit Latrine, Bathing Cubical, Double chamber Dustbin for organic and non-organic waste, Drain cleaning, Latrine desludging, maintaining of Fecal Sludge Management (FSM), giving the high preference for Solid waste management through barrel compost units as there is a huge gap of this and we will have plan to distribute compost fertilizer produced by barrel compost units among nearer farmers from host community and as well transfer the non-organic item to nearer recycle plant, aware beneficiaries regarding hygiene knowledge through regular hygiene promotion through individual HHs visit, group session and Mega phone and distribution of ICE materials etc. In addition, our beneficiaries plan to conduct an evaluation to collect feedback. We may conduct some repair and renovation work on existing wash facilities so that people of all age groups, genders and PWDs can use our wash facilities very comfortably and we can include all of them and serve them through this project. Disinfection activities and Key message forwarding have included in our plan considering COVID-19 pandemic. In every stage, we will collect beneficiaries’ feedback before going to implement any activity and we will design our plan as per their demand so that we can ensure inclusion of all age groups, genders and persons with special needs to use WASH facilities. As most of the facilities has already constructed that’s why we won’t scope to re-install those facilities rather we will conduct some repair work and install some components with the existing facilities to make those facility gender and PWD user friendly so that to all age groups, genders, and persons with special needs get access to use those facilities quite easily. All of above mentioned activities are included in our plan to ensure that 100% of targeted beneficiaries have proper water, sanitation and hygiene facilities throughout the project duration.

1 - Identified Needs

Improve water service: As per WASH sector analysis 23% of Refugee and 12% of Host communities in Teknaf area don’t have the access of safe water and water scarcity is very high here in Teknaf region as it’s a saline area and underground water is also contaminated with saline. Access to water in Teknaf still remains a major challenge during the dry season with water trucking and water diversion required again during 2021. During the March fire of camps 8E, 8W and 9, handpumps repairs to melted internal plastic parts proved to provide rapid provision of water and a viable source of drinking when supplemented water with Aquatab distribution. People are depending on the rain water of this area as a consequence during dry season water scarcity becomes intense here. So ensuring safe water for 100% beneficiaries is a challenge and need to fulfill this gap as well as who has access to safe water, need to maintain the quality, quantity and supply of water regularly.Access to Sanitation: 99% of Rohingya refugees have enough hygienic latrine in contrast host communities have 63% and many qualitative challenges remain with 20% of sanitation infrastructures requiring O&M and 10% being built in flooding areas as per ISCG. Regarding waste management, 44% reported not having a bin and 69% throwing household waste into the open. Although the gap in bathing spaces has reduced, there has been a significant increase in private spaces, with 86% of households reporting private bathing space inside or attached to their shelter for but those bathing places sometimes associated with laundry spaces (79%) and dishwashing places (77%), very often those spaces are unhygienic and poorly maintained or used as alternatives for urination and defecation, with 11% not connected to any form of drainage. Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night they risk harassment and violence with 95% preferring private bathing spaces. Pilot programmes have been instigated to install women friendly spaces and WASH blocks but are challenged by space and the lack of direct water supplies to each facility. One of the major concerns regarding sanitation is the quality of the faecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades and improvements. Solid waste management is still a huge gap in our targeted area.Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: Hygiene knowledge and access to soap remains high although behavior change remains an issue that requires further research to assess the impact of knowledge on hygiene habits. Access to MHM remains a barrier for many females through stigma, taboo and restrictive practices but also from a lack of MHM kit individuality due to common practices of one size fits all or variations of inter-sector kit and insufficient coordination. It also requires a wide scale of Hygienic Practice monitoring, staff capacity building and meaningful community engagement. On the other hand, host’s communities have been less supported by hygiene items and awareness raising initiatives, but findings show encouraging data as 92% of the HH reported having access to soap.
