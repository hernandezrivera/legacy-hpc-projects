Project Summary

SSSD will concentrate on setting up the life-saving WASH services in 7 locations in Dara’a, Rural Damascus, and Aleppo. SSSD will target IDPs living in collective shelters, unfinished / damaged building and host accommodations, and expending the coverage to host community members as well.
SSSD will observe the guidelines from the IASC GBV toolkit and WASH Chapter ensuring that the water and infrastructure designed and maintained through this program will reduce the exposure of women and adolescent girls to GBV. Control measures include gender separation of latrines and bathing cubicles (excluding household facilities), installation of lockable doors, visible and accessible water points equipped with lights where possible, and waste disposal facilities situated in locations visible and easily accessible. SSSD will ensure access to WASH facilities to persons with limited mobility and older people through various types of interventions and access planning, including positioning those in need in shelter units situated in the vicinity of services and installing accessibility improvements such as ramps, railings, and wider toilet stances for improved access.
SSSD always strives to reduce the environmental footprint of its interventions. Using the 3R approach (reduce, reuse, recycle), SSSD will sensitize the program beneficiaries in regard to the challenges of waste management under conditions in which access to disposal sites and containers is limited.

Needs

According to 2019 HNO, and based on WASH assessments there are an estimated 15.6 million people who require WASH assistance (people who are at elevated risk of consumption of unsafe water, including people vulnerable due to displacement), 6.2 million of whom are considered in acute need (displaced people in camps, informal settlements and host communities). Due to changes in control of many areas across Syria, the sector has observed a discontinuation of WASH service provision to extremely vulnerable populations in areas including Rural Damascus, southern Syria and Aleppo. During hostilities and in the months following changes in control, access has remained restricted for partners operating from inside Syria. The delay in reestablishing assistance and services to these areas, limited funding and numerous (additional) conditionalities applied by donors have been a significant driver of needs.The WASH Household assessment indicates that at least 10 per cent of garbage is left in public areas, potentially contributing to the increase in leishmaniosis cases observed since the beginning of 2018 in some areas.Particular groups also face specific WASH challenges. IDP and returnee female-headed households have more difficulties to afford a majority of hygiene items in comparison with female-headed households in host communities. . The major barrier to accessing hygiene items is therefore the severely reduced purchasing power of families in Syria, particularly those who are displaced, enhancing the need for Market-Based Wash Programming.As a result of the crisis at least 70 per cent of sewage is untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional, exposing communities to significant health risks such as Acute Bloody Diarrhea, Typhoid or Jaundice.SSSD will observe the guidelines from the IASC GBV toolkit and WASH Chapter ensuring that the water and infrastructure designed and maintained through this program will reduce the exposure of women and adolescent girls to GBV. Control measures include gender separation of latrines and bathing cubicles (excluding household facilities), installation of lockable doors, visible and accessible water points equipped with lights where possible, and waste disposal facilities situated in locations visible and easily accessible. SSSD will ensure access to WASH facilities to persons with limited mobility and older people through various types of interventions and access planning, including positioning those in need in shelter units situated in the vicinity of services and installing accessibility improvements such as ramps, railings, and wider toilet stances for improved access. SSSD is engaged in providing protection services in eight governorates in Syria; with more than 3000 trained volunteers and employees working in around 120 locations; SSSD has the capacity to deliver quality services in WASH/shlter/health/ GBV / PSS / PSN / CP programs. Protection interventions is mainstreamed in shelter activities mentioned in this project. Through the observance of our team in the areas of intervention in Aleppo, it is found that there are many needs in the areas of Tel Daman, Al Hader and Aleppo city. Where 800 people with special needs were monitored in the city of Aleppo in the Bab Al-Hadid area, who are in need of cleaning materials. There has also been a need to provide cleaning hygiene kit for many low-income families with more than 10,000 families, and there is a great need to provide large metal waste containers with more than 20 containers.The infrastructure of water and sanitation in both Zabadani and Harasta is almost destroyed and needs to be rehabilitated or re-installed. So that parents can have access to portable and safe water.It was also found that many people in the targeted areas in the province of Daraa need to raise awareness about health and hygiene issues, to prevent the spread of diseases.

Protection Risk Analysis

1-Protection Risk/Threat- Inequality in distribution;- Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; - Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV.Mitigating Measure- Promote Establishment of adequate management plan;2-Protection Risk/Threat:- Movements of garbage trucks in congested, camp like settings; - Reliability of services.Mitigating Measure:- Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding SWM services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedule; - Provide household level solid waste containers/plastic bags to ease disposal of waste from H/H to the public SW collection points. Establish clear routes and schedule for garbage trucks that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; - Promote involvement communities in distribution planning and feedback on package for improvement.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Protection Risk/Threat:Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards.Mitigating Measure:Monitor water quality and quantity on a daily basis, implement corrective actions
