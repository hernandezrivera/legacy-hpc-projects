Project Summary

The project aims to provide urgently needed WASH assistance to detained or stranded migrants and refugees in detention centers and urban areas in Libya, in line with two WASH strategic objectives focusing on the most vulnerable and affected population. The proposed interventions include: 1) rehabilitation of WASH facilities; 2) provision of essential hygiene items to Libyans and non-Libyans; 3) increased awareness on hygiene practices. Through those activities implemented in urban settings, IOM will also address urgent needs of a very limited number of people requiring assistance within host Libyan communities, as is practically not feasible to separate potential beneficiaries. In addition, IOM will abide by the division of labour agreed with other humanitarian stakeholders in terms of locations for implementation, to avoid potential overlapping.

Needs

There are between 6,000 and 10,000 migrants and refugees detained in the government held detention centers in Libya at any given time. The conditions of water and sanitation facilities at all detention centres remain dire and inadequate. As a result of overcrowding, the conditions are exacerbated and all government-run detention centres for migrants and refugees need trequire o improvements to the water, sanitation, garbage collection and hygiene situation. Assessments at affected detention centres in/around Tripoli during the hostilities of August-September 2018 revealed that at times there was statistically only one latrine and shower per 200 individuals. The, and that sewage tanks were also insufficient for the needs, leading to resulting in over flooding of the holding cells and , leading to alarming and life-threatening sanitary conditions for the detained migrants and refugees. As it concerns urban refugees and migrants, about a third of the respondents to a late-2018 assessment noted that they lack access to sufficient drinking water, highlighting the need for dedicated outreach of WASH interventions to urban refugee and migrant communities. Needs for distribution of the hygiene kits have been also observed among the Libyan population during the displacement.

Activities

1. Repairing of water and sanitation networks, including repairing of water pumps; 2. Construction of latrines and showers; 3. Emptying of septic tanks 4. Distribution of hygiene kits and soap ; 5. Fumigation and disinfection campaigns in DCs; 6. Hygiene promotion activities 7. Provision of water on emergency basis to detained and urban migrant populations

While directly implementing various activities to address migrant and refugee needs, the project will mainstream protection as an integral part of its overall intervention. IOM will ensure that access for these particularly vulnerable groups are prioritized, that latrines/showers built in DCs are separate for women and children, have lighting, locked doors, are safe etc and that HKs will be age and gender sensitive eg. female dignity kits, diapers for children, elderly diapers for adults etc will prioritize assistance to persons in situation of vulnerability, including unaccompanied/separated children and youth, women at risk, persons with disabilities, persons with severe medical conditions, victims or persons at risk of trafficking/smuggling, and survivors of GBV . The project will identify specific protection needs of vulnerable groups, utilizing DTM’s Mobility Tracking with protection enhanced indicators. The findings will be taken into account throughout the project cycle including beneficiary selection, service modality selection, and delivery of assistance. Whenever cases with protection concerns are identified, they will be referred to IOM’s Protection Team for further assistance or referral. Additionally, the project will be implemented in line with the international humanitarian principles and protection values, including ‘do no harm’, non-discrimination, meaningful access, safety, dignity, participation, empowerment and accountability. IOM will raise awareness and enhance capacities of national actors through various capacity building activities on rights and protection of migrants as well as alternatives to detention.
