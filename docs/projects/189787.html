Project Summary

CISDA is proposing an Emergency, Food Security, and livelihood support in two major hot spots food insecure States of Unity and Warrap States. The proposed project runs from January 2022 to December 2022.
The key purpose of the project is to increase 20000 Vulnerable households ( 120,000 people) access to food through the support of increased food production The project is in line with HRP strategic objective 2 ( SO2) and is in support of FSL cluster objective 2. The program will be delivered through direct distributions of inputs, provision of key agronomic messages, vaccination, and treatment of livestock, and provision of messages that will prevent the spread of COVID-19 transmission amongst the targeted households. These activities are mainly agricultural and livestock-related and they will be timely implemented during the seasonal calendars in the three states. The project further proposes to be accountable to the affected community by working through the Accountability to the affected population framework which will be established during the project implementation period. Through this AAP framework, feedback about project implementation will be enhanced. The protection-related issues affecting women , children, girls that are at high protection risks throughout the implementation of the project will be addressed by ensuring that protection risks-related issues are incorporated from the design phase of the project. This will help eliminate the protection-related issues.
The main project activities are: The main activities for this project include distribution of main and lean season agricultural seeds, tools, and fishing kits, provision of key messages on better agronomic practices, provision of key messages that will help reduce the impact of COVID-19; training of farmers vaccination, treatment of livestock and Training of Community Animal Health Workers and provision of climatic information for detection of the early warning system. Additional activities include: training and capacity building of farmers, AAP, Protection integration and PSEA.
CISDA has over five years of strong experience in implementing similar Emergency food security and livelihood projects in the same geographical context-. their activities have been funded by diverse donors such as FAO, SSHF, and IOM. The project thus will leverage on the experiences gained during the past implementations in order to adequately address the food insecurity problem.
The project will integrate or work closely with both the protection cluster and the nutrition cluster. With the protection cluster, the project will support beneficiaries identified by the protection partner that is high protection risk. They will be mapped out and supported during implementation. With the nutrition project, the project will set up demonstration sites at OTP centers and train households with malnourished children. These households will also be supported with high nutritious vegetables which they will produce to address the nutritional needs of their malnourished children.

1 - Needs

South Sudan still grapple in huge Humanitarian crisis. According to UNOCHA need alaysis overview report about the Humanitarian situation, 8.9 million people are in need which include 8.54 million South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 328,000 refugees and asylum seekers. Two million people are internally displaced; of these 1.4 million require urgent support, and 1.8 million are returnees, of these 1.2 million have urgent needs (UNOCHA 2022). The driven factor for the high Humanitarian is due to high food insecurity .The high food insecurity in the Country according to Food security Cluster analysis report to the Humanitarian response plan is as a result of covariate climatic , conflict and economic shocks ( such as floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices an the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or fear of violence and household level idiosyncratic stressors ( such as death of bread winner, gender -based violence, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential services) which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks. In Tonj South, Tonj East, and Tonj North and Mayendit Counties where this specific project is targeting , localized conflict and flooding are the key disaster that have exacerbated the food security situation or have made it worsen; the conflict limited most of the farmers from not cultivating. The few farmers who cultivated were met with an unprecedented flooding that damaged most of the crops in the field.Further evidences indicated in the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs overview indicates that most households rely on Emergency coping strategies inorder to meet their food need. Intersectoral analysis of the current food insecurity situation indicates that the main factor affecting food insecurity are food availability, access, utilization, stability and seasonality . These factors are also other sectoral impact that undermined food insecurity in these states such as the rate of malnutrition, protection related issues and water and sanitation.CISDA proposes to address food access , availability , utilization and stabilization through livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock support
