Project Summary

This project will implemented in Roj and Alhol camps (in Al-Hassakeh, where 100% of sub-districts are at education severity 4 according to the 2024 HNO) to provide protection and education programmes. The proposed intervention will build on the previous phases of the programme in AlHol and Roj camps, where progress has been achieved in engaging the community; empowering children; strengthening their resilience; responding to their integrated education, mental health and psychosocial needs; and improving the quality of services. We will continue delivering a multi-sector programme centred around the NGO's CoP approach. The proposed intervention will continue supporting the communities’ access to Child Protection and Education services, with a specific focus on:
Reducing vulnerabilities resulting from factors such gender, age, and disability by enhancing the response to their unique and diverse protection needs ,Providing non-formal pre-school and basic education in Roj camp including strengthening family and community capacity to facilitate children’s learning and Increasing the affected communities’ resilience to threats and vulnerabilities by promoting community engagement and agency.
The NGO will continue to monitor and respond to serious child rights violations. We aim to reduce risk factors related to these violations through prevention activities and services for children and their parents, training and awareness raising for key stakeholders, and support for children’s recovery from violations through a robust referral mechanism. The referral mechanism will link a variety of services and ensure a continuum of care through case management, the NGO adopts an integrated, multisectoral approach to protect at-risk people from harm, to mitigate any negative impacts and maximize positive impacts of its interventions, this project will ensure that children and adolescents have increased access to quality violence prevention and response services and have improved resilience that strengthen positive coping mechanisms. Prevention actions will focus on building psychosocial resilience and enhancing positive coping mechanisms that foster stress processing, problem-solving and positive peer support, and strengthening of contextualized life skills for children and their families. This will be achieved through provision of adapted and structured PSS approaches such as Healing and Education through Art (HEART), Peer-to-peer Psychological First Aid (PFA), and the Youth Resilience Program. Recreational and play activities will be provided in a structured manner to leverage PSS outcomes, in addition this project will ensure that Girls and boys aged 3-18 have safe access to quality and protective education services including non-formal education for displaced and marginalized children, and as a kind of integration between education and child protection this program will Strengthened family and community capacity to protect children and adolescents, and to support their safe learning, development, and psychosocial resilience.
The most common risks are GBV risks and to sexual exploitation exacerbated by the overcrowded conditions, so the mitigation actions already are in place by include community awareness on gender equality, measures to promote equitable and meaningful participation and the empowerment of women and girls. Particular efforts will be made to ensure women are integrated in the community-based decision-making and leadership structures, such as CPCs and PTAs. The NGO will also continue to have ‘girls-only days’ at the CFSs to overcome barriers to girls’ participation and ensure equitable access. These activities also allow girls to strengthen relationships with peers and build their social capital in a safe space. As for education, the teaching staff and facilitators will be supported to promote positive gender norms in the group and classroom activities.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

To improve camp safety, the NGO will conduct GBV safety audits and work with community members to develop strategies to address identified risks; these will include advocacy with camp authorities and others humanitarian actors. GBV prevention and risk mitigation will be mainstreamed across Education and CP through GBV community awareness on risk factors and available services and by encouraging community-led initiatives to mitigate GBV risks. Frontline staff will receive refresher trainings on gender-responsive implementation, GBV risk mitigation and response. Needs assessment: According to recent assessment conducted in the area of project implementation the analysis shows that children and adolescents do not have full access to quality violence prevention and response services, and have negative coping mechanisms because of resilience weaknesses, in addition it was clear that Girls and boys aged 3-18 do not have safe access to quality and protective education services including non-formal education for displaced and marginalized children. M&E: The MEAL plan will guide the data collection by the field team through activity reporting and monitoring (e.g. training reports, activity reports, attendance lists). Where appropriate, MEAL team will collect data through Primero, to ensure a higher quality of data collection and analysis; paper-based data collection will be used only if most appropriate for the context (both in terms of sensitivity using online data collection. The MEAL team will update the Indicator Performance Tracking Table (IPTT) on a monthly basis to track progress vis-a-vis targets, and with all data disaggregated by sex and age. The IPTT will form the basis for the monthly meetings between the programme and MEAL team to discuss progress, root causes behind any variances, and agree on needed action plans for the coming period. In addition, qualitative data will be routinely collected through monitoring visits, classroom observations and meetings with members of local committees, families, community leaders– and most importantly, children– to provide real-time feedback on the quality and effectiveness of programme activities. Coordination: Across the broader response, the NGO is an active member of the Syria INGO Regional Forum (SIRF), sitting on the Board, and is an active member of the Policy and Advocacy Working Group. The organisation is a member of the NES NGO Forum and sits on the Steering Committee. Additionally, actively participates in intersectoral working groups, including in CP, GBV, Nutrition, FSL, WASH, Durable Solutions, Early Recovery and Livelihoods and CAAFAG, participates in joint risk and needs assessments and response planning of the clusters at WoS level and Working Groups at NES level. The NGO has good coordination and has built a good rapport with the local authorities; this has helped in ensuring the smooth implementation of the programmes in NES.
