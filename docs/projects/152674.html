Project Summary

In response to the growing humanitarian crisis in South Sudan, UNIDOR is proposing to enhance the provision of critical child protection services to 30,000 vulnerable populations in Unity State, covering Koch, Leer and Mayendit. These areas were selected based on the needs assessment that revealed that the needs were severe and the fact that UNIDOR already has running projects in the areas.
The proposed one year’ emergency intervention is designed to :i) support extremely vulnerable, children including unaccompanied and separated children through individual case management ii) identify and register unaccompanied and separated children and ensuring that they are reunified with their parents/caregivers ii)provide psycho social support to conflict distressed children through structured gender disaggregated games and activities at child friendly spaces, community based and school based PSS. iii)Raising awareness on child protection risks on landmines and ERWs in schools and communities and iv) integrating child protection monitoring and mainstreaming into the protection intervention to improve the overall context analysis to respond to the identified needs and enhancing the understanding and analysis among humanitarian actors on the child protection context and needs.
UNIDOR will also integrate child protection into the already existing UNIDO Nutrition, WASH, FSL and Health programs in Leer, Mayendit and Koch counties. The already existing Community Based Child Protection Networks (CBCPN) will be trained to provide a platform for the identification of and response to child protection concerns, the development of community-based mitigation plan, prevention of separation messaging on FTR, forced recruitment , early marriage and child protection surveillance mechanism. CBCPN will also identify and refer vulnerable children in need of individual support. UNIDOR will also operate three safe healing and learning spaces for children that facilitate their recovery, restore a sense of normalcy, and promote their cognitive, physical, social and emotional development and provide parenting support.
Youth engagement to provide peer support, recreational activities and referrals to FSL programming will also serve as an entry point to develop future interventions with Children Associated with Armed Groups and Armed Forces. An ongoing monitoring and reporting of grave violations and collaboration with local authorities will be needed to prevent children from being recruited into the armed forces and armed groups.
.
Through the child protection program, UNIDOR intends to work with the Nutrition program and teams to identify unaccompanied and separated children including children living within vulnerable families that affect their care and nutritional well being and refer them for Nutrition screening and support. UNIDOR Nutrition team will then provide support the identified children to ensure continuity of care and support at home within the family setting. The CP team will provide follow up support to malnourished children receiving support within the family environment. Complementary funding supports over 150 caregivers in providing appropriate, positive care for children. This project will also support population within the communities, host and IDPs with awareness messages and other community level support services such as WASH, Health and food through static and community outreach.
UNIDOR is the only agency providing child protection services in Leer,Mayendit and Koch,hence there is no likelihood or possibility of duplication of services.

Needs

Southern and Northern Unity States, mainly Mayendit, Koch and Leer counties continued to experience repeated attacks and threats as well as armed youths cattle raiding from April to September,2018. This situation led to continued displacement of communities that continued to expose them to numerous risks, with children and women being the most affected. Communities fled to Bentiu PoC, Bentiu, Mayom ,Panyinjiar and Rubkona counties for their physical safetyChildren continued to be recruited by both Armed Forces and Armed Groups in Leer, Mayendit and Koch by both Government and In Opposition (IO).With the continued clashes between warring parties and armed youths, who continued to raid cattle and displace communities, the psychological well being of men, women, boys and girls was negatively affected. The fighting also disrupted and broke down community protection systems thereby exposing children to protection risks such as abuse, neglect ,exploitation and violence. Many children became unaccompanied, separated and orphaned and as a result would need family tracing and reunification services. Children, both boys and girls are distressed prompting the need to provide life saving interventions through the provision of psychosocial support, family tracing and reunification, case management inorder to support their resilience to cope with the existing environment.Gender roles have changed as women take up the role of bread winners in the family by moving in search of food, water, firewood and petty jobs which expose them to exploitation, sexual violence and abuse. Girls have been forced to marry and at times they have also been married at an early age inorder for the parents to acquire wealth to support their families. The previous insecurity disproportionately affected adolescent girls. Adolescent girls were particularly vulnerable to multiple forms of violence and have limited support systems and access to information . As a result of the early marriages, girls have faced a lot of medical complications which sometimes has resulted in their deaths during delivery. UNIDOR will continue building capacity and provide training and guidance to the already existing Community Based Networks to prevent and respond to child abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against children. As of September,2018, the teams on ground reported that communities were returning back to their places of origin despite fears that fighting might reoccur. UNIDOR will work closely with the community based child protection networks to raise awareness within the communities on child rights, child labour, forced and early marriage as well as prevention of separation and recruitment by armed forces and armed groups.With the signing of the peace agreement, there is also a likelihood of movement of communities back to their previous homes, which might also require having a contingency plan of preventing and responding to child protection needs. Responding to these emerging issues requires a targeted emergency response inside of the current UNIDOR programs sites in Northern and Southern Unity (Leer, Mayendit and Koch), increased staffing and mobile emergency programming that meet the immediate needs of dispersed populations affected by humanitarian crisis. UNIDOR will utilize existing internal capacity to respond quickly and effectively to emerging crisis and will provide emergency child protection response over one-year period.30,000 boys, girls, women and men and including the under18 years and adults in Southern Unity both IDPs and host communities shall benefit from this UNIDOR protection programming. IDP and host community children and their caregivers will participate in age-appropriate psycho-social support and parenting activities and children will be actively involved in the design and implementation of the activities. Children will help shape and design the psycho-social support activities and determine the days that are most convenient.

Activities

UNIDOR will provide the following activities inorder to addreaa the identified needs.-Provision of psychosocial support activities through gender,age and sex aggregated activities through child friendly spaces. -Provide Family Tracing and Reunification services to unaccompanied and seperated children.-Provision of comprehensive case management services to children with protection needs.-Identify and refer vulnerable children for services.-Mapping available service providers in the areas of intervention to establish referral pathways.-Build the capacity of caregivers and foster families on good parental skills.-Integrate child protection into other sectors.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

UNIDO staff will be recruited from local communities and will most likely remain in these location in case UNIDOR may take international staff for evacuation due to insecurity. UNIDOR will identify local and community based child protection networks and train them to support programming activities and on basic PSS to enhance longer term sustainability. UNIDOR will always ensure that regular consultations are held with children, boys and girls quarterly to ensure all programming responses are informed by their voices, experience and recommendations. Regular meetings will be held with community leaders, government officials, local and international partners. All UNIDOR staff will adhere to and uphold the humanitarian principles of Do no harm. Prior to initiating any intervention, rapid assessments are conducted to assess identified locations and current services. Participatory methodologies, which include focus groups discussions and key informant interviews are held with communities for them to provide a situational analysis of any unintended, provision of services in a highly militarized zone where access is not guaranteed or affected population could be placed at risk when accessing services. In all the undertakings, primary consideration will be given to Accountability to Affected Populations, to identify, understand and support their own protection measures. Different segments of affected populations need to be meaningfully engaged in all decisions and actions that have a direct impact on their well-being.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A context analysis will be done at the project implementation areas to better understand the norms,values and culture of the community. The messages will be contextually specific so that the beneficiaries will understand them.This would include designing the messages in a way that is well understood by the community, taking into consideration, issues of gender and age. UNIDOR staff are hired from the local communities and as such are aware of the context.They will help in ensuring that the response is context specific. CFS activities will also take into account the age and gender aggregation where children will be grouped according to their age groups.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes the project will take into consideration protection from sexual exploitation and abuse by ensuring that men, women,boys and girls are protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. Awareness raising sessions will be conducted in communities,schools and churches to ensure that the communities are aware of what PSEA is all about. UNIDOR will have a focal point responsible for receiving all reports of prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse.A register will be in place for recording any complaints and feedback provided. Every UNIDOR staff member will sign a Code of Conduct agreeing that he will abide by the rules and regulations on Child Safeguarding and PSEA. Suggestion boxes as well as telephones,in areas with network will be used by communities to send their complaints and feedback. The focal point person will be responsible for ensuring that action is taken and feedback is provided to the survivor.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Communities will be consulted from the inception of the project till the end of the project for them to own the programme. UNIDOR will ensure that community leaders,community based child protection network members and men,women,boys and girls are represented in all project activities and decisions.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

UNIDOR will have feedback and complaints mechanism in place by making sure there is a focal person in the organization and a Committee that represents communities.This will comprise men,women,boys and girls.Awareness raising sessions will be conducted for the community to be aware of this service.Suggestion boxes as well as a telephone number will be provided for community members to report. A register will also be provided to register complaints and the feedback.This will have a timeline so that at the end they will be able to see if complaints and feedback were handled in time.

Communities will participate freely in all stages of the project cycle. This will involve men,women,boys and girls. Joint monitoring will be done with representatives of the community as well as the donor community to gauge the success or failure of the programme and also adjust where need be.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

UNIDOR will ensure that before the implementation of the project,a risk analysis is done and the response tailor made to address the needs. Communities will participate in the risk analysis process. During provision of responses,UNIDOR will ensure that community activities are done at safer areas making sure communities do not travel long distances to access services risking their lives. Efforts will be made to make sure the teams moves to the communities and if possible allocate staff in those communities to reach out to beneficiaries. Efforts will also be made to ensure that people with disabilities,including the elderly,children with protection needs and survivors receive appropriate responses.

The project will identify and address barriers to access that different population groups could face by holding meetings with the various communities,working with them to find ways of dealing with the barriers and also discussing with community leaders and representatives. Some of the barriers,which might include distances of where the services are will be minimized through mobile responses. Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project seeks to address inequalities through awareness raising and ensuring that existing structures are utilized. The local authorities will also be sensitized on the project itself. The team will focus on strengthening the relations among groups tobuild confidence and mitigate the divisions that can lead to conflict, violence andunderdevelopment. This will be achieved through addressing inequality through inclusion,fairness, responsiveness, accountability to all social groups, and measures to strengthen inter-group/inter-culture relations. Indicators will also be disaggregated by more categories to show attainment of development benefits by different groups within society, and an inequality measurecould be added for each indicator.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The volatile security situation and limited humanitarian access to some locations is a serious risk. UNIDOR will develop preparedness and contingency plan to deal with fluctuating security and replicate successful approaches to other locations in Northern and Southern Unity, especially in Mayendit, Koch and Leer. UNIDOR will develop contingency plans to ensure minimum services remain in place, including recruiting and training staff from the locality that can maintain services in the event of limited access or evacuation of international/relocatable staff with support provided remotely. As part of UNIDOR’s contingency plans, key triggers will be identified for a variety of likely scenarios in all location. Security management plans are in place in all location and are regularly reviewed and updated based on emerging threats and changing security. UNIDOR is well positioned to provide services in Mayendit, Leer and Koch as offices and the areas is relatively safe. However, UNIDOR anticipates access becoming a possibility in areas surrounding Southern and Northern Unity Sates and sees mobile response model as an appropriate complementary solution to the volatile context.
