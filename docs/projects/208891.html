Project Summary

SFPA is a Member Association(MA) with IPPF and working in line with the fifth-year strategy of IPPF 2023-2028 which is aligned with SDGs 2030 and ICPD commitments. It is a leading NGO in the area of maternal health, women empowerment, and youth protection through providing SRHR targeting hard-to-reach and underserved populations in 12 states (Gazira, Red Sea, Gaderef, Kassal, Sinnar, River Nile, Northern state, North &South Kordofan Khartoum, North, South and West Darfur (prior to the war). SFPA works in partnership and in line with the protocols of FMOH and SMOH. It is also collaborating with all partners in the area of SRHR including government bodies, INGOs, UN agencies, NGOs, and CSOs. SFPA is using various modalities to deliver SRHR services, namely; static clinics, mobile clinics, outreach medical teams, Community Health Workers(midwives) plus the community mobilizers and youth. SRHR package of services provided by SFPA is FP, RH services which include HIV/AIDs &STIs, MISP package that is provided in emergencies (including GBV and counseling), PHC, EPI, and nutrition services for women and children. SFPA developed women's training centers attached to static clinics to upgrade women's capacities in income-generation activities.
This accumulated experience working with needy women, the wide geographical coverage of SFPA services, the strong governance and institutional setup plus the effective partnership with stakeholders and partners qualify SFPA to be among the national NGOs that can support economic empowerment for women.
Since the beginning of the war, SFPA provided 38508 SRHR services including GBV services. Out of these services, 20917 were delivered to less than 20 years of age clients, and 26346 to female clients. SFPA is committed to providing SRHR services to the increasing needs of women and girls in needy areas of Sudan. SFPA also provides referral services for survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV).
It has been estimated that there are around one million women and girls of reproductive age, including 120,000 pregnant women, who require immediate access to sexual and reproductive health care services. Additionally, more than half a million refugees are expected to benefit from integrated GBV/SRH programs. To address the needs of one million women, girls, and vulnerable populations, GBV response services, dignity kits, and SRHR clinics are necessary.
Under the HRP project: 1- SFPA teams will offer SRHR services to IDPs and host communities in the White Nile and Red Sea states. Mobile clinics will be working at gathering sites and underserved areas in the host communities in both states. 2- SFPA adopting the theory of change in working with the community using BCC strategy (2021-2026) to address SRHR issues among the community, the package of interventions reflected in the BCC strategy has been implemented since 2021with other projects like WISH, SPRINT, and Stream. Community mobilizers and youth will conduct group sessions, and one-on-one meetings to provide health education. SFPA and the Health Promotion Department in the FMoH will coordinate to design GBV health messages at the national level. The messages that have been approved will be disseminated across the nation to create a strong awareness of GBV, enhance knowledge of effective prevention methods, and educate both communities and victims about the available resources and necessary support required to combat GBV. 3- Training women center will be established to improve IDPs\Host community women’s income generation skills (Sewing, Soap making, Henna, Makeup, marketing, bookkeeping…. etc.).

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The International Planned Parenthood Federation (IPPF) and the Sudan Family Planning Association (SFPA) have put in place safeguarding policies that put the safety, dignity, and rights of individuals, groups, and affected populations first, which is similar to Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Policy on Protection in Humanitarian Action (2016). IPPF\SFPA safeguarding policies are designed and implemented through all interventions so that protection risks and potential violations are taken into consideration at all levels to ensure that all staff \ clients, regardless of their background or circumstances, feel protected and supported. In case any concerns or issues arise, both clients and staff members are encouraged to report them through the IPPF website, where they will be taken seriously and addressed with the utmost care and attention. Feedback and complaints mechanisms are responsive and effective. IPPF\SFPA safeguarding policies ensure people’s access to assistance and services - in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. for PLWA, people living with disability, etc.).SFPA and IPPF have trained staff members at all levels to recognize who is at risk, from what or whom, and the consequences that their actions may have on the threats that people experience. This training aims to mainstream protection by enhancing staff members' understanding of vulnerability and capacity through these threats. SFPA health providers provide referral services through GBV referral pathways. IPPF\SFPA safeguarding policies are to assist individuals in developing the skills and knowledge necessary to protect and empower women and individuals to advocate for their rights. We believe that by providing these essentials, women and individuals are better equipped to lead fulfilling lives and contribute positively to their communities. We recognize that each person's needs are unique, and we are committed to working with individuals to identify and address their specific challenges and barriers. Under the HRP project cycle SFPA will demonstrate an understanding of the different needs, concerns, and priorities of different age groups, gender, and age-disaggregated data will be collected and analyzed, and targeted individuals and groups receive assistance, which will be tailored to the needs and interests of the different age groups. Project targets to reduce and mitigate gender-based violence, and provi services to women, girls, boys, and men for all age groups at IDPs gathering sites and host communities. At all phases of the project targeted groups are encouraged to participate in and influence decisions taken. Information and the lessons learned about the project will be shared appropriately with target groups and other stakeholders. SFPA will develop clear monitoring and evaluation plan in order to ensure participation & empowerment of affected population and whom will have a right to equitable and safe assistance, and where and how to obtain it.

SFPA and IPPF are committed to implementing a safeguarding policy to ensure the well-being of everyone associated with the organization. This commitment extends to clients, service providers, partners, and all individuals involved in the association's activities. The focus on protection from all forms of harm, including sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment, reflects a comprehensive approach to creating a secure environment.The provision of safe and easy reporting mechanisms for any violations is crucial in fostering transparency and accountability. This not only empowers individuals to report incidents but also contributes to the overall goal of maintaining a safe and protected environment for everyone involved with the association.Such policies and practices are essential in promoting trust, confidence, and the overall effectiveness of the organization's services.
