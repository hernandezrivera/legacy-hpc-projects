Project Summary

The proposed project aims at improving access and scale up response to emergence health services and strengthening essential primary health care services provision through an integrated service delivery package using emergency interventions with focus on the major causes of Morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable community members (Mothers, under five 5 years children and rehabilitation services for the disabled). This project will support two mobile clinics and two PHCUs in Mayom and Rubkona Counties. Through this project a total of 2,500 (1,100 Male, 1400 Female) Boys and Girls are included among these, will be reached with Preventive and curative health care services. Of these, 1250 (50%) are Host community, 625 (25%) are IDPs and 625 (25%) are Returnees. HACO will provide OPD consultations and treatment to 2500 clients at her health facility (Koythei) in Bentiu and through mobile health service delivery in Mayom and Rubkona counties will enhance integrated service delivery especially in building capacity of healthcare service providers in proper management of common illnesses (diarrhea, Malaria and pneumonia) and provision of essential medicines (amoxicillin, ORS, ACTs) for these common illnesses among the adults and children according to the national treatment guidelines) in Bentiu town and through her mobile health care services in Mayom County during the course of the project. The project will reach 20 disabled persons with rehabilitation service through either facility based or community based Rehabilitation approach. The project anticipates reaching a total of 187 pregnant mothers with skilled delivery using the trained birth attendants, mobile maternity and ambulance system to bring mothers with complicated deliveries to PHCUs and PHCCs for skilled management. The project will ensure enhanced service on Mental Health and Psycho social support by working with two PHCCs in each of the two target counties of Rubkona and Mayom. The PHCCs will be centers of provision of integrated Health, Nutrition services and basic MHPSS services to GBV survivors through trained community health workers and their engagement in screening and identification of severely malnourished (SAM) children from the community. The complicated cases of MAM and SAM shall be referred.
HACO will ensure access to Immunization of 1300 children with EPI and Measles by revamping the cold chain and mobile immunization activities. HACO will also support and strengthen the diagnostic capacity of two PHCUs from each of the target counties through training, equipping and stocking with required facilities in order to properly diagnose malaria, TB and HIV/AIDS according to MOH standards. The project also targets a total of 2000 households to be reached with Health education and awareness sessions to enhance community level surveillance, Disease prevention and early health care seeking behaviours and enhancing resilience among crisis affected populations through trained Community Health workers. HACO will work towards enhancing synergies for the emergency humanitarian response to focus on lifesaving activities while working towards creating more sustainable solutions from the beginning. The urgent life-saving needs in South Sudan currently is a considerable challenge which calls for a concerted efforts for collaboration and creating synergies with both the development and humanitarian responses.

1 - Needs

Rubkona County has the largest internally displaced persons’ camp in the country, accommodating 120,000 people. Cattle camps along the river and swampy areas inside the county are very difficult to access round the year due to rain, flood and poor roads, insecurity and lack of logistics, maintaining a cold chain for immunization is always an obstacle to delivery of medical supplies, access to health facilities, routine immunization, as well as immunization campaigns. Most payams do not have functional cold chains. Accessibility, river, rain and flood are three crucial factors. Most parts of Mayom County become flooded during the rainy season and remain isolated throughout the season which affects health service delivery and particularly dangerous with regard to maternal and child health services. Rubkona often becomes inaccessible due to cattle raiding. HMIS facility report showed that Malaria, Diarrhea and Pneumonia are the leading causes of morbidity and mortality in the counties. The WASH infrastructure in health facilities is a rudiment and none existent. Child health cold chain facilities continues to be a challenge, hence EPI service need to be rejuvenated. HACO will be managing Koythei PHCU in Bentiu to be able to reduce on the infant and adult mortality due to the afore mentioned cases . The population of Greater Rubkona county is 137,000, IDPs in Koythei 24,180, Returnees 32,130, Bentiu Town 32,000. Displaced populations face the most complex challenges in accessing healthcare services. Children under 5 years are one of the most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases, owing to poor nutrition and low levels of immunity and immunization coverage. The coverage in 2017 and 2018 for all vaccine preventable diseases remain under 50 per cent, and as a consequence 42 per cent of children less than 1 year are at risk of measles. Women of reproductive age face serious health risks and only about one in five childbirths involve a skilled health care worker. Survivors of gender-based violence lack access to adequate services. People with health issues like HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, mental health, disabilities and non-communicable diseases are also largely being cut off from life-saving treatment.Despite article 31 of the transitional constitution entitling them equal access to health, people with disabilities who according to the 2008 census were 5.1% of the total population in the country (although this number is far behind the reality of the population of disabled persons in south Sudan) struggle to access and afford healthcare, including assistive devices (Forcier et al, 2016). Many of the people them never or rarely see a doctor. People with disabilities struggle to access healthcare due to the long distances and poor road networks; the lack of ambulance services, inadequate drugs supply in the health centers; negative attitude towards people with disabilities by health workers; inadequate and in accessible health information and knowledge and inaccessible health physical infrastructure. Internally displaced persons with disabilities were amongst those most vulnerable to malnutrition. The project intends to strengthen the integration of disability programming in maternal newborn child health and nutrition services in Mayom and Rubkona counties. It’s estimated that over 50,000 returnees from neighboring counties arrived in Rubkona hence need for constant alert for eventual outbreak of immunisable diseases i.e Measles and polio. Humane-Aid for Community Organisation (HACO) will support existing local facilities and resource persons and work hand in hand with the State MoH to man and equip the health units with basic equipment to PHCU level and staffing, to strengthen the facility’s cold chain and embarking on providing major PHCU level services and upgrade which will enable the team to provide BEmONC care.

2 - Activities

To forestall the public health crisis, HACO proposes the following: Providing outpatient care services to the children 0-5, women, men, boys and girls including those with disabilities through facility based and mobile clinics. Screening for malnutrition among Children under 5yrs and Pregnant Mothers using MUAC through Community Health Workers, Mobile clinics and PHCU . Those identified with moderate malnutrition will be managed accordingly using RUFT, Plumpy Nut and BP5. Those found with severe malnutrition will be referred to PHCCs and Hospital for management. Screening for Malaria through testing using RDTs for children under 5yrs and pregnant women at all levels of service delivery. Those with uncomplicated malaria will be managed using ACTs by the mobile health team and PHCU facility at Koythei and severe cases will be referred to PHCCs and Hospital. This will also include malaria prophylaxis for pregnant women using Fansidar(SP).Conduct capacity building among health care workers in Assessment and Management of common illnesses’ (case management) for (Diarrhea, Malaria and Pneumonia) by mobile teams and PHCU at Koythei in Bentiu using the recommended training guidelines. The trainings will include ICCM at community level and IMNCI at facility level. The training will focus on using the recommended treatments (ACTs for Malaria, ORS+Zinc for Diarrhea and Amoxicillin for Pneumonia). Strengthen basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care including the clinical management of rape through capacity building of skilled birth attendants that will be trained in BeMONC / CeMONC and providing F-ANC and Post Natal Care service check-ups among pregnant mothers. Intensifying surveillance and support immunization of children against measles, polio and other vaccine preventable diseases, through coordination, supplying health facilities with drugs and other medical supplies and screening children under 5yrs and pregnant mothers for vaccination. This will also include routine deworming, provision of folic acid for pregnant women, giving doses of TT and routine Vitamin A supplementation for the under 5yrs (every 6 months).Provision of support for epidemiological surveillance and data collection at facility level and community level and conducting outbreak investigations. This will strengthen active surveillance both at community and facility level, engaging Boma Community mobilizers to be part of the HIV/TB case finding and suspected cases will be referred to nearest facility.Improving the social welfare of people with disabilities with rehabilitative interventions through a Community Based Rehabilitation (CBR) strategy that involve: community mobilization, training of resource persons, training of CBR volunteers, identification and assessment of disabled, awareness and sensitisation of the disabled persons, securing and distribution of assistive devices for the disabled, community awareness and advocacy and providing psycho-social support, treatment and referral.Enhancing the capacity for the Home Health Care promoters (HHCPs), these HHCP teams will be the arms of social mobilization and awareness creating team that will ensure early case detection and referral of cases to nearest mobile unit and Primary Health care Unit where applicable.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

South Sudan is a country that is affected by complex emergencies resulting from prolonged conflict, climate change, a broken health system and outbreaks of communicable diseases, This called for concerted efforts of the international community to avert the situation. Many people have been displaced out of the country and others are within the country living in Internally displaced situation as IDPs. Several funding mechanisms have been initiated by humanitarian agencies to ensure that people affected by these calamities are supported to live healthy and forge ahead with life. HACO is one of the National partner involved in delivering health services to people in need during emergencies including those displaced Therefore it is our deepest interest to provide health services to the displaced persons wherever they will be.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project is closely linked and work along the locally available public health system and building synergies with the Government and community systems such as us of Home based health workers. the project also work towards building referral networks with government systems such as local chiefs and other related agencies.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with national objectives for the relevant ministry ministry of health including the South Sudanese health sector development plan 2019-2020. the South Sudan health strategic plan 2016 -2020 and the National Health policy 2016- 2026.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The proposed project is part of the humanitarian efforts to ensure safe and stable livelihoods of the people in Rubkona and Mayom counties. The project will along other intervention players and development partners work to ensure peace and security of the beneficiaries through campaigning for peaceful means of resolving conflicts within the communities. With the current peace environment in the country, the proposed project will through awareness and dialogue meetings to ensure long term and durable solutions for the displaced, refugees, returnees and host communities. HACO will work towards ensuring that the project is linked to other long term solutions such as WASH facility development, Livelihood and food security and ensuring all children of school going age are given the opportunity and supported to go to school.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, HACO is committed to ensuring that the project is sustainable in the long run. This will be done in consideration of six key factors for a sustainable health framework are outlined as: effective disease prevention and health promotion, effective health and health care systems, funding models that drive desired behavioral changes, leveraging innovation and innovative technologies, optimal development, alignment, and support of human resources and strategic alignment with determinants of health.
