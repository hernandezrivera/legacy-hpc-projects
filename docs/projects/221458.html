Project Summary

The socio-political crisis in the Northwest and Southwest regions continues to cause massive population displacement. According to the September 2024 Multi Sectoral Need Assessment (MSNA) Round 10 exercise, the armed conflict has forced the movement of approximatively 828,545 persons in the Northwest and Southwest regions, of which 334,098 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and 494,447 returnees. Moreover, 34% of assessed households reported that they were affected by a safety/ security / protection incident which affected their freedom of movement, while 19% said they had been affected by a safety, security or protection concern. The top three protection concerns were (i) Lack of civil documentation (57%), (ii) Early pregnancy (40%) and (iii) Arbitrary arrests and detentions (30%). The 2024 HNO reported that out of a total population of 28.8 million, 3.4 million people were in need of humanitarian assistance, of which 2M were in need of protection assistance. As of xxxx 2024, xxxx people are in need of protection in the NWSW. The protection environment remains fragile in both regions, with civilian population continuously facing severe protection threats. The protection environment in NW and SW regions is characterized by human right violations against civilians, of which Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) are the most affected. IDPs represent around 60% of human rights violation victims. The most recurrent violations/protection incidents are: Arbitrary or unlawful arrest and /or detention; Physical assault or abuse (not related to SGBV) and Killing (incl. extra-judicial, arbitrary or summary execution). From January to October 2024, 4377 protection incidents were reported by Protection Monitoring partners including UNHCR & Intersos, DRC and IRC. This number is an estimate given the geographical limitations of Protection Monitoring projects. xx out of xx divisions are covered by UNHCR and its partner INTERSOS.
The North-West and South-West regions continued to be marked by high levels of insecurity in 2024 with armed conflict, criminal offences against civilian populations, beside the lockdowns imposed by NSAG, and frequent roadblocks. UNHCR identified 4 key priorities in 2023 and 2024 for intervention and to contribute to the protection response and play its role as last resort provider and the Protection Cluster lead: 1) strengthen the centrality of protection, 2) coordination of lead clusters, 3) emergency preparedness and response and 4) securing solutions. To secure protection and provide solutions, UNHCR intends to implement the following protection and shelter activities in 2025:
1. Protection Monitoring and responses through community-based approach in the Northwest and Southwest regions.
2. Legal Support, Civil documentation in Northwest, Southwest, West and Littoral regions.
3. GBV response through comprehensive case management and support to community empowerment initiatives for prevention and response to GBV (including Quick impact projects) in the Northwest and southwest regions.
4. Shelter and NFIs assistance to vulnerable IDPs and host communities in NW and SW regions.
In 2025, UNHCR will continue reinforcing protection monitoring and empowering communities to contribute to their own protection, respond to protection incidents as much as possible and search for solutions, and ensure peaceful coexistence among their members. The intervention will monitor and report human rights violations perpetrated against displaced and vulnerable persons and use these findings to provide adequate protection services including legal assistance, civil document support, psychosocial support, GBV case management, community empowerment opportunities to reinforce auto-protection mechanism, as well as shelter and NFI assistance through the Cash-Based Interventions (CBI) and in-kind when appropriate. CBI as a modality, and referrals to possible available services will be an integral part of UNHCR’s response.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

UNHCR will address the needs assessed through regular Protection Monitoring reports from the Protection Taskforce, UNHCR’s participatory assessment conducted in August 2024 as well the MSNA Round 10 results. According to Protection Monitoring reports, the top 3 protection incidents faced by displaced communities are Arbitrary or unlawful arrest and /or detention; Physical assault or abuse (not related to SGBV) and Killing (incl. extra-judicial, arbitrary or summary execution). According to MSNA Round 10 results, The top three protection concerns were (i) Lack of civil documentation (57%), (ii) Early pregnancy (40%) and (iii) Arbitrary arrests and detentions (30%). Through this project centered around 3 key areas (Protection Monitoring/Response including GBV, Legal support & Civil documentation, and Shelter/NFI) UNHCR aims to:1- Improve the protection environment and enhance access to life-saving assistance and basic services for 91,000 individuals affected by the Anglophone crisis in the Northwest (NW), and Southwest (SW) through Protection Monitoring and Response. This will include establishing a robust protection monitoring mechanism that includes assessment, identification, and referral processes, and which is led by Community Protection Committees (CPCs) in the NW/SW regions. This will also include supporting 300 GBV survivors and other victims of human right violations through community empowerment initiatives for prevention and response to GBV (including Quick impact project (socio economical activities and vocational trainings) and providing comprehensive case management including psychosocial support to GBV survivors. This will be achieved by engaging the local community in providing appropriate assistance to vulnerable populations, enabling them to access basic social services and identify their needs. The local community will also be engaged in identifying the drivers of ongoing displacement, protection risks, and communication opportunities, particularly for individuals with specific needs, including those with disabilities. Targeted Assistance: UNHCR and its partners will identifying individuals requiring shelter and non-food item (NFI) assistance, with a response modality that includes cash support. UNHCR and its partners will ensure that women, girls, men, boys, and persons with specific needs (PwSNs) from both internally displaced persons (IDP) and host communities, especially survivors or those at risk of gender-based violence (GBV), can access case management services in safe spaces. Assistance modality: Protection Monitoring and support to GBV survivors and other victims of Human rights violations will be complemented with the delivery of protection services through CASH to mitigate protection risks. The main focus of this project is to enhance the protection of communities affected by armed conflict while ensuring that vulnerable populations receive the support they need.2- Enhance access to civil documentation services in the Northwest, Southwest, West, and Littoral regions of Cameroon. UNHCR and its partners will facilitate the acquisition of 1,600 civil status documents (including birth certificates, and National Identity Cards) for vulnerable displaced youths. This initiative is crucial for enabling access to essential services such as education, employment, and livelihood opportunities. 3- Shelter and NFIs assistance to vulnerable IDPs and host communities in NWSW. UNHCR aims to support 3,000 displaced, host, and vulnerable non-displaced households (approximately 18,000 individuals) with shelter and non-food item (NFI) assistance in the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon. This component will be implemented through cash transfers, allowing affected households to purchase preferred materials and make improvements to their shelters. This component aims to create a harmonized effort to ensure that affected individuals receive adequate housing and can live with dignity.
