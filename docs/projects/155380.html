Project Summary

Under this Flash Appeal, Food Security sector partners plan to address the most urgent food security needs of people in rural areas across the country, who are adversely affected by multiple crises, including last year’s poor harvest for many small-holder farmers, economic shocks and recent cholera and typhoid outbreaks. Building on the funding already received to reach an estimated 900,000 people as of January 2019 and the Government’s Food Deficit Mitigation Strategy, partners aim to expand the geographic coverage of assistance to reach an additional 700,000 people in food insecure rural communities through primarily cash-based assistance. Focus will be on the low-lying areas relying on rainfed agriculture disproportionately affected by last year’s erratic rain. Market-based interventions are viable despite the current economic stress, as essential food commodities remain available on rural as well as urban markets, according to the national market assessment conducted by WFP and partners in November 2018.
The IPC analysis conducted in January 2019 projects that nearly 3.8 million people in rural areas are in need of urgent humanitarian assistance, including 2.9 million who are severely food insecure (IPC phase 3 or 4) and a further 900,000 people who are currently receiving humanitarian assistance, without which they would be in IPC phase 3 or above.
In the face of the high prevalence of food insecurity (IPC phase 4 and 3), and the high probability of another El Niño phenomena in 2019 (90% likelihood according to the most recent SARCOF analysis), partners will provide a specialised food ration in the form of CSB++ specifically targeting children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women to increase the nutritional value of the food basket provided as part of the general household ration/cash.
Based on the findings of the national market assessment conducted by partners in November, partners will select the most appropriate transfer modality for each district (cash or in-kind). According to the latest price information, partners aim to cover approximately 80% of the targeted individuals’ daily food energy needs (based on a 2100 kcal diet) to supplement other available food sources, thus enabling households to meet their basic food and nutrition needs.

In view of the high probability of another El Nino, food assistance at this juncture can be considered as an early action to prevent further deterioration of food insecurity amongst particularly households already in IPC 3 (Crises) and 4 (Emergency).

Needs

Drought has been the most frequent climate-shock in Zimbabwe in the past three decades and continues to have significant consequences on the rural people’s livelihoods, incomes and food security. The frequency, intensity and impact of droughts is increasing, due in large part to climate variability. The recent El Niño-induced drought in the 2015/16 season was considered one of the worst in 35 years; it left 4.1 million rural people requiring emergency food assistance. The 2017/18 rainfall season was erratic and characterized by late onset, long mid-season dry spells, and incessant rains, especially at its tail end. The poor distribution of rains in the first four months of the season negatively affected crop development, leading to permanent wilting of the early crop in most areas. This poor distribution of rainfall and limited availability of agricultural inputs led to a reduction in area planted to major crops in the 2017/18 agricultural season. Crop performance was further negatively affected by the outbreak of the fall armyworm, with approximately 53% of the households in the rural areas having their crop affected. The 2019 Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) Rapid Appraisal estimates that 4.5 million people, or 51% of the rural population, will require food assistance during the peak of the 2018/19 lean season. According to the IPC analysis conducted in January 2019, between February and May, an estimated 30 percent of Zimbabwe’s rural population (2,878,957 people) require urgent action to protect and save livelihoods, reduce food consumption gaps, and minimize acute malnutrition. 1,891,777 people (20 percent) are in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis), while 985,131 (10 percent) have been classified in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), and a further 900,000 people who are currently receiving humanitarian assistance, without which they would be in IPC phase 3 or above. Most of these vulnerable people reside in drought-prone rural communal areas, relying exclusively on rain-fed agricultural production for their food consumption and income, and where existing social safety nets and Government input programmes have lesser reach and impact. The high reliance on subsistence rain-fed agriculture renders a large majority of the rural population extremely vulnerable to climate-related shocks and seasonal stressors. Until such time that the currently under-resourced social safety net programme more predictably addresses their needs, this population will be highly reliant on seasonal food assistance for their food and nutrition security. Geographic targeting is guided by results of the ZimVAC as well as the IPC analysis, determining the rural population that is likely to be food insecure in the consumption year, their geographic distribution and the severity of their food insecurity by quarter. A further analysis then combines the findings of the ZimVAC and IPC with the information from the District Profiles to identify the main characteristics of the districts/wards, livelihood groups, households and when necessary (i.e. for nutrition programme targeting) the individuals. The combined (multi-pronged) targeting methodology has been useful in increased accuracy and efficiency of assistance by minimizing the inclusion and exclusion errors. Furthermore, the District Drought Relief Committee (DDRC), which also includes WFP and NGOs operating in the district, convenes a meeting to prioritize the wards and allocate numbers of people to be assisted. At the ward level, villages are prioritised by the Ward Assembly at a meeting where the DDRC representative and operating NGOs will be present to facilitate the process. At village level the beneficiary households are selected by the villagers of that particular village using a community-based targeting approach with partners facilitating the process. Activities

Complementing the efforts of the Government’s Food Deficit Mitigation Strategy which addresses needs across all rural districts, the Food Security Sector partners plan to address the most urgent food security needs of 1.6 million people across 36 rural districts (all households classified as IPC4, although a majority of districts are classified as IPC3) during the peak of the 2018/19 lean season based on the findings of the 2018 ZimVAC Rural Livelihoods Assessment and the IPC analysis, taking into consideration other social safety net interventions and the response capacity of the Government. Informed by the experience of the previous years’ lean season assistance, Partners also plan to provide a protective nutritional Super Cereal Plus ration to all households with one or more under-five children (approximately 160,000 children) to prevent a deterioration of their nutritional status during the peak of the lean season. The food security transfer will seek to fill the food gap with an aim to stabilize the food consumption of severely food insecure people and reduce the risk of households resorting to negative coping mechanisms, including selling off critical assets that undermine future food security or resilience gains made in the recent past. WFP and partners use the lean season assistance as an opportunity to provide capacity strengthening and training to communities focusing mainly on livelihood-related activities, gender, water and sanitation, community asset maintenance and nutrition as well as on Social Behaviour Change Communication. To further ensure that the resilience of the targeted households and communities are promoted, the response will in most districts be implemented through partners with existing resilience-promoting programmes in place. In close collaboration with local stakeholders, provide monthly food assistance through either in-kind or cash to food insecure rural households to the 2 million people assisted with 1,1 million getting cash, 700,000 getting food and 200,000 getting a mix of food and cash. In close coordination with the nutrition sector partners, provide a protective nutritional ration to increase nutritional value of the food basket provided to households with about 274,000 children under five years (6-59 months old) to prevent a deterioration of their nutritional status during the peak of the lean season.
