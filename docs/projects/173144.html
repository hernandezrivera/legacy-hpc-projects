Project Summary

UNHCR will implement comprehensive nutrition programmes in 18 integrated nutrition centres across 14 camps and two stabilization centers within the Cox Bazaar refugee operation in partnership with nutrition partners. These will include: community management of acute malnutrition among children under-five years; pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerable groups in the population; blanket supplementary feeding programmes; infant and young child feeding programmes and community management of mothers at risk and infants 0-6 months old; nutrition programmes for adolescent girls; and support to the annual nutrition surveys to monitor the health and nutrition situation among the Rohingya refugee as per the nutrition sector strategy and UNHCR public health strategy. UNHCR will continue to provide nutrition support to the new arrivals in the transit centre through nutrition screening, onsite treatment of children identified with malnutrition, IYCF-e counselling and the provision of hot meals. COVID-19 pandemic response activities will be ensured through the provision of appropriate PPE for the nutrition partners and provision of meals for the quarantined families being hosted in the quarantine centers. Age and gender mainstreaming will be observed with a focus on improving support to persons with special needs, including disability, ensured throughout the programme cycle. The provision of accountability to the affected population (AAP) will be ensured through implementation The continued operation of the complaints feedback mechanisms, increased community engagement and timely addressing of the complaints and providing feedback to the refugees.

1 - Identified Needs

The nutrition status has significantly improved in the Cox Bazar refugee camps since the 2017 influx. The prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) decreased from 18.2% (2017) to 11.1 % (2019). However, the situation is still categorized as a serious state of acute malnutrition as per the WHO classifications (GAM 11-14%). Chronic malnutrition rates are above the WHO threshold of <30% and anaemia prevalence continues to be high with rates of 39% among children 6-59 months and above 55% for children 6-23 months (standard <40%). IYCF-e indicators are still poor with exclusive breastfeeding at 63%, access to minimum acceptable diet at 44% and complementary feeding of less than 50% across all camps. In 2019 a nutrition causal analysis (NCA) was carried out in the newly established camps in Kutupalong and Nayapara registered camps to determine the factors contributing to the levels of malnutrition among children under five years. The findings identified inadequate food, limited dietary intake, poor maternal and childcare practices, early marriages and poor WASH services as the factor’s contributing to high anaemia prevalence and acute and chronic malnutrition in the camps. UNHCR will support the development of the multisectoral action plan to address the risk factors identified in the NCA in order to improve the health and nutrition status of the refugees living in the camps. The nutritional needs of older adults and other vulnerable categories such as children above five years, medical cases and other specific needs, will be considered and supported as per the sector guidelines. Continued capacity building of the nutrition staff will be strengthened to ensure implementation of the nutrition services as per the protocols to ensure good quality of care and overall outcomes and the overall nutritional wellbeing of the beneficiaries. Integration of nutrition services with the other sectors such as health, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), food security, WASH, protection, cash based interventions (CBI) and livelihoods will be prioritized to ensure a collective response in addressing the high levels of malnutrition in the refugee camps. UNHCR is supporting the GOB in strengthening localization of the service provision through engagement of national NGOs and is working towards scaling up the number of nutrition facilities. In 2020, progress of implementation was greatly affected by the COVID-19 pandemic. Restrictions led to the suspension of the majority of the community based programmes, modification of the treatment of acute malnutrition, IYCF programmes and the distribution modality of the BSFP for children under five years as approved by the ISCG. In 2021 UNHCR and the nutrition partners will continue to implement the nutrition programmes within the evolving context in the operation – such as the COVID-19 pandemic, changing Government policies, and contingency planning for any new activities and the monsoon response. Scaling up of the mother-led MUAC concept will be done and evaluated to ensure sustainability. The total targeted number of beneficiaries of UNHCR funded nutrition programmes is 170,227 across 14 refugee camps. This includes an estimated 71,227 children under five years, 46,426 adolescent girls and 36,786 pregnant and lactating mothers as per the sectors needs analysis.

2 - Activities or outputs

Objective 1: Community based screening, detection and referral of acutely malnourished children aged 6-59 months to the nutrition centre. Inpatient management of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications in the stabilization centre. Implementation of outpatient therapeutic feeding program (OTP) and supplementary feeding program (SFP) for the treatment of severe and moderate acutely malnourished children respectively.Objective 2: Implementation of the blanket supplementary feeding program for the under five (U5) children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Implementation of infant and young child feeding programmes and community management of mothers at risk and infants 0-6 months (CMAMI). Implementation of adolescent nutrition programmes IFA supplementation and counselling. Nutrition support to the new arrivals in the transit centre and the quarantine centres for the families affected by COVID-19. Objective 3: Conduct the annual nutrition survey based on SENS/SMART guidelines to strengthen effective nutrition emergency response planning, implementation, and monitoring and come up with recommendations to support nutrition programming.
