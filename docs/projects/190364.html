Project Summary

The activities proposed under this program will provide SGBV Prevention and Response support to approximately 45,340 of the most vulnerable people in West Wollega, East Wollega and Kellem Wollega in Oromia, and Kamashi in Benishangul Gumuz. Action Against Hunger will address the acute protection needs by providing a holistic response on SGBV through support to health and social services, and through community engagement and women empowerment in conflict-affected communities including IDPs, returnees and host communities.
Specifically, this project aims to support vulnerable groups whose needs have become priorities in areas affected by violence, unrest and security issues, frequent displacement or secondary displacement, limited basic services, poor reintegration and worsening by the prevalence of COVID-19 and its restrictions and the impact of the war. Action Against Hunger will build on its presence and integration with government agencies to prevent and address SGBV cases, improve psychosocial wellbeing and protect the most vulnerable groups – particularly pregnant and lactating women, women, older men with disabilities, adolescents and young girls, and SGBV survivors – while encouraging participation by the wider community including men, older adults and community leaders. In addition, the project will focus on strengthening systems and improving capacities of government service providers, particularly in health care facilities.
This complex humanitarian emergency manifested by on-going fighting in these areas, secondary displacement, lack of strong reintegration processes and poor co-existence of IDPs are exacerbating the population's negative coping strategies, including unsafe coping behaviours and practices, resulting in higher rates of mortality and morbidity especially among the most vulnerable. Through this project, Action Against Hunger will address these urgent protection needs and build capacity of local government, national and local stakeholders to raise awareness, encourage readiness in provide basic lifesaving services and build community resilience. Action Against Hunger has been working well in this complex region and established strong relationships with local government, mindful of the need to balance a multi-sectoral response that considers the needs of IDPs and the host community – an important aspect in promoting peacebuilding and equality between the communities.
Under this project, Action Against Hunger's overall goal is to reduce SGBV and improve the quality of response by supporting active community and local institutions engagement on SGBV prevention and response activities. This will be achieved through: 1) Provision of Basic training on SGBV and PSS which will be provided for Persons of Concern (including those key influencers selected from community of concern), 2) Provision of Capacity building training to health care staff working in two zones Hospitals and Health Centers on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) as per WHO Guidelines, 3) Establish and maintain Case Management and safe referral systems among health and other service providers, BoWCYA, Justice/legal, etc., 4) Organize and conduct awareness raising sessions SGBV, Mental Health, PSEA at nutrition sites, health facilities, community gatherings, etc., 5) Conduct Protection Monitoring, 6) Work with local health-care providers to strengthening and support One-Stop Centres to ensure SGBV survivor women and adolescent girls and boys have immediate and appropriate Essential medical service (CMR, etc.), 7) Support and strengthen economic empowerment of women, adolescent girls and Person with disability through IGA activity or Cash Assistance, 8) Establish and strengthen existing safe spaces for women and girls to provide Psychosocial support activities, 9) Targeted support for most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men with disabilities and 10) Dignity Kits for SGBV survivors, vulnerable individuals and women and adolescent girls of rep

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Action Against Hunger has a participatory approach to ensure active involvement of beneficiaries and local authorities at all levels of the project (from design to M&E). In order to ensure accountability for what the organization is doing, Action Against Hunger will conduct a series of consultation sessions with the local community including women, men, youth, religious leaders, influential community members, minority groups, people with disabilities and local administration to better understand their needs and to help the designing of interventions that are responsive to these needs. The design of this program will be made to reflect the cumulative learning acquired through the engagement of the population. Community representatives, beneficiaries and partners will also participate in project launching workshops and targeting process. Information about the project, target beneficiaries and beneficiary selection criteria, their entitlement, and distribution time and places will be shared through media that are accessible for every partner involved in the process.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Action Against Hunger with its commitment to be the voice of the most vulnerable and affected population, beneficiaries and communities are always consulted on how they would like to raise their concerns, and provide feedback about the program. Based on the community consultation, multiple channels of complaint and feedback mechanism will be promoted and established for this action. Besides to community appeal committee, hotline number, face to face discussions and regular focus groups discussion, toll free hotline will be used to seek the feedback and complaint of the community and partners about their satisfaction with the program and staff behaviour. The field team would collect and document the beneficiary’s feedback from the community using the standard tools. As soon as feedback and complaint received, the MEAL department will immediately liaise the issue with relevant staff and management team based on the type of category and sensitivity of the issues. According to the agreed closing data, the MEAL department and respective senior managers have followed the status of each case. It is also well understood that the NFI distribution are high potential areas where abuses and misconducts take place. In response to that, Action Against Hunger conducts remote monitoring after every NFI distribution /assistance to track if there is any abuse in all the NFI assistance process (explained more under the monitoring Section below). Action Against Hunger feedback and complaint mechanism considers potential dangers and risks to all parties and incorporates ways to prevent injury and harm. This includes ensuring confidentiality and addressing the possibility of retaliation against witnesses. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) - Action Against Hunger recognizes the rights of people everywhere to be treated with dignity, respect, and be protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. To uphold and promote the highest standards of ethical and professional conduct, all employees and representatives of the organisation, must adhere to related policies and procedures in place, including on-going training, induction and coordination with the internal Safeguarding Unit to ensure PSEA policies are enacted fairly and transparently throughout the mission and activities. PSEA awareness and its reporting mechanisms will be integrated in all program activities to ensure beneficiaries have the understanding, and means to report cases of abuse.
