Project Summary

As part of its education response, PUI is targeting vulnerable and underserved communities with a bundle of activities, with the overall objective of enhancing the access to quality education provided in protective and suitable learning environment for affected children, to ensure that they don’t become a lost generation. Indeed, with the protracted crisis approaching its twelfth year in Syria, an entire generation of children and youth is being subject to persistent lack of opportunities to access quality education. This situation could have long-term negative consequences, affecting children’s mental health and wellbeing and preventing them from developing in good and safe conditions, as well as gaining essential life skills and knowledge.
The proposed project in brief:
- Will target locations where high education needs are documented by Education Sector.
- The cost per beneficiary is 40$ for remedial classes, 40-50$ for Self-Learning Program, 60$ for Early Childhood education (which covers incentives for teachers, stationery for the ECE cycle, children kits and furniture for the ECE space), 10$ for awareness sessions, 10$ for back to learning campaigns, 170$ for teachers’ capacity building and 100,000$ for the school rehabilitation activity as the works will vary to address all the needs including addressing protection, safety, WASH and electricity issues and might include installing a solar power system. The cost per beneficiary is around 104.455$ (Calculated by dividing the total budget by the total number of people the project will target.)
- It is estimated that around 679 individuals of targeted beneficiaries may have disabilities where all concerned teachers and educational staff members will attend specialized sessions related to child protection inclusive education and how to deal with CwDs, PUI will ensure to include at least 1 CwD in each established children club. Furthermore, these children will be referred to relevant stakeholders based on their needs.
This project aims at encouraging out of school students and dropped-out students to resume their education through tailored back to learning campaigns and empowering those children with self-learning program conceived as transition programs to enable them to catch-up and reintegrate the formal education system after this transition period. In addition, PUI will assist children in their education by providing them with kits,
education supplies and remedial classes to cover for any gaps and ensure every child’s educational needs are met and to prevent them from dropping out of school. Furthermore, PUI will contribute to enhancing the quality of education by providing capacity building activities to teachers and MoE staff, training them on active learning, PSS, and life skills. PUI will also initiate parenting awareness campaigns for parents and caregivers in order to provide guidance on parenting best practices and how to deal with different situations and age groups, in addition to raising their awareness regarding children’s rights and their importance for the children’s future. Lastly, in parallel to the abovementioned activities, PUI will ensure that children have access to safe and protective learning environment where they can access quality education by rehabilitating damaged schools in targeted locations.

3 - Needs

According to the HNO 2022 draft, more than 7.6 million children between the age of 3 and 17 and in need for education assistance. The protracted nature of the conflict, and economic duress continue to impact overstretched education services. With 25 verified attacks on schools and seven verified instances of military use of schools in nine months, attacks affecting schools remain high. Self-reported attendance at national level is 82 per cent. Economic factors and negative coping mechanisms, including children working to support the household, continue to be a key reason why children are not attending school as most often education services are not flexible enough to accommodate children working to support their households. In addition to distance traveled, children face potential risks when traveling to school which may limit access to education services. At national level, the data reveals that 18 per cent are out of school and half of the children who are not in school have never enrolled. Also, the formal system is unable to absorb and retain all school age children. Many students continue to learn in unconducive and/or unsafe learning environments. At the national level there is an average of one operational classroom for every 54-school aged child. Furthermore, many children who are in school continue to have reduced learning time due to multiple shifts and school suspension due to health and security issues.

4 - Implementation of activities

In terms of organizational capacity, working in Syria since 2008, PUI directly implements integrated programs, including education and protection, livelihood, WASH, shelter and NFI components, with the overall objective of bringing the Population of Syria back to their initial standards of living, to foster self-sufficiency and to promote social cohesion. Through the implementation of this integrated response, PUI gained good acceptance in its areas of intervention and increased the positive impact of the interventions implemented. PUI always tends to create synergies between its interventions; the proposed intervention will therefore complement other interventions implemented integrated areas. The design of the response is based on consultation with key informants from local stakeholders and the targeted communities to design the best way of implementation without leaving any one behind. Also, PUI introduced several tools to ensure monitoring of implementation at all stages of project. Moreover, coordination at field level with local stakeholder, humanitarian partners and Education sector is ensured by the Local representative officers to share knowledge and to avoid duplication of provided services. Furthermore, PUI coordinators work closely with other sectors when needed. Lastly, PUI education and protection staff directly monitor the activities while the independent M&E team conduct regular monitoring visits and evaluate the impact of eachactivity

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Security situation deteriorates and learning facilities in some locations became insecure. (Likelihood: Low. Impact: High) Mitigation measures: Assess the situation in targeted areas before the intervention; Safety protocols are set in the learning facilities; Raise awareness about existing protection and health service providers where victims can seek help. Risk: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to inequality of services provided. (Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High) Mitigation measures: Perform conflict analysis before planning intervention; Try to provide equal services in areas where conflict exists; Ensure local community anticipations to ensure the buy in and build element of success. Risk: Incidents of child labour, youth marginalization & GBV may take place. (Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High) Mitigation measures: Raise the awareness on existing service providers where victims can seek help; Enforce policies with/through all partners to prevent such incidents; Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train staff on code of conduct; Address any SEA complaints.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Children are exposed to harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities. (Likelihood: low. Impact: High) Mitigation measures: Raise the awareness on existing service providers where victims can seek help; Enforce policies with/through all partners to prevent children exposure to harm; Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train teachers and other involved personnel on positive discipline; Train staff on code of conduct; address any SEA complaints. PUI has well-established PSEA policy and repotting methods for any breaches.PUI has a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse, and is committed to implementing the six principles adopted in 2012 by the Inter Agency StandingCommittee Task Force on preventing and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse.Therefore, PUI has put an alert mechanism in place to allow anyone to come forwardto report any incident or abuse. Furthermore, and in line with this policy, a specificcomplaint-reporting mechanism for beneficiaries is put in place by the MEAL team through a dedicated hotline.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1)No curfew or lockdown imposed by government due to pandemic or security situation 2)Acceptance of local population for PUI´s interventions remain high 3)Humanitarian access to the proposed locations continues 4)Approvals for the proposed activities are obtained 5)No mass movements of populations into/out of intervention areas 6)No major fluctuation in exchange rates Risks: 1)lockdown or curfew due to pandemic or unstable security situation > Mitigation measures: 1- PUI will keep good coordination with the concerned stakeholders to anticipate such measures. 2)Lack of accountability creates high unmet expectations > Mitigation measures: PUI will ensure the full engagement of the community leaders and local stakeholders in project design and well introduce the benefits to the targeted community. 2)Aids is diverted to armed groups > Mitigation measures: 1- Intervention areas are assessed and perceived safe by PUI and its stakeholders; 2- PUI has the capacity to follow the full process of activities implementation; 3- PUI increases its capacity of visibility by selecting activities where direct monitoring is possible. 4) Variations in exchange rate impact local procurement > Mitigation measures: PUI times procurements in line with exchange rate trends and will reduce scope of work.
