Project Summary

Somalia and its people continue to reel from the consequences of years of armed conflict, violence, and recurrent climatic shocks. According to the 2020 Somalia humanitarian response report, nearly 6.3 million people were acutely food insecure in December 2019, and over 2.5 million individuals needed to access essential health and nutrition services. In 2021, 1.3 million boys and girls and pregnant and lactating women will suffer from high acute malnutrition, with 180,000 children under 5 affected by life threatening severe malnutrition, thus requiring emergency nutrition services. Children under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women and girls (PLWGs) are among the most vulnerable people in the event of humanitarian crises, being particularly susceptible to micronutrient deficiencies and risk of malnutrition in the first 1,000 days of life. The proposed intervention will scaleup the provision of Nutrition services in Benadir, Galmudug and Hiiran regions for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and vulnerable host communities. The project will support OTP, TSFP and SC centres in Benadir, Galmudug Hiiraan regions with an aim of targeting 33,025 people.
Integrated essential lifesaving preventive and curative services will be provided through outpatient therapeutic and supplementary feeding programs in addition one stabilization centre in Galmudug. The IRC aims to increase access to quality Nutrition services for children under five and pregnant and lactating women in host and IDP communities. IRC is currently supporting integrated healthcare and nutrition services in Dharkenley, Howl-wadag, Kaaran and Deynille in Benadir, Beletweyn in Hiiran and Dhusamareb and Galkacyo in Galmudug region. Under the proposed program, services will be provided through 2 static facilities and 4 outreach mobile teams in the targeted regions. CHWs will conduct community education and IYCF promotion activities. the proposed intervention will address immediate needs i.e. mitigating the spread of COVID-19 and supporting the public health consequences of the pandemic in target locations including Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) including awareness raising through media and training more community health workers on case definitions as well as to scale up hygiene response in the targeted areas.
Creating access to services: The facilities and mobile ooutreaches will receive nutrition supplies (RUTF, RUSF) and PPE supplies.
Ensuring quality of services: The IRC will continue to work to improve the nutrition status for the selected areas in Benadir, Hiiraan and Galmudug regions to the most vulnerable and marginalized communities.
Increasing demand for services: This will be done through nuttion promotion, community engagement (IYCF). The IRC will continue the community outreach components which will strength the work for Community health workers for health and nutrition education messages through community awareness group sessions while exercising social distancing principles and others including MUAC screening and referrals at the community level. The IRC will also continue building the capacities and skills for the CHWs to enable community health staff to provide a rapid, effective response in case of a health emergencies disaster like in the current situation, COVID-19. The CHWs will work to dispel rumors, fears and misconceptions received/heard in the community about COVID-19 using the existing community structures and channels of influence, including religious leaders and other respected community leaders. IRC will provide the CHW staffs trainings related to infection prevention control and EWARN. In collaboration with IRC WPE and Gender equality section IRC will organize session for the frontline health workers in regards of training of clinical care of sexual assault survivors/ GBV survivors and clinical management of rape, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).

This is one of the forms of getting feedback from the beneficiaries in order to improve quality of health services.

Mitigation Measures

Ensure robust community engagement through sensitization session with all community segments encouraging communities to adopt inclusive representation in community committees and groups.Establish inclusive selection criteria that provides especial emphasis to women, children, people with special needs/disability and marginalized groups.Strengthen the IRC client response mechanism to provide enough room for community engagement to prevent lack or poor inclusion of women, children, people with special needs/disability and marginalized groups in the project.Provide regular awareness raisings at community and during community outreach activities to improve community role in the project activity implementations. The IRC’s client responsive feedback and complaints mechanisms, which are designed through multiple channels to be anonymous and accessible to all groups, including women, girls, IDPs and people with disabilities, collect and analyse data on clients’ perspectives on the quality and relevance of project interventions, and are used to inform timely responses, guide decision-making, and ensure that the program does not negatively impact the communityThe IRC implements and monitors programs through the inclusion of clients and other stakeholders in the feedback mechanism on its delivery performance. Room will be given to clients to report any IRC related misuse of resources, lack of effectiveness, timeliness or responsiveness. A communication structure is in place in collaboration with Complaints Response Mechanism and includes guidance on how to handle complaints and report them to the IRC Management. The community health committees established will serve as forums where feedback on program implementation can be shared and ideas on further improving activities generated. An exit interview will be conducted within the project period to get the feedback from the communityThe main barriers to accessing healthcare in these areas include lack of quality health services including lack of emergency obstetric and neonatal care and minimal staff capacity. In addition to the above, the recurrent conflicts have disrupted routine health programs leaving a large sector of the population without access to health services. Hence IRC will recruit and engage qualified service providers to provide services and outreach responses that create more access to health services.The proposed project supports the gender theme in that it promotes health service provision to all sections of the population including men, women, girls, and boys, without discriminationBuild strong working relationships with respect local authority and line ministries to ensure no potential influence that can alter the project design and resource allocation among target areas.Maintain the employment of the IRC values and transparent mechanisms during the implementation to suppress stakeholder expectationsBuild close collaboration and working relationships with the IDP and host communities to ensure fair project resource distribution based on participative selection criteria that informs the vulnerabilities.
