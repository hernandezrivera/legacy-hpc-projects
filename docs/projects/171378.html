Project Summary

The proposed project will work towards increasing access to basic and specialized services for the most affected population of people with disabilities living in area C of the West Bank, besides persons with injuries due to great return of March (GRM) in Gaza by providing multidisciplinary rehabilitation services; through outreach and in home services, including but not limited to, basic chronic rehabilitation services, post-operative and psychological services. Due to COVID19 crisis, the proposed project will adopt the tele-rehabilitation and self-rehabilitation management support, besides activation of operational partners' centers under COVID19 measures that will definitely include transported beneficiaries to those centers. Moreover, the proposed project will aim at covering the non-rehabilitation (medical and mental health) interventions through specialized referrals to private contracted medical centers at the project targeted governorates. That shall come with COVID19 key awareness messages at the community level as well. The package of services will be based on pre-identification and assessment of individuals' needs by the combination of well trained and skilled rehabilitation professionals. The project will promote accessibility through the implementation of home adaptation for the beneficiaries who developed permanent disabilities. This ultimately will improve community participation for the targeted population through progressing of their independency, and regain well recovery. The project will support preparedness efforts via prepositioning of assistive devices and hygiene kits in terms of crisis eruption. At the level of prevention, and detection the project will increase the awareness raising on disability mainstreaming for healthcare professionals. In addition, to increase mobile teams’ competencies on Gender-based Violence (GBV) detection including boosting referrals mechanism for persons with disabilities to increase their access to the GBV services required as per their identified needs and demands. Nonetheless, the project will work on a holistic approach to ensure proper and better access to other needs beyond project deliverables for the targeted beneficiaries through the coordination mechanism of local referral networks and in particular by the supporting of the rehabilitation taskforce, that will ensure standardizing of rehabilitation protocols and information sharing among actors. Moreover, the proposed project shall contribute to community awareness on COVID19 crisis through dissemination of key educational awareness messages via social media tools, national radio channels and besides visual materials production. This shall come in coordination with MoH for active cases, and collaboration with community-based rehabilitation (CBR) programs in Gaza and West Bank.

1 - Needs

HI conducted three main assessments in the course of 2020. 1) A Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA-March 2020), 2) In-depth sectorial assessment (DSA – August 2020); both conducted in the Gaza Strip. In addition 3) a Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA – April 2020) in West Bank.In Gaza, whereas the assessments show extensive and multifaceted needs and gaps, the following priorities prevail: 1) The need to ensure continuous provision of multidisciplinary rehabilitation services in order to cover the gaps among people with disabilities and persons with injuries (past and new cohort). The majority of the participants of the RNA highlighted the need to receive long-run physiotherapy and occupational therapy treatment and assistive devise provision; the relevancy to receive psychosocial services, particularly for children with disabilities and their families. They also stressed the importance to enhance affordability of the rehabilitation service provision, as medications and assistive devices result expensive. Transportation cost represents an additional burden. The RNA flagged also the need to access information of available services and receiving a proper referral. In the aftermath of the Great March of Return (GMR), coupled with the overwhelming COVID-19 situation, hospitals along the Strip are not able to provide the required comprehensive care and rehabilitation for persons with injuries and person with disabilities. The lack of support could increase the susceptibility to disability; therefore, it is crucial that services are provided to ensure the continuation of care (trauma pathway) and recovery. 2) The need to strengthen the coordination among rehabilitation actors: the DSA study revealed a defect in coordination among different implementing actors. Although efforts are under way, the need to distribute the caseload of patients to the various actors and to avoid duplication and triplication of service is still valid. The Patient Allocation Tool (PAT), which allows tracking trauma patients at the postoperative and rehabilitation stages, is still not properly used, nor used from all. Part of coordination efforts should also aim towards the improvement of a unified referral mechanisms. Currently, the referral network lacks of feedback system, of adequate referrals documentation as unified referral form and sometimes, long list of referrals brings to long waiting time that could cause further complications for the patients. On top of these faults, proper of the referral system itself, one of the main concerns by people with disabilities and persons with injuries was the affordability of the service, in particular the transportation cost to benefit from centers' service provision. Long-term rehabilitation treatments for some conditions is necessary to ensure quality and good level of recovery. Finally, even once the referral has succeeded; there are concerns about the quality of the service. 3) The need for Mental Health and Psycho Social Support (MHPSS). A factsheet produced by disability working group (DWG) - August 2020, reports that COVID19 pandemic has increased the difficulties facing persons with disabilities particularly in the Gaza Strip, and made their conditions more complicated. In West Bank, during the confinement, and through its partner BASR did a rapid assessment for people with disabilities in the Southern areas, having the following findings: the community workers registered 1590 urgent needs for 647 persons with disabilities in Bethlehem and Hebron. 538 medical supplies and consumables required for 363 persons with disabilities. 418 needs for sanitizers and hygiene materials registered for 244 persons with disabilities. 38 needs for mental health for 38 persons with disabilities. 70 needs for medicines for 68 persons with disabilities.

2 - Activities

Project Outcome: Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health, and rehabilitation services to vulnerable communities and reduce vulnerability of persons with injuries and persons with disabilities; especially women, children and elderly. Output 1: Persons with injuries and people with disabilities have increased independence through equitable access to basic health, rehabilitation and psychosocial services by the multidisciplinary outreach and center-based services in line with the continuum of care. 1.1: Provision of multidisciplinary rehabilitation services using center-based, homebased and/or remote tele-rehabilitation to 1,300 people with disabilities and 200 persons with injuries who have limited access to services due to outbreak of pandemic, in Gaza and West Bank. 1.2: Training and follow up of at least 1,500 family members and caregivers (1,000 females and 500 males) on basic rehabilitation therapeutic exercises to support persons with disabilities and persons with injuries in Gaza and West Bank. 1.3: Purchase and donation of 500 assistive devices for the targeted beneficiaries as per their identified needs in Gaza and West Bank. 1.4: Purchase and prepositioning of 400 assistive devices, and 400 hygiene kits to support affected population of persons with disabilities and persons with injuries in any crisis in Gaza. 1.5: Purchase medical materials and nursing disposables (150 kits) to support post-operative care and wound dressing services for persons with disabilities and persons with injuries in Gaza. 1.6: Support independency through reasonable accessible works for 100 houses for persons with disabilities in West Bank and Gaza to enhance mobility and daily life activities. 1.7: Technical support to partners’ rehabilitation teams though monthly technical trainings, quarterly exchange meetings and monitoring field visits in Gaza and West Bank. Output 2: Support the coordination efforts through the rehabilitation taskforce to ensure continuum of healthcare through referrals and sharing information. 2.1: Support standardize rehabilitation intervention and boost referrals for persons with injuries and persons with disabilities in Gaza. 2.2: Provide technical training to rehabilitation mobile teams on early disability detection and referral in collaboration with protection cluster (GBV working group) in West Bank targeted governorates. 2.3: Conduct awareness raising sessions on disability mainstreaming in collaboration with disability working group (DWG) in Gaza targeted governorates and in coordination with BASR in West Bank targeted governorates. 2.4: Provision of financial support to vulnerable people in need of acute center-based health and rehabilitation services in Gaza and West Bank targeted governorates. Output 3: Support the urgent needs of affected population to mitigate COVID19 impact through the provision of essential materials and awareness campaigns. 3.1: Increase public awareness on spotting the signs and symptoms of COVID19 using different media; through development and dissemination of accessible prevention/ hygiene messages for community including people with disability using social media and community volunteers. 3.2: Coordination with community-based rehabilitation (CBR) programs and organizations of people with disability to support the dissemination of preventive accessible messages for community. 3.3: Support referral mechanisms to beneficiaries suspected having CoVID19 to MoH and MHPSS services following the existing referral pathways. 3.4: Provide essential hygiene and disinfectant materials for families and people at higher risk of being affected by COVID19 in community and quarantine centers. 3.5: Support partner’s rehabilitation centers with PPEs to ensure the continuity of service provisions to people with disabilities and chronic diseases.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The ‘Do No Harm’ principle has been considered at different levels in relation with both the services and the individuals, by considering gender differences, by promoting local community ownership of the project through local partnerships. A psychologist will be part of the rehabilitation service team and will be trained to adequately answer psychological needs of affected persons. The tools used for the need’s assessment will be tailored to suit both Gaza and West Bank contexts and specificity of persons with injuries and people with disabilities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will be implemented in support of local organizations within partnerships agreements. HI has developed strong local partnerships supporting access to beneficiaries. Some criteria of partners’ selection were the location of partner’s centers in order to cover the identified areas. The local partners' teams will allow access to remote areas and will be recruited within their communities, ensuring though fine understanding of the context and public acceptance.
