Project Summary

The project will focus on enhancing household food and nutrition security, and self-reliance of the poorest and vulnerable households of both the host and refugee communities. The targeted households will get support for food production in their homestead with innovative multi-layer technologies. The activity will cover vegetable and fruit production in the homestead with low irrigation. The project will promote self-reliance of the host and refugee communities with selected youth female beneficiaries who will receive skills training on the targeted trade and will be engaged with wage-earning opportunities, e.g. cloth making, mask, sanitary pad, and jute bag production.
In both communities, more than 80% of the targeted beneficiaries will be female. The project will work for empowering the women through engaging them in production and wage-earning and develop market linkage. The project will complement the Government’s Social Safety-net programme by providing monthly allowances to the elderly, women, and person with a disability. Overall, the project will raise awareness of the local institutions and communities for improving social cohesion.
UNHCR emphasizes the need for synergy between livelihoods and other sectors in order to bolster livelihood interventions and support to the host community. Also key is the collaboration with other UN agencies, the World Bank, the Asian Development Bank, and stakeholders (national and international NGOs), to ensure effective use of resources and support advocacy. The refugee crisis represents multiple challenges that require a comprehensive solutions strategy. While efforts to create conditions in Myanmar for safe, dignified and sustainable return will continue, their humanitarian needs, rights and well-being must be met in Bangladesh. It will also be important to address the needs of host communities. UNHCR will continue dialogue with the Government of Bangladesh on durable solutions and will take a comprehensive and balanced approach that benefits both refugees and host communities.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya refugees remain highly vulnerable (REVA 2020, MSNA) and 100% dependent on humanitarian assistance. Since April 2020, responding to COVID-19 outbreak, UNHCR has adopted prevention and risk mitigation measures in programme implementation modality and continued intervention both in the host community and camps. However, FSS partners activities in the camps halted or slowed down due to movement restriction. Considering the host community, Cox’s Bazar District remains one of the poorest in Bangladesh, making it highly susceptible to environmental and economic shocks such as cyclones, monsoons, and COVID-19/health pandemics. The Government of Bangladesh (GoB) states that 17% of people live below poverty line across Cox’s Bazar on average and 57% of poor individuals do not receive safety net support (GoB, December 2019) and according to CARE and World Bank, 33% of the population in the district live below the poverty line (ACAPS, September 2020). The pandemic and its subsequent containment measures impacted food security and livelihoods, exacerbating existing poverty in the host communities (ISCG 04/2020; WFP 04/2020; WFP 05/2020; BBS, 2011). Shortages and unavailability of agricultural inputs due to supply chain disruptions and movement restrictions amid the lockdown, lead to a hike in the cost of inputs and shortages of labour (FAO, June 2020). Due to COVID 34% of Rohingya women and 43% of Rohingya men reported that they or their family had completely lost their income due to containment measures, while 36% and 39% of Rohingya women and men respectively reported that containment measures limited their income. In the host community, 84% and 57% of host community women and men respectively reported containment measures had limited their livelihoods activities, and 62% and 61% of host community women and men reported having completely lost their income (ISCG, October 2020).

2 - Activities or outputs

Nutritious vegetable production through vertical gardening technology. Skill based self-reliance intervention for ensuring wages. Ultra-Poor Graduation (UPG) programme to uplift the poverty of the most vulnerable. Agriculture-based livelihood support to the marginalised households affected by the influx.Create and strengthen market linkages between supply and demand for all livelihoods initiatives in the host community.Agricultural training for marginal and small farmers to increase food production. Establish and promotion of aggregation centers (AC).Reinforce efforts to support vulnerable Bangladeshis impacted as a result of influx through supporting food and unconditional cash assistance in response to shocks and to support GoB social safety nets.Advocacy activities with GoB and development actors on development-oriented approaches to support refugees and host community.
