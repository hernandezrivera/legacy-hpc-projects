Project Summary

This Project seek to meet the emergency and Primary health care needs of vulnerable and Internally displaced population in 5 Counties, with focus on post conflict movements in Raga, Aweil West, Bor South , Twic East and Duk counties, meeting significant gaps in affected areas targeting 16,667 beneficiaries, segregated as 7,928 Males,8,739 Females 3,782 Boys and 42,97 Girls. Our focus will be Increasing access to quality integrated lifesaving Primary Health Care, Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases and Integrate Wash/Nutrition/food security and protection and strengthen the capacities of health services to reduce Morbidity/mortality of common diseases among the vulnerable groups. This project will improve the health status of the IDPs and the Host communities through provision of effective and Equitable basic Primary and emergency health care services in form of PHCCs, PHCUs and Mobile units to reach hard to reach areas, Supporting the most vulnerable group of rural communities to have access to improved health services.
The Primary health care package & the emergency services include: Screening, detection and management of malnutrition in Under 5 yrs, OPD consultation, Immunization Services, against Measles, Polio, and others childhood diseases, Maternal& newborn services (ANC) Reproductive health,distribution of Vit A, De-worming, Health Promotion AND Education and awareness raising of the availability for SGBV survivors, TB and HIV and campaign mobilization and diseases Surveillance. Our Humanitarian response is to support a chain of 10 Health facilities (4 PHCC/3PHCUs and 3Mobiles Units) in the 5 targeted Counties. Bor South County: 1PHCC/1 mobile Unit, Duk County 1PHCC/1PHCU/1mobile, Twice East 1PHCU, Aweil West 1PHCCs and Raga County with 1PHCC/1PHCU and 1Mobile units)

1 - Needs

The combined effect of ongoing conflict, increased Inter-communal violence in some areas and heavy rains leading to major floods in South Sudan continues to devastate the lives of affected communities, with major effect on displaced person and vulnerable group such as women, children and disable people. SMC proposes to implement emergency lifesaving interventions in five Counties of Jonglei, Western Bahr al ghazal and Northern Bahr al ghazal state in the year 2020. Owing to the high mortality rate of under 5 years (90.7/1000), maternal mortality rate (789/100,000) An estimated 6.4 million people in South Sudan, approximately 54 percent of the national population of 11.7 million People faced crisis IPC3 or worse levels of acute food insecurity and required urgent food assistance in August according to an IPC analysis. The 1.3 million, being the highest recorded figure of malnutrition in under 5 years since Independence of South Sudan in 2011 is just the tip of current situation in the country. Based on September 11 issued IPC AMN, all the 79 counties were included in the analysis of which 58 counties are classified as IPC AMN Phase 3 and above (GAM prevalence above 9.9%). An estimated 292 300 children are suffering or will likely be suffering by December from severe acute malnutrition. Of them, some 10% are expected to develop associated medical complications. (WHO Report- published 18 Oct.2019) – In August 2019, Former Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 1.25 million people, Our target Population for the project is 16,667 People with Jonglei Counties representing 65 % of the population (10895) and Greater Bahr Ghazal state with 35 % (5772 ) of the population. the segregation by Counties is as follows: ( Duk county target is 5,029 (F:2658 ,M:2,371) IPC phase 4, Bor South with 3,353 (F: 1,777 M:1576) and Twice East (2,513) IPC phase 3, while other part of the country faces the same situation with Aweil West targeted being 2513 (F:1305, M: 1208) people an area classified as IPCH phase 4, while raga in Western Bahr al Ghazal is classified as IPC AMC phase 3, with a targeted of 3259( F1694, M:1565). The Prevailing health system challenges are exacerbated by the lack of well-trained health personnel, poor drinking water Quality, infection control and prevention, waste management, Expansive malnutrition, which necessitate holistic integrated responses to ensure health securities to mitigate diseases spread and enhance beneficiaries. Additionally, humanitarian access has improved during this period, further contributing to the increased access to food, nutrition and livelihood support, as well as service delivery. However, acute food insecurity persists, driven by localized conflicts, climatic shocks, pests and diseases; Inadequate provision of essential healthcare services due to inadequate funding led to inadequate access to improved services such as RH, MPSS and Low Immunization coverage services. The insecurity, unfavorable weather conditions such as floods current have cause displacement and loss of assets and food stocks and dry spells; instability and diminished activity in the markets as a result of degraded infrastructure because of the rains are likely to cause food shortages and lead to an increase in food prices; limited employment and income generating activities in the current context of economic collapse will also limit households’ consistent ability to access adequate food. Some of the current location are cholera hotspot ( Duk) for cholera outbreak and will require constant surveillance and increase level of service deliveries, likewise Bor, twice East, Aweil and Raga, areas with sizeable IDPs and vulnerable host communities with need of health Nutrition and sanitation facilities, will require our robust response in form of integrated services by opening 4 PHCCs, 3 PHCUs and 3 mobiles Unit to ensure hard to reach areas.
