Project Summary

This concept note analyzes the gaps/needs in Akobo, Kajo Keji and Morobo counties and projects the requirements to respond to critical health service for 67,529 returnees, IDPS and host community in these areas. The total budget required to reach this population is 3,444,000 at 36.5 to 65.2 USD per capita with an average of USD 51 per capita. This costing conforms to the health cluster cost per capita of 36.5 USD for populations in remote areas and 65.2 for populations in IDP camps for all selected clinical packages; PHCC, PHCU and mobile teams (SSD Costing model, 2017). All the intervention areas for this response (Akobo, Kajo Keji & Morobo) are remote and host IDPS.
The selected counties are experiencing Acute Food Insecurity situation at IPC phase 5 (5% in Akobo) and IPC phase 4 (Kajo Keji and Morobo).
As guided by the health cluster HRP strategy 2021, the project will address the following health intervention areas.
1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases.
2. Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems and
3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly.
The proposed implementation methodology uses a mix of static and mobile teams to increase population coverage. These will include; 1 PHCC, 1 PHCU and 2 mobile teams in Akobo East county; 1 PHCC , 3 PHCUs and 1 mobile team in kajo Keji county and 1 PHCC, 1 PHCU and 1 mobile team in Morobo county.
The population targeting is based on severity of vulnerability and will include; 26200 (39%) boys and girls under 5 years, 20529 (30%) boys and girls above 5 years, 13400 (20%) boys and girls 18 years and 7400 (11%) the elderly. It projected that 49% of the beneficiaries will be men and boys and 51% will be women and girls.
The activities planned for this response include;
• Outbreak investigations
• OPD consultations
• Case management for common diseases
• Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases
• Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) (ANC, Skilled deliveries and PNC)
• Clinical management of rape,
• Mental health and psychosocial support
• Capacity building,
• Infection control and prevention and other activities as guided by the basic package for health and nutrition services, specific cluster strategy and resource envelop.
The Rescue Initiative-South Sudan commits to provide regular updates through the financial tracking system (FTS) on any resources secured to support this response plan.

1 - Needs

Local population projections for Akobo, Kajo Keji and Morobo counties indicate that their populations are increasing. A biometric registration exercise in Akobo East County conducted by IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix in 2020 shows a total of 41,644 individuals (8,390 households). While movement is also observed from Akobo to Ethiopia, it appears most of the population are resident and cross the border for trading purposes. According to REACH 2020 report, 365 individuals cross from Ethiopia to Akobo on monthly bases giving an annual figure of 4,380.Akobo experiences numerous insecurity incidences related to inter-clan and inter-communal violence. Equally, flooding has intensified and persisted for most half of the year. DTM reports for September 2020 indicate a total of 10,745 individuals forced by flood to move to more elevated areas, with limited health services and lack of clean and safe water for human consumption. All these situations constrain the community’s capacity to produce and become self-sustaining further deteriorating the already dire food security situation.According to the recent IPC projections (December 2020-July 2021), 5% of the population of Akobo will likely face famine if no actions are taken to avert the situation.Kajo Keji and Morobo are in IPC phase 4 or emergency food security situation. The key drivers to vulnerability include insecurity, displacement of local population by cattle keepers, movement restrictions due to COVID-19 limiting cross border trade eventually affecting local markets.Latest reports suggest that despite the challenging environment in kajo Keji and Morobo, the refugee population in Uganda continue to return voluntarily. Unfortunately, with limited access to food and health care, their vulnerability continues to increase.The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) for Morobo 2020 shows a mix of population with a total of 1,474 IDPs and 2,355 returnees. While it is difficult to accurately estimate the population of kajo Keji due to frequent movements between the refugee camps in Uganda, local projections from the RRC show fluctuating figures from 60,000 people to 90,000 people.The health System indicators for health infrastructure, human resources for health, pharmaceutical supplies, health financing and governance are low indicating a low service functionality and thus low access to health services for all three counties.Already, WHO analysis (March, 2020) indicate that ARI trends are rising nationally with ARI proportional morbidity higher than 20%. Recent morbidity data for Akobo show high threat of epidemic prone disease like diarrheal diseases at 9%, Malaria at 26% and skin disease 12% (TRI-SS, IDSR, Morbidity data, September, 2020).In Kajo Keji, malaria is the leading morbidity at 70% followed by acute respiratory infections (ARIs), urinary tract infections (UTIs), acute watery diarrhea (AWD), skin infections, eye infections, injuries, peptic ulcers disease (PUD), fungal infections and worm infestations respectively (IRNA, March, 2020).In Morobo, the risk of importation of Ebola from Congo stands as the highest health threat in addition to COVID-19. Already, local reports suggest that all disease prevalence is about 75%, 70% being children. The most common infections include; malaria, diarrhea and acute respiratory infections.The national COVID-19 infection rate and deaths show a surge signifying a second wave. Latest data show a total of 6931 cases, 4014 recoveries and about 87 deaths. While this data is lower compared to the other countries in the region, the current food security situation poses a health risk with lower immunity and increased transmission of COVID-19 and the risk of death.However, the World Health Organization remains cautious in shifting resources based on current data saying that COVID-19 is not the leading morbidity. Malaria, diarrhea and Acute Respiratory infections (ARIs) remain the top morbidities and leading cause of death nationwide.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

We have a well established PSEA system and a dedicated focal person. Information about PSEA is provided during project inception meeting, community consultative meetings and project review meetings.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The plan will put in place strategies to ensure inclusivity. Feedback from individuals living with disabilities will be collected through scheduled interviews and exit interviews. Data collection tools will be translated to the local language, read or interpreted for these groups. We will utilize visual aids to encourage conversation in groups with disabilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The community feedback mechanism in designed in such a way that feedback collected from beneficiaries are analyzed and results presented to stakeholders and project implementer for their response. A compiled report containing responses from implementer are presented to the beneficiaries during community meetings as well as to their leaders during project review meetings.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries are analyzed and categorized to yield actionable points that can be used to modify or redesign the project

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

We have a dedicated GBV focal person and a PSEA officer. We also have a well established GBV referral pathway. We will improve on monitoring, reporting and management of cases of GBV including psychosocial support and clinical management of rape.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Our Food Security and Livelihood focal person is well experienced in Kitchen garden design and implementation. We will ensure all health facilities have kitchen gardens in place. During health talks, topics that encourage food production at the household level will be introduced.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will adopt a gender sensitive and inclusive programming. We will ensure health facility staff are oriented on such approaches ensuring there is no discrimination in service provision. People with special needs will be prioritized in times of need. All data will be dis-aggregated by age, sex and disability.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The rescue initiative South Sudan will use an integrated approach in health programming ensuring that nutrition surveillance is conducted at all health facilities and a proper referral system established. The project will support the treatment of children with severe acute malnutrition and medical complication at selected health facilities.
