Project Summary

This project aims to provide health services directly and indirectly to the affected People, IDPs and host communities,
Considering the area fragmentation in Syria due to the conflict, and the scattered health facilities; the support of an ambulatory system will allow a maximization of the remaining functional health resources to continue providing life-saving services to the patients, including:
1. Increasing the coverage scope of medical evacuation using the ambulatory system.
2. Enhancing the efficient utilization of the referral system through applying TRIAGE to hospitals for emergency cases only.
3. Enhancing the response speed by linking the ambulances’ systems with a communication network that would contribute to a better use of existing resources and increased coordination.
4. Empowering the health directorate in managing the referral system by sharing one communication network and the same operating room to avoid all types of duplication and overlapping.
The project aims to support running of existing ambulatory referral system with 5 ambulances and 10 paramedics & 10 drivers.
These ambulances will work in Hama in the most crowded areas with IDPs.
All ambulances are equipped now with all the medical equipment and devices needed for first aid and critical cases.
The ambulances will be distributed in 2 work branches in Rural od Hama and the nearby Rural of Idlib as the following:
Muhardah
(3 ambulances) it will serve Rurl of Hama communities.
Kafr Nobol
(2 ambulances) it will serve Kafr Nobol and its communities.
There is another Ambulatory system in Hama and Idleb now but it is working in totally different sub districts and communities and there is fully coordination with the health directorates in the need assessment and in the implementing of the project and distributing the ambulances among the communities.
There will be a main center of the system and we will use the same communication system with the health directorates to direct the ambulances to targeted areas or to respond to the calls of the hospitals to refer the patients.
The staff of each ambulance are 2 drivers with basic first-aid experience and 2 paramedics with advanced first aid experience.
The five ambulances will work daily 7 days a week and 24 hours a day.
The system will work to mobilize the critical patients from the field or between specialized hospitals or to transport critical patients to diagnostic centers and also to refer to the Turkish hospitals.
All services will be provided free of charge and there will be no discrimination based on age, gender or race.

Needs

The conflict in Syria resulted an extreme trauma and increased the number of disabilities. the largely hampered health system was unable to deal with this drastic effect of the conflict not only due to destroyed infrastructure but also due to an acute reduction in health professionals either due to direct targeting or departure for fear of being targeted. This has led to a lack of knowledge and skills required for dealing with complicated cases by the remaining health providers; and an acute reduction in the scope covered by remaining ambulatory services and life-saving services and system. This lack of knowledge and skills in ambulatory services has been one of the leading causes of high mortality and morbidity rate among people, especially among women, children and the elderly. the intensive conflict inside Syria and increased numbers of injuries among civilians, especially complicated cases, requires high levels of coordination between ambulatory system and health facilities. according to the 2017 reports, the number of beneficiaries from Sham Ambulatory system is 12339 2430 trauma cases 28 % 5172 other medical 59 % 242 deliveries cases 3 % 946 new born services 11% The needs assessment is done with engagement of local health directories in Idleb governorates, the project is highly needed and serving marginalized community with limited access.to health especially after the last influx of IDPs to Idleb governorate According to the information of health directorates in the North of Syria, still there are a gap in covering the needs of the referral system in the governorates of Hama and Idlib so this project will serve the non-covered areas with the ambulatory services based on a working plan from Hama health directorate.The importance of our project, which will provide professional service in the ambulances through a highly trained staff and through cars which are equipped with specialized medical devices to be able to address the critical and traumatic cases.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The availability of Maintenance & spare parts of ambulances.The availability of the qualified trainers. Minimal or no team change.The ambulance system using TRIAGE as the ideal criteria to sort injuries, clashes, air strikes, bombing, the targeted area, as the main reasons of the injuries in the area, whereas, in case of resettlement, the system will act as the standard first aid and referral systems.Availability of the current system including the personnel and the infrastructures is highly critical to operate. Risks:Escalation in hostilities in project location.Not enough qualified local staff.Low cooperation and competing NGOs.Market prices change continuously inside Syria, local currency fluctuates against USD.Core members leaving the project before completion.The invasion of the area leading to collapsed humanitarian and medical situation, besides the unavailability of the resources, human and infrastructure to operate the system, further, the disability to maintain the existed ambulances or replace it. Mitigation:AHF Health Program Manager will be responsible for the ongoing monitoring of the local security situation and will communicate with all personnel, any changes in the conflict which require reactionary activities to minimize disruption.Project proposal will thoroughly evaluate the existing availability of staff working within the ambulatory system and surrounding areas to ensure the proposal is realistic and salable. Continuous coordination with existing health structures and NGOs in that area, conducting a baseline survey to ensure service areas not covered by other agencies.Tendering in both Syria and Turkey.Using USD as main currency for project transactions.Allow a margin of price fluctuation in budget.
