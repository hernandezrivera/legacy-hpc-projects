Project Summary

This project is in line with WHO’s strategic shift to the Global Programme of Work (GPW-13) goals. This will take an integrated approach towards ensuring healthy lives and promoting well-being and the three strategic priorities of achieving UHC, addressing health emergencies and promoting healthier populations, while optimizing synergies between one another. This project will support integrated approach in building the capacity of the States to ensure continuity of essential health and nutrition services in an integrated manner to promote value for money and sustainability. WHO will progressively integrate health service delivery platforms into stronger local health systems by setting up a referral mechanism and increasing the capacity of the health system to cope with life-saving interventions and needs through the following activities:
• Organize a training of trainers for a pool of master trainers on in-patient management of SAM with medical complications in the BAY states. Through regular supportive supervision, WHO will collaborate with the State Ministries of Health to ensure that the knowledge and skills acquired during the training of trainers are applied to ensure delivery of quality nutrition services
• Promote an integrated approach for the management of SAM as a minimum package of maternal, newborn and child health (MNCH) services
• Strengthen government capacity on quarterly joint supportive supervision/monitoring of nutrition services
• Support the systematic implementation of the Nurturing Care Framework in health facilities to ensure that children are developmentally on track
• Provide access to comprehensive and high-impact health and nutrition services for people in areas with low/no health service coverage in the BAY states through the H2R mobile health teams(linked to functional PHCs) and CORPs/CHIPS delivery platforms. Under this approach, WHO will deliver extended basic and essential health packages in areas with low/no access. This will be through 80,000 sessions of systematic screening for malnutrition and referral of all identified cases to treatment centers while those at risk will be counseled through infant and young child feeding (IYCF) in addition to treatment for minor illnesses, vaccination, vitamin A supplementation and deworming of children. Nutrition screening is done by MUAC (mid-upper arm circumference) measurement using a simple coloured plastic strip, a quick and simple way to determine whether or not a child is malnourished. MUAC is suitable to use on children from the age of 12 months up to 59 months.
• Enhance nutrition surveillance, prevention and control across newly accessible areas and the conflict-affected LGAs in the BAY states. This will be through the expansion of the WHO Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS). EWARS deployment entails the restoration and stimulation of existing disease surveillance through a network of focal points and key informants to collect weekly report on a subset of 17 key diseases, including measles, malaria, cholera and SAM.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 HRP, There are 8.3 million people in need across the BAY states with 3,004,450 children under five in need life-saving nutrition services. The Nutrition sector targets 2,530,489 children, of these 831001 are IDPs; 848,867 returnees and 850,861 in host communities. Women and children are the most affected and those living at the reception centers are extremely vulnerable as they have not accessed nutrition services prior to their arrival at the reception sites. Children who are severely malnourished are 12 times more likely to die (all-cause of mortality) compared to a well-nourished child. A non-breastfed infant under 6 months is 14 times more likely to die from pneumonia/diarrhea than an exclusively breastfed infant <6months. According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 10 (NFSS X),the nutrition situation in Borno and Yobe State is classified as Phase 3 (Serious) and Adamawa at Phase 2 (Alert) level, with GAM levels of 14.1% in Yobe, 11.8% in Borno and 6.1% in Adamawa. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates continues to remain high in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states. The marked deterioration in the food security situation is linked to the upsurge of insecurity, COVID-19 related challenges, limited access to markets, farming and grazing land which are essential for livelihood opportunities. This situation has stretched remaining accessible communal resources, due to increased dependency from IDPs and returnees. The nutritional situation is also expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying issues including increased displacement and the impact of COVID 19 on the socioeconomic status. The levels of acute malnutrition among new arrivals from inaccessible areas for the month of October is Critical (Phase 4 IPC Acute Malnutrition Classification). The overall Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates were 22.0% and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) at 9.0%. The high levels of acute malnutrition indicate an extremely stressed population including food insecurity, poor water and sanitation access, and poor health conditions as the key underlying causes of acute Malnutrition.Children and women in hard-to-reach areas e.g. Magumeri, Kukawa, Kala/Balge and Gubio, host community areas with low humanitarian activities/presence and poor infrastructure development e.g. Yobe State and newly arrivals from inaccessible areas are more likely to suffer from acute malnutrition. Women in rural setting are less likely to exclusively breastfeed their infants less 6 months due to increased workload and lack of access to skilled IYCF counselling at health facilities. To reduce morbidity and mortality associated with global acute malnutrition, there is need to urgently increase the capacity to treat SAM cases with medical complications by establishing additional inpatient care facilities in Yobe and Adamawa States and in LGAs near to inaccessible areas in Borno State especially with the planned resettlement of IDPs in their ancestral homes. There is also need to scale-up the identification, referral and treatment of children from hard to reach areas and in the newly liberated or return locations and other areas planned for the returns. The main thrust of WHO in emergencies is to strengthen, through information dissemination and training; the national, sub-national and international capacities of partners to meet the nutritional needs of populations in emergencies but with the BAY states emergency becoming protracted there is need to build a resilient system that will adequately respond to the acute nutrition shocks and at the same time have a strong system in place to ensure continuity of services. WHO’s unique status as a science- and evidence-based organization that sets globally applicable norms and standards makes it vital in a rapidly changing world especially in the context of emerging pandemics e.g COVID-19 and recurrent shocks from Measles, Cholera/AWD outbreaks and flooding.

2 - Activities

WHO will progressively integrate health service delivery platforms into stronger local health systems by setting up a referral mechanism and increasing the capacity of the health system to cope with life-saving interventions and needs through the following activities:• Organize a training of trainers for a pool of master trainers on in-patient management of SAM with medical complications in the BAY states. Through regular supportive supervision, WHO will collaborate with the State Ministry of Health to ensure that the knowledge and skills acquired during the training of trainers are applied to ensure delivery of quality nutrition services• Promote an integrated approach for the management of SAM as a minimum package of maternal, newborn and child health (MNCH) services.• Strengthen government capacity on quarterly joint supportive supervision/monitoring of nutrition services• Support the systematic implementation of the Nurturing Care Framework in health facilities to ensure that children are developmentally on track• Provide access to comprehensive and high-impact health and nutrition services for people in areas with low/no health service coverage in the BAY states through the H2R mobile health teams and CORPs delivery platforms. Under this approach, WHO will deliver extended basic and essential health packages in areas with low/no access. This will be through 67,600 sessions of systematic screening for malnutrition and referral of all identified cases to treatment centers while those at risk will be counselled through infant and young child feeding (IYCF) in addition to treatment for minor illnesses, vaccination, vitamin A supplementation and deworming of children. Nutrition screening is done by MUAC (mid-upper arm circumference) measurement using a simple coloured plastic strip, a quick and simple way to determine whether or not a child is malnourished. MUAC is suitable to use on children from the age of 12 months up to 59 months. It can also be used for children over six months with a length above 65 centimetres.• Enhance nutrition surveillance, prevention and control across newly accessible areas and the conflict-affected LGAs in the BAY states. This will be through the expansion of the WHO Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS). EWARS deployment entails the restoration and stimulation of existing disease surveillance through a network of focal points and key informants to collect weekly report on a subset of 17 key diseases, including measles, malaria, cholera and SAM.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The beneficiaries through their care givers(Mothers) will be enlightened to gain an understanding of PSEA including the reporting mechanisms.The project is designed to prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of accessing health interventions. In addition, WHO treatment standards will be followed to ensure interventions achieve the desired effect and not cause harm. There will be no discrimination and anyone within the targeted areas can access the services. Patients seeking treatment will be treated with respect and dignity. Also, training on GBV and PSEA policy/standards of conduct will be integrated into all WHO trainings sessions

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

WHO’s Community health champions and Community Resource Persons(CORPs) will be engaged to continuously interact with the community members to inform them about the projects and services obtainable at the SCs, they will also support community mobilization as well as nutrition screening and referrals. Since the CORPs reside 24/7 at the communities, they will also update us on the feedback on the quality of service delivery. These will be analyzed with the MoH focal persons during the monthly CORPS supervisors review meetings. And feedback shared with the community leaders.Also the Ureport system already establishedin several locations will be utilized for feedback and complaints. The complaint desk of WHO through its dedicated toll-free lines that is stationed at the public health EOC will also closely monitor the activity by closely analysis the complaints sent and it will be followed by visit to the field to verify and provide feedback to the community.This project will ensure that beneficiaries are not harmed.WHO will periodically solicit opinions and thoughts of beneficiaries (including of the vulnerable groups and those with special needs- including people living with disability) through community engagement sessions. Feedbacks from the community will serve to guide the project implementation. • Through established network of LGA facilitators , the beneficiaries will be able to give feedback on the quality of service delivered and how well the activities have addressed their needs. This feedback will be used to modify services and activities implementation to the vulnerable population.
