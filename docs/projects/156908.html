Project Summary

The project will target the girls, boys, women, and women in north Syria through awareness raising sessions on the risks of mines and explosive remnants of war. SR Teams will be trained well technically. Then they will conduct the awareness-raising sessions for the targeted communities. The audience of the sessions will be divided into groups and SR Teams will reach 1 or 2 groups in each location daily. during the sessions, SR Teams will distribute handouts and printouts that they may share them with their family members to share the related information with the largest number of individuals.

Needs

The prolonged conflict in Syria significantly influenced the smallest unit of community in Syria, leading to normalizing negative coping mechanisms affecting the lives of children. Child rights continued to be violated, where thousands of children and adults were killed or injured due to the increasing level of violence, which increased their vulnerability to different explosive remnants of war. According to Protection Cluster 2019 HNO HRP findings, the covered communities in non-GoS controlled areas reported lack/loss of civil documentation, HLP, and movement restrictions as Protection issues. The findings and PMTF also mentioned the main coping mechanisms in non-GoS controlled areas namely children dropping out of school to work, early marriage, and relying on humanitarian aid.An estimated 8.2 million civilians are living in communities reporting contamination by explosive hazards. Explosive hazards cause injuries and deaths limit safe access to services and impede the delivery of humanitarian aid. While all population groups are at risk, children, especially boys, Agricultural workers and people on the move are at particular risk. explosive hazardsGiven the extensive threat posed by explosive hazards, humanitarian mine action activities can and should be more strongly integrated into other sectors. Key infrastructure such as roads, housing, schools, health centers, and water/sanitation systems have been contaminated as a result of the conflict. Those which have not been destroyed will remain unsafe for use because of contamination. Lost access to large quantities of productive land for livelihoods and settlement only serves to reinforce poverty, further destabilize communities and undermine opportunities for recovery.The project will address the immediate humanitarian needs by providing Risk Education (RE) to targeted communities, including children, IDPs, and frontline humanitarian workers and by providing mine action actors with the skills and knowledge to respond to the threats. The Mine Action response will be strengthened, developed and sustained through the training of Community Focal Points (CFPs) to deliver RE and through the collection of information on ERW related accidents and casualties and the piloting of a marking project to help people avoid the persistent dangers. These objectives will enable a better-informed humanitarian and mine action response, through providing awareness sessions in targeted areas.

Implementation of activities

SR will hire experienced gender-balanced teams and train them on delivering/training RE sessions. Then the teams will deliver RE sessions to at-risk communities. The RE teams will also train community focal points (CFPs) and teachers on RE delivery. SR field teams will also conduct follow up visits to the trained CFPs and teachers. In addition, the teams will also conduct ERW contamination surveys and do ERW accident/ victim data collection in the targeted locations. Moreover, the RE teams will mark the contaminated areas if local authorities allow them to do this activity.The project monitoring activities will be conducted by SR MEAL Team in the field with technical support of SR teams in Turkey. MEAL planning and implementation are based on procedures and quality standards defined in the internal Syria Relief MEAL Policy and guidelines. The project will have a MEAL Plan developed by the Program and MEAL staff as part of project cycle management. Initial MEAL planning will be completed before commencing the implementation to ensure that MEAL activities are well incorporated into the work schedule and that the MEAL budget is fully reconciled with the project budget. SR’s MEAL will lead the development of standard MEAL tools and approaches that will be used.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Girls, boys, women and men’s physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated armed parties; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access, and lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure during and directly after service provision.Mitigation 1: Consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed-site service provision. Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (route and location) by boys, girls, women and men, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical, visual/hearing deficits, and psychological issues). Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community.Risk 2: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups.Mitigation 2: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what servicesare available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for Code of Conduct and PSEARisk 3: There is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others.Mitigation 3: Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can enable SR RE Teams reach a variety of beneficiaries (IDPs, Host).

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mitigation 1:Emphasis on "Stop, Warn, and Report" messaging in risk education and conduct regular KAPB surveys to monitor changes in risk behaviour, as well as qualitative evaluations to understand the issues underpinning risk behaviour.Mitigation 2:Promote community participation in priority-setting.Identify and highlight humanitarian priorities independently when cooperating with local councils.
