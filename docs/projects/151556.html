Project Summary

DKH, through the partners Daryeel Bulsho Guud (DBG), Center for Peace and Development (CPD), Somali Youth for Peace and Development (SYPD), and Nomadic Assistance for Peace and Development (NAPAD) will contribute to the reduction of barriers to assistance, support the formation and capacity building of Camp Self-Management Committees (CMC), ensure access to quality basic services, enhance the accountability of humanitarian actors to affected populations, mitigate risks of SGBV, fire and flooding, and strengthen community participation and empowerment through consistent engagement.

Needs

More than 1.5 million people displaced in Somalia since November 2016 as a result of drought, conflict and flooding. The total number of IDPs is assessed by 2.6 Million. CCCM Cluster estimates the people in need at 1.8 Million. About the half of the IDPs stays in camps in Mogadishu, Beletweyn, Dusamareb, Adadado, Abudwak, and Galkcayo-South. Mogadishu hosts the largest estimated protracted displaced population in the country, in addition to new IDPs. Banadir IDP camps are mostly stretched along Afgooye Road into Lower Shabelle and far away from the tarmac road into the bush. Here. IDPs often lack access to basic services. In camps closer to town Thousands of IDPs have been evicted.Many IDPs reside in overcrowded areas with extremely poor living conditions and limited safety provisions. Basic needs of IDPs are not satisfied due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion from accessing services, or from the sheer absence of services due to remoteness and poor infrastructure. Some camps show a high rate of SGBV. The most vulnerable IDPs may not receive any assistance, such as single female-headed households, disabled people, minor headed households or orphans and separated children. The informal settlements are mostly only informally managed by the informal leaders in a camp. There is no streamlined leadership and local authorities are often absent or overwhelmed. There is need to formalize leadership with all informal leaders to be represented in camp self-management committees (CMC). Especially gender and minority groups must be represented in the CMCs. Furthermore, the chaotic settlement of IDPs does not leave space for life saving services, such as access to camps for distributions, fire breaks, community spaces/centres. There is need for support to some households to move their shelter from fire breaks and other communal spaces. The CMC must get trained in humanitarian approaches to assist and facilitate humanitarian service delivery.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH) undertakes regular intentintion surveys in IDP camps. As a result, DKH already implements skills training and IGA activities in camps along Afgooye Road. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The most vulnerable IDPs may not be getting the assistance they need, such as single female-headed households, disabled people, minor headed households or orphans and separated children. Civilians arriving from areas controlled by non-state armed actors risk discrimination simply on the basis of their area of origin and alleged affiliations, with repercussions for their access to assistance and protection services. Shifting or disrupted community dynamics as a result of displacement, as well as power imbalances and marginalization, severely limit IDPs’ access to information about the availability services and entitlements, as well as IDPs’ opportunities to voice their views and concerns. Forced evictions inhibit the ability of partners to improve living conditions at sites, and low levels of hindered community participation inhibit the ability to improve beneficiary targeting to include marginalized populations.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Safety conditions especially for women and girls against SGBV need to be improved. These gender groups are mostly afraid of rape during nights when visiting communal latrines. Therefore the foot pathes need to be arranged that they do not provide hide-outs for attackers and the paths and its environs can be overseen due to illumination. Training on alert practices shall be included into CMC training activities and they can be multiplied by the CMCs in their camps.The project will also target on a rate of 30% female mebers in the CMC to ensure that women, girls and youth in general are represented in camp management and the included in the delivery of humanitarian services.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The most common threat of violence in the camps is forceful eviction. Such evictions can be very brutal and life threatening. CMC will be trained to cope with potential evictions through negotiations with landlords to tolerate their stay and if they wish to access their land to give sufficient notice to allow a peaceful withdrawel and moving to other land with agreements of the landlords of the new sites.Apart from forceful evictions, armed attacks against IDPs by armed groups have not been reported so far.
