Project Summary

The project aim at improving the protection of IDP and affected children from violence, exploitation and abuse. To achieve this we will identify children in need of mental health and psycho-social support (MHPSS) and ensure child rights violations are documented to inform additional child protection interventions. This will be carried out by monitoring the situation of affected people in hard to reach areas, the establishment of Psychosocial Support Units (PSSU) for psychosocial support, by identifying and documenting violations against children; undertaking preliminary identification of unaccompanied and separated children for the provision of alternative care /support by “community based” agents; family tracing and reunification, as well as long-term foster care where family tracing is not successful; and the provision of capacity development for “community based” agents; Monitoring and documenting violations and helps in the better protection of children; Providing children at risk of violence, abuse and exploitation during humanitarian situations with child sensitive case management (multisectoral services such as psychosocial support, education and family economic empowerment); Providing support to issue birth certificates to humanitarian situation-affected children; Identifying and implementing measures to prevent risks of violence, abuse and exploitation of children during humanitarian situations (sensitized parents and caregivers and community leaders about key measures to prevent family separations in humanitarian context; informing children and caregivers about risks related to explosive remnants of war; sensitizing caregivers (women, men) on positive parenting;). Project will also build the capacity of Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms
These interventions will include direct support provided to child victims or witnesses and/or referrals to specialized services as needed. All response interventions will be informed by COVID-19 risks and reinforced with integrated C4D capacities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

1 million people have been displaced by armed conflict in the NW/SW regions of Cameroon. Just over half of those displaced are children. IDPs in the NW/SW regions of Cameroon have been displaced on multiple occasions and either live in collective IDP sites or are hosted by already resource constrained host communities. The nutritional status of children has suffered, driven by poor access to safe, drinking water and water borne diseases. Children remain predictably vulnerable to violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect. Many have experienced fear and have been affected by armed conflict firsthand, many have lost or have been separated from their parents and primary caregivers and some have experienced gender-based violence triggered by the conflict. The vast majority has been deprived of a supportive and effective community protection from violence and exploitation. Forced displacement has also resulted to the lost and destruction of personal documentation and civil registries. Significant number of IDPs, particularly children and adults do not possess birth certificates and are the risk of being stateless and deprived of their rights. Births certificates also being a constituent document to produce identification cards makes it difficult for persons above the age of 18 to have these documents. Lack of ID Cards places people at the risk of arrest, restricted freedom of movement, lack of employment, and non-access to public services. Lack of means to cover transport and fees delay in court proceedings and shortage of birth certificate booklets, are the mean reasons which prevent IDPs to acquire their civil documents. Most of the civil registration services are no longer functional in some localities or have relocated temporally and are not easily accessible to persons in the villages. This proposal aims to address these predicaments. Child protection actors will identify 4,000 children through the above interventions, who need protection and refer them to services equipped to address their needs and those of their caregivers. All interventions will integrate strategies that prevent and mitigate the risk of gender- based violence and support feedback mechanisms to enable better accountability to affected populations.
