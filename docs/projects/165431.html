Project Summary

This project aims at ensuring that children in NWS are safe in their homes and communities as a preventive measure and are able to receive CP support if they experience harm. Through providing CP and ECD integrated specialized and non-specialized services, the IRC will respond to the immediate protection needs of the most vulnerable population in the governorate of Idlib.
Throughout the period of the project, the IRC will cover the towns of Salquin, Azmarin and Darkoush in both Harim and Jisr Ash-Shughur districts with specialized and non-specialized CP services with the plan to extend those services to the Maar Tmasrin sub district by the sixth month of the project. As IRC has established, in 2018, a CP hub in the town of Salquin represented by its Safe Healing and Learning space (SHLS), it will continue operating the SHLS with its full capacity to meet the CP needs of both host community and displaced people living in the area. While maintaining services in static centers, the IRC will also continue its outreach and mobile activities through providing the same services implemented in the SHLS but in semi-static centers that are safely identified and approved by the community. The types of services provided includes provision of structured psychosocial support (Focused PSS), Social Emotional learning (SEL) and the parenting skills branded IRC material, families make the Difference (FMD).
The IRC also provides non-structured activities in the form of awareness raising to local communities on different topics concerning CP including topics that are chosen upon the preference of local community members for their importance. At the community level, the IRC will continue its approach to have a community-based programing through building the capacities of key community members and service providers on the key CP concepts and practices including safe referrals, types of vulnerabilities, advocacy for child rights and prospects and finally, basics of CP in emergencies. This is also added to the activation of three child protection committees (networks) to act as community front liners in supporting children needs and advocating for CP in the communities where they are based. Meanwhile, Case Management (CM) is still a key service provided by the IRC for the most vulnerable children. Six case workers will be managing cases of different types with focus on Child labour, domestic violence, psychological distress, sexual abuse and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC).

4 - Needs

The continuously growing numbers of displacement waves to safer areas accompanied by violence, poverty, lack of social support mechanisms and poor access to needed services shape the image of childhood in Syria. The 2020 HNO shows that 13.6 million people are in need of protection services in Syria with 42% of them being children. The MRM4Syria 2019, as cited in the 2020 HNO, stated that grave violations to child rights have been reported with the most happening in areas where armed conflicts still persist. 1,792 incidents were reported to be against children which stresses the fact that violence against children is clearly observed as a common practice. Child protection reported risks in the 2020 HNO contained the most prevalent issues facing children in conflict areas, with a protracted and prolonged type of crisis. 84% of the assessed communities reported child labour as being persistent. Child marriage, on the other hand, was reported to be prevalent in 69% of assessed communities. The crisis in Syria has also led to other types of CP risks starting from separation from the family, use of children in the armed conflict. The situation in Idlib remains at the top of other governorates. The Northern Syria Child protection Dashboard for February 2020 shows that around 88% of surveyed communities in Idlib expressed the need for focused non-specialized CP services. This need links firmly with the community’s understanding of the high levels of psychological distress developed due to the crisis in Syria. Psychological distress was reported as being one the main concerns by communities in the 2020 HNO with 42% surveyed HHs stating that. Adding to that, changes to children behaviours were reported by communities in Idlib as a result of the security situation. The February 2020 IRC Rapid Protection and Needs Assessment for Idlib in NWS showed that 62% of surveyed caregivers started noticing unusual crying happening with their children, while 46% reported noticing sadness

5 - Implementation of activities

The IRC will continue services from Safe Healing and Learning Space (SHLS) reaching 3,800 children with Focused PSS curricula to effectively apply the knowledge and skills necessary to manage emotions and set and achieve positive goals through the Social Emotional (SEL) sessions. Moreover, 1,500 caregivers will participate in parenting education to enhance their knowledge of positive parenting practices. The IRC will continue engaging local communities in its CP activities to build their capacities on the basics of responding to CP concerns at the community level. To achieve this, the IRC will continue supporting 3 child protection committees district. Meanwhile, awareness raising sessions to 6,000 adults and children will conducted on different topics related to CP to enhance the community’s knowledge and practices. IRC will also be targeting children taking into consideration equal access to services by both genders and will pay much attention to the voice of children through its child-friendly feedback and response mechanism and respond to all concerns raised through this accountability systems. The IRC will provide case management services to 550 boys and girls. IRC will be focusing on child labour, domestic violence, sexual abuse, unaccompanied and separated children, forced early marriage and children with psychological distress. The IRC will identify, invite and train 200 community leaders and 200 service providers, including teachers, on the core child protection principles and practices, interventions and services provided for children, how to identify risks associated with CP, PFA and safe referrals. The IRC will use case trackers and plans, follow up records to monitor CM. Structured activities will be monitored through attendance records, monitoring visits reports, baseline and end line caregivers’ survey, SHLS observation tool, and finally, pre and post training tests that ensure the provision of quality services.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- IRC will have moderate access to targeted locations.- CP services and locations are safe and services are continued with open access to all. CP staff are settled in their towns and perform CP services safely.- More specialized service providers to take part in NWS humanitarian services.Risks:- Limited or no access to targeted locations due to security situation.- Attacks on the existing CP locations with possible suspension of CP services and displacement of CP service providers.- Shortage in the number of specialized services providers that can make referrals for some cases a bit exhaustive and might impose the recipients to more harm.Mitigations:- The Humanitarian Access Team (HAT), in collaboration with program and FM teams contribute to identifying possible risks that may impact IRC’s works through the daily situation reports and the security management plan that is updated regularly. SOPs for safety and security in NWS are also in place and activated once needed. Staff are not allowed to travel in the field without getting clearance from the HAT. The HAT shares analysis of the situation and possible scenarios alongside potential risks on beneficiaries, staff and IRC’s operation as well as contingency plans to ensure the safety of beneficiaries, staff and assets.- The CP team developed an SHLS closure plan to ensure the safety of beneficiaries, staff, assets and data. IRC uses low-visibility profile in NWS to avoid being subject to attacks or breaks in. Moreover, the duty of care policy helps IRC take the required measures that ensure the safety and well-being of its staff regardless of their ability to perform work or not during a crisis. Having a neutral independent accountability that receive children and adults feedback helps IRC understand the context better and provide adequate responses to peoples’ needs.- The IRC update their service maps on a quarterly basis to be aware of all services available per sector and refer people
