Project Summary

The project has two components; Front-line and Pipeline. As a pipeline manager UNICEF will preposition essential education supplies to the target population affected by conflict. UNICEF will procure and provide essential supplies to children, teachers and schools for quick resumption of education services. It is a critical component of the project addressing learners and teachers’ need to continue quality education services in the conflict affected area. The proposed supplies include essential learning materials; textbooks, students’ kits, teachers kit and supplies for schools. In addition, UNICEF will provide dignity kits of girls for uninterpreted going to schools due to health-related issues.
UNICEF will procure basic education supplies and deliver to the affected children in order to resume their education. The project will provide education supplies ( students kits, school in a box, recreational kits, school supply, and assorted items) for 729,000 (Boys-419,175; Girls – 309,825) conflict affected children, adolescents/ youth and provide teaching kits to 5,500 teachers.

Needs

The conflict in South Sudan has had an inevitable impact on an already fragile education system. From the ongoing conflict large number of schools were destroyed and resulted almost 41% schools non-functional and deserted (Education Cluster 2017). The condition has worsened children’s access to education even greater, particularly for girls. Most of the schools are operated in the open sky or under the trees. The situation has left an estimated 2.2 million children of primary school age are out of school due to the ongoing violence across the country (UNESCO 2017). The quality of teaching in the country remains a challenge due to several factors; such as crowded classrooms, insufficient teaching and learning materials and an overwhelming proportion of unqualified teachers. Approximately 63 per cent of teachers in South Sudan are untrained and, in the schools supported by the EEP, the proportion of untrained teachers are even higher (UNICEF press release April 24, 2017 - 25 Million Children out of Schools in Conflict Zones – UNICEF). Primary completion rate remains alarmingly low, with 10 per cent of boys and girls completing primary education (EMIS 2016). Arguably, the low levels of education attainment among children has also contributed to a high level of child marriages, child pregnancies, gender-based and family violence, and low health indicators in general, but especially for women and girls in South Sudan. The conflict has also reinforced existing inequities and power asymmetries between males and females, particularly in favour of males. As of 2018, a total of 2,851,201 (2,808,299 girls and boys between 3 and 18 years of age and 42,902 teaching personnel) are in need of education services (HRP 2019). UNICEF launched in 2017-18 the Integrated Essential Emergency Education Services (IEEES) with funding from USAID, which was based on a needs assessment carried out in the conflict affected states. As a result, more than 600,000 children have been reached through the emergency education response. This project will build on the results achieved in 2018 under IEEES project. It is designed to increase enrollment of OOSC, with the focus sustained learning opportunities, level of protection, resilience and recovery of an additional 729,000 Children (Boys-419,175; Girls – 309,825) living amidst violent conflict. UNICEF will provide capacity development of 5,500 (25% women) teachers and 8,500 (30% women) for improved teaching and learning. The project has two components; Front-line and Pipeline. In the pipeline project component UNICEF will procure and provide essential supplies to children, teachers and schools for quick resumption of education services. The supplies include essential learning materials; textbooks, students’ kits, teachers kit and supplies for schools. In addition ECD kits and classroom mats are included for the 3-5 years old children and provide dignity kits for girls for uninterpreted attendance and participation in schools due to menstrual hygiene issues.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

UNICEF and its implementing partner has established Parents Teachers Association with School management committee to provide continuous feedback to the community and parents regarding the projects/education services. There are regular meetings held with parents and community influential to enroll children to schools and provide support for functioning of schools. Social mobilization is part of the education intervention.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context-specific and tailored for gender and age groups. This includes consulting community leaders, religious leaders, schools, women's groups, youth groups and children. All the messages have been presented and discussed with relevant clusters and working groups and task force. All messages have been field tested to ensure cultural and context specifics and involvement of affected population as well.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

UNICEF has conducted a detailed situation analysis through community based consultations including INGOs and NNGOs. Recently education cluster has carried out education need assessment in South Sudan where community and relevant stakeholders have been consulted. UNICEF Has standard arrangements for Annual reviews, mid-year reviews have been conducted in 2018 as preparation for next country program which is three years 2019-2021. UNICEF's annual work plan is approved by the Government, there are also state level action plans and child protection working groups where issues raised by communities can be resolved. Community based child protection committees also provide a mechanism for consultation with communities. Focus group discussions are another means of consultation used in child protection projects.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

UNICEF education section has informal process of getting feedback from the community, parents and children regarding the education services being provided. These feedback are mainly collected through PTA/SMCs regular meetings and addressed on time.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

PTA/SMCs are engaged in monitoring of all education intervention at the school level. PTA/SMC has the formal role in ensuring education project including setting up of TLS and delivery of supplies are done on timely and effectively.

Yes

All education project interventions conducted as part of UNICEF's programme seek to address inequalities and protection threats and violence. The programme includes working with CAAFAG, PSS, and case management, prevention and response to GBV, and reduction of corporal punishment in schools.
