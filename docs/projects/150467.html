Project Summary

PCDCR under this project targets the children affected by the conflict related violence and violations in the Jordan Valley as well as the child protection workers namely the community-based organizations work on children protection in the Jordan Valley due to the bad psychosocial status that the children suffer from as a result of dropping out of schools, working in Israeli settlements, facing sexual harassments, suffering from health diseases and others. The Jordan Valley is targeted since it is located in area C and people their particularly children face many protection challenges by the occupation at different levels as well as it is one of the concerned geographical areas proposed by HRP 2019 and protection cluster. Particularly, the following (six) areas are targeted in the Jordan Valley as they are mostly affected by occupation Ein Shibli (follows Nablus governorate), AlAqaba, Kardala, Bardala, & Ein Elbeida (follow Tubas governorate), and AlAuja (follows Jericho governorate. PCDCR located in Nablus and has the ability and capability to access these targeted locations and populations to provide its services.
PCDCR’s mission is “PCDCR seeks to promote democratic principles, human rights, social peace, good governance, and gender equality. In addition to psychosocial and legal support to vulnerable and marginalized groups”. Thus, PCDCR link to the targeted communities lies in targeting all vulnerable groups particularly in the vulnerable areas. Those targeted groups include children, women, youths, and disabled people. As children in the Jordan Valley suffer from conflict related violations & violence and the Jordan Valley is one of the vulnerable areas for the Palestinians, PCDCR targets children both males and females there along with their parents and community-based organizations (CBOs) in order to achieve a rather complementary work to meet children’s need. PCDCR plans to ensure access to the targeted areas through the following: 1-CBOs:The CBOs exist in the targeted locations as PCDCR has a previous long experience in working with them and implementing different activities through them particularly activities related to combatting violence against women. Such CBOs includes Ein Elbeida Women Center, Bardala Women Center, Kardala Women Center, Rural Development Society in AlAqaba and others. 2- Municipal councils and village councils: PCDCR has great and good coordination, partnerships, and relations with the municipal councils and village councils in the targeted locations as it has conducted plans with them and implemented different activities. For more illustration, PCDCR has cooperated with Ein Shibli village council which resulted in conducting plans, trainings, meetings with different beneficiaries, and other activities related to women economic empowerment. In addition, PCDCR has a recent cooperation with AlAuja municipality as it has conducted different activities related to accountability, transparency, and municipalities’ development. 3-Schools: PCDCR has cooperated with different schools in the targeted locations particularly in combatting child sexual abuse project as PCDCR had a capacity to coordinate with schools to get lists of dropped out children or most affected children in the targeted locations. Through the aforementioned plan, PCDCR can easily ensure the accessibility to the targeted locations and communities.
PCDCR under this project aims at (Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence on children in the Jordan Valley). To achieve this overall goal of the project, PCDCR is looking for attaining the following strategic objectives: 1.The wellbeing of children who have been affected by conflict related violence and violations is improved through MHPSS services.2.Child protection workers are empowered in mental health and psychosocial support for children affected by conflict related violence and violations. By doing so, PCDCR contribute to and align with the HRP 2019 objectives as well as protection cluster objectives related to children protection especially in area C.
Mainly PCDCR will prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence & the immediate humanitarian risk faced by occupation on children in the Jordan Valley through the following interventions:
1-Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support and programmes aimed at supporting vulnerable children & families who have been affected by conflict related violence and violations. It is a responsive activity under which individual and group counselling will be provided for (134) children both (males and females) in need.
2-Child Protection workers are trained on the guidelines on mental health & psychosocial support. It is a remedial activity under which (6) CBOs working on child protection in the Jordan Valley will be trained and empowered on the guidelines on mental health & psychosocial support. A guideline on mental health & psychosocial support will be conducted and distributed to the engaged CBOs’ representatives. A total of (24) participants will benefited from the training and the opportunity will be open for men and women.
PCDCR guarantees and commits to maintain protection for all the targeted group and the staff. In cases where the targeted location are unsafe, PCDCR will conduct plans to tackle the issue and may move the beneficiaries to safe places after getting the approval from OCHA.PCDCR commits to children safeguard policy that it has as well as it follows principles as do no harm and respects children protection agreements.
Furthermore, PCDCR considers the child protection standards under this humanitarian response as follows: 1- PCDCR ensures the quality of child protection response through: 1.1. coordination with other entities as CBOs, NGOs, OCHA, municipal and village councils and others. 1.2. Human resources as PCDCR will employee a qualified staff to work under the project and will invest the capacities and capabilities of the project staff as they will sign and commit to the PCDCR’s child safeguard policy and respect the international child protection laws and agreements. 1.3.Project’s cycle management will be directed towards mainly protecting targeted children and all targeted groups as well as ensuring the quality of service provision. It also will tackle any challenges or violations against child rights at national or international laws1.4. PCDCR will manage information under this project to exchange them with partners and enrich its database; thus, PCDCR will provide its further services according the needs of the collected and managed information and will measure the quality of service provision and develop them as needed. PCDCR also will maintain the privacy of the children and it has internal procedures prevent any abuse for the information related to children including names, photos, ..etc. 1.5. PCDCR will monitor child protection through designing the project, monitoring the planning, and implementing of the project activities. 2- PCDCR develops adequate child protection strategies through community-based mechanisms as it integrated CBOs efforts to train and empower them on the guidelines on mental health & psychosocial support due to the significant role they play for the local community, as they are local references for them. In addition, community-based mechanisms are highlighted in order to ensure more protection and care for the children in their environments.
The gender is taken into consideration as PCDCR will provide its interventions for both male and female children since both of them suffer from conflict related issues.
PCDCR has 20 years of solid experience serving the local community focusing on the vulnerable group including women, children, disabled, and youths. PCDCR has 3 main programs which are child rights protection, women empowerment and rights protection, and democracy and human rights. PCDCR employs different approaches to implement the aforementioned programs including Human Rights-based approach, Capacity Building approach, Awareness Raising approach, Community Mobilization& Advocacy approach, and Service Delivery approach.
As PCDCR has a separate program for children, it has implemented different projects for children as follows:

2- Protective Environment for Children: This 4 year project concerns with protecting children from sexual abuse by educating them along with their caregivers and the local community as well as by supporting them at the psychosocial and legal aid levels.
There are other projects dedicated for children, implementing this proposed project will complement with our previous and current works for children as this project concerns with children suffers from occupation violations at psychosocial level and education level. Thus, PCDCR will achieve full cycle of support for children recently at social, education, policy, individual levels.
PCDCR is always looking for getting resources to serve children at different levels to portect and empower them in all their aspects as they are considered one of the vulnerable groups within the Palestinian community and they should enjoy the children rights.
Furthermore, PCDCR has conducted a project concerned with safe withdrawal from settlement in the West Bank under which PCDCR teach land owners near settlement how to deal with their lands and keep them along with themselves safe. Thus PCDCR has a good experience in the domain also.

Needs

The Palestinian children suffer from conflict-related violations particularly children living in the Jordan Valley (JV). According to an article on Agriculture in the Palestinian JV in 2018, the Palestinian JV constitutes 25% of the total area of the West Bank & it includes more than 60,000 persons, including 52,000 Palestinians. It’s considered one of highest economic resource since its climate zones & agriculture nature. However, as the JV is in area C, Palestinian face restrictions in accessing most of the lands there resulting in Palestinian labour in Israeli settlements; knowing that there’re 35 settlements in the Jordan Valley. Women & children are the most affected groups in working in the settlement.The Palestinian children (males & females) in the JV are the most affected group than other areas. Children in JV face many conflict related violations resulting in negative effects on their mental health & psychosocial status. According to PCBS in 2017, the highest levels of illiteracy were in JV particularly in North JV follows Tubas with 4,7% of illiteracy & South JV follows Jericho 5,5%. Based on a 2016 article published on Al-Hadath news website around 5.5% of settlements workers in JV are children under the age of 16 (2016). Based on report published by Human Rights Watch (2015) it showed the result of 38 child as a sample for their report, 33 child stated the following violations: lower wages than other workers in West Bank, exposure to poisonous chemical materials without the required safety procedures or tools, no legal contract resulting in informal labour conditions & thus no labour insurance. The sample mentioned a forced school leave because of poor life conditions caused by the Israeli control over land & water. Furthermore, referring to our contact with Directorate of Labor office in the JV in Jericho, they indicate that children living in the JV forced to leave school & work in Israeli settlements where they face violence at different levels as follows: they leave the school, they are exposed to sexual harassments, drugs, health diseases since they work in unhealthy environment with unjust conditions. The percentage of children resort to work in Israeli children increases & this leaves negative impact on their psychological & social wellbeing due to harsh living conditions.Referring to the aforementioned circumstances, children living in the JV suffer from lack of receiving protection response interventions to prevent & mitigate the effects of the occupation & conflict related violence.This problem & need is due to lack of the MHPSS services for the affected children which let their psychosocial status getting worse as well as of the capabilities of children protection workers on providing MHPSS in these areas.To meet these needs, PCDCR under this project is looking for participating in providing protection response mechanisms for affected children in the JV through providing them with MHPSS services & empowering the CBOs their in providing MHPSS for them.PCDCR takes into consideration both male & female children as they both suffer from the same occupation violence as well as PCDCR targets children since according to the statistics the percentages of dropping out of school & children working in settlements increase annually with different violations. Such vulnerable group should be in schools not in labor market.The protection concerns are considered in the design & implementation of the project to ensure the safety & security for the children, engaged parties, & staff. PCDCR also has a children safeguard policy.Referring to the community engagement, PCDCR depending on its long years of experience work on/ for children builds & design the project. PCDCR’ll also hold focus groups with the children (males & females) & caregivers (women & men) to assess children need & organize the MHPSS services properly.PCDCR encourages children & their caregivers to submit any complaints or suggestions .

Activities

Considering PCDCR a local NGO that is capable of accessing the targeted locations & population easily, the following activities will be implemented successfully in order to achieve the goal of the project and to contribute to the strategic objectives of HRP 2019 and protection cluster objectives under which our project is proposed. Noting that the following activities consider gender needs, age category & protection for all engaged entities.1.Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support and programmes aimed at supporting vulnerable children & families who have been affected by conflict related violence and violations (Responsive).To that end, the following will be done:1.1. Training for (10) graduates of psychology (both males & females will gain equal opportunities) to qualify them as educators run the group MHPSS sessions (group counselling) for the children in need. The training will be 20 hours.1.2. Holding (6) preparatory meetings for targeted community-based organizations (CBOs) working on child protection; one in each targeted CBO to initiate the project and explain its goals along with their roles. 15 participants will attend in each meeting including both males and females.1.3. Holding (4) focus group discussions in order to assess the needs of children affected by the occupation & conflict related violence and violations in the Jordan Valley. Thus, based on these discussions the MHPSS interventions (both individual & group counselling) will be build appropriately. (2) group focus discussions will be held for the children (one group for each male children and female children) & the other (2) group focus discussions will be held for parents/ caregivers (one group for each of women and men). Each focus group discussion will consist of 12 participants.1.4.Providing (84) children who have been affected by conflict related violence and violations and dropped out of school or involved in labour with MHPSS services (group counselling). This will be done through groups, 12 groups of children will be targeted; 2 groups in each location and 7 children in each group will receive four sessions of MHPSS. The MHPSS services (group counselling) will be run by the trained specialized educators for both female & male children particularly 6 groups of female children and 6 groups of male children.1.5. Providing (50) children who have been affected by conflict related violence and violations and dropped out of school or involved in labour with MHPSS (individual counselling).This will be done by psychosocial specialist in PCDCR premise. At least (25) of the children will be females & (25) are males.1.6. Preparing and publishing (1000) copies of an educational brochure to distribute it to children (both males & females)who are affected by conflict violence & occupation in order to reinforce the knowledge and services they receive in the MHPSS sessions (both the individual and collective counselling) 2.Child Protection workers are trained on the guidelines on mental health & psychosocial support (Remedial). To that end, the fooling will be done:2.1. Holding (6) meetings for CBOs working on child protection in order to prepare them for the training; 1 meeting for each CBO2.2. Holding 2 central trainings for 6 cooperated CBOs working on child protection on the guidelines on mental health & psychosocial support. Each central training will be for 3 CBOs working on child protection and for 20 hours. Each CBO will have 4 representatives in the training (both males and females).2.3.Preparing & conducting a guideline to be distributed for the representatives of CBOs working on child protection who will attend the training. 500 copies of the guideline will be printed.PCDCR maintains protection as it has children safeguard policy and takes into consideration Do No Harm principles. PCDCR commits to implement its interventions without any harm for its all beneficiaries & staff
