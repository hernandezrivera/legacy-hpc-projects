Project Summary

COVID-19 is having an unprecedented impact around the world, both in terms of the need to drastically scale public health preparedness and response, as well as putting measures to protect vulnerable populations. Nigeria as the most populous country in African with high population density in many cities, coupled with a weak health system, poor hygiene practices and an ongoing humanitarian crisis in the North East makes it particularly vulnerable to outbreaks like COVID-19.
UNICEF and partners will ensure timely identification, documentation, tracing and reunification of UASC children as a result of conflict and COVID 19 those exposed to conflict related violence including CRSV, children living in IDP camps who are likely to be affected in the event of an outbreak, children recruited and used by armed groups, and other vulnerable children to be provided and referred for immediate assistance. UNICEF will support partners to implement COVID19 mitigation measures and response for children and community members and conduct close monitoring for the adoption of negative coping mechanisms fueled by COVID-19.
The response will include other interventions such as raising awareness of COVID-19 and putting in mitigation measures at Child Friendly Spaces Safe Spaces and Interim Care Centres. Remote and restricted MHPSS services andcase management services for UASC will be provided for children in high risk areas. Children and caregivers will be provided with radios for COVID-19 messaging and girls who have been exposed to sexual gender based violence would be support with among others, dignity kits. Cash transfer modalities or payments may be provided to caregivers who are providing alternative care for UASC and children while monitoring grave violations against children.
Child Protection will support with remote training (e-learning), CPIMS mobile reporting of COVID-19 affected children. Further, referral pathways will be developed, and technical guidance provided to social workers and community volunteers to enhance their skills in identifying cases of severe distress and protection concerns linked to COVID-19. Messages on positive coping strategies will also be shared as this is critical particularly for children in alternative care and interim care centres, to mitigate negative economic effect of lost livelihoods for household members in isolation, or to reduce the economic burden of children reintegrated from armed groups.. Overall, Best interest of the Child and ‘Do No Harm’ principles will be applied across the response.

1 - Needs

Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.The ongoing hostilities between the Nigerian Security Forces and non-state armed groups (NSAGs) the humanitarian crisis in North-east Nigeria continues. Civilians bear the brunt of a conflict that has resulted in widespread displacement, lack of protection, destroyed infrastructure and collapsed basic services. The spread of COVID 19 affected the humanitarian response and increase the burden for affected population and the north-east response will be more expansive. The crisis in north-east Nigeria has led to overcrowded internally displaced persons (IDP) camps and conflict-affected host communities, where physical distancing and stay-at-home measures are nearly impossible to implement. Infectious diseases like COVID-19 can cause disruptions to children, families and communities., Daily routines and the wider community care and protection system which normally have negative consequences for children’s well-being, development and protection is even made worse in times of a pandemic. In addition, measures used to prevent and control the spread of COVID-19 including lockdowns could expose children to violence, exploitation and abuse . UNICEF Child Protection programme and other partners have partnership with several implementing partners focusing on Child Protection in Emergencies, for vulnerable children including Almariji children in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States. The response including case management services for girls and boys for conflict related sexual violence affected due to travel restriction and mitigation measures implemented to protect vulnerable population from COVID 19. UNICEF will adapt these programmes and approaches to ensure the safety of staff and beneficiaries. UNICEF will ensure adequate supplies are prepositioned, as well as a functioning response team with respective government agencies, INGOs or NGOs. In addition to the general needs of children impacted by the conflict, there are thousands of girls and boys required knowledge and protective equipment’s including face masks and handwashing facilities to protect from COVIA 19 infection in Northeast of Nigeria.

2 - Activities

Please describe the project activities that will address the needs identified and how they will be implemented. UNICEF will implement the following activities in close collaboration with the state governments, international and national non-governmental partners as well as traditional, religious and community-based networks: Key implementation modalities;• MHPSS will be delivered remotely during COVID 19 period using the radio broadcasting services as a strategy to reach out to children and their families and utilizing Megaphones in IDP locations to provide services to children through recorded dramas, storytelling, jingles. Almajiri Children will be provided MHPSS services at isolation centres in line with WHO guidelines • Case management support will be provided for vulnerable children including UASC due to COVID19 and other critical case of GBV/GP as a result of COVID 19 will be benefitted from the case management services. The case management will be provided in line with COVID 19 prevention measures including social workers and beneficiaries utilizing PPE’s. All children and caregivers receiving NFR support will be assisted by direct unconditional cash transfer. • Child Rights Monitoring: UNICEF will work with state facilitators and other key informants to conduct remote and compressed protection monitoring. This would be a joint effort including building remote capacity of other partners, creating networks. The verification of incidents will be available as soon as access is granted. • Dissemination of Information on broader Child Protection Issues and COVID 19: UNICEF will utilize mass media, U Report and other social media platform to disseminate protection and COVID 19 messages for children and community members.• Mainstreaming COVID-19 in the remote or online training for caseworkers, social workers and community volunteers.
