Project Summary

West Hararge zone is one of Oromia Regional State Zones recurrently affected by drought and erratic nature of rainfall. The West Hararge Multi Agency Assessment report released on February 17, 2022 disclosed that in the past year (2021) during both rain seasons (Belg and Meher) late start and early cessation of rain had occurred. Apparently, the 2021 belg crop production had completely failed and 35% reduction of the Meher crop had occurred. This year’s (the 2022) Belg rain that should have started in mid February has also failed to rain to date. The report also showed over 900,000 populations of the zone failed to feed themselves and are exposed to need of food assistance due to this drought. According to this report out of the 17 Woredas of the zone 9 of them namely, Hawi Gudina, Burka Dimtu, Gumbi Bordode, Meiso, Doba, Oda Bultum, Gemechis, Boke and Darolebu, are severely affected by the drought. Due to this drought ponds and rivers have dried up, and then the existing motorized water schemes were overloaded and became non functional due to damages occurred to the electromechanical components. On the other hand there are also areas whose water schemes were damaged by flood occurred by a single day heavy rain occurred in some pocket areas. Because their scheme is damaged by the flood and alternative water sources were died due to the prolonged dry season population of flood affected kebeles are facing serious critical shortage of water.
Due to these problems currently 389,665 (209,354 male, 180,311 females) people are facing critical shortage of water. The West Hararge Multi Agency Report indicated that a minimum of 31 water trucks are needed to provide water trucking services for the affected population. However, so far only 9 trucks were deployed due to shortage of budget. Apparently, the affected population are receiving extremely blow average amount of water; one jerry cane of 20 liter volume in every two days (i.e. only 1.6 lit/day/person) time. To fill this gap women and children are moving very long distances (on average more than 10kms) on foot to fetch water from river valleys. Male household heads have migrated to very far away areas (30 - 40 kms) in search of animal feeds and water. The drought had also caused huge protection problems. In the critically affected woredas early marriage, polygamy, child-trafficking, child labour and rape were reported. The data obtained from the West Hararge Zone Women and Children Affairs Office indicated that 66 rapes and 188 early marriage associated with drought induced problem were reported. Most of these incidence happened when women and girls travel far distance to fetch water. In some areas attacks by dangerous wild animals were reported. For example in one of the affected kebele of Darolebu Woreda a women was bitten by a hyena while moving to fetch water from river early in the morning (at 4:30 AM) and survived by intensive medical treatment. To address the urgent critical water shortage and to alleviate the problems in a sustainable manner the West Hararge Multi Agency assessment team had proposed two major activities; namely, water rationing and rehabilitation of non functional water schemes located in the affected kebeles. Hence, the purpose of this intervention is to alleviate the critical shortage of water in three highly affected kebeles of Daro Lebu and Oda Bultum Woredas through emergency WASH intervention. The project has targeted to increase daily water consumption of the project beneficiaries from the current less than 2 lit/day to an average amount of 10-15 lit/day with inclusive, safe and dignify manner. The project will mix short term life saving interventions with durable solutions to alleviate the pressing needs of the targeted communities. The overall target of this project is then to address critical WASH problems of a total population of 80,352 (41,401 females and 38,951 males) affected by the prevailing drought.

1. - Projects activities

Activities proposed for this intervention Rehabilitation of Biyyo Hoda water scheme:- Biyyo Hoda Water scheme is located in Darolebu Woreda. This kebele is among the currently drought affected areas of the zone. The scheme had become non-functional by a flood that happened in the kebele on August 29, 2021, which damaged the electromechanical equipment and part of the water supply system. Following this, the people were using unsafe surface water, which was also dried by drought. Thus, beneficiaries of this scheme are facing critical shortage of water because all alternative water sources; ponds, rivers and springs found in their kebele have dried up due to the prolonged dry season. Thus, the project will rehabilitate the system and will make a total of 5,478 (2,695 females, 2,783 males) people beneficial. Rehabilitation of Borte Water Scheme: Borte Water Scheme is located in Darolebu Woreda and became non functional because the pump burnt due to overload. The activities planned to activate this scheme are; supply of pump and generator, well servicing and pump installation and expansion of the scheme to remote villages. After rehabilitation the scheme will benefit 9,600 (4,619 females, 4,981 males) people affected by the drought. Rehabilitation of Burka Water Scheme: This scheme is located in Odabultum Woreda. The kebele is among the currently affected areas by critical shortage of water. Burka is big motorized spring water scheme damaged because the surface pump had burnt. Hence, activities needed to be implemented to activate this scheme are; supply of surface pump, supply of generator (the existing generator is too old), maintenance of spring eye fence, cleaning and maintenance of the collection chamber, construction of flood protection around the collection chamber, maintenance of the generator and surface pump house, maintenance of the gravity and pressure lines, maintenance of the water distribution points, maintenance of the reservoir, conduct some expansion works. Rehabilitation of this scheme will address critical water shortage of 18571 people living in Burka kebele and other three adjacent kebeles namely, Gebiba, kebele 26 and kebele 27 affected by the drought.  Water rationing: Water rationing is included in this project to alleviate the critical water shortage in Biyyoo Hoda, Borte, Burka kebeles until the schemes will be maintained and start services. CISP has planned to deploy three water trucks of each with 20,000 litres capacity for 60 days. The plan is to distribute a minimum of 7.5 lit/day per person for 15078 people. The project will also supply 5 water tanker of 10,000 lit capacity as a drop-off and distribution point for the trucking. The service will benefit up to 15,078 (7,314 females, 7,764 males) people for one month. Enhance hygiene and sanitation practices of the target community: .in the intervention areas the hygiene practices of the target population is observed to be very poor; open defection is common, personal hygiene of the population especially that of children is poor, solid waste are observed on open fields etc. Hygiene promotion and subsequent behavioral change can help to keep people and their environment clean. So the aim of hygiene promotion is then to encourage (aware) people to modify their actions, so they reduce unhygienic behavior and adopt appropriate behavior and use of appropriately designed facilities; such as hand washing units, latrines, waste disposals and water storage vessels. The project will use different appropriate and attractive means to promote the HS practices. CISP will also try to provide sanitation materials for very poor population who cannot afford to buy; for example provision of laundry and body soap, household water storage materials (jerry cane), temporary latrine Slab (sanplat), sanitary pad for poor females and girls etc. • Capacity building training and support to community based water management committees and operators

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The project will be managed by CISP, and activities will be implemented in collaboration with community and Woreda and Zone Water, Energy and Mineral offices. The regional bureau will provide continuous coordination and monitoring throughout the project implementation period. In addition, West Hararge Zone Water, Energy and Mineral Office will mobilize its experts and service rig for the installation of the pumps and jointly facilitate trainings of community operators. The Women and(Children) Social Affairs will monitor the participation of women, children and people living with disabilities. It will also make sure that the systems are children, women, and disabilities friendly. CISP will be also responsible for direct implementation of the project, supply project inputs, provision of logistic and overall coordination. The organization will provide all staff necessary for the implementation of envisaged activities. CISP works in the area in partnership with local and International Humanitarian Organizations implementing emergency response projects and during the implementation will be applied the usual principles of the of coordination, complementarily, synergy and harmonization with other development actors operating in the area. Intervention kebeles of this project have been systematically selected in such a way that there will not be duplication of resources/efforts rather complementing with their interventions, for example, the rehabilitation of the water systems will shorten the distance of water source not only for this project but also other partners (UNICE, CARE and the government) working water trucking. GOAL IMC, and the government are working on nutrition project in the operational areas for host communities and IDPs. Hence, our intervention complements the already existing interventions, and no activities will overlap activities of other partners. Particularly, the project will integrate with nutrition projects supplying water to health centres. All the emergency interventions in the zone are coordinated by the zonal emergency task force led by zone administration while WASH related activities will be coordinated by the zone water office. CISP is an active member of the zone emergency taskforce as well as of the federal and regional WASH taskforces.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The project will consider the Feedback and Compliance Mechanism (FCM) as accountability for the affected populations (AAP) and the donor to deliver the project objectives to the best satisfaction of end beneficiaries/clients through client response programming approach (CRP). The CRP will systematically listen to the perspectives of our clients and use their feedback to make decisions and communicate and explain to our clients how their feedback has informed our actions. The mechanism involves people at every stage and has been developed in a participatory way with representatives from all stakeholder groups including the most vulnerable and excluded. This was done through involving the community in the process of assessments, program design, monitoring and evaluation and then set up complaints and response mechanisms including listening exercises; perceptions studies; community score cards; story-telling, mobile phone calls or text messaging (SMS), SOP and others. In case that a project receives complaints or feedback that fall outside our remit, we will have a system in place to refer the feedback onwards to someone who is in a position to deal with the issue (known as referral pathways). The mechanism will handle sensitive and non-sensitive complaints with the proper procedures to handle both types. The mechanism for handling complaints will also take into account specific contexts and safety to recognize potential dangers and risks to all stakeholders involved in each process, including staff, complainants, witnesses and others. The mechanisms will include ways to prevent harassment, injury or harm to those wishing to raise an issue or complaint. The system will ensure the confidentiality of program participants when managing their complaints. This will assure that any information given is restricted to a limited number of people on a need-to-know basis and is not disseminated more widely. Feedback and complaints will be handled objectively and with fairness. All users will be made aware of the procedures; so that they understand its purpose, have sufficient information on how to access it and understand how it works. It will also be accessible for all groups within the community, including the most vulnerable and those with disabilities; this will be done through multiple channels to ensure everyone can be reached. Staff will be trained to ensure that standards are met in receiving, processing, reviewing, ensure everyone can be reached. Staff will be trained to ensure that standards are met in receiving, processing, reviewing, responding and resolving complaints. The system will be guided by policy and procedures that set out the key characteristics of the mechanism, such as who is responsible for receiving and handling complaints, how feedback will be recorded, reviewed and actions communicated, who has access to what information. Response time. The policy or guidelines will also clearly articulate, or signpost the process for managing sensitive complaints. The FCM will also be complemented by post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to measure the clients' feedback and satisfaction on items distributed by the project.
