Project Summary

The aims of the project are to 1) promote social cohesion between host communities and IDPs, as well as newly arrived returnees (from inside and outside Syria); 2) Enhancing resilience-building through Vocational Training matching labor market characteristics, including through the facilitation of the encounter between labor demand and supply.
Whenever possible in the areas where market is functional COOPI will be aiming to provide cash and voucher assistance to vulnerable families in urban and peri-urban areas to reduce their socio-economic vulnerability and their negative coping strategies but also to move from in kind assistance to restricted cash-based transfers and provide sense of dignity to beneficiaries.
The project will be implemented in Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Homs, Hama and Dara governorates. In all of these areas COOPI already implements projects or the implementation will start in near future.
In 3 of the above-mentioned governorates COOPI has solid presence since 2018 (Rural Damascus and Aleppo) and 2019 (Dara), while intervention in Hama and Homs started in 2021. COOPI will also be able to achieve fruitful synergies with the currently implemented projects focused on protection and food security and livelihood as the current activities are allowing COOPI to enter in contact with the most vulnerable beneficiaries. Additionally, COOPI has already established very good relations with community leaders in the AoI resulting in greater access and acceptance by communities and easier provision of assistance in a timely manner.
The project will contribute to Strategic Objective 3: Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities reaching in total 11.000 people.
More precisely the project will contribute to SO 1 by organization of vocational trainings, support to entrepreneurship, rehabilitation of productive assets and SO3 through the support to community-based initiatives, support of civic engagement activities and fostering IDP and refugee integration.
The experience gained in Aleppo since 2018 where COOPI successfully implemented 25 community based initiatives reaching 7.000 people will be utilized during the preparation and implementation of the activities. COOPI will ensure that proper coordination with local authorities will be maintained and, at the same time, will deeply involve communities in the design and the implementation of the project. Coordination will be ensured with the Sector and its members but also with international and national actors active in the same areas in order to implement harmonized response, avoid overlapping and promote synergies. Synergies will also be carried out by COOPI through the contemporary implementation of other projects submitted to HRP 2021 taking place in the same governorates of this project in an integrated approach.

4 - Needs

After 10 years of conflict, the crisis in Syria continues to strain the socio-economic foundations of Syrians. HNO data shows that a major change in terms of the proportion of PIN occurred since the development of the 2020 HNO, where the PIN for ERL Support increased from 9.9 M to 11.6 M, due to the growing need for job opportunities after the massive destruction of jobs and livelihoods that the crisis and covid-19 pandemic resulted in, in addition to the increased pressure on essential services and infrastructure. This worsening situation was also documented in Al Nashabiyeh where COOPI conducted assessment on impact of COVID on HH level when 46% of the interviewed HHs reported losing their jobs due to spread of COVID 19 pandemic. HHs with lost or separated family members -consequence of years of crisis- have difficulties finding a functioning system to address their social, legal and safety issues. The presence of IDPs in host communities that are in lack of infrastructure and have been affected by the crisis, produces challenges, lack of access to services and markets for the entire population of the affected areas. This influx of population in already-vulnerable areas also produces important effects inside homes, leading to the deterioration of the family environment and the breakage of the community social fabric. COOPI continues to reinforce the capacity of the community-based structures and initiatives to reduce and respond to risks and promote social cohesion. The lack of early recovery activities leads to an increased reliance on harmful coping mechanisms (high-risk jobs, child labour) and the long-term instability of the livelihood for the less resilient people.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented in Rural Damascus (where COOPI is present since 2018 with FSL and protection activities and provision of food vouchers), Aleppo (COOPI is running 2 community centers and implement FSL activities), Dar’a (where COOPI is implementing FSL and protection activities since 2019) and Hama and Homs (new areas of intervention where COOPI implements FSL and protection activities through distribution of in kind assistance and organization of vocational trainings). CBIs will be implemented in all project locations.COOPI is aiming to implement 50 CBIs reaching in total 10.000 beneficiaries. CBIs will be identified by the community and closely monitor by COOPI staff. After the implementation of the CBIs a report will be produced, which will contain information on activities conducted and a description on its impact. The CBIs can include rehabilitation of communal spaces, repair of public lighting, rehabilitation of the children playground etc. CBIs will be implemented by the community and whenever possible through CfW modalities.Cash/vouchers assistance will be distributed to the most vulnerable families in urban and peri-urban areas to reduce their socio-economic vulnerability and their negative coping strategies but also to move from in kind assistance to restricted cash-based transfers and provide sense of dignity to beneficiaries. Specific case-by-case assessment will be conducted targeting 500 beneficiaries. Vocational training will be organized for at least 500 beneficiaries. The trainings will be organized in cooperation with specialized institutions, the participants who attended the whole training and passed the final exam will receive a small grant to start their own production.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

COOPI staff and beneficiaries could be exposed to protection risks during the implementation of the Action. The assessed risks are the following: sex exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers, violence from HHs against aid workers or among themselves, interference in the HHs targeting, HHs list not correctly updated by local authorities, aid diversion, hostilities in new accessible areas, tension among thecommunity due to the HHs selection and diversion of input/assets. By prior experience in similar projects COOPI evaluate this risk as “low” and “medium” To mitigate these risks, the project foresees the following measures: establishment of feedback mechanism, balanced trained staff between men and women, training and follow up on code of conduct, distributions with strong planning and logistic attention dedicating special lines for vulnerable beneficiaries, training to staff on humanitarian principles and targetingmodalities and criteria, regular verification of list of HHs before and during the project implementation, FGDs before, during and after the distribution, appropriate locations for distribution points in line with security situation in new accessible areas in consultation with relevant stakeholders, preparation of contingency plans, clear, public and transparent criteria for the selection of HHs, community based beneficiary selection.
