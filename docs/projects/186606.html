1 - Needs

As of Sept 2021 assessment in Adamawa, by INTERSOS identified an increase in risks of further exposure to GBV and negative coping mechanisms of about 67% of the target population. Up to 76% of the target population confirmed occurrence of GBV in their communities. Moreover, 100% of female focus-group participants affirmed that the most common risk they are exposed to is denial of resources from partners and very limited or lack of livelihood for them and their partners. This situation frequently exposes them to violence from their partners, basically physical assault, and psychological and emotional abuse as they demand for financial support from their spouses to meet family needs and personal their basic needs. There are also serious safety concerns for women and girls who are exposed to survival sex due to extreme poverty and hunger. Incidents of rape and other forms of GBV and SEA against adolescent girls perpetrated by adult men has been reported too. Intimate partner violence recorded 49% in the last GBVIM result presented for the month of Sept 2021 by UNFPA. Despite this precarious situation, there are no active GBV/PSS response partners in the target locations of Adamawa where UNHCR supports protection assessments only. The activity by INTERSOS will be addressing needs of the GBV survivors by providing standardized case management services for GBV survivors and victims of violence as well as a comprehensive psychosocial support and mental health package that mainstreams the response to the effects of COVID-19 as well as the psychological and psychosocial impact generated on the distressed populations because of the ongoing crisis. The actions are to alleviate human suffering through the provision of timely, unhindered, and equitable multisector- humanitarian assistance. With the aid of structure group and using Start awareness support action, community members will be empowered with behavioral modification activities that create opportunities to questions social norm and cultural belief. Thereby making women and girls feel safe where they live, work and learn. By working with the CCCM INTERSOS team this project will be able to address protection needs associated with camp coordination and camp management for a more dignified and safe return
