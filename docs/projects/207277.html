Project Summary

"The project's primary objective is to empower young women and men aged 18-24 from both Rohingya camps and host communities. It aims to provide them with essential skills, knowledge, assets, attitudes, and networks necessary for a successful transition to decent work and adulthood. The project will target adolescents who are 18 years and above from Rohingya and Host communities and those who have engaged in formal or non-formal education. Therefore, the project will provide market-driven skill training to selected adolescents and youth in camps and host communities through different approaches to meet their varying needs and challenges.
Participation in skill training will be based on individual interests and trade availability, determined through consultations and capacity assessments. Various training approaches, including outreach centers, a blended model, and referrals to government and private providers, will be employed. Host community participants opting for formal sector jobs will undergo an assessment process for skill level and certification.
To ensure a relevant curriculum, a gender-sensitive capacity assessment will be conducted at the program's onset. This assessment will consider labor market conditions for various groups, identifying barriers to employment in the host community. It will guide the project in identifying sustainable and environmentally friendly livelihood options. The curriculum will be developed/revised in collaboration with partners and validated through workshops with the National Skills Development Authority (NSDA) and employers.
Instead of a comprehensive labor market assessment, the project opts for consultations and capacity assessments to inform skills placement, training packages, and beneficiaries' suitability for different Income Generating Activities (IGA). Trainings will be conducted in safe, accessible centers, with a focus on gender and disability-friendly facilities. The project will renovate existing centers and establish new ones in consultation with community representatives.
Participants will receive input support to build productive assets, including financial literacy training and loans for host community members. For camp participants, support may include homestead gardening, poultry rearing, and jute production. Entrepreneurship hubs will be established in selected sub-districts, run by young men and women identified from the project.
The project emphasizes engagement with family and community gatekeepers to support adolescents and youth, particularly girls and young women, in pursuing livelihood options. To ensure refugee self-reliance and employment, the project will conduct capacity assessments, utilize education facilities as entry points for vocational education, and address social and cultural barriers. Additionally, a Teach to Transfer program will train and mentor unemployed women for careers in teaching.
For host communities, the program aims to enhance resilience through livelihood activities, diversifying income sources, developing skills aligned with changing markets, creating financial safety nets, and linking participants with markets for adaptive strategies.
We have planned to conduct our activities in Camps 07, 09, 10, 15, and 21. Within these camps, we will prioritize Accelerated adult learning across all five, non-formal technical training in camps 07, 09, 10, and 21, and Vocational training in each of the five camps. Additionally, we aim to provide non-formal technical training, Vocational skill development, and Support livelihoods for Host communities based on gained skill within host communities. "

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Lack of income opportunities and skills, coupled with government regulations, makes Rohingya refugees highly vulnerable and dependent on humanitarian assistance, with 95% of households (HH) moderately to highly vulnerable. According to SC compiled market assessment, 62% of the youth at Cox's bazar are self-employed, while 39% are unemployed or work part-time. The skills acquisition process of the host community is traditional, and employees or self-employed persons gained skills from friends or family members. Only 11% of youth have received either vocational training or apprenticeship/on-job training indicating that they could not reach out to technical vocational education (TVET) system or other available vocational training. Besides, the project builds on evidence highlighting the positive impact of vocational skills training on employment, particularly for adolescent girls and young women. Market assessments reveal differences in livelihood opportunities between camp and host community youth. For instance, within camps, day labor is predominant (86%), with variations between genders. Small businesses are mentioned by 69% of young Rohingyas, with a gender difference (74% males vs. 60% females). Host community opportunities include agriculture (16%), mobile service centers (12%), sewing (9%), driving (7%), electrician work (7%), vegetable gardening (7%), and cloth business. In Addition, the situation of girls and young women is disturbing. In Bangladesh, they face several barriers both in accessing and completing quality education programs, employment, and entrepreneurship. On one hand, they need validation or permission from family members for their professional choices, and face discrimination in employment and pursuing upward mobility. A study on ‘Labor Market and Vocational Skill Gap Assessment in Rohingya and Host Communities in Teknaf and Ukhiya, Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh’ conducted by Friends in Village Development Bangladesh (FIVDB) showed that around 69% of businesses are not willing to allow flexible working hours for female workers. Male perception is dominant, and businesses are not child friendly, posing an additional barrier of child-care for women and girls, and jeopardizing women's opportunity to gain quality skills and knowledge. On the other hand, one of the biggest challenges of self-employment is lack of access to bank loans. The study showed that only 24% of the host community and 14% of the camp youth have bank loan access."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"The proposed project in Cox's Bazar exemplifies an integrated approach, addressing the diverse needs of Rohingya refugees and the host community. Focused on education, livelihoods, and child protection, the initiative recognizes the interconnected challenges faced by the displaced population. Prioritizing youth, the project aims to break the cycle of vulnerability by transitioning them from education to skills development, fostering long-term resilience.The strategic inclusion of protection activities, such as case management and psychosocial support, acknowledges the unique challenges faced by adolescents in the context of displacement. Moreover, the project demonstrates a commitment to predictability and continuity by outlining a clear timeline for enrollment, skills development, and ongoing support spanning multiple years. This phased approach reflects flexibility and responsiveness to the dynamic nature of the crisis.The project aligns with the principles of adopting an integrated approach and ensuring predictability and continuity. By addressing immediate needs and providing a pathway for sustained development, the initiative lays the groundwork for positive and lasting impact within the affected communities."

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The Project has been designed to reflect the Centrality of Protection policy by integrating protection activities across all four results. As part of the Centrality of Protection Policy, a sectoral integration approach will be adopted, including through referrals between the sectors and capacity building of staff and volunteers on identifying the most affected and grave violations of their protection rights and on other cross-sectoral themes. CP interventions will be integrated with the Education and Livelihood and Skills sector through capacity building on the CP component and referral mechanisms. In accordance with the CP Minimum Standard in Humanitarian Action, Sphere, Core Humanitarian Standard protection principles and considering Centrality of Protection, the project prioritizes safety, dignity of the targeted beneficiaries and avoids causing harm through preventing and minimizing any unintended risk. To ensure meaningful access, the project will pay special attention by following a non-discriminatory approach regarding disability, age and gender related barriers. SC has an established accountability, feedback, and response mechanism to involve affected population throughout the project cycle. This project capacitates and empowers affected children, caregivers and community people to claim their rights. To address Safeguarding and PSEA related risk the project follows ‘Zero Tolerance’ policy, different reporting channels are actively in place. In addition, all relevant project people including staff, volunteers, will be trained on SC core policies, including SFG PSEA.

3.3 - Age & Disability

"To ensure meaningful access, the project will pay special attention by following a non-discriminatory approach regarding disability, age and gender related barriers. Highlighting the creation of a gender-sensitive skills/ vocational training/ learning environment, efforts are focused on strengthening the capacity of staff and partners through initiatives on gender and inclusion. This aims to enhance sensitivity, particularly concerning the young women. Active involvement of community for raising awareness on young women access to skill development and vocational courses. At least 45% adolescent/youth girls will be engaged in the skill and vocational training in order to enchance their livelihood opportunities. Ensuring sex-segregated WASH facilities and training centre and providing dignity kits for menstrual hygiene management contribute to a safer environment. Similar importance will be provided for the inclusion of adolescent and youth with disabilities in the skill development opportunity will be enhanced for inclusive livelihood programming. It is also incorporated into project cycle management through the inclusion of protection mainstreaming indicators for different activities"

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"The project aims to establish a robust Feedback Response Mechanism (FRM) to ensure an inclusive and accessible accountability system for both host and camp children. Utilizing 17 age-specific tools adapted in consultation with young women and the community, including methods such as home visits, feedback boxes, hotlines, help desks, and participatory sessions, children and adolescents will have avenues to provide valuable feedback. These systematic sessions will play a pivotal role in addressing protection needs, supported by staff capacity strengthening.The FRM will be reinforced by an information sharing plan, incorporated into the implementation plan, and communicated through easily understood materials for participants with low literacy levels. Feedback will be uploaded to an online FRM system, and the project manager will investigate, address, and resolve issues based on feedback categories. The accountability focal point will verify feedback using established Standard Operating Procedures (SOP), enabling the program team to investigate, resolve, and close the feedback loop. Captured feedback will be presented in an accountability report and project implementation review meetings to enhance program effectiveness.To enhance accountability, the project will implement a contextual information sharing plan and disseminate an accountability message to all staff, fostering a unified voice from Save the Children (SC) and partners. Quarterly accountability reports will be provided, and in alignment with SC's localization objectives, training and support will be extended to implementing partners to strengthen their existing Accountability to Affected People (AAP) mechanisms and practices."

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project underscores a comprehensive commitment to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) within the context of Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox's Bazar. Recognizing the heightened vulnerability of adolescents and youth, particularly women, the initiative integrates PSEA measures into its Women Friendly Response Mechanism (CfRM). Through age-specific tools, including participatory sessions, the project provides a platform for children and adolescents to report instances of sexual exploitation and abuse. The Feedback Response Mechanism (FRM) incorporates specific channels for reporting such cases, ensuring a confidential and accessible system for victims. Staff members will undergo necessary capacity strengthening to effectively handle and address PSEA concerns. The project emphasizes the importance of a unified approach, incorporating an information sharing plan to disseminate PSEA-related guidelines among staff. Quarterly accountability reports will highlight PSEA efforts, reinforcing a commitment to transparency and accountability. By prioritizing PSEA within the broader project framework, the initiative aims to create a protective environment, safeguarding the well-being of vulnerable populations and ensuring that instances of sexual exploitation and abuse are promptly addressed and mitigated.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

SC will carry out a disaster risk reduction (DRR) and protection emergency response unit (PERU) training in the first year to provide basic foundational knowledge, skills, and capacities of DRR among staff and volunteers. In the second and third year, SC will lead refresher trainings so that staff will not face any knowledge gaps (using non-PRM funds; budget-permitting, this cost would be added to the budget in Y2 and Y3). In addition to the refresher training (under PRM), new staff will also be linked to the DRR team for training (not budgeted under PRM). The staff will also have access to training provided by other sectors such as Health and WASH – and in every sector DRR training is also included. DRR related issues are also being discussed regularly, ensuring the continuous practice of DRR among team members. Staff roll out those trainings at camp level to spread awareness and educate the community.
