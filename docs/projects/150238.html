Project Summary

This project aims to urgently respond to the current emerging needs among acutely vulnerable farmers, breeders, and female-headed households in order to enhance their access to food, other essential basic goods, and restore livelihood capacities through urgent cash assistance. Through this project, Mercy Corps will target 428 vulnerable households (2,397 individuals) from Khan Younis and Rafah, including 88 female-headed households with limited economic opportunities, 220 crises-affected farmers impacted by the economic deterioration and at risk of losing their livelihood, 113 venerable farmers severely affected by power and irrigation water crises, and 7 small poultry breeders who’s their livelihood in urgent need for rehabilitation and has incurred losses resulting from high-cost alternative energy.
Mercy Corps will identify, assess eligibility, and distribute cash assistance to each household in monthly instalments to eligible vulnerable farmers to rehabilitate open land, or rehabilitate water ponds and have durable photovoltaic solutions. Breeders will receive cash to rehabilitate their farm and will be provided by durable photovoltaic solutions. Women and Female-Headed households will receive two-months Cash-for-Work opportunities. This urgent cash support will allow vulnerable farmers, poultry breeders to have reliable, and timely financial support to addresses their urgent needs, restore their livelihoods, build resilience and improve agricultural production cycles; women and female-headed households will have improved access to food and other essential basic goods for themselves and their dependents.
Through well-defined vulnerability criteria, Mercy Corps, in partnership with two local NGOs [Al Foukhary Association for Country Development (in Khan Younis) and Yaboos Charity Society (Rafah)], will identity, assess and verify eligibility to select vulnerable households to receive designed assistance, monitor activities, and ensure full coordination and engagement with the community and all relevant stakeholders.

Needs

In the wake of a collapsing economy in Gaza, Palestinian living standards have continued to decline, with aid flows no longer providing the impetus for growth (World Bank). For 12 years, a comprehensive land and sea blockade imposed by Israel has further deteriorated the humanitarian and socio-economic situation. As poverty deepens, vital services are underfunded, inaccessible, or non-existent; the poor quality of essential services do not meet individual’s most basic needs and are inadequate to respond to the heightened vulnerabilities presented by the current crisis. All socio-economic indicators and humanitarian conditions continue to decline as more than half of the population is living in poverty and without a job (WFP). Unemployment in Q2 of 2018 was 53.7% (PCBS), and almost two thirds of women are unemployed with more inequality of opportunities, including limited access to productive resources and assets and employment (ILO). Salaries of about 62,000 employees were cut by PA since May 2017, while another 22,000 de-facto government employees receive only part of their salaries on irregular basis (OCHA). Poverty rates in the Gaza Strip have significantly increased from 39% in 2011 to 53% by the end of 2017 (PCBS) and WFP has reported that 39% of people in the Gaza Strip are food insecure. The prolonged daily power blackouts and high price of fuel to operate water pumps, wells, and electric generators reduces the quantity and frequency of the water supply to farmers who depend on wells to irrigate their crops, limiting agricultural production. Additionally, the imposition of a ban on the exit of agricultural produce from Gaza has adversely effected farmers by reducing revenues.In September 2018, Mercy Corps conducted a needs assessment to identify needs, possible interventions, and ways to address the food insecurity among vulnerable groups, with a particular focus on identifying gender disparities. Across the Gaza Strip, 73 households (46 men, 27 women) participated in the FGDs from different groups (farmers, fishermen, and unemployed male & female skilled and unskilled worker households). The results of the assessment confirmed the challenges mentioned above, and needs concluded in pervious assessments (2017). The current challenges have forced vulnerable households to adopt negative coping strategies such as: (i) fishermen reducing the operating cost by reducing the number of vessels they operate; (ii) early marriage for girls to reduce family expenses; (iii) farmers cultivating less land or using less quantity of material inputs to reduce incurred costs especially related to irrigation water, fuel, and herbicides; (iv) families selling food and other vital household non-food items received through humanitarian aid programs in order to purchase more relevant or cheaper goods to meet household needs; (v) adopting primitive cooking means, such as wood fire instead of cooking gas, and (vi) purchasing lower quality food and goods. Needs and priorities for farmers and unemployed men and women (more groups in the report) were as follow:Farmers: i) job opportunities or cash assistance; ii) alternative and durable power solutions; iii) rehabilitation of livelihood infrastructure; iv) material inputs; and v) female farmers emphasized their heightened needs for employment and income opportunities. Unemployed women and men: i) job opportunities and cash assistance; ii) provision of food and essential household items; iii) new livelihood opportunities; iv) education and tuition aid; v) seasonal clothing; vi) free or low-cost healthcare & medical treatment; and vii) household rehabilitation and maintenance.Based on the needs assessments, Mercy Corps used the “Gender Analysis Framework” tool to help identify the gender specific information needed to design and deliver smart, targeted project activities that meet the needs of both men and women.Please see Mercy Corps’ “Needs Assessment - FSS Gaza” Link: https://goo.gl/m5qvWd

Activities

Project activities are designed to address the needs of most vulnerable community groups based on well tested approaches, ensure protection to beneficiaries, and do-no-harm to the targeted communities. To maximize the impact, the activities are designed to provide reliable, and timely support that addresses the urgent needs of venerable men and female headed household to recover their food security. Mercy Corps (MC) uses standardized social and technical vulnerability selection criteria consistent with humanitarian principles and practices. MC will target vulnerable women and FHH who possess no profession, no source of income, and assist them to access cash to cover food and other essential basic needs through specific gender-appropriate CfW activities that provides unemployed women with job opportunities that help them in rebuilding their livelihood skills and provide services to wider community such as children. Built on proven methods using community mobilization and local ownership, Mercy Corps and local partner NGOs will identify beneficiaries for each activity through an open application processes, a strict scoring database system, and verification though social and technical visits by experienced staff. A set of criteria consisting of socio economic vulnerability and technical eligibility will be applied to the selection of beneficiaries under each of the specific interventions. Social criteria will include: income, family size, FHHs, living conditions, and PWD, and other vulnerable groups. Technical criteria include: rights over the asset, land area, access to water issues. Final lists of selected beneficiaries will be cross-checked with Food Security Sector partner’s database, NGOs and INGOs providing similar assistances, including ICRC, PUI, and Oxfam. Act01: Urgent cash assistance to venerable crises-affected small farmers for repair and restore their agricultural livelihood assets through open land rehabilitation, access to seeds and seedlings, rehabilitation of irrigation networks and other essential inputs to restore their production capacity. MC will identify and target most affected and vulnerable (220) farmers through an open application process from Khan Younis (135) and Rafah (85). Act02: Urgent cash assistance to vulnerable small farmers severely affected power and irrigation water crises through the restoring their farmlands water storages by rehabilitation/expanding their water ponds, and the provision of durable solar energy solutions (PV) to have regular access to irrigation water, overcome irregular irrigations cycles, and to have a quick restoration to their production capacity. Mercy Corps will identify and target (113) most affected and vulnerable farmers from Khan Younis (69) and Rafah (44). Act03: Urgent cash assistance to vulnerable small poultry breeders through the rehabilitation of their farms, and the provision of durable solar energy solutions (PV) to overcome the high cost of energy resulting from long hours of power outage, for lighting and ventilation during daylight and at night. Mercy Corps will identify and target (7) most affected and vulnerable breeders from Khan Younis (4) and Rafah (3). Act04: Assistance to vulnerable women and female headed households and other vulnerable community members from Rafah through production and distribution of nutritious food: 44 unskilled women and FHH from Rafah, will work producing traditional pastries. The activity will be hosted by a CBO, ensuring easy access to the beneficiaries. Each beneficiary will receive a 2-month CfW opportunity. Processed food will be distributed to 1,500 vulnerable families. Act05: Assistance to vulnerable unemployed women through the production and distribution of clothing items to vulnerable children from Khan Younis: 44 unemployed women and FHH from Khan Younis will be selected to produce clothing. Each beneficiary will receive 2-month CfW opportunity. The finished products will go to 3,000 vulnerable children.

Indicators

Mercy Corps’ Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning unit will be involved from the beginning of implementation and ensure the outputs and outcomes of the project are on track by conducting regular site visits, spot checks, Data Quality Assurance, Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), and beneficiary feedback surveys. Project team from Mercy Corps and local partner NGOs will oversee the day to day implementation of the project and produce regular reports. All project activities will be closely coordinated with Food Security Sector coordinator in Gaza. Regular progress reports showing the project activities progress and deliverables will be prepared and shared with all relevant stakeholders and UNOCHA.Mercy Corps promotes a participatory approach to project cycle management. Thus, women, men, farmers, breeders, and other venerable groups were involved in the assessment and design of the project. Community participation in the decision making and design of activities is ensured through the Community Committees (CCs). The CCs are setup in each community through the engagement with local CBOs/NGOs s as well as a cadre of key informed decision makers who represent their respective communities' interests and needs, and assure that the grassroots knowledge of community needs is considered in the design and implementation of activities. Their engagement ensures a fair and transparent selection of beneficiaries. Moreover, to promote local community ownership, Mercy Corps’ partner local NGOs will participate in the actual implementation, monitoring and evaluating the project through assigned project coordinators and social workers, regular meetings and field visits to get timely feedback about the different interventions and emerging needs, and to make corrections if necessary.As part of our accountability to beneficiaries, Mercy Corps maintains a “Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism – CARM” system, that creates accessible, and secure reporting mechanisms to all beneficiaries;
