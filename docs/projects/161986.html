Project Summary

The purpose of the intervention is to provide vulnerable populations with equitable access to safe water supplies, dignified and safe latrines and bathing facilities and to provide information on critical hygiene promotion information and training with the aim of changing behaviour away from potentially harmful practices. The provision of the services will focus on inclusive access for all and will consider especially the needs of women, children, the elderly and people living with disabilities. The target population are 241,754 Rohingya refugees living in 8 refugee camps and 150,000 Bangladeshi people living in Teknaf and Uhkia who are affected by the refugee influx.

Objectives

1. To ensure regular, sufficient, equitable and dignified access for all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities to sufficient and safe water for drinking and domestic needs. 2. To ensure all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities have adequate, appropriate and acceptable sanitation facilities to allow rapid, safe and secure access at all times.3. To ensure the change of potentially dangerous behaviours through access and exposure to innovative hygiene promotion (including hygiene items) for all refugees living in camps and affected host communities.

Needs

Improved Water Services: Around 30% of refugees in camps are now accessing chlorinated water through piped distribution networks which are being constructed under the Sector’s Water Network Plan. The remainder continue to access water from tubewells fitted with handpumps. There is a need to complete the Sector Water Network plan and ensure all refugees are accessing safe, chlorinated water from the piped networks and to reduce the reliance on tubewells and handpumps which have a risk of contamination. Access to Sanitation: 99% of households report using pit latrines in the camps however functionality is recorded at 78%. There is a need to improve operation & maintenance including community engagement for cleaning & safe operation. Just under half of the refugees state facing challenges of accessing toilets due to overcrowding or distance from households. There is a need to ensure inclusive use of all latrines by women, children, people living with disabilities and the elderly and that all latrines are appropriate for safe and dignified use by all. There is still a significant need for further construction of sanitation and hygiene infrastructure to bring coverage up to minimum Sector standards and ensure equitable distribution. There is a need to increase coverage of FSM sites to ensure all latrines are being emptied regularly and the sewage safely treated and disposed of. Use of safe bathing spaces is reported to be low at 37% and people, especially women, are using makeshift bathing spaces inside their shelters. Solid Waste Management (SWM) remains a challenge in the camps, with only 33% of the refugee households reporting using household bins or shared garbage bins Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: appropriate hygiene practices for specific risk behaviours remain low with for example, rates of households reporting washing their hands after handling children’s faeces at 32% , and appropriate safe water handling at household level where there re high levels contamination of stored water. Overall, 26% of households reported presence of human faeces in their areas , likely due to the lack of safe disposal of faeces of children under 5 years old, who are also reported as practicing open defecation regularly (46%). A total of 91% of households reported having soap, with 94% of men and 93% of women reporting washing their hands after using the latrine. In respect of Menstrual Hygiene Management, 24% of women and girls were reported as not receiving MHM materials within the last 3 months. In addition, 20% of women and girls were reported as practicing safe disposal of MHM materials.

Activities or outputs

SO 1. Institutional Capacity Building activities to strengthen the quality insurance system related to the provision of water and sanitation services. It includes the development and management of WASH infrastructure databases, the surveillance and monitoring of water quality, the development of quality standards and their monitoring. Complete construction of piped water networks in line with Sector Water Master Plan and development of sustainable operation and maintenance frameworks. Major and minor repairs of handpumps and tubewells including decommissioning where necessary based on risk assessment. Contingency planning for provision of safe water in the event of monsoon, cyclone or other natural disaster. In Host Communities, construction of piped water networks and construction, repair and maintenance of deep tubewells fitted with handpumps. Development and promotion of water safety plans. SO 2. Complete construction of FSM sites and systems providing 100% coverage to ensure all latrines are regularly emptied and kept functional. Development of operation and maintenance frameworks. Construction of new latrines and bathing cubicles to sector standard, including inclusive latrines and bathing facilities for the most vulnerable such as people living with disabilities and elderly people, as needed based on risk assessment. Adaption of existing latrines for inclusive use. Care and maintenance of latrines and bathing facilities including decommissioning where necessary based on risk assessment. Solid waste management systems based on collection bins, construction and operation of intermediary disposal sites. Contingency planning for provision of sanitation in the event of monsoon, cyclone or other natural disaster. In Host Communities, use of CATS approaches and sanitation marketing. Construction and rehabilitation of household latrines. WASH in Schools and Health centres. SO 3. Development of WASH Sector partner's capacity to implement and monitor efficient and effective hygiene promotion programmes. Identification of barriers to behaviour change and development of approaches to overcome these barriers. Training of outreach workers to mobilise and engage with communities and encourage health seeking behaviours. Support outreach workers to implement activities. Planning and delivery of communication campaigns. Provision of specific items, through voucher schemes wherever possible, for household water treatment, water transport and storage, personal and menstrual hygiene. Capacity building activities for WASH sector partners to be able to promote the protection, safety and dignity of targeted population. Supporting outreach activities to enhance planning and decision for women and for women and girls of reproductive age on menstrual hygiene management. Support outreach activities to consult and monitor with girls, boys, women, men, including older people and persons with disabilities to ensure equitable access and minimise risks of violence. Contingency planning for provision of hygiene promotion messages and supplies in the event of monsoon, cyclone or other natural disaster. In Host Communities Hygiene promotion through CATS approach implement the 3 Star approach in Schools.
