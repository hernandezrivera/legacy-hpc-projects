Project Summary

General objective: Contribute to a comprehensive Protection system of vulnerable
communities in the North-West Bank and Ramallah Governorate.
Outcomes:
1. Mitigate the impacts of conflict related violations (Settlers violence, demolitions,
excessive use of force by ISF, displacement) on the mental health and
psychosocial (here after called MHPSS) wellbeing of Palestinian communities living
in North West Bank including the North Jordan Valley and Ramallah Governorate.
2. Strengthen the resilience and positive coping mechanisms of Palestinian
communities living in North West Bank including the North Jordan Valley and Ramallah Governorate.
3. Third States Parties, notably France & EU Members are deeply mobilized and
take systematic and concrete measures toward the occupation practices which
negatively impact the protection of Palestinians and their effective access to
services in the WB
Within this action, MDM will contribute to the Protection Cluster Objectives 1 and 2,
both relating to the Strategic objective 1 of the HRP: The rights of Palestinians living
under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions,
are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL.
In relation with the Objective 2, MDM intervention focuses in the provision of MH and
Psychosocial services. MDM implement Emergency MHPSS support as well as
resilience-based interventions (to improve positive coping mechanisms) and case
management in the communities of the North-West Bank and Ramallah governorate most exposed to Settlers violence, demolitions, ISF violations and possible forcible transfer.
MDM advocacy efforts will also contribute to the Objective 1 of the Protection
cluster “Increase Respect of IHL & HRL”. The Advocacy contribution of MDM will be
through field briefings to diplomats and international media, production of periodic
reports and factsheets to reflect the impact of the violations in the MHPSS of the
Palestinian communities, in addition to a lobby tours in Paris and Brussels
addressing the same topics.

1 - Needs

Conditions of prolonged or protracted conflict and political violence have significant adverse effects on all members of society. The level of exposure to violence and the resulting elevated levels of distress may result in an increased risk to develop psychopathology; including, post-traumatic stress symptomatology, mood disorders and disruptive behaviours, together with somatic symptoms. This is especially the case where exposure to traumatic events is repeated and cumulative. Indeed, the resulting post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and trauma may be chronic as a result of repeated and pervasive exposure to political violence. In the WB (West Bank), the humanitarian impact of the Israeli settlers violence, ISF use of force and demolitions is significant in terms of fostering a chronic environment of fear and intimidation, characterised by episodes of violence and harassment. This environment has a serious psychological impact on victims and witnesses of violent attacks, affecting the psychosocial well-being of women, men, girls and boys. The types of impacts recorded by MdM during the current provision of MHPSS to victims of settler violence include strong feelings of frustration (particularly for men), constant feelings of fear and insecurity, eating disorders, sleep disturbances (particularly for children), anxiety (particularly for women), depression and behavioural problems. These symptoms lead to aggressiveness, violence, social withdrawal, and academic deterioration and poor school performance for children. The psychosocial impact of Israeli settler violence against Palestinians has a greater impact on females, showing that the lifetime prevalence of PTSD is significantly higher in women than men, and reinforcing the evidence that gender is an extremely salient risk factor, even when controlling differences in the type of events that are experienced by men compared to women. Far for moving toward a more optimistic scenario, up to October 2019, MDM has observed a general increase in the number of Settlers Violence incidents with Nablus and Ramallah being the most affected , that has been reflected in the number of people in need of MHPSS support. In the first ten months of 2019 MDM France has provided MHPSS to 3120 persons (1301 women, 307 girls, 1203 men and 309 boys) Out of this people assessed, 933 persons (30%) were in need of emergency psychological aid (394 females,96 girls, 100 boys and 343 men); with up to 58% of survivors reporting severe signs of acute stress. Psychological First Aid (PFA) is important to mitigate legitimate distress and diminish the chances to develop severe disorders in the future, being a key intervention in the Palestinian context. However, PFA alone may not be sufficient to support the affected communities who face complex traumatic events, as there are people in need of more specialized services including advanced counselling, short term psychotherapy and psychiatric services. From April 2017 and until October 2019 MDM referred 86 cases to specialized services (40% females,15% girls, 16% boys and 29% men). Unfortunately, up to 50% of whom did not reach the services. Insufficient skilled MH staff at primary health care level, together with the reluctance of the individuals to seek more advanced services mainly for stigma related issues, economic reasons and geographical access make necessary to reinforce MHPSS services at community level through innovative approaches, such as low intensity psychological interventions (WHO 2016) and capacity building of community key actors as schools counselors. In complement to the above actions which aim to treat moderate psychological problems, other complementary activities aiming to strengthen the resilience and coping skills of individuals and communities are needed. 2 - Activities

Output 1: Increased access to immediate MHPSS and to a holistic protection response through case management for the victims of critical events. The objective is to mitigate the psychological and psycho-social impacts of the critical incident on individuals, families and communities. These cases are identified by MDM from different sources: direct referral from communities/local authorities, referrals from MHPSS working group and other protection cluster partners. The main activity is the provision of MHPSS Emergency Response which includes a comprehensive series of activities:(1) Support visit including PFA within 72 hours.(2) Support visit including a Psychosocial Assessment within 10-15 days. (3) Referrals to ensure the response to other basic and protection needs done through the Protection cluster and OCHA, including livelihood, shelter, legal and health actors. (4) Low intensity psychological interventions for individuals affected by moderate psychological problems such as moderate anxiety and/or depression.(5) Referrals to MH specialized services for the management and treatment of detected mental health conditions in need of advanced support. There are 2 systems, 1 internal within MDM and another external to MHPSS working group actors and MoH according to needs, user preference and geographical area. MDM is directly reporting its MHPSS interventions to the OCHA Settlers violence dashboard and in regular and monthly basis to the MHPSS Working Group.Output 2: Increased Resilience capacity of the communities through Community Psychoeducation and MHPSS preparedness at community level. 1. Community Psychoeducation: the main aim is to make the Communities aware about the impact of traumatic events on their psychosocial wellbeing and mental health as well as the resources and services available to alleviate it. These sessions usually address critical incidents impacts on women, men, girls and boys and the potential ways on dealing with each impact based on the gender and age of participants. Brochures (10,000 copies) will be distributed within the sessions. (2) MHPSS Preparedness at community level including schools: the objective is to reinforce the community emergency response capacity, through school emergency PSS teams (MOEHE established 17 PSS emergency teams within schools in the WB to enable them to respond to critical incidents within the schools). MDM will reinforce 6 Schools MoEHE mobile PSS teams to respond to critical incidents in the NWB. MDM and the MoEHE started already to work with 5 MoEHE PSS teams in 2017 in the NWB, training and supervising its members. This project will continue the technical supervision, operational follow up and accompany in the field. It will target the PSS Team of Ramallah Governorate and will work on its capacities and follow up. MDM will train the school counsellors and teachers of the hotspots schools on provision of PFA within their schools Environment. This activity is implemented under the umbrella of the General School Counselling Department of the MOEHE which is in charge of the coordination of this activity with the Education Cluster. MDM and the Education Cluster discussed the importance of this activity on several occasions and it is the result of the assessment workshop conducted in December 2018 and confirmed by MOEHE Output 3: Advocacy initiatives that aim to enhance the accountability for violations of IHRL & IHL and the engagement of Third States Parties. MdM collects quantitative + qualitative information to raise awareness of public opinion and mobilize EU decision makers to effectively act according IHRL and IHL standards. The activities will include: (1) Production of MdM own documentation and testimonies about the impact of critical events on MHPSS wellbeing. (2) Contribution to joint reports (Settlement expansion and Practices Group, AIDA, UN, ...).(3) Organization and participation in decision-makers briefing.(4) Lobby tours in Paris + Brussels

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

When feedback or complains are received through any of the mechanisms in place, an immediate reply will be provided to the user with a reference number so the user can follow-up and track the process. The complain will be processed first by the MEAL officer who will categorize feedback within different categories (misconduct, abuse, dissatisfaction, suggestions...) and based on that will take up the feedback to coordination level. If feedback is considered relevant to proceed with further actions, a review ethical committee will be formed (in which members of the community involved will take part) to analyze the case and make decisions. The committee will gather information and conduct hearings and visits. The user(s) who provided feedback will be continuously updated about the process through email or phone, and final decisions and actions will be officially communicated through official mechanisms (letter).

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

MDM team providing the MHPSS ER to victims and vulnerable community members is a well-trained, supervised and gender balanced team who is updated about the cultural, religious, context and security situation in MDM operational area of coverage including towns, villages and Bedouin communities. MDM interventions are provided through outreach to the households of the victims (MHPSS ER) and within their neighborhoods and communities (psychoeducation) that reduce the exposure of the victims to harm and provide equal access. MDM security department and team are continuously assessing the locations of the activities conducted in the field to ensure safety and security of beneficiaries and MDM team. When MDM team receives a trigger of critical incident from OCHA, Protection cluster, community members, …, the team ensure gathering the proper and enough information about the incident from different sources to avoid the repetition of the story by the victims and avoid further exposure to Trauma. Confidentiality and privacy of MHPSS interventions is a crucial principle applied by all MDM team , within their interviews , data and information collected and shared even when referring clients to other services , a clear referral protocol developed by MDM and MOH in 2015 is used for that , MDM is always ensuring that individual sessions are conducted in privacy even from same family members . Since June 2019, MDM is using digital data collection tools, through the use of barcode system, MDM is anonymizing the data collected and ensure beneficiaries data protection. MDM MHPSS team is programing the interventions based on community members adequate time intervening with women within the morning hours , with children after the school time and with men within the evenings and the weekends when needed . Within its case studies and advocacy documentation , MDM is keeping the publications anonymous to avoid the identification of cases , even though these publications are published with a previous written consent from the beneficiaries . Within its resilience based activities , MDM is reinforcing the local community positive coping mechanism and mobilizing the community resources towards community protection .

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Impartiality, non-discrimination , gender and inclusion are adopted by MDM within its intervention in Palestine . Within its MDM intervention , when MDM receive a trigger about a critical incident , MDM team gather all the related information about the community members affected , communicate with the beneficiaries and set an appointment for the intervention , female members of MDM team intervene with females and girls and male members intervene with males and boys respecting the culture of traditions of the local community . Within its psycho-education activities, MDM is conducting specific sessions for males and females , reaching females at community level through the village councils and CBOs and reaching males through the sport clubs and within working places . Within 2020 strategy, MDM will reinforce the capacity building and supervision of MDM field staff on gender, GBV and inclusion of PWD. MdM will strengthen the development of specific methodology to address needs based upon multi-gender approach (gender specific health) (for instance, developing different IEC for women and men, habilitating different spaces, developing different protocols based upon gender based different manifestations…) and also addressing all the barriers preventing PWD to access to services. By its outreach program MDM is able to reach the majority of the victims affected by the critical events , by the support provided by a well prepared Logistic department , MDM team is able to reach the scattered Bedouin communities , farmers and shepherds in their communities and place of work . To ensure basic and protection needs , MDM is using several referral approaches through the Protection cluster and its working groups , by liaising with the local authorities or by mobilizing the community resources in cooperation with CBOs and village councils , ensuring access of community members in need for such services . By having an outreach program, MDM MHPSS emergency response team is targeting and reaching all victims of critical incidents including PWD in their households and schools. PWD are referred with a closer follow up to basic and protection services in coordination with operational protection actors under the umbrella of the Protection Cluster, the Ministry of Social Development and other local authorities. Within its psychoeducation sessions MDM is delivering individual invitations to PWD through the village councils and CBOs with whom MDM is coordinating these sessions. By 2020, MDM will add PWD to all its monitoring, evaluation and reporting process.
