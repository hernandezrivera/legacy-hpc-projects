Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to strengthen the resilience and livelihoods of Gazan fishing communities through the provision of emergency inputs and technical support that would protect and sustain endangered fishers in the Gaza Strip.
This project will target the fishery sector as a whole, particularly as Gaza’s fishery sector is a significant source of employment, income and a supply of high protein food for the population, yet due to external forces and ongoing access restrictions, fishing households are some of the most affected by food insecurity in Gaza. Over half of this population group are food insecure and have become increasingly so, compared to other population groups. The fishing community will be supported through the provision of specialized fishing materials that would allow fishers to reach deeper and wider fishing areas without being endangered by the Israeli restrictions. In doing so, this project will contribute to socio economic
stabilization and will increase the resilience of fishing households and communities
through increased income and subsequent reduction in vulnerability and aid dependency.
The current project is aligned with FAO’s global, regional and national level frameworks and objectives, as well as with the national development strategies defined by the Palestinian
Authority (PA) and the United Nations Development Assistance Framework (UNDAF). This project also aligns with the published 2030 UN Sustainable Development Goals; in particular Goal 2: End hunger, achieve food security and improved nutrition and promote sustainable agriculture; Goal 8: Promote sustained, inclusive and sustainable economic growth, full and productive employment and decent work for all and Goal 14: Conserve and sustainably use the oceans, seas and marine resources for sustainable development.
The project is also fully aligned with the National Agriculture Sector Strategy “Resilience and Sustainable Development” 2017-2022, developed by the MoA, where supporting economic growth, food security and private sector resilience through optimized agricultural value chains remained a top priority as clearly expressed in the SOs 1, 3 4 and 5 of the strategy. The project is also aligned to CPF output 3.1 “National Capacities to protect highly vulnerable groups livelihoods further enhanced.” Under indicator 3.1.1. SO 5.4.2. The project falls under Strategic Objective 3 of the Humanitarian Response Strategy "The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including
from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of
threats and shocks are pursued." And Food Security Cluster objective 1 "The resilience , and food security and productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored, enhanced and livelihoods protected".
This project is planned for one year.

1 - Needs

Fish consumption in the Gaza Strip is very low (3.5kg/person/year) compared with the average consumption of the 5 neighbouring countries (Egypt, Israel, Cyprus, Turkey, Lebanon: approx. 15kg/person/year); as is economic access to fish for Gazan households. Both realities are largely due to low catch/production output per year (Approximately 3,200 MT of caught fish in 2017, though a slight increase from the 2,700 MT reported in 2014); high production costs leading to high prices for fish in local markets and high poverty levels, particularly within Gazan fishing communities. Further influencing limited growth of the fishing sector are access restrictions off the Gaza coast. The Oslo II Accord stipulates a fishing distance of 20 nautical miles within the coast, though in practice, the Israel limits the fishing zone to between 3 and 12 nautical miles. Better quality fish, with higher resale value are located in ocean areas with deep ocean floors. For fishers to capture most valuable fish, special nets able to penetrate deep waters provide a viable alternative to other low-tech fishing solutions. This project aims to respond to this existing problem through the provision of time critical in-kind support to 400 vulnerable fishers such as echo sounders and GPS devices, drift nets, long fishnets. Notwithstanding these restrictions, sea fishing currently accounts for the majority of domestic fish supply in the coastal territory (95%- sea catch, 5% aquaculture, 2014). Yet given the current and fluctuating access restrictions to the sea imposed by Israel, there is a risk that this source of livelihood for around 3,617fishers (as of 2017) and their families (particularly females) would become increasingly unreliable and less resilient. Moreover, the fisheries sector is mostly comprised of a poor workforce using traditional, artisanal methods with low labour productivity providing minimal income. Other challenges facing fishermen include: import restrictions of essential equipment for restoring/maintaining productive assets and often violent exchanges with Israeli Security Forces off-shore leading to damages and/or confiscation of assets. According to the detailed Needs Assessment and Recovery Framework for Gaza, there are up to 24,000 households in Gaza dependent on agricultural livelihoods including farmers, herders and fishers; all livelihood types suffered damage and losses during the conflicts and particularly female-headed households. This included destruction of productive assets such as farmland, greenhouses, wells, herds, animal sheds, boats, fishing gear and food processing plants. These successive shocks are having detrimental immediate and long-term impacts on the capacity of food producers to sustain themselves and the local demand for agriculture products.Finally, the ongoing blockade of Gaza, enforced by Israeli and Egyptian Forces, has resulted in drastic reductions of basic (re)construction materials, certain medical supplies and a variety of agricultural inputs. This has led to stagnancy and low resilience within most agricultural value chains, in particular, the fisheries value chain. Most value chains are now characterized as low in productivity, profitability and investments. In close coordination with the Department of Fishery at the MoA, FAO has identified the following pressing needs that need to be addressed for HRP 2020: 1) Highly vulnerable, impoverished sub sector with major production restrictions (i.e. limited access to sea, adequate fishing tools and maintenance equipment); 2) Current catch methods are endangering future stocks (i.e. over-fishing due to undersized mesh nets); 3) Fish consumption is low. There is potential to expand by a factor of 4 when comparing nearby countries, leading to increased nutritional value of diets; 4) Consumption grows faster than production, leading to imports increases, which worsen the economic perspectives.

2 - Activities

Accordingly, FAO has planned the following activities to address the emergent needs. With the suggested interventions explained below, FAO will be able to address these needs and enhance fish consumption in the Gaza Strip which will increase the nutritional value of diets and provide food security for a bigger number of households. The interventions will provide support to 400 vulnerable fisher’s households. The fishers will be selected in coordination with MoA and the fisheries syndicate based on four main requirements; having a fisher’s license, practicing fishing, owning a boat and being an active member of the fisher’s syndicate. Prioritization of vulnerability factors will be further detailed in the preparation phase. 1. Provision of time critical in-kind support to 400 vulnerable fishers such as echo sounders and GPS devices, drift nets, long fishnets. This will allow fishermen to better identify fish shoals and avoid approaching areas where their lives or boats are endangered by Israeli restrictions.2. Distribution of essential repair and maintenance materials of boats to serve 400 fishers, fisher’s cooperatives, and fishers’ social business of the fisher’s syndicate . The targeted boats are ones that have not been used for a while or need maintenance to better serve the purpose. FAO will engage in coordination efforts with the relevant authorities to allow access of materials. FAO is aware of the dual-use limitations list and will ensure the facilitation of delivering the support with no conflict 3. Provision of tailored technical support to fishers, fisher’s cooperatives, and fishers’ social business to ensure best use of the in-kind support provided towards more resilience and improved income. Technical support will be coordinated with the MoA.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable fishers whose livelihoods are under the threat. The selection of fishers is solely based on needs and the most food-insecure and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory factors. The interventions proposed under this project do not put beneficiaries in risk, however, FAO aims to ensure that all efforts will be made to prevent and minimize as much as possible unintended negative effects of the interventions. As part of the project inception phase, FAO will develop a risk log identifying all potential risks, their probability and ways of mitigation. The assessment and analysis of risks will be followed by a coordinated application of resources to monitor, minimize and control the probability and/or impact of risks. External risk factors will continuously be considered in order to ensure an adequate understanding of the context of FAO operations during the implementation of the project. Through FAO’s participation in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the development of the political situation in the WBGS will regularly be monitored and reviewed in line with project objectives. FAO contributes to policy-level initiatives to protect Palestinians from further reduction in access to land, sea and livelihood resources (e.g. water and agricultural inputs) owing to the political situation, through its participation in the UNCT Commendation Group and HCT Advocacy Working Group. All advocacy efforts will be conducted under the banner of the UN in the WBGS to ensure that FAO’s mandate is not compromised.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

FAO’s approach provides a framework for addressing and integrating issues such as gender equality, protection, the inclusion of the aged and people living with disability, and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse by FAO and partner staff. Throughout the process of beneficiary selection, disability and age will be a factor taken into consideration. Beneficiaries with this vulnerability will be prioritized. The project also seeks to engage with targeted stakeholders and mainstream FAO’s commitments in each phase of the project cycle. FAO maintains its commitments through regular stakeholder engagement and participation, two-way communication, transparent disclosure and a grievance mechanism.
