Project Summary

A number of IDPs and vulnerable and marginalized communities are faced threats of eviction in IDP settlements in Lower Juba, Lower Shabelle and Bay Regions. Reasons for eviction are majorly landowners needing their land whereas the motive is not very clear. Disputes on land ownership are commonly faced by both IDP and host community household in terms of HLP and the vast majority of households do not have formal land tenure documents to prove their occupancy arrangement. Further unrealistic increase of rent or failure to pay rent are cited as some of the main reasons for evictions in Somalia. Further still, the arbitrary increase of rent or failure to pay rent are some of the main reasons for evictions in Somalia. Whereas displacement is in itself is a very traumatic event for households from both a material (loss of land and goods) and a psychological perspective (isolation and separation from family), there is limited access to justice for those affected by these evictions.
IDPs do have a range of HLP rights under the mix of statutory, customary, and Islamic regimes used in Somalia. Despite these protection, IDPs who seek to vindicate their rights are further disadvantaged by the nature of Somalia’s legal institutions that are not very clear on such. Of particular concern is the fact that reporting HLP specific cases face several obstacles within both formal and informal justice systems. Furthermore, there policy and legal frameworks governing the conduct of humanitarian action in Somalia. However, implementation of these policies remains varied due to fragmentation and frequent changes in government institutions and coordination structures. The situation is further worsened by the fact that justice, law and order sector structures in most Federal States have either been destroyed, or are not functioning due to their long absence from impacts of past conflicts. In some cases, the informal structures and mechanisms have substituted the role of authorities particularly in dispensing law and order and justice. Moreover, returns in several locations remain unsustainable due to ongoing insecurity and lack of support and resources.
The project targeted a total of 72000 person (29952 women, 27648 men, 7200 boys and 7200 girls) as well as those with disabilities and the elderly in Lower Juba, Lower Shabelle and Bay regions with appropriate HLP support.

1. - Projects activities

1. Provision case management services including assisting with mediation of disputes for IDPs in need of greater support to access their entitlements in the project locations in Bay, Lower Shabelle and Lower Juba Regions, Somalia 2. Provision of legal counselling and technical services for complications associated with obtaining land tenure documents that requires working with authorities to facilitate a resolution.3. Provision of legal aid to individualized legal services to male, female, boys and girls and those with disabilities on how to overcome specific obstacles related to the enjoyment of HLP rights.4. Provision of referral services for individuals/male, female, boys and girls and those with disabilities who require assistance beyond the HLP area of expertise5. Conducting workshops and trainings on HLP and dispute resolution for humanitarian actors, government officials, and local authorities in Bay, Lower Shabelle and Lower Juba Regions6. Conducting awareness campaigns and information sessions to provide information that is reliable and updated on how to claim and exercise HLP rights and entitlements during displacement. This includes the development, design, and dissemination of informational materials.7. Undertaking eviction risk assessments and monitor eviction trends in targeted areas in Bay, Lower Juba and Lower Shabelle regions, Somalia 8. Provision of cash/voucher assistance to support 4500HHs victims of eviction in IDP settlements in Lower Shabelle and Lower Juba Regions, Somalia

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash/vouchers will provide immediate assistance to the most vulnerable victims of eviction to meet their immediate food needs and that of their family members owing the fact that at the time of eviction the lose everything at the disposal including little food items at their houses/shelter. This is worsened by the fact that they are living in deplorable conditions with completely eroded purchasing powers and resort of marginal coping mechanism hence need to support them through cash/voucher/

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project will ensure that the value of the cash/voucher distributed to the targeted families/victims of eviction is enough to redeem particular food items for a particular period of time. This will be ensured by conducting market survey to determine extent of availability of food items in the local markets at the project target locations in terms of supply against existing levels of demand by the local communities.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1. Ensuring inclusion of minority and marginalized groups such as minority clans, IDPs, women,the elderly and persons with disabilities in the consultations on the project right from the commencement at the needs identification stage and ensuring they are part of key decision made regarding the project particularly during regular monitoring exercises where their voices and concerns with be incorporated in the suggestions and recommendation on the way the project is implemented till completion.2. Protection of women and girls from sexual Gender based violence by ensuring increased protection services by ensuring convenient access to the project planned services such as having different distribution points to ensure that they don't cover long distance to access them. Furthermore, the project services/activities will be conducted at convenient times of the day to limit women and girls from walking in the cover of darkness when cases of sexual violence are very rampant . The project will in addition be implemented under very strict adherence to PSEA policy and ensuring women and girls are fully aware of the project to mitigate sexual exploitation and abuse during the project time. Additionally, the project will ensure that complaints and feedback mechanisms are in place for easy access by the targeted beneficiaries and other community members.
