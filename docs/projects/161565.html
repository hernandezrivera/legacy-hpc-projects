1 - Needs

Malaria, acute respiratory tract infections and acute watery diarrhea are three of the top causes of morbidity and mortality among Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in three most affected states of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. The three states harbor over 5 million people who have been affected by the insurgency and malaria contributes 50% of all cases and deaths in these States. Malaria is endemic in NE and transmission is perennial with a marked seasonal peak from July to November each year. The onset of the rainy season (July through October) is expected to limit humanitarian relief access and exacerbate the shelter, food and health situation, with direct risks related to sanitation, nutrition and malaria.Although humanitarian and government actors have made progress in reducing morbidity and mortality rates to below emergency thresholds, the affected population remains at high-risk for malaria. Malaria remains the major cause of morbidity and the leading cause of death among children under the age of 5 years.Data from early warning and response system (EWARS) highlighted malaria as the biggest cause of morbidity and mortality in BAY state. Analysis commissioned by the WHO Global Malaria Programme to guide the next steps in the response to the malaria interventions in the complex humanitarian emergency in Borno concluded that “malaria is the number one health risk that the populations in the affected areas in NE Nigeria are confronting. It was not only the number one cause of morbidity during the past 9 months, but one can predict with great certainty that it is going to be the main cause of disease and death over the coming months as malaria transmission picks up.

2 - Activities

WHO developed a framework for addressing the malaria scourge in the area in consultations with the Global Malaria Programme and other humanitarian and malaria partners, Federal and State ministries of health. The guideline emphasizes the need for a stratified approach towards malaria control in the affected states as follows:1. Strengthening the surveillance system through routine Health Management Information System (HMIS) as well as expanding EWARS coverage.2. Vector control based on IRS at eligible IDP camps (camps with proper housing) as well as mass distribution of LLINs for all the population.3. Chemoprevention (SMC) to all children less than 5 years (ideally 4 to 5 monthly cycles throughout the peak transmission season) and IPTp to all pregnant women4. Increasing the population’s access to case management, including the use of RDT and ACT.
