Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve the access of 6000 newly displaced persons from Idleb and Aleppo countryside(1680 men , 1800 women , 1200 boys , 1320 girls)especially children , women, people with disabilities and the elderly, to emergency food assistance in the modality of cooked meals for a period of 6 months until the new displaced have access to adequate food from other sources.
The project will focus primarily on new arrivals from southern of Idleb and western of Aleppo who arrived to the reception centers, collective shelters , informal settlement and collective homes, each beneficiary will receive one cooked meal per day and cover 60% of the daily needs of the individual, ranging from 980 to 1422 calories and with the average of 1244 calories
This will be done by constructing and operating two kitchens(one in Maaret Elekhwan and one in Dana) to provide cooked meals for the new IDPs who arrived from Ma'arrat An Nu'man , Ariha, Ehsem , Atareb , Saraqab , Teftnaz ,Sarmin , Bennsh, sub-districts and has a capacity of 6000 meals per day
The meals will be prepared according to a weekly program where this program will achieve a variety of cooked meals basing on the standards of Sphere in the amount of protein and fat taking into account the percentage of calcium in each meal for the health of pregnant and lactating mothers and be compatible with the culture of the beneficiaries and their habits with the possibility of changing the meals according to the feedback from the beneficiaries. After cooking the meals and packaging them in the kitchens , SCA will move them to the to the sites of IDPs and distribute the cooked meals in coordination with the committees representing the beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

According to the figures issued by the CCCM Cluster, the top 2 sub-districts that received IDPs in NW Syria are Dana (209,705) and Maaret Tmesrin(62,537). SCA team conducted a quick needs assessment of those camps with largest numbers of new IDPs to determine best practices and most suitable methodologies to provide food for those camps. Sample of the assessment was 2 camps which suffer food gaps 200 (of them 20 female-headed HHs) families. So, the following results were - 38% of the sample responded that they are having one meal daily. - 19 % of the sample responded that they are not able to cook even though they have food raw materials because they cannot afford having cooking fuel and they lack the cooking space in the tent, especially for large families- 13% of the sample emphasized that they don’t have enough and food suitable kitchen utensils including cooking stoves, especially those who newly were displaced to camps- 62% of the sample responded that they are not able to meet the food needs for their families during winter because of the total absence of jobs and any savings.- 17% of the sample meets the common standard vulnerability criteria (Female headed HHs, disabled or elderly headed HHs, HHs with more than 2 children under 5, HHs having disabilities). reached: - 23 women of the sample is pregnant and lactating women.By analyzing the previous results we found that the best suitable way to provide emergency food is in shape of cooked meals. Taking into consideration availability of kitchen assets at SCA and this way of providing food was welcomed by beneficiaries of the previous project implemented by SCA in the same areas and locations. In addition to, SCA wide experience in making and distributing food in shape of cooked meals.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1.1.1: Selecting beneficiaries and distribution points for men and women separately.and providing each HH with a voucher to facilitate distribution processes and ensure that each family receive its portion of assistance.Activity 1.1.2: Establishing community committees for men and women separately in each camp and prompting people to participate in project and applying accountability procedures.Activity 1.1.3: Purchase of food items for cooking meals, dry materials will be purchased in batches according to the supply plan compatible with warehouse capacity, fresh and perishable items will be purchased daily and transferred to the kitchen.Activity 1.1.4: Preparation of cooked meals in the kitchen on a daily basis 6000 meals per day according to the weekly meal program with the possibility of changing numbers and quantities according to developments in the context and displacement movements.Activity 1.1.5: Distribution of cooked meals to the beneficiaries (1680 men, 1800 women, 1200 boys, 1320 girls) on family basis, considering family member numberActivity 1.1.6: Implementation of the M & E Plan and accountability to affected population and response to any deviations or remarksActivity 1.1.7: Distributing utensils for beneficiaries in average of 2 pieces for each HH Activity 1.1.6 :Printing brochures and illustrations and distributing them to the beneficiaries in suitable places within the project implementation areasActivity 1.1.7 : Conducting 2 raising awareness sessions for men , women and children separately at the implementation areas.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Possible new emergency displacementsStatus : high Mitigation Measures : 1-SCA M&E team will assess the needs and share them with cluster2-SCA will operate the kitchens with full capacity 3-SCA will discuss any changes in project and services provided closely with FSL cluster Risk 2 : Potential changes in the context, especially movements of IDPs from the planned temporary sites to be covered within this projectStatus : high Mitigation Measures 1- SCA will reassess and identify gaps based on the new context in collaboration with FSL Cluster and making the necessary changes to fill the updated gaps2- SCA will do the necessary changes in locations, number of beneficiaries, number of daily cooked meals after the approval of the FSL cluster. Risk 3: risks related to storing large amounts of food such as damage and security risks. Status : low Mitigation Measures :1- SCA will procure the dry foodstuff for the kitchens in batches congenial with the capacity of the stores in each kitchen 2- SCA will procure the wet foodstuff that might spoil on daily bases such as yogurt , meat etc.. 3- SCA M&E team will check daily the status of dry foodstuff before using

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk : Risks arising from armed groups clashes or exposing areas of implementation to military attacks that expose the safety of the staff and beneficiaries to real risk.Status : Low Mitigation Measures : 1- SCA security officer will follow the security situation in the areas of the project and report any risks of this kind to the project Manager in Turkey 2- In case of any urgent shelling or bombardment to any of the locations targeted within the project , SCA will work with the FSL cluster and take the appropriate decision(suspension of project activities or changing the geographical locations of the project to much safer locations
