Project Summary

Humanitarian relief assistance to suffering people in Njikwa - including IDPs, Host Communities, Victims of the crisis by way of NFI (shelter kits and core relief items, WASH services and kits), Food supply to over 3000 household targeted with varying needs. This will cost about US$80,000.
This project targets about 3000 households as IDPs and host community for NFI - Core Relief Items, WASH Kits and nutritive food assistance, 1200 household whose homes were either burnt or damaged and looted in need of shelter kits. 208 Adolescents and young adults at risk and associated with armed groups and 300 teenage mothers as victims of the crisis in need of clothing for children and livelihood income generating activities as quick impact projects - poultry, piggery and agriculture. .
There are about 300 Mborolo community households in Njikwa that are highly affected and threatened for their purported involvement in the crisis supporting the government that caused much pains and loses for the people. As a consequence trust was lost and the Mborolos are being chased away and have suffered a great loss of their livelihood -cattle and sheep and some have been killed in the course of the crisis. Most of them live in forest and will not come out even when they are sick. They are in need of clothes, blankets, pullovers, basic CRIs and even Shelter Kits.
This project hopes that this time Njikwa will be remembered by the international humanitarian responders and the feeling of being abandoned wiped from their minds and consequential recruitment of young adults and adolescents greatly reduced or stopped

Besoins / Needs

For over three years now Cameroon has been hit by the "Anglophone Crisis" in the English speaking regions of the country. Towns, Villages and homes have been disrupted with all fundamental institutions in the society affected including basic family units. Many families have been disintegrated, others managed by single parents - mostly women, in some only children are left and taking care of by external caregivers who might not even be related to the children. Many deaths recorded and others missing, while some have ran away from their homes because of threats to life, persistent harassment for ransoms. Nutritional values and quality have fallen, healthcare services limited or absent, some have no shelters, no access to education and moral values have drastically dropped.Njikwa Sub Division has been highly hit especially Ngwo and Oshie Villages where all public and social services are situated and have been shut down. The two villages have been the battle ground, about 1000 home were either burnt or damaged and all the occupants fled to either bushes or nearby villages. Two major markets were looted and burnt. A massive recruitment of NSAG from the angry community sprung up in self defense and today there are no schools, no healthcare services, no administrative services, no market, churches occasionally shut down. The district hospital in Ngwo - Njikwa was damaged and looted, the Health Centre in Oshie and other health post in the various villages were shut down and all the doctors and nurses fled. All the schools in Njikwa - 7 secondary/technical/SAR-SM and 24 primary are closed and teachers fled affected with a total of about 7000 children who dropped out of school and about 3000 who missed the opportunity to begin schooling. 68 homes were burnt down affecting over 80 families who lost basic needs -shelters, clothing and bedding. 48 shops were burnt as the only source of supply of basic needs. Over 1000 homes are damaged and looted and about 3000 IDPs within Njikwa. An estimated 208 boys and girls adolescents and young adults are associated with armed groups. About 300 cases of teenage mothers noticed. Broken water pipelines with no repairs since all technicians fled. Any gunshot already put the community into confusion and at flight into the bushes leaving the old and children behind who end up suffering. Those who fled into the bushes sleep in the wild with nothing to cover, no lamps for lighting, no matches to lit the fire. So there is great need for temporary shelter kits, basic core relief items and lamps, Njikwa has no electricity, no radio or TV signals, the telephone network damaged and looted and so no way to transmit information on any sudden attack for the safety of the people and so most often people are caught in cross fire where they end up sustaining many injury. The sporadic shooting by both the military and NSAG even when there is no confrontation between them, the frequent patrol, arresting of people and invasion of homes by the faction and the continuous closure of the market and hospital when they timidly open has greatly affected people especially the children, women and the aged. There are about 300 Mborolo community households in Njikwa that are highly affected and threatened for their purported involvement in the crisis supporting the government that caused much pains and loses for the people. As a consequence trust was lost and the Mborolos are being chased away and have suffered a great loss of their livelihood -cattle and sheep and some have been killed in the course of the crisis. Most of them live in forest and will not come out even when they are sick. They are in need of clothes, blankets, pullovers, basic CRIs and even Shelter Kits.This project hopes that this time Njikwa will be remembered by the humanitarian responders and the feeling of being abandoned wiped from their minds and restore trust to responders.
