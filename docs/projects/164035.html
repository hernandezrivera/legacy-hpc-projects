Project Summary

IRC’s Integrated Protection program will deliver activities across all protection sub-sectors through IRC’s Women’s Protection and Empowerment, Child Protection and Protection Rule of Law teams in Raqqa, Hasakeh and Deir ez Zor governorates and through partnerships with LNGOs, reaching 31,000 vulnerable conflict-affected individuals. By working through an integrated approach, IRC aims to respond to the protection needs of all community members, including children, adults and persons with specific vulnerabilities such as the elderly and PwD. This integrated approach sits within a multi-sector response provided with IRC’s Health and Economic Recovery and Development programs, with all three sectors – where possible - programming in the same locations. IRC Integrated Protection activities are delivered through multiple modalities, including through a center-based approach (e.g. WGSS or SHLS), through mobile teams (e.g. the Protection Rapid Response mechanism) and through community-based interventions (such as CP committees and home-visiting programs).

4 - Needs

Preliminary findings from the 2020 HNO reinforce that there continue to be a range of complex and inter-linked protection concerns that are further compounded by protracted and repeat displacements. The recent displacements caused by the conflict is further exacerbating protection risks as already vulnerable populations are displaced and sheltered in often poor conditions with lack of WASH facilities, privacy and safety, increasing the risk of GBV. 108,514 people remain displaced from Hasakeh, Raqqa and Aleppo. A needs assessment with conducted by IRC in November 2019 in Hasakeh and Raqqa found that 50% of displaced children were experiencing changes in their behavior, including inability to sleep, nightmares, aggression, withdrawal and general fear, pointing to psychosocial and psychological distress and trauma. RNAs conducted in host communities and collective shelters since this wave displacement identified a number of protection concerns including a lack of services and access to services for vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities as well as unaccompanied and separated children. Lack and loss of civil documentation persists as a feature, limiting individual’s freedom of movement and access to services. The risk of injury due to contamination is heightened both as a result of new contamination from recent hostilities as well as due to the cessation of clearance of explosive hazards. Women and girls are disproportionally affected by GBV, such as early marriage, sexual violence and domestic violence. As reported during a protection assessment conducted by the IRC , women and girls do not communicate their needs if there is no communal area or women-only space, particularly in relation to adolescent girls. The same is true of other excluded community members, such as PWD, the elderly, children and persons with mental and physical health concerns.

5 - Implementation of activities

IRC’s Integrated Protection program will deliver activities in Raqqa, Hasakeh and Deir ez Zor and through partnerships with LNGOs. IRC Integrated Protection activities are delivered through a center-based approach, mobile teams and community-based interventions. Activities will include:Awareness-raising sessions on issues such as early marriage, child rights and legal topics;Community-based protection services to strengthen community and family support (IASC MHPSS Pyramid Level 2) and structured group support activities tailored for men, women, youth, boys and girls;Protection training on GBV and CP core concepts, safe identification and referral and humanitarian principles for local actors, INGOs, community members, local authorities and others;Protection monitoring and reports to be shared with the PWG;Case management for PSN targeting PWD, older persons, persons with serious medical needs and others requiring dedicated support;GBV response non-specialized services will be divided into structured curriculum-baed and unstructured PSS activities;GBV case management services for survivors, in line with IASC GBV Case Management Guidelines and a survivor-centered approach and in adherence with principles of confidentiality, non-discrimination, dignity/respect and right to safety;Structured CP PSS for boys and girls in the form of Social and Emotional Learning sessions, and for caregivers in the form of parenting skills education;Parenting programmes (Reach Up and Learn) for children aged 6-42 months and their parents and caregivers, using home visits or an adapted center-based, group model depending on context;CP case management for at-risk children and parents and caregivers; Protection mainstreaming assessments and implementation plans to ensure meaningful access to services.M&E: Well-being and satisfaction surveys; feedback mechanisms including child-friendly channels; monitoring visits to IRC static facilities

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Community and family stigmatization and discrimination of and violence against children, women and persons with specific needs receiving specialized services provision such as case management services, Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: Use community acceptance strategy, including through the delivery of non-specialized services as entry-point and trust-building with the community. Ensure specialized services are properly messaged to the community. Embed specialized services in broader service-provision to de-stigmatization, using confidential and discrete spaces (WGSS).Risk: Lack of community acceptance/community tensions resulting content that is not properly contextualized (e.g. PSS curricula, awareness raising messaging). Risk to IRC and staff also. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: Ensure review of standardized materials with national team members and through community-consultations, including through age and gender disaggregated FGDs, contextualizing content accordingly. Implement feedback mechanisms to collect beneficiary feedback Risk: Community tensions resulting from expectations of service delivery following protection monitoring and by asking sensitive questions, also placing IRC and its staff at risk Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: Train staff on introduction of protection monitoring to communities. Ensure information from protection monitoring is disseminated with relevant stakeholders so that any information collected is adequately acted upon. Review protection monitoring questions and adapt to context.Risk: Barrier to beneficiaries’ access of protection services due to physical impediments (such as the selection of their location) as well as normative restrictions to access Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: Conduct inclusion analysis, deploy community outreach teams to support with referrals of vulnerable individuals and integrate protection service delivery across other sector responses.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Data protection, including breaches in sensitive data, particularly case management and health data. For Protection, this will be mitigated by the upcoming roll out of the CPIMS+ which IRC is co-leading with SCI and upcoming implementation of GBVIMS.Community acceptance, especially when working with sensitive protection topics in conservative areas. A gradual, phased approach to programming is adopted, building trust through non-specialized services before the introduction of more sensitive and specialized programming.Diversion: IRC shares FARM materials and explains to beneficiaries that they are not expected to provide anything to anyone to benefit from the assistance. IRC closely monitors risk using the FARM and PDM, and any evidence of malfeasance is reported to IRC’s Ethics and Compliance Unit to be investigated and addressed in a prompt, comprehensive and safe manner. The IRC’s reporting system provides employees with multiple channels to report concerns, while consolidating all issues in the internal EthicsPoint hotline and case management system. The IRC screens all suppliers using the LexisNexis Bridger database.Loss of access: IRC proposes to implement activities directly and in partnership, with additional risk mitigation measures in place for remote management. IRC ensures regular review and updating of contingency plans for its operations inside Syria to mitigate against multiple potential scenarios regarding changes in territorial control and access interruption. Humanitarian access staff conduct negotiations with local actors, work closely with program leads to identify and manage risks and engage with local councils to avoid any interference or diversion of aid. IRC has zero tolerance for interference and\or diversion of aid to any parties to the conflict. IRC has a Security Management Plan (SMP) and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) to guide IRC operations and staff to provide lifesaving assistance using the safest possible methods.
