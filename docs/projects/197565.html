Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Syria has been exacerbated by the widespread use of explosive ordnance (EO), posing significant risks and requiring different mitigation strategies to reduce its impact on the civilian population and the humanitarian sector delivering aid to those in need. While the full scale of contamination is not known, preliminary findings for the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimate that around 10.2 million, that is to say, 1 in 2 people is at risk from EO, and a third of communities are potentially contaminated. EO contamination in essential civilian infrastructure, agricultural land, roads, and private property is a lethal barrier to a safe movement which deprives civilians of accessing essential basic services, hampers delivery of humanitarian assistance, endangers those seeking refuge from violence or returning to their areas, and precludes resumption of livelihood activities, thus amplifying the social and economic crisis aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic. A comprehensive humanitarian mine action response is a humanitarian necessity and vital precursor for delivering humanitarian aid and early recovery.
Mine action is a key humanitarian priority and enabler for the wider humanitarian response and is critical enablers for safe delivery of humanitarian assistance, The overall aim of UNMAS project is to reduce the impact of EO on the civilian population and humanitarian workers through effective sector coordination and implementation of humanitarian mine action activities, in line with humanitarian priorities as identified in the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), and within the framework of agreements between the Syrian State and UN agencies, funds and programs, as well as Security Council resolution 2585 (S/RES/2585, 2021). Under this project, in accordance with the HRP, UNMAS will lead, manage and coordinate humanitarian mine action activities as defined by International Mine Action Standards (IMAS). All components of humanitarian mine action (risk education, victim assistance, survey and clearance) remain critical, but in 2022-2023, UNMAS will focus on expanding survey and clearance, as the only way to permanently remove the threat posed by EO and subject to access and funding. UNMAS will also continue the delivery of EO Risk Education (EORE), tailored to age, gender, localized threats, and activity patterns of different at-risk groups, a critical, life-saving activity for raising the awareness of civilians and humanitarian personnel about the dangers of EO, thus directly contributing to HRP strategic objective 1: “Save Lives'' and strategic objective 2: “Enhance Protection.” As the Syrian crisis has been characterized by large-scale conflict and displacement, survey, data collection, and mapping of EO are critical to informing humanitarian programming, needs-based prioritization, and the expansion of clearance to remove the contamination threats, particularly in high-humanitarian priority areas. UNMAS will support the expansion of victim assistance also and the provision of specialized services for persons with disabilities, including survivors of EO and their families, thus contributing to strategic objective 3: “Increase Resilience and Access to Services”

3 - Needs

In the absence of comprehensive technical survey & clearance, continuous hostilities for the last 10 years have been creating new layers of explosive contamination. UNMAS records showed in the past 5 years, an average of 148 explosive incidents per day recorded, equivalent to 1 incident every 12 minutes. UNMAS explosive ordnance assessment team, deployed in 2020-Rural Damascus, surveyed 13,2 M Sqm and confirmed 7,8 M sqm of hazardous areas, 59% confirmed contaminated, as of December/2021. According to the 2022 HNO,1 in 2 people is at risk from EO, and 1/3 of the communities are potentially contaminated. While all people in contaminated areas are vulnerable, certain groups are at greater risk. Men & boys considered to be the most exposed to the direct threat of EO, representing 85% of victims. Women and girls are indirectly affected, but also become at-risk when they carry out their livelihood activities in contaminated areas. EO accidents can result injury or death, reportedly 41% of incidents take place in agricultural fields, 39% in residential areas, and 24 % on roads or roadsides. Meanwhile, the widespread EO contamination continues to hamper access to basic services and the delivery of humanitarian assistance. In this context, humanitarian mine action is a key activity to prevent downward vulnerability spirals. Scaling up humanitarian mine action is essential protection measure that will have a direct positive impact on all humanitarian interventions in Syria.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

For its operations in Syria,UNMAS promotes among the Mine Action Sub Sector&Sub Cluster the PSEA approach defined for Syria,under the leadership of the WoS Strategic Steering Group/UNCT &guidance of the PSEA Specialist designated by the UN Country Team.UNMAS has an organizational Code of Conduct/Rules&Regulations that outlines acceptable&unacceptable behaviors in terms of interactions within the organization &with external stakeholders,including beneficiary communities and casual laborers. The code of conduct condemns any illegal or unethical activity (sexual, physical or psychological abuse, or exploitation, sexual harassment, trafficking, use of mira, alcohol, corruption, use of child labour, environmental and property damage, etc.). This code of conduct also includes disciplinary and financial measures to be applied in case of any breach of the code by UNMAS’ personnel.Further, UNMAS contract and grants has standard clauses for implementing partner’s (IP) requirement for(“PSEA”),Sexual Harassment&incorporates UN Supplier Code of Conduct where there is a requirement for the IP to put in place an “Organizational Code of Conduct/Rules and Regulations”.Therefore UNMAS requesting the IPs to have complaint &feedback mechanisms as part of their internal quality management system. IP’s CFM included provision of CFM dedicated contact(email & whatsapp) to beneficiaries, monitoring by IP’s ME focal persons, ensuring feedback confidentiality&responses given within a month.
