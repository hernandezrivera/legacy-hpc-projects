Project Summary

Somalia HNO 2021, estimates that 1.4 million school-aged children (45% girls) are in need of humanitarian assistance to either retain or enroll children in schools of these, 1,139,632 are children from non-displaced families whilst 300,398 are from displaced families. It is further projected that 10% of these children accounting for 216,005, are living with disabilities. Southern and Northern regions account for the highest number of children with immediate need for Humanitarian Assisstance. Assessments reveal that 63% of school-aged children in Qardo, 62% in Qandala, 61% Calula and 61% school-aged in Xarardheere and Dhuusamareeb being in need of humanitarian assistance. Lower Shabelle and Juba regions continue to record the highest number of school-aged (66% and 65% respectively) children requiring immediate humanitarian support. This is attributed to high levels of instability and consequent displacements and access constraints exacerbating existing vulnerabilities.
The situation is further exacerbated by closure of schools due to COVID-19 pandemic which affected an estimated 1.1 M school children across the country. Besides missed learning opportunities, the pandemic had both social and economic severe impact on vulnerable boys and girls who had no access to distance learning opportunities, education materials and technological platforms, posing protection risks like child labour, early marriages, recruitment to armed groups and exposure to other HTPs to children. This is compounded by pre-existing vulnerabilities like lack of facilities in IDP camps and other settlements.
This project is intended to provide access to basic protective and quality education services for vulnerable children affected by crises and emergencies in Middle Shabelle Region and Banadir. The target population of this project is 3000 children (1380 girls and 1620 boys) either affected by floods and other vagaries of weather or displaced by ongoing conflicts in the country. The project targets seven schools (2 in Balcad 2 in Jowhar, and 3 Banadir IDPs to provide access to basic protective and lifesaving education to children in need.
The purpose of this project is to use school attendance as important tool for the prevention of child protection risks, particularly for the girl-child as well providing learners with quality and accessible lifesaving education. The project will employ interventions such as provision of teachers incentives, training of teachers on child-friendly pedagogy, training of teachers on basic child protection skills and counseling/PSS/PFA, construction of gender sensitive latrines for schools, provision of safe drinking water, the establishment Child-to-Child clubs, provision of teaching and learning materials-including recreational materials, capacity building of the Community Education Committees to ensure sustainability of schools and creation of child protection and GBV awareness sessions for women groups and men in the target villages and conducting routine hygiene promotion to raise public awareness on the prevention of Acute Watery Diarrhea/Cholera. These interventions are envisaged to prevent child protection risks and protect children and women from abuse, exploitation and violence such Gender Based Violence, sexual exploitation, early marriages, child labor and recruitment of children into the armed groups.

Mitigation Measures

IDPs children particularly girls and children living with disabilities may be at risk of exclusion from access to education. Aid vision, through this project aims to advocate for and enforce the equal access to education and protection services, regardless of gender, social status, disability, or economic situation of a child. At risk of exclusion are also minority communities who may be locked in decision making by predominant communities. Aid Vision pay attention to this ensure such communities cushioned from exclusion and are present at decision making table. Aid Vision will negotiate with the local authorities and community leaders to ensure children’s equitable access to education, especially girls and those living with disabilities. Where practicable, the project will advocate for 50-50 gender representation in all positions of leadership in the project, for example: teachers, CECs, Protection Focal Points etc. The other strata of the community like representation of the youth and elderly will also be considered for community capacity building and awareness sessions
