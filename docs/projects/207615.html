Project Summary

FH will provide unconditional food assistance to 13,881 new and exisiting IDPs affected by the armed conflict in Metuge and Mecufi districts in Cabo Delgado. The project will also provide agricultural inputs such as seeds and tools to 5,000 individuals (IDP and Host community) to enhance food production and improve thier short-term livelihood recovery and resilience. Agricultural inputs will include certified cereal seeds, vegetable seeds, hoe, matchete and watering cans. Beneficiaries will be provided with technical support on conservative agriculture and pest management for increased production. The combination of FSL support will address both food security and livelihoods challenges among IDPs and host communities. Farmers will be organized into groups of 15-20 members who will be supported by trained farmer leaders. FHM will implement the Farmer Field Schools approach for knowledge sharing and demonstrations of results. The project will also establish VSLAs in the target communities. Experience from recent emergency response projects in the area have proven that VSLA groups are effective in enhancing livelihoods and access to loans by community members.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The ongoing-armed conflict, insecurity and climatic shocks have resulted in thousands of households losing their properties, disrupting agricultural activities and affecting the main sources of income for local residents. A number of existing IDPs are still forced to depend on humanitarian food assistance due to limited access to livelihoods activities. At resettlement centres, the government has allocated land for agricultural activities to IDPs to facilitate their quick recovery and reduce dependence of humanitarian food assistance. The 2023/2024 agricultural season will be characterized by unstable rainfall due to the ongoing El-Niño phenomenon that will affect the southern and central provinces of the country and some parts of the province of Nampula. Although there is a significant number of IDPs returning to their homes to prepare for particularly in Quissanga, Macomia, Mocimboa da Praia and Palma districts, the prevailing localized attacks are resulting in new IDPs that require life-saving food assistance. In response to the humanitarian crisis resulting from the displacement of people in Cabo Delgado and Nampula provinces, FHM will implement life-saving and early recovery humanitarian response activities to ensure safe, equitable, gender-sensitive and inclusive assistance to IDPs and host communities directly affected by the violent conflict and insecurity situations. The project will support 18,881 people in critical need. FHM will provide unconditional food assistance through commodity vouchers to 13,881 individuals in Metuge and Mecufi districts for at least four months (February to May 2024). Food Voucher Transfer (FVT) will be preferred over the in-kind modality because it is logistically light and simple to handle as well as the fact that it gives self-esteem and dignity to the beneficiaries. The project will target 20% of the total beneficiaries from host community members with agricultural inputs such as vegetable seeds, watering cans and a hoe for home gardens. Beneficiaries of the agricultural activities will be provided with technical support on conservative agriculture and pest management for increased production. Engaging IDPs in agricultural and livelihoods activities will help them to recover from the disrupted livelihoods, reduce dependents on emergency humanitarian assistance and enhance their resilience. The combination of FSL support will address both food security and livelihoods challenges among IDPs and host communities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Output 1:Internally displaced people living in resettlement centres / host communities in Metuge and Mecufi district have improved food security. The project will provide 13,881 IDPs with a monthly food voucher consisting of 50kg maize meal/rice, 10kg dried beans, 2kg iodized salt and 4 liters of vegetable oil per the FSL cluster standards. Each HH will be able to redeem the vouchers monthly for at least 11 months. Activity 1.1 - Beneficiary registration and verification of HHs for food assistance, which will be conducted simultaneously with a baseline survey Activity 1.2 - Establish community-based management committees Activity 1.3 - Put in place community and feedback mechanisms (CRM) Activity 1.4 - Distribute food vouchers for 11 months Activity 1.5 - Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring . Output 2: Improved short-term livelihood recovery by providing agricultural inputs and technical support in order to improve food production and resilience targeting 5,000 people in the districts of Metuge and Mecufi in Cabo Delgado and Rapale in Nampula province. Activity 2.1 - Provide kits of Agricultural inputs (seeds and tools) including vegetable seeds; Activity 2.2 - Provide training and support to farmer leaders on climate smart agricultural practices; Activity 2.3 - Train groups of female farmers on kitchen gardens, organic manure and good agricultural practices;

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Consultations will be held with affected populations, including discussions with specific target groups such as the elderly, disabled and women and girls on access and provision food assistance and agriculture based livelihood services and supplies. All voucher distributions will comply with the FSL cluster starndards. The placement farmer field schools and kitchen gardens will include consultations with the elderly, the disabled and women and girls to ensure that these groups feel safe in accessing and using the sites. Continuous feedback will be sought from the IDPs through different feedback channels including regular community meetings with the IDPs, help desks, linha verde, and community committees. The project will also conduct a gender analysis during the first quarter of its implementation and results from this analysis will be used to improve on the implementation of the project.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

FH/M will ensure that gender associated risks are assessed and adequately addressed in the project design. FH/M has ensured women participate in the project through purposeful targeting of at least 50% women headed HHs, having 50% representation by women in project steering committees and help-desk committees. The committees will not only be trained in management but also on the vital role of women in HH and community development. Gender awareness will be extended to the entire community at every point of interaction with the project. An in-depth gender analysis will be conducted by FH/M to guide project implementation and ensure sustainable empowerment of women beyond the life of the project. FH/M will promote shared power between men and women - including control of resources and decision-making - and support for women’s empowerment as a pathway to reducing gender inequalities. Women with babies will be prioritized during food voucher distributions and voucher redemptions to allow them ample time to travel back to their homes to feed their children and perform their other roles (reproductive, productive, and community managing activities). FH/M will engage both men and women as part of the project steering committees for meaningful involvement in decision making by both sexes. In this project, FH/M will reinforce gender empowerment and transformation by engaging women who will comprise at least 50% of beneficiaries and project committee members respectively. FH/M will ensure that distances from homes to voucher distribution points or vendor shops are within walking distances (less than three kilometers) of the beneficiaries. To ensure protection and inclusiveness, FH/M will ensure that voucher distribution is conducted during daylight and will provide space for nursing mothers. FH/M will take advantage of pre and post distribution speeches to convey key messages on issues such as gender and protection.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

FH will create or revitalize disability management committees to ensure identification and effective involvement of PWD and other vulnerable people in the project. Personalized support particularly in transportation of entitlements after distributions will be provided through community based management committees. The project will seek to strengthen the local social-networks to ensure continued support to PWD. Distribution sites will be set in consideration of the inclusion requirements.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

This project will have minimal impact on the environment as a result of beneficiary gatherings for food voucher distributions. FHM will sensitize all beneficiaries to ensure proper conservation and disposal of empty bags and bottles after consumption of rice, maize meal and cooking oil. Distributions will be conducted at community centers with basic sanitation facilities to avoid environmental issues. Additionally, farmers will receive training on climate smart agriculture to avoid practices that are harmful to the environment.
