Project Summary

The project aims to enhance vulnerable households’ ability to withstand recurrent and future shocks in Ethiopia through early recovery and resilience interventions. In Afar region, recurrent droughts and ongoing conflict in Northern Afar have caused the displacement of more than 360,000 HHs, disruptions of livestock and agriculture activities in the entire region. A total of 48,981 livestock heads have been lost in 12 woredas of Zones 1,2,4,5. The regional government of Afar has recommended to treat, vaccinate to save 2.6 million core breeding animals and support the production of fodder, vegetables and fruits, restocking for the poorest women headed families affected by both the conflict and the current drought. In Somali region 85 woredas are affected by drought and 977,838 heads of livestock have already died in 77 woredas of 10 zones. Livestock migration to different directions is at an alarming rate with over 1.5 million heads of livestock already moved in Fafan zone. Pasture availability is worrying and there are widespread livestock disease outbreaks (PPR, Anthrax, sheep and goat pox and CCPP, camel pox, FMD and LSD, etc.). In Oromia Region, according to the regional DRMC reports, 17,903,138 people have been affected by the drought with 3,605,304 (20.1%) people are in need of emergency humanitarian response. 10,345,706 livestock heads have been affected by the drought with 626,590 heads that are already died and 386,271 heads need human assistance to stand up and walk. In Amhara region, according to the regional DRM and other partners’ reports, all freed areas from the conflict in 8 zones, 76 woredas and 1095 kebeles, 3,302,541 people are in need of agricultural, for Belg and Meher seasons, and livestock interventions. There are 1644, 777 IDPs in North Shewa only (10,000 in collective sites). COOPI will target 40.000 households (HHs) in Afar, Amhara, Oromia and Somali regions as detailed here: Afar 15.000 HHs, Amhara 5.000 HHs, Oromia 15.000 HHs and Somali 5.000 HHs. Individuals are estimated as 220.000 disaggregated as follows: 47% male, 49% female, 2% elderly men and 2% elderly women. Totally 30 woredas (11 in Afar, 3 in Amhara, 12 in Oromia and 4 in Somali) will be targeted. The woredas were selected based on the HRP 2022 priority selection by severity with all woredas in the most severe category (severity 4) such as: Afar Region: Mille and Chifra in Awsi /Zone 1; Abaala, Megale and Berahile woredas in Kilbati /Zone 2; Amibara, Gewane and Hanruka woredas of Gabi /Zone 3; Teru, Yalo and Gulina woredas in Fanti /Zone 4. Amhara Region: Antsokiya Eferetana Gidem and Kewet in North Shewa. Oromia Region: Gursum and Chinaksen in East Hararge Zone; Meda Welabu and Gura Damole in Bale Zone; Lege Hida, Rayitu, Seweyna, Dawe Ketchen and Dawe Serer in East Bale. Liben, Saba Boru, Gora Dola in Guji Zone. Somali Region: Tuliguled in Fafan Zone; Dolobay woreda in Afder Zone; Filtu, Deka Suftu in Liban Zone. COOPI will ensure integration with its WASH and Shelter&NFI support and will strictly coordinate with humanitarian partners involved in Health and Nutrion. The main activities are: 1. support animal health treatment (23.500 HHs); 11.000 out of those will be supported also through animal feed for 90 days (3 core breeding animals); 3. deliver multipurpose cash transfer (7.000 HHs); 4. provision of emergency seeds and farming tools (8.500 HHs); 5. Capacity building for stakeholders at Zone and Woreda levels (220 individuals). The proposal includes cross-cutting activities related to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), as training of staff and the support to referral mechanism to specialized agencies. COOPI will mainstream gender by ensuring the participation of women and men, the use of gender sensitive selection criteria, collecting disaggregated data. AAP will be ensured through the establishment of feedback and complaints mechanisms whose functionality will be also assessed by the PDM survey.

1. - Projects activities

The project will implement the following activities:A1. Preliminary activities-Signature of MoU with regional authorities; briefing on safety and security protocols for staff, including guidelines for distribution in COVID-19context; training and briefing on PSEA based on the Ethiopia Network Training Package; protection mainstreaming approach.-Market Assessment to determine the value and delivery mechanisms.-Sites‘selection in the target woredas, prioritizing those most affected; needs/gaps and protection threats.-Procurement of the materials/items according to the type of response and the technical specifications and quantities as designed by the Cluster/Prepositioning of service providers for cash response. -Set-up of selection committees made of village elders; religious leaders; kebele managers; Woreda office focal person; IDP and no displaced representatives, COOPI Staff. The project will ensure equal participation of women and men.-Beneficiaries‘ identification, selection, verification and registration considering the following criteria: displaced households/non displaced households who have not received any FSL support; female-headed, HHs with 6 children or more, with member(s) part of the vulnerability groups (infant, children, elders etc.) or households with specific needs (a member with disability, pregnant women, persons with critical medical conditions, member with poor nutritional status, among others). The criteria stated above will be revised and agreed according to the feedback of the main stakeholders in line with humanitarian principles (neutrality,independence, humanity and impartiality) with local authorities, IDPs representatives and the cluster. COOPI will ensure that data will be disaggregated by gender and age and, when it applies, the type of disability (neurological, mental,sensorial and physical). COOPI will ensure that its data protection policy is in place.A2. Support 70.500 livestock heads health, owned by 23.500 households, through treatment and vaccination against locally and economically important livestock diseases in Afar, Amhara, Oromia and Somali Regions. Furthermore, 11.000 out of these households will receive animal feed determined by providing 3.5 kg of fodder (bale of hay at $4) and 2.5 kg of concentrate (1 quintal at $20) for 90 days for 3 core breeding animals. A3. Delivery of multi purpose cash transfer targeting 7000 households. The amount is estimated at $40 per household equivalent to about 90% of the Productive Safety Net Programme (PSNP) wage rate.A4. Support 8500 households with emergency seeds and farming tools.A5. Capacity building for 220 individuals from Zone and Woreda levels. The training will be carried out in collaboration with regional offices.A6. Post Distribution Monitoring PDM, involving households receiving in kind and cash support, will assess the quality of the process and the degree of satisfaction of beneficiaries regarding the following dimensions: Quality control of process (Assessment, verification, distribution) / Effectiveness of the response; Accountability to affected population (Information provision/Sharing, participation,feedback and complaint mechanism); distribution process; timeliness of the response; Protection; quality control of the items, appropriateness, usage and satisfaction/use of cash for the intended purposes.

COOPI will ensure proper engagement and participation in the clusters at sub-national and zonal levels, in order to avoid duplication of efforts, maximizing use of resources and supporting an integrated emergency response. The coordination with the PSEA Network in Ethiopia will be strengthened in order to receive support incase of guidance on most appropriate referral systems for identified cases in intervention areas and share good practices and lessons learnt. COOPI in agreement with the cluster will identify and select the most suitable National NGOs for a joint implementation of the project. According to the internal MEAL system, the PMs allocated in each area will be directly responsible for the implementation of theactivities, results and he/she will be supported by the Emergency response Lead, Headof Programs, MEAL and the Program Coordinators. Specific Term of Reference documents are prepared by the PMs and field workers for each planned activity and are being approved by Emergency Lead/Area Coordinator. During the project, standardized check-lists and tools will be used to verify the progress of the action and to analyze the achievement of results and indicators against the planned outcomes and outputs. Distribution Monitoring reports as well as complaints handling mechanisms with direct beneficiaries. 3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

COOPI adhere to the humanitarian accountability principles and all COOPI Ethiopia staff is trained of the 5 commitments of the Accountability of the Affected Population.COOPI will make sure that beneficiaries know they have a right to equitable and safeassistance, where and how to obtain it. Following COOPI Standard Operating Procedures, to ensure informed decisions COOPI staff will involve partners, communities, local authorities, beneficiaries and especially vulnerable groups in the planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating of this project. This includesengaging beneficiaries at the design stage by conducting focus group discussions andindividual interviews with both men and women in various age groups, and ensure thatassessments include the most vulnerable, including elders and people with disabilities.During the action, beneficiaries at all levels will take a leading role in the formation/consolidation of the committees described in the proposal (selection committees). These committees, jointly with local authorities and project staff, will co-lead in the selection of the recipients and in the monitoring of the action. COOPI willprovide opportunities for women, people with disabilities and older people to holdpositions of responsibility and be represented on community-based committeesincluding as community focal points for assessment teams. An accessible, wellunderstood and confidential mechanism will be set up for receiving suggestions andcomplaints, with a clear follow-up system on all reports. COOPI’s feedback andcomplaint mechanism includes information sharing on: • Project objectives, outcomesand outputs, implementing agency and donor. • Selection criteria and mechanisms toreport issues for beneficiaries; • Composition of the ES-NFIs kit/entitlements. Thevalidation and agreement with the main stakeholders on the selection of sites andbeneficiaries of distributions will take place through meetings with communitymembers, local authorities and cluster representatives. COOPI Project managementteam will strengthen local capacities, foster local knowledge and community copingstrategies, as well as enhance protection mechanisms providing where possiblepsycho-social services to the community and beneficiaries. COOPI management teamwill make sure that field teams are sensitized and trained on the importance of makingassessments tools age-friendly, by including questions on the needs and capacities ofelderly and vulnerable people. Particular attention will be provided on PSEA. COOPI willprovide training on its internal policies to the project staff and main stakeholders. Thepolicy includes the concepts related with PSEA, preventive measures, referralmechanisms as well as feedback and complaints mechanisms. Besides that, COOPIwill brief the staff also using the Ethiopia PSEA Network Training Package. COOPIwill set up a feedback and complain mechanism to allow beneficiaries’ participation,improving monitoring and accountability reporting and promptly adjust theimplementation of the project. Mechanisms to report issues will be done through:- Provision to local stakeholders of main contacts for COOPI staff (phone and email),especially for the SMT program staff- Provision of help-desk contact(help@coopi.org)- Establishment of complaints and feedback box and notice panel atthe field (depending on level of literacy of beneficiaries)- Participatory selection offocal points at community level, for oral complaints/feedback.COOPI will record all possible complaints and feedbacks. According to the severity of the issue COOPI SMT will respond, in at least 4 days, involving different staff at different level:- Minor complaints/ request of information: social promoters/wash advisor/emergency lead.- Major complaints: Programme Manager/Emergency ResponseLead/CountryAdministrator/Head of Mission/HQ.
