3 - Needs

2.9M people in NWS are in need of WASH assistance. IDP sites have even greater needs. CARE’s Rapid WASH Assessment in NWS found that 88% rely on water trucking to meet their water supply needs and 73% rely on humanitarian actors to provide this. This is largely due to existing water systems being broken, lacking electricity, fuel or maintenance, and the effects of climate change and drought. Communities in NWS are not able to raise adequate funds to cover the cost of operation of the systems due to the increase in fuel costs, a lack of resources, and the additional burden caused by displacement of IDPs into those communities without adequate job opportunities. CARE’s Rapid Water Supplies Assessment in NWS in September 2021 found that 77% of HHs reported less water available from water sources in 2020/21 compared to previous years and 95% of all respondents reported that the cost of water had increased. CARE has found that Health Centers in areas without functioning water networks are reliant on water trucking to meet their needs which is costly, has reduced quality control and leaves these institutions vulnerable to water shortages.

4 - Implementation of activities

CARE will undertake the following activities: Activity 1: Rehabilitation and operation of water systems. Activity 2: provide water trucking and water storage. Activity 3: Rehabilitation of sewage and drainage. Activity 4: Installation of latrines and desludging services. Activity 5: Solid waste management in IDP camps. Activity 6: Hygiene Promotion and distribution of Hygiene NFIs. Exact locations will be decided in coordination with the WASH cluster depending on gaps identified and communicated through the 4Ws as part of proposal design, planning, implementation and close out. CARE will ensure beneficiaries received 35-40 litres per person per day. Water storage will be provided to ensure enough water for a minimum of 1-2 days.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks include gender based violence, sexual exploitation, sexual harassment, child abuse. CARE is committed towards mainstreaming protection and gender into all of the projects and does so through the use of several mechanisms and tools. These mechanisms include the creation of feedback and complaint mechanisms for projects, capacity building of all staff in relation to mainstreaming protection and gender throughout all phases of implementation. The delivery of project services and assistance is designed to be safe and accessible by women, men, girls and boys, and members of marginalized groups taking into account the social and cultural dynamics in the communities. GBV mitigation measures will be considered in the implementation of the intervention in line with the IASC Guidelines for integrating GBV in humanitarian action, and all the precautions will be taken to reduce the risk of exposure to GBV including SEA in the project locations. Increasing the availability and accessibility of water for women, girls, and other at-risk groups, and involving these groups in decision making processes around water management, design and monitoring of WASH facilities, and hygiene promotion activities will constitute some of the areas that will be looked at very closely to improve the safety and security of the response. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

CARE recognizes we have a responsibility to protect people we work with, and who work for us, and we will continuously strive to prevent sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse and child abuse from happening. We take seriously all reports of sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse. Our actions are informed by a survivor-centered approach which means that the needs and wishes of survivors guide our response, that survivors are treated with dignity and respect, and the rights of survivors to privacy and support are prioritized. CARE has a zero-tolerance approach toward sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse. We will carefully examine allegations and investigate, and take appropriate disciplinary action where this is needed, taking into consideration the rights and interests of the survivor, consistent with CARE’s survivor-centered approach. We make very clear that sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse in any form, perpetrated by our staff, partners or other related personnel, towards anyone, will not be tolerated. This policy applies to all CARE Employees and Related-Personnel.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risks to CARE staff as a result of armed conflict: CARE maintains strong security protocols and closely monitors conflict developments. In spite of close monitoring and strong systems and protocols, it is impossible to fully mitigate this risk. Risks of aid diversion from both armed groups and civil governance entities:CARE works on developing strong systems, identifies growing risks trending in the context, and continues to work with UN and donors to strengthen the community-wide response to this risk. CARE also continues to directly monitor project activities at several layers at the area and country office levels. The risk of aid diversion does however continue to be a high risk that is difficult to fully mitigate. The spread of cholera and COVID-19 does not undermine CARE's ability to conduct field activities. CARE has developed contingency plans to continue to implement during a significant outbreak in NWS while also mitigating risks to field staff.
