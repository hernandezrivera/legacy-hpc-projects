Project Summary

This emergency recovery and rehabilitation food security and livelihood project is a combined response that will be implemented in a span of 12 months from January to December 2020. Main goal of the project is to strengthen food and nutrition security and resilience among food insecure insecure households of the most disadvantaged and vulnerable households in multiple counties in Northern and Western Barh el Ghazal respectively reaching 24000hh.The beneficiaries will be targeted basing on a vulnerability matrix tool to be agreed on with the stakeholders and community.
To achieve this, the project will support the immediate nutritional needs of vulnerable HHs through the provision of cash/vouchers transfers. Using cash transfers, HHs will access suﬃcient food to meet daily caloric and nutritional requirements. This will address the immediate impacts of food insecurity, reducing the incidence of malnutrition and malnutrition-related morbidity and mortality. Transfers will be both unconditional and conditional upon the completion of work to rehabilitate and/or construct communal assets that have the potential to enhance longer term food security and livelihoods including physical assets such as dykes and community access roads, as well as natural assets. In addition to meeting basic and immediate needs, short term cash transfers will ensure HHs have the energy and time to participate in activities that enhance their ability to deal with disruption and change. By improving the availability and accessibility of these productive assets, the project will strengthen long-term food security by improving agricultural productivity, and supporting the regeneration of the natural resource base. As production increases, availability of food for consumption and/or sale increases, resulting in improved livelihoods and an increase in local, regional and national food security.
To enhance emergency food production, main season agriculture seeds & tool, ﬁshing gears and fast maturing vegetable & crop kits shall be distributed. This project will also contribute to the reduction of pest and crop disease e.g. FAW surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication and also conduct Livestock vaccination; disease prevention & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF;
Through these interventions, this project will be contributing to the FSL 2020 strategy of preventing famine and building households coping capacities by increasing food production and restoration of productive assets for more diversified livelihoods. Special attention will be given to the marginalized people like persons with disability, the elderly, chronically ill, orphans, girls and women to make sure they are not left out in the distribution of inputs); 3) basic agronomic training and extension support. Here, demonstration plots will be established at nutrition centres where beneficiaries will be taught how to prepare nutritious food, through community-based extension workers; SPEDP will also use a community based participatory approach where beneficiary identification and registration process will involve community members.
Project management committees will be established and will take an active role in the beneficiary selection, monitoring implementation and also handling complaints and feedback mechanisms and monitoring of the project. Stakeholders will be invited during the launch and all project details will be shared to foster accountability to the affected populations.
SPEDP will strive to integrate with other projects like health, WASH and nutrition through passive referrals and also establishment of demonstration plots at nutrition sites.
SPEDPs added value is that the organization has an already established presence in NBeG and WBeG since 2013 to date has implemented a number of projects since them in different thematic areas. SPEDP is currently implementing FSL SA2 project in Raja, FOA-SAFER Project in Aweil Center and North and so is familiar with the local settings.

1 - Needs

According to the August 2019 IPC analysis, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes remain pervasive in South Sudan. An estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes at the July-August peak of the lean season in the presence of humanitarian assistance and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes or worse will likely remain prevalent. A risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) will continue to persist in areas already classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse.Despite the decline in conflict and anticipated improvement in food security, many households have lost key livelihood assets and coping strategies as a result of the protracted conflict. Consequently, high levels of acute food insecurity and a risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) will persist. Based on past trends, Famine (IPC Phase 5) would be likely if conflict shifts and prevents populations from moving in search of food sources or restricts humanitarian access for a prolonged period of time.Although food security has slightly improved, high levels of acute malnutrition have endured. Nutrition data collected during the 24th round of Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Systems (FSNMS R24) classified global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence by weight-for-height z-score (WHZ) in 58 counties as ’Serious’ (GAM WHZ 10.0-14.9 percent) or ’Critical’ (GAM WHZ 15.0-29.9 percent), which are indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4), respectively.In the projections for January – April 2020, a total of 14 counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), 53 are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), and 11 are classified in Stressed (IPC Phase 2). 10.4% of the population (about 1.21 million people) are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency); 36.7% of the population (about 4.29 million people) are in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis); 34.9% of the population (about 4.08 million) are in IPC Phase 2 (Stressed); and 18.0% of the population (about 2.10 million) are in IPC Phase 1 (Minimal)In Northern Bahr el Ghazal out of 946,905(Mid-2019 population NBS), 145,000 are under IPC 4, 400,000 under IPC 3, and 275,000 under IPC 2. In total, 58.0% represents crisis, emergency and humanitarian catastrophe (August 2019 IPC analysis)In Western Bahr el Ghazal out of 646,245(Mid-2019 population NBS), 20,000 are under IPC 4, 260,000 under IPC 3, and 240,000 under IPC 2. In total, 43.1% represents crisis, emergency and humanitarian catastrophe (August 2019 IPC analysis) Across all the former states in the greater Bahr el Ghazal region, the key drivers of food insecurity are climatic shocks(Particularly in Northern Bahr el Ghazal where flooding has been a hazard in the recent past), increased instances of inter-communal violence and unfavorable terms of trade for majority of the households. This, coupled with reduced market access due to the worsening road conditions as a result of the seasonal rains, led to increased food prices resulting in widespread acute food insecurity. Additionally, GAM prevalence deteriorated due to the limited access to and availability of food during this period coupled with seasonal increase in morbidity – such as malaria, and lack of adequate access to health services

2 - Activities

Unconditional and conditional cash & voucher based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives - Main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fish, vegetable & crop kits distribution - Fishing kits/ rapid response kits (fishing kits/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed) distribution - Disease and pest surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication - Livestock vaccination and treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF - Conditional cash transfers (social skills and safety nets)/ Conditional cash transfers (investments in assets, training, or communal work groups/ planning committees - Support value chain addition e.g. through milk and fish preservation and marketing - Training and capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc. To implement the above activities, SPEDP will employ a range of strategies including the following; Carrying out Rapid Market survey and risk analysis, this will help in determining local level capacities of commercial sector to source and provide food to the beneficiaries. • Sensitization and selection of traders/vendors, given the location context, this will be done through less formal mobilization of vendors. Vendors sign an agreement acknowledging full understanding of the voucher process—no guarantee of sales, vendors ensures security of their own merchandise, respect for beneficiaries as a humanitarian intervention. • Set up of Community Committees for beneficiary selection, a Community based approach will be used since its effective both in terms of providing a mechanism for implementing a relief operation in a relatively short period of time, and in terms of meeting the principles of transparency, accountability and fairness. This will have a maximum of 12 members • Selection, registration and verification of beneficiaries, this will be done by the enumerators with recommendation from the community committees. Registration lists will be finalized using vulnerability and targeting criteria. • Sensitization of beneficiaries and vendors on voucher redemption process, for beneficiaries this is primarily to underline key aspects of the approach such as families being represented by the female adults, the use of the coupons • Community participatory identification of productive assets for rehabilitation • There will also be training of beneficiaries on basic agronomics through demonstration plots and modern methods of fish preservation. Lead Farmers will be identified and trained, besides, 25% of the beneficiaries will also be trained Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring exercises and post harvest surveys will be conducted to ascertain the impact of the project at household and Community level and broader economic level

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the life of the project. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee’s to implement, with support from project staff. A core component of this will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to identify multiple information dissemination channels that leverage, where relevant and appropriate, multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. Low literacy options will be included, and women specifically consulted to identify gender-responsive content and channels. The CFM will also outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). This will be done through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by PMCs. Information will be fed back to Project Management Team who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC via scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing two-way communication. PMCs will also publicise and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints. PMCs will use project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to complaints.
