Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to tackle the consequences of 3 years of conflict and protracted displacement on the population of Ninewa and Salah Al Din Governorates. The intervention will contribute to reduce morbidity and mortality rates of the most vulnerable populations in Iraq by supporting the re-establishment and the strengthening of primary health care, reproductive health services in the targeted areas. Specifically, through this intervention, 70,665 vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in west Ninewa and SAD districts will have improved access to quality life-saving health services, including reproductive health. The package of services provided includes: Provision of primary health care services to children, adults and elderly patients; Integrated management of childhood illness (IMCI) (0-4 years), including support to vaccinations; Sexual reproductive health, including ANC/PNC, and gynaecological consultations; detection/screening and treatment/referral of acute malnutrition among children; establishment of 24hX7 emergency rooms where needed, support of community level healthcare awareness and referral activities;; in addition to the logistical and managerial support to PHCCs, where needed.
As people slowly continue to return to their areas of origin, healthcare services, and especially specialised ones such as RH and MHPSS, are either completely absent or not able to keep up with the increasing number of people in need. This is likely to cause severe discrepancies between the demand for services and the public offer, which can badly worsen the precarious situation of both returnees and people who did not leave the areas. Through providing support to Primary Health Care Centres (PHCCs), the impact of the project will expand to serve people of larger communities, who will access the centres for both the diverse and quality services, including drugs, and for the fact the such services are for free (outside governmental hours).
Along with the quantitative aspect of the project, INTERSOS will be focusing on the quality of services provided. This will be realised through the provision of regular training and on-the-job coaching to medical and paramedical staff in the PHCCs, as well as through the administrative and managerial support which aims as well at making the centres eﬃcient and effective.
Finally, the project will benefit from the long-standing dialogue and cooperation INTERSOS has established with local DOH and Health Sector offices in the targeted areas. Such cooperation is going to remain at the core of INTERSOS intervention as a pillar to ensure effectiveness, efficiency and sustainability.
INTERSOS exit strategy at the end of the program will be based on two factors pursued and developed during through the year: technically and materially equipping the supported PHCCs, and constantly liaising and cooperation with relevant DOH offices. On the one hand, INTERSOS, through the provision of material support, i.e. medical equipment and supplies, drugs and equipment, and technical capacity building to both medical and non-medical staff, i.e. on-the-job coaching, trainings and technical supervision, strengthen the sustainability of the project both terms of continuation of services and in term of keeping high standards. On the other hand, coordination with relevant DOH offices aims not only at easing the implementation phase of the project, but as well at ensuring proper hand-over of the activities to governmental institutions in order to continue the work and keep up with the quantity and quality of services provided.
The program will be moving in two directions: maintaining the support to the existent PHCCs when governmental authorities will not be able, or are not willing, to keep up with the same amount and level of services; moving to new areas, mainly remote and hard-to-reach areas with new intervention in support of new health structures.

Needs

7.3 million people are in need of health response in Iraq including 4 million returnees, 1.9 million IDPs in camps or in host community, 0.2 million Syrian refugees and 1.2 million host community suffering by the impact of conflict and the devastation of infrastructure. (WHO situation report #8 2018) 1,548,108 individuals (38.4% of 4,028,694 total returnees in country) returned to their area of origin in Ninewa governorate by end of August 2018. Among total returned 923,586 individuals have returned to Mosul and 312,816 to Telafar, while 589,578 individuals are still internally displaced and the majority of them (386,538) is from Mosul districts. 99% (584,190 individuals) IDPs from Ninewa are displaced within same governorate and settled in camps, rented houses and unfinished shelters within governorate premises (DTM round 102 - August 2018). Even if a total of 563,022 persons returned into Salah al-Din governorate and 69,306 IDPs of them returned to Baiji district - according to the 103 Master list of returnees- more than 40,000 families are reported still displaced out of Salah al-Din. According to integrated location assessment – III by DTM team and supported by IOM, 90% (8,593 out of 9,611 total families returned) of families reported health service as basic need in Baiji district. Following the same assessment, 15 out 23 locations of return require health services improvement. At country level, 59% of health facilities (Hospitals and primary health care centers) are fully or partially non-functional, 34% health facilities fully (16%) and partially (18%) damaged while only 33% of health facilities are supported by partners and only 23% of health facilities have fully functional referral services in Ninewa governorate. Concerning the services provision, 65.3% provide outpatient services, 6% provide BEOC services, 23.5% PNC and 22.2% ANC, while only 3.11% provide CMR and 15.6% MHPSS. Many differences are reported among districts, for example, 78% PHCCs in Telafar provide outpatient service and 9.8% ANC & PNC services while no PHCC provide services on BEOC, CMR and MHPSS (WHO- HeRAMs– NINEWA March 2018). INTERSOS conducted a rapid health assessment of PHCCs in Baiji and findings revealed that 50%of the primary health care centres are partially or fully non-functional to provide health care services on reducing morbidities and mortality through immunization activities and management and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases. Assessment findings includes unavailability of resources, which affects the capacity of centres on provision of services and create potential barriers on access to primary health care services for returnees. The lack of resources (drugs, medical personnel and equipment) effect on provision of reproductive health services on ante natal and post-natal care, gynaecological consultation, family planning, treatment of minor complications and referrals of complicated cases to main general hospital in Tikrit. Additionally, the lack of vaccination stock, storage means and continuity of routine vaccination, keeps the under 5 boys and girls at risk and expose them to be infected by preventable communicable diseases. INTERSOS conducted health rapid assessment in Telafar and Mosul too. It revealed a significant gap in provision of health services in rural PHCCs, where almost all of them are partially or fully non-functional. The lack of human resources and female medical staff (i-e Female doctors, midwifes, nurses and laboratory assistants) contributes to limit the access of women to health services. Adding the lack of staff to the poor condition of the infrastructures, the lack of equipment and the diﬃcult access to these rural areas, the result is that basic reproductive health services, mechanisms of prevention and response to GBV, mental health support services, vaccination for mothers and new born babies are often not available in PHCCs.

Protection Risk Analysis

Target population is not supportive with the prevention component of the program. Reluctance from governmental oﬃcials to provide services to certain beneficiaries due to their background or perceived aﬃliation. Request from different actors to share confidential information about beneficiaries served, due to their background, vulnerability or perceived aﬃliation. Increase of fees to access health services. Changes in displacement flows due to deterioration of situation Reduced or no access to areas of intervention due to instability of the security situation and/or inability to implement activities due to constraints or blockages from the governmental/security actors. Cultural and religious norms prevent individuals and/or segment of the population to fully benefit from the health services provided. SRH activities may result in community misperception of the project and limit women benefits from it.

Mitigation Measures

Involvement of the community in the design of the prevention activities including in the elaboration of the key messages. INTERSOS, though the whole range of programmes it runs in the target areas will constantly seek the involvement of communities through activities at individual, group and community level. The ideas, feedback and needs retrieved from such activities will allow INTERSOS to better design and implement programs targeting different levels and vulnerable groups within the communities.Constantly monitor the situation of movements of the population and keep regular flow of communication with relevant Clusters. INTERSOS is an active member of different coordination fora and has a good grasp of population dynamics thanks to the capillary presence among the communities with a wide range of activities and programmes (Health, Education and Protection). This strengthens capacities in term of timely identifying and addressing issues related to population movements and returns, as well of reporting and coordinating with relevant fora and Clusters.Introducing different stakeholders to INTERSOS program and humanitarian principles in the concerned area. Through its work with authorities and public institutions (such as PHCCs) INTERSOS not only provides services to vulnerable individuals, but it actively engages with relevant actors in the areas to raise awareness and build capacities in regard to humanitarian principles and assistant best practices. Protection principles and capacities are particularly pursued through their inclusion in trainings and on-job-coaching for medical and non-medical staff of all primary healthcare centres.Maintaining relations with local authorities (mayors, civil directorate oﬃcials, mukhtar, judges.) and increasing negotiations with those if needed on specific issues (civil documentation to access PHCC). INTERSOS has been building positive relations and cooperation with relevant health-related authorities in all targeted areas. Such approach valuing coordination and mutual support will be pursued in all new programmes through constant coordination, reporting and open dialogues.Inform cluster of developments and advocate at cluster level for overarching challenges. INTERSOS is an active member of different Cluster and Working Groups at both national and local level. Particularly relevant for the purpose of the proposed program are the Health, both national and local, and Protection Clusters, both national and local (of which INTERSOS is co-lead), who will be constantly kept informed and consulted whenever issues will emerge.

How is your project accountable to affected population

INTERSOS humanitarian activities are always performed according to the highest standards of accountability and transparency. Particularly, within the framework of the proposed project INTERSOS will be involving affected communities in all stages of the action and will be continuously seeking their feedback through a number of mechanisms and channels with the purpose of improving the quality of services provided and adapt the action design to real needs. A mixed system of complaint mechanisms and Satisfaction Surveys will be set up in all areas where the activities will be ongoing. Complaint and feedback boxes will be installed and managed taking into consideration protection risks through a AGD lenses, thus allowing every individual to access them and freely express themselves. Complaints and feedback will be then collected and systematised to be used both internally and to be shared, preserving anonymity and privacy standards, with medical and non-medical staff working in the PHCCs. This restitution system will allow for the improvement of the work in the centres in the short-term as well as it will support the sustainability of the project in term of protection-related capacity-building of the project staff. Additionally, INTERSOS will continuously foster accountability towards beneficiaries through the implementation of satisfaction surveys to be conducted on samples of beneficiaries, of both PHCCs and community activities, by trained project staff. The surveys will be looking at both quantitative and qualitative aspects of the services provided, as well as perception and proxy indicators to gain the clearest possible picture on the capacities of the PHCCs. Again, the results of such surveys will be systematised and analysed before being fed back to both INTERSOS and PHCCs staff with the aim of enhancing the level of service provision to vulnerable individuals. Finally, all medical and non-medical staff working in and for the targeted PHCCs will be also delivered training and GBV, Protection, PSEA and Accountability by Protection-specialised staff, thus further increasing the overall accountability of the action towards beneficiaries. Such training will be repeatedly conducted by specialised staff, and monitored in order to ensure the active participation of the staff and their understanding and engagement with the process implemented. The implementation of all mechanisms will be design in order to encourage the participation of all sections of the community, paying particular attention to the gender, age, and diversity of the target population. All complaint and feedback mechanisms will benefit from the provisions and standards set out in the PSEA and CP policies developed at the organisation level, as well as from the numerous efforts made to set up proper and reliable accountability frameworks in all INTERSOS missions and activities. This will include the set up and management of the complaint and feedback mechanisms not only for targeted beneficiaries, but for medical and non-medical staff collaborating for INTERSOS as well. Where and if deemed relevant, INTERSOS will be also setting up ad hoc complaint and feedback mechanisms for other stakeholders with different level of involvement in the action.
