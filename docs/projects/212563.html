Project Summary

According to the latest HNO, there is a significant increase in the number of people requiring humanitarian assistance in Syria, with a total of 16.7 million individuals identified as in need, including 8.4 million females and 8.3 million males. This marks a rise from 15.3 million in 2023. Among those in need, 5.5 million are displaced, with over 2 million residing in last resort sites. The vast majority is in Aleppo (3,505,148 or 22%) and Idleb (2,596,397 or 16%) governorates.
In 2023, SARD conducted a Yearly Needs Assessment across various sectors, revealing urgent livelihood challenges in Aleppo and Idleb. Many people rely on daily work, facing issues like reduced job opportunities (66%), safety concerns hindering work (62%), and insufficient local job options (82.3%) with low pay (69%). Men rely more on daily work, while women often work in retail or health. Solutions like job training, financial aid, and improved market access are crucial. Respondents prefer business cash grants for sustainable livelihoods. Targeted programs with vocational training and financial support could foster resilience and sustainable recovery in these communities.
This project, delivers a strategic and integrated set of interventions to restore the local economy in the aftermath of protracted conflict and a deteriorated economic situation. The main objective is to enhance the impact over short, mid and long term by addressing funding gaps, aiming for comprehensive robust response that contributes to achieving resiliency. This involves improving overall well-being of targeted population. The project proposes an integrated protection and livelihood intervention where the most vulnerable households (HHs) will be directly targeted to enhance their resilience and promote their access to employment and entrepreneurship opportunities.
By pursuing the referred main objective, the project aims to achieve the following outcomes:
-Support the maintenance and improvement of equitable access to basic services through the creation of short-term work opportunities and rehabilitation work in markets, schools, roads, and solar systems. This includes road graveling and repair linking markets with IDP sites and creating job opportunities linked with rehabilitation work.
-Increase access to livelihood and jobs by supporting home-based businesses, with a focus on women, PwDs, and existing MSMEs. Support grants will help them maintain resilience and livelihoods, with additional training provided to enhance recovery capacity from the earthquake's adverse effects.
-Strengthen social cohesion by empowering communities to lead early recovery processes together. A Community Committee will lead ERL plans development, playing a key role in leading community projects and organizing social cohesion events, with SARD offering support and facilitation.
To ensure meaningful participation of women within proposed interventions, such as the provision of market-based modalities assistance for vulnerable households, several strategies will be implemented. SARD follow Gender-sensitive Approach so that it design interventions with a gender-sensitive lens, considering the specific needs, priorities, and constraints faced by women in accessing and benefiting from market-based assistance. SARD specify a target for women in Cash for work activities as well as in community committee. To ensure their representation, SARD give extra marks to female .
SARD has 100+ field staff being trained by Humanity and Inclusion organization on Disability inclusive programming which show SARD commitment to Disabilities. For PwDs engagement, SARD do its best to include diverse group at the design stage when SARD consult Community representatives on their needs. SARD has also started a pilot project in collaboration with its child protection programming directed specifically to adolescents and based on this pilot SARD is welling to scale up to integrate youth group into its programming

0 - Project Summary (extended)

After 12 years of conflict and economic crisis, Syria faced additional hardship with earthquakes in February 2023, leading to significant displacement, nearly 5,900 deaths, and over 12,800 injuries. The project aims to achieve the following: Equitable access to basic services are improved through the rehabilitation of basic economic and educational infrastructures linked with the creation of short-term livelihood opportunities. Access to inclusive livelihoods, entrepreneurship support is enhanced through an innovative and unique three-phase scheme (one-month vocational training, one-month apprenticeship, and four month job placement). Local workshops owners, home based businesses are supported to enhance their capacities to expand and increase their production to sustain longer-term employment and enhance the economic cycle. As well as to train and host the three-phase scheme applicants (VT, apprenticeship, and job placement. Empowering communities through a community committee (CC), the project promotes community engagement and Early Recovery initiatives, including Cash-for-work programs that provide job opportunities, foster a sense of belonging, and enhance social cohesion.
