Project Summary

Rohingya refugees have been arriving in Bangladesh in successive waves since the late 1970s, though most of the current caseload of 911,000 has arrived since August of 2017. MERCY Malaysia (MM) along with Community Initiative Society (CIS), a FD-1 & FD-7 registered NGOs have been working to provide foods and many health needs such as physical injuries treatment including emergency obstetric care services, reproductive health, and GBV case management including clinical management of rape, mental health support for PTSD patients at Balukhali, Hakimpara & Jamtoli since the beginning of the Rohingya crisis in Cox's Bazar. A Comprehensive Primary Health Care centre was established at Jamtoli Camp-15 in Dec 2018. At present CIS & MM is continuing the PHC centers at Jamtoli Camp-15 by providing different health services like primary health care with medication, Emergency care, Emergency obstetrical care, safe Normal delivery, ANC, PNC, Pathology, USG, ECG, Weekly special doctor consultancy in the center, Emergency Ambulance service for the referred patients, Emergency referral system, Home Visit by CHW, CHWs visit the selected block which is coordinated by CHW Group. Provision of Family Planning commodities to the FDMNs in this fascility after proper counselling during their visit at the center and public health awareness program for the displaced people from Myanmar as Wuhan, China, where COVID-19 is believed to have started and began to spread exponentially. Moreover, the vast majority of refugees live in one-room shelters made of tarp-covered bamboo, with nearly a third having more than six persons per household. Households do not have basic sanitation facilities meaning that refugees must leave home and often queue to use toilets, bathe, collect water or receive distributions. Even now, most of the Rohingya refugees do not have enough knowledge on preventive measures of COVID-19 which is necessary to slow the spread of the virus. Basic preventive measures such as wearing face mask, social distancing, frequent handwashing other public health educations are esential to ensure. The Jamtoli centre is providing these facilities by strictly maintaining the COVID-19's health rules & regulation like handwashing point set up, digital non contact thermal scanning, ensure wearing of face mask, establishing flu corner, marking the distace level at the waiting bench to ensure social distancing. The existing PHC center of Jamtoli, camp-15 follows the minimum package of essential health services for PHC facilities in the FDMN/Refugees camps and the both parties like CIS & MM will jointly provide the support for smooth running of the PHC. CIS is regularly attend the different coordination meeting at different level and share the challenges as well as provide the feedback where needed. All the activities of clinic are operating in collaboration with different working groups such as Health Sector, SRH, CHW, CIC, RRRC, UNO & DC and their regular visit & guideline help us a lot to improve our facility. CIS will leverage the extensive experience of health by the help of Dhaka Community Hospital for refugees and host communities and MM will provide overall project management, technical support, monitoring & other things when needed. The project seeks to continue providing these essential lifesaving health care services for the vulnerable populations living in Jamtoli's camp-15, in line with Strategic Objective 2 of Delivering Life Saving assistance to populations in need.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya refugees from Myanmar have fled to Bangladesh in several waves over the decades, fleeing violence and unrest. With limited access to regular income and livelihoods opportunities in the camps, the Rohingya refugee population is highly reliant on humanitarian assistance. There is also a lack of formal education in camps, insufficient health facilities, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) provisions, and weak shelter infrastructure. The outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic and associated containment measures put in place in camps on 24 March 2020 further severely restricted humanitarian access and service delivery to the highly aid-dependent refugee communities. With only a limited number of essential services having been provided, pre-existing levels of need are likely to have been exacerbated, with health seeking behaviors also having been affected by the COVID-19 pandemic. Knowledge and support of maternal health care is still an issue as well in the camps, with maternal deaths still being reported. The 2020 Joint Response Plan (JRP) has identified the lacking in emergency health services pertaining specifically to obstetric care, new-born care, clinical management of rape/intimate partner violence services and first line support for survivors of gender-based violence. In a recent assessment of Rohingya refugee households, 57% were unable to identify any existing GBV services. A study by UNFPA found nearly half of refugee women (46.5%) receive no antenatal care during pregnancy and only 3.5% attend the minimum of four ANC visits recommended by WHO and the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (Report on Demographic Profiling and Needs Assessment of MCH Care for Rohingya Refugee Population in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh; UNFPA/ICDDR,B; July 2018; pg. 28) . Women’s access to facility-based delivery increased significantly in 2019, but still remains below 50%. Not surprisingly, data gathered over a four-month period in 2019 indicated that 25.9% of the total deaths reported were women of reproductive age, stillbirths and neonatal deaths (Rohingya Crisis in Cox’s Bazar District, Bangladesh; Health Sector Bulletin; Oct 2019; pg. 16) . MM and CIS has been building trust with the community of Jamtoli camp 15 through the provision of ANC/PNC care over the years. MM & CIS proposes to continue the support through existing Primary Health Care Center and emergency health response through mobile clinic when needed in Jamtoli Camp – 15 of Ukhiya to provide health service to the Rohingya people and Host Community by following the minimum packages of essential health service for primary health care level in the displaced people Camp which are as follows:Out patients departmentMental health care & psychological supportFlu cornerMaternity service with ten bed facilitiesLaboratory Diagnosis,ECG & USGPharmacy

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1:Primary health care with medication.(The PHC center is running as 24/7 & semi-permanent structure with provision of safe water, separate toilet for male, female & disability with bathing facility. We have hot line number of PHC manager mentioned in front of the centre and also have consultation room where patients privacy is maintained properly. All the record & files are locked up in cabinets. Here maternity bed, inpatients bed & isolation room with one bed are available. 24 hrs.' roaster-based doctor, nurse, midwife & other staffs are available to ensure the primary health care & proper medicine distribution. Medicine are distributed according to the doctor prescription and essential medicine list. Few medicines are kept in refrigerator with appropriate temperature and well documentation and records are maintained. Proper temperature is maintained in medicine store room through fanning system. Activity 2: ANC and PNC services ( 24/7 gynecologists' , Nurse, maternity staffs are available to ensure ANC & PNC cares. Pregnant mothers are getting the services through health card during their routine ANC visit. The mother gets help through ambulance service when needed any referral) Activity 3: Safe normal delivery service (24/7 gynecologists' as well as sonologist , Midwife, Nurse, TBA & other maternity staffs are available to provide safe deliver and essential new born care) Activity 4: Pathology, ECG and USG services (CBC, Blood sugar, Stool & Urine RME, Dipstick, RDTs, Creatinine, lipid profile, ECG, USG services are provided 24/7 to provide proper diagnostic treatment. NCDs & CDs are identified through these services so that these patients can get awareness session about the risk factors of relevant diseases. Emergency patients are referred by ambulance service after diagnosis through ECG & USG. Activity 5: Mental health Care & Psychological Support (CMR trained staff and mhGAP trained physician provide the regular counselling session for the vulnerable groups like children, rape victim, GBV survivors, disable persons, post apportioned mother etc. Severe cases are referred if needed.) Activity 6:Weekly special doctor visit( Special cases like long term uncontrolled DM, Uncontrolled HTN, Cardiovascular diseases, COPD with HF are identified and every week the specialist doctors are provided the special health services to the beneficiaries. Activity 7:Provision of Family Planning commodities to FDMNs ( When the patients visit the facility or CHWs visit the selected block or during family planning awareness session the peoples take the short or long-term family planning commodities on his/her willingness after several family planning counselling sessions) Activity 8: Outreach program through CHW (CHWs visit the home of the beneficiaries in selected blocks and identify the needs for awareness like Personal health & hygiene, COVID-19, ANC & PNC care, safe delivery, breast feeding, depression, ill mental health etc. Activity 9: Public Health education program ( Centre based public health education program are arranged based on public health issues like Dengue, COVID-19, Risk factors of NCDs & CDs, ANC & PNC care , safe delivery, benefits of breast feeding, benefits of safe hospital delivery , Vaccination , Maternal & child nutrition, STIs, Anemia, mental health etc. for the benefits of FDMNs)
