Project Summary

In Syria, coordination and HIM are essential. Humanitarian needs are increasing: Accessibility, availability, functionality of health services are directly and indirectly impacted by compounded crises, worsening mortality and morbidity, related to economic decay, inflation, climatic shocks, disrupted services, massive displacement and possible return movement, hostilities and UXO contamination, outbreaks and endemic diseases.
This project aims to enhance HIS & coordination across all areas of Syria to effectively collect and analyze routine data to:
-monitor service availability, health resources and facility readiness
-inform/prioritize humanitarian actions & resource allocation
-identify the most vulnerable among affected populations
In 2024, WHO WOS will continue to support an all-modalities approach with requisite tailoring of actions to local contexts. Overall, in 2024, WHO as CLA in Syria will:
• Coordinate & excute CLA duties across the HPC
• Continue applying humanitarian principles during the emergency & recovery phases
• Expand humanitarian health assistance in line with HNO/HRP
• Deliver assistance in a transparent, equitable, non-discriminatory, respectful, dignified and non-politicized manner based on evidence.
• Prioritize most-affected/vulnerable geographic areas & population groups
• Support health strategies, plans and policies that are inclusive, non-discriminatory and target the most urgent needs of the population
• Promote standardization/alignment across response areas with local adaptation
• Advocate for the sanctity of health care facilities, patients and health workers
• Mobilize resources for humanitarian health interventions
• Engage communities to ensure civil society voice in revitalizing the health system
• Boost inter-cluster action & coordination, especially on joint programs with WASH, Nutrition, CCCM, Protection, and GBV clusters
• Intensify efforts to strengthen the overall HIS, include. monitoring morbidity & mortality trends and humanitarian impact over time.
• Launch/maintain online platforms for data collection & analysis that is accessible to partners, donors, and key stakeholders to aid joint and unified planning for health.
• Maintain DHIS2, HeRAMS & SSA platforms, by guiding and supporting the monitoring and verification processes.
• Public regular information management products under the responsibility of the health sector/cluster, including 4W, dashboards, GIS maps, EWARS/N, COVID-19, Cholera, MHPSS, Trauma, EQ, medical supplies, ambulance services, blood bank & MMTs
WHO utilizes the health sector PRA to ensure protection of the most vulnerable within the delivery of its services, including health care workers themselves. Additionally, both hubs have a dedicated PSEA focal point and have instituted a pillar-based approach to PSEA to ensure awareness AND response capacity within WHO and its implementing partners. These activities are further overseen by regional focal points. WHO also pursues and integrated approach by aligning GBV, PSEA and MHPSS to enable comprehensive response capacity. Finally, both hubs have developed and regularly update a risk matrix with specified mitigation measures. Business continuity plans are updated quarterly and WHO global has also rolled out readiness checklists to ensure offices are ready to respond to emergencies.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

WCO SYR: At Syria HCT level, WHO as CLA, will continue coordination, at strategic and operational levels, of some 50-70 active health sector partners. The work of 9 sub-national health sector coordination groups will be continued as well 6 sub-national working groups and 15 sub-national sub-sector working groups. A priority will be given for further integration and enhancement of national NGOs in the health sector..NWS: The WHO Field Presence Office in Gaziantep manages & coordinates the 172 organizations comprising the Health Cluster in NWS together with 16 WGs & TFs such as advocacy & comms, PHC, MHPSS, NCDs, trauma & disabilities, CHWs, referral system, dialysis, Thalassemia, TB, surveillance & outbreak response, SRH (co-led by UNFPA), RCCE, medical supply chain and HIS. The absence of a governance system for health in NWS has also significantly impacted the collection, sharing, and analysis of health information. This gap has been filled both by WHO and health cluster partners by designing and aligning data systems for all partners. ..WOS: WoS Health sector team based in Amman works closely with health cluster/sector coordinators across the HCT, HLG and NES cross-border response areas and other WOS-level partners. The team provides a coordination and information-sharing platform to all, coordinates and leads humanitarian program cycle-related deliverables, collects and compiles information at the WoS level, ensures a harmonized and aligned humanitarian health response approach across all three response areas, ensures common advocacy messages and contributes to resource mobilization efforts and finally contributes to strengthening overall emergency preparedness and response capacities through an all-hazard approach. -----> Please note that the % for NES budget breakdown actually represents the WOS HCC budget portion (but there is no field for this).
