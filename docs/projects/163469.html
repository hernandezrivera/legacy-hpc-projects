Project Summary

Sociopolitical issues in and concerning the Anglophone regions of Cameroon have been a
matter of contention throughout the post-colonial period. Political protests against perceived
marginalization intensified in late 2016 and 2017, led to violence in the North-West and South-West regions prompting violent clampdowns by security forces. With the increased
deployment of defense forces and proliferation of non-state armed groups, the crisis has
increasingly become characterized as one of armed conflict. Increased insecurity, the burning
down of homes, violence and consequent widespread injury and civilian loss of life have
forced thousands of families to flee their homes. Displacement continues to have serious
consequences on the livelihoods and living conditions of the affected populations. According
to the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) Situation
Report No. 3 As of 31 January 2019, the insecurity has forced more than 430,000 people to
flee their homes, more than 380,000 people need shelter, and some 418,000 people need Non-
Food Item (NFI) assistance. This situation has also affected many children and young adults diversely; many are now heads of households, many unidentified and are separated from their primary families. Youths have had to stop schooling and many have pulled out of vocational activities and are now jobless. National integrity and the essence of living together has been undermined. There is a high and urgent need to provide immediate humanitarian support and put in place reconstruction mechanisms to enhance recovery efforts and sustain peace in these affected regions. To meet these need, the Cameroon Baptist Convention Health Services through this project seeks to (1) provide trauma healing counselling to these children and youths traumatized by the crisis, (2) provide micro-enterprise trainings and financial support to these children and youths affected by the crisis to start quick income generating activities for sustainability, (3) provide legal assistance to abuse children and young adults including those living with disabilities. The Community of Practice (CoP) run by the CBCHS made up of front line service providers within the referral pathway shall be strengthened to ensure that children and young adults who have been raped or abused in any form get urgent clinical intervention in any CBCHS’ facility.

Besoins / Needs

For the past two years the Anglophone regions of Cameroon have experienced a sociopolitical crisis like it has never been in the country. This sociopolitical turmoil has affected every other sector in these regions most especially the economic, social, education and health sectors. Above 80% of the more than 400 killed persons in the middle of the crisis are the younger population (Amnesty International). Also, more than 437.000 people are internally displaced (United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs, Cameroon Situation Report). The crisis has forced many children and young adults from the villages to take refuge in major towns and cities. This has lead led high rates of Juvenal delinquency, over crowded cities, high crime wave, dependency and mistrust of authorities. As indicated by reliefweb.int report on Cameroon, most schools have been closed for the last two years and more precisely, schools have become an important focus of the crisis due to the implementation of school boycotts, severely affecting a whole generation of children. Not only due to the intrinsic dangers and physical violence involved, but because forced absenteeism has exposed these children to significant risks of diverse forms of sexual violence, association to armed forces and armed groups, and family separation; all leading to extended psycho social distress and mental disorders. Equally, the implementation of the “Ghost Town” days (when the population is not allowed to engage in any commercial activities), the daily nighttime curfew, and the closing and burning of schools, health centers, and other public services have critically disrupted the social stability of the region. According to the Geneva Palais briefing note on the situation for children in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon, some 1.3 million people, including around 650,000 children, are now in need of some form of humanitarian assistance in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon, as the security situation and living conditions continue to deteriorate. Strengthening immediate integrative response to children and young adults in need stands as a priority for this conflict-driven regions. This project will carry out a broad base intervention that will encompass major stakeholder to provide needed comprehensive response to the target population.
