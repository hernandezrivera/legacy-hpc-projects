Project Summary

AMAN's initiative, "Enlighten the Underprivileged Candle," symbolizes our unwavering commitment to providing essential educational support to Rohingya Refugees/FDMN. Our primary focus is on the academic development of children, adolescents, and youth, and we are dedicated to enhancing formal education quality through collaboration with the education sector and UNICEF, integrating the Myanmar curriculum.
In our pursuit of educational rehabilitation, the project outlines comprehensive plans for 4 Learning Facilities at Camps 8E-block D, E, Camp 15-block D, and Camp 12-block B. We prioritize academic development and emphasize beneficiary well-being through critical enhancements in WASH facilities and a complete disaster risk reduction strategy. A fundamental aspect of our approach is holistic health and hygiene maintenance through well-segregated facilities.
Key initiatives include teacher training, student assessments aligned with professional development, distribution of educational materials following the Myanmar Curriculum, and school feeding programs fostering a conducive learning environment. AMAN is committed to continuous teaching and facilitation staff training through Master Training and Facilitation Expert programs provided by BRAC and the Education Sectors, supplemented by specialized training from external experts. AMAN also provides training for its participants. This training covers a wide range of topics, including Protection GBV, Good Touch/Bad Touch-Child Protection, Hygiene, Adolescent Hygiene and Wash Management, Person With Disability Inclusion and Rights of PWD, Nutrition, Health Care, Capacity Building (Language Learning, Sports Orientation, Cultural Program, Debate), Mental Health, and Gender & Gender Equity.
Safeguarding is paramount, with designated GBV and PSEA focal points across operations for Rohingya Refugees and Bangladeshi communities. Our comprehensive Safeguarding Policy aligns with the "do no harm" principle and is embedded in staff performance assessments, background checks, reference verifications, and adherence to Codes of Conduct (CoCs). To ensure commitment permeates every facet of the operation, AMAN provides comprehensive safeguarding training for all staff, associated individuals, and program participants, cultivating a culture of vigilance.
Thorough risk assessments identify potential safeguarding risks, maintaining a risk register to monitor and measure progress periodically. Multiple reporting channels for safeguarding/PSEA incidents and case management and response mechanisms ensure efficient, sensitive handling of reports.
AMAN's unwavering dedication to safeguarding underpins continuous efforts to maintain a safe environment. This commitment extends to targeting marginalized groups, collaborating with stakeholders, and strategically locating projects in Camps 8E (Block D, E), 15 (Block D), and 12 (Block B). We aim to enroll 3% of disabled learners and LGBTQI individuals, aligning with principles and actively participating in ISCG and PSEA networks. The budget of US$188,809 for 2024 solidifies our commitment to this comprehensive educational and safeguarding program.
Six hundred (600) students will benefit from educational facilities and training, while 20 individuals, including 15 teachers and facilitators, will receive mentorship, job opportunities, and full-fledged capacity-building training. Also, 1200 parents (600 mothers and 600 fathers) will participate in either a capacity-building or community engagement workshop along with detail-oriented training.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The educational landscape for Rohingya refugees and the host community in Bangladesh reveals multifaceted challenges and distinct barriers hindering access and quality of education for children and adolescents. This understanding is derived from various sources and data points, including the Education Sector Dashboard (J-MSNA – 19 October 2023), the Education Sector Priority list for 2024, the 2023 JOINT RESPONSE PLAN, and the Bangladesh Humanitarian Situation Report No. 65 (30 September 2023).One notable issue highlighted by the Education Sector Dashboard is the literacy rate disparity between recent Rohingya refugees and those from prior influxes and the host community. The data suggests that limited access to educational systems and cultural norms affecting gender and age roles significantly hamper enrollment and practical learning, particularly for newcomers.The information from the Education Sector Dashboard emphasizes the impact of cultural norms on access to education. For example, girls are often forced out of school after puberty, perpetuating gender disparities and denying them access to education. Similarly, boys encounter challenges such as irrelevant learning materials, sometimes leading them to work instead of attending school and contributing to high dropout rates.The data from the Education Sector Dashboard also sheds light on the multifaceted nature of refugee children's hurdles. These challenges are rooted in financial constraints, cultural beliefs, gender-specific concerns, and security issues. Security concerns, especially for girls and children of older ages, pose significant risks, impacting their ability to attend school.Additionally, the challenges children face in the camps are exacerbated by limited resources and security threats, as highlighted in the 2023 JOINT RESPONSE PLAN findings. This report mentions that the closure of Learning Centers due to COVID-19 mitigation measures halved the number of children able to access learning in 2021, and barriers for older Rohingya students persisted.Furthermore, the Bangladesh Humanitarian Situation Report No. 65 (30 September 2023) underscores the efforts made by UNICEF in supporting formal and non-formal education for Rohingya children. The report indicates that, with UNICEF's support, 225,650 children accessed education, including early learning, and outlines the funding status for various components of UNICEF's response, including learning materials and access.The identified needs, drawn from these diverse data sources, underscore the imperative for comprehensive educational reforms. These reforms should address financial, cultural, gender-specific, and security challenges for both Rohingya refugees and the host community. The focus should extend beyond academic enhancement to foster safe and inclusive spaces for all children, regardless of gender, age, or sexual orientation.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Our project strategically aligns with the Rationalization Principles, utilizing extensive data from sources like the J-MSNA report, Education Sector Priority list for 2024, 2023 JOINT RESPONSE PLAN, and Bangladesh Humanitarian Situation Report No. 65. A detailed analysis of enrollment and attendance data, despite an 83% enrollment rate, reveals an 82% attendance disparity, emphasizing the need for an integrated approach. Gender-based differences in enrollment and attendance, particularly for adolescent girls, highlight our commitment to addressing gender-specific barriers. Learning challenges identified through the J-MSNA report support the need for an integrated strategy. Our focus on safety and environmental conditions, as outlined in the J-MSNA report, aligns with adopting an integrated approach. The Education Sector Priority list for 2024 and the 2023 JOINT RESPONSE PLAN reinforce our commitment to addressing educational requirements through a structured and integrated approach.Additionally, our project's alignment extends to inclusive education for children with disabilities (CWD) and the LGBTI community, evidenced by the continuous operation of learning facilities (LFs) since 2019. Implementing the Child Safeguarding Policy, coordination with stakeholders, adherence to sector guidelines, and regular reporting solidify our alignment with the Rationalization Principles, ensuring predictability and continuity in service delivery. In summary, our project demonstrates a comprehensive and integrated approach, reflecting unwavering commitment to the Rationalization Principles in addressing challenges faced by Rohingya refugee children in Cox's Bazar.

The project significantly addresses the recommended actions in the 2024 JRP Guidance Note concerning protection mainstreaming. By weaving protective elements throughout the project design, we ensure the safeguarding of vulnerable populations, particularly children, adolescents, and those with special needs, among the Rohingya refugees and host communities. Our approach aligns with the zero-tolerance policy for exploitation and abuse, resonating with the protection mainstreaming emphasis in the guidance. Robust safeguarding procedures, including Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Rights and Safeguarding, are integral to our operations, aiming to create a safe environment. We prioritize comprehensive training for staff and key stakeholders, fostering a culture of vigilance and responsibility to protect those we serve. Our strategies include rigorous background checks, reference verifications, and the implementation of Codes of Conduct (CoCs), aligning with the recommendations for safeguarding vulnerable groups. Furthermore, our contract clauses with partners and subcontractors include explicit terms on safeguarding practices. Thorough risk assessments and a structured risk register facilitate our proactive approach to identifying and mitigating potential risks, as the guidance advises. Establishing multiple reporting channels, including supervisors, safeguarding focal points, helplines, and whistleblowing processes, aligns closely with the guidance note. Comprehensive case management and response mechanisms are in place to handle reported incidents efficiently and sensitively, adhering strictly to established protocols. Our project's commitment to protection mainstreaming, as per the 2024 JRP Guidance Note, ensures that the most vulnerable individuals' safety, rights, and well-being remain a central focus throughout all our activities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

AMAN's project emphasizes Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) by actively engaging in numerous measures aligned with the 2024 JRP Guidance Note. Information, Communication, and Transparency: AMAN commits to routine information dissemination, ensuring stakeholders, including parents, caregivers, teachers, and master trainers, are well-informed about the organization's vision, mission, mandate, and accountability commitments. This includes transparency regarding feedback and complaints procedures and promoting open communication channels. Participation, Engagement, and Inclusion: The project ensures the active involvement of beneficiaries by employing the Do No Harm framework. This framework not only guides AMAN's staff but also encourages and facilitates the participation of beneficiaries, creating an inclusive and participatory educational environment in the refugee camp. Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism: To address issues effectively, AMAN will establish transparent mechanisms for feedback and complaints, allowing for a swift and efficient response. This engagement will ensure that concerns and issues are addressed promptly and constructively. Learning and Adaptation: The project emphasizes continual learning and adaptation, ensuring that lessons learned are integrated into the program's operations. The project can evolve and adapt by analysing feedback and complaints to serve affected populations better. In summary, the project's multifaceted approach aligns closely with the 2024 JRP Guidance Note principles, actively promoting transparency, inclusive participation, responsive feedback mechanisms, and adaptive learning for greater accountability to the affected populations.
