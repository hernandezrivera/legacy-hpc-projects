Needs

DRC selection of target geographical areas will be flexible based on needs and appropriateness of livelihood interventions in Borno and Adamawa states. However, DRC has pre-selected two LGAs in Borno state (Mafa and Jere LGAs) and one in Adamawa state (Michika LGA), which will be verified during inception phase through baselines and sectoral coordination with the Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector Working Group and the Food Security Sector Working Groups (both responsible for the coordination of Livelihood interventions). In September 2017, DRC participated in the OCHA-led Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) in Adamawa state. The report found that 65% of the population in Michika faces difficulties accessing livelihoods. Other LGAs not targeted by DRC such as Hong, Gombi, and Yola North had lower percentages of problems related to people accessing livelihoods (Hong – 40%, Gombi – 47%, Yola North 25%). Generally, however, the report findings demonstrated that on average in Adamawa 55% of the population has difficulties accessing livelihoods. DRC has pre-selected to target Borno (Mafa, Jere LGAs) and Adamawa (Michika LGAs) states, however DRC will be flexible to revise target locations based on multi-sectoral needs through utilizing recommendations from the Cash Working Group, DRC multi-sectoral assessments and routine monitoring findings to finalize the final target locations

Activities

Assisted households (women and youth) report at least 50% increase in household income from livelihood restoration activities: The proposed DRC livelihood activities are aimed at increasing access to income generating sources through the restoration or expansion small-medium enterprise (SMEs), supporting economic recovery and strengthening market systems in the affected communities in the proposed operational areas. DRC will also expand or revitalise existing small business of women and youth that have been affected by the conflict, with the aim of assisting increased business activities and supporting early recovery of households and communities. The targeting criteria to be used is described in the table below. These interventions are expected to build self-reliance and resilience of vulnerable households. A total of 400 households (or approximately 2,800 individuals) – mainly women and youth – will be supported with small business management training and business cash grants to improve their skills and increase their access to safer and more sustainable livelihood and income generating opportunities. Improved access to cash through entrepreneurship and employability, skills enhancement, business development, product development, and market access opportunities (value chain development) will increase income opportunities, contribute to addressing financial needs, and improve purchasing power of beneficiaries – thereby increasing resilience. 2,930 households (approximately 20,510 individuals) have received multipurpose cash grants to meet their basic needs for at least 3 months: DRC will provide unconditional multi-purpose cash assistance to 2,930 highly vulnerable households who cannot access employment or other income generating activities (IGAs). The proposed multi-purpose cash assistance will be distributed in tranches of 25,000 NGN (approximately 82 USD) per month for three months (75,000 NGN / 245 USD in total at current currency exchange rates). The value of the monthly cash assistance is based on the estimated Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) for MMC LGA, developed by the Cash Working Group (CWG) in Borno State in coordination with multi-sector sector leads of various working groups. As per the CWG MEB for MMC, the multi-round payment is designed to support household expenses to cover household needs for NFIs (personal and household goods), Shelter (including rent, utilities, fuel for various purposes), WASH (water, sanitation, and WASH-related NFIs), Education, and Services (transport and communication costs). DRC multi-sectoral assessments have determined that these multi-sector needs can be met by functional markets in the pre-selected target geographical locations – where commodities and services are available at acceptable and affordable prices. DRC proposes to implement the multi-purpose cash assistance on a short-term basis (3 months), with the objective of covering emergency needs of extremely vulnerable households. While DRC recognises that three months is a relatively short period to provide assistance, the programme is designed to meet household needs with a view to expediting their transition to recovery and self-reliance through inclusion in other types of programming. Therefore, wherever possible, DRC will select youth and women who have received the MPCA to be trained in business management and receive business cash grants under the ERMS component of this project or other DRC Livelihoods programmes funded by other donors. The MPCA is intended to complement and provide the support required to transition households to recovery and build their resilience.
