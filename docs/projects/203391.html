Project Summary

Our creative solution to the consequences of the humanitarian crises on children and communities in out-of-reach areas in Lebanon, is PSS on Wheels. Buses serving as mobile units, are equipped to facilitate access to hard-to-reach communities and complement War Child’s ongoing integrated programming of Psychosocial Support (PSS) and Child Protection (CP).
War Child interventions range from lower-intensity interventions that aim to promote well-being and prevent problems, to higher-intensity interventions targeting only children experiencing significant and enduring distress. The integrated approach embraces a socio-ecological approach, with services targeted at different ecological levels, which recognizes that children’s development is inextricably linked to the families, communities, economic situation, social values and cultural influences that surround them and provide for their basic needs and protection.
The mobile unit will facilitate access to all groups, especially the most vulnerable and girls and women. In addition, and in case of any rising humanitarian emergency, the mobile unit will be able to respond efficiently and in time, delivering vital services such as Psychological First Aid (PFA) and referrals to specialized services through case management.
Key objectives and activities
1. Boys and girls, men and women at risk, residing in hard-to-reach communities, have safe and equal access to an integrated package of PSS and CP services, leading to improved mental health and well-being of children.
2. Vital protection and psychosocial support services are delivered efficiently and in time by the mobile unit.
An inclusion specialist will be on board to ensure safety of and accessibility for children with disabilities, to support in adapting/adjusting the activities and provide needed guidance when it comes to identification and referrals to a specific specialized service.
The PSS on wheels will provide the following services on board and in the outside setting:
 Community-based PSS activities through TeamUp intervention that offers structured movement-based activities for children age 6 up to 17 to learn how to deal with their emotions. The skills they gain from the activities are linked to social-emotional themes and can be of use in their daily life. The intervention includes a Disability Inclusion Guide that will enable facilitators to engage children with disabilities and ensure their full and active participation.
 Puppets, theater and music are integrated in the community-based PSS sessions.
 CP awareness sessions for children and caregivers, in addition to internal referrals that will take place from the activities to War Child services and/or external referrals to existing external service providers.
 The Caregiver Support Intervention that will enhance caregivers well-being through enabling them to have a better understanding of their emotions and providing them with techniques to cope with the difficult ones , and effective parenting techniques to improve the interaction with their children.
 Provision of focused psychosocial support (FPSS - Level 3 of the IASC MHPSS pyramid) for children and families whose coping capacity is overwhelmed. These might include children in acute distress due to recent exposure to serious stressors or children who are not growing and learning according to their age and developmental phase.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Responds to children and community needs through integrated approach embeddingprotection, PSEA, gender and social inclusion. WCH staff, associates and partners adhereto the PSEA elements of WCH's Code of Conduct and its Child Safeguarding Policy;includes knowing their responsibilities in keeping people safe from sexualexploitation/abuse and ensuring no harm comes to participants by conduct of staff,associates and partners. Identification and referral of protection concerns will be on-goingin project as in all our projects. Girls and boys, as necessary, including with disabilities willbe referred to services of case management unit, and to external services, whenappropriate. A set of sessions on PSEA, gender and social inclusion will be provided to themembers of the project Parent/ Caregiver groups and teachers. We continuously increasethe knowledge and skills of partners on these topics in all projects so they will be thetrainers/facilitators with WCH supporting.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Through it’s community-based approach, WCH effectively accesses vulnerable hard-toreachpopulations, in line with its commitment to strengthen local capacity through amutual learning process. WCH is a member of the CHS alliance and is taking steps toaddress the participation and accountability as pillars in the CHS commitments andwithin the principles of protection mainstreaming. Ongoing feedback and reflectionsessions are held between the implementation teams and partners by communityfacilitators/teachers/parent groups with project participants through feedback groupexercises, FGDs or progress tools used to evaluate all aspects of the project during face toface & remote modalities. Drop-out assessments are conducted to assess the reason andrespond to it. Feedback and lessons learned are documented, analyzed and sharedamong decision makers, field/technical staff and communities/partners through learningevents for a more effective and efficient processes and results.
