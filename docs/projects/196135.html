Project Summary

This project aims to increase food security and enhance the livelihoods of IDPs and host communities (50% IDPs, 50 % host communities) in Idlib and Aleppo Governorates through supporting the agricultural and livestock value chains during 2023 as follows:
The first stage: (Support Value Chains for strategic crops such as wheat and olive crops).
This intervention will seek to support these households to begin to restore this production, as the main source of livelihood and to enhance their resilience by enabling them to have more food availability through personal production of the crop products in addition to improving the crops value chain by addressing the gaps during the chain (Production, processing, marketing).
The number of beneficiaries from agriculture interventions will be 25000 HHs.
Key interventions will include:
• Provision vouchers (500 $) to buy agricultural inputs for service 5 dunum.
• Capacity building targeting farmers and local community members.
• Support olive oil marketing and wheat marketing by purchasing olive oil (2 oil cans) and wheat( 1ton) from beneficiaries to help farmers to market their products. olive harvesting, etc.).
• Support the production of bread by milling wheat and providing free flour for bakeries to produce bread and distributing it free of charge to the 5000 IDPs families in the camps (2 bundles of bread per family).
• Distribution of olive oil cans to the 2000 IDPs families.
The M&E department will conduct an assessment of gaps and bottlenecks within the value chains of strategic crops and ATAA will design an intervention that addresses these gaps along the chain stages.
The second stage:(Support the Value Chain for livestock).
This intervention will include:
1- Supporting 7000 livestock breeders "sheep, goats, and cows) with veterinary services (vaccines "FMD, PPR, Entrotoximia, brucella, sheep and goat pox", anti-parasitic, artificial insemination, and treatments).
2- Supporting 1500 livestock breeders" cows and sheep" by distributing 1000 MT fodder.
3- Support 1500 HHs with poultry (12 Layer hens and 2 roosters, feed, and chicken coop).
4- Establishing a veterinary laboratory to support animal breeders and veterinarians by providing appropriate diagnosis and effective treatment of antimicrobials quickly and effectively.
The number of beneficiaries from livestock interventions will be 10000 HHs.
The selection criteria for livestock activity will be regarding FSS_so2, so3 response package-HRP2022.
ATAA also plans to increase the awareness of the host community and the IDPs related to the Coronavirus, and cholera risk and the need to take preventive measures.
ATAA will identify, verify, and profile target beneficiaries through local councils according to the eligibility selection criteria:
• The shortlist will be identified depending on the vulnerability criteria
• Women-headed households (widows, divorced, GBV survivors).
• Large families (more than 5).
• Families with disabled members.
• Families with low income (less than 50 USD)
• Families with elderly (men-women).
 ATAA will remain AAP through open dialogue, that accurate and timely information (including goals, outcomes, and any eligibility criteria to participate) will share with them.
Note: Funding the project is not secured.

3 - Needs

The overall estimated Sector PIN for 2022 – of food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity – is estimated at 13.9 million, with 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The acute food insecure figure includes 1.9 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure (HNO 2023). The decline in agricultural production output was not only limited to rainfed food production systems but also to irrigated systems as cereals, legumes, barley, and vegetables, indications are that wheat production for 2022 was generally lower than LTA, especially due to the failure of most rainfed planted areas.Further, the crisis in Ukraine will likely lead to higher fertilizer, diesel, power prices for agricultural production, and electricity for food processing in Syria. Livestock hasn’t also been spared from the impact of prevailing water shortage & scarcity, limited access to livestock production inputs especially feed, and the generally unfavorable weather conditions. The unfavorable 2021 – 2022 rainfall season resulted in a sharp decline in pasture growth and development, increased demand for fodder, limited supply, high prices, especially imported feed and this trend is likely to continue if the forecasted below-average rainfall for 2022/2023 is realized. The worsening economic crisis affecting Syria has also resulted in less efficient animal health services.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ATAA PSEA policy developed in June 2022 and with official approvals from PSEA network and UNICEF PSEA focal point and delivered to all staff to be signed, on the other hand ATAA became low risk partner in SEA with UNICEF for 5 years after conducting PSEA assessment.ATAA adopted a core concept in all intervention to make PSEA and CSG as mandatory in all projects and activities, in this regard, all ATAA staff, volunteers and contractors will get orientation sessions about PSEA and CSG policies and sign both PSEA and CSG code of conducts.All project locations will include banners and complaint boxes with delivering messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels in addition to stand alone system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case starting from receiving the report, to investigation process, and provide aid and support to the survivor till closure of the case after achieving the objectives of investigation.ATAA is PSEA network member participate in all regular network meetings, in addition to submit monthly reports to the network, in addition to conduct training for all staff about PSEA and target humanitarian workers in the intervention location to support the role of PSEA network.ATAA is operating PSEA stand-alone project focusing on raising the awareness of targeted communities (78 community) in NW Syria about PSEA and enhance the ability of BNFs to reach reporting channels of ATAA and PSEA network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Markets are functioning and all the inputs are available. The crops varieties are stapled and adapted to the environment. There is social acceptance for the project and the people are prepared to interact with it. Risk has been classified into environmental and security risks. Environmental risks: 1-Plant diseases will be addressed through the distribution of pesticides in a timely manner in addition to strict observation by the competent agronomists through continuous tours of farmers' fields to investigate any diseases and advise farmers ac2- Fire during the harvest season: there is a high risk during the harvest due to factors such as high temperature or neglect, in addition to the Deliberate shelling during harvest. Raising the awareness of the local community about the dangers of fire and methods to mitigate it by: -Removing the weeds on the roadside -Work the lines of fire (firebreaks)between farmers' lands to prevent the spread of fire from one field to another.Security risk: This will be processed with strict follow-up by the S&S Department and develop contingency and evacuation plans.For livestock intervention the risks will be addressed as follows: In the case of difficulty in securing the quantity of required sheep from one location: We will purchase from more than one supplier according to the approved procurement policies and alternative supply plans. Ataa will take into account the risks of cholera spreading during the project phases
