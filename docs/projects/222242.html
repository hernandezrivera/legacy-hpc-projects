Project Summary

Access to education in South Sudan faces severe challenges due to interconnected crises stemming from civil wars, extreme poverty, displacement, child marriage, early pregnancy, and the devastating impacts of climate change, such as extreme flooding, which have destroyed schools and displaced communities. Despite some initial progress following independence in 2011, these challenges have left 2.8 million school-age children out of school, with only 11% of girls enrolled in secondary education. The prolonged conflict has compounded risks like violence, exploitation, and psychosocial trauma among children, exacerbated by the economic fallout of COVID-19, which increased poverty and learning losses. Furthermore, the quality of education remains critically low, with crowded classrooms, scarce textbooks, and inadequate support for children with disabilities. Only 19% of teachers are qualified, and there is just one functioning National Teacher Training Institute, contributing to high teacher absenteeism due to irregular wages. Despite high pass rates in final exams, these figures raise doubts about the credibility of assessment systems in the country. ESA 2023 . In Aweil East, the 2023 AEC reported a high dropout rate, with a student-to-textbook ratio of 8:1. The 2023 ENA revealed that over 50% of schools in Aweil East lack space for outdoor games, and only 11.3% offer safe recreational activities.
The proposed project aims to expand access to quality education and establish a safe and supportive environment for children affected by conflict and displacement. The IRC will respond to the needs and train 255(54female,201Male) on basic pedagogy skills, Child Safeguarding, Psycho-Social Support (PSS) and Psychological First Aid (PFA) and strengthening of referral pathways and establishment of child help desk for identification and referrals for the children to access basic services including GBV and other form of harm against children and education personnel. Further the learners will be supported through schools’ clubs that will focus on peace building, PSS support and Adolescence health and Reproductive health. Provision of teaching and learning kits for leaners, teachers and schools will be provided to 15,000 Children including children with disabilities, 255 teachers and 15 schools across Aweil East County in 3 Payams. The project will also engage existing community structures by ensuring 165(75F,90M) Parent Teacher Association members will be trained in child safeguarding and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) as well as engagement in strengthening student’s enrollment and attendance in schools through mass campaigns and household visits. The project will ensure all groups are reached including girls, boys, men, and women as well as children with special needs. This project will be achieved through the 2 approved project within IRC education sector one being the Education Research in Conflict and protracted Crisis (ERICC) which will support on generation of evidence for education stakeholder and the second being the project that focus on improve the education and protection services to affected populations.

1. Needs

South Sudan’s independence in 2011 brought educational improvements (2012-2016), but subsequent civil wars (Dec 2013, Jun 2016) reversed some of these gains. Ongoing crises, including poverty, inadequate infrastructure, child marriage, displacement, conflict, and extreme weather, severely hinder provision of education services. Climate change exacerbates these challenges: extreme flooding has submerged 887 schools in multiple states including Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Unity (HNO 2023). Often, schools are used as evacuation centres, shelter, and safe spaces for displaced populations (HNO 2023). This complex situation has left an estimated 2 million children, comprising 1,535,257 residents, 311,447 internally displaced persons, and 189,185 returnees, in dire need of educational support. Only 57% of school-age children are in primary school, 11% in secondary, 6% in pre-primary, and 2.8 million children are out of school (ESA 2023). Conflict has increased risks of violence, exploitation, and trauma among children. COVID-19 further worsened economic conditions, increasing poverty and educational disruption and communities and learners are yet to recover.Nearly 41,000 teachers are grappling with the challenges posed by the humanitarian emergency, particularly in crisis-affected locations where access to basic resources is severely limited ( Education Cluster HRP2024 Infographic Report). Consequently, the quality of education in South Sudan is extremely low, characterized by crowded classrooms, textbook shortage (textbook ratios are at 8:1 for primary children) (HNO 2023) and insufficient support for learners with disabilities. Literacy and numeracy rates at primary level are low, despite primary leaving exam pass rates of 90% (2020) and secondary exam pass rates of 92% (2021), which raises questions about its credibility (ESA 2023). Only 19% of teachers have post-secondary qualifications and there are only three functioning National Teacher Training Institutes (HRNP 2024). Teacher absenteeism is high in government-funded schools due to low and irregular wages. One-third of teachers are untrained and average pupil-teacher ratio is 93:1. Only 20% of teachers are female at primary level and 9% at secondary (HRNP 2024). The low presence of female teachers in the country has serious ramifications on the achievement of SDG's especially on goal no. 5 on gender equality that seeks to achieve gender equality and empower all women and girls. In 2024, 41,000 teachers in South Sudan will need support and incentives to uphold educational standards (HRNP 2024). There is a notable lack of comprehensive research on availability of mental health psychosocial support (MHPSS) resources in schools (NEA 2021). Most headteachers surveyed reported that 50% of schools do not have a mentor or counsellor for learners. Among teachers who have received training since 2018, only 38.7% were trained on Gender-Based Violence, 35% on child rights, 34.5% on MHPSS, and 34% on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE). Additionally, NEA 2023’s preliminary findings revealed 40.3% of teachers reported absence of school policies, training, or practices to reduce bullying, harassment, and discrimination and 45.3% of teachers stated that there is no system for reporting safeguarding concerns.The IRC 2023 - Mult Sectoral Needs Assessment- identified in Aweil East, education emerged as a significant unmet need alongside food and medical care. In Aweil East, 64% of respondents reported a lack of education, following food (74%) and medical care (73%). These findings underscore the critical need for interventions to address education gaps in both areas, compounded by other pressing basic needs.

2. Project activities

The project will promote school enrollment through household visits and Back-to-Learning campaigns in Mangar Tong, Wunlung, and Mangok Payams, targeting vulnerable children, including those with disabilities. Local authorities will lead mass campaigns for a smooth 2025 school reopening. To enhance learning quality, teaching and learning kits will be provided for 15,000 students, 225 teachers, and 15 schools in Aweil East County, addressing material shortages reported in the 2023 Education Census. Materials will be locally sourced to ensure timely delivery, supporting uninterrupted learning and aligning with education strategy goals for the 2025 academic year.Aligning with Education Cluster Strategic Objectives 1 and 2 and INEE Standard 9, the IRC Project addresses critical gaps in safeguarding and psychosocial support in Aweil East County.Child Safeguarding Training: Schools face significant challenges in protecting learners, especially girls, with limited teacher training and inadequate referral mechanisms (Education Cluster, 2021). To tackle this, IRC will conduct a three-day safeguarding training for 255 teachers (54 women, 201 men) in Mangar Tong, Wunlung, and Mangok Payams. Delivered in collaboration with IRC’s Child Protection program, the training will focus on identifying and preventing child safety risks, implementing safe referral processes, and reinforcing MoGEI’s Teachers' Code of Conduct. A referral pathway system will also be integrated.PSS and PFA Training: The PSS–MYRP survey highlights gaps in psychosocial support, with fewer than half of surveyed schools having a PSS mentor or counselor and only 34% of head teachers reporting PSS-trained teachers (Education Cluster, 2021). IRC will deliver a three-day training for 165 PTA members (75 women, 90 men) and 255 teachers, covering Psychological First Aid (PFA) and MHPSS. The training will establish referral pathways to link students in need with nearby healthcare and protection services, ensuring a coordinated response to student needs. These initiatives aim to create safer, more supportive learning environments.Following child safeguarding and PFA training, the project will establish child help desks in schools, managed by trained focal point teachers with support from an education protection officer. These desks will ensure timely case referrals through school mechanisms, linking students to health, nutrition, and protection services, aligned with education cluster objective 3 and INEE standard 10.To address gaps revealed by the 2023 Education Census—only 18% of schools implement life skills and peace education—the project will establish 15 school clubs engaging 750 adolescents (375 girls, 375 boys). Activities include a three-day training on Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health, sports, and peacebuilding to foster social cohesion and teamwork, supported by mental health professionals.Additionally, the project supports evidence-based advocacy through the ERICC research initiative, aiming to transform education policy and practice in crisis contexts by promoting sustainable, high-quality education systems that enhance children’s holistic learning and development as emphasized in education cluster strategic three.
