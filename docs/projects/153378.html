Project Summary

The level of humanitarian emergencies in South Sudan is still high as communities are still witnessing conflict and drought. In Mid -June 2018, thousands have been forced to flee out of their homes after fighting broke in different location in Wau County, The displaced people were mainly women, children and elderly who had arrived from Great Baggari in Wau to Agok, Hai Masna about 8km from Wau town. The nutrition situation still remains dire, with many counties Greater Bahr el Ghazal, and Eastern Equatoria expected to experience a critical or very critical status during the lean season, based per the IPC acute nutrition analysis. Furthermore, humanitarian access remains a major challenge in implementing life-saving interventions. Islamic relief World Wide (IRW) South Sudan Operation, has been part of Humanitarian response in Wau (Bazia and Baggari), Tonj North and Kapoeta East. During the 2018 interventions in health and nutrition IRW have witness the humanitarian gaps and needs to Vulnerable population in Wau (Bazia, Baggari County) and Eastern Equatoria Kapoeta East, that still needs to be intervened.
In Kapoeta East, Narus Payam, Lopua Boma, IR managed to construct a PHCU, and Established OTP and TSFP with WASH facilities that supported, 8,000 Individuals from 16km walk to access basic health and nutrition service. IR is willing to maintain the facility and establish 2 OTP sites (Lochua and Lomuta) given the population in this areas are still Vulnerable moreover the latest SMART survey done in June 2018, GAM – WHZ Score level of serious (10.4%) and initial trend of Critical and emergency (IPC 4 and 5).
WAU

Though, access to the sites were major challenge due to the renewed conflict and lack of timely approval/ security clearance from the government bodies, the level of GAM- in Wau remains high ≥ 24% (MUAC); Proxy SAM rate for all children aged 6-59m is 4.6 and Proxy MAM rate for all children aged 6-59m is 19.4% while % of PLW with MUAC <23.0cm is 13% from IRRM data;
thus there is need for multi- agency support in-order to reach wider coverage of conflict affected beneficiaries.

Purpose of Intervention
The overall purposes of the intervention is live-saving and contribution to reduction of acute malnutrition related morbidity and mortality within the stated 3 Counties. However IR is mainly focused to contribute to the HRP Strategic and Nutrition Cluster Objectives. Both HRP and cluster objectives (SO1) that focuses on saving lives and contribute to reducing prevalence of acute malnutrition and death rates among under-five children, pregnant and lactating women. IR also intends to Contribute to saving children and promoting access to basic preventive nutrition services.
Proposed Objective activities - IR intervention shall contribute into following through
SO: 1 Deliver quality life-saving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at-risk under-five children, pregnant and lactating women as this contribute to reducing prevalence of acute malnutrition and death rates among under-five children, pregnant and lactating women
A. Treating and rehabilitation of 3629 children 6 -59 months and 2560 pregnant and lactating women suffering from Moderate Acute malnutrition through a Targeted Supplementary feeding programme (TSFP).
B. Treating and rehabilitation of 891 children 6 -59 months suffering from Severe Acute malnutrition through Out Patient Therapeutic feeding programme (OTP).
SO:2 Increased access to maternal infant and young child nutrition programs preventing under-nutrition for the most vulnerable and at risk
A. Prevention of MAM and SAM, through promotion of Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN), health and hygiene practices among women and children will be conducted. This Contribute to saving children and promoting access to basic preventive nutrition services
B. Enhanced nutrition situation monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information for early warning timely coordinated response decision making.
SO:3 Increased access to integrated nutrition, health, WASH, FSL and protection interventions/responses in counties
In Kapoeta East, IR has been implementing Integrated programing, of Health, WASH, FSL and referral /Linking to protection services and thus this shall be maintained in Kapoeta East and strategically re-established in Wau if there will be stability; However the humanitarian situation, mainly lack of access to the project site may hinder this and the entire process of integration; though, IR will try as much as it can to integrate health and nutrition component as minimum integration intervention in Bazia and Baggari.

Needs

Kapoeta East Protracted conflict, increased drought necessitated by climate change, large-scale displacements, high food prices, market disruptions, macro-economic collapse and exhaustion of households’ coping mechanisms. The situation in Kapoeta East is currently serious (IPC Phase 3) and is anticipated to recline into Emergency (IPC Phase 4) due to the massive crop failure in the main agricultural season for 2018. Kapoeta East is in former Eastern Equatorial state where according to the current IPC, 39.2% of the population is estimated to be in Crisis, Emergency & Catastrophe and therefore in need of humanitarian assistance. The nutrition status show serious levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM 10.4%). The nutrition situation is expected to deteriorate significantly possibly reaching the upper limit of the critical levels as a result of unprecedented levels of food insecurity, high morbidity, outbreaks of diarrhoea and other illness, poor infant and young feeding practices as well as limited access to services due to insecurity and heavy rains in the second season. The majority of the inhabitants of Kapoeta East County are livestock keepers and depend entirely on livestock for their livelihood and also practice seasonal crop production. However, due to the three years drought in the area they have become vulnerable as their livestock lack water to drink and pasture to feed; crop production is now disrupted and Hunger remains a key challenge. Some household spent days without having a meal on the table. Livestock owners have to migrate to a far distant looking for pasture and water for their animals at the neighbouring border of Kenya, Ethiopia and Uganda. WAUThe resent conflict in Western Bar el Gazhal (Wau) identified the displaced people as mainly women, children and elderly who had arrived from Great Baggari in Wau.The nutrition GAM rate in Western Bar el Gazhal was 13.2% (WHZ score in 2017 within, while in Baggari it was 18.8. (MUAC ) due to conflict, lack of humanitarian response, the GAM is ≥ 24% (IRRM data). On 23 August 2018, Islamic Relief in collaboration with State Ministry of Health (SMOH); County Health Department (CHD) and HealthnetTPO conducted a rapid needs identification led by the CHD Wau county in Barlow, Mbili, Hai Masna and Agok; where by the conflict affected displaced community from Baggari, Bazia, Basilia and Wadalela are sheltering in Hai Masna and Agok PHCC compound. Some of the critical gap identified were:- Inadequate number of staff especially in Hai Masna ; lack of nutrition services and lack of other basic services including immunization and ante-natal care. Tonj NorthIn Tonj inter and intra-communal conflicts have distracted the agricultural production cycle in the target Payams of the two counties of Tonj North and Tonj East. Homes and farms have been abandoned and populations displaced to market centres as IDPs. Akop PHCC and Athieng-Puol PHCU are the only health facilities in the county covering all the six Payams and because of this, there is an overwhelming number of patients that surpass the capacity of the two facilities. Particularly in the case of acute malnutrition. Bad roads because of rain, Transport cost and long distance have remained a challenge for people to access services. The previous GAM rate was ≥ 14% (WHZ score); with the current lean season and inter communal conflict the situation might go to critical level in absence of humanitarian assistance.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

IRSS staff at the field office openly interact with the community during meetings, and it is in this meetings whereby a series of issues are discussed. At the commencement of the project, the project team clearly explain to the affected population the objectives of the project, the duration, the target villages, the number of the affected people the project intend to serve and how the response will be, their roles and contribution in the project, entitlements etc. IRSS will display project related beneficiary entitlements, activities through visibility boards using pictures/illustrations and in local languages for all community members to understand the purpose. IRSS will also recruit community mobilizers who act as translators, interpreters and who live in the community as a community resource person to make communication as smoothly as possible. Finally, regularly monthly meetings with the community members will be undertaken so that they will be informed and sensitized on the project, their roles, entitlements and their contribution. IRSS establish health and Nutrition committee at facility level are formed with members selected from all sections of the target beneficiaries (male, female, elderly, disabled etc) also have the responsibility of explaining the entitlement to the rest of the beneficiaries and community members.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All the messages related to health and nutrition promotion are made contextualized or tailored to the local needs especially needs of women, girls, boys and men are considered and focused. Banners and other printing materials are also made in local language so that targeted communities (men, women, boys and girls) understand it easily. For example, nutrition promotion activities will involve women and men. The CNVs are sensitized on referral pathways so as to ensure timely and appropriate referral for services in the facility.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IRSS will integrate proactive measures to minimize SEA risks, through staff capacity development and codes of conduct. All new IR staff complete a mandatory induction which includes the Code of Conduct (reviewed and signed), and where a session (1:1 or group) is provided by both Protection and Gender staff. IR also has policies on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), child protection and a Safeguarding focal point to address any allegations of sexual exploitation or abuse and all staff are trained on these policies. In addition, Programme staff participate in training sessions on protection and gender (general principles of gender mainstreaming and safe programming and specific themes). Particularly on being able to detect indicators of violence and ask the right questions; the training of project staff on gender equality and protection; undertaking GBV risk analysis to make sure that no-harm is done; undertaking monthly monitoring of access to nutrition services for women, girls, and vulnerable populations such as elderly and people with disabilities and the collection of monitoring data that is sex and age disaggregated.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

IRSS has established a feedback and Complaints mechanisms and reporting procedures at the field offices where all the beneciaries are given the leeway to communicate their grievances directly to the office. A complaints and feedback mechanism focal point (CFM focal point) is available in each of the field offices with a dedicated hotline phone number where network is available. During the project implementation, accountability committees (community health and nutrition committee) will be formed on community need vulnerability criteria. The committee will be trained on the purpose of the complaints and feedback mechanisms as well as how to collect the information from the community and report through the established system. Visibility boards will also be installed at the project sites with the hotline telephone number put on it and the name of the CFM focal point. The CFM focal person therefore records grievances for each complainant in the complaints/feedback form while greatly considering the confidentially of the complainant and share with the management team at the country Office for appropriate decision to be taken. IR responses to this complaints/feedbacks are always clear, accurate, balanced, simple, fair and easy to understand. All the issues raised in the complaint will be addressed. In summary, below are the mechanisms IR South Sudan is employing in tracking complaints/feedbacks • In person, i.e. verbal feedback or complaint to any staff member of IRSS especially the CFM focal person. This is really beneficial as people with low literacy levels are also given a platform to express themselves and make recommendations on how IRSS can further improve on the project. • By phone/Mobile (call or text SMS). Focal person is assigned to collect and record feedback and complaints • Via a ‘suggestion/ complain box/visibility boards erected at the project sites

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A project monitoring framework will be developed based on the approved logical framework approach and targets in consultation with different community stakeholders. The project monitoring framework will have indicators to track the progress and capture information on gathering evidence to inform results achieved against the project targets. The proposed monitoring system will provide an ‘early warning’ to issues that might risk the project completion and allow for timely appropriate action to be taken if the project is not implemented according to the approved target plan. The project monitoring will be conducted on weekly and monthly basis by Health and nutrition coordinators supported by the MEAL staff. A joint monitoring and supervision will also be conducted on quarterly basis with the participation of government departments and local community representatives. Monthly monitoring reports will be produced in order to document the project progress and share with management. Moreover, at field level, IRSS will involve community nutrition volunteers who collect data at the level of community and participate in the monitoring and follow up of the project implementation at site levels. Due to insecurity and access challenges, involvement of the local committees in the implementation will be very significant and help IR to have representatives from the local communities that participate in the project implementation as well as monitoring. Having such strong role from the target communities will not only help to better monitor the progress towards project objectives but it will also help to build local ownership of the project services and infrastructures in the long term. This will lead to gradual take over by the local community and authorities after the project ends and IR exits

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IRSS gender analysis shows that women and girls have been disproportionately affected by this crisis. In the target areas women have an important role in providing economic support to their families amid these hard times and yet lack decision making power at both the HH and community levels hence are more exposed to protection threats. Women and girls are particularly affected by a lack of basic health and nutrition services, IRSS will address these needs through active participation of health and nutrition committee members in the project activities, stress on engaging volunteers from community especially women and equal opportunities for recruitment for males and females. The staff and community committee members will be train on how to provide awareness about equal opportunities for all vulnerable groups specially women and girls.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IRSS has a gender and protection policies in place that guide staff conduct and equal employment opportunities. The participation of women and girls in community health and nutrition committee, address inequality and protection threats in the target community as members of the committee are being train and aware on handling the challenges in the community. During service intervention are delivered to every person regardless of age, gender, religion and social diversity. Understanding that women, men, boys and girls are affected differently, IRSS will build local capacity on protection threats both gender.
