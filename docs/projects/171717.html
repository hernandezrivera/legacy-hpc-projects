Project Summary

According to the last FSNAU report on May 9, 2020, – Up to 2.7 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse outcomes through December 2020. Moreover, an estimated one million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through mid-2020. An estimated 2.6 million people across Somalia remain internally displaced which will worsen humanitarian needs. Current high levels of food insecurity are driven by drought conditions and compacted by continued displacements and conflict. Data obtained from UNHCR indicates that an estimated 126,000 people were displaced be-tween Jan and March 2020, mainly because of insecurity (76%), mostly affecting Lower Sha-belle, Bay, Galgaduud and Gedo regions. As of 26 October 2020, according to Somalia’s Ministry of Health and Human Services, Somalia has a total 3941 cases and 14 deaths. Data obtained from the Somalia Nutrition Cluster indicates a 13 percent increase in monthly new admissions of acutely malnourished children between January and March 2020 compared to the first-quarter average monthly admission for 2016-2019. This can be attributed to sporadic disease outbreaks (acute watery diarrhea-AWD/cholera and measles out-break), and worsening of the food security situation in many areas.
Maternal and child health indicators are among the worst in the world in Somalia, with maternal mortality rates at 732 per 100,000 live births, under-five mortality rate at 137 per 1,000 live births and skilled delivery rates of only 29.7% (UNICEF Somalia Annual Report 2017). The Nutritional surveys conducted by FSNAU in June and July 2019 indicate the persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices. At the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10–14.9%) over the past three seasons (13.8% in 2019 Gu, 12.6% in 2018 Deyr and 14.0% in 2018 Gu). Concern has directly run Health and Nutrition programmes in multiple regions Banadir (Mogadishu) and Afgoye communities. Lower Shabelle and Gedo since 2008 and developed long-standing, trusting relationships with key actors including communities served, MoH authorities, peer Non-Government Organizations and UN. In 2019 Concern undertook direct service provision in 10 integrated health and nutrition facilities in Banadir (6) and Lower Shabelle regions (4), including over 100,000 Out Patient Department (OPD) consultations for children and adults, serving a total catchment population of 400,000. Furthermore, since late 2017 Concern has managed the stabilization center at the National Referral hospital in Mogadishu, which provides treatment for children with life-threatening complications of severe acute malnutrition. Concern is proposing to support and increase access to health and nutrition services, particularly maternal & child health services.

Project Activities

Concern is proposing to support and increase access to health and nutrition services, particularly maternal & child health services at ten sites in Benadir, five Lower Shabelle, two Gedo and two sites in Bay regions. The basic service provision will support communities through the prevention and treatment of malnutrition (SAM and MAM), immunizations, ANC, PNC, and outpatient treatment for diseases as per the IMNCI approach for < 5 children and vaccinations as per the Somalia EPI schedule. OPD consultation services will be offered to all members of the community in addition to PLWs. These will be Primary Health Care services as per the EPHS. The project will support the work of Community Health Workers in sensitizing and mobilizing communities in accessing health and nutrition services by focusing on IYCF messaging at both community and facility levels for better detection of malnutrition and other illnesses and thus shock responsive early referral to relevant services at community and facility levels. Concern and partner’s health worker staff will be trained on the provision of EPI vaccines as well as IMNCI. In all of these facilities and their catchment areas Concern is proposing Covid 19 preparedness, prevention and response activities in reference to the MOH Covid 19 response and preparedness plan as well as the Somalia Inter-Agency Contingency plan for COVID-19. Proposed activities include: Infection Prevention and Control measures, recruitment and training of health workers and Community Health workers, planning, coordination, implementation and monitoring to enhance EWARS, PPE provision, Case referral, Risk Communication and Community Engagement, establishment of extra triage areas and handwashing facilities as well as psychosocial care. Considerable efforts will be made to ensure mainstreaming of GBV interventions i.e. provision of CMR services, GBV awareness, sensitization as well as referral linkages, a separate room will be made available in the ANC sections to ensure confidentiality access to services especially for GBV cases as well as promote adolescent friendly services. Outreach services will be provided in hard to reach locations to ensure beneficiary protection by preventing them from walking long distances in search for Health and Nutrition services. To ensure WASH integration, all facilities will be incorporated with WASH elements i.e. setting up of handwashing stations as well as incorporation hygiene messaging in the broader IYCF / e curriculum.

Mitigation Measures

Project implementation staff consider the IOM displacement registers for each camp to ensure they are aware of potential new arrivals that may not be aware of the services; have CHW conduct sanitization at field level to ensure maximum awareness; clearly demarcate the facility (were security permits) with both graphics and signage to increase awareness in communities; work with community groups for inputs and ensure they have a diverse demographic of the community. Work with DAC forums and other community groups to ensure the displaced person are being included to decision making processes and meetings (men and women); accurate assessments, both individual and joint, are conducted throughout the implementation period to determine caseloads and levels of need taking into consideration extremely vulnerable persons; project activities are designed with the specific and additional needs faced by displaced communities in consideration; registrations at health facilities are aware and sensitive to communities dynamics: clans, host vs displaced etc. so as not to increase possible conflict triggers; Facilities are, as much as possible, located centrally to affected populations so as not to cause undue pressure on patients trying to attend; clinic hours take into consideration the daily responsibilities of patients, particularly women who culturally have responsibility for domestic duties; Code of Conduct and patients right information is displayed clearly within facilities, patients and attendees of the clinic are made aware of CRM mechanisms. People with disabilities will be given a key consideration in this programming. Considerable efforts will be made to enhance disability inclusiveness in provision of health and nutrition services i.e. efforts would be made to ensure disability pathways are put up in health facilities and wheel chairs provided to enhance mobility of physically handicapped beneficiaries. In the planning, implementation and evaluation phase’s resources will be set aside to ensure beneficiary involvement with respect to the 15% global calculation. Community health structures i.e. Village health Committees will have a criterion for inclusion of people with disabilities and as required efforts would be made to use basic sign language communication as deemed necessary for the hearing impaired. Advocacy and multi – sectoral collaboration efforts would be made to work with protection actors and actors directly involved in assistance of people living with disabilities
