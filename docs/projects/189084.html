Project Summary

The food and nutrition insecurity level in South Sudan is alarming. A 7% increase from 7.7 M in 2021 to 8.3M people projected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May – July).(HNO2022). This intervention aligns with the 2022 Food Security and Livelihood cluster Strategic Objectives (SOs); SO1: Lifesaving, SO2: Centrality of protection and SO3: Resilience/ Coping mechanisms. As well as the FSL Cluster Objectives (COs) CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 3, 4 & 5); CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks as we uphold MOH & WHO guidelines on COVID-19. Out of the cluster target of 6,040,483, RuCAPD targets 479,266 individuals = 79,878HH segregated by age and gender as well as considering persons with disability.
This will be achieved through distribution of agricultural inputs (assorted vegetable, crop & fishing kits), setting up 120 kitchen gardens setup at Nutrition facilities in areas of operation, treating and vaccinating 900,994 livestock. Capacity building & training in basic agronomy, fisheries, post harvest loss, refresher training for the CAHWs will also be undertaken in this intervention.
This is Dec 2021 inflation rose by 12.7 %, resulting from increase in the price of food and non-alcoholic beverages triggered by fuel shortages, supply chain disruptions linked to COVID-19 restrictions, and floods (S/2022/156). South Sudan hunger rate is alarming (GHI 2021). Pockets of populations in IPC 5. (UN News, GPH 27th Jan 2022). Exacerbated by constrained humanitarian access, conflict, effects of COVID-19, economic challenges and elevated food prices. All these in addition to severe flooding which has led to widespread displacement, damage to agricultural production, destruction of livelihoods as well as other compounded existing issues in many parts of the country. Already existing high levels of different vulnerabilities caused by complete asset depletion and impoverishment experienced since the start of the protracted crisis & compounded shocks experienced by South Sudan driven by insecurity, multiple population displacements, declined crop production, diseases and pests, the economic crisis. Constraints to humanitarian access experienced in 2021 and early 2022 really hampers the work. (HNO/HRP2022/ CFSAM DEC2021/ UN OCHA/ DG ECHO). On the same note, South Sudan is the 4th highest globally on the 2021 INFORM Index for Risk Management. Most affected and most vulnerable are Child HHs, persons living with disabilities, elderly, women-HHs. Youth are affected due to economic meltdown, unemployment, climate change. Youth are affected too. Distribution of assorted livelihood kits ,training in basic agronomy as well as livestock and fisheries services and refresher training for CAHWs will be carried out.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback, (positive and negative) from the population we serve is invaluable. It is one of the AAP pillars too. RuCAPD, has mechanisms in place to help with this. In case of any problems, RuCAPD will be able to address them on time thus save time and money. Because of the transparency and accountability brought about by the feedback ( and closing the loop), the people we serve are not only empowered but the relationship between RuCAPD and the community as well as other stake holders grow positively. Improved quality of programming will come about as RuCAPD addresses people’s feedback thus adapting interventions leading to greater impact. We strengthen our interventions based on learning and best practice from the feedback we receive from the community we serve. We have community communication agents at all the project sites. Their main function is to collect all feedback from the community and channel it to the concerned personnel for further communication and action as well as receiving feedback on the community concerns and closing the loop by reporting back to the community on the way forward/ action taken. Use of local language, chief’s barazas to deal with matters arising as well as use the public address systems to announce issues concerning the interventions. Currently, we have suggestion boxes at our distribution sites (managed by two officers -one male one female) to help people communicate their concerns. Both formal and informal feedback and complaints are duly registered and dealt with to ensure closing the loop. In case of issues beyond our intervention for example health matters, we refer to the relevant partners/institution and ensure necessary action has been taken.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

This project takes into consideration all the feedback from people ( men, women, boys and girls) with different vulnerabilities including the elderly, persons with disability. And using available referral pathways to close the loop on all feedback and complaints registered by the the different categories and vulnerability of the people we serve. Rural Community Action for Peace and Development clearly recognizes that more substantive efforts toward inclusion of persons with disabilities will need to continue concurrently in relation to how disability issues are not only considered but holistically included in all our projects / intervention designs, monitored and evaluated. This involves recognizing the need for a wider capacity building effort focused on disability inclusion not only throughout all our interventions. One of the selection criteria for registration as a vulnerable household is having or headed by someone living with disability and elderly persons and therefore will be eligible to receive inputs during the distribution. Both formal and informal feedback/ complaint collection strategy will be used by RuCAPD. Since our staff are within the community, the field visits and home visits to the beneficiaries is one of the avenues we use to obtain feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RuCAPD will ensure that all our staff get a copy of the PSEA policy and they undergo training to understand what it entails. On the same note, all staff will read and sign the Code of Conduct policy.We also ensure that we have PSEA focal points in all our areas of intervention throughout this project. Above all, RuCAPD will endeavor to reinforce community participation and do more awareness. During this project, we will run awareness sessions with boys, women, girls, and men in the communities we serve as well as with other stakeholders. It is our role to make the people we serve aware of their rights as well as their entitlements. E.g., they are not supposed to give any form of sexual favours, and such requests/ demand by anyone such favours is proscribed. The how and where to report SEA incidents confidentially and safely will be explained to the community. RuCAPD PSEA focal points will coordinate with other partners on the ground ( especially those fully engaged in Protection and GBV responses) to help close the loop in the feedback and complaints mechanisms concerning SEA. Our feedback mechanisms are aggregated by age, gender and diversity (AGD) and accessible to all including persons living with disabilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

RuCAPD aims to work with all the stakeholders especially the communities we serve to shape all the decisions and actions in relation to this intervention. Be it: challenges, problems, opportunities, outcomes as well as solutions. It is about having the communities actively participate and be consulted throughout the project cycles. During assessment and project initiation and indeed throughout the project cycle, it will be clearly communicated that their feedback or complaints are highly welcome. The setting up of feedback/complaint desks at the different distribution sites is also key. We will have phones and numbers dedicated to receiving feedback (positive/ negative). Our AAP staff will have suggestion boxes and will also gather feedback from the beneficiaries during the Throughout our intervention, RuCAPD is guided by the following community engagement principles: • Integrity: RuCAPD upholds integrity in our engagement with the community. We are transparent and clear in scope and purpose of all our interventions. • Inclusiveness: Community engagement is accessible and balanced therefore capturing a full range of values and perspectives in the community. • Dialogue: in our Community engagement, we promote dialogue as well as open genuine discussions supported by timely and accurate information; which we use to weigh the options we have, and develop common understanding on way forward. • Influence: Community engagement is always reflected in outcomes. The community is able to see and clearly understand the impact of their involvement in the intervention.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

RuCAPD interventions are geared towards inclusion of all gender, age and diversity- disabled and the chronically ill members of the community. This is key in helping avoid exclusion of women and girls in consultations and decision making. RuCAPD has a working PSEA policy which is upheld at all levels and the principles set forth in this policy apply: To all RuCAPD staff, whether expatriates or national; At all times, i.e. during and outside of office hours and during periods of leave. To all people or entities or stakeholders who have signed a contract with RuCAPD: consultants, day laborers, suppliers as well as partner organisations. On the same note, RuCAPD is part of the founding and steering committee of Nuswan Giddam which is a movement that seeks to change the narrative about the woman and girl of South Sudan. That South Sudanese women and girls are born to lead. Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection, for example) - RuCAPD is part of the Protection cluster, child protection sub cluster as well as the GBV sub cluster. and will also work hand in hand with the other protection partners in our areas of operation like Nile Hope, DRC, RI,UNHCR,AHC, HDC, CAFAD, among others to ensure that matters protection are amicably and ably dealt with. Beneficiary registration is done after the criteria has been clearly explained during mobilization and project awareness. This is based on the FAO selection criteria. After clearly agreeing and disagreeing with the different stakeholders including the local authorities, representatives of the different vulnerable groups (women, the chronically ill (e.g. HIV/AIDs) youth, people living with disability, the elderly) as well as religious leaders. Therefore all the selected beneficiaries will be registered accordingly. Contamination of targeted project areas with explosive hazards- Bearing in mind that some of our areas of intervention have faced the brunt of the protracted crisis, the risk of explosive hazards is real. We will continue working together with UNMASS and G4s and other partners in this field to ensure the safety of our staff as well as the community whom we serve. Where land is required for purposes such as group farms and demonstration plots, written consent from the owners if private or local authorities if communal will be sought before use of such lands. Beneficiaries risk to violence during registration and distribution will be minimized by having teams assisting women and men on separate desks. The distribution will also be restricted to between 8am and 5pm to allow the beneficiaries to reach home in time- for the women to perform their family roles and avoid GBV caused by delays at the distribution sites; the timing also helps in safeguarding against abduction ( of boys and girls)as these perpetrators fear daylight. The number of Distribution points will also be increased to make sure beneficiaries do not travel over long distances to access our services. This will also be helpful in this era of covid-19 as it goes a long way into making work flow faster.
