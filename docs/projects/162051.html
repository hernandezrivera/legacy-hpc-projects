Project Summary

The proposed projects would ensure fresh food supply to almost 13000 Rohingya refugees living in 5 camps in Ukhiya and Teknaj upazillas through voucher modality. The projects would also ensure livelihood support initiatives for the host communities living near Camp 24. Project would provide skill and business training for the host communities so that they can diversify their income sources.

Needs

Two years have passed since the new phase of the influx of Rohingya people began. Since August 2017, 740,000 new Rohingyas have escaped into Bangladesh raising the number to 1.2 million. Though the primary need for providing emergency support like food and shelter to this massive number of people has been successfully met, the local community and its environment are negatively affected.The host community, especially the poor households are seriously affected by this influx. In the beginning, the local community showed sympathy and helped them with essentials but soon after, their empathy started to wane due to the associated economic hardships. As the number of Rohingya in Bangladesh territory exceeded the capacity of the host communities of Teknaf and Ukhiya upazilas (UPZs) of Cox's Bazar, the challenges of inflated and imbalanced commodity prices, increased competition among locals and refugees for income generation opportunities and depleting forest/natural resources have emerged. As the markets do not have the capacity to supply products meeting the additional demands of the 1.2 million Rohingya, brokers and vendors from outside have entered the markets resulting in increased cost and prices of daily essentials. The host communities increasingly feel threatened as they perceive Rohingyas to stay for a longer period and being involved with different anti-social activities. The conflict situation has aggravated further as the cheap labour provided by the Rohingyas have decreased the daily wage rate significantly. In addition to this, problems related to environmental degradation due to unplanned & spontaneous settlement, haphazard disposal of solid and plastic waste—all have created a sense of tension among the host community members. The vast amount of forests which used to be common property resources for the host community has been lost. As the land has become totally barren, the chances of landslide and congestion of the water drainage systems have increased threats for both the host and camp settlements.The proposed projects aim to scale up the durable economic activities of the poor and vulnerable host communities, marketable technical skills for Rohingya women for their income generation, development of enterprises and entrepreneurs for increased employment opportunities and community-led social cohesion initiatives that will lead to peaceful coexistence and social justice, broader inclusion and sustainable development. The interventions will be strongly embedded in the communities ensuring ownership.
