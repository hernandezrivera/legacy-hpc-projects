Project Summary

Sudan was recently identified as 1 of 15 countries experiencing multiple and overlapping deprivations of severe wasting, severe food insecurity and severe food poverty. According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), about 11.7 million people are food insecure (IPC phase 3 and above). The prevalence of global acute malnutrition is 13.6%, with wide disparities across the states and localities (S3M report, 2018). Many states are affected by high levels of acute malnutrition well above international thresholds for an emergency. the project will contribute of 70% life-saving and 30% to resilience. Nutrition activities that support life-saving actitiveties 55% and nutrition activities contribute to resilience is 45%.. and health activities contribute to Life-saving activities is70% and health activities contribute to resilience is 30%..
Children under 5 years with SAM require urgent life-saving treatment. About 3.2 million children under 2 years and 3.8 million pregnant and lactating women face limited access to nutritious and affordable food to meet their nutritional needs.
Access to health services is limited in Sudan. Results from S3M II 2018 showed that only 65% of the population have access to a health facility within 30 minutes of travel and 81% have access to HFs within 1 hour of travel. In addition, only half of the people who visited Health facilities were attended to by any health worker. Additionally, 24% of children suffering from diarrhea and ARI were brought to a healthcare provider for treatment. According to the same data source, only 78% of children under one year have received their 3rd dose of the pentavalent vaccine, with a dropout rate of 14% from the first dose. Those who received their first measles dose were 73%, while those who received their second dose were 56%. The geographic variation of most of these indicators is large. For example, only 44.1% of localities have 80% of children less than 12 months that received the first dose of pentavalent vaccination. Similarly, only while 3.7% of localities have 80% of children less than 12 months that received third dose pentavalent vaccination.
NPO will address the high GAM rates through nutrition services at the 10 existing health facilities although the SMoH pipeline has always been insufficient to meet the large coverage area. The gap is still exists in staffing, community sensitization on community-based management of acute malnutrition and IYCF that justify continued funding by UNICEF. It is imperative for NPO to continue to deliver the integrated services of health and nutrition. for the nutrition services the project will provide with MUAC screening including the mother support groups to ensure all eligible children and PLWs are screened, acute malnourished children under five years of age (CU5) and PLWs (SAM) services are integrated within the health system referred to TSFP nutrition program and Children/PLWs needing consultation for other medical conditions are seen on the spot by medical staff working within the HFs (SAM) cases referred to SC.

1 - Needs

Acute and chronic malnutrition have been problems of public health significance in Sudan. Malnutrition are multi-sectoral leading to epidemics, current high food prices contributing to inadequate food intake and dietary diversity at household level. Inappropriate feeding practices and cultural norms affect children’s growth and development from an early age. The current humanitarian crisis increased the nutrition related protections risks especially among young children that might engage in child labour, early child marriage that increase risks of being malnourished. The overall nutrition sector people in need (PIN) remain at elevated levels, under-five children accounting for over 76% and 23% pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in need of life-saving treatment and preventive nutrition responses. Millions of people remain without access to even the most basic of health services, many of health care that providing nutrition services been out of services, the impact of health facilities, affected negatively on nutrition centres and the gap on lack of medical personnel, medical supplies, water and electricity in negatively affected on children health and pregnant women.Jabel Awlia locality classified as most vulnerable residents with high malnutrition among children and pregnant women, the current conflict increase the malnutrition, the needs to be address in this project divided in two parts first to response to current humanitarian issue by building health/nutrition cadres capacity and providing training for health and Nutrition community workers during the first quarter and maintaining the health facilities centres regular to provide quality services and provide vitamin A , running of health and nutrition PHC. The second quarter will shift to address the health and nutrition monitoring for under 5 children and pregnant women. Strengthen the institutional capacity for SAM treatment, the nutrition information system, health and nutrition Supply chain management, also to address the continuation delivery of immunization services and scale up of IMCI and iCCM strategies at both health facility and community levelsProvision of essential medicines supplies and equipment and monitoring to improve access to health and nutrition services in underserved communities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

 Service integration: This will be done through fixed sites (health facilities/OTPs), mobile clinics, and community levels to provide essential primary health care services. This aims to increase the coverage of high-impact preventive and curative health and nutrition interventions. Delivery of health & nutrition package: Through the Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) programme, an essential package of preventative and treatment interventions will be implemented. These include;o the early detection (screening) and treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) for children 6- 59 months; o immunization vitamin A supplementation; o Maternal, Infant and Young Child Feeding Nutrition (MIYCN) counselling for pregnant women and caregivers of children under 2; o Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI) o Iron and Folic Acid supplementation during pregnancy Provision immunization serviceso Session implementation in consultation with government partnerso Conduct home visits as part of defaulter tracingo Use established SOPs, protocols and existing national structures for maximum coverageo Vaccine stock management  Community engagement: Strong community mobilization will be done for early case finding and referral of children suffering from SAM through mother MUAC screening, community volunteers and mother support groups (MSG); counselling on recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding practices (through a network of MSGs; Integrated Community Case Management (ICCM) through Community Health Workers (CHW) as well as engagement and capacity building of health care workers and key community leaders.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The annual Participatory Assessment process promotes participation through structured dialogue on the specific protection risks and assistance issues that different groups face. Gender inequalities are persistent across Sudan. The protracted nature of the conflict, particularly in Darfur states continues to reinforce pre-existing gender inequalities, with women having lesser opportunities. The exits records worrying rates of gendered violence especially sexual violence. The efforts to prioritize gender across the humanitarian response are in line with the government commitment to gender equality, visible through the advancement of laws such as the Criminal Act and the National Civil Service Act. These laws are related to the criminalization of the FGM and the right of abortion in the case of rape. The recent amendment is indicative of the possibility of a Sudan with more gender-equal laws and a better future for girls and women in the country. Taking lessons from the 2020 response, in 2021 will make efforts to address the needs of women, girls, men and boys through strengthening capacity on preparedness and making investments on systems and institutions which support gender inclusion. In 2021 humanitarian operational will continue efforts to ensure that the standards and commitments of the IASC Gender Policy are reflected in the response. Gender will be part of regular advocacy and key messages.

The PSEA Network will establish clear linkages with key sectors and actors (including affected populations) to implement the Joint Framework of Action for PSEA to include: • Ongoing capacity building and development of PSEA network members and focal points on specific measures and resources to enhance awareness amongst staff/personnel on PSEA; create and maintain an environment which prevents SEA;foster a common understanding on reporting. • Advocacy for stronger management and coordination of PSEA within agencies including through: (a) Incorporating PSEA focal point roles and responsibilities into work plans and performance evaluations; (b) Establishing codes of conduct where they do not already exist; (c) PSEA trainings for staff/personnel on PSEA. • Work closely with the GBV and CP sub-sector/actors to ensure that support for victims of SEA is integrated into GBV response services and fully accessible to them. Protocols and guidance will be developed to outline how agencies respond to victims of SEA and referral system.
