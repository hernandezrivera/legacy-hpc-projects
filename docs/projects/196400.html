Project Summary

The aim of this project is to ensure equitable access and quality education to adolescent girls and boys and young adults who have not engaged ever with education or currently not engaged in study. This project will provide Inclusive Education and Psycho-Social Support (PSS) along with COVID-19 prevention and preparedness and Disaster Risk Reduction Strategies. FIVDB will adopt the Mynmar Curriculum (MC) and primers for the Rohingya community and FIVDB’s adolescent primers (aligned with the NCTB curriculum) for the host community. The courses and contents and teaching-learning methodology will be adapted in a way that, the adolescent learners find it relevant for themselves. The adaptation has already been started as FIVDB is implementing 110 both established and Community based Learning Facilities (CBLF) in the camps with AHP funding and our education team is working with the beneficiaries at the learning center level for adaptation. 2,400 Rohingya girls and boys will be enrolled in the learning centers in 3 targeted camps (Camp-1 W, 2E & 14), while 20 government or non-formal schools will be selected for rehabilitation from 5 targeted Union in the host communities. The participants' ages will be between 5-14 years. Among the learners 70% will be girls (in Camps-as enrollment is low of the feamale students) and 3% will be differently able person. There will be hand washing facility and hygiene kits for girls and boys separately in each learning center and school. Teachers will be recruited from the communities and will be trained by the FIVDB education team with support from other sector partners, as it is done currently. There will be close monitoring and supervision of the learning outcomes and regular assessment of the progress in learning.
There is a gender equality identified in access to education services,
specially on inclusion of Adolescent girls (age group, 5-14 years), this project will work to achieve Gender Euality. Different types of services will be provided to different groups according to the suitability of affected population segments.The teachers will be provided training on protection and inclusion, PSS and disaster risk reduction as they can ensure the participation of all learners including differently able persons and safety of the participants. Location for Learning Centres will be choosen given priority to safety issues of learners, Train teachers in first aid (including psychological first aid) and that first aid kits are available/equipped In every LC, proper drainage system, waste management system and separate washroom facilities for males and females will also be ensured. There will be a 7-member learning center management committee consisting of members from the participants, guardians and community members. At least 3 members of the committee will be female. This committee will be involved in learning center management as well as all kinds of protection activities. Psycho Social support will be ensured by training teachers , LC Management Committees and SMC members on Psychological First Aid (PFA), Stress Management and Active Listening and cascade these sessions to learners.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Despite significant progress during last few years in providing safe and systematic access to learning opportunities, there are still gaps in rohingya community in education sector as well as in host community. 27% of household reported a loss or diminished access to education as an impact of the COVID-19 outbreak (J-MSNA, Preliminary findings, 1 October 2020). More than 20 percent of Rohingya children and youth aged 3-24 years old still require access to education. 69 percent of Rohingya refugee households reported at least one 5-17 years old aged child not benefiting from any learning opportunities. Furthermore, 76% of the adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years old have access to educational or skills development activities . Impact of the lockdown for covid-19 on education is a general concern. There was consensus that the disruption of education poses a great concern and aside the loss of education, less regularly daily routine as a risk to children’s wellbeing of both rohingya and host community. There is a need to improve education technology to be used as a complementary solution for learning. According to J-MSNA preliminary findings September 2020, the main barriers for rohingya community to studying effectively are- Inability of other household members to support children; lack of money to pay for private tuition; support on remote education generally reported to have been limited across the surveyed camps; children needed to help in household. Main barriers for the host community in particular among poor families, inability of parents to support due to a lack of education and lack of money to pay for private tutor. Adolescent girls to be less likely to be sent back to school due to socio-cultural norms and Girls on the other hand may already be helping in the household and instances of child marriage. Children over the age of 10 reported to be less likely to be sent back due to a lack of appropriate learning opportunities. COVID-19 poses High rate of children drops - out and early child marriage. From our observation community people and parents are not engaged in education program in camps and host. There is lack of awareness about the importance of education of adolescents, that is why parents and community people should be engaged in the education program. In the 3 targeted camps (1 W, 2 E, 14) total gap are 18,870 individuals of 3-24 age groups. In the host community there are according to 2022 the target was 7674 approximately in 5-14 age groups and outreach is zero due to closure of the schools because of the COVID-19 outbreak (BlockWise_JRP-2023_EduSector_PIN_Target-Gap_Calculation_Final_12-Oct-2022). A noticeable fact is that gap between girls and boys are huge among the 6-18 age group. We have targeted 400 adolescent girls and boys (12-24 age olds) from each targeted camps. In the targeted host community 5 union would be selected (Holdiapalong, Jaliapalong, Palongkhali, Whykong and Hnila). for the school rehabilitation and 4 secondary registered schools would be selected from each union.

Protection will mainstream in this project through Accountability, meaningfull access, participation and empowerment.

3.2 - Gender

Friends In Village Development Bangladesh (FIVDB) is very conscious about Gender inclusion and mainstreaming in its all programs. In proposed Education program it will include 70% of Female learners (in Camps) and 50% in Host Community whereas targeted male is 30% (in Camps). In Learners identification, FIVDB will cautious about LGBT Community and if identified will include with other learners.

As the program will initiate Mynmar Curriculum (MC) in the camp, so it will incorporate the age group of 5-14 years boys and girls for regular Learning centres.
