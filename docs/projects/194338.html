Project Summary

South Sudan has undergone years of protracted conflicts from December 2013 and perennial floods for the past three years. These have resulted into massive population displacements in and outside the Country. The situation has further been exacerbated by outbreak of COVID-19 pandemic that struck the Country from the beginning of 2021. It is estimated that 8.2 million people, including refugees and Abyei are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of 2023 lean season (May-July). The main drivers being (1) covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks (such as floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or fear of violence); and (2) household-level idiosyncratic stressors (such as death of breadwinner, gender-based violence, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene, and lack of access to essential basic services) which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks. It is believed food insecurity is greatest when these shocks and stressors are compounded by the persistent high levels of vulnerability associated with the protracted crisis that has existed since December 2013.
Coalition for Humanity post distribution monitoring and other assessments point out to the dire situation which require urgent intervention across all the counties targeted by this project.
In line with HRP and Food security and livelihood cluster objectives, this project will focus on objective CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19, CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 and CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19. Through this project, Coalition for Humanity will target 50,000 HH (300,000 Individuals) to improve food security and livelihood across 15 counties. Segregated by gender
Men = 40,000
Women = 60,000
Gils = 100,000
Boys = 100,000
Coalition for Humanity envisages to implement the following activities integrating strong protection mainstreaming and prioritization of people with special needs including women and children.
1. Community mobilization and sensitization of households
2. Identification, registration verification and distribution of quality livelihood kits to targeted households.
3. Formation and training of lead farmer groups in every county on identification and reporting of early warning signs of pests, diseases and floods.
4. Training of Agricultural extension workers, per county on pest and crop disease control e.g. detection, prevention & eradication.
5. Provision of farm level support to farmers in every county on improved production/modern farming methods.
6. Provision of emergency livelihood support to farmers through distribution of crop kits, fishing kits and fast maturing vegetable seeds
7. Training community animal health workers (CAHW) on livestock diseases, prevention & treatment especially quarantine measures on zoonotic diseases e.g. RVF, Anthrax, Hemorrhagic septicemia (HS), black quarter among others that are common in these counties.
8. Mobilization and sensitization of communities and identification of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs)
9. Collection and distribution of Livestock Kits to the CAHWs and regular replenishment.
10. Conducting monitoring, surveillance and reporting of diseases in the target areas.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has undergone years of protracted conflicts from December 2013 and perennial floods for the past three years. These have resulted into massive population displacements in and outside the Country. The situation has further been exacerbated by outbreak of COVID-19 pandemic that struck the Country from the beginning of 2021. It is estimated that 8.2 Million people, including refugees and Abyei are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of 2023 lean season (May-July). The main drivers being (1) covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks (such as floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or fear of violence); and (2) household-level idiosyncratic stressors (such as death of breadwinner, gender-based violence, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene, and lack of access to essential basic services) which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks. It is believed food insecurity is greatest when these shocks and stressors are compounded by the persistent high levels of vulnerability associated with the protracted crisis that has existed since December 2013.Food insecurity is highest in Jonglei where 79% of the population will likely face severe acute food insecurity, followed by Unity (75%), Warrap (70%), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (66%), Lakes (61%) and Upper Nile (60%) with the highest levels of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) at the peak of the lean season 2023. Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted emergency coping strategies. The precarious situation will further be exacerbated by the country’s reductions in funding against increasing humanitarian needs in recent years which has forced humanitarian agencies to reduce entitlements and prioritize the most vulnerable households.The analysis of the stratified data revealed that displaced people are more likely to be severely food insecure (IPC 3 or worse) with 61.1% of total IDPs being severely food insecure. This is particularly significant in Western Bahr el Ghazal (69.9% of the total IDPs), Central Equatoria (67.3%), and Unity (61.2%) States. Coalition for Humanity has undertaken a number of assessments, post distribution monitoring in the selected areas and augmented its findings with available data which all point to food security gaps and other humanitarian support requiring urgent assistance in 2023.

3 - Project indicators

Coalition for Humanity has robust monitoring and evaluation framework that will be used to monitor and gauge the success of the project. The following indicators will specifically be measured to determine project results.1. # of individuals benefiting from livelihood kits, crop kits, fishing kits, and vegetable kits across counties. 2. # of farmers trained on climate resilient agriculture, and reporting of early warning signs of pests, diseases and floods. 3. # of Agricultural extension workers, trained per county on pest and crop disease control e.g. detection, prevention & eradication. 4. # of households receiving farm level inputs and support of farmers in production/modern farming methods.5. # of community animal health workers (CAHW) trained on livestock diseases, prevention & treatment especially quarantine measures on zoonotic diseases e.g. RVF, Anthrax, Hemorrhagic septicemia (HS) and black quarter among other diseases common in the counties. 6. # of individuals reached with awareness information on weather, climate change, pests’ diseases and proper farming methods and food storage 7. # of animals receiving livestock kits and vaccines across counties. 8. # of Post distribution monitoring and evaluation conducted and reported. 9. # of disease surveillance reports in the target areas

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Local staff are leading the process of collecting the feedback from the beneficiaries especially individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are illiterate, women and girls. Most of the meetings and consultations are delivered in local languages hence helping members to effectively contribute. We also encourage beneficiaries who have skills in different languages(Nuer, Dinka, Arabic) to provide it as they know it and if need be get our translators to interpret for us the content they shared. This helps in ensuring nobody is left behind in our project intervention.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the beginning before project role out, a number of inception meetings will be carried out. These consultative and engagement meetings with beneficiaries will help further explain the nature of support to the beneficiaries and obtain their feedback on how such support will be delivered to them. The inceptions and other consultative meetings will build on earlier engagements (pre project design engagements) done with members of the community while this proposal was being prepared. Setting up complaint mechanisms and referral channels during and after the project are some of the important tasks to be carried out. Effective community mobilization meetings and Focus group discussions, safety audits with different groups mostly women and girls will be done to seek their views on safety at distribution points.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Coalition for Humanity has Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy in place. All staff members including consultants and volunteers sign it before engagement. The organization protection focal points in the field locations organize standalone sessions on PSEA. PSEA discussions are also incorporated in meetings and trainings that are conducted from time to time. PSEA is therefore an ongoing process and is one of the important aspects of our project implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback gathered from the beneficiaries during inceptions meetings, monthly and complaints feedback boxes placed in strategic locations are collected, analyzed and the feedback used to improve and adapt the project implementation strategy. An action on feedback from the beneficiaries is incorporated into the project implementation matrix

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

There is a risk of external stakeholder interference, Coalition for Humanity will conduct a stakeholder engagement meeting at the start of the project to define objectives of the project, their role and responsibility. The meeting will spell out mechanisms on how beneficiaries are identified. Coalition for Humanity will hire non partisan enumerators to support registration and distribution process. There is a risk of minority, people with disability, women, girls and boys not participating effectively in the project. Coalition for Humanity will conduct focus group discussions targeting people with disability, boys, girls, women separately to make sure their needs and concerns are captured. Coalition for Humanity will ensure latrines constructed are segregated by gender and are disability friendly. Coalition for Humanity has a PSEA code of conduct, all field staff are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, Community leaders are also trained on PSEA to raise awareness in the community so that beneficiaries can know they have a right to humanitarian aid without their dignity being compromised. Beneficiaries will be provided with suggestion box, telephone contacts for feedback. They will also have an opportunity to participate in post distribution survey on beneficiary satisfaction. Beneficiaries will also be given an opportunity to channel complains through local authority.
