Project Summary

Heartland Alliance International (HAI)and the Center for Research and Protection of Women’s Rights in Syria (the Women’s Center) NES are pleased to offer DRL a collaborative partnership to implement the 12-month Atfal al Nahrain- أطفال النهرين Children of the Two Rivers: Enhanced Protection for Iraqi and Syrian Children project which will build on previous national and international efforts to address critical gaps in child protection in NES, including working with NES authorities to enforce positive provisions of existing law and policies, identify deficits, advocate for changes, and tangibly improve the quality of life for thousands of detained children. Four years after the defeat of the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL), thousands of Syrian and Iraqi children still reside in detention centers and returnee camps with inadequate protection and services and experience high rates of gender-based violence (GBV), and sexual abuse. Many still face trial for ISIL affiliation and some are held without charges. In NES, nearly 10,000 children and their mothers are entering their fifth year restricted to detention centers and the Al-Hol and Roj camps. Current protection systems do not fully adhere to internationally recognized best practices and frameworks, such as the Minimum Standard for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action, the Convention on the Rights of the Child, and the Optional Protocol on the Rights of the Child on the involvement of children in armed conflict, which prohibits the use of child soldiers and sets forth protection standards for children who are forcibly recruited to participate in armed conflict, whether as combatants, service providers or laborers. The civil administration in NES has neither the budget nor the technical capacity to adequately provide services or fully protect these children, and delays in repatriation of foreign children to Iraq and to Europe risk creation of a population of detained minors transitioning to adulthood under circumstances that will result in long-term functional deficits and potentially further radicalization. Mechanisms to support cross-border coordination and knowledge exchange on child protection in the MENA region are minimal.
HAI will work in the detention centers of Houri, Panorama - Guhwrein, and Alaya in NES. Training materials produced for this project will be made available to the NES administration and organizations accessing other displaced or detained children in camp settings, such as in Al Hol, as well as in non-camp settings, and Orkesh. HAI and partners will leverage decades of context-specific, culturally relevant, child protection expertise in the above detention centers and has designed a series of mutually reinforcing activities that contribute to Children of the Two Rivers’ Project Goal: At-risk children and their families at detention centers in NES (Houri, Panorama - Guhwrein, and Alaya) will have improved access to safeguarding and legal protections. HAI and partners designed the following Theory of Change (ToC) to maximize Children of the Two Rivers’ results: IF (1) legal and policy safeguards for detained children are enforced, (2) public awareness of the rights of children is enhanced, and (3) a cross-regional knowledge exchange between child protection stakeholders occurs, THEN at-risk institutionalized children in NES will be better protected. HAI will realize this ToC through the following three interrelated Strategic Objectives (SOs). SO 1: Legal and policy frameworks are improved and implemented to safeguard the rights of children and at-risk children. SO 2: The public is aware about the rights of children and holds government institutions tasked with upholding child rights to account. SO3: A cross-regional knowledge exchange supports child protection stakeholders to improve practices to protect children. HAI estimates that Children of the Two Rivers will reach 3,144 direct participants and 18,864 indirect participants.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

HAI Approach, Risks and MEL plan - Strategic Partnerships for Child Protection Advocacy :HAI and the Women's Center have established close ties with Houri and are poised to forge similar relationships with DC managements, Through engagement with the CAAC TF , HAI will further strengthen its commitment to this cause. - Inclusive Development, Youth Engagement, and Gender Equity :Adhering to global principles of DEIA, HAI and its partners vow to adopt an intersectional approach. Recognizing that certain communities face compounded challenges due to various aspects of their identities, including gender, ethnicity, religion, and disability. - Do No Harm Approach and Ethical Standards :Aligned with Sphere Standards, the IASC Guidelines, and the core principle of "Do No Harm," HAI ensures that its activities prioritize the safety and well-being of affected communities. In its provision of case management and psychosocial support, HAI adopts a survivor-centered approach, emphasizing confidentiality, safety, respect, and non-discrimination. Furthermore, the organization maintains a zero-tolerance policy towards SHEA and has implemented robust policies and procedures to prevent and respond to such incidents. - MEL plan: HAI implements robust (MEL) practices to ensure the effectiveness and impact of its initiatives. Data collection occurs monthly using ActivityInfo, a professional platform ensuring systematic, rigorous data collection, cleaning, analysis, and reporting. To enhance accuracy, a monthly verification process compares databases with means of verification and contacts a 10% stratified random sample of beneficiaries. Routine monitoring includes unannounced site visits by the MEL Team , enabling observation, interviews, and discussions to identify successes, lessons, and operational challenges. Additionally, HAI collects Most Significant Change (MSC) stories from beneficiaries to showcase the program's long-term impact, Through these MEL practices, HAI ensures accountability, learning, and continuous improvement in its humanitarian interventions. - Risks: HAI anticipates serious protection gaps for NES children in DCs , including risks of violence and sexual abuse. Mixing children of different ages in the same facilities poses a significant risk, with older adolescents potentially perpetrating violence against younger detainees , child seperations . Age separation is a key policy objective. Staff require training to assess incidents of sexual behavior, distinguish between abuse and experimentation, and respond appropriately. Detection and prevention of sexual abuse by staff are crucial but challenging due to cultural barriers. HAI aims to address these barriers by providing training and emphasizing the importance of controlling staff-initiated abuses , enhance staff capacity to recognize and report signs of abuse, ensuring a comprehensive response to sexual violence in detention facilities.
