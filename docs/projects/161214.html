Project Summary

The project will target a total of 19,000 IDPs, host communities, and returnees, and will focus on three main components; Protection analysis and advocacy: Nile Hope will focus on protection data collection and analysis, for the purpose of ensuring an in-depth understanding of the protection environment, allowing for appropriate prevention and response activities. In parallel, Nile Hope will ensure protection concerns are captured in protection information products, such as assessment reports, monitoring reports, and protection snapshots, allowing for appropriate and relevant advocacy with the clusters, humanitarian partners, and other main stakeholders. Nile Hope protection programming will ensure evidence-based interventions, at the individual and community levels, ensuring appropriate and principled action is taken to prevent and respond to protection risks. Protection risk prevention: Nile Hope will continue to work closely with communities, particularly existing community structures, and vulnerable groups and individuals, to identify protection risks, coping strategies, community, and individual intentions, and resilience among the affected population, to ensure preparedness and resilience of communities and at-risk individuals are enhanced. Nile Hope will work with community-based protection networks, facilitate focus group discussions, awareness raising, training on human rights and protection mainstreaming, aiming to prevent and mitigate identified protection risks. Protection response: Nile Hope provides timely, coordinated tailored and complimentary protection responses based on the identified protection needs. Protection will be central to all interventions which include service mapping, safe referrals, individual protection assistance for at-risk individuals, information sharing, awareness raising on fundamental rights, protection mainstreaming training to multi-sector teams alongside partners and key community stakeholders. Nile Hope will ensure that, protection is integrated with other sectors including shelter and non-food items (SNFIs), Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) and Health, Education, Nutrition and Peace building programming. Nile Hope will put focus on the centrality of protection, ensuring the safety and dignity of persons of communities affected by displacement are prioritized and a do-no-harm approach is mainstreamed during all aspects of the project cycle. Accountability to affected populations will be ensured through information dissemination activities to communities alongside beneficiary feedback sought during post-intervention monitoring. Nile Hope has got static presence in Akobo, being the headquarter of Nile Hope's operation, we have presence in the Leer, Maiwut, Nasir, Fangak and Pigi Counties. Project implementation will start as plan because of the active presence and good relationship with the the communities.Nile Hope will conduct ad-hoc assessments in the proposed project location, priority will be given to the highly affected locations by mainly floods and that is Nasir, Maiwut, Fangak, Pigi and Akobo for Refugees and returnees that comes from Ethiopia.Nile Hope considers duplication issues, we shall first conduct service mapping to know the number the service providers in the location and the different activities they carry out, we shall strategized our implementation plan to avoid duplication with other partner. Akobo is vast, we shall mainly focus in the Western and Central Akobo for project implementation.

1 - Needs

Internally displaced people’s access to services has eroded with insecurity and economic decline. Rape and other types of gender-based violence are pervasive but go largely unreported—the 1,324 cases reported in the first half of 2018 represent just a fraction of the aggressions faced mostly by women and girls, in a situation where undertaking daily survival tasks, such a collecting firewood and water, places them under threat.In spite of the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan, R-ARCSS, on the 12th September 2018, fighting and displacement have continued to affect the civilian population, and is an indication that political peace may not necessarily lead to an end of ethnic conflict. Displacement, caused by conflict and rights violations, continues to be one of the main protection concerns. Lack of access to food and basic services are some of the challenges IDPs face, while South Sudanese refugees in neighboring countries face harassment and different types of violations, sometimes leading them to return to South Sudan, in turn exposing them to additional protection risks. The South Sudanese population continues to be subject to different human rights violations, such as deliberate attacks, destruction of property and assets, forced displacement, conflict related SGBV, forced recruitment, restrictions on freedom of movement of civilians. Areas of the Upper Nile and Unity are affected by attacks, as well as Northern Jonglei. With presence in Upper Nile, Jonglei and Unity States, Nile Hope has been able to obtain a strong understanding of the protection environment. However, there is a need to continuously monitor and analyze the situation and needs of the population, to inform responses, and adhere to the ‘do no harm’ and protection principles. Nile Hope has identified the need to continuously mainstream protection into all humanitarian programming, and ensure protection principles are adhered to. There is also a high need to continuously advocate for improved access to services, particularly for PSNs, the most vulnerable, and displaced persons, focusing on remote locations. Nile Hope emphasizes the need to work closely with affected communities to identify needs and empower them to become more resilient, based on their protection risks. Nile Hope will therefore continue to have a mobile approach to its programming based on occurring needs and new shocks. Nile Hope also recognizes that displaced individuals are identifying solutions to their displacement situation; these solutions include local integration, return to their area of origin, or moving to a different location they deem safe. Men, women, boys, and girls may have different interpretations of their situation and make decisions based on which solution may seem best for them. It is important to provide assistance to IDPs and returnees based on their informed decisions with high regard for their safety and dignity.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To mitigate such risks several sector specific measures are recommended that Nile Hope will ensure that Protection risk assessments are conducted and protection mainstreaming is included throughout the program cycle, such as aligning to the protection sector recommended response packages for a harmonized response, ensuring the understanding and monitoring of the contextual environment is well known, up to date and integrated into the distribution processes, and applying needs based targeting / selection criteria. On mitigation aspects, Nile Hope will conduct ad-hoc need assessments in Akobo, Maiwut, Nasir and Leer Counties to examine the protection risks facing the communities. Nile Hope considers the following factors that might bar access; • Inclusion and exclusion errors leading to tensions in the communities • Shift of power dynamics within the households • Increasing exposure to exploitation by employers, clients, suppliers • Inciting backlash from family or community members when women start earning money • Increasing vulnerabilities of theft and violence due to greater assets and wealth • Reinforcing inequalities by continuing to limit choices to more vulnerable groups • Causing false expectations and dependence on short term surge of cash or assets • Increasing costs and drawing on resources, such as providing small livestock without fodder or veterinary care • Exacerbating tensions between host community and affected community Nile Hope’s integrated approach programming begins with an analysis of target beneficiaries to understand the barriers. Our analysis takes into account a protection issues and concerns to including internal and external shocks that are often the immediate barriers Nile Hope’s program design is consequently built on a model that is focused on sustainability by designing interventions that address the multi-dimensional (individual, relational, and structural) barriers to achieving gender equality.
