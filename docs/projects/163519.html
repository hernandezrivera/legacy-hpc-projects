Project Summary

The Anglophone crisis that started in 2016 as a result of marginalization of the English speaking North West and South West Regions of Cameroon by the majority French speaking has intensify for close to three years now and has witness increasing killings by both the separatist arm men and government forces on men and youths. This has lead to the affected and non affected families to flee the area and seek refuge in nebouring towns and villages in the French speaking Regions in Cameroon. The increase population especially women and children is largely driven by poverty.
Women and girls make up about 52% of the Cameroon population and the gross inequalities and inhuman conditions they endure are both as a result of the crisis. The situation of women in the Noun Division of the West Region of Cameroon is particularly dire and presents real concerns for their fair treatment, access to justice and overall human rights protection. Of the close to 8.000
people that are currently displaced from the North West Region to the Noun Division of the West Region close to 6,000 are women of reproductive age. Many of the displaced women are widows and heads of households with hardly any access to property, health care and education.
Girls and women who give birth at a young age between the ages of 12-45 participate in the domestic hard labor force, odd jobs for very poor payment just to earn a living. Some of the girls have already involved in prostitution as to survived the abject poverty they have been subjected to as a result of the crisis.
Women bear unequal brunt of the hardships occasioned by poverty and conflict as a result of Internal displacement.
A critical element of hardship emanates from the women’s increasing roles as providers of basic needs or amenities to the members of their households – which are extracted from the natural resources, i.e. land, water, vegetation etc. In these circumstances, women come under extreme pressures and violence under stressful conditions that exist in Noun Division.
Our proposed project is a result of wide and inclusive consultative meetings with all the stakeholders within the area who identified the needs for possible action.
Women’s gender roles have been stretched beyond traditional limits to meet the new domestic, social and economic needs of the family and local community.
The project is focusing on capacity building of the women who are socially and economically disadvantaged and internally displaced into the Noun Division of the West Region .
The critical failure in resolving the Anglophone crises have created gross poverty and unemployment levels among the Internally Displaced persons (IDP) especially women both in urban and rural communities in Noun of the West Region. These challenges amongst other issues have precipitated a vicious cycle of poverty and hopelessness from which many women and families find impossible to overcome.
The project objective is to increase the financial abilities of women to enable them purchase food for their families, Educate their children (girl child focus), Afford better health, clothing and shelter for their children and Reduce poverty and attain improved standards of living.
The project is to work with 6000 youths and women economically empower them by building their competencies and capacities to participate constructively in development processes through provision of training courses for income generating activities including Market gardening, cassava production and processing and thrift and loan revolving loan scheme for women.

Besoins / Needs

As the Anglophone crisis continues to deteriorate, more population are being displaced which has led to an increasing population in need of humanitarian assistance. According to the recently conducted Cadre Harmonise assessment (October 2019), an estimated nine hundred and sixty eight thousand people in the NWSW regions are identified to be food insecure and considered to worsen as current assistance provided are at small scale compared to the fast-increasing needs. The current displaced people in need identified under the physical and mental well-being humanitarian consequence in the West Region is significantly higher than the three hundred and thirty thousand from last year which is mainly due to the deteriorating security situation with weeks of imposed lockdowns, lack of access to farmlands, population displacements and locations being inaccessible. The most needs are in the North-West region while security situation remains fragile in both regions and the IDPs population group in the bushes and inaccessible areas remain the prioritized group of people to receive assistance. Because host families especially those in relatively safe areas have been receiving frequent influx due to displacements, the host community population are also becoming more vulnerable. Markets are either non-functional or overstretched in some areas leaving in-kind assistance as the option in these areas. The protracted Anglophone crises in the North West Region has highlighted unique challenges and opportunities facing humanitarian efforts suggesting a need for greater innovation to respond effectively to the evolving economic and protection challenges faced by displaced persons. Economic insecurity has increased the protection risk for the displaced as they seek income-generating opportunities, creating a need for a more integrated approach to livelihoods initiatives. In a community where 80% of the population depends on agriculture for livelihoods and food securities, the absence or shortage of agricultural seeds for gardening and food is a serious threat to livelihoods and food security for these vulnerable IDPs and their host communities.The activities that will be caried out to meet the needs include; Project activities include;1. Preliminary planning and sensitization meeting with all project stakeholders.2. Regrouping of 6000 IDP beneficiaries in koutaba and Foumbot.3. Structure beneficiary households into 90 Common Initiative groups4. Carry out trainings on market gardening, cassava production/ processing and marketing of products.5. Acquisition and supply of garden seeds, crop seeds and support small farm Equipments and materials6. Cary out training for leaders on group dynamics and leadership7. Encourage thrift and loan schemes amongst group members to promote social cohesion.8. Link beneficiary products to the public through open days and participation in Agricultural shows
