Project Summary

The project will focus to improve and strengthened access to integrated life-saving Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and response services to the IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States (BAY States). The minimum initial service, and comprehensive SRH services covering but not limited to antenatal care, postnatal care, support for delivery services, clinical management of rape, STI management and family planning services to women and girls in need will be strengthened through the supported health facilities, women and girls safe spaces and one stop service centers. In addition, the project will also strengthen the integration of MHPSS in SRH service provision and make sure that clear referral channels are available for those with the severe mental health problems. The survivors of sexual violence and women with obstetric emergencies who need tertiary-level care will also be benefited from the specialized referral support services of the project. UNFPA will also procure inter-agency emergency reproductive health kits, including clinical management of rape kits and other essential medical items to support the integrated SRH/GBV service delivery, as well as for the prepositioning to ensure immediate response are provided in the case of emergency, for example flood response. UNFPA will ensure the last mile distribution of the kits and medical commodities to the Health facilities and partners upon the identified needs.
The unpredictable situations of cholera outbreaks will also be considered in the response while also ensuring the continued provision of vital SRH services. In order to do so, UNFPA will provide capacity building trainings to different service providers and partners. The efforts of advocacy and leadership of the SRH sub-working group under the health sector will be strengthened to maximize the momentum of SRH response, including the preparedness for any emergency across BAY States. In addition, the clinical management of the rape task force (CMR TF) under SRH SWG will be strengthened and ensure the integration of SRH and GBV, to ensure the GBV survivors receive appropriate, timely health care services and mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS). The ultimate aim of the project is to ensure the continuation and strengthening of SRH/GBV services through the provision of reproductive health supplies, capacity building and enhancing coordination across the BAY States.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria has been ongoing for 15 years leading to the dire need for essential health and SRH services. The Northeast region of Nigeria has the highest maternal mortality in Nigeria according to a recent 2022 smart survey, estimated at 1849 per 100,000 live births, which is higher than the national average of 512 per 100,000 live births. According to the WHO/UNFPA report, a third of these mortalities can be averted by ensuring access to and using modern contraceptives. According to the recently released NDHS (2023-2024), the modern contraceptive prevalence among married women in Northeast is around 13% (9.5% for Borno State while Adamawa and Yobe is 20.4% and 9% respectively). Unmet need for family planning is the highest in Borno State with 26% while Adamaw and Yobe has more or less the same need at 24.5%, however, the total demand for family planing is constantly increasing. Due to the pre-existing inequality, traditional norms and harmful practices compounded by the humanitarian crisis, women and girls, including adolescents and people living with disabilities (PWID), are more disadvantaged by the events which leave them more vulnerable to be able to exercise their fundamental rights. In addition, natural disasters, such as devastating foods, have resulted in thousands of new displacements, and subsequent cholera and Diphtheria outbreak has placed an extra burden on the already weakened public health system. The Floods crisis, especially some LGAs in Borno, has exacerbated the vulnerabilities of people affected by the con icts and displacements. The living conditions in the displacement sites, such as overcrowding, insecurity, lack of privacy and inadequate/unsafe WASH facilities, lack of shelter, malnourishment and lack of health functioning facilities,have resulted in increased stress. Therefore, it is noticed that there is an increasing need for humanitarian assistance, including the MHPSS services in the North-East of Nigeria, where the people are already struggling to access quality health care services. Maternal mental health and distress during pregnancy and immediate postpartum have negative impacts for both mother and baby. WHO states that 1 in 5 women will experience a mental health condition during pregnancy or in the year after the delivery. The UNFPA assessment on MHPSS also reported the need to strengthen integration of MHPSS as part of SRH service provision, since many of the frontline health service providers are not aware nor trained on mental issues around SRH. Currently, the number of people in need of health assistance is 4.9 million, and 3.1 million will be targeted for 2025 according to the health sector. Among those in needs of health assistance, 1.2 million people are women and girls of reproductive age (15-49 years) and 588,000 adolescent girls (10-19 years) who are most in needs of SRH information and services. (projected by MISP Calculator). Additionally, 154,350 currently pregnant women will give birth in the next 12 months, who will require lifesaving support and referral services. The insufficient infrastructure, the gaps in medical commodities and staff capacities are the identified challenges of the healthcare system to meet the population's demand. The funding had been a significant challenge in 2024, primarily for the procurement of family planning related kits and commodities. As a result, some health facilities and humanitarian organizations had some gap in family planning related commodities, despite UNFPA has been trying to cover the gaps as much as possible.

2 - Activities

The proposal focuses on the following Activities.1. Procurements of Inter-Agency Sexual Reproductive Health Kits (IARH kits) to deliver them to the Health facilities in needs, within the BAY States. UNFPA will ensure that the distribution plan is prioritized on need bases in collaboration with key stakeholders and that the utilization is well monitored. SRH supplies will ensure the beneficiaries have access to timely health care services, which is crucial for women and girls to avoid the three delays in maternal mortality. 2. UNFPA Strengthening SRH technical working group coordination under the health sector to ensure the delivery and access to quality SRH and GBV services; This also includes hosting regular SRH / CMR Task Force Coordination meetings with all relevant stakeholders to facilitate coordinated action to ensure the implementation of the SRH services. In tandem with health/GBV/HIV coordination mechanisms, mapping and analysis of existing SRH services, supportive supervision, monitoring and mentoring local partners and government agencies implementing SRH and leading joint inter-agency assessment. UNFPA will prioritize Health system strengthening and ensure planning for comprehensive SRH for a more structural response phase for the humanitarian- development nexus.3. Training and capacity building of Health Care providers/organizations on Minimum Initial Ser Package (MISP), Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), Emergency maternal and neonatal Obstetric Care, Family Planning (FP), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) within BAY State prioritizing in the areas of return and deep field locations. 4. Provision of essential specialized GBV Integrated SRH services in IDP camp clinics, integrated health facilities, women and girls safe spaces, referral centers and mobile medical outreaches in hard-to-reach communities. Services include adolescent friendly SRH services, covering CMR, antenatal, deliver and postnatal care, management of obstetric complications, STI management and referral support. Provision of technical guidance on reinforcing the infection control measures and universal precautions as guided by MISP for SRH service provisions in the health facilities and the clinics to ensure the critical SRH life-saving SRH services are continued even in the context of the flood and cholera outbreak. 5. Community engagement and improved awareness of key SRH issues to create demand and promote accountability to the affected population. Promotional messages in local spoken languages will be developed and shared, ensuring feedback from the community members is considered. Behavioral change initiatives to mitigate harmful traditional practices and promote the acceptability of life-saving SRH services. UNFPA will continue to coordinate and work with the State Government in the BAY States and partners to prioritize the SRH needs of women and girls, including adolescent girls and boys, and Persons with Disabilities (PWD) for inclusivity , ensuring no one is left behind.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure that the concerns of women and girls are addressed from the beginning of the project, focus group discussions (FGDs) with them will be organized. Community mobilization and awareness activities will also ensure the participation of beneficiaries during the implementation of the project. Additionally, the project will also strengthen the integration of Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) coordination mechanisms to ensure that protection concerns are taken into account in the health programming. To prevent Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH), all community mobilizers, including volunteers, will be trained on the principles of PSEAH, and the community will be made aware of it.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Community meetings will be organized to assess the needs within the community and determine the most effective way to provide assistance, ensuring the utilization of existing community resources. Through the existing complaints and feedback mechanisms (toll-free line, email, SMS, etc.), beneficiaries or anyone who would like to complain about the project interventions can submit their feedback confidentially. All feedback and complaints received will be effectively handled using the established UNFPA and inter-agency complaints and feedback mechanisms. UNFPA will continue to ensure that the affected populations, including those speaking the marginalized languages, are informed about the project and services in a way and format that they understand. Additionally, post-distribution monitoring will be conducted to ensure that beneficiaries receive the most suitable items and assistance. This will help to guarantee that the assistance is tailored to the specific needs of the beneficiaries and that existing community resources are maximized.
