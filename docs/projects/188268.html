Project Summary

The crisis reached its 11th year in Syria. According to OCHA reports 2.2 million people living as IDPs in NW Syria who are mostly living in inadequate shelters and tents for years. As announced in HRP 2021, one of the most prominent problem in NW Syria is shelter issue. Living in tents and inadequate shelters increase burden on people who are already facing difficulties of being displaced.
IHH aims to target 1000 IDP HH in NW Syria with assistance to repair their unfinished shelters. People in NW Syria seeking alternatives to tents and building more dignified shelters which are designed as appropriate shelter alternative for more dignified life of IDPs with using local materials. The project aims to cover shelter needs of most vulnerable 1000 families in the region by using CFW and voucher modalities which is recently become more critical for Syrian context and local market.
The project begins with beneficiary selection process which focused on people of concern and female headed families primarily.
The second step is about HLP process which will be conducted by IHH in coordination with local communities and local authorities as a help to IDPs. HLP process will be conduct sensitively to protect beneficiaries against any kind of problem which violate the right of IDPs such as forced displacement, any handover of land and forcing rent payment for land.
Selected families firstly will be informed about their rights and limits (if there is) on land which shelters they are building. IHH will provide technical guidance including recommendations on materials and standards, some flexibilities in implementation and 3 kind of cash and voucher assistance according to beneficiaries current situation and needs.
The estimated cost of the finishing shelters is 500 dollar and can be totally established by benefiting local market. The houses designed as generally 48 m2 and includes some flexibility according to needs of families. The design process will be conducted by considering IHH's previous experiences (IHH has built 8000 dignified shelter in the field), protection standards, territorial climate conditions and IDP's needs. Based on our previous experience and feedbacks of beneficiaries coming through our compliant mechanism, establishing dignified shelters mitigate the risks of inadequate shelters and tents such as vulnerability to fire, flood and winter, also enable them to built their houses properly with guidance of our regular monitoring and technical visits.
Additionally, IHH will assist IDPs in North West Syria with shelter kit and essential non food items in emergency situations including but not limited with winterization, flood and fire. The target population is approximately 4000 HH across the NW Syria. The response will be implemented in coordination with SNFI cluster and emergency response standards will be considered.
The fund for this project secured partially to be implemented in 2022.
IHH is in coordination with SNFI cluster and other Emergency Response Working Groups to avoid any overlap and to share our field observations to contribute coordination and rapid response.
IHH will provide coal, mattress, carpet, blanket for winterization and shelter kit and kitchen set for the families who are affected by different crises and do not have sources to cover their needs. By our high access capacity at district and sub-district level through NW Syria, IHH will cover HHs in targeted areas with applying rapid need assessment right after the emergencies.

Accordingly, for emergency relief projects, when a security situation arises that threatens the safety of staff, the staff is redirected to a nearby, safer location until the area is safe enough to conduct the aid or distribution project. 11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

IHH depends on the security situation in the targeted areas will be stable during the project period due to the continuous agreement between the main actors in the area, Ihh in implementing the project will ensure the following important things:They do no harm should adhere throughout the project. The most vulnerable should be protected from violence and other human rights abuses. The risk matrix for this project: Risk1: Lack or loss of civil documentation of the people in the targeted community, this will lead to no receive the assistance. Likelihood: medium, impact: medium Mitigation: During the registration process, IHH in coordination with the local authorities will verify from the people who missed the documentation. Risk2: HLP issue in the targeted areas, During the Syrian crisis 57% of the owners of the houses and lands lost their documentation, this will lead to do not benet from the project. Likelihood: high, Impact: high Mitigation: IHH in coordination with the local authorities will prepare a declaration paper signed by the houses or lands' owners and approved by the local authorities. Risk3: Rental issue, the owners of the houses may be asking the families to pay house rent after the rehabilitation of the house. Likelihood: medium, Impact: high Mitigation: The local authorities in the project locations will ensure the sustainability of bene ting from the project for one year.Risk4:A new wave of Covid and increase the number of infected among IDPs in the camps
