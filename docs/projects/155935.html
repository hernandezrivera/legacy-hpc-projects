Project Summary

Violet aims to respond to the increasing needs in WASH activities by implementing the following activities:
1. supporting the water units and water networks by covering the water units personnel salaries, operation costs, maintenance, and rehabilitation to the water systems and water units in Al Ma'ra and Al Janudya.
2. Expanding the existing sewage system in kafrnbol through the construction of 3000 meters sewage network and link it with the existing sewage system.
3. Garbage collection and expanding the landfill in Salqin.
4. Constructing 160 latrines in A'aidoon camp replacing the old prefabricated and unfunctional latrines.
The project seeks to make a balance between males and females, girls and boys focusing on disabilities basic needs category. The project aims to meet the specific needs of women and men/ boys and girls by providing equal opportunities to access. Also, gender-sensitive WASH facilities and considering the needs of the disabled and children with special needs will be taken into account by installing ramps in front of the latrines and other WASH facilities. Moreover, sex will be taken into account when constructing the latrines where they will be sex-based separated, to ensure the privacy and dignity as well as the equal access of all project's beneficiaries.
The civil community will be engaged in the project design and implementation through signing MoUs to ensure the local participation and ownership of WASH services and facilities. Moreover, the project will contribute to increasing the awareness of the community concerning the hygiene and good WASH practices either through distributing awareness brochures or through conducting awareness and hygiene promotion campaigns.
Furthermore, in order to ensure the project's sustainability, Violet will rehabilitate WASH facilities (water units, sewage system, and latrines ..etc) and then these facilities will be handed over to the community at the end of the project to be operated by them. Moreover, the managerial skills and cost recovery training will be provided for the operators, local authorities, and community representatives so they can continue providing the services when the project ends
Additionally, Violet will distribute 2300 Hygiene kits for the newly displaced people who fled to the much safer areas in northern Idleb and northern Aleppo governorates

Needs

As consequence to the years-long conflict in Syria, access to safe water remains limited for a large part of the population; water quality assurance is not considered a priority despite the need to ensure the continues access to safe water. Despite small investment in water treatment systems and provision of water disinfectants the main WASH need across Syria remains access to sufficient quantities of safe water. Up to 55 percent of the households are relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs. Results of free chlorine (FRC) testing are even more concerning and indicates that countrywide only 40 percent of water received from networks have a sufficient level of FRC at the point of use (below both national drinking water and emergency standards of min. 0.2 mg/l). In Aleppo governorate, only around 16 percent of the population is served only via piped water, while the others are adopting alternative solutions such as water trucking. As for the garbage collection, at least 10 percent of garbage is left in public areas, potentially increasing leishmaniosis cases observed since the beginning of 2018 in some areas. The situation seems to be particularly dire for some areas with a high returns ratio, with 35 percent of returnees reporting leaving garbage in the public areas. In addition, some places used for garbage dumping are not prepared and managed well that could contribute to increasing vector breading. There is a significant need for leishmaniosis prevention activities including solid waste management, particularly in northwest and northeast Syria where the more serious form of the disease (Visceral Leishmaniosis) exists. IDPs living in IDP sites are amongst the most vulnerable groups in Syria. In the northwest the scale of displacements to IDP sites witnessed since the beginning of 2018, has contributed to a deterioration of already overused WASH facilities and increased the need to scale-up operations and enhance the focus around more sustainable solutions (connection to existing water networks and/or establishment of simplified water and sewer networks). . Sanitation needs are also considerably higher among displaced people living in informal settlements and camps compared to the rest of the population, with over-crowding, cleanliness and protection concerns linked with substandard conditions of sanitation facilities frequently reported. (Syria HNO 2019). According to the HRP, 2019, the efforts must be continues to improve the quality of sanitation facilities in the collective centers and in temporary transit camps aiming to reach SPHERE standards, both in terms of the quality of services and gender/protection considerations. Additionally, The HRP 2019 prioritized the WASH sector activities in IDP sites including camps, collective centers, transit centers and informal settlements, where the high concentration of people and rudimentary conditions increase people’s risk of the public health risk. As these last resort sites are hosting the most vulnerable part of the population it is unconditionally vital to sustain adequate and continuous support there.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Violet has a strong Safety and Security Plan in place which governs operational activities of Violet and staff movement. Risk (1): changing of control over the targeted areas might prevent access to project locations, Likelihood: low, Effect: Very High Mitigating Measure:Through our experience access could be restored within a few days so the project will continue. Also, Violet will sign an MOU with local councils and will ensure the local ownership of the project Risk (2): Airplane shelling,Likelihood: low, influence: Very High. Mitigation measures:The likelihood of WASH facilities being targeted is very low due to the stability of security situation in the targeted areas due to the Astana negotiations and the truce conducted between the conflicted parties, However, Violet will train the project staff on how to deal with security incidents including the shelling and being targeted by the airstrikes. Risk (3):Unequal distribution of services inadvertently empowers or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors.Likelihood: Medium, influence: high Mitigation measures:Violet will coordinate with community representatives and local councils to promote the equity and “do no harm principle”. Also, Violet will sign MOUs with the local councils and will ensure the community participation in the project design in order to provide quality services and ensure the equality of service distribution as well as ensuring local ownership of materials and WASH facilities Risk (4):The possibility of explosion and human casualty and asset destruction because of war remnants or Mismanagement of WASH materials and fuel.Likelihood: low, influence: High Mitigation:Violet will ensure the proper storage of fuel and WASH supplies in the proper conditions. Also, Violet will train the staff on how to deal with WASH supplies, fuel and war remnants.
