Project Summary

Triggered by displacement, conflict and food accessibility, food insecurity is high in Idleb Governorate with concerning trends of increased food-related negative coping strategies, particularly among displaced households. To meet the food security needs, NGO X proposes a Food Distribution project that will target 4,771 highly vulnerable households (28,785 individuals) in 34 town and villages in the sub-districts of Harim and Salqin (Harim district). The selected modality is food voucher distribution which has consistently been preferred by NGO X beneficiaries in distribution and post-distribution monitoring activities and has shown positive impacts on the local markets and vendors. The selected locations are currently facing a funding gap in NGO X’s larger Food Security Programme funded by Food For Peace (FFP), leaving many people in need without assistance.
The project will be implemented over a duration of four months starting January 2018 and will comprise three rounds of food voucher distribution to highly vulnerable displaced and host community households meeting the Food Security Programme beneficiary eligibility criteria. Priority will be given to displaced households, especially households headed by single or widowed women, unaccompanied minors, persons with disability and elderly persons. The most marginalised beneficiaries will be eligible for additional support (such as home delivery) as well as referral to other services identified through a service mapping exercise in the targeted locations. NGO X’s Social Workers will accompany distribution teams to identify and refer households / individuals in need of protection or other assistance that is beyond NGO X capacity. Indirect beneficiaries of this proposed project will be NGO X selected shopkeepers that will benefit from a more stable income throughout the life of the project, as well as improve their capacity to assess and meet local market demand.

Needs

The Mid-Year Review of the Food Security Cluster estimates some 67% of the total Idleb population falling under the People in Need category. This includes 37% of the population acutely food insecure and 30% of population at risk of food insecurity. Displacement, conflict and physical and financial access to food have been key drivers to food security in Idleb. According to recent data from REACH (August 2018), 38% of assessed communities in Idleb reported food to be a priority need. In more than half of the assessed communities (58%) households reported to have faced barriers to access food markets primarily due to safety and security. IDPs appear to heavily rely on humanitarian assistance as the primary source of their food and livelihoods. This explains the high percentage (63%) of assessed communities that resort to food-related negative coping mechanisms such as purchasing food on credit, reducing meal sizes, selling productive assets and skipping meals. For this proposed action, NGO X will target the Harim and Salqin sub-districts of the Harim district in the Idleb Governorate. A recent Vulnerability Assessment (July 2018) conducted by NGO X in Idleb has found some concerning trends related to food security. The surveys under this assessment were used to establish (1) food consumption scores; (2) food expenditure shares; and (3) livelihoods coping strategies indexes. While most groups were in in-line with the overall responses in terms of the percentage of respondents who were either ‘moderately food insecure’ or ‘severely food insecure’, these percentages were substantially higher for former NGO X beneficiaries (26% in comparison to the overall 21%). Respondents living in towns (rather than villages or cities) were most likely to be ‘food secure’, with 29% of respondents answering this way in comparison to 18% overall. Respondents from female-headed households and former NGO X beneficiaries were least likely to be fully food secure, with only 12% of respondents in each group answering this way, as compared to the 18% overall. Under the same assessment, the majority of respondents reported not having received aid over the past six months. Stratification of the data by vulnerability reveals that some more vulnerable households were more likely to have received aid than the overall population. IDPs seem to score higher on the vulnerability scale due to their lack of social network. The assessment showed that IDPs were less likely than the overall population to report receiving aid from family, friends, or the community; this is likely attributable to the fact that IDPs are likely to have fewer and weaker social connections with their host community and thus are less likely to receive help and support from these sources. Households headed by women, unaccompanied minors or people living with disability have consistently shown higher vulnerability, including in terms of food security, in this and across other recent assessments conducted by NGO X. NGO X has been implementing food voucher programming since 2014 in the targeted locations. Each cash-based programme was based on rapid market assessments to identify the most feasible and appropriate modality (In-kind or Voucher). The markets in Salqin and Harim were classified, in previous assessments, to be in good shape due to availability of goods, big number of shops for all type of items, linkages with several source markets, quality and pricing. NGO X will conduct new rapid market assessments to update the information shared above and check again on the market’s functionality. Specifically, the market assessment will look into aspects related to shops, capacity, competition, accessibility, prices and supply chain.

Implementation of activities

NGO X proposes a Food Distribution project that will target 4,771 highly vulnerable households (28,785 individuals) in 34 town and villages in the sub-districts of Harim and Salqin (Harim district). The selected modality is food voucher distribution which has consistently been preferred by NGO X beneficiaries in distribution and post-distribution monitoring activities and has shown positive impacts on the local markets and vendors. In the selected areas, NGO X has already been delivering food voucher distribution programming with support from FFP and other donors as part of its larger Food Security Programme since 2014 with a current caseload of 7,812 households, and NGO X has capacity to conduct regular market analysis and monitoring. The proposed activities, locations and beneficiary targets have been consulted with the Food Security Cluster and NGO X Syria field-based teams. Through the proposed action, NGO X will seek to target the most vulnerable households in need of immediate food assistance. The project will target areas with high food security needs. Currently the proposed geographical area in the sub-districts of Harim and Salqin are not covered by NGO X’s larger FFP-funded Food Security Programme. NGO X has undertaken extensive efforts to cover this gap. This proposed action will seek to meet the urgent food security needs of displaced and vulnerable host community households in these sub-districts thus improving the food security status of the targeted populations. NGO X will continue to identify funding to support the most vulnerable in need of food assistance throughout and at the end of this project.

Protection Risk Analysis

NGO X have designed this project with consideration to aspects related to gender-based violence, child protection, risk of armed recruitment and other concerns related to beneficiary and staff protection, including safety and security. Specific vulnerabilities are also taken into account in programme design and implementation. In NGO X’s routine needs assessments, as well as in NGO X’s Vulnerability Assessment carried out in mid-2018, households headed by single/widowed women, unaccompanied minors, elderly or persons with disability, as well as households caring a person with disability or elderly, households with pregnant and lactating women and households with children under five have shown to be more exposed to food insecurity as well as to protection risks. To mitigate these risks, NGO X have revised the organisation’s Food Distribution SOPs to include ‘home deliveries’ (door-to-door approach) for households facing security or protection risks or with restricted mobility. This also applies for voucher distributions. Additionally, NGO X have recruited four Social Workers (three women and one man) to be based in the field and who will accompany distribution and monitoring teams to identify the most vulnerable beneficiaries in need of assistance that may go beyond the project scope, NGO X’s mandate or capacity. Such beneficiaries are referred to services identified through a recently carried out service mapping exercise by NGO X with support from the Protection Cluster and these referred cases are then followed up on in line with the Protection Cluster guidance on referral and case management. Furthermore, in its programming NGO X are now working on establishing a Special Needs Fund for identified vulnerable households that may require additional material or service support to access aid. The tools for this fund are currently being finalised and will be rolled out to the field team during the life of this project. Finally, NGO X have identified field-based Safeguarding Focal Points who have been trained to identify and report on any safeguarding incidents (witnessed or reported to them by staff/beneficiaries) using an established Safeguarding Reporting Mechanism linked with NGO X Syria’s Community Complaints and Response Mechanism. This allows beneficiaries to report on any misconduct by NGO X or other project stakeholder staff that affect beneficiary protection.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, supported by continued adherence to NGO X's acceptance strategy and the JOPs; - Security conditions remain sufficiently stable to allow NGO X and partner staff to carry out distributions and monitoring activities; - Food price inflation does not outpace overall inflation and household economic gains; - Food inflation and currency exchange rates remain relatively stable and predictable;- Cash transfer channels remain open and sufficient to handle the volume of payments; - Local markets continue to function with an adequate supply and diversity of food items. Risks:- Continued military offensives by the Government of Russia and inter-factional fighting, including the targeting of towns and villages in NGO X’s area of operation (AoO) from the air and other conflict exposure presents the risk of death or injury to NGO X staff; damage to or destruction of NGO X premises or assets; reduced access to programme areas for NGO X staff / beneficiaries; - The cash modality in this proposed action poses risks of aid diversion whereby sanctioned groups may attempt to interfere and influence programme implementation (e.g. beneficiary selection, access to shop, appropriation of vouchers, etc.) Mitigation:- Safety & Security Evacuation Plans and SOPs in place, and an experienced security team will continue to monitor trends and share information to enable rapid and appropriate changes to implementation plans;- Ongoing communication with actors operational across NGO X's AoO, and continued adherence to Acceptance Strategies built on community and stakeholder understanding of NGO X's programming; - NGO X has a Complaints and Response Mechanism in place which offers a myriad of channels to programme participants to give feedback and lodge complaints on any irregularities faced or witnessed in NGO X’s programme. This mechanism serves as an early warning system to identify potential aid diversion incidents and initiate further investigation. NGO X’s Whistleblower mechanism allows stakeholders and staff to report on any issues that may indicate to potential aid diversion, mismanagement or fraud. - NGO X will carry out 100% verification of beneficiaries before commencing food distribution. If there is any interference or lack of co-operation from LCs, NGO X reserves the right to halt operations in affected villages.- NGO X is currently revising its Food Distribution SoP to introduce validation of signatures at the distributions. - NGO X’s Markets team will monitor shop-keepers and oversee and document the process of voucher redemption to ensure that vouchers are used for their original purpose.- NGO X plan its field activities based on latest updates on the security & operational environment.
