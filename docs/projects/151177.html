Project Summary

The livelihood assistance project has been designed to tackle the structural drivers of Gender based violence through a mix of livelihood interventions. Socio-economic vulnerability posed by conflict and insecurity often becomes breeding ground for gender based violence. Adeso will build on its work with communities who have benefited from socio-economic projects in Puntland state, such as working with community cooperatives on skills development, organizational/cooperative management - financial support- market linkages; vocational training for youth, cash for work and cash grants for vulnerable and at risk groups. The project will also be linked to the Adeso SHF funded emergency WASH and food security and nutrition projects in North Somalia.
As part of prevention measures Adeso will mobilize IDPs, returnees and marginalized groups into organized group savings and loan groups through interventions ranging from giving predictable unconditional cash transfers to vulnerable women; providing livelihoods inputs (farm tools, seeds, business startup kits); cash grants for business and cash for work in rehabilitation of community productive assets such as water structures and roads. As part of GBV response Adeso will work with the protection cluster members for referral of GBV survivors who require economic recovery. The GBV survivors will be integrated into the cash transfer program and provided with livelihood inputs and training on business skills to enable them to stabilize and support their families. For girls in school Adeso will liaise with the education cluster to ensure economic support of their families and smooth return to school and economic recovery process.

Needs

According to the Somalia protection cluster 2018 mid year review, incidents of GBV continued to be reported, predominantly in IDP sites throughout the country. More than 76% of recorded GBV survivors were reported to be from IDP communities. GBV, particularly sexual violence, is a key protection concern for women and girls in Somalia. Capacity building activities enabled the GBV service providers to provide time critical, life-saving comprehensive assistance to GBV survivors including the drought affected populations, IDPs and other vulnerable persons. GBV services reached a total of 19,371 individuals (3,888 girls, 1,650 boys, 12,426 women and 1,407 men). GBV response services include lifesaving medical assistance including the post-rape treatment, as well as temporary protection accommodation for GBV survivors, legal, psychosocial, material and livelihood assistance. However, socio-economic related support to GBV survivors is missing in the referral mechanism where Adeso seeks to compliment, based on its already existing programs in Somalia and Somaliland. In an effort to mitigate GBV, the only preventative activities undertaken within the humanitarian sphere have been the provision of street lighting, fuel efficient stoves and lockable doors in IDP settlements. While such interventions are important, they focus on contributing factors to GBV but not on the cause of GBV. The latter is evidenced by lack of support for the vulnerable population through improved livelihood assistance as a form of protection from gender based violence and stabilization of the survivors.The number of organizations providing livelihood support to survivors of GBV is extremely low. This is a critical gap as survivors are often stigmatized and treated as social outcasts who are required to fend for themselves and their children. Many survivors are therefore in an extremely marginalized position following the incident which puts them at high risk of further victimization.

Activities

- Gender analysis of the socio economic context that will be suitable and viable in prevention of GBV and socio-economic support of GBV survivors.- Work with the Somalia protection clusters to identify vulnerable women and female survivors of GBV.- Linking women GBV survivors and households at risk of GBV with existing Self Help Groups such as cooperatives and farmers’ associations.- Capacity building through business and marketing skills trainings, mentoring and coaching.- Providing cash transfers to vulnerable individuals and groups at risk including GBV survivors.- Community sensitization and mobilization campaigns to enhance knowledge of the communities on harms related to GBV including sexual violence, economic violence, Female Genital Mutilation/Cutting (FGM/C) and child marriage, and information on the available GBV services for timely service provision. - Commemoration of world days related to GBV information sharing like: day of the African child, 16 days activism against violence on women and girls, international human rights day, International Women’s Day and International Day of Zero Tolerance for FGM.- Offer training and business opportunities for vulnerable women that are also suitable for persons with disabilities and older persons – these groups are often excluded from such projects and find it difficult to source funds or other inputs. - Ensure that marginalised and disenfranchised groups have equal and fair access to livelihood(grants for business)/agricultural assets (eg. land, tools, seeds, fertilisers).- Provide back to school learning opportunities or technical, vocational training for school drop outs.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This project is intended to provide socio-economic support for vulnerable women at risk of GBV and GBV survivors. The linkages established with cooperatives and business enterprise groups will enable the project beneficiaries work to continue in an effective and sustainable way after its withdrawal. Such sustainability will be determined by: scalability of the interventions; extent to which talent is identified and incubated; and the extent to which the interventions strengthen the local capacities. In this project, we envisage sustainability being achieved through the following ways: 1) addressing the immediate needs of the GBV survivors through cash transfer and linkage to groups savings and loans groups; 2) facilitating training and enhancing business skills, finance and business expertise; The project will address the factors hindering access to socio-economic benefits for GBV survivors, vulnerable and marginalised groups through gender and conflict analysis that will enable us to identify gender and power dynamics in the different locations of the project. The process of socio –economic empowerment will enable incubation of ideas, facilitate access to skills, financing, business expertise and create market linkages. The project will work closely with the Government ministries of women, labour and the private sectors including the Chamber of Commerce, Industry and Agriculture, local authorities and actors in the productive sectors. The project will also work with the local government, as they are best placed to provide enabling environment for the businesses. The project activities will be implemented in consultation with and delivered in the name of the government to ensure its legitimacy.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Adeso will use a rights-based approach to analyse inequalities and injustices, and to develop policies, programmes and activities in all areas of work focusing on process and outcome to redress these obstacles to the enjoyment of human rights. These will ensure identification of rights-holders (i.e. women, girls, boys and men) and their entitlements on the one hand, and corresponding duty-bearers (i.e. principally the State and its agents; however, there are duty-bearers at various levels of society, including in the household and community, and at the national and international levels) and their obligations on the other hand, and the project will seek to strengthen the capacities of rights-holders to make their claims and of duty-bearers to satisfy these claims. ADESO will also emphasize pursuing accountability for violations through staff training and signing of prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policy as commitment. ADESO has an accountability system that enables the community, beneficiaries and other stakeholders to launch any complaint anonymously without fear of being victimized. The complaints are addressed immediately and referrals facilitated where Adeso is unable to provide the kind of support required. Given the inequalities and discrimination that women and girls face, their participation and empowerment are crucial to making real and sustainable improvements. The project will ensure capacity building and in partnership with rights-holders and duty-bearers by developing policies and programmes to build the capacity of rights-holders to claim their rights and duty bearers to meet their obligations, with attention to marginalized and vulnerable groups.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

- Adeso will facilitate a gender and conflict analysis to identify potential barriers to access and utilization of socio-economic benefits for vulnerable GBV at risk individuals, households, groups and GBV survivors. The gender and conflict analysis will also ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination and exclusion of GBV survivors and marginalized groups.- ADESO will collect and use data disaggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming.- ADESO will adhere to the highest ethical standards of confidentiality while handling GBV survivors using a survivor centered approach to provision of services.- ADESO will communicate information through various means to reach the broader community and to account for different literacy levels (eg. door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms).

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

By addressing socio-economic challenges of the most at risk individuals, households and groups to gender based violence as well as GBV survivors, these improves their living standards through improved productivity and access to income, improved access to finance provides incentives for stable conditions by creating opportunities for youth, women and men making them less likely to engage in violent activities. Access to livelihood opportunities for GBV survivors and vulnerable populations at risk of GBV offers a source of income and increased employment and provide goods or services that address public needs, both of which address elements of stability and peacebuilding. Access to finance will play a catalytic role in efficient allocation of resources, thereby contributing to trade, investment and economic growth. Therefore, strategic investments can address the drivers of conflict through employment and provision of critical goods and services to the people which in turn are catalytic in nature to drive growth which provides revenue to the government. Well selected investments establish partnerships that support commercially viable enterprise that address the social contract, builds coalitions, fosters trust and local ownership of solutions, all of which contributes to peace and stability in Somali society. It must be noted that growing small enterprises is vital for the reconstruction of Somalia. These enterprises have contributed and sustained Somalia’s populations for more than 25 years without proper government policies and regulations in place. SMEs have also been responsible for employment opportunities for the budgeoning mass of youth as well as generating revenue for the government.
