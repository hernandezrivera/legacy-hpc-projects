Project Summary

The COVID-19 pandemic and the worsening economic situation has stretched and disrupted the already weak and fragile health system in Syria, thus affecting the availability and quality of health services across the country, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population. SC in coordination with Health Sector Partners, will aim to tackle and mitigate health service delivery challenges including resource limitations and implementation capacities by capacitating local partners and utilizing community engagement and partnerships approaches for an inclusive, whole-of-society approach to health system recovery, inter-sectoral collaboration and integrated programming.
The project will work to reduce maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality through provision of ANC, safe delivery, early initiation of breastfeeding, and PNC. Services will be provided through the reproductive health units/clinics within the supported nutrition centres. Furthermore, the following RH, ANC/PNC services will be will be provided through two fixed and one mobile clinics within IDP camps and hard-to-reach areas, and with the support of community health workers. These mobile units will provide the full spectrum of recommended community level services within the essential service health package and the MISP. This would include:
- Antenatal iron and folic acid supplementation
- Prescription for antenatal calcium supplementation
- Tetanus toxoid (in coordination with vaccination team), women will be referred to the nearest vaccination team after assessing their tetanus vaccination status.
- Basic blood tests (RH group, blood glucose) (available in the mobile clinics)
- PSS services for all PLWs;
- Identification and referral for specialized care for all persons with disability.
To address the challenges to follow-up visits, community health workers (CHWs) will be trained to conduct basic ANC and PNC visits at household level and/or virtually, and to support with referrals and transportation. In light of COVID-19, new protocols and operational guidelines have been adopted for the clinics including screening and triage zones and limiting waiting room areas to 3-4 people and ensuring physical distancing between health staff and beneficiaries. In response to COVID-19, specific actions will be ensured to protect healthcare workers by providing stress and coping resources, and timely logistics to maintain sufficient levels of personal protective equipment (PPE). Safe and quality inclusive health services also require WASH interventions in 3 health facilities, including medical waste management. SC will also aim at increasing activities that improve IPC in health care facilities by including WASH response (e.g handwashing facilities, and medical waste management) within the supported health facilities and communities along with sterilization and proper storage of medical supplies.
SC will also provide EPI services to reduce morbidity and mortality due to vaccine-preventable diseases in North West Syria. All EPI activities will be implemented in coordination with the Syria Immunization Group and our local implementation partners. SC will aim at 95% vaccination coverage among children 6-59 months of age by restarting the routine vaccination program (DPT3). Recent SC survey findings indicate BCG=79.8%; Penta1=91.9%; OPV1=92%; Penta3=79.6%; OPV3= 79.5; MHR2= 72.3.
Through the 7 EPI centers and 7 additional RH centers, SC will aim to reach PLWs, Women of reproductive age, Children between 6-59 months and Persons with Disabilities (PwD). SC will also aim to integrate MPPSS, MIYCN (Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition) and CMAM (Community Management of Acute Malnutrition) Services with the RH services in order to provide psychosocial support to all PLWs and address the challenges of early initiation of breastfeeding and skin-to-skin contact (First Golden Hour of Delivery), across all supported EPI and RH centres.

3 - Needs

15.3 million people are in need of health services in 2023, a 3.2 million increase from 2022 (HNO 2023). Youth and young persons, which comprise 1.98 million of the health sector PiN, are also reporting increasing stress and mental health concerns (MSNA – WoS Health Sector 2022). Within the health sector PiN, 2.7 million persons are estimated to have a disability which places them at greater risk for exclusion from health services. According to the 2022 MSNA, HHs affected by disability were most likely to report living more than 1-hr from a health facility, paying for health care, non-availability of needed services, and lack of access at health facility for persons with disability or mobility challenges. The governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Hama, Ar-Raqqa, DeZ, Aleppo and Rural Damascus have experienced greater barriers and service non-availability in comparison with other areas of Syria (HNO 2023). SC will primarily target Aleppo (and Idleb) governorates. SC provides comprehensive health response those subjected to continuous displacements, crowded and overburdened areas, and informal camp-like settings in NWS. These vulnerable populations (PLWs, PwDs including people living with mental disabilities, Adolescents and Older people) living in hard-to-reach areas (High PIN Severity) are at increased risk of poor health outcomes due to shortages in water and hygiene supplies, risk of communicable disease and likelihood of overcrowding and long waiting times at health facilities.

4 - Implementation of activities

The health response in NWS will aim to provide comprehensive RSH across 3 health clinics, besides, Integrated RH and EPI services across the 7 EPI centers. RH services include the BEmONC package: ANC, PNC, vaginal/normal delivery, family planning, basic blood tests, and referral to the next level of care. The CHW components are linked to these centers to ensure access to health services and deliver awareness-raining messages. These activities are implemented in partnership with local partners. M&E is mainstreamed within all projects, where data collection tools are designed to collect the data required (incl. registration forms, CHWs visit tracker, patient files, recoding forms).Data is collected on a monthly basis to the health sector through sector 4Ws. When possible, SC conducts third party monitoring to evaluate the progress and the project’s relevance to the population’s needs, also to ensure beneficiaries have meaningful access to two-way complaint and feedback mechanisms. SC and the partners will coordinate with Health Cluster to avoid overlapping and duplication in efforts. This also enables to have referral pathways in place. All staff will be trained on recommended health modules of Nutrition (MIYCN and CMAM), GBV, MHPSS, WASH in HFs, waste management and IPC trainings for ensuring integration and referrals for specialized care (incl. service mapping, information sharing on availability, coordination between providers, supporting ambulance services).

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks: Women and girls not able to access services or participate in income generating activities due to sexual harassment; exploitation and abuse by aid workers; female heads of HHs and adolescent girls in particular risk of sexual exploitation in exchange to services (Voices from Syria, 2022); risk of not reporting SEA for fear of retaliation; lack of awareness on reporting channels; stigma and shame and fear of losing on the services making it difficult for SC to hold staff/partners involved accountable. Mitigations: SC has a Child Safe Guarding policy which includes PSEA. To mitigate the risk of PSEA during the assistance delivery, the following will be included: mandatory training and regular refreshers on Child Safeguarding, Safe programming and PSEA policy to SC and partner staff; working closely with the WoS PSEA TF to strengthen feedback, reporting and response mechanisms for SEA; conducting community awareness and sensitization sessions on PSEA with emphasis on reporting and ensuring safety of survivors through referrals; regular participatory safety audit to identify protection and safety concerns arising as a result of project implementation and developing action plans to mitigate identified risks collaboratively with the community; including PSEA messages and topics in all IEC materials and training sessions conducted under the project; ensuring that centrality of protection is embedded in all projects to mitigate risks and prevent potential harm.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Unstable security situation: SC regularly monitors the local context, and SC employs security focal points. Context-specific Safety and Security Management Plans are regularly reviewed and updated. Money transfer issues and currency fluctuations (devaluation of SYP): SC mitigates this risk by using multiple money transfer channels and agents with strong reputations to deliver funds in almost all circumstances inside Syria. All SC agreements are denominated in donor currency. Risks due to the disease outbreaks (e.g. cholera): The situation may result in risks to beneficiaries due to lack of access and spread of infection amidst beneficiaries and health staff members. Relevant program adaptations will be developed and health sector SoPs implemented. Fraud management: SC staff complete mandatory trainings, incl. Fraud, Bribery, Corruption and Aid Diversion, and Financing of Terrorism. Financial, and supply chain procedures eliminate the risk through multiple vetting processes and checks, including partners. Weak logistics capacity: Field staff have received technical and systems trainings on processes and management and contingency plans are in place in case of loss of access. SC signed framework agreements with local suppliers in Turkey to be able to deliver aid across Syria. Loss of access due to the non-renewal of the XB resolution: SC coordinates closely with UN agencies and the donor community on handover to (I)NGOs for uninterrupted health service delivery.
