Project Summary

Early childhood that lasts till the age of 8 years is the best age for holistic development of every human being. It has been proven through scientific research that most of the brain development in the form of building of neurons takes place during these years which then shapes the personality and ability of the child for the rest of his or her life.
The current project will be developed in all 12 refugee camps of Amnabak, Iridimi, Touloum, Mile, Kounoungou, Farchana, Breidjing, Treguine, Gaga, Goz Amir, Djabal, Ourecasoni and the site of Kerfi. Currently, the community in all the four camps of Farchana takes care of the incentives of the pre-primary education. Hence, it is not included in the budget. We hope to replicate the same model in the remaining camps as well in order to reduce the dependence on aid.
As a child, every individual is capable of developing his or her abilities, naturally by active use of sensory organs. Development of a child is not only physical but emotional, mental and social too. Ideally everyone should be subjected to an environment that provides ample opportunities to explore and build ones abilities around it and thus nurture a balanced growth of all these aspects. Unfortunately, in Chad everyone is not privileged to have the same in their social or family context, especially the kids in the refugee camps. Hence, the pre-primary centers and schools can fill in the gap by providing a nurturing environment therein helping build a strong foundation of life for the refugee children. This alone is sufficient to emphasize the importance of early childhood education (ECE). When one thinks about designing a curriculum for education of this age group, it is important that it should be designed by considering that the brain is an organ of learning and anything that prohibits the brain from performing this should be avoided.
Based on modern theories of education and research on brain, educationists have come up with some elements that describe what should be an ideal environment of ECE. Some of the key elements are as follows:
1. Learning through activities
2. Experience based learning
3. Learning in a Stress free & Fear free environment
4. Exposure to age appropriate experiences
5. Learning by use of educational materials
6. Multi-grade multilevel learning
Studies by Nobel Prize winner James Heckman’s have shown that high-quality public preschool for 3- and 4-year-olds can more than pay for itself over the long term, as low-income children who attend are more likely to live productive lives. His research also shows that the return on investment is even higher for high-quality programs that care for low-income children from infancy to age 5. Children in such zero-to-five programs are more likely to graduate from high school, less likely to be incarcerated than their counterparts who stayed home or enrolled in low-quality programs, had higher IQs and were healthier during the course of their lives.
The pre-primary centers in the refugee camps need to take into consideration these aspects of ECE. It has a great potential for bringing about change for people working in the field of education. As a part of the plan, the current project envisages working on the educational inputs to be provided in these
centers, in addition, with rehabilitation of the centers where necessary and teacher training for the pre-primary teachers.

Needs

Early Childhood Education is underdeveloped in Chad even though the creation of the first framework for early childhood dates back to 1956. The situation in the refugee camps is even more difficult because of a variety of socio-economic problems. Despite the history and importance of ECE, it seems that very little results are achieved so far. Additionally, having a robust pre-primary education programme is indispensable for preparing the children for the primary, secondary and tertiary education. The sector faces several challenges that hinder its development. The three main challenges are: • Limited access and institutional management of preschool structures;• Limited didactic programs and materials;• Lack of qualified pre-school teachersAs per the recent SIGE data suggests, out of 34276 children aged 3-5, only 44% (15232) had access to pre-primary schools in all 12 camps and a site in the Eastern Chad in total 118 pre-primary centres. However, almost all of these centres lacked sufficient supplies for making a hospitable environment for the pre-primary education. Plus, the teacher-student ratio is extremely high in these centres (1 teacher per 66 students). Today there are total 257 pre-primary school teachers (251 female teachers, 6 male teachers), out of which only 24 (all female teachers) have official diplomas. Due to budget cuts, the number and frequency of teacher training programmes for pre-primary teachers have reduced considerably. Earlier, they used to take place in all twelve camps, however today they are limited to only four camps of Farchana.

Activities or outputs

The quality of education depends on the interactions in the classroom. The basic objective of the proposal shall be to Objective 1: Create a strong base for pre-primary education in the camps: Activities: • Organize awareness campaigns for discussing the ever-lasting impact of a proper pre-primary education on children’s life in general • Encourage the community participation in ensuring the functioning of the pre-primary centers in the camps Objective 2: Provide extensive teacher training to pre-primary teachers: Activities: Since teachers are the basis of interaction in the classrooms, through the training they will: • Understand the different terms and concepts in education• Understand the psychological and cognitive development of the child• Strengthen their own theoretical and philosophical knowledge • Practice different methods for effective curriculum transactions • Understand the various theories and philosophies behind these methods • Identify the achievement levels of the child with ongoing evaluations apply the method appropriate evaluation system Objective 3: Improve the conditions of learning environmentActivities: • Build new pre-primary centers wherever necessary • Rehabilitate the current pre-primary centers with child friendly infrastructure and classroom decorations • Provide learning materials, toys, and other materials for children• Provide teaching material for pre-primary teachers• Pay the incentives to the pre-primary teachers • Provide daily-kitchen meal supplies to the pre-primary centers in order to ensure the nutritious value of the food
