Project Summary

This project forms an important foundation for integrated primary nutrition services and community-based and outreach activities, it will include a network of Community Health Workers (CHWs), to provide nutrition services focusing on IYCF counselling and screening of children under 5 and referral for treatment of malnutrition at community level. These CHWs will be linked with health facilities protection centers. The project will also focus on building the capacity of the CHWs on IYCF counselling to provide integrated health and nutrition services. They will be trained in aspects related to child health care and nutritional screening and referral; they will also be provided with sufficient education to help raising the awareness of the related communities. Relying on CHWs will cover a big gap resulting from poor coverage of nutritional services.
Through implementing this project, BINAA will develop the management of SAM cases through referring these cases.
The project in the meantime will aim to proceed the support of distribution nutrition supplies plumpy nut, plumpy doz, and micronutrient powder for infants and PLWs. Furthermore, the project will contain training of health staff on IYCF and CMAM, referring the malnourished cases to the referral centre and support the additional staff with salaries.
This project will provide the appropriate nutrition services and support the nutrition situation for children under 5 years of age including infants and young children and their care-givers, the children with disabilities, and the women in reproductive age and PLWs without any sexual discrimination, among the populations.
The RRT will conduct MUAC and refer children who have MAM or SAM cases to specialized centers. The team will also do raising awareness using child friendly spaces and the regular mother and baby groups build resilience and increase networks of social support. They provide a non-stigmatizing way of supporting vulnerable women and children exposed to violence.
The community will contribute to the program by conducting FGDs for PLWs and women, to make the decisions realistic and appropriate to the community, A SMART survey will take place at the initial of the project to find out the needs in the community
The total number of targeted beneficiaries is 85000 (IDPs 16000, Host communities 66000, Returnees 3000), the number of targeted children under five years is 60000and PLWs is 25000.

4 - Needs

according to HNO 2020, Chronic malnutrition is evident in most sub-districts throughout Syria – notably in the Northwest of Syria (Aleppo, Idlib) and North East Syria (Deir Ez Zor, Al-Raqqa and Hasakeh). While at least one out of eight children in Syria is chronically malnourished, this ratio is worse in areas affected by displacements, returns, and over-burdened communities such as North East Syria, Rural Damascus, Idlib (1 out of 5 children), and Aleppo (1 out of 4 children).Acute malnutrition remains relatively high among internally displaced children and mothers as well as population affected by conflict. Throughout Syria, about 240,000 children under the age of five years and over 50,000 pregnant and lactating mothers are in need for urgent lifesaving curative nutrition services. At least 36,000 boys and 35,000 girls are acutely malnourished and are at increased risk of health complications, including death. Furthermore, it is the youngest children that are the most vulnerable and affected, with higher numbers of acute malnutrition amongst infants under 6 months of age. Anemia remains a challenge in Syria where at least one out of every three mothers and children are anemic. Anemia among mothers and children is a multifaceted problem caused by limited access to health care, reduced quality meals (diverse diet) as well as negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage, which could lead to negative outcomes such as increased maternal mortality and morbidity, particularly in conflict-affected, overburdened, and underserved areas. Currently, over one million children and over three million mothers in Syria are in a need of multi-sectoral support to treat and prevent anemia. Without this support, the cycle of chronic malnutrition in Syria will continue, over 90% of mothers will develop complications, and 5% of them could possibly die.

5 - Implementation of activities

1- Establishment of rapid response team (RRT) in the targeted communities and conduct MUAC screening, distribution of Supplies, distribution of awareness brochures, referral of malnutrition CU5 and PLWs, and providing key messages about feeding and hygiene promotion.2- Distribute of Nutrition Supplements and micronutrient powder to children 6-59 months, Supplements and micronutrient tabs to PLWs for preventing anemia and malnutrition, hygiene promotion kits to children 12-59 months, breastfeeding covers to PLWs.3- Conduct awareness-raising sessions and mother to mother discussion group sessions. These sessions will include well-being activities for children and their caregivers, in addition, to provide one to one counseling to mothers through these sessions as we will provide counseling for 2 hours after each session. The one who will provide the counseling services will be selected carefully from persons who have the required capacity and he/she will be also trained by a specialized trainer in order to have all needed technical information. (the awareness and counseling sessions are separated from RRT screening activities)
