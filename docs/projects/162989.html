Project Summary

In August 2019, the Government of Zimbabwe declared the 2018/19 drought and cropping season “a State of National Disaster” and appealed for international humanitarian assistance. This came after poor and erratic rainfall received between October 2018 and May 2019 resulted in a decrease in the area harvested and average yield of crops in Zimbabwe leaving the country facing a national cereal deficit estimated at over 900,000 MT . 7.7million People in Need (PIN) have been identified through the Humanitarian Needs Overview with at least 5.8million reported to be facing Crisis (IPC 3) or worse levels of acute food insecurity. The situation is being compounded by the current prevailing inflationary environment where prices of basic commodities continue to rise at an increasing rate. As of August 2019, the annual inflation rate was approximately 300 percent, while the food-price inflation rate was nearly 400 percent. At the same time, the 2019/20 rainfall season is forecasted to be below average characterized by a late start and erratic rainfall. This combined with low purchasing power and anticipated widespread poor access to agricultural inputs, will likely lead to below average cropped area in the country. Unless the prevailing and forecasted conditions are mitigated, a second consecutive below-average season is expected to result in lower than normal livelihood opportunities, household incomes, and 2020 harvest, driving atypically high humanitarian assistance needs.
Targeting: ADRA to scale up the response targeting 8,000 households (40,000 individuals) in IPC 3 and above from 4 districts namely Bulilima, Tsholotsho, Gokwe North and Mbire. The main objective of the project is to prevent further deterioration of living standards for acutely food insecure population in the 4 districts, by providing emergency agriculture support aimed at ensuring they can achieve food security and resilience to repeated exposure to multiple shocks and stressors. Livestock farmers - select farmers whose housing meet recommended standards using local materials.
Specific Objectives:
SO1: Minimize the risk of further food insecurity deterioration by improving access to basic crop and livestock inputs for 8,000 households in Bulilima, Tsholotsho, Gokwe North and Mbire
SO2: Enhance access, functionality and availability of critical community assets for crops and livestock production through productive infrastructure rehabilitation and construction.
SO3: Optimize the benefit of agricultural support through extension and advisory services to improve agricultural yields and manage crop pests and livestock diseases.
This project proposes to reduce food insecurity by leveraging the organization’s extensive background in climate smart agricultural practices, emergency response and food security. The overall approach of the proposed project will be hinged on improving the capacity of vulnerable households to recover from shocks without resorting to negative coping mechanisms. ADRA will use the promotion of drought tolerant early maturing crop varieties, conservation agriculture and capacity building of lead farmers – which have all proven successful for achieving food security and recovering farmer capacity in ADRA’s Drought Mitigation and Food for Assets Projects. Adaptive capacities to counter weather extremes will be built through the mainstreaming of climate smart agriculture techniques, with little or no emphasis put towards reactive measures. ADRA will strengthen and expand current livestock support programs that prevent further deterioration of livestock value and livestock deaths through the promotion of fodder production and preservation, which comes highly recommended by ZIMVAC. Building on experience & capacity, ADRA will use seed fairs through vouchers to manage the distribution of drought tolerant and early maturing seed inputs, and will build the capacity of farmers to diversify food sources and develop sustainable agricultural practices.

1 - Activities

a)Provision of vegetable seed packs for community and school nutrition gardensSeed packs of different vegetable such as leafy vegetables, onions, tomatoes and butternut will be provided to farmers targeting community and school nutrition gardens. Priority will be given to nutrition gardens that are located near functional water sources and those to be rehabilitated for provision of water for agriculture production and livestock. Demonstration sites will be established in each community/ school nutrition gardens where Lead farmers will learn and then transfer the knowledge to their respective members. The farmers will be trained in climate smart agronomic practices, soil and water conservation; establishing organic thermal compost using locally available materials, fertility trenches and Vermi compost. Organic manure will be used to improve the soil fertility in the garden for sustainability purposes and to reduce the cost of inputs for vegetable production. b) Provision of drought tolerant seeds: ADRA will promote and distribute drought tolerant and maturing seed varieties. The target households will each receive seed packs consisting of sorghum/pearl millet (5kg), Cowpeas (5kg) for human consumption and Velvet beans (5kg) for fodder production. The small grains have potential of alleviating food insecurity and improving household dietary diversity as farmers can sell the surplus to purchase other nutritious foods such as meat, sugar beans and other condiments. c)Livestock Support: promote poultry production since they have a shorter reproductive cycle for communities to realize incomes and supplement household protein requirements. Distribution will follow the Agriculture Input Guidelines i.e distribution of 7 chickens (6 hens and 1 cock) to each household with a 50 kg feed starter pack for sustainable production. Model FAO poultry housing guidelines will be showcased through demonstration sites to improve poultry housing using locally available materials. Medium to long term activities will entail brooding of eggs using local indigenous chickens for multiplication of the chickens at household level. The project will procure and install hatcheries at selected community centres which beneficiaries for hatching of eggs to sell chicks. All beneficiaries will be trained on VSLA approach to enable them to mobilize savings received from sale of eggs, vegetables and chickens and invest these in savings groups as well as access loans for diversification into other IGAs. The project will support fodder (velvet beans) production - input pack provision. ADRA and AGRITEX will also train the beneficiaries on supplementary feeding and feed formulation for chickens, goats and large stocks using harvested crops (velvet beans), other crop residues and locally available materials. d)Department of Veterinary services & ADRA will train Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) who in turn will support the communities on disease surveillance and reporting. A CAHW who completes the training will be provided with a veterinary kit which will be used to cascade the training and support a group of 15 -20 farmers within a selected demonstration site close to the group. The CAHWs will train larger stock farmers (15 -20) in livestock management (nutrition, health and breeding) to improve herd condition and reduce mortalities. A consultant will be engaged to train AGRITEX officers in Livestock Emergency Guidelines and Standards (LEGS), these standards will be used to advise the community on stocking capacity, health, nutrition and proper livestock management before, during and after an emergency.e) Enhance access to water for productive purposes to improve access to water supply for multiple use through rehabilitation of strategically located water sources in the 5 districts. The rehabilitated water sources will be equipped with solar powered irrigation systems to support the start-up of community gardens/ school nutrition gardens.
