Project Summary

Although famine in Somalia was averted in 2017, it remains a looming risk in the coming years and vulnerability continues to increase. The devastating protracted dry spells over the last 25 years, the unprecedented drought spanning the last four rainy seasons and ongoing conflict have severely aggravated vulnerability while humanitarian needs continue to rise. Decades of instability and drought have disrupted basic social services and livelihoods, devastated human capital and physical infrastructure and contributed to severe impoverishment, food insecurity and protracted displacement. The propose action aim to respond to the Somali needs implementing this three main area:
1)H&N: basic quality health services will be offered, through both fixed health care facilities and mobile outreach to guarantee access to treatment and routine vaccinations to the most isolated populations; the creation of community health workers network trained on nutrition prevention and treatment is encouraged and active MUAC screening will be carried out on children U5; this methodology is in line with the "Somali Nutrition Strategy" endorsed by the MoH, focused on strengthening health systems, improving access to services by the most vulnerable groups of the populations living in the poorest urban areas and in rural areas.
2) Food security/livelihood: loan and savings activities will be implemented in the communities, establishing self-help groups to support livelihood. The activities aim to address the immediate consequences of drought and to increase the resilience of the populations, also through the distribution of economic inputs and the resumption of business assets.
3) WASH: construction or rehabilitation activities will be implemented for water point, water transport, promotion of hygiene and distribution of hygiene kits; the WASH intervention is designed in synergy with H&N activities in order to enhance the promotion of good practices, infection prevention and disease control.
The intervention will be implemented in Banadir and Lower Shabelle regions covering the following districts: Wadajir, Wardhiigleey, Deynille districts (Banadir) Afgooye District (lower Shabelle). Project target beneficiaries include both IDPs and Host Communities with a particular attention to women (mothers), children U5 for H&N and FS, while in the WASH sector the project will have a special focus on the new arrivals in the IDP camps (together with the Host Community and the IDPs already in the camps). Total target population of the intervention in Banadir and Lower Shabelle is 228,981.

Needs

In early 2018, Somalia faced the fifth consecutive rainy season below average and estimated 4.6 million people are affected by acute food insecurity. The impact on the availability and quality of water, on crops, on livestock, on access to agriculture as a livelihood activity, on water and food prices has been severe and is still ongoing. The Deyr season of 2018 slightly relieved the situation, but the rains were still poor. The already weak health system is overstretched with insufficient resources and limited capacity and with less than 1 health facility per 10,000 inhabitants. High prevalence of communicable diseases including acute diarrheal disease (AWD), malaria and measles is recorded. Maternal mortality ratio remains one of the highest in the region and globally at 732 per 100,000 live births. The combination of poor access to drinking water, the lack of adequate sanitation (particularly in IDP settlements) exacerbate the risk of outbreaks such as AWD / cholera. 6446 cases including 43 deaths were reported with Banadir region recording 1/3 of all cases (2,453) of AWD/Cholera since December 2017 (WHO report, August 2018). With a rate of open defecation around 60% in rural areas and 39% nationally, more than 4.4 million people urgently need waterhygiene and public health services. According to UNHCR-PRMN, Lower Shabelle is the region that report the highest number of departures: 39,000 in Sept 2018 for a total of 176,000 from January 2018, where 124,00 due to conflict and insecurity, 43,000 due to the drought, 8000 for flood, 1000 for other causes. On the other hand Banadir is recording the highest number of arrivals for a total of 155,000 with 39,000 recorded in the only month of September 2018 (PRMN Somalia). The new arrivals either join congested settlements or they establish new temporary camps around the area, overstretching the essential and life-saving medical services in Mogadishu and L. Shabelle which are already insufficient.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Having considered that without access to basic services and livelihood opportunities, durable solutions cannot be achieved and recognizing that displacement is not simply a humanitarian issue but a development and state-building challenge, Cesvi will implement a strategy that involves all key stakeholders with a multi/integrated approach of wash, H&N and food security activities. The urgent life-savings needs of the affected communities and the livelihoods component will be addressed together with the long-term visiontargeting the needs and vulnerabilities of drought related IDPs and host communities simultaneously through a building resilience approach. This strategy together with a stable environment will allow displaced people to have the opportunity to opt for local integration solution in the areas where they took refuge.Since the primary responsibility to provide durable solutions for IDPs needs to be assumed by national government, Cesvi will work with MoH and local authorities to guarantee the IDPs’ right to health contributing to the IDPs participation to an adequate standard of essential medical services. The project will be linked to the government system in terms of ownership, localisation and accountability of duty-bearers. Provision of health service alone is not the only aim of the intervention;central to the action is the training of duty-bearers and medical personnel with the empowerment of the community to re-build the Somali health system. In accordance with the institution building process, the staff will be under the health public system, while the capacity building component will be held by Cesvi. Project health staff is MoH staff hence building their capacity, the action will strengthen the Somali health system too. The building of local human capital will empower the community and build resilience

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The intervention specifically targets vulnerable groups such as women, children both under 5 and above 5, elderly people, urban and IDPs communities ensuring their participation through the implementation of the activities. As a first step, the action will update the existing map of the risks and exclusion in order to identifying appropriate target and mitigation steps. A continuous monitoring in the area will be necessary to adapt activities and identify main barriers to access services or discrimination against particular marginalized groups. Cesvi has consolidated acceptance in the area of intervention gained through years of support to the communities and it implement inclusion of vulnerable groups in all sectors of intervention, as a cross-cutting issue:Health and Nutrition: teams of CHWs and CNWs will be active to reach rural and displaced groups which are the most likely to lack access to quality health and nutrition services. WASH: hygiene promoters will be actively engage the community’s members in all wash related activities ensuring inclusion of the most vulnerable groups.FSL: in consultation with community’s leaders and local authorities’, the beneficiary’s selection criteria will be outlined in order to ensure inclusion of most vulnerable categories (especially women). The beneficiaries privacy will ensure the respect of cultural and religious practice, as well as the participation. Teams of community’s mobilizers will actively engage the community’s members ensuring involvement of the most vulnerable groups. Once mapped and targeted, they will build direct contacts with each marginalized group ensuring effectively representation and avoiding discrimination.Information related to the services provided and implementation of activities will be provided through various means of communication to ensure the whole community is reached: health community workers consultation, door-to-door, poster and radio will be used.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

A gender sensitive, participatory approach will be adopted at all stages of the project to ensure that adequate and efficient services are provided by equally involving women, girls, boys and men of all ages in the project design. Adequate access to appropriate wash and health facilities is fundamental in the protection of people, particularly for female. The staff will work in coordination with protection actors putting in place measures to prevent and respond to physical and sexual violence arising from use of or lack of wash and health facilities. During the implementationof the activities all categories and layers of the populationwill be involved. Consultation with women and also marginalized groups will be organized to collect accurate information about specific needs and preferences for location and design of facilities (Women participation to the Water Management Committees will be promoted to guarantee decision-making and responsibility for water are equally shared by women and men. Moreover, Health & Nutrition activities will be implemented through a protection sensitive approach that will foster the dignity of the affected population. This will include a proportionate number of female health staff and experienced staff working with children. Also, consultations and documentation will be kept confidential and private: separate examination rooms will be provided and it will be established an information sharing protocol for abuse survivors avoiding to expose the victim to further trauma. Confidentiality will be a priority unless informed consent has been given; lists of beneficiaries and personal data will be kept safe from unintended use trough effective data protection measures implementation. Moreover, heath facilities will be provided with PEP kits for GBV survivors and CMR clinical management will be implemented.In order to ensure the accountability towards the affected population, mechanisms for anonymous feedback submission will be put in place.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

One of the major risk of the Somali environment with a potential high impact is the worsening of the security situation due to clan conflicts, terrorism and political controversies that can limit the movements, resulting in a restriction of access to facilities, increased demand for health care services, population displacement, increased morbidity and mortality of the vulnerable population. It is also likely that insecurity will interfere with the monitoring visits of the facilities.The followings are the mitigation measures the action puts in place: Cesvi has operational safety procedures that include clear risk assessment procedures, evacuation plans and suspension for the staff and it collaborates with local authorities and with community to guarantee acceptance, access and security of properties and staff. Restriction access to the facilities and the increase of the demand of health care will be mitigate thanks to the mobile clinic and in coordination with others humanitarian actors to guarantee adequate response. One of the consequences that can arise from armed conflict and violence explosion is the lack of support from communities, governmental and local authorities, traditional leaders. with significant jealousy and disharmony particularly between the host community and the IDPs. As a mitigation measures Cesvi can counts on strong existing relationships as it has a consolidated presence in the areas of project proposals characterized by the spirit of cooperation and trust between staff and local authorities and the community. This will contribute to ensuring a smooth development of the project, at the same time promoting Cesvi's perception as a capable and reliable agency. Regular meetings with the communities will be held to explain the program and to share the criteria used for the selection of beneficiaries. Cesvi works closely with local actors, opinion makers such as community / religious leaders, community health workers.
