Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to achieving the main objective that is:
Support the livelihoods of affected communities and households by increasing agricultural production, protecting productive assets, and restoring or creating income-generating activities to prevent negative or irreversible coping mechanisms for 2000 HHs in Aleppo
and Idleb governorates.
Main targets:
1- Improved the socio-economic status in targeted areas for 2000 beneficiaries (1000 HH of Males & 1000 HH of Females) in the 2023 season.
2- Increased sustainable livelihood opportunities in Aleppo and Idleb areas for 2000 (1000 HH of Males & 1000 HH of Females) Beneficiaries during the 2023 season.
This project by the two implementation methodologies will contribute to creating work opportunities, Horan Foundation (HF) can summarize the opportunities as follows:
1-The first methodology depends on cultivating one dunum with summer vegetables so each farmer will get profits by selling their products in the market at the end of the harvest season which allowed them to have a source of income to plant their lands for the next season and HF will select 1000 (200 Unique Beneficiaries from Host Community) local farmers whom their lands are near their houses with no affordability to agricultural supplies, financial resources or guidance in cultivating their lands by providing them with Seeds, fertilizer, and technical support for the targeted harvest.
2- The other methodology is to distribute 4 sheep (Vaccinated and pregnant) with fodder enough for seven months, also veterinarian’s support will be available for seven months.
Among the targeted 2000 HH of beneficiaries, HF will select 1000 (200 Unique Beneficiaries from IDPs) internally displaced people who fled previously from their houses.
the selection criteria will be according to the RNA that will be conducted before starting the project plan.
towards northern Syria. HF will increase the skills and knowledge of these beneficiaries by conducting practical and theoretical training.

3 - Needs

Based on the HNO-2023, the PIN is 15.3M, food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance In 2023, representing 68 percent of the total population. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 percent food insecure, and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity, an alarming increase of people at risk who could shift to become food insecure during 2023 if the crisis continues at same worsening levels.to meet other basic needs, Lack of access to basic services due to non or severely reduced functionality of critical service, infrastructure in almost half of all sub-districts and increasingly aggravated by reduced affordability. The estimated number of people living below the poverty line with 85% of the total population has passed more than six years of war, and GDP shrank more than two-thirds. The unemployment rate of 57%. More than 50% of households are buying food, and a higher rate for the displaced and internally displaced persons. Besieged areas have the highest percentage of households that are used to buy food and other negative coping strategies. Livestock numbers declined sharply since 2011: the number of cattle has dropped by 30% and sheep by 40% and poultry by 50%. Animal feed prices have risen by 168%. based on UN official statistics.

4 - Implementation of activities

1. Distribution of agricultural inputs; and related training. 2. Support to asset building and asset protection (small livestock and animal feed distribution) including related training. HF will divide the activities as flowing: Agriculture activities:- Hold training sessions.- Coordinating with the local council to determine the benefited farmers. - Registering the targeted farmers according to the agreed criteria. - Signing agreements with farmers. - Choosing a contractor according to the procurement policy of HF. - Preparing irrigation network and distributing fuel. - Distributing agriculture inputs to families to receive the agricultural summer kit (including Sprayers, Sickle, Garden Shears, Garden Hoe, and Vegetables and fruits summer seeds) from awarded suppliers. - Irrigation process. - Providing the necessary agriculture service. - Holding Practical training in the chosen seven typical fields. - Harvesting the crops. Livestock activities:- Hold training sessions. - Coordinating with the local council to determine the benefited IDPs according to their Vulnerability, number of family members number, economic status of the HH, disability, and taking other assistance. - Registering the targeted IDPs according to the agreed criteria. - Signing of agreements with beneficiaries. - Rehabilitating suitable places for raising sheep. -Procuring 4000 pregnant and vaccinated sheep and fodder enough amount to cover a period of approximately 5 months as package

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk/threats: -Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages ( Likelihood Medium Impact High) Mitigation Measure: Use the Cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content. If different from recommended standard, identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. -Tension between host communities, IDPs, and returnees (Likelihood High Impact ) High Mitigation Measure:1. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 2. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. 3. Make the effort to target 35% of the vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs 4. Establish a feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be. -Not having an exit strategy ( Likelihood Low Impact High) Mitigation Measure: Use the advocacy channels to ensure the continuity of the support and avoid any cut in the provision of services before the end of the project, while having the full procedures to phase out in case of not having a secured fund.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection risk/threats: 1. SEA by humanitarian workers male-dominated, gatekeepers,and potential other beneficiaries. 2. Risk of violence from beneficiaries among themselves/with humanitarian workers when huge onset occurs (crowd control)Likelihood: High. Impact: High in terms of reputation, and trust. mitigation measures: 1. Establish a feedback mechanism 2. Recruit female staff and train them. 3. Train staff on code of conduct 4. All staff contracts to include PSEA 5. Community committee with equitable representation of women 6. Train staff on Inter-Agency Coordination in Community-Based Complaint Mechanisms 7. Ensure distribution is well-organized (e.g. identification of sites/environment first; set up several distribution spots on sites; do joint distribution with another partner). Protection risk/threats. 1. Interference with beneficiary targeting/ 2. The beneficiaries list is not regularly updated by the Local councils.Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Train staff on targeting/vulnerability criteria as well as community mobilization 2. Staff work/support the local councils in preparing the beneficiaries list 3. Meetings with community leaders/gatekeepers on targeting criteria etc. 4. Set community feedback mechanism Use well trained and gender-balanced outreach team that could check if some locations are not included in the beneficiaries listThe target group for this project was both hosts and IDPs who are vulnerable and crisis-affected.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. The security situation does not severely degrade 2. Strong cooperation with local council, Representatives of targeted communities. 3. Stability of food items prices 4. High control of COVID-19 and Cholera during distribution, and commitments for all stakeholders involved in the project for the IPC measures for COVID-19 and Cholera.Risks and mitigation: 1. The bad security situation in the target location Likelihood: Low - Impact: High Mitigation: since the targeted location is in Afrin which is controlled by the Turkish government it is unlikely for security to deteriorate inthis area. in case of occurrence: Suspension of project activities, reporting to the donor, continuous monitoring for all potential security risks, and sharing plan B with Stakeholders.2. Local authorities and representatives of local council or other forces interfered in project activities and selection of BNFs Likelihood: Low - Impact: High Mitigation: Signing MoUs with local councils and representatives before implementation. 3. Fluctuation in prices and availability of proposed food items (Seeds, Sheep, and fodder) Likelihood: High – Impact: Medium Mitigation: Updating the market assessment for the items, after advance coordination with the FSL cluster. 4. risk of selling the distributed sheep and /or their fodder Likelihood: Medium- Impact: High Mitigation: targeting the BNFs how needs such assistance and support.
