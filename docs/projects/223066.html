Project Summary

For FY 2025-26, HICRA will implement a multisectoral, integrated approach to enhance the well-being and resilience of communities hosting or impacted by the Rohingya refugee crisis, targeting approximately 114,000 households. HICRA focuses on four key result areas: expanding income-generating activities, enhancing access to basic WASH services, strengthening sustainable natural resource management, and improving the ability of communities to mitigate, adapt to, and recover from shocks.
Under the WASH component for FY 2025, HICRA aims to:
• Provide basic drinking water to 22,500 people by conducting comprehensive assessments, deploying sustainable technologies, and installing 180 water access points, supported by detailed CIP, EMMP, and CRM plans.
• Improve sanitation access for 22,000 individuals in high-poverty areas through household surveys, latrine construction, and community engagement.
• Train 8,000 adolescent girls and women on menstrual hygiene management (MHM), equipping them with MHM kits and linking them to local hygiene product providers.
• Establish waste management infrastructure in 40 communities, including collection points, segregation bins, and recycling stations.
• Develop climate-resilient fecal sludge management (FSM) plans for 10 communities in collaboration with key stakeholders, ensuring long-term sustainability and impact.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

HICRA Result Area 2: Enhance Access to Basic WASH services targets critical WASH challenges impacting community resilience, equity, and well-being across Cox's Bazar and the Chittagong Hill Tracts (CHT) regions. With increasing demands on limited resources, access to clean drinking water is a major issue, particularly in Cox's Bazar, where heightened salinity further restricts supply. Additionally, inadequate sanitation infrastructure across both regions leads to the widespread, uncontrolled disposal of fecal and solid waste into the environment, posing significant health and environmental risks. HICRA’s targeted interventions focus on improving water quality and establishing essential sanitation infrastructure to promote healthier, more resilient communities. Through these WASH milestones, HICRA aims to strengthen community resilience, ensure equity, and support sustainable resource management. In the first quarter of FY 2025, 500 water samples from 51 unions will be collected and analyzed to document physical characteristics, chemical composition, and biological contaminants. During FY 2025, HICRA will repair or install an estimated 200 water access points, including ponds, wells, boreholes, and tube wells with in-line chlorination systems will be included where feasible. To address water scarcity, particularly in hilly regions, it is estimated that 30 rainwater harvesting systems, and 20 gravity-fed systems will also be installed. However, despite these estimates, HICRA will establish, or repair systems as needed to achieve milestones. HICRA will construct and/ or repair 5000 latrines in Cox's Bazar and CHT region. During FY 2025, HICRA will complete two milestones “WASH 15.01 and 15.02 Adolescent Girls and Women Trained on Menstrual Hygiene Management and Linked with a Hygiene Product Provider” involving the training of a total of 8,000 adolescent girls and women in menstrual hygiene management and establishing connections with hygiene product providers. HICRA will prioritize nature-based an estimated 40 - 45 solid waste management solutions for efficient waste collection and processing, aiming to serve 4,500 households within the 51 FY 2025 prioritized unions. In FY 2025, HICRA will initiate activities for fecal sludge management (FSM), setting the stage for full-scale interventions in FY 2026. Preparations will focus on selecting communities based on population density, existing sanitation conditions, and waste disposal systems. A tailored FSM plan will be developed, outlining systematic approaches for sludge collection, transportation, treatment, and disposal, and stakeholder roles to increase coordination among community members, local authorities, and private sector partners. The plan also addresses unique geographic and climatic conditions to promote sustainability. HICRA plans to train and develop 220 WASH LSPs to enhance water and sanitation infrastructure across 51 unions. These LSPs will support the construction and/or rehabilitation of an estimated 5,000 latrines, 220 water access points, 30 rainwater harvesting systems, and 50 Gravity Flow Systems (GFS). The WASH LSPs will coordinate their efforts with DPHE, Ministry of Local Government, Rural Development and Co-operatives (LGRD), NGOs, private companies, and local stakeholders. This collaborative approach will significantly improve access to basic water and sanitation services in the targeted communities, thereby contributing to better health and environmental outcomes.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

HICRA (Host and Impacted Community Resilience Activity) addresses protection mainstreaming in Cox’s Bazar by embedding safety, dignity, and inclusivity into all aspects of its program design and implementation. The initiative adopts a "do no harm" approach, ensuring activities are designed to minimize risks and avoid unintended negative consequences, particularly for vulnerable groups such as women, children, and persons with disabilities. Infrastructure improvements, like WASH facilities, are planned with community input to enhance safety and accessibility. HICRA emphasizes inclusive participation by actively engaging marginalized groups in project planning and implementation, fostering ownership and equitable access to benefits.

2.3 - Age & Disability

HICRA will ensure that people of all ages and abilities have equal access to safe WASH services. The project will focus on including vulnerable groups like children, older adults, and people with disabilities at every stage, from planning to implementation. To start, the team will assess the specific needs of groups such as pregnant women, older adults, and people with disabilities. This assessment will use targeted questions to identify key needs and plan services accordingly, while avoiding overlap with other organizations. WASH facilities will be made accessible with features like ramps, handrails, and clear signage for those with mobility or visual challenges. Local facilitators and staff will be trained in safeguarding and disability-sensitive practices to provide respectful and inclusive services to all community members.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

CA’s Community-Based Approach to Accountability centers the community in all interventions. Project staff and volunteers communicate key project information to engage the community, providing details on the project’s goals, timeline, participation criteria, and feedback and complaint channels. Staff share this information directly at households and community engagement points, with a hotline number prominently displayed on banners and other materials.HICRA Implementing Partners are trained to collect feedback using the Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM). Special awareness activities focus on engaging children, youth, and at-risk groups.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

HICRA will fully adhere to USAID's rules and regulations on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) by integrating these principles into all program activities. This includes ensuring that all staff, partners, and stakeholders are trained on PSEA standards, codes of conduct, and reporting procedures in line with USAID’s requirements. HICRA will establish and promote accessible, confidential reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries, ensuring that all reports are handled in a survivor-centered and sensitive manner. Furthermore, HICRA will implement ongoing monitoring and regular assessments to identify potential risks and ensure compliance with PSEA policies. By fostering a culture of accountability, transparency, and respect for human dignity, HICRA will ensure that all interventions are aligned with USAID's PSEA guidelines, safeguarding the rights and well-being of vulnerable populations in Cox’s Bazar.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

As part of increasing access to basic WASH services, HICRA will first develop a construction implementation plan (CIP) that will cover all construction related activities to be carried out under HICRA, including those required for providing safe drinking water, water treatment plants, water distribution network, deep tube wells, shallow tube wells, pond sand filters, rainwater harvesting, basic sanitation, latrines, WASH blocks, solid waste collection point, waste transfer station, small landfills, composting facilities, waste management, fecal sludge management, drainage infrastructure, stormwater drains, installation of culverts and channels, slope stabilization, access road, fecal sludge treatment plant, fecal sludge holding and transfer station and aquifer storage and recovery systems. The CIP will include the standard operating procedures and risk mitigation measures for all construction related activities to be undertaken throughout the life of the project and describe HICRA’s approach to construction activities including how the Activity will address/manage any/all concerns and associated requirements such as designs, cost estimates, construction schedules, permits, safety, monitoring and quality control, etc. HICRA will develop Environmental Management and Mitigation Plan (EMMP) and Climate Risk Mitigation (CRM) plan that covers all the initiatives HICRA will take under its WASH result area. The goal of an EMMP is to ensure sustainable progress towards HICRA’s objectives while minimizing adverse environmental effects. At its core, the EMMP will identify potential environmental impacts arising from the HICRA’s activities and outline strategic measures to mitigate these effects. The goal of CRM is to identify, assess, and manage the risks posed by climate change to ensure the resilience and sustainability of HICRA WASH interventions.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Under Result area 4: Enhanced Capacity of Host and Impacted Communities for Disaster Resilience, HICRA will work to improve the resilience of host and impacted communities by strengthening 100 Disaster Management Committees (DMCs). This will begin with an assessment of current DMCs to identify strengths, gaps, and areas for improvement. Based on the findings, new groups will be formed or existing ones reactivated as needed, followed by targeted training to enhance their disaster response capacities. A key focus will be on fostering coordination between DMCs, communities, NGOs, and the private sector, with an emphasis on inclusive representation from women and marginalized groups.Additionally, HICRA will assist upazilas, unions, and villages in developing 440 Climate Resilience Action (CRA) plans. These plans will be informed by assessments of climate-related risks, including impacts of flooding and cyclones on local infrastructure and livelihoods, with practical solutions proposed to address identified vulnerabilities. HICRA will collaborate closely with communities and relevant stakeholders to develop these plans.The project will also promote disaster preparedness through community meetings, public campaigns, and educational workshops, while training frontline staff involved in all result area interventions to spread preparedness messages.To further bolster resilience, HICRA will support the construction or repair of 45 flood control drainage systems and improve natural drainage maintenance through a community-based management approach.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the HICRA project actively engages youth, particularly those aged 15-24, in various activities aimed at building resilience and promoting community development. During FY 2025, HICRA will complete two milestones, “WASH 15.01 and 15.02 Adolescent Girls and Women Trained on Menstrual Hygiene Management and Linked with a Hygiene Product Provider,” which involves training a total of 8,000 adolescent girls and women in menstrual hygiene management and establishing connections with hygiene product providers. Youth are also engaged in community mobilization for WASH initiatives, including hygiene education, water management, and sanitation awareness. Furthermore, HICRA plans to train and develop 220 WASH Local Service Providers (LSPs) to enhance water and sanitation infrastructure across 51 unions. These LSPs will support the construction and/or rehabilitation of an estimated 5,000 latrines, 220 water access points, 30 rainwater harvesting systems, and 50 Gravity Flow Systems (GFS). They will collaborate with local stakeholders, including DPHE, LGRD, NGOs, and private companies, improving access to basic water and sanitation services. By engaging youth and providing opportunities for leadership and skills development, HICRA strengthens the capacity of young people to contribute to sustainable development and resilience-building in Cox’s Bazar.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

By focusing on WASH infrastructure, the project directly supports the district’s priority of improving public health, environmental sustainability, and resilience against climate vulnerabilities—core aspects of Cox’s Bazar’s development agenda.
