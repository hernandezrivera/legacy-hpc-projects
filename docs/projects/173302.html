Project Summary

Sudan faces obstacles on the social, economic, security, political and governance fronts, with direct and indirect impact on peace stabilization, food security, displacement crisis, child welfare. This situation is further exacerbated by local conflicts, inflation, inadequate basic services and furthermore issues concerned to covid19, climate shocks like flashfloods, presence of large number of refugees from South Sudan, Eretria and now the Tigrian/Ethiopian conflict spillover. The situation is precarious and is negatively affecting not only the average Sudanese, but the lives and livelihoods of refuges as Sudan is among the largest hosting countries of South Sudanese refugees, with over 729,530 individuals reported across the country by September 2020, while 46,412 refugees fleeing the conflict in Ethiopia have been registered in Sudan as of 2nd December 2020, according to UNHCR. Against this background, UNDP has put in place this response plan which aims at: 1) enabling access to livelihoods and lifesaving assistance to refugees as part of protection of refugees and 2) providing equitable access to livelihood and economic opportunities and basic services for refugees and host communities while strengthening opportunities for resilience and self-reliance. UNDP will prioritize community-based assistance that will include refugees and host communities and will work with UNHCR, COR, Implementing Partners and actors in other sectors to implement this project.

1 - Needs

Sudan shares a 2,000 km border with South Sudan, and refugees from South Sudan cross into White Nile, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, East Darfur and South Darfur states. Refugees entering Sudan via East Sudan corridor move onwards, becoming vulnerable to criminal networks involved in human smuggling and trafficking of people. Along these migratory routes, refugees and unaccompanied child are at risk of exposure to various forms of exploitation and protection concerns that can result in human rights violations, including SGBV. Moreover, onward movement also puts refugees at further risk of refoulement. SKS, WKS, WNS and Kassala State are among those States hosting larger number of refugees. The large presence of these displaced populations in the affected states has outmatched the available basic services and economic opportunities thereby triggering a clear need for humanitarian assistance. The situation has also led to growing competition and tension between displaced populations and host communities over the available basic services, land resources, livelihoods and economic opportunities, thereby creating increasing pressure in host communities with the propensity to precipitate social instability except something was done to remedy the situation. The arrival of the Ethiopian refugees has exacerbated the situation and urgent action is required to address the current situation. In a joint analysis by UNDP and its partners, it was estimated that over 50,000 people are in need of short terms assistance including food, medicine, emergency jobs, seeds and tools, livelihood and economic opportunities. The needs assessment also shows the clear lack of economic opportunities, high unemployment, diminished absorption capacity of host communities and high dependence on humanitarian aid. Food security remains an issue among refuges, with most refugees unable to afford the local food basket or purchase firewood. New refugees arriving from Ethiopia into Eastern Sudan are particularly in dire need of improve and extended nutrition and food assistance, access to clean water, latrines, health and medical supplies, shelter, family tracing, to name but a few. The rapid influx of refugees requires continuous resource mobilization and ramp up services to response to the urgent need of refugees alongside support to host communities who are facing rapid depletion of basic services due to the continuous influx of refugees in their communities. This response plan has been prepared based on observed local needs and through consultation with all stakeholders including refugees, host communities, local government authorities, implementing partners, etc. All partners are committed to engaging refugees in programme design and implementation, create accessible communication channel for feedback and increase transparency with all stakeholders on planned activities. To ensure an effective response to refugees in the targeted States, the response plans will follow an age, gender and diversity approach by using participatory methodologies to promote the role of women, men, girls, and boys of all ages and backgrounds as agents of change in their families and communities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Livelihoods – support to emergency agriculture and temporary jobs creation – seeds and tools distribution; provision of fertilizers; agricultural trainings; extension services; support to vegetable and backyard gardening; small ruminant restocking; vocational skills training in various trades including the provision of start-up kits and start-up capital; support to small businesses including training in business entrepreneurial skills, provision of start-up capital to enable beneficiaries establish and operate own businesses. Additionally, the intervention will build the capacity of local communities for improved preparedness to handle new emergencies and to cope with future shocks and stresses through the restoration and or delivery socioeconomic assets which are depleted/destroyed as a result of overuse due to the presence of large number of refugees. Community management committees (CMCs) will be established and trained to management livelihood activities, but also to act as the main decision make body on the project regarding needs identification, ranking, prioritization, implementation and monitoring. The CMCs will be a gender-responsive and an-inclusive body that will draw membership from all population groups including the full and active membership of refugees, giving them the opportunity to be involved in needs assessment, planning and delivery of assistance. The body will promote refugee engagement and participation through structured dialogue on the specific protection risks and assistance issues that different groups face. Co-existence committees will be established within CMCs and given appropriate trainings to promote peaceful co-existence and social cohesion between refugees and host communities. Energy – provision of solar stoves for preparation of household meal to reduce reliance on firewood collection which is detrimental to the environment. WASH – construction of water yards for refugees and host communities; expansion of existing water points into a more reliable water distribution yards to cater for additional consumption attributed to the presence of refugees; construction of gender segregated latrines; provision of sanitary kits – soap, handwash, hand sanitizer, etc. for the prevention of covid19. Shelter and NFI – provision of non-food items; construction of temporary shelters for new arrivals of refugees.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

During needs assessment carried out by UNDP in the proposed areas in 2019, it was discovered that ravaging forest and other natural resources was among the coping mechanisms of displaced populations and vulnerable members of host communities. Actions such as firewood collection, use of burnt bricks for housing, collection of wild fruits, uncontrolled burning of farm sites, improper disposal of refuse, to name but a few were among the environmental hazards practiced in the targeted communities. Hence the proposed intervention will incorporate environmental safeguards in all aspects of the proposed interventions to reduce vulnerability to climate change-induced risks. Interventions will seek to promote improved natural resources management, efficient energy utilization and environmental protection through training on NRM and community environmental action plan (CEAP) development and implementation. Proper refuse disposal will be promoted among beneficiaries. The use of improved fuel-efficient stove will be promoted especially among women who are frequent users of natural and forest resources. Soil and water conservation techniques such as minimum tillage, controlled burning of farm sites, mulching, the use of organic manure for backyard gardening, etc. will be encouraged.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will contribute to Sudan’s protection environment by conducting awareness raising trainings and campaigns on civil, social and economic rights of refugees to increase self-reliance and opportunities for solutions. Focus will be on awareness and sensitization initiatives on basic rights with local authorities, host communities and service providers to reduce social tension and promote social cohesion. Capacity building trainings of CMCs, beneficiaries and key community members will include topics with focus on human rights, refugee rights, gender equity, SGBV, rights of people living with disabilities among others. UNDP has adopted the strategic plan (2018-2021) which aligns with the 2030 agenda and SDGs. The principle of equity and or “leaving no one behind”, targeting the most vulnerable and most in need with all its interventions. Targeting criteria will be used throughout to ensure the most vulnerable are targeted. Protection specific activities have been incorporated in planned activities. The project will ensure vulnerable people have access to project assistance including sustainable livelihoods in addition to ensuring the use of a well-defined selection/identification criteria of beneficiaries for project assistance. The project will also ensure to share information on protection issues that the project will come across with humanitarian and protection partners working in the state. The project will also ensure stronger coordination with humanitarian actors, especially UNHCR to avoid missed opportunities for integrating protection information in refugee profiling for project assistance. The project will seek for cooperation with NGOs, civil society and community-based organizations on any protection issues that will be countered during project implementation.
