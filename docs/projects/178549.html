Project Summary

QRCS aims to continue integrating nutrition-related activities into the health facilities and community levels are currently operating. The intervention is intended to support the nutritional status for children under 5 years of age (boys and girls) as well as pregnant and lactating women, caregivers including people with disabilities across northwest Syria through the integration of the preventive and curative nutrition-related activities into primary facilities across NW Syria that are operated by QRCS in Idleb governorate (Darkosh, Zardana, Bennsh, Fos-Zuf, Ein El-Bayda, Haranbush, Qah, Harim, Ma'arrat Tamsrin and Salqin), and rural Aleppo (Al-Bab). QRCS will implement programmatic adjustment in the context of COVID-19 to protect health care providers, mitigate the risk of infection and ensure continuity of life-saving nutrition services. The service package entails:
1- Facility-based and outreach messaging about infant and young child feeding IYCF practices.
2- Nutritional assessment for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 years of age (Application of mid-upper arm circumference MUAC and Z-scores among other assessment tools) in addition to family MUAC approach and referral of undernourished cases to the appropriate facility as needed.
3- Nutrition counselling for pregnant and lactating women, in terms of the infant and young child feeding practices for children 0-23 months.
5- Outpatient and inpatient management of moderate and severe acute malnutrition cases of children under 5 years of age.
6- Deployment of rapid response teams and nutrition mobile clinics to detect malnutrition cases and provide treatment.
7- Distribution of preventative and curative nutrition supplements for PLWs and children under 5
The project will contribute to the protection of the most vulnerable groups of women, men, girls and boys in all age groups especially 26,400 children under 5 (boys and girls), 17,400 PLWs and 1750 disabled people in the targeted communities, moreover beneficiaries have been involved in the project designing and implementation through close coordination with local council also beneficiaries will be engaged in evaluation through satisfaction survey and complaint mechanism.

4 - Needs

The escalation of hostilities in the Northwest Syria region comprising Idleb and northern rural Aleppo governorates has displaced 960,000 people since December 2019, mostly children and women. The proportion of children and women among those displaced is estimated to be 60 % and 21 %, respectively. According to the HNO2021,1.7M pregnant and lactating women and 3.3M children under 59 months of age in Syria are in need of nutrition assistance in 2021 in addition nearly 11% of mothers are malnourished in NW Syria. While the humanitarian community is scaling up its response to the ongoing crisis, gaps across sectors persist due to the scale of humanitarian needs. The last SMART survey, which was conduct by PAC in Idlib and rural Aleppo with collaboration with nutrition cluster and TWG reported GAM and stunting rates among children 6-59 months of 0.73 % (0.4 – 1.3, 95% C.I.) and 19.3 % (16.9 – 22.2, 95% C.I.) respectively. Furthermore, The nutrition cluster for N.W. Syria has been tracked nutrition and IYCF indicators through facility and community nutrition surveillance system. The surveillance reports (March 2021), showed GAM 2.2% (0.9 – 5, 95% C.I.), and prevalence of global malnutrition among girls 2.53% (1.1-5.8, 95% C.I.). Additionally, exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) 41% (31.7% - 48.1 % C.I). Regarding the statistics mentioned above, although the overall GAM rate is low, the rise in acute malnutrition of children 6-59 months at sub-district level, especially in Idleb and rural Aleppo reflects deteriorating livelihoods, challenges in accessing health care, and ongoing hostilities in parts of the country. Increasing trends of acute malnutrition have also been observed among internally displaced children and mothers.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project intervention will be throughout:1- Conduct training for staff at facility and community levels on endorsed modules of CMAM, IYCF-E, Identify visual signs of abuse/violence against childU5/PLWs, referral pathway and COVID-19 pandemic considering the nutrition programmatic response alterations.2- Detection and referral malnourished cases of PLWs and children under 5 (boys and girls) and roll-out of a family MUAC approach in which caregivers, PLW and family members are trained on MUAC screening and referral.3- Deployment of rapid response teams.4-Provision of treatment SAM and MAM cases at OTPs and community levels through rapid response team.5- Screening and referral of children under 5 (boys and girls)with danger signs and medical problems through RRTs.6- Referring identified signs of abuse/violence against children U5 (boys and girls) through RRTs.7- Provide one to one emergency IYCF counselling for mothers with moderately malnourished children, With the adoption of approved caveats to mitigate the risk of COVID-19 infection including distancing and other IPC measures.8- Distribute micronutrients among children and pregnant and lactating women for preventing anaemia and malnutrition in the targeted locations includes IDP camps.9- Promoting and provision health, hygiene and nutrition key education messages using additional platforms such as mobile text messages, and social media on following topics IYCF-E, dietary diversity, hygiene promotion, COVID-19 at facility and community levels using appropriate IEC and promotion materials.10- M&E activities including planning, monitoring field visit, beneficiary satisfaction survey, IPTT tracking, Conducting PDM & Complaints mechanism-hotline feedback

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) is at the core of QRCS responses and all staff must comply with it. QRCS has a policy of zero tolerance for sexual exploitation. QRCS field team is trained on communication approaches, i.e. how to present the program transparently in a way that mitigates conflict, anticipating perceptions of unfairness or other issues that could create conflict, and managing expectations. QRCS recognizes that providing any material humanitarian assistance during a conflict in which needs are dire and resources are scarce having the potential to cause conflict and protection concerns for all actors involved. BINAA considers the different needs of the elderly, mobility-impaired, women, and youth in implementing the project activities to ensure equitable delivery of assistance by ensuring that the service well provided to them. Effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to acts of SEA by integrating PSEA in QRCS policies and making sure that all personnel read and signed the code of conduct, and that all contracts with partners/contractors include clauses on PSEA. QRCS ensure that complaint mechanism for reporting PSEA are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties. QRCS will take appropriate action to protect persons from retaliation.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

COVID-19 Pandemic affects northwestern Syria with multiple confirmed cases or free movement from other territories with confirmed COVID-19 cases Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigate Measures: Until this moment (April 15, 2021), the total confirmed cases were 21,486, 11,636 are in Idleb, and 9,850 are in Aleppo. QRCS have been taken the necessary measures, procedures, and precautions to protect health care workers and beneficiaries, and QRCS mitigate the pandemic effect based on WHO, NWS health and nutrition cluster & CDC recommendations and guidelines as follows:1. Activating Online Consultations for PLWs and children and Online Community Health outreach activities such as nutrition awareness sessions / IYCF-E and CMAM consultations for the vulnerable.2. Providing staff with Personal Protection Equipment PPE (Gown, Gogglers, FFP Masks, caps, and gloves) with multiple access to Alcohol for rubbing.3. Limiting the accessible areas for patients to eliminate virus spread. 4. Developing remotely/online raising awareness methods, especially on COVID-19 prevention and proper handling. 5.QRCS already trained CHWs on WHO-COVID-19 protection procedures and precautions. Also, QRCS will train new CHWs and staff on COVID19 Protection measures.Risk #2: Targeting OTPs & RRTs by direct bombardment, military control change, and multiple dominant military bodies. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigate Measures: Security assessment to choose safer and less bombed places. QRCS develop an emergency plan for each OTPs RRTs that includes the evacuation of beneficiaries, staff, and equipment (if possible)—additionally, Training all personnel on first aid and emergency evacuation, Equipping all staff with the full safety equipment.
