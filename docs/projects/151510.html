Project Summary

This project will focus on GBV response in humanitarian contexts. The main focus will be on service provision in line with SOPs and GBV guiding principles. The program will offer immediate lifesaving services to survivors of GBV including comprehensive case management, facilitating access to clinical care for sexual assault survivors, dissemination of key messages on GBV, referrals and capacity building of relevant actors to ensure services are quality and responsive to the protection needs of women and girls. The project will seek to enhance access to GBV services. IRC will provide PSS services at the safe spaces where women and girls (including survivors of GBV) will get psychosocial and economic skills including counselling services, skills building, listening sessions and build social networks. The safe spaces will be a hub for women and girls to build agency and those that are survivors to learn strategies on how to protect themselves as well help them get reintegrated in the community as they undergo the healing process. The IRC will work with various relevant stakeholders to ensure women and girls are protected from GBV risks. Some of the stakeholders include the local administration, the police, and women leadership at community level, community and religious leaders and will establish protection networks for women and girls. Community action plans and protection mechanisms will be developed and implemented to increase reduction of protection risks. GBV key messages will be disseminated to increase awareness on GBV and the available services including when and where to access them- emphasis will be put on accessing services within 72 hours of the incident. Dignity kits will also be provided to vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age to restore dignity.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Internally displaced women and girls stand the risk of SGGBV and not affording quality health care because of their poor socioeconomic status, long travel distances, and lack of proper public transport system, insecurity and marginalization as a result of local clan dynamics. IRC will ensure services are provided to IDPs impartially and at no cost.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women and children remain the most vulnerable to GBV risks because of their multifaceted vulnerabilities and gender roles and power relations. IRC’s interventions are tailored in such a way that they will address specific needs of women and girls. GBV response services for women and girls will be provided on a survivor center approach and community structures will be put in place to reduce the GBV risks.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Internally displaced people strand the risk of further displacement because of continuous inter-clan conflicts. IRC will work closely with camp coordination and management and security to ensure women and girls are not exposed to further GBV risks during displacements
