Project Summary

CHADO will provide GBV response and prevention services to a total of 16000 beneficiaries disaggregated 6000 women, 4500 girls, 3200 boys and 2300 men. This will be done by providing critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in Maiwut, Nasir and Longochuk in Upper Nile State and Cueibet, Rumbek Centre and East in Lakes States. CHADO will conduct comprehensive case management and referrals to 350 women, 185 girls, 85 boys and 30 men and also provide individual/group specialized Psychosocial Support (PSS) and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services) in the women and girls friendly spaces to 4050 women, 2150 girls and also provide mobile PSS to 900 boys and 450 men.
CHADO will equally engage and actively advocate for equal participation and representation of all persons of concerns in all project life cycle to prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience including training of 70 women and 30 men community/front line workers on comprehensive Case management and additional 100 community front line workers on Psychosocial Support, raising awareness and increase community knowledge on GBV prevention and how GBV negatively impacts the social and economic life of the community.
Furthermore CHADO will establish 6 Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) in Mathiang and Maiwut Center in Upper Nile and Mayenthon, Wereboi, Malou Phankait in Cuiebet and Rumbek Centre and East. The WGFS will provide safe spaces for women and girsl to feel physically and emotionally protected and where they can freely express their emotions and feelings without fear of being judged. They will be able to receive case management and psychosocial support services from CHADO case workers and also from other women and girls in the WGFS.
By providing trainings on moulding and use of fuel efficient stoves production to mitigate against GBV and also to strengthen their economic and livelihood aspects, CHADO will enable durable solutions for IDPs and other population. This will also be done through awareness raising and training of 60 community mobilizers on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning including conducting Focus Group Discussions on peace building and peaceful co-existence and finally CHADO will distribute dignity kits to the most vulnerable women and girls in Upper Nile and Lakes State, vulnerability criteria will be done according to the vulnerability criteria check list including using the Washington Group Short Set of questions to help identify PWDs.

1 - Needs

After 8 years of Independence, South Sudan remains in the grip of a serious humanitarian crisis, the effects of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic GBV response and prevention services on women, girls, boys and men continues to be felt throughout the country. While the situation is no longer escalating at a rapid speed, the continued conflict, violence and abuse of human rights has left an estimated 1.85 million South Sudanese in need of GBV response and prevention services. The August 2019 OCHA situation report states that there’s about 180,000 IDPs in Upper Nile. The IDPs are largely disadvantaged due to their lack of social networks, their dependence on humanitarian assistance and facing conflict with the host communities over limited resources. women, girls, boys and men face GBV risk factors such as distance to fetch water, food insecurity, incidents of armed conflicts and the cantonment sites that have been established in the county. There’s under reporting of GBV incidents including rape, sexual assault, intimate partner violence and insufficient lack of community support systems such as community based protection structures, psychosocial support services, case management services and safe referrals On the 7th- 10th October 2019, Nasir County in Upper Nile State experienced floods due to outburst of the Gilo and Sobat rivers coupled with the heavy rains. The flood has worsen living conditions of the populations affecting 31 villages leaving about 22,507 individuals in need of lifesaving humanitarian assistance. CHADO together with partners on ground including ROSS conducted an IRNA assessment on the flood and some of the identified issues facing the communities include wholly or partial displacements, under reported number of GBV cases, communities lack basic social support including Psychosocial Support services, there’s potential risk of conflict between the host communities and IDPs over limited resources, overcrowding at the borehole points causing women and girls to stay out late at night hence risks of SGBV, destroyed livelihood by the floods hence high risk of poor coping mechanism leading to GBV, Generally there’s need for GBV case management services including safe referral services to connect GBV survivors to much needed services. Inter clan conflicts In Upper Nile over resources and cattle raids e.g ciewang in Mandeng fighting Cielang in Makak Payams, Jikany clan fighting Lou nuer and the clashes between IO forces and government are some of the triggers that threaten overall community safety including heightened risks of intimate partner violence, rape, sexual assault, physical assault and emotional abuse. Lakes State has experienced particularly high levels of violent conflict, threatening stabilization and security throughout the State’s eight counties. Tension is largely driven by inter‐ethnic clashes over resources. The years of conflict, displacement and underdevelopment have limited livelihood opportunities of a population that is 80 percent dependent on pastoral activities and have to move to other payams and states to look for grass and water especially during the dry season. The conflict has also catapulted the risks of GBV including rape, misuse and abuse of power, early or force marriage, sexual assault and physical assault. Due to insufficient access to GBV services including case management, psychosocial support, CMR and awareness raising on GBV in the locations, CHADO intends to provide static and mobile GBV response and prevention services in Upper Nile and Lakes State through women and girls friendly spaces for women and girls to feel physically and emtionally safe . CHADO is on ground in Nasir providing lifesaving GBV services and in Cueibet, Rumbek centre and east providing communications for Development (C4D) including community led total sanitation services funded by UINICEF.

2 - Activities

CHADO will provide GBV response and prevention services to a total of 16000 beneficiaries disaggregated 6000 women, 4500 girls, 3200 boys and 2300 men.Services will include comprehensive case management and referrals to 350 women, 185 girls, 85 boys and 30 men, training of 70 women and 30 men community front line workers on comprehensive case management. Furthermore CHADO will provide individual/group, specialized Psychosocial Support in the women and girls friendly spaces to 4050 women, 2150 girls and also provide mobile PSS to 900 boys and 450 men and additionally train 70 women and 30 men community front line workers on Psychosocial Support (PSS).CHADO will establish 6 Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) in Mathiang and Maiwut Center in Upper Nile and Mayenthon, Wereboi, Malou Phankait in Cuiebet and Rumbek Centre and East. The WGFS will provide safe spaces for women and girls to feel physically and emotionally protected and where they can freely express their emotions and feelings without fear of being judged. They will be able to receive case management and psychosocial support services from CHADO case workers and also from other women and girls in the WGFS. CHADO will distribute 2000 dignity kits to the most vulnerable 1200 women and 800 girls and will further conduct awareness raising on GBV prevention including dissemination of lifesaving information on available services targeting 2450 men, 1200 women, 500 girls and 1800 boys. In addition, CHADO will also train 550 women, 350 girls, 80 men and 20 boys on moulding and use of fuel efficient stoves in Longochuk, Maiwut, Rumbek East and Center and Cuiebet as a GBV risk mitigation measure and also for women and girls to improve their livelihood. Lastly CHADO will train 40 men and 20 women community mobilizers on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning and also conduct 12 Focus Group Discussions on peace building and peaceful co-existence targeting 500 men, 200 women, 150 boys and 100 girls.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

All rights of all beneficiaries irrespective of origin, background, tribe, religion, political affiliations or sex will be legally recognized and project activities will be accessible to all persons of interest without discrimination. CHADO will also ensure services are provided in a safe environment in which beneficiaries can feel safe and respected and can access GBV response and prevention services without fear of exploitation or abuse by any aid worker, and in which aid workers themselves feel supported, respected and empowered to deliver such assistance in working environments free from sexual harassment.Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Sexual Harassment (SH) will not be tolerated; they are an unacceptable breach of the fundamental rights of the beneficiaries we serve and of those with whom we work as well as a deep betrayal of CHADO core values.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project will involve equal representation and participation of all displaced persons including PWDs, who will be able to access and benefit from the project activities. Activities will also be implemented in locations that are easily accessible to all beneficiaries including PWDs and all barriers to inclusive participation will be mitigated through active engagements with beneficiaries to determine how best services can reach them.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project sustainability approach is to establish /strengthen community structures that will be able to provide services after project completion, hence CHADO will work with the government systems, community based Protection structures to provide services to all persons of concerns.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The entire project is designed to Reduce morbidity, mortality, and suffering from protection threats and incidences of most vulnerable in severity levels and ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity levels Intesctorial objective SO1 and SO2. The activities are also aligned with protection cluster objectives of ensuring the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience including enabling durable solutions for IDPs and other population PO1, PO2 PO3. Furthermore the project is aligned with CERF life saving criteria objectives of promoting early action and response to reduce loss of life, enhancing response to time-critical requirements and strengthen core elements of humanitarian response in underfunded crises. CHADO will conduct all activities according to the above objectives and follow all humanitarian minimum standards according to the sphere handbook including protecting all persons from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation.

Activities will be implemented in secure locations that does not cause any more harm to the beneficiaries including staff and furthermore CHADO will work with community based structures including the local authorities, strengthen their capacities through multiple trainings on GBV including general protection as such, these communities will be able to provide services in the long term. 8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Experience from South Sudan highlights key risks that require careful and measured approaches. These include: exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence caused by living in highly insecure settlements, communities leave their habitual place of residence for the purpose of getting assistance (humanitarian assistance as a pull factor and risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services.CHADO will make monthly security forecast analysis and GBV assessments to mitigate on possible risks of women, girls, boys and men living in highly insecure locations and make possible measures like providing services before dark, encouraging women to walk in groups. CHADO will also work with community protection structures to help identify the most vulnerable beneficiaries, register them and provide them with services. CHADO will also work with community members in identifying beneficiaries including identifying the most vulnerable community members that were not initially planned for and come up with service provision plans and how services can reach them as well. CHADO will involve the community based protection structures and conduct protection assessments, including how community members living in some locations can be reached with humanitarian assistance without causing harm to beneficiaries including the staff and also hold peace talks with warring sides to allow safe passage of humanitarian workers to reach the beneficiaries.CHADO will use local content human resources especially persons from targeted communities to build trust while also being transparent to all project stakeholders in providing project objectives, targets, outcomes, period of project, who are being targeted as primary beneficiaries, how the project will benefit the community, CHADO will be transparent with the local authority, all beneficiaries through thorough consultations and involvement of women, men, boys and girls including PWDs

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will have an easily accessible feedback mechanism/complaint desks to all and will be strategically placed in points which are easily accessible to all project stakeholders including persons with disabilities and elderly. Activities will involve equal participation and representation of women, girls, boys and men in all project life cycle. Feedback will be generated through one on one interviews, household visitations and community meetings/FGDs where community members will discuss project activities, challenges and how the complaints/feedback can best be addressed with their input. The feedback mechanism will strengthen efforts and commitment of CHADO to use power responsibly by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the beneficiaries.This will be done through giving communities meaningful influence over decision making in a way that is inclusive, non-discriminatory and accounts for the diversity of communities. Listening is not enough therefore, CHADO will incorporate the feedback into our own strategy as well as in the collective response to the crisis. CHADO will also give account; through sharing of information in an effective and transparent way across women, men, boys and girls. This will include giving information about the project, its roles and responsibilities, entitlements and targeting criteria, as well as how to provide feedback or how to raise complaints. Information will be shared in a way that communities can actually understand it, can be empowered by it and become active participants in the project life cycle.Lastly CHADO will be held to account by ensuring communities have the opportunity to assess and, where feasible, alter or sanction CHADO actions. Their view on the appropriateness and quality of the service or response will be consistently sought. This will also involve consulting communities on what they think about the quality of the response.
