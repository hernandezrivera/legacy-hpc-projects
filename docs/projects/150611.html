Project Summary

The proposed project provides emergency response to the most vulnerable schools in Area C through integrated and targeted protection.
The response will be implemented by upgrading the educational environment in schools through the rehabilitation of classrooms and WASH facilities. It will also supply and construct new classrooms in overcrowded and primary schools to facilitate separate spaces for students from different grades. Activities will also target the schools’ staff and parents to improve their capacities in delivering knowledge as well as dealing with risk reduction; risk mitigation (settlers attacks, clashes and other natural hazards); and how to give the students the needed support during these risks and hazards (evacuation, first aid, etc).
Simultaneously, the project will respond to psychosocial issues faced by students and teachers as a result of the coercive environment they are subjected to. The proposed psychosocial response will be innovative; various artistic and recreational tools targeting students, teacher and parents, will be adopted to ensure the maximum effect on the targeted students.
The proposed interventions are in line with education cluster 2019 strategic objectives 1 "Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children” and objective 2 "Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, catch-up classes and school-based psychosocial services", more over all the proposed activities are classified as responsive activities for HRP 2019.
The project uses a community-based approach, with students (girls and boys) and teachers participating at all stages of the project including at the assessment, design and implementation stages. Hence, the students, teachers as well as parents’ fears, needs and ideas will be discussed and analysed to inform effective technical and psychosocial activities considering gender and age disaggregated data, resulting in adapted assistance.
As outlined, the proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability to affected population (AAP) as well as participation and empowerment.

Needs

The ongoing protracted conflict in the Palestinian territories has had a significant impact on all aspects of people’s lives, varying according to the relevant sector and its relation to the complex political context. The education sector in Palestine has been affected in a variety of levels. According to the 2019 HNO, in Area C and East Jerusalem, more than 44 education-related violations and incidents were documented, affecting 11,588 children (10,335 in the WB and 1,520 in Gaza). 46 schools in the West Bank’s Area C of have received demolishing orders hence they are under threat of demolition, many students are obliged to travel long distances, exposed to delays at military checkpoints, harassment and violence, military activity in or around the school, or have to cross military areas and Firing Zones in order to access schools. Students have to travel between 2 to 8 kms through unpaved and difficult roads by walking or using unregistered cars to reach the nearest school, consequently their daily trips are associated with various risks (long distance, weather conditions, wild animals, settler and IF violations). Moreover, school children face additional challenges that negatively affect educational attainment and a sense of safety and security, due to the poor educational facilities, unsafe and unfriendly overall educational environment, and the lack and inadequacy of school facilities. All these factors affect the educational level of enrolled students leading to high dropout rates, particularly among girls. The Palestinian Authority has been unable to significantly intervene in these communities as they are in Area C i.e. under Israeli Occupation. Additionally, all the existing school structures have been implemented under emergency conditions following minimum standards aiming to ensure an access to education and keeping a space for further upgrading works in future. Consequently, all the schools have become uncomfortable spaces for students who are already struggling to reach them due to access restraints, particularly during winter. In most of the schools, especially the older ones, the uninsulated classrooms, the inadequacy of WASH facilities, overcrowding, mixing students from different ages in the same class, and the absence of safety measures such as school fences, clean and paved surrounding, have driven many families to adapt negative coping mechanisms such as withdrawing their children from school or displacing part of the family to city so the children can go to better schools there, and make the students unwilling to go to schools. In July 2018, AAH updated a multi sectorial needs assessment and protection analysis involving 80 communities in Area C using a community based approach. The assessment indicated that communities in the E1 zone, Firing Zone 918, Jordan Valley and Road 317 clusters are the most affected by Israeli restriction policies, these polices affect the educational sector in the targeted communities. Girls’ school dropout, access to schools and schools’ unsafe environment were the main concerns for the children in these communities.

Activities

In September 2018, AAH, VIS and GTC conducted needs and technical assessments in 12 schools adopting a participatory methodology targeting the students, the school teachers and households. This was developed with a comprehensive understanding of the problems schools face due to the unsuitability and inadequacy of school facilities, unsafe educational environment and the associated psychosocial problems faced by students and their families. Therefore, based on its experience in implementing over 40 educational interventions in Area C, AAH has proposed the needed technical solutions for each school in order to create a more healthy, safe, and friendly educational spaces for students and considering ICA construction restrictions in Area C. It will do so by rehabilitating the existing infrastructures to ensure the basic and humanitarian needs that will thereby support students in continuing their education. To complement, VIS, who has extensive experience in conducting psychosocial activities at both community and school level, and its local partner GTC, who has more than 23 years’ experience working on child and families psychosocial and mental health related issues, has conducted different focus group sessions with students, teachers and parents from different Area C communities. Throughout these sessions VIS and GTC’s social experts identified the psychosocial problems that targeted groups suffer due to school access and environment; out of which they were able to propose technical solutions and share them with relevant stakeholders (MOEHE) and school headmasters, etc. AAH and VIS have experience in implementing educational activities in coordination with (MOEHE). For this project the coordination with MOEHE will be on two levels, ministry level: to ensure that all the project activities will be implemented according to the ministry strategies and priorities, and on directorates level: to ensure that the education directorate in each area have been consulted regarding the assessments, designs and even during the implementation, moreover the activities will be handed over to the ministry of education as the service provider of the educational sector. The following activities will ensure meaningful access to education and psychosocial support, prioritizing safety and dignity: A1 Provision of emergency education supplies: targeting schools that suffer overcrowding or merging students from different grades into one classroom; schools that face a deficit in power supplies, affecting the safety, health and educational attainment for students. Under this activity simple classrooms will be added, and power supply solutions will be developed (including solar systems for isolated communities). A2 Basic emergency rehabilitation to schools: to enhance the students’ learning environment, classrooms and other facilities will be rehabilitated/upgraded, creating a friendlier, more enjoyable and safer space for children. The upgrading of sub-standard structures in the most vulnerable communities will ensure a more protective educational space and the improvement of WASH facilities in schools (water fountains, latrines, water cisterns) will decrease the number of students per fountain and latrine to create more healthy and comfortable facilities. A3 Provision of school-based psychosocial support to vulnerable school children and staff: the activity will target the most vulnerable schools that suffer from a variety of threats including settler violence, natural hazards, and those with a generally unsafe and unhealthy environment. Detailed psychosocial assessments will be conducted in each school and consequently GTC and VIS’ psychosocial support teams will identify the appropriate psychosocial intervention in each school. They will use various artistic and recreational events and sessions with students, teachers and parents, including activities focusing on stress management, trauma and class communication.

Indicators

Technical and psychosocial baseline surveys will be conducted by VIS, GTC and Action Against Hunger to identify the target groups’ needs; the survey will facilitate the supervision, monitoring and evaluation of the activities. Teachers and students (girls, boys and persons with disabilities) will be involved in the design, monitoring and evaluation of the activities to ensure the full participation of all groups. A post line and KAP surveys will be conducted to assess and evaluate the satisfaction and impacts of the activities on all groups, including an evaluation of improved access to schools and the students’ psychosocial status. Project activities will be monitored by 1) Working field staff; 2) Community representatives; 3) MOE visits and participation in the activities; 4) Baseline and post line reports; 5) Donors and cluster monitoring visits; 6) Project progress and impact reports. The communities’ representatives and village councils will support AAH, VIS and GTC to provide ongoing field monitoring of the rehabilitated schools and advise AAH on ongoing security status to determine optimal timing of field operations. In order to ensure accountability, AAH has a Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism (FBM), as well as a Prevention of Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (PSEA) mechanism, which allows beneficiaries to provide their comments and complaints on the activities and is designed to increase their protection, in compliance with sensitive data management and adhering to the “Do No Harm” principle. Beneficiary knowledge of this system has been promoted through several field assessments and monitoring visits. In addition, a service phone number will be distributed to the population in the proposed target communities enabling them to report feedback or complaints relating to project implementation by AAH and its partners. AAH will process and treat all information collected through an ad-hoc committee composed of project staff to understand the situation, identify appropriate solutions and include technical adjustment or modifications if needed. Output 1: 3 schools (125 students and teachers) benefited of the emergency education supplies (caravans, solar systems, educational tools). Output 2: 5 schools (813 students and teachers) received emergency rehabilitation for the school building and facilities. Output 3: Psychosocial support through using various artistic and recreational tools (20 events) provided to 1459 students, parents and teachers received. The expected objective verifiable indicators and targets from the project are as follows:Outcome1: 50% of the targeted students reported that the schools became safer. Outcome 2: the total educational attainment improved by 20% in the targeted schools.Outcome 3: the average absenteeism rate decreased by 30%.
