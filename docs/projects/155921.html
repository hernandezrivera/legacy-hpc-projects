Project Summary

The project aims to scale up safe and equitable access to formal education for crisis affected children (IDPs and Hosts), through restoration and preparation of schools by providing students with learning materials (stationary, pens, notebooks), school bags, fixing all damaged components of affected schools like windows, doors, painting and remodelling the buildings of 16 targeted schools, establish, expand and rehabilitate new classrooms, and to enhance the quality of formal education for children and youth (aged 5 –17 years) within a protective environment through providing teachers and education personnel with incentives and salaries, furnishing schools, provide children with recreational materials, and Provide teachers and education personnel with teaching resources, kits and guides in targeted schools.

Needs

The changes in control in central and south Syria in early and mid-2018 as well as increasing hostilities in northwest Syria in September 2018 caused new waves of civilian displacement into Rural Damascus, Idleb and Aleppo. Existing education capacity in these areas was already limited prior to recent displacement influx. With the addition of over 1.9 million displaced school-aged children in these three governorates, many of whom have been displaced multiple times, education capacity has been nearly exhausted, with widespread implications on the ability of children to access quality education. The ability of schools to absorb more IDPs into the education system is very limited. Some schools operate in double shifts (morning and afternoon classes) to accommodate the large influx of displaced children. This reduces the teaching learning hours per day, adversely affecting learning outcomes for IDPs and host community residents alike. As the conflict continues, effective teaching becomes increasingly challenging due to mixed-aged, mixed-ability, over-crowded and under resourced classrooms. In addition, having been exposed to the psychological trauma of hostilities, displacement and family upheaval generated by the crisis, many children and teachers are affected by psychological trauma. A 2018 survey across multiple governorates indicate 1 in 8 children per classroom have psychosocial support needs requiring specialized interventions for effective teaching and learningMore than 40 per cent of the out-of-school children in Syria are between 15 and 17 years of age and have differentiated learning, skills development and livelihoods needs. Of these children are with disability, over half of children with a disability have an unmet need for education, and over two thirds require specialized health services which are not available in their area.The impact of the crisis included all categories of students (IDPs and hosts), such as the high pressure on schools in the host communities due to displacement to the target area, and the lack of resources and equipment in schools, and the lack of specialized teachers, as a result, the expertise of all teachers in the target area, both hosts and IDPs, has been used.According to other reports, more than one out of every three schools has been damaged or destroyed, while others are used as mass shelters.Most of the targeted schools in this project have been targeted by Beyaz Eller since 2014, and we regularly assess the needs of students and staff in these schools. The shortage of educational materials, books and stationery came first on the list of needs that students need, secondly poor conditions in schools, The third is the lack of income. The need was essential to bring these students back to their schools; to provide them with an appropriate educational environment; to provide education, during emergencies, with stability and organization, to help children live normally and to protect them from exploitation by providing them with the necessary skills to cope with difficulties. Support the various elements of the educational system and contribute to raising the efficiency of its operation, in order to maintain the continuity of students in the educational process within the Syrian and meet their needs from various elements of the educational system to follow their educational process and reduce the negative effects of the crisis. According to ( WoS education sector: 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview - People in Need)In Aleppo governorate the number of boys in need (5-17 years) is 681,078, the number of girls in need (5-17 years) is 623,064, and total teacher in need 27,169.In Idleb governorate the number of boys in need (5-17 years) is 455,607, the number of girls in need (5-17 years) is 397,681, and total teacher in need 17,777.Severity 4,5 and 6 : 61%.• HNO 2019. • ACU.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks/ Threats• Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire.• Attacks on schools.• Risk of long distance and young children walking alone.• Children may get lost, be attacked or lose interest in learning.• Tensions due to different/inadequate standards being used insufficient safety and health standards at schools.• Risk of children being out of school due to unavailability of school furniture upon completion of school rehabilitation and construction of prefabricated classrooms.• Risk of not having enough WASH facilities for boys and girls. This might cause lack of privacy to the children.Mitigating measures:• Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct a security assessments. They will ensure that staff members and teachers are trained in safety and• Security best practices, including mine risk education.• If and when the local security situation deteriorates: a) Immediately inform internal emergency focal point on the situation; b) Coordinate with partners, cluster and hibernate/suspend the operations if necessary; c) Examine the possibilities of adopting different modalities to implement the project. All important documents will be saved as soft copies, so that they will not get lost due to an attack. Decisions are taken in advance whether hard copies will be destroyed or not.• Communication with donors to accept flexibility and support relocation of activities to safer spaces whenever needed.• Training of partners and local teams on Child safety, development of schools specific child safety policies; establishment of complaint mechanisms in school.• Alternative locations (community centers, homes, etc…) and methodologies of education should be considered to avoid attack-related risks.• Duty of Care policies are in place, and the staff are informed on the responsibilities as well as limitations of the organization.• Security/safety training for organization, students and school staff.• Establish ECCE within the neighbourhoods whereby children will be dropped to the center by their parents.• Sector/cluster agreed minimum standards with members and local authorities commitment• Planning and on-going communication with local education authorities, community leaders, schools, etc.• Security/safety training for organization, students and school staff.• Do-no-harm training for organization staff.• Strengthened advocacy.• Include school furniture in school rehabilitation plan or sign an MoU with authorities to ensure provision of school furniture by them upon• completion of school rehabilitation• Work in collaboration with WASH section who will establish WASH facilities in schools.• Tension/competition over salary/incentive due to different scales, interruption of payments, etc.• Education staff switching schools due to different scales• Offer salaries that are in line and comparable with public school teacher salaries• Standardize teacher's incentives at the levels of ESWG/cluster and other major donors.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:- Suspension of financial support.- Attacks on Schools.- Changing in Prices of fuel.- Harassment and exploitation. Mitigating:- Continued support for the coming years for students to continue in their schools.- Develop a quick evacuation plan for schools until the danger ends.- Communication with suppliers to Install the price.- Distribute the bags inside target schools under the supervision of teachers and managers.
