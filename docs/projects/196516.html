Project Summary

The overall objectives of livelihood interventions include fostering sustainable reintegration of IDP and refugee returnees who have spontaneously returned to their respective communities, limiting the adoption of harmful coping strategies including strategies that undermine the sustainability of the natural resource base and achieving more coherent and coordinated actions between UNHCR and other mandated stakeholders. UNHCR will support interventions which will improve the prospects of POCs to secure employment opportunities, including through self-employment, internship programmes and on-the-job training. UNHCR will also provide business training with small business start-up projects and support to small-scale agriculture projects of returnees in rural areas. UNHCR will engage in facilitating access to livelihoods support services provided by national actors. Rehabilitation of key social, productive and marketing infrastructure will include accredited vocational training centres/schools as well as small-scale irrigation infrastructure.

3 - Needs

According to the draft 2023 HNO, more than 15 million people are in need of multi-sectorial humanitarian assistance. An estimated 85% of Syrians live below the poverty line, becoming increasingly vulnerable due to the loss or lack of sustainable livelihoods. Food and fuel prices have increased dramatically by end of 2022 as WFP's standard food basket registered the highest ever recorded price since monitoring started (2013). The situation is particularly serious for the most vulnerable who may be compelled to resort to harmful coping mechanisms e.g. child labour due to lack of meaningful income opportunities. The gap between income and expenditure per year is increasing among all population groups coupled with deterioration in the access of basic services e.g. electricity.The project will take into account the above factors to improve of the accessibility of the most vulnerable groups to livelihood and economic support. Where possible, socio-economic assessments will be undertaken to understand the socio-economic profile of target communities as well as the capacities and needs of targeted population in order to better identify livelihood opportunities. The agriculture sector continues to remain an important part of the economy and the second largest contributor to GDP.The HNO emphasises the requirement for rural livelihoods-related interventions supporting vulernable farmers. It also emphasises the importance rehabilitation of basic infrastructure to boost livelihoods.

4 - Implementation of activities

The following main activities will be undertaken as part of the project in collaboration with local and International NGOs:1) Rehabilitation of key community economic and social infrastructure such as vocational training classes/schools.2) Provision of business advisory services (including referrals) regarding apprenticeship, business startup processes, business management and pursuing employment opportunities. 3) Provision of support to Small Start-up business projects. Basic essential assets and tools will be provided to IDPs and returnees in rural and urban areas to help them resume former profession or previous means of living or to start a new business.Agriculture1)Provision of key tools and services to support farmers, fishers and others dependent on the natural resource base for their livelihoods.2) Rehabilitation of key community economic and social infrastructure such as vocational bakeries and irrigation channels.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: There is a risk of discrimination when selecting beneficiaries of livelihoods interventions, and risk of the most vulnerable being excluded (GBV, persons with disabilities). Mitigating measures: • Needs assessment will be conducted to gather information about the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees to be targeted through community centres and outreach volunteers. • Participatory assessments and community consultations will be applied in all stages of the programme cycle to enable beneficiaries to actively engage in the assessment, planning, implementation and evaluation of the project.• Capacity building of Partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with guidlines;• UNHCR will clearly inform community on beneficiary selection criteria and ensure that community leaders, local council, elderly people are engaged in community sensitization and beneficiary selection process. An active and informed complaint mechanism will be in place. • Enhance monitoring and evaluation of livelihood toolsProtection risk: Existing labour market opportunities limit the participation of youth, women and persons with disabilities resulting in lower remuneration and return to harmful coping mechanisms. Mitigating measures: • Analyze access to market opportunities with an age, gender and diversity lens. Compare this information with protection needs assessments.• Conduct feasibility assessments for each project to identify viable livelihoods interventions.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closelywith other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA networkto ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regardto the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition tomandatory trainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment hasbecome mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet thestandard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership withUNHCR in the following year.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk description: Lack of access and approvals hampering ability to conduct needs assessments, impact and protection monitoring of the programs results.Mitigation measures: • Capacitate Partners to capitalize on information collected through livelihood staff and in the community centres and systematize methodologies used for focus group discussions in community centres;• Expand monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring e.g. KOBO• Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. • Maintain and expand UNHCR field presence and monitoring capacity.
