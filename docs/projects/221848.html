Project Summary

In September 2024, nearly 5.83 million people, 46 percent of the population, experienced high levels of acute food insecurity and classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) in South Sudan. Malnutrition continues to affect the well-being of women and children and is driven by flooding, economic crises associated with depreciation and high food prices, recurrent conflict, forced displacement, low agricultural production, and a reduction in humanitarian assistance. In Rubkona and Koch Counties, 116, 000 people are in emergency and 15,000 people are in famine. Among the people in famine, the majority, 14,000 South Sudanese returnees fled from the ongoing conflict in Sudan. As per the nutrition cluster update of September 2024, the number of SAM and MAM admissions has subsequently increased for five years from 608,584 to 758,914 in South Sudan with a 24% increment. Protracted shocks including three years of regional flooding have disrupted livelihoods and availability and access to food which has contributed greatly to food security deterioration at the household level. The severe flooding in Unity has impacted health and nutrition service provision in Koch and Rubkona, counties. At the PIDO field observation, various locations in Koch and Rubkona have been completely submerged in water while the rest remain under threat of being flooded, and communities continue to move to relatively dry highlands. Nutrition sites and WASH facilities in Koch and Rubkona counties have been damaged, and the rehabilitation of infrastructure is needed to continue providing life-saving services. PIDO will strengthen life-saving nutrition services and scale up the quality and coverage for community-based management of malnutrition (CMAM) by extending services to hard-to-reach areas and underserved communities. The project will target 87,228 children U5 with acute malnutrition as direct beneficiaries.84,919 children will receive Vitamin A supplements and deworming tablets. 69,943 caregivers of children will be reached through Maternal and Infant Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN)programs in outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) sites, as well as during nutrition outreach interventions in Koch and Rubkona counties. This project will establish three new outreach units in these counties to provide outreach nutrition services to communities with limited access to services due to displacement. In Rubkona and Koch Counties, PIDO will be supporting 33 static nutrition centers, including three stabilization centers in Rubkona Hospital, Sector Two in Bentiu IDP camp, and Koch town. A gender-targeted and nutrition-sensitive service delivery approach will be used, and nutrition services will be integrated with WASH, health, gender and FSL programming through social mobilization on critical handwashing and food safety, prevention of GBV, cooking demonstrations using locally available food produced in the kitchen gardens. In addition, nutrition screening services will be implemented in the health facilities to ensure that all malnourished children are referred to nutrition centers. In the end the project aims to reduce morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable children and women in these hard-to-access locations, while promoting and engaging in recovery and resilience activities in the community. Project activities will be monitored through supportive supervision done together with the County Health and Nutrition Department, the MOH, and UN Agencies. Activities will be monitored by nutrition project staff with support from the PIDO Juba office program team. Relevant reports will be shared with the nutrition cluster and other reporting partners. Community-based complaint and feedback mechanisms will be established within the nutrition centers and in the community to ensure accountability.

1. Needs

According to the IPC report of September 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people need humanitarian assistance, representing 46% of South Sudan's population. The need for humanitarian assistance will be high due to ongoing issues related to increased food insecurity, economic crises associated with depreciation and high food prices, flooding, protracted conflicts, and flooding continue to play an adverse impact on the livelihoods, availability, and access to food, which has contributed greatly to the food security deterioration at the household level and has impacted health and nutrition status of children under five and PLW. As per the nutrition cluster update of September 2024, the number of inflates the number of persons needing assistance and overwhelms the need for a health and nutritional services response. Furthermore, the reduction of funding to humanitarian agencies greatly reduced the scope of service and the amount of food consumed at the household level and resulted in increased SAM and MAM cases. WRSS health program reports indicate that malaria, diarrhea, and upper respiratory infections are the major cause of illness in children. Intersectoral needs assessment of October 2024, indicated that 93.9% boreholes or protected wells are their main source of drinking water and 0.9% reported feeling unsafe while collecting water in Bentiu IDP camp. The SAM and MAM admissions has subsequently increased for five years from 608,584 to 758,914 with a 24% increment. The food security outlook of September 2024 projected that in Unity state bordering Sudan, the influx of South Sudanese returnees continues to drive high humanitarian needs, putting an additional burden on the host households who are sharing scarce resources with the newly arrived. In Rubkona and Koch Counties, 148,000people are in Crisis, 116, 000 in emergency, and 15,000 are in famine as per the latest IPC report. Among the people in famine, the majority, 14,000 South Sudanese returnees fled from the ongoing conflict in Sudan. The IPC classification report of September 2024 predicted that crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes persist across South Sudan with an increased need for humanitarian assistance. A KAP Survey conducted by PIDO in March 2024 reveals only 62.8% of children 6 to 23 months of age met the minimum dietary diversity requirements by consuming from five or more food groups in the past 24 hours in IDP camp in Bentiu. The proxy GAM rate for Koch and Rubkona is 26% and 29.8% (PIN 2024). Observations by PIDO also indicate that malnutrition continues to be a high threat in the IDP camp. Experience has shown that when the influx of South Sudanese returnees, flooding, and conflicts escalate; increases the number of people traveling from neighboring areas to Koch and Bentiu IDP camps to receive basic services and support, and malnutrition condition of the vulnerable communities in the proposed areas is aggravated by the preexisting inadequate household food production and limited access to the market for diversified food as their livelihood greatly depends on aid food assistance and the current crisis in Khartoum affected the market supply. Gender-based violence is also a major concern in the areas. According to OCHA, HOP - August 2024, 0.7% of the IDPs in Bentiu reported that members in their households had to restrict their movement. The main reported reasons for restricting their movement were due to insecurity including death or injury(66.7 %), kidnapping (33.3%), and sexual violence (33.3%).

2. Project activities

Objective1: Conduct behavioral change communication by individual counseling and biweekly mother support group sessions and disseminate key messages to 59,943 caretakers and PLW on optimal MIYCN and hygiene practice by using the MIYCN Counseling card and Early Childhood Development (ECD) in Koch and Rubkona. Conduct 174 cooking demonstrations at nutrition facilities and community to support optimal complementary feeding. Provide vitamin A to 84,919 children aged 6-59 months and deworming to 69,934 children aged 6-59months in the nutrition program that were missed during the routine National Immunization Days (NIDs). Objective 2: Provide therapeutic and routine medical treatment to 21,631 (11,032 girls and 10,599 boys) aged 6- 59 months with Severe Acute Malnutrition without medical complications at 30 OTP sites in Koch and Rubkona. Provide therapeutic and medical treatment to1,139 (581 girls and 558 boys) aged 0 59 months with Severe Acute Malnutrition with medical complications in SC of Rubkona Hospital, Sector 2 Bentiu IDP camp and Koch Hospital. Provide therapeutic treatment to 45,726 (23,320 girls and 22,406 boys) aged 6- 59 months, and 18,732 pregnant, lactating women with moderate acute malnutrition. Support early pre-positioning of specialized nutrition products for the treatment of SAM and MAM at project sites. Provide refresher CMAM training to 68 MoH and PIDO nutrition staff on the national CMAM protocol will be the core of the training provided (22 Koch and 36 Rubkona). Refresher training of 87 community health/nutrition volunteers on screening, identification and referral pathways, and family MUAC approach for acute malnutrition cases identified (33 Koch and 54 Rubkona). Train 68 nutrition staff, MoH health facility workers on MIYCN by using MIYCN Guideline for South Sudan. Training 116lead mother support groups, and provide support groups to deliver Key MIYCN messages at nutrition centers and community level to improve optimal MIYCN practices. Train 116 mother support group lead mothers/PLWs on nutrition packages (MIYCN, WASH, Health, Use of Nutrition supplies etc.). Objective 3: Support weekly facility-based data collection through integrated capacity building of PIDO and MOH staff. Support monthly joint supportive supervision and on-job trainings with MOH at nutrition sites to ensure quality of nutrition service. Conduct monthly community meetings to raise awareness and provide information to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement in the nutrition
