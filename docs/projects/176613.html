Project Summary

In order to ensure that the entire population, both host communities and IDPs living in resettlement camps may access basic health care services, also in the most remote communities, far away from the nearer health centre, CUAMM’s intervention aims at facilitating the organization of the integrated mobile clinics (BM- Brigadas Moveis) in 5 districts in Cabo Delgado Province, namely Ancuabe, Chiure, Metuge, Montepuez and Balama in order to support the District Health Authorities in the reactivation of the primary health care service provision, covering the support of the 20 planned BMs organized by each District for the year 2021. Moreover, the proposed Action intends to improve equitable and universal access to quality health care services ensuring effective triage for IDPs and host communities through the establishment of Temporary Advanced Medical Posts (TAMP), to alleviate the national health system burden. Patient will be referred to TA. In this context, access to the services offered at the health unit level therefore represented the only place for providing essential and emergency care. CUAMM will install the TAMPs in the areas with a higher density of IDPs, final selection will be done, primarily, with the Community Activists support, CUAMM entry points to be accepted by the communities. CUAMM will recruit 42 activists and 6 supervisors (per district) to carry out and oversee triage activities at a household level to identify people in need of medical assistance. The selection criteria will be established beforehand by CUAMM, which will train the activists on triggers for referring a person to the TAMP. The criteria will also be clarified to the beneficiaries to avoid bias and to ensure accountability. At the same time, with strong community engagement
activities, it will be ensured that all community members understand TAMP referral criteria. Most of IDPs
live within host communities; therefore, CUAMM’s activists will work in those areas indifferently with IDPs
and host community in order to avoid tensions, working towards improving health at a community level,
and ensure the ‘do no harm’ principle is respected. This project aims at strenghtening the Government capacities of each District to properly offer quality and integrated health care services to the new arrivals of IDPs in their areas as well as to the host communities, reaching the very last mile of the whole districts.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

District of Metuge, Ancuabe, Chiure, Balama and Montepuez. In all the districts involved in the intervention CUAMM will both establish the TAMPs (especially in the resettlment sites) and support the Government Health Authorities in carrying out their calendar of Mobile Health Brigades. The objective of this project is to ensure an overarching triage for all IDPs (with particular attention to themost vulnerable population, such as persons with disabilities) through the establishment of TemporaryAdvanced Medical Posts (TAMP) will be established both in the communities hosting a consistent numberof IDPs and in resettlement sites, in order to alleviate the national health system burden of anoverwhelming number of patients.TAMP will operate as treatment and referral centres. Critically ill patients will be stabilised and referred tothe nearest health centres to receive a higher level of medical services, while stable patients will bereferred to APEs / CHWs for follow-up. All patients will be registered within the national HMIS (calledSISMA in the Mozambican system) for appropriate follow-up within the national scheme, without creating aparallel system.The TAMPs will carry out screening for:- COVID-19- Pregnant women (ANC, etc.);- Malnourished children (MAM, SAM);- HIV, TB, Malaria patients;- Chronic patients (Diabetes, hypertension);- Mental health (patients who have suffered trauma as a consequence of the humanitarian crisis)Each TAMP will be provided (and regularly replenished) with a basic medical kit to respond to acuteemergencies and to stabilize the patient as well as needed equipment (e.g. beds).If needed, next to the TAMP, COVID-19 isolation tents will be established to isolate the suspect casebefore referring him/her to the government health facilities in Pemba to be treated. In this case, the referralwill not occur via the moto taxi but with appropriate and equipped government ambulances in order to ensure sanification of the means and to follow the government protocols. The referral system will be ensured by working with an existing moto taxis system called Okalihera, initiallyestablished as a pilot project by the Swiss NGO Solidarmed. Okalihera works as a private-publicpartnership with drivers directly selected among the community. The system consists on drivers who rentthe motos for 3,000 MZN per month to Okalihera, which ensures the maintenance. They are allowed touse them privately, with the only mandatory requirement of referring timely and effectively all obstetricemergencies coming from their communities to Chiure District Hospital. On top of this, the Action willforesee the additional task for the drivers to refer all kind of emergencies/cases in need of a higher level ofcare, from the TAMPs to the nearer advanced health facility. In doing so, the Action will put in place avoucher scheme through which, for all the referrals which are not obstetric emergencies, the driver willreceive a fuel refund. The idea is to ensure a strong development-humanitarian nexus in a context wheresuch a system has been experienced and proved effective, and it is well accepted by the community andthe health system.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

With strong community engagement activities, it will be ensured that all community members understand TAMP referral criteria. Most of IDPs live within host communities; therefore, CUAMM’s activists will work in those areas indifferently with IDPs and host community in order to avoid tensions, working towards improving health at a community level, and ensure the ‘do no harm’ principle is respected. They will also systematically gather formal and informal feedback from them to identify unintended risks or secondary harmful effects created by the activities, and device corrective/mitigation measures in a participatory way.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

There will be a strong community engagement and sensitization that will ensure there is the right level of empowerment

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

To tackle the three main problems identified, and within the overall framework of protection mainstreaming principles, the Action has adopted an integrated approach throughout its activities, focusing on the most vulnerable people (including PwD, elderly, and people suffering from psychological distress and other protection-related issues) with the objective of ensuring a universalaccess to services by placing a specific focus on PwD and vulnerable persons (elderly and people withspecific needs). It will be make sure that needs of the most vulnerable people are taken into consideration. On the other side, local organizations of persons with disability (OPDs) will be involved to ensure meaningful participation of people with disability and provide awareness sessions to the public.

7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?
