Project Summary

The combination of conflict, insecurity, recurrent droughts, flooding, and extreme poverty, coupled with very low health services coverage, has significantly increased the population’s vulnerability to diseases and even deaths. The health system remains weak, poorly resourced, and inequitably distributed.
Lack of drugs and equipment; fear associated with the most recent Covid-19; responsibilities such as taking care of young ones hindering mothers to go to the hospital; inadequate (qualified) personnel, and long distance to health facilities are major barriers to access to health services not only to IDPs but also to the host communities.
Children in Somalia are still deprived of a fair chance to a strong healthy start. Four in 100 Somali children die during the first month of life, 8 in 100 before their first birthday, and 1 in 8 before they turn five. This accounts for more than 60 percent of the under-five deaths in the country (UN IGME, 2019). One in 12 women die every year from causes related to pregnancy and childbirth, high fertility rate (6.6 – UNFPA, 2015) and FGM/C (98%); low immunization levels among children (32% immunization rate par SHDS 2020), Low access to well-trained midwives and nurses during antenatal / postnatal period (only 31% & 11% respectively, SHDS 2020), Few women delivering at a health facility and 32% with skilled birth attendants (SHDS 2020). Only 46% of facilities in Somalia offered BEmOC services & 45% offered routine childhood immunization services (SARA 2016); Low access to clean water, handwashing, breastfeeding, skin-to-skin contact, proper cord care, and good nutrition (MICS 2011).
With the ambition of providing essential healthcare to the IDPs and host communities in 6 districts, and contributing to the health cluster objective of Improving equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for the crisis affected population – IDPs and the host communities/non-displaced population and reducing excess morbidity and mortality due disease outbreak; STS intends to take the essential services to the people (who are often hindered by insecurity and long distances to the health facilities) through mobile clinics and also by provision of the primary health care services through established health facilities in Afgooye (target 22,971), Marka town (target 17,400) in Lower Shabelle, Warsheikh and Balaad (target 17,800) in Middle Shabelle, Dhusamareeb district (target 14,300) in Galgaduud, Kahda and Deynile (16,200) districts in Banadir.
Although inadequately equipped, because of lack of funding, STS already has existing health facilities in 21 October, Howlwadaag, Janaale, Warsheekh. Gadon, Mareergur, Kahda, and Deynile have acute health needs, and will use this as well as introduce 8 mobile clinics that will take the health services to the people so no one is left out. We are targeting a total of 349, 000 beneficiaries in total, where 31,410 will be pregnant and lactating women and 69,800 will be Children Under 5 Years of age.
Because of the huge unmet need, STS will provide vaccination to children under 5, treatment of common illnesses, skilled birth delivery, Maternal and Child Health, Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmOC); Antenatal coverage, and general attending to outpatient cases.
In providing the primary health services, we shall integrate our health program with our other programs – Nutrition, Protection, Nutrition and WASH programs. For instance, patients who are victims of violence or show related signs shall be treated then referred to the for further attention.
We shall also continue sensitizing the communities under which we shall implement our programs of Covid-19. We realize the importance of health worker capacity building and constantly upgrading the skills of the health workers, and as such, we shall conduct training for the staff at least twice during the project implementation period.

Project Activities

Project Activities In order to improve access to quality essential and emergency health care services, STS International will carry out the following activities, in addition to sensitization and awareness on Covid-19 at our fixed facility and mobile treatment sites level: 1. Immunizing/providing 3 doses of vaccination to all children. The rates of children coming for the subsequent vaccinations after the first vaccination is low. We shall do thorough tracking and follow up through calls and other follow-up measures where necessary to ensure every single child receives vaccination children under 5 in 6 districts (Afgooye, Marka, Balaad, Dhusamareeb, Kahda, and Deynile). 2. Maternal and child careWe shall attend to the health needs of pregnant women, breastfeeding/lactating women and women of child bearing age (15-49 years). These will include family planning, skilled birth-delivery services, antenatal care (ANC) services, Post-natal care (PNC) services, basic emergency obstetrics, and other services.• Children under 5yrs of age, if not well taken care of are also vulnerable to childhood killer disease. We shall attend to these too through our outpatient services. Target 17,734 children under 5 in 6 districts (Afgooye, Marka, Balaad, Dhusamareeb, Kahda, and Deynile). 3. Basic emergency obstetric and newborn care will be made available in all of our 6 health centres and 8 mobile treatment clinics to pregnant and lactating women. Target 7,980. 4. Treatment of common illnesses and injury - Through our fixed and mobile treatment clinics, quality and adequate provision of curative services for common ailments and injuries should be made to the communities. Such common illnesses will include Malaria, Diarrhoea, Pneumonia, etc. • STS International shall educate our beneficiaries and the communities where we operate of the varied common ailments and health problems, as well as methods of prevention and control of the ailments. 5. Essential drugs - The most vital drugs will be available at zero costs in all our health facilities both fixed ones and mobile treatment sites.6. Community sensitization and awareness raising on the prevention and spread of COVID-19 and other communicable diseases. Target 88,671 people in 6 districts.7. Disease surveillance and notification of notifiable diseases outbreaks.8. Joint monitoring and support supervision with the federal and state level ministry of health. Target 1 per quarter.9. Regular, timely and accurate submission of weekly surveillance and monthly reports to the ministry of health at federal and state level and through platforms like HeRAMS, ReportHub, DHIS2. Target 6 static and 8 mobile clinics.For best results, we shall integrate our services with the WASH, Protection, and Nutrition Programs.10. Training of health staff on IMCI, BEmOC, and iCCM (including clinicians, nurses, midwives, auxiliaries, and Community health workers).

hire and train local midwives• Avoid community tensions, by providing the local/host community with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities• Consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities• In cooperation with protection actors, establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidents concerning hospitals to advocate against attacks on hospitals and military use of hospitals eg. use as a weapons depot, filtering patients in search of wounded armed group/forces members, preventing passage of wounded seeking health care, use of hospital as military operational centre. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities• Collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming.• Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalised persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for health centre location, design and services.• Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups• Ensure the location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups.• Introduce special arrangements for persons unable to access health facilities eg. mobile health services, transportation/taxi services, facilitate accompaniment arrangements (e.g. youth groups assisting the elderly/persons with disability; community networks).• Make infrastructure adaptions to the health facility so that it is accessible to persons with impaired mobility (e.g. persons with physical disabilities, older persons, bed-ridden individuals).
