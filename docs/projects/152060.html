Project Summary

REACH proposes to conduct an in-depth profiling of IDP communities in Libya with particular focus on protection in order to draw a better understanding of household vulnerability amongst IDPs. The aim will be supplement the evidence base on the protection concerns of IDPs in Libya to help the protection sector develop their interventions. The assessment will involve a mixed method approach including FGDs, KII and direct interviews with IDP communities.

Needs

Building on key findings from the REACH's multi-sector needs assessment (MSNA), IDPs tend to display higher vulnerabilities compared to other communities in Libya including higher proportion of school un-enrolment amongst IDPs children, eviction, poorer food consumption score and inability to pay for healthcare services. The aim of this study will be to shed light on IDP communities in Libya, their access to protection, education and other key issues pertaining to the vulnerabilities of IDP communities to pave the way for more targeted protection programming.

Activities

The assessment will be conducted using a mixed-methods approach, combining a survey of IDPs in major IDP hubs, focus group discussions in select IDP a communities, and a series of Key Informant interviews with authorities and relevant stakeholders currently providing support to these populations groups. Interviews will be conducted at the case level. Data collection will be undertaken by an experienced mixed-gender team of REACH enumerators and directly supervised by a Field Coordinator and Operations Coordinator. Enumerators will interview the head of case; if unavailable, another case member 18 years or above who is willing to participate will be interviewed instead. In households comprised of multiple cases, a member from each case will be interviewed to enable aggregation to the household level for relevant indicators. All data will be recorded using Open Data Kit (ODK) collect, an Android-based mobile application, to ensure data entry directly during the interview, while also mitigating data entry errors typically found when administering pen-and-paper questionnaires. The research design will be coordinated with of the Information Management and Assessment Working Group as well as the Protection working group to ensure that there is no duplication and to ensure a large buy-in of the findings. Furthermore, REACH through ACTED will ensure it also coordinates closely with relevant government agencies where functional. ACTED is registered with the Commission of Civil Society (CCS), the recognised body in Libya for coordinating all INGO operations and also submits narrative reports to CCS on quarterly basis, financial accounting reports, as well as annual narrative report summarizing project achievements.

REACH will follow best practices to ensure the best interests of IDPs and international best practices in the ethics of research can be maintained, including the training of REACH staff and its partners in Libya on the ethics and practicalities of data collection with IDPs ensuring that they abide to international protection standards. Furthermore, gender considerations will be made central during any interview and survey conducted by REACH staff and its direct network present in the field. To ensure that all IDPs perceptions and needs in the targeted geographic areas are captured and that their vulnerabilities and protection concerns are reflected in REACH’s information management, REACH will prioritize data sets disaggregated in terms of gender, age, and vulnerability at all levels of collection and reporting.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

The assessment activity is inherently designed to solicit viewpoints from IDPs in Libya to improve accountability of the overall humanitarian response to the population. Furthermore, humanitarian partners, who are the direct beneficiaries of REACH research, will be involved from the very outset of the action through the above mentioned working groups. All enumerators directly involved in data collection will be required to adopt Ethical Procedures designed by REACH in partnership with the protection sector, and fully comply with its requirements. All staff which will conduct interviews or focus group discussions with IDP will be trained on sensitive issues related to IDP issues in Libya.
