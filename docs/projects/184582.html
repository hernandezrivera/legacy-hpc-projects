Project Summary


Somalia's health indicators are extremely low due to poor health systems and aggravating conditions which have resulted in wanting humanitarian needs. Excess mortality is driven primarily by malnutrition, disease outbreaks, (e.g. COVID-19, AWD/cholera, measles, malaria), and violence as well as non-communicable diseases.
According to IGME 2020, the under-five mortality rate was at 177/1000 live births and women risk 14 chances of dying from pregnancy-related complications with only 32 percent of skilled births attendants. Sequentially, less than half of healthcare facilities do not provide EPI services, driving coverage below 50 percent (SARA, 2016). In addition, one in three people is affected by mental health disorders (WHO, 2016).
DEH aims to provide equitable and inclusive emergency primary health care and mental health services to host communities, refugees, IDPs, and returnees in Galgadud and Mudug in central south Somalia. The intervention will address basic curative and preventive health care needs, improve psychosocial wellbeing and mainstream disability. Aligning with health strategic objectives, DEH will continue providing essential health services in 2 static and 3 mobile sites targeting 9000 men,10000 women,12500 boys,12500 girls, and 1000 PLWDs in Hobyo,Galkacyo,xarardheere cadaado and Ceelbuur.
DEH will deliver a minimum basic package of essential PHC services as per the MoH guidelines which include maternal and child health, immunization, reproductive health focusing on ANC, safe delivery, and support voluntary family planning. The reproductive health care would provide Minimum Initial Service Package and the Basic Emergency Obstetric and New Born Care (BEmONC) components. Subsequently, the project will uphold respect for the inherent dignity and provide non-discriminatory equal opportunity to PLWDs. The medical facility and equipment will be inclusive and accessible to all vulnerable populations irrespective of their abilities. DEH purposes to build the capacity of both medical teams and community volunteers regardless of their disability to enhance quality services delivery. The sessions will cover antenatal/postnatal/voluntarily family planning and Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EmONC) training. Thereafter the village-trained midwives will be supported with basic case management supplies including clean delivery kits. They will also participate in the Infection prevention and control (IPC) and Universal precaution training, IMCI, EPI, eWARS, and HIS training based on their cadres in the health facilities.
Routine growth monitoring for under five years boys and girls will be conducted at the MCH and designated ambulances availed for emergency referrals and linkages.
Again, DEH will focus on training and empowering the Community health volunteers on implementing communication for development (C4D) activities that would raise awareness on available services, prompt health-seeking behavior, disease prevention measures, and early identification of ill children and pregnant women for care at the health facility. The community will be sensitized against discrimination and stigmatization of PLWD, diseases prevention, health promotion, immunization, and antenatal and postnatal services. In addition, volunteers will disseminate information on reproductive health, nutrition education and practices, Covid 19, hygiene, and mental health care.
Communicable diseases management will be incorporated in all intervention strategies and referral pathways established.
Adopting IASC MHPSS guidelines and WHO recommendations, DEH will establish an ambient environment and train health care providers in the identification, management, and referral of people presenting with mental health disease. Community sensitization sessions on mental health will be conducted and psychosocial interventions established 3 outreach and 2 static health facilities. COVID 19 containment and vaccines administered following WHO protocols.
