Project Summary

The project overall objective is to ensure that most vulnerable households exposed to impacts of natural disasters (harsh weather and flooding) are supported to meet their basic needs and enhance their coping capacity by providing a timely, efficient, and effective response with emergency shelter support and NFIs (Shelter/NFI Cluster Objective 2). Providing essential shelter support meets basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation (Strategic Objective 2) and strengthens their capacity to cope with the protracted crisis (Strategic Objective 3).
As part of its emergency response for the 2018/2019 winter season, NRC will support already identified 480 most vulnerable households exposed to harsh winter climate (flooding, storms) by providing winter emergency repair & winter NFI support. Winter emergency repairs include basic sealing off for roof, external door and walls, and windows to ensure that affected households are protected from harsh weather (modality: conditional cash). Winter NFIs include sealing off-kit, bedding-kit, and winter clothing sets for boys and girls of climate-affected households (modality: in-kind/voucher/cash).

Needs

77.500 households in Gaza are unprotected from harsh winter weather & flooding (HNO 2019), especially those with poor roofing (asbestos/corrugated sheet) which makes them more vulnerable to potential flood-related displacement. NRC’s Community Consultation (Sep 2018) confirmed that people living in high risk flood areas are displaced every winter for one or more weeks. Most affected from natural hazards are families in acute poverty with exhausted coping capacity especially in flood-prone areas. Selling basic assets to meet other needs is used as common coping strategy.NRC’s recent assessments already identified a most vulnerable caseload in need of winter emergency support: based on data from MoSD (Dec 2017), 593 hardship-households are in need for emergency shelter support. In Oct 2018, NRC conducted household vulnerability assessments of 41 out of 593 households in 3 vulnerable areas in Middle Area (Deir Al Balah refugee camp), Gaza (Al-Shati refugee camp), North Gaza (Jabalia refugee camp). 17% are exposed to flood risks. 61% live in shelters with poor roofing. More than 50% are not protected against harsh weather due to major gaps in core shelter items (56% without sealed windows, 56% without functioning external door, 66% without sealed roof; 56% with cracks and holes in external wall). 68% lack essential winter-NFIs (bedding set). Based on data from MoEHE (2018), 12.000 children registered in MoSD’s hardship-database are in need of winter clothing and shoes especially those who live/go to school in flood affected areas. NRC also conducted KIIs with & vulnerability assessments of 10 GMR-affected male/female households in Gaza & Khan Younis Governorates (Sep/Oct 2018). 10% are exposed to flood risks. 50% live in shelters with poor roofing. 80% are not protected against harsh weather due to major gaps in core shelter items (80% without sealed windows, 70% without functioning external door, 80% without sealed roof; 70% with cracks and holes in external wall). 70% lack essential winter-NFIs (bedding sets). According to NRC’s FGDs and vulnerability assessment (Sep 2018), renters – due to their inability to pay rent – have become a vulnerable group either at risk of eviction or already evicted. The latter are visible in public areas with temporary shelters (tents) if they cannot resort to another alternative shelter solution, while others move to already overcrowded hosting conditions or to unsafe shelters (garages, storage-units etc.). The assessment of 10 renter-families with low shelter vulnerability found that 63% lack essential winter-NFIs (bedding set) mainly due to high/severe socioeconomic vulnerability. NRC’s IDP assessment (2018) found that out of remaining 4,450 (now 3,000) IDP-families who are still waiting for a permanent housing solution, 12% live in host families & 10% in tents/caravans/make-shift shelters in inadequate & often unsafe conditions unprotected from harsh winter weather. Up to now, NRC identified 102 families as 2014-IDPs living in host families & lacking emergency shelter support. Based on above assessments and available data, NRC identified the most vulnerable cases out of those in need of winter emergency support based on a rapid household vulnerability assessment. Out of the identified 480 households living in acute poverty, 170 are social hardship-HH registered with MoSD, 110 are Great March of Return-injured, and 100 are 2014-conflict IDPs living in host communities. In addition to 100 households lives in rented housing unit in need to winter NFIs (bedding and clothing).In the above mentioned recent NRC assessments (Sep/Oct 2018) males and females were consulted & specific needs and concerns documented. Women and girls & those with limited physical mobility are most affected as they are culturally expected to spend more time at home being more time exposed to climate effects. Boys and girls studying at home are also affected by the exposure to cold/humid climate inside the shelter.

Activities

As part of its emergency response for the upcoming winter season, NRC will respond to harsh winter climate (flooding, storms) out of the already identified caseload by providing: 1) Winter emergency shelter support to ensure that affected households have core items essential for climate protection such as a sealed roof, external door and windows (modality: conditional cash). 2) Winter-NFIs such as sealing off-kit, winterization-kit, household kit which includes – based on need – bedding/lighting/clothing sets (modality: in-kind/voucher/cash) As part of its emergency response for the 2018/2019 winter season, NRC will support already identified 480 most vulnerable households exposed to harsh winter climate (flooding, storms) by providing winter emergency repair & winter NFI support. Winter emergency repairs include basic sealing off for roof, external door and walls, and windows to ensure that affected households are protected from harsh weather (modality: conditional cash). Winter NFIs include sealing off-kit, bedding-kit, and winter clothing sets for boys and girls of climate-affected households (modality: in-kind/voucher/cash). NRC engages male and female beneficiaries of all ages in designing the optimal response to each households’ specific needs and protection concerns and supports them in prioritizing their needs considering budget limitations. Male and female beneficiaries are also involved in identifying the most suitable modality of implementation based on preference and special needs of each case.

Indicators

NRC’s M&E framework ensures that relevant M&E tools are used to meet planned targets with quality. Project staff, in collaboration with M&E unit, ensures that a tracking and reporting system is in place to document project progress toward desired outcomes. An indicator matrix records data from the field on a monthly basis to measure progress and provide corrective measures where needed. NRC supports quality control measures and accountability towards beneficiaries. In addition, the M&E unit conducts regular field visits to monitor project progress and feedback key findings to project teams. NRC conducts a post-project lessons learnt exercise to continuously improve efficiency and effectiveness in implementation. On a technical level, NRC shelter field and office staff tightly monitor the progress in implementation and provide continuous technical support and monitoring visits to target-households during implementation. Additional technical support is provided if heads of households do not feel comfortable/able/experienced to undertake the shelter works themselves. In the monitoring and evaluation stage, post-distribution monitoring surveys, baseline and endline data collection considers sex and age.Community outreach and beneficiary engagement: during the design phase of the project, male and female households of vulnerable and affected communities especially youth and elderly as well as PWDs were consulted to reflect winter emergency shelter & NFI needs. At the start of the project, essential project information including selection criteria and access to NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism is communicated to the target communities through coordination with municipalities, community representatives, neighbourhood committees, key community members, and local NGOs/CBOs. Project information is communicated to both male and female family members in direct communication and through information leaflets which also informs them about NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism. NRC engages male and female beneficiaries in its regular monitoring visits throughout project implementation to ensure the quality of the intervention. NRC conducts post-distribution monitoring and post-project surveys with beneficiary households to measure the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance and the satisfaction of male and female beneficiaries of all ages. NRC’s local partner IDCO is conducting the activities related to the communication with communities at the beginning of the project, and supports the post-distribution monitoring and beneficiary satisfaction survey at the end of the project. A close coordination with the Shelter Cluster partners ensures to maximize efficiency and impact in target locations and avoids duplication of assistance. In the monitoring and evaluation stage, post-distribution monitoring surveys, baseline and endline data collection considers sex and age.
