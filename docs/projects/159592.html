1 - Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) is a key protection concern in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) and not a new phenomenon in East Jerusalem. Based on the HNO 2020, an estimated 54,407 women have been exposed to GBV in East Jerusalem. Although residents of East Jerusalem theoretically have access to Israeli services and protection mechanisms, this access is in reality severely impeded by the fear and stigma involved in turning to Israeli organizations for help. The Separation Barrier, severing off Jerusalem from the West Bank, has also resulted in jurisdictional ambiguity between the Palestinian National Authority (PNA) and the Israeli authorities, further fragmenting the vulnerable communities in East Jerusalem. The scoping mission conducted by the GBV Sub-Cluster in 2018 found that the most common perpetrator is a current or former husband. Single women and girls report high rates of sexual harassment from strangers. Factors that exacerbate the incidences and intensity of GBV included poverty, societal factors including attitudes towards sexuality, reproductive health and sexually transmitted infections (STIs) as well as entrenched patriarchal norms (which may cause or excuse crimes committed in the name of ‘family honour’), as well as violence by the Israeli police or settlers, discriminatory Israeli polices and structural inequalities. In addition, women living in poor neighborhoods in East Jerusalem stated that male unemployment and disillusionment often leads to substance abuse, again exacerbating existing trends. Overall, the lack of adequate prevention and protection mechanisms and services has had a significant impact on women and girls’ vulnerability to violence.

2 - Activities

In 2017, UNFPA together with PFPPA established two safe spaces (‘one stop shops’) for GBV survivors in existing health clinics in East Jerusalem; one in Bab al Zahra and the other in the Old City. This project will ensure the continuity of these two space spaces and in addition, and will establish 4 counseling rooms in the 4 hospitals in East Jerusalem (Al Makased, Saint Joseph, Palestinian Red Crescent and Augusta Victoria). The project will continue to use health as an entry point in providing multi-sectoral GBV services for women and girls and build on past achievements, including the establishment of the Jerusalem community protection network “HEMAYA”. • Provide case management and SRH services and counselling, including health, psychosocial and legal services, to 1000 GBV cases including women with disabilities identified and treated through the regular services of the counselling centers or health providers’ centers. The project will target GBV survivors and women and young girls at risk inside the old city and Jerusalem suburbs outside the wall, which includes Esawya, Anata, Al Ram, Kufr Aqab.• Provide support for 50 women GBV survivors to start small businesses and empower them with vocational and life skills to enable them to be actively engaged in their communities.• Provide MHPSS services (psychological counselling and individual and group MHPSS support) for 3,000 women and family members at risk through HEMAYA network in East Jerusalem and its surrounding villages, including Esawya, Anata, Al Ram, Kufr Aqab. • Establish Family counselling rooms at 4 hospitals in East Jerusalem (Al Makased, The Palestinian Red Crescent, Saint Joseph, and Augusta Victoria hospital) similar to the Family Counselling rooms established in cooperation with MoH in West Bank hospitals. These counselling rooms will be furnished with medical couch, chairs and desk, ultrasound, file cabinet, AC and other needed procurement.• Provide dignity kits for 2000 women and girls survivors of GBV including women with disability and breast cancer patients and their companion coming to Jerusalem hospitals from Gaza strip and West Bank where they face restricted movement. • Refer 100 GBV survivors to advanced mental health and psychosocial support services through HEMAYA network.• Conduct 3 open recreational days for a total of 300 GBV survivors through HEMAYA network.• Improve the quality, accessibility, acceptability, and availability of the protection and GBV services provided by HEMAYA network in Jerusalem. This will be achieved through conducting 3 capacity building workshops targeting 100 service providers, strengthening cooperation among the HEMAYA network members through promoting the referral pathway.• Conduct quality assessments to assess the survivor’s satisfaction with the provided services (communication with communities). • Conduct needs assessment in East Jerusalem with women, girls, men, and boys at risk of GBV to assess their knowledge about GBV and the available services.• Conduct 150 awareness raising sessions on GBV and available GBV services among 3,000 women and girls at high risk of GBV. • Develop a Mobile Application to share information for survivors allowing a quick and easy way to have access to information about available GBV services (what, where, how). This software will be developed and updated as a mobile application to be downloaded easily by women and family members seeking assistance.• Produce communication materials such as TV& radio spots, printed materials, and social media massages on GBV issues and available services.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No harm” is one of the main GBV guiding principles and approaches according to UNFPA minimum standards and all our partner GBV service providers, including HEMAYA network in Jerusalem, know how to apply these principles in their work with GBV survivors. The service providers have been trained to protect the identity and safety of any GBV survivor by not disclosing any personal information about the survivor or the incident to anyone without the survivor’s explicit permission and consent. Also, they deal with survivors on a case-by-case basis and have robust experience in ensuring GBV survivors are protected from harm and in providing psychological and emotional safety to survivors who may be at high risk from the perpetrators or from their family members due to notions of family honor. In addition, survivors always have the right to choose which service to access.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is mainly designed to provide and expand safe and equal access to adequate multi-sectoral services for East Jerusalem women, girls, men, and boys who are survivors of GBV, including women and girls with disabilities, through UNFPA partner GBV service providers. It will build on two existing safe spaces and increase access to GBV services through the establishment of 4 counselling rooms in hospitals. With the absence of PA jurisdiction in East Jerusalem, implementing the West Bank referral system in this area has proved to be challenging., Based on its strategic plan, and through this project, he HEMAYA network will respond to this issue by providing GBV survivors in East Jerusalem with full access to GBV multi-sectoral services and ensuring referral between its members In addition, the project has been designed based on the experience and feedback from partners about the importance of targeting GBV survivors both inside and outside the Separation Barrier, especially in vulnerable communities. Through its awareness and outreach activities, it will ensure that all targeted communities have a better knowledge of available GBV services and gender and GBV issues.
