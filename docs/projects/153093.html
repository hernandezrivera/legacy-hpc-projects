Project Summary

South Sudan is still facing alarming Humanitarian situation .Recent September, 2018 reported that it is expected that 6.1 million1 people (59% of the total population2) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity3 at the peak of the lean season (July – August), of whom 47,000 were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4).
According to same report the food security situation in the Western Bahr-El Ghazal State is deteriorating in Septemeber 2018 compared to the same time last year (Wau urban in phase 4, Greater Bagari in phase 4, Raga in phase 4 and Jur River in phase 3).The main driving factors includes Insecurity, looting of livelihood assets, poor access to food due torestricted movement to productive farmland,limited access to natural food sources, Seasonally poor feeder road conditions causing low market supply andirregular salary payment to the civil servants. However, with presence of Raga and Greater Bagari in phase 4 and few population amount to 5% IPC phase classification are catastrophe phase. The key message of the IPC report further indicates that between Based on the September 2018 IPC analysis, the overall IPC phase is projected between January to March 2019 to be in( phase 3 in Wau Urban, phase 4 in Greater Bagari, phase 2 in Jur river and phase 3 in Raga County respectively). The main driving factors includes Reduction in purchasing power,decreased projection in humanitarian assistance, Possible returnees – affecting market by increasing local demand, depleting households stock level livestock disease prevalence, limited health facilities and services, limited clean and protected water sources, increase number of returnees, Cattle movement far away from homestead.
Raja and Greater Bagri have limited access to Humanitarian assistance- area faced with persistent fighting.In 2017, interagency assessment conducted in greater Baggari, wau County (Nutrition report 23rd to 26th August, 2017, acute malnutrition prevalence remains at castratophic in Mboro (SAM 16.7%, MAM 21.3%, GAM 38.1%). Famine in Greater Baggari area in Wau is eminent with the nutritional trend. With huge humanitarian response, the nutrition situation improved. However with the recent blockade to these areas for Humanitarian services, the area is expected to face significant food insecurity. Similarly inn 2017, the South Sudan crop watch update (september 2017) reported that the cropping season is characterized by heavy infestation of crop weeds, diseases and insect pests, including Striga (parasitic weed on sorghum and maize) in most areas and presence of relatively new pests particularly Fall Armyworm (FAW) in some areas. Common diseases were sorghum smut and groundnut leaf spot. Similar field reports from COER shares the same issue
Nearly 50,000 people are expected to be highly food insecured. Food insecurity is affecting all categories of people but the most vulnerable people at risk are women and youths. Women in addition to the food insecurity that they are faced are also at high protection risk of been raped, kidnapped or kill as they fend their livelihoods. Youths are at risk of been conscripted into the fighting forces. This is also another trend happening because of lack of access to food.
Despite these challenges faced in South Sudan, there is need for a proactive Food Security livelihood intervention in the short run and need for resilience based activities in the long term so that communities will cover from the shocks.. Central to addressing this food need is consideration of accountability to affected population, centrality of protection in Humanitarian action and multisectoral programming especially coordinating with other clusters
