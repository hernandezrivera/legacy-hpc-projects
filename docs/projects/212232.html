The project aim is to respond to the critical challenges in the access and functionality of basic health services that remain the main challenge affecting all population groups in Syria, according to HNO-Syria 2024, 40% of primary and secondary care health facilities serving over 4.8 million people in need of life-saving health services are either partially functioning or not functional, leaving over 150 sub-districts with 2 million people in need “underserved” by the health sector, mainly due to funding gaps and a shortage of skilled healthcare workers. non-functioning primary health care and specialized facilities increased from 16 percent and 18 percent in 2022 to 24 percent and 31 percent in 2023 respectively. Furthermore, because of damage, many health facilities are operating in structures not fit for purpose concerning capacity, WASH, and infection prevention and control, as well as accessibility – particularly for persons with mobility challenges. Moreover, the devasting earthquake destroyed 67 health facilities in northwest Syria, of which six were destroyed. The earthquake damaged health facilities and fragile medicine equipment and devices such as C-arms, MRIs, and CT scans, many of which were already operating well beyond their normal lifespan due to a lack of eligible funding for the replacement of major equipment. Furthermore, many machines are non-functional due to unilateral coercive measures which prevent the importation of spare parts and expertise needed for repairs. These factors impacted the timely and effective delivery of life-saving trauma and surgical care during the earthquake.
The project will enhance access to basic and specialized healthcare services for all the population in need in the implementation areas, with a special focus on the most vulnerable groups, including :
o Children under five years (U5),
o Women of reproductive age (WRA, ages 15-49),
o Older persons (60+).
o Persons with disabilities.
o People with non-communicable diseases (NCDs).
o IDPs and persons living in areas of restricted access.

MEAL field employee makes a regular visit to the centers to evaluate the work, and directly meet beneficiaries and other stakeholders, he will collect the required data regularly as per a monitoring plan, and use a checklist to check including:1- Readiness of the centers’ infrastructure (electricity - water - building condition - access - waiting room - security and safety)2-Readiness of the organizational structure (signs - records and forms - complaints and feedback mechanism - staff commitment – center hygiene)3-Services (reception and clinics - infection control procedures - pharmacy - laboratory - equipment)then he feeds it back to the project manager, this will help to triangulate the findings as well as to gather impact information from them.The data is evaluated and analyzed every month and is analyzed statistically for proper results and early detection of any outliers.
