Project Summary

This project aims to provide immediate access to safe drinking water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices for 12400 returnees, host communities, and internally displaced persons (IDPs) affected by flooding, conflict, cholera, malnutrition, and lack of access to WASH services. It targets the counties of Panyikang, Malakal, Baliet, and Maiwut in Upper Nile State, along with Fangak, Uror, and Akobo in Jonglei State, projected to experience IPC Level 4 emergencies in 2025. ACRA will coordinate with the WASH Cluster to implement emergency water supply, hygiene, and sanitation activities compliant with SPHERE minimum standards, ensuring community engagement, sustainability, and water quality.
The project aims to mitigate WASH-related health risks and reduce GBV among vulnerable populations, particularly women, children, and individuals with disabilities. It specifically addresses the needs of returnees, IDPs, and host communities, ensuring equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services. The project targets 7600 individuals in Upper Nile State (2508 male children, 2584 female children, 988 adult males, 1140 adult females, 152 elderly males, and 228 elderly female) and 4800 in Jonglei State (1584 male children, 1632 female children, 624 adult males, 720 adult females, 96 elderly males, and 144 elderly females) across the counties of Panyikang, Malakal, Maiwut, and Baliet in Upper Nile State, and Akobo, Uror, and Fangak in Jonglei State. Activities will include supporting 7 nutrition facilities fortified with WASH-NFIs.
The project will establish access to clean water, ensuring each person gets at least 15 liters of safe water daily. It will comply with water safety standards, maintaining a free residual chlorine concentration of 0.5 mg/L and pH levels between 6.5 and 7.5, with zero coliform bacteria per 100 ml. Along with water supply, the project will enhance hygiene and sanitation. ACRA will train 70 community hygiene promoters in essential practices like water safety, handwashing, food hygiene, and environmental sanitation. These promoters will share hygiene messages within households, schools, health facilities, and community spaces, and will identify households with acute watery diarrhea (AWD) to promote cholera prevention.
ACRA will improve sanitation facilities at crowded sites and nutrition centers to reduce open defecation and associated health risks, focusing on the safety and privacy of women and girls. Community education will promote safe water and sanitation practices, distributing hygiene kits, including menstrual hygiene management supplies, and establishing handwashing stations.
To assist at-risk households, ACRA will identify and register the most vulnerable families for WASH non-food item (NFI) distribution, incorporating post-distribution monitoring to evaluate effectiveness and gather insights for future improvements. The project will include GBV risk mitigation, ensuring WASH facilities are safe and accessible for everyone, especially women and children. Local leaders and community volunteers will participate in Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) committees, enhancing oversight in planning and monitoring WASH interventions.
Expected outcomes include rehabilitated flood-resilient water points, increased access for women and girls to improved sanitation, establishment of disability-inclusive facilities, and outreach through emergency WASH kit distributions. The project aims to improve sanitation access at 7 nutrition facilities and support 28,00 children suffering from acute malnutrition with hygiene kits and ongoing activities.
ACRA aligns this project with WASH cluster objectives, seeking to foster community resilience amid floods and crises, reduce health risks, and enhance well-being. By integrating WASH, nutrition, and protection efforts, ACRA aims for a sustainable impact that upholds the dignity and health of the most vulnerable community members.

Implementing Partners

ACRA will directly implement this project without sub-implementing partners; however, the project implementation team will collaborate with static partners engaged in WASH, GBV, and nutrition initiatives to align priorities and avoid duplication, following Sub-national WASH cluster coordination strategies. Key government ministries, including the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation (MWRI) and the Ministry of Health (MoH), will be engaged to ensure alignment with national policies. ACRA will work closely with local authorities through project management committees and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) groups, promoting accountability, sustainability, and effectiveness. Joint monitoring and evaluation will track project progress, allowing for context-specific analysis of the emergency WASH intervention's impact, ensuring transparency and enabling adaptive management throughout implementation.

1. Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan, particularly in Upper Nile and Jonglei States, has led to a significant rise in water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) needs, alongside food insecurity and malnutrition. Around 3.5 million individuals currently lack access to safe drinking water, with Jonglei and Upper Nile being the most affected regions (UNICEF, 2024). The lack of clean water and sanitation facilities has increased the prevalence of waterborne diseases, posing serious health risks, especially to children.Counties in Upper Nile State and Jonglei State that are entry points for returnees and refugees are projected to be in IPC 4, with others like Baliet and Maiwut expected to worsen from IPC 3 to IPC 4 by 2025. Malakal and Panyikang are projected to remain in IPC 4. In Jonglei State, Fangak and Uror are anticipated to stay at IPC 4, while Akobo is also projected to deteriorate from IPC 3 to IPC 4. Ongoing conflicts, mass displacements, and the influx of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia have increased the number of vulnerable households falling into IPC 5 as per the Acute Food Insecurity Analysis for South Sudan (2024). IOM reported on that from August to September 2024, the total number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees in displacement sites and host communities in South Sudan was estimated at 1,844,914 IDPs and 2,895,002 returnees, an increase of 559,398 returnees from the previous round (IOM DTM, November 2024).Amidst this crisis across the country, sustainable WASH initiatives are crucial for enhancing community resilience against future shocks and improving living conditions through immediate interventions are necessary to provide safe water, sanitation facilities, and hygiene education to reduce disease transmission and improve health and nutritional outcomes. As per joint needs assessments and joint fact checking missions, the severe lack of safe drinking water and sanitation has forced communities to rely on contaminated river sources, raising the risk of waterborne diseases including Cholera. This crisis has heightened social tensions and weakened community structures, increasing the vulnerability of women and girls to gender-based violence (GBV), particularly as they travel long distances for water and sanitation. Flooding has devastated livelihoods and food stocks, worsening malnutrition risks among children and vulnerable populations. The humanitarian situation in Upper Nile and Jonglei States is primarily driven by severe flooding and influx of returnees and refugees, leading to overcrowding and limited access to essential resources. Contaminated water sources are contributing to the rise in waterborne diseases, while inadequate hygiene supplies further increase the vulnerability of women and girls to GBV. Ongoing conflict undermines humanitarian efforts and disrupts infrastructure, complicating the provision of essential services. Health facilities are not adequately prepared to handle public health emergencies, including outbreaks of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and typhoid. Many settlements lack proper latrines, leading to increased open defecation, and handwashing practices are often neglected in densely populated areas, resulting in women losing dignity due to inadequate sanitation and hygiene access. Urgent humanitarian assistance is essential to restore critical services, ensure safe water access, implement protective measures against GBV, and provide nutritional support to affected families. It is vital to integrate WASH efforts with GBV prevention, nutrition, and public health initiatives to strengthen community resilience amid the complex crisis and looming catastrophe.

2. Project activities

1 - Rehabilitation of 25 Nonfunctional Water Points: This activity is aimed to restore and sustain access to clean safe water for 12,400 individuals by rehabilitating 31 identified non-functional water points. Improvements access to water will enhance accessibility for persons with disabilities and promote flood resistance. The boreholes are located in Panyikang (3), Malakal (5), Maiwut (4), Baliet (2), Akobo (5), Uror (3), and Fangak (3) Counties. Repairs will involve fixing damaged platforms, ensuring barrier-free access, chlorinating the boreholes, and replacing worn-out parts. Each repair will include safety audits with beneficiaries. The water points will be handed over to Water User Management Committees and local authorities, focusing on priority groups such as those in cholera hotspots. 2 - Training of 31 Water User Management Committees: To sustain the water points, 25 Water User Committees will be formed, comprising 175 individuals (105 females and 70 males). Each committee will receive two days of training on management, meetings, and minor repairs in collaboration with local authorities. ACRA aims for at least 60% female representation in these committees. Training will cover operation and maintenance of water sources, with toolkits provided for practical support. 3 - Participatory Community Hygiene Promotion: ACRA will train 70 hygiene promoters (28 male, 42 female) on hygiene practices, including water safety and handwashing. Promoters will come from returnees, IDPs, and host communities. Training will utilize materials from the WASH Cluster and UNICEF, addressing hygiene behaviors and misconceptions. Hygiene promotion sessions will target the same beneficiaries as the water supply program, focusing on preventing waterborne diseases and GBV awareness. Weekly FGDs will engage households in hygiene planning, and hygiene promoters will distribute key messages and identify vulnerable households for WASH NFI distributions. Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) will be incorporated by providing kits to vulnerable women, including solar lamps for safety. Daily monitoring will be conducted by Hygiene Promotion Supervisors. 4 - Distribution of WASH Non-Food Items (NFIs) to 716 Households: WASH NFIs will be distributed to most vulnerable households, selected through community consultations. Distribution committees will oversee the process, ensuring special consideration for returnees and those with limited WASH facilities. Selection criteria include severely affected households, returnees, extremely poor households, and those with vulnerable individuals. Each household will receive essential items, including PUR sachets, buckets, and soap, promoting proper handwashing and hygiene practices. ACRA will also distribute household water treatment kits and support beneficiaries receiving nutrition services, particularly for children with malnutrition. 5 - Establishment of Gender-Segregated Sanitation Facilities: Two gender-segregated sanitation facilities will be established, ensuring safety and accessibility for both male and female users. Facilities will be reinforced with sanitation awareness and safety audits focused on GBV protection needs. Well-lit and equipped with handwashing points, these facilities will be located in crowded areas. Protection measures will include fixing doors with internal locks and ensuring barrier-free access for persons with disabilities. 6 - Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM): ACRA will conduct a Post-Distribution Monitoring assessment two weeks post-distribution to gather beneficiary feedback on the received items. A brief PDM questionnaire will target 15% (645) of beneficiaries for data collection. The findings will inform future project designs and improvements, with mini-assessments and comprehensive evaluations conducted to assess the effectiveness of the distributed items.
