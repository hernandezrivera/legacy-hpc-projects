Project Summary

The project will support to communities facing unprecedented levels of malnutrition, water-borne/ related diseases as a result of cyclic waves of conflict and seasonal flooding by targeting 24,000
beneficiaries comprised of 7500 girls, 7500 boys, 4500 men and 4500 women – includes people with special needs and youth amongst host and IDP communities in areas where counterpart agencies in Education are providing school feeding program including health centers where nutritional IYC feeding is undertaken by other agencies working in Nutrition/health sectors. Another key objective is to put in place actions to reduce/prevent incidences of GBV, sexual violence and other protection matters facing thousands of women and girls as they undertake their care-giving roles and responsibilities.
These will be undertaken by working closely with schools implementing school feeding programme and partners providing nutrition and health support by rehabilitating water and sanitation facilities(including hand washing), distribution of relevant NFIs and intensive hygiene promotion/cholera preparedness actions.
In addition 100 men, 100 women, 100 boys and 100 girls will be identified to work as volunteers
to support the provision of life-saving, time-critical WASH support in the targeted locations. The wider community will be reached indirectly through community awareness campaigns and messaging.
Cross cutting issues including protection, GBV, HIV/AIDs, Accountability to Affected Populations, protection, gender equity, environmental conservation and youth engagement will be mainstreamed at all stages of the project.
PCO has been operational since 2006 in Jonglei Western Bahr el Ghazal and Lakes through UNICEF among other donors and runs the WASH core-pipeline in Pibor in addition to supporting coordination roles across the three states. The organization has experienced technical teams and relevant coordination offices in Pibor, Wau and Rumbek towns, with logistics, warehousing facilities and strong grass root networks that can be easily deployed, including staff redeployment to proposed areas for scale up interventions.

Needs

Protracted conflict in the country, compounded by cyclical waves of inter-clan violence, territorial, generational/age-set and inter-ethnic violence which is often triggered by revenge killings, children and women abductions, cattle raiding as well as seasonal flooding continues to cause multiple displacements of people, among them boys, girls and youth in Jonglei, Lakes and Western Bahr el Ghazal states. These events have exacerbated the already unprecedented levels of food insecurity with consequent malnutrition-IPC findings of January-March 2019 estimating 5.2 million people estimated to be extremely food insecure; and an estimated 5,712,000 South Sudanese women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2019. This is in addition to a soaring inflation (850 CPI), dysfunctional markets, rapidly deteriorating economy forcing girls and boys to drop out of school in search of food while occasionally girls get married off at an early age or undertake transaction sex(when their husbands/male relatives are off to cattle camps) that exposes them to negative reproductive health consequences and worsening incidences of GBV (IPC, 2018; Protection Trends January-May 2018; WASH Cluster reports 2018). Two thirds of the population in targeted states do not have access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30-minute distance, with many relying on surface water or unprotected water sources. In addition direct observation by PCO and other agencies on the ground indicate that hundreds of Boreholes get damaged during flooding and in some cases vandalized or destroyed during cycles of conflict thereby forcing women and girls to trek for long distances to fetch drinking water during the dry season or ponds or nearby rivers in the dry season thus not only exposing them to protection risks and water borne diseases/potentially cholera (WASH Cluster Updates,. Knowledge, attitude and practice of personal and environmental hygiene, sanitation, cholera preparedness and nutrition services is extremely low. The above scenario is especially prevalent in conflict/floods prone counties such as Pibor Yirol East and Yirol West among others; where only a few schools, markets and health centers have functional sanitation facilities that are poorly oror not used at all. According to IPC projections report of September 2018, In the worst-case scenario, large-scale Catastrophe in Pibor leading to Famine (IPC Phase 5) is likely in protracted absence of humanitarian assistance and conflict-related restrictions to population movement, therefore, delivering large-scale multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance is needed urgently to save lives. Recurrent waves of conflict have presented possible recruitment of most vulnerable children (mainly boys and youth) into armed factions (UNICEF Situation Report, 2018). Internally displaced children and youth have particularity been vulnerable to protection risks such as sexual exploitation, abuse (girls) and physical violence on girls, boys, young women and men including attempted recruitment by armed groups (boys). At least 19,000 children are estimated to have been recruited by armed actors and groups in 2017, up from 17,000 in 2016 (HRP 2018). PCO proposes the provision of life-saving time critical WASH services with which inter-cluster synergies with other actors in Health, FSL, Nutrition, Protection, Education will be explored to ensure holistic response. In addition actions aimed at reducing water borne diseases, increasing cholera preparedness, access to safe water and improving hygiene will be undertaken particularly amongst girls and women in order to reduce protection risks associated with their care-giving responsibilities and GBV. Cross cutting issues including protection, GBV, HIV/AIDs, Accountability to Affected Populations, protection, gender equity, environmental conservation and youth engagement will be mainstreamed at all stages of the project.

Activities

• 100 dysfunctional boreholes and water points in schools offering school feeding programmes, health centers, OTP sites and market centers are rehabilitated and fully functional to ensure equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected 24,000 beneficiaries in Jonglei, Lakes and Western Bahr el Ghazal States. • Rehabilitation of 200 safe and lockable latrines and equipping them with hand-washing facilities to ensure 24,000 targeted beneficiaries practice safe, sanitary, dignified and gender appropriate. Sanitation.• Carry out gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers in Jonglei, Lakes and Western Bahr el Ghazal• Provision of WASH NFIs, inlcluing MHM kits requested from WASH Cluster Pipeline among admitted among 3,000 households SAM at OTP/SC till the household level • Mobilize and train 400 hygiene promoters/ Health/Nutrition workers/volunteers trained on the WASH minimum package of activities• Carry out hygiene promotion campaigns among 24,000 beneficiaries on appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. • Mobilize and train 400 health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within handwashing with soap at critical times at their household level • Provide timely WASH minimum package of interventions targeting 4000HHs on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community in Jonglei, Lakes and Western Bahr el Ghazal

The project will prioritize and focus on communication and information dissemination among targeted beneficiaries through public address campaigns, community meetings, radio talk shows, FGDs, interviews and feedback sessions. Active participation of beneficiaries will be encouraged as well.
