The total number ZOA reached under the Education sector during 2022 was 955 beneficiaries.
• ZOA has plans to secure funds for educational activities during 2023.

12 years of conflict have led to the fracturing and under-resourcing of the education system, consequently limiting the ability of children to access education. COVID-19 and Cholera outbreak have added another layer of complexity, further acerbating education constraints. This project is aimed at increasing access to quality education for the most vulnerable children affected by conflict in target locations and build the capacity of the education personnel. Proposed activities are rehabilitation of schools/classrooms to increase access to protected, safe and friendly learning spaces and to ease overcrowding and encourage smooth return to schools; improvement and rehabilitation of gender-sensitive WASH facilities, provision of education supplies for both students and teachers; provision of school furniture, school bags and teaching/learning materials; teacher training and capacity building; and PSS activities together with the provision of COVID-19 PPE and Cholera awareness. Proposed target locations are underserved areas in the governorates of Aleppo, As-Sweida, Dar’a, Hama, Idleb, Quneitra and Rural Damascus.

Through the project during 2023 ZOA is going to target 2,100 beneficiaries (without double counting), taking the special needs of the PWDs into consideration in its activities. In the event of limited funding, ZOA will prioritize areas falling under higher severity scales. The project targets 7 different schools in 7 different governorates, particularly in rural areas.

The core beneficiaries of the project are 5-17 years school age children as well as teachers, who will benefit from the main project activities, while parents will benefit from awareness sessions. Apart, a Parent Teacher Association will be established per school and 1 parent engagement initiative will be supported per school/community.

The project is in line with the Education Sector’s objectives since it is focusing on the Sector Objective 1 “Availability & Access - Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth”, and Objective 2 “Quality -Enhanced quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth”. Also, the project aims at responding to HRP 2022 Strategic Objective 3.1: “Improved access to basic services, electricity, water, education and health.”

Also, ZOA is aiming to provide humanitarian services with a holistic approach. Therefore, ZOA is either present already in the targeted locations providing different services, or the locations are part of ZOA’s 2023 plans to have intervention in various sectors, so that resilience of beneficiaries is built. ZOA will choose hybrid modality of implementation, some of the activities will be implemented through partners and some self-implementation. ZOA will choose the best positioned partner from its local implementing partners pool to carry out the activities.

3 - Needs

After 12 years of crisis, sufficient availability & access to continuous protective quality education remains a critical need in Syria. An estimated 6.9m people need humanitarian education assistance in severity scale 3-5 areas (HNO Edu Sect. Findings 2022). At national level 18% of the children are out of school (HNO 2022-23 Education chapter). Educational infrastructure is also a major concern & challenge within Syria, there is 1 classroom for every 54 students. This is verified with the latest study which states that “Over a quarter of households with children who were not attending school, did not send their children to school as there was no school to send their children to; & 44% of households with school aged children stating that opening more schools &/or increasing the numbers of classrooms in existing schools is a top priority” (Findings 2022).The number of children in need of education support in Syria will continue to be a great challenge due to protracted crisis, displacements, destruction of schools, closure of learning facilities due to COVID-19, Cholera & economic instability (Education chapter). The CB of the teachers & school personnel as well as students with disabilities are considered part of the most affected population. Therefore, the project aims at working on these gaps. Also, all the selected sub-districts fall under SS 3-5 according to the PiN shared by the sector. Priorities will be discussed with the sector and MoE prior to implementation.

1 ZOA will coordinate with MoE to select schools; 2 Rehabilitation of schools/classrooms - Self implementation through contractors; 3 Improvement & rehabilitation gender sensitive WASH facilities - Carried out by an experienced contractor following WASH SPHERE standards for WASH in schools. ZOA staff will supervise & insure the quality of activities 2 & 3; 4 Provision of education supplies for both students & teachers; 5 Teacher CB - The training will be coordinated with the Ministry of Education to fill the needed gaps; 6 Conducting PSS activities together with the provision of COVID19 PPE & Cholera awareness - Activities carried out in line with approved curriculum & guidelines; 7 PTA & parent initiatives; 8 reporting to Education sector through 4Ws and coordinate MEAL activities with the sector e.g. field visits.ZOA’s MEAL is the responsibility of the country-base Manager of Program Quality. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework indicators and activities with sex, age & disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability & for demonstrating impact. The Project Manager & Education Officer will make frequent site visits to monitor CB/PSS activities, initiatives & contract implementation. Monitoring data will be used to identify opportunities to adjust the implementation strategy of the project thus contributing to improved results.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Children continue to be exposed to multiple protection risks and severe rights violations as an impact of the crisis. Specific vulnerabilities and protection risks for children include domestic and family violence, sexual violence and harassment, forced/early marriage, child labor including in its most dangerous and hazardous forms such as recruitment, begging and heavy industrial work. Disruption of traditional safety nets, lack of livelihood opportunities and sudden displacement have aggravated protection concerns and led to violence, neglect, exploitation and abuse resulting in negatively impacting the psychological wellbeing of children and their families. Children are also facing limited care and support from parents and experiencing separation from their primary caregivers. Additional risks are: 1) Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls and boys (and their caregivers). Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1. Provision of specialized education services; 2. Provision of trainings. 2) Risk to physical safety of education service providers if perceived to be interfering in family-based concerns. Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1. ZOA/partners will continue involvement of coordination with relevant authorities and school administration; 2. ZOA/partners will train and coach educators and staff to ensure that communication with the family is professional and not confrontational.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended, 2. Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation to the service providers ensuring confidentiality of the reporting, 3. Consult periodically with the population receiving the services/assistance, through regular and targeted sessions, including separate sessions with different groups that may be more at risk, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 4. Implement specific complaint mechanism and assign adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 5. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA, 6. Perform post-distribution monitoring.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Risk of renewed conflict in areas where we plan interventions; Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 ZOA/partners will monitor the political & security situation to be able to adjust operationally to this risk. 2 ZOA/partners would adjust operational plans for the locations in question.R2: Risk of access and/or delay of approvals by related ministries, resulting in a delay of the start of operations Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Prioritize implementation through local partners; 2 Selecting well positioned partners; 3 Increase coordination & well planning. R3: Risk of not responding to the real gaps. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Consult stakeholders during design of project; 2 FGDs with different age groups. R4: Risk of fraud & corruption. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Clear communication with target populations on their entitlements using multiple channels; 2 ZOA has a clear conflict of interest policy; 3 Procurement, Logistics, Fraud and Corruption Policies in place for the mitigation; 4 All staff are trained on policies that address fraud, corruption and abuse; 5 Documentation of all financial transactions. R5: Risk of exchange rate instability. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Continuous monitoring of the markets; 2 Transfer funds into Syrian banks according to the needs. 3 Hold maximum possible amounts in Syrian banks in hard currency.
