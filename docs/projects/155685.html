Project Summary

In the Far North region in Cameroon, Boko Haram insurgencies have disrupted the economic, agricultural, and pastoral activities, causing a depletion of livelihoods and household assets. Consequently, the existing chronic vulnerability and structural food and nutrition insecurity have been aggravated.
The main goal of WFP nutrition programme in the Far North region is to contribute to reduction of undernutrition and associated levels of mortality by 2020. WFP through the implementation of the nutrition programme under its Country Strategic Plan(CSP) will facilitate access to appropriate nutritious foods for vulnerable groups to prevent or treat malnutrition and thus makes a crucial contribution to saving lives and strengthening physical resilience to shocks in affected communities.
Therefore, as a nutrition intervention in the Far North Region, WFP will prioritize integrated approaches for prevention and treatment of acute and chronic malnutrition targeting 87,600 children aged 6–23 months through the blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP) in targeted health districts and 2,400 moderate acute malnutrition(MAM) children aged 24–59 months in food-insecure areas with high malnutrition rates for 12 months in 2019.

Needs

Across the country, the 2014 Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) found stunting to affect more than 31 % of children under five, 13 % of them severely. The situation was worst in the Far North where 42 % of under fives were stunted, Preliminary results of the September 2017 SMART survey reveals global acute malnutrition rates of 4.5% for the Far North region. The SMART survey also estimated that three quarters of stunted children in surveyed areas were in the North and Far North regions. The results of the Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Analysis (CFSVA), jointly conducted by the Government (Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development, or MINADER) and WFP in May 2017, revealed that 3.9 million Cameroonians are food insecure, and among them 211,000 are severely food insecure. The Far North region shows the highest figures, with 33.7% food insecure households (around 1,460,000 people), of which 3% are severely food insecure. With this situation, the vulnerable population, particularly children aged 6 – 59 months, are severely exposed to the danger of malnutrition. Therefore, WFP’s nutrition intervention to prevent and treat the Moderate Acute Malnutrition(MAM) is required.
