Project Summary

In alignment to one of the Protection Sectors’ four principles and Protection Sector Objective 2 this project seeks to promote and protect the rights of the most vulnerable and encourage disability inclusiveness in the Protection Sector and to promote a community-based approach to the response and protection mechanisms and facilitate meaningful access to tailored services for persons with specific needs (older persons, persons with disabilities, women and children) with the aim of mitigating exposure to protection risks. This will be done through implementing a comprehensive strategy to develop the capacity of Protection Actors including field level staff and Authorities which include trainings on disability inclusive GBV programming, Gender and Disability intersections, and Disability Inclusive Project Cycle Management. Hosting roundtable meetings on the operationalization of IASC guidelines and Humanitarian Inclusion Standards for Older Persons and Persons with Disabilities, producing good practice documentation, IEC material, video publication and conduct on-sight field level disability mainstreaming guidance for the Protection Sector as well as the Authorities (RRRC, CIC,ISCG, District and Upazila administration). Onsite visits will be conducted with protection actors to showcase accessible shelter models in Rohingya and Host communities to ensure accessibility and safety of camp shelters while mitigating against injury risks. This project will also promote disability inclusion through establishing and empowering (15) disability Self Help Groups (SHGs) in the Ukhia host communities (Haldia Palong, Ratna Palong, Raja Palong) and (25) Disability Support Committees (DSCs) in five camps (8W,11,13, 18, 19) through trainings on advocacy and rights, SHG/DSC concepts, leadership development Humanitarian architecture, accessibility, monitoring and evaluation and through conducting monthly group meetings and exposure visits. CBMG and partner CDD will actively work with SHG/DSCs to advocate for inclusive practices, claiming their rights to meaningful access to humanitarian services including protection services while brokering relationships between SHG/DSCs with mainstream actors to promote the participation of persons with disabilities in Humanitarian project design, implementation, monitoring and community disability awareness sessions. A Baseline study on the status of SHG/DSCs will be done.
Community awareness raising initiatives will be done on Disability and Gender intersections, GBV targeting men, women, boys and girls to address stigma against persons with disabilities while ensuring their inclusion in protection programs. Awareness raising will also be done through 16 days of activism against gender based violence and International Day of Persons with Disabilities commemoration.
This project seeks salaries of Disability technical advisory roles within CBMG and CDD for the effective coordination and delivery of disability inclusion technical support and empowerment of people with disabilities. It also seeks funding for the implementation of activities to steer forward the agenda of disability inclusion and administrative costs.
The project implementation team will be comprised of; Humanitarian Disability Inclusion Advisor x 1– Head of project and responsible for overall project management, Disability Inclusion Co-ordinator (Responsible for field level project co-ordination) x 1, Disability Inclusion Officers (responsible for SHG/DSCs identification/formation of SHG/DSCs in camps and host communities, capacity building activities, provision of disability technical advisory support including onsite technical support) x 2, Disability Rights Advocate Officer x1 (from an established Organisation of Persons with Disabilities responsible for SHG/DSCs engagement), Accounts Officer (responsible for maintaining project accounts) x 1, Support staff (monitoring and evaluation officer, capturing learnings) x 1.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

CBMG-CDD have been responding to the Rohingya crisis together since December 2017, providing rehabilitation services in the camp and host community as well as disability mainstreaming technical advisory to promote inclusion of persons with disabilities across sectors. CBMG and CDD are founding members of the Age and Disability Working Group (ADWG), extending technical support under the Protection sector to ensure the humanitarian response is inclusive of older persons and people with disabilities. CBMG-CDD developed and shared several age and disability guidelines, standard Operating Procedures, IEC materials, tip sheets and provided trainings, technical guidance/advice to humanitarian actors from various sectors. Although some improvements have been noted in disability inclusion by humanitarian actors across different sectors there are still glaring gaps that still need addressing. This observation comes at a time the ISCG has proposed the downsizing of several working groups in 2023 and that they are merged with different relevant sectors or be part of the ISCG coordination structure. In this process, the Age and Disability Working Group (ADWG) risks being dissolved and will no longer fall under the protection sector. Through this project CBMG-CDD seeks to continue the disability inclusion influencing work to ensure that disability inclusion is prioritized in the humanitarian response and more specifically the Protection Sector. People with disabilities in the Cox’s Bazaar Rohingya refugee camps and host communities are facing significant barriers to accessing the humanitarian services they need. Findings from the Age and Disability Survey by Reach (May 2021) found that, fifty two percent (52%) of the Persons with disabilities who constitute 12% of Rohingya population reportedly faced barriers related to mobility in shelters, and 76% around camps, particularly among older persons with disability. The majority of the older people and people with disabilities struggle to access essential services and community participation because of stigma, hilly/flood-prone terrain, lack of adapted facilities and inclusive interventions. Older people and persons with disability are facing difficulties in accessing complaint and feedback mechanisms meaningfully. There is insufficient data on gender-based violence towards women with disabilities and older women in the camps. These findings were also validated by the GTS Research on “People living with disabilities in Cox’s Bazar” (July 2022) which revealed that long wait times, overcrowded health facilities, and far distances to the clinics make access to healthcare difficult and oftentimes outright painful for people with disabilities. In the Rohingya Humanitarian Response, engagement of people with disabilities has been limited as beneficiaries of humanitarian services, however self-representation by people with disabilities is essential in strengthening the voices of people with disabilities so that they have equal opportunities to be active agents in their own development and that of their communities. People with disabilities are the ones who best understand what their needs are, and how these needs can be met. By ensuring that people with disabilities have a high level of engagement in the humanitarian program design and implementation, participation, and influence decision-making over design and monitoring, the effectiveness of the response can be dramatically improved. An SHG/DSC mapping exercise that was undertaken by IOM, CBMG and Organisations of Persons with Disabilities (OPDs) in June 2022 in Cox Bazar revealed that although SHG/DSCs would like to engage more in humanitarian action they lack knowledge and experience and would need capacity building for them to engage more effectively. The GTS research also confirmed that indeed people with disabilities want to share their experiences, discuss their problems and have their voices listened to more.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is well aligned to the rationalization principles, in that it adopts a multi-sectorial approach which will entail active involvement with the ISCG engaging Humanitarian agencies operational in both Refugee and Host communities as well as the Local Authorities to mainstream disability. Project activities which include on-site technical support and capacity building of SHG/DSCs and brokering of relationships with mainstream agencies will be well co-ordinated with the agencies in the camp and Host communities. The capacity building of SHG/DSCs to advocate for their rights to meaningful participation in the response will influence disability inclusion in the long-term creating structures and systems to ensure representation of people with disabilities in Local governments committees and various project committees to promote their rights by themselves for accessing different services. CBMG-CDD will work to ensure continuity of mainstreaming, influencing, advocacy and technical support interventions. Through the project guidance notes, tip sheets, training modules and IEC material will be developed and shared with sectors. CBMG-CDD will participate in ISCG co-ordination, PWG meetings and Local Authority CiC meetings to ensure smooth co-ordination of activities in the host and refugee communities. The project will develop a risk matrix to identify, analyse and mitigate against likely risks. The project will share monthly monitoring and project update report with the Protection Sector Working Group.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection will be a cross cutting theme throughout the project cycle. The project will adopt the four protection principles of prioritizing safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation and empowerment. The project will be designed and adapted to ensure that it is inclusive of and accessible to everyone, including persons with disabilities, older people and gender diverse populations. The project will also identify a Protection Focal Point to report of protection issues in the project and ensure staff participate in protection trainings and adhere to protection guidelines.

The project will ensure that services such as capacity building workshops and meetings are provided close to the community people and are scheduled in consultation with women and men to enable safe access to services. The project will also employ female staff and establish referral system for GBV issues.
