Project Summary

Lack of national health sector blueprint based on the 6 WHO building blocks of Health system and limited government contribution to national health budget of 1.8% as given rise to a huge burden of epidemic prone diseases such as Malaria, Cholera, Meningitis, Anthrax and vaccines preventable diseases such as Measles, Yellow Fever, Whooping Cough, Rubella [Health Cluster] despite fatigued donor contributions of 10% for meeting health needs in South Sudan. Implementation of the R-ARCISS by warrying parties is giving humanitarian actors and the citizen mixed scenarios due to delays in implementation of major milestones as the date of formation of government of national unity on November 12th 2019 is approaching. The ongoing returns of IDPs and refugees to their homeplaces from within and neighbouring countries is increasing the risk of outbreaks and is worsening the already overstressed broken health system with lack of access to basic services such as healthcare, water, education, food etc. from high risks countries [IOM DTM September 2019]. In the past week, from 14 to 20 October, 21 new confirmed Ebola virus disease (EVD) cases were reported from five health zones in two affected provinces of North Kivu and Ituri in the Democratic Republic of the Congo [WHO 22nd October 2019]. With the ongoing Ebola outbreak in Eastern Dr Congo, THESO will continue working with partners in Ebola response preparedness, prevention and control interventions in high risks location of Morobo County which is the nearest border area with closer proximity to locations such as Ariwara approximately 40 miles away with confirmed case on June 30 2019 [USAID 18 July 2019]. Currently, flooding which started in mid-June 2019 continue to cause fatalities, destruction of homes, infrastructure, water sources and crop fields in Uror County. As of October, an estimated 25,000 people have been displaced and severely affected by the floods across the county of Uror [ACAPS October 2019]. The flooding is putting at risks lives of returnees and host communities to waterborne and water related diseases that can leads to outbreaks such as Malaria and Cholera, etc. [ACAPS 20 October 2019]. With the national high mortality rate of under 5 children (90.7/1000) and maternal mortality rate ((789/100,000), vulnerable groups such as children under-fives, women of reproductive age, people with disabilities and mental disorders are being prioritised as high-risk group in this project to be supported during implementation.
In 2019 THESO has been responding to the emergency health needs of 0ver 50,000 direct beneficiaries in Paluony Boma of Yuai Payam – Uror County from March 2019 and in Morobo county from September 2019 with funding from SSHF SA1 and SA2 allocations respectively. In 2020, THESO intend to strengthened the current emergency healthcare deliveries in the counties of Morobo and Uror where it is currently providing lifesaving emergency healthcare services with funding from South Sudan Humanitarian Fund and support from Medair. This 2020 HRP will strengthen emergency healthcare services deliveries and conduct active disease surveillance and response in case of any disease outbreak in Morobo and Uror counties while complimenting and strengthening current services deliveries at fixed facilities & mobile clinics reaching the last miles populations. This 2020 plan aim at contributing to the three cluster objectives addressing the major causes of morbidity and mortality, Capacity building, diseases prevention & control as well as outbreaks response ensuring lifesaving emergency healthcare meeting 2020 HRP. THESO in collaboration with government structures at State and County level, will use health cluster strategic approach of PHCC, PHCU & Mobile Clinics to provide lifesaving emergency healthcare services to the IDPs, returnees and host communities in 2020.

2 - Activities

Output 1: Reduce morbidity and mortality due to epidemic prone diseases.1. Prepare emergency preparedness and contingency plan for epidemic diseases outbreak and response with county health departments2. Deploy mobile medical teams and RRM ensuring case management of selected diseases in all interventions3. Provide immunisation against vaccine preventable diseases to children under-fives and women of reproductive age4. Ensure quality inpatients care in 2PHCCs 5. Timely distribution of emergency health kits (drugs & equipment) and consumables to all intervention sites 6. Train 30 health workers on integrated diseases surveillance and response (IDSR)7. Train 30 health workers on rational use of essential health care commodities, cold chain management and handling of vaccines 8. Conduct continuous epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control and submit timely IDSR reports.9. Conduct social mobilisation and sensitisation of communities on epidemic diseases and other diseases of public health concerns using approved IEC materials. Output 2: Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations with dignity. 1. 5000 vulnerable populations provided outpatients consultations 2. Health facilities (2PHCCs, 2PHCUs) operational seven days a week providing health care services 3. Scale up rapid response mechanism and mobile clinics outreaches in hard to reach locations 4. Conduct facility based skilled birth services to expectant mothers by skilled birth attendance 5. Provide antenatal care and postnatal care services to pregnant and lactating mothers6. Increase access to BeMOC and CeMONC services 7. Provide CMR services to suspected clients. 8. Ensure management of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications at designated health facilities9. Train skilled birth attendants on BeMOC & CeMONC services Output 3: Increase access to psychosocial and mental health services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and client with mental health disorders. 1. Train 30 health workers on CMR services using standard treatment protocols 2. Train 30 health workers on MHPSS services using standard guidelines 3. Provide MHPSS and CMR services to clients at selected health facilities

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

THESO approach to feedback and complaints handling is part of a broader organisational commitment to accountability, that promotes information sharing, transparency, participation and learning with project participants. Feedback and complaints mechanisms are based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use. All feedback is recorded by Accountability and Feedback Mechanism focal points at each project location, responses are given to community members or groups and a monthly report of the feedback received and responses given is sent to THESO head office in Juba. THESO has identified CRM focal persons in each county project with specific responsibility for supporting the mechanism, such as Quality Assurance Officer, Accountability Officers or Community Animators.
