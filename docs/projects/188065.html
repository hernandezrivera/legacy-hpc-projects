Project Summary

White Hands (WH) proposes a 24-month action that uses a flexible integrated programming approach that includes agriculture and livestock interventions, using a pre-positioned in-kind, voucher, and cash for Work (CFW) assistance modalities.
This program is a planned action, that contributes to the second sector objective of the FSL cluster which is "Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income-generating opportunities to save and sustain lives" and aims to increase the resilience and livelihood opportunities for most vulnerable male and female farmers and livestock breeders of both IDPs and affected host communities, living in the sub-districts of Afrin, Jandairis, Azaz, Mare and Daret Azza (Aleppo Governorate), and the sub-districts of Harim, Dana, Salqin, Kafr Takharim, Qourqeena, Armanaz (Idleb Governorate) in NW Syria, in order to revitalize of agricultural and livestock sectors in the target locations in the medium-long term.
The program comprises the following Key intervention areas:
1. 9,000 Targeted households (3000 olive farmers and 6000 winter & summer crop farmers) are able to resume agricultural activities, increase household income, and improve consumption through i) provision of agricultural inputs, including improved access to water; olive harvesting and pruning, and ii) improving/restoring agricultural infrastructure (for example irrigation/drainage), IV)Cash for harvest, V) extension services, VI) purchasing wheat.
2. 3,000 Targeted households (2,000 sheep breeders own1-10 sheep/or goats and 1000 cow breeders who own one cow), living in the sub-districts of Jandairis, A'zaz, Harim, Mare', and Daret Azza are able to increase household income and maintain livestock activities through i) provision of livestock inputs (compound fodder- mixed (forage + pellet)); ii) associated training, iii) vaccination campaigns per health care calendar (2 campaigns, 10500 head per campaign owned by 3,000 livestock breeders).
3. 1000 Targeted households (1000 women & men over 18 years, of them, 500 of the host community and 500 IDPs) stabilize, maintain or increase their income through Cash-for-Work activities, which build and strengthen community-related value chain infrastructure, disaster risk reduction, mechanical pest & vol control, olive trees harvesting, Purification of wheat fields, and afforestation.
4. Rehabilitation of related value chain infrastructure, irrigation channels, Mills, bakeries, storage, fodder mixers, feeder roads, and silos.
WH will identify conduct a multi-sectoral needs assessment to verify needs, priorities, and gaps, determine the level of severity and identify specific vulnerable groups within communities. To ensure the most vulnerable households are selected, WH conducts a socio-economic vulnerability assessment scoring exercise based on aggregated-vulnerability scores. The beneficiaries will be prioritized according to clear vulnerability and eligibility criteria.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 13,000 households/ 65,000 individuals (9000 HHs supported with agriculture interventions, 3000 HHs supported with livestock interventions, and 1000 HHs supported with Cash for Work assistance modality).
Note: This is a planned intervention (No secured fund yet).
50% of this program will be implemented during 2022, while the other 50% are planned for 2023.

4 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO FSA, 14.6M people are in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance. Of this, 7.3M are female & 4.1M are PWDs. Generally, the agriculture sector in the targeted locations had severely impacted by the ongoing crisis in Syria. High cost and limited availability of agriculture inputs, agricultural services, degradation of livelihoods and income-earning opportunities, COVID-19, and other climate-induced shocks such as floods and erratic rainfall have all contributed to the worsening situation in targeted locations. The agriculture sector has had significant losses since the beginning of the crisis in 2011 affecting both crop and livestock production with a noted decline of 49% and 51% respectively. Widespread damage to irrigation systems and shortages of labor and quality inputs such as seeds, fertilizers, and fuel due to imposed sanctions on Syria has affected the country’s production. It is estimated that during the first 3 years of the crisis sheep numbers fell by 45%, goat numbers by 30%, and cattle numbers by 40%. Women-headed HHs & PWDs remain to be one of the most vulnerable populations within the target locations, as they are restricted by both movement and access to the labor market. Income-generating small businesses had been crippled totally or partially as a result of socioeconomic situation, spending of savings, and the accumulation of debt that financial inability to continue the income generation businesses.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. Support 3000 Livestock breeders who own 1 cow or a maximum of 10 sheep/goats with compound fodders. Each sheep/or goat will be supported with 0.75kg of compound fodder & each cow will be supported with 10kg compound fodder daily sufficient for 4 months. 2. Establishment of 4 mobile veterinary clinics & provision of veterinary services and vaccinations campaigns (Enterotoxemia, Smallpox, and Foot-and-mouth). 3. Distribution of agricultural inputs through the most appropriate modalities for 6000 winter & summer crop farmers & supported them with NPK & Urea fertilizer 46% and 50 L of fuel for supplementary irrigation. 4. Support 3000 small and mid-sized olive framers (owned 3 to10 dunum) with agricultural inputs, agricultural services, and harvesting support (each olive farmer will be supported with 100 kg compound NPK, 2 L pesticides, 1 Scissor, 1 Hand Saw & 12 tins (capacity of 16 liters). 5. Delivery of training program on the best practices for livestock breeding & GAPs. 6. Wheat, olive, and agriculture infrastructures such as will be rehabilitated. 7. The procured wheat from farmers will be processed into flour to be distributed to bakeries to subsidize bread prices and increase the availability & accessibility of bread in the targeted communities. 8. CFW: WH is seeking to improve or protect agriculture and bread-related infrastructure or assets through the implementation of CFW activities, which will enable the provision of 1000 labor opportunities ($6/day/beneficiary).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety). L: M/ I: H. MS: Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set up, postponed, or canceled, Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding, Training to staffs on establishing and managing distributions and crowded management, Application of checklist at distribution sites, Ensure that inputs/vouchers distribution is completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight, Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk, Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women and Set-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs. Risk2: Child labor (involving children in CFW or farming activities). L: M/I: H. MS: WH will be sure that the staff working on this project are trained and fully aware of child safeguarding and child protection. Risk3: Tension between HC, IDPs & returnees. L: H/I: H. MS: Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs & not based on status, Selection criteria are transparent & groups are fully informed, engage with the communities to sensitize the population, Make an effort to target a % of the vulnerable HC in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs, Establish feedback mechanism, conduct follow-up visits, and Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts& Address any SEA complaints.Risk2: Agricultural/Livestock Inputs distribution not safe to access or not accessible, especially for women (exposure to GBV risk), elderly, and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design, and implementation phases; Make sure that distance of food distribution points remains at a minimum distance as outlined in-country policy document as to ensure that this does not overburden women and adolescent girls with the extra workload in particular.Specific attention concerning complaints/feedback mechanisms (CFMs) will be given to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Following the ‎PSEA Network recommendations, WH's focal points will ensure the availability of information to the beneficiaries ‎concerning CFMs through informative sessions at the beginning of the activities, making sure that beneficiaries are ‎aware and engaged, if necessary in improving the design, monitoring, and review of the CFMs.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: WH has full access to the targeted communities & the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the project. Risk: Escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in the targeted area affecting WH’s ability to operate and access it. L: M/I: H. MS: WH will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities.Assumption2: The project does not create distortions in the local economy.Risk2: As a consequence of the project, there is a shortage of program inputs in the area and the prices increase. L: M/I: M. MS2: Vendors will be identified at the initial stage of the project and provided with necessary information regarding expected sales and the amount to be distributed to allow the supply of sufficient stocks. The price of the items should be agreed upon with vendors and advertised to the beneficiaries. Risk3: Spread of COVID-19 pandemic cases in NWS. L: M/I: M. MS3: Follow strict preventive measures, building on the FSL guidelines and recommendations. Those will be reflected in each activity, All project teams and staff will receive sensitization on COVID-19 precautions. Risk4: Aid diversion by armed groups, including looting of warehouses. Probability: L/Impact: M MS4: Invest in sound security measures at the warehouses, Train the staff on how to deal with aid diversion, Establish timely reporting mechanisms & info sharing with other actors on the ground, donors, and clusters.
