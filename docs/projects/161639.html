Project Summary

GBV integrated activities carefully design to address GBV both in protracted crisis situation and new liberated communities, this include lifesaving services that will address the needs of new arrivals and survivors (including child survivors) such as safe spaces/shelters, CMR, Case management, MHPSS, Frontline Mobile services, procurements and distribution of menstrual, dignity kits, cultural appropriate clothing’s , access to justice as well coherent field coordination and updating referral pathways. Other complementary activities will focus on strengthening community resilience and promoting GBV/SEA risk mitigation mechanism in humanitarian setting. Our target is to reach 60% (795,466) of the People in needs (1,325,776) in the BAY States, owing from the fact that UNFPA and its implementing partners reached 40% of all GBV response which contributed to 80% of targeted Needs in 2019. Special emphasis will be put on strengthening joint inter-agency coordination, M&E, capacity building, assessment and partnership that will translate to better outcome.

1 - Needs

Despite the progress attained in 2019 response, the current vulnerability scale rating associated with the current humanitarian context in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States, shows the disproportionally of Women and girls who are at risk of life-threatening health consequences resulting from GBV. Most of the areas affected has minimal presence of State authorities including police, judiciary and health systems especially in the remote environments. LGAs have been categorized into different levels of severity based on three severity indicators: number of vulnerable women and girls, number of single females headed households and density of women and girls. These indicators were combined to rank the LGAs into five different levels of severity as agreed by the ISWG and endorsed by the HCT. The availability of data at LGA level had to be considered when selecting indicators. The severity mapping and analysis has been validated with expert opinion by sector partners. Data sources for indicators and needs analysis include vulnerability screening, the DTM and a secondary data review on various assessments conducted, including the multisectoral needs assessment (MSNA).The severity analysis demonstrates that 20% of LGAs (13/65) are in the ‘emergency’ category. 22% of LGAs (14/65) is in ‘crisis’, 17% in ‘stressed’ (11/65), 25% in ‘minimal’ (16/65), and the remainder are classified as ‘normal’. As Abadam and Marte remain hard-to-reach by international humanitarian actors and there is no data on severity, the entire population has been considered as in need of humanitarian assistance. The severity indicators used in formulating the severity map are in one way or the other very much determined by the number of IDPs. As such, the percentage of IDPs used in estimating the people in need in the remaining population is also linked to the severity map. The results of this analysis show that the density of PiN at LGA level concurs with the severity map. In host communities or IDP camps were humanitarian actors provides services, the capacity of GBV service providers is grossly inadequate with limited awareness of both the survivors and the community where the live, this translate into attitudes and beliefs of community members that hindered access to services. Generally, both incidents and prevalence of GBV are under-reported. The poverty index was used to estimate the most vulnerable people in host communities, by looking at the percentage of people living below the poverty line (less than $1 a day, United Nations Multidimensional Poverty Index, Nigeria). It is assumed that poverty and economic challenges are underlying factors to GBV. The percentage of IDPs at the LGA level was applied to the population from host communities and hard to reach areas below the poverty line to calculate the people in need. The underlying assumption is that high percentages of IDPs in the host community puts additional stressors (e.g. sharing of resources) on the host population, which can increase the risk of GBV. The percentage of IDPs at LGA level was applied to calculate the people in need out of the remaining population.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project ensure the affected persons and the beneficiaries are involved in planning , implementing and monitoring of the programs. The survivor centered approach is an integral component of this project were the survivors drive the aid delivery process toward personalized need. The production of contents of the dignity kits will be purely managed by women themselves who are beneficiaries of the project, The packaging and distribution of the kits will managed by the women led organizations. As part of the accountability to affected population the following steps will be taken• Consultation with the affected population, especially women and girls on culturally appropriately contents • Community Engagement on menstrual hygienic education and awareness • Engaging women in key decision making process like the vulnerability assessment• Conduct Post distribution monitoring to guide the next batch of production• Use of locally source materials and engaging women and girls in production process
