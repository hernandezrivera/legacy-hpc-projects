Project Summary

This project will be targeting 50,000 IDP, host communities and people returnee from Uror, Fangak & Pigi in Jonglei state with significant impact aiming at improving continuation support at helping children and young people affected by armed conflict. With provision of comprehensive Case Management and FTR, process, organize structural and semi-structural and strengthening access to community led Child Friendly Spaces, Psycho-social Support, developing healthy and safe coping mechanisms to reduce the risk of children and adolescents resulting in recruitment by armed groups/forces, becoming street working children, or being left without care takers in hard-to-reach of Uror, Fangak and Pigi counties as prioritized areas, improve protection of conflict affected males, females under the age of 18 years and internally displaced children, especially separated and unaccompanied children are better protected through: 1) comprehensive case management system and provision of dignity kits and life-saving material assistance; 2) building the capacity of the community-based Child Protection network on mechanisms and approach; 3) improve Referral system for GBV and CP cases; 4) provide alternative care support to children without care givers, conduct Family Tracing and Reunification; 5) CP mainstreaming into Education, WASH, Health, and FSL and conducting awareness raising to prevent and respond to child protection issues.

Needs

The persistence of conflict between armed groups, inter-communal violence and displacement continued to cause protection concerns to Boys, Girls, Women and men in Jonglei during the first quarter of 2018. Reflective of the intensification of fighting between armed groups in Uror, Ayod, and the Greater Akobo in January and February 2018. The prolonged effects of conflict and displacement have majorly affected women and children in the locations prioritized. The counties targeted in Jonglei (Uror, Fangak and Canal/Pigi ) jointly have populations of 214,140 people, (NBS, Apr.15) of which more than 50% are IDPs. (IOM DTM, ’18). Jonglei recorded as hosting the greatest IDP sites without access to work. The target group includes unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children associated with armed forces and armed groups, children who are victims of or at risk of GBV, domestic violence, physical and sexual abuse, GBV survivors, women and girls at risk of GBV. According to the Humanitarian Situation Bulletin on South Sudan (Issue 15/10 October 2017 and July 2018); following increased threats of insecurity approximately, 25,000 people (women, girls, boys, men, elderly and disabled) were displaced at risk of facing critical protection threats including GBV, family separation, with boys and girls targeted by armed forces and armed groups. Based on the Data obtained from the protection cluster, the present program covers the humanitarian gaps which have been identified with the aim of guaranteeing a minimum package of protection services for the conflict-affected population. Assessment findings in Uror, Fangak and Canal/Pigi underlines a high level of UASC, child survivors of violence, abuse and exploitation and GBV cases. IDP children are not attending school and considering the high presence of armed actors, they represent an easy target for armed groups' recruitment. There are no recreational activities in the area and the psychosocial needs of these children and young people are not addressed. There is need for timely protection services (on gender-segregated latrines, access to hygiene and dignity kits, etc.): from the Focus Group Discussions held with IDP community, GBV concerns included early and forced marriage, domestic violence and SGBV cases. No any service available in order to guarantee PSS to the GBV survivors. CAO through its intervention with UNICEF in Uror, Fangak, Canal/Pigi observed high child recruitment, abuse and exploitation are still main concerns. UASC have been identified and there is still consistent need to proceed with further identification, registration and reunification with their families. In the hard to reach prioritized areas locations, CP interventions are very limited while the needs are constantly increasing. Thus, CAO will scale up its activities in protection in order to complement the current multi sectorial interventions and address the gaps in coordination with the CP and GBV sub clusters. CAO intends to respond to the needs of the youth, who have been exposed to armed activities. CAO will address the identified needs and achieve the expected outcomes through a response strategy which includes:, improve protection of conflict affected males, females under the age of 18 years and internally displaced children, especially separated and unaccompanied children are better protected through: 1) comprehensive case management system and provision of dignity kits and life-saving material assistance; 2) building the capacity of the community-based Child Protection network on mechanisms and approach; 3) improve Referral system for GBV and CP cases; 4) provide alternative care support to children without care givers, conduct Family Tracing and Reunification; 5) CP mainstreaming into Education, WASH, Health, and FSL and conducting awareness raising to prevent and respond to child protection issues.

Activities

Conduct Identification and documentation of UASC and children with protection concerns 300 (150 Boys and 150 Girls) in Uror, Fangak and Pigi. Conduct assessment and support to Girls and Boys who are in vulnerable situations through case management and referred to specialized services. 200 ( 100 Boys and 100 Girls) in Uror, Fangak and Pigi. Provide training to CP staff, volunteers on Child Protection in Emergencies and case management 60 ( 40 men and 20 Women) in Uror, Fangak and Pigi. Conduct continuing Family tracing and reunification of 200 (100 Boys & 100 Girls) UASC who their parents are successfully traced. Support the vulnerable children through identification and placement of UASC children in alternative care 100 (50 Boys & 50 Girls) in Uror, Fangak and Pigi. Support staff and CBCPN through Capacity building training on CPiE training 200 ( 120 Males & 80 Females) in Uror, fangak and Pigi. Provide parenting skills session to caregivers and community members based on existing tools 300 (150 Males & 150 Female) 100 Per County. Provide structured community-based psycho-social support activities to the children in the community existing structure 40,000 Children (20,000 Boys & 20,000 Girls) in Uror, Fangak and Pigi Provide life skills and resilience programming to adolescent girls and boys 1000 (500 Boys and 500 Girls) in Uror, Fangak and Pigi. Mainstream Child Protection and psycho social support in schools through capacity building events 10,000 (5000 Boy & 5000 Girls) in Fangak, Uror and Pigi. Awareness raising on prevention and response to Child Protection issues through campaigns in Uror, Fangak and Pigi targeting 20,000 People at the community level or through outreach. Establish peer to peer support groups/ clubs/ for adolescents at the schools and TLS in Uror, Fangak and Pigi.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

This project was design in consultation with community members. The beneficiaries will be briefed through project inception meetings before project kickoff, CAO will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational implementation procedures, to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organization and its affected populations over information provision.CAO CP team will actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that response and complaint are streamlined, and deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from affected individuals regarding violations and/or physical abuse that may have human rights, psychological or other implications, and employ measures for handling these should be adapted accordingly. The affected populations will be allow to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have reached on monthly basis.

CAO will ensure meaningful and safe women and girls’ participation during assessments to ascertain that its interventions are context appropriate. Speaking to local people or colleagues with in-depth knowledge on women and girl’s problems is an important criterion to understand sensitive and politicized issues regarding women and girls’ decision-making power in regards to risks associated with protection issues during the implementation.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The confidential complain mechanism will be establish and all the issues that may arise will be addressed without discrimination. At every population catchment point during hygiene promotion campaigns, CAO will position a complain desk and box in a visible area and place a caseworker from its protection unit to receive complains. Complains received will then go through the case management process in a bid to generate appropriate solutions and feedback.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CAO will work closely with local leaders and authorities to create community awareness on the targeting and selection criteria. It will also engage some host community and refugee members during the identification of project beneficiaries in a bid to build a strong relationship between refugee or IDPs and their host community members. The monitoring team will give the priority to the community who are also the beneficiaries. All age group and gender will be considered during the monitoring.
