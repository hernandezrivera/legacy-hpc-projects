Project Summary

The project aims to improve food security and livelihoods for IDPs and vulnerable households in Harim and Salqin camps, Maarrat Misrin, and Azaz in Aleppo and Idleb.
Objectives:
1. Free bread distribution for displaced people.
2. Promote CFW bread-baking and distribution jobs.
3. E-cash for food transfers to help IDPs and vulnerable households get food and other necessities.
4. To provide agricultural supplies, financial assistance, and guidance to local farmers.
5. To improve livelihoods by giving families egg-laying hens and fodder batches.
The project will reach 5841 households (29205 people), including 560 PWDs people. The project will implement free bread distribution, E-cash for food transfers, and agricultural and livestock programs.
Timeline: Contracting with vendors and bakeries, hiring field staff, and renting cars will take the first month of the eight-month project. Bread distribution will last 7 months, cash transfers will occur monthly for 6 rounds, and agricultural and livestock support will occur for 6 months.
Key Deliverables:
1. 6,933 bread packs will be baked and distributed daily in 25 camps.
According to the Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) group, each person should get 200 grams of bread per day. We will distribute 800-gram bread packs to families of various sizes to meet this recommendation. For the entire month, families with 1-4 members will receive 1 pack, 5-8 members will receive 2 packs, and 9+ members will receive 3 packs. Daily 800-gram packs will be distributed for 30 days. This distribution program will ensure that all family members get the recommended daily bread intake. 800 grams of loaves make up the bread pack.
2. IDPs and vulnerable households will receive six rounds of Conditional Unrestricted Vulnerability-based Cash e-vouchers worth $120 per household per round.
500 local farmers will receive agricultural supplies, financial assistance, and guidance.
Mixed-farming will reduce the risk of crop loss from climate change-related diseases and insect invasions.
Parallel lines will divide the half dunum of land. These crops are chosen by need.
The selection of crops is based on a need assessment that considers local farmers' experience, soil suitability, and natural water sources. Peas, beans, and spinach will be grown on the same land unit in polyculture.
4. Support for 500 families with 10 egg-laying hens and fodder for 6 months, including veterinary services. For six months, recipients will give 500 families 10 eggs a week to improve nutrition and social cohesion.
In order to apply voucher schemes for all beneficiaries, especially to ensure that beneficiaries supported by (Poultry kit) will give 10 eggs weekly/free to another family, we will use vouchers mechanism for receiving (Agriculture kit, poultry and fodder)
-500 vouchers for agriculture kit distribution (on-off)
500 in-kind poultry lit (10 hens) and 500 vouchers * 6 months for fodder = 3000 MWL will support 500 families to provide them with 10 Egg-Laying-Hens as one-time with fodder batches for 8 months and veterinary services. The kit composition/response package for SO2 and SO3 matches the poultry flock (10 hens, 1 rooster). Meanwhile, those families supported by poultry kits will give another 500 families under (specific criteria) 10 eggs weekly/free for 6 months to improve social cohesion and nutrition.
Afrin's Field Logistic assistant will ensure accurate procurement and contract documentation and transparent procurement. He or she will research suppliers, tender bids, vet suppliers and contractors, and process procurement approvals.
Expected Results: 1. Improved food security for displaced people through free bread distribution.
2. Improved food and basic needs access for IDPs and vulnerable households.
3. Improved livelihoods from agricultural support.
4. Improved livelihoods by supporting livestock.
5. Support egg-laying hen families to improve community nutrition and social cohesion.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year.In 2023,an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent of the total population.At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity, an alarming increase of people at risk who could shift to become food insecure during 2023 if the crisis continues at same worsening levels.There is a need for emergency provision of agriculture production inputs for vulnerable farmers and livestock breeders affected by the decline in agricultural production during the last two seasons,including training on climate-smart agriculture approaches with crop inputs and access to irrigation water. There is a similar need of livestock production inputs (fodder and veterinary medicines and vaccination).Livestock has also not been spared from the impact of the prevailing water shortage and scarcity, limited access to livestock production inputs especially feed,and the unfavourable weather conditions.Unfavourable 2021/ 2022 rainfall season resulted in a sharp decline in pasture growth and development, increased demand for fodder,limited supply and high prices especially imported feed and this trend is likely to continue if the 2022/2023 rainy season is not favourable

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

MWL remains committed towards mainstreaming protection through all of its projects using several practical mechanisms. Ensuring the integration and voice of the local community throughout every stage of the project is key in commitment to the Do No Harm principle. By conducting regular consultations, as well as establishing mechanisms to gather feedback and complaints, MWL will have the tools to encourage community ownership of the project. Protection Mainstreaming and GBV will be essential principles for MWL planned interventions during all stages of the project taking in consideration equal access, safety and dignity and ensuring accountability to affected newly IDP in temporary camps and host communities. The targeted beneficiaries will be selected based on defined vulnerability criteria to ensure that the needs of the most vulnerable (especially elderly, women, PWDs, pregnant women) had been covered.MWL will ensuring accountability toward the population by applying the different tools set by the M&E team and Compliant Mechanism and ensuring access the vulnerable people to services without any discrimination that is originally not allowed in the MWL Code of Conduct.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

1- Posters will be published to inform the targeted populations that all services provided under the project are free of charge. This also help to ensure transparency and trust with the targeted population.2- Adherence to Zero-Tolerance principle, MWL will take a strong stance against any form of exploitation, abuse, or mistreatment, whether it be by staff, partners, or any other individuals or groups involved in the project. This includes any form of gender-based violence, discrimination, or misconduct. This will be communicated through posters and flyers to ensure that the beneficiaries are aware that such behaviors will not be tolerated, and that appropriate action will be taken if any complaints are received.3- Complaints mechanism will be shared with the beneficiaries to raise any issues, feedback or complaints directly through the MWL's complaints mechanism. This includes a dedicated hotline and email address that beneficiaries can use to raise their concerns, as well as a set of procedures and protocols that will be put in place to ensure that complaints are handled efficiently and effectively. The complaints mechanism is designed to be easily accessible and user-friendly, and MWL will work to build trust and confidence among the beneficiaries so that they feel comfortable using it.
