Project Summary

The project aims to Improve the protective environment and make accessible the comprehensive child protection supports in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action for conflict affected Syrian children based on the identified needs, gaps in the proposed communities. Orange aim to reduce CP concerns and establish safe friendly spaces for children with a focus on community-based approaches including awareness raising, psychosocial assistance through community centers and outreach mechanisms.
Orange will establish one static Child Friendly Space (CFS) and will have 1 mobile unit in Idlib Governorate. The CFS will be a hub for the provision of static services and the movement point for one mobile team across the towns, communities and the nearby areas of Idlib. The project aims to reach about 3055 beneficiaries including 1000 through psychosocial support activities within the CFS and through the outreach mobile unit team, 200 through case management, 40 through capacity development, 1500 beneficiaries, 1200 children and 300 adults through awareness raising sessions and 100 parents/caregivers through parenting sessions and 15 through child protection committees/networks.
The project will supervise for reduce the risk and ensure safety for girls and boys with protection monitoring and its action taking mechanisms, service mapping activities, and referrals to the other specialized services.
Eventually, the main project outcome that Orange aspire to achieve is improvement of psycho-social wellbeing of children through increased availability and access of child protection mechanisms for the conflict affected child populations. This will be done through achieving the following result and activities:
Result 1.1: Children have access to child protection services;
Activity 1.1.1: Provide Structured Psycho-Social Support: In this CFS, 750 children, 375 boys and 375 girls will receive these services during the project period.
Activity 1.1.2: Carry out Case Management:. 200 children (100 boys and 100 girls) will receive case management services during the project period.
Activity: 1.1.3: Conduct Child Protection Mobile Outreach Support: During these activities, 250 children (125 boys and 125 girls) will receive mobile outreach activities during the project period.
Result 1.2: Caregivers/parents and/or relevant community members are sensitized about child protection:
Activity: 1.2.1: Carry out Parenting Skills Sessions: 100 parents/caregivers (50 males and 50 females) will receive these sessions. The sessions will be conducted by the PSS facilitators, Social workers.
Activity 1.2.2: Conduct Community Awareness Raising Sessions: During these sessions 300 community members will be receiving these services (150 men and 150 women in addition to 1200 children.
Result 1.3: Local child Protection frontline workers capacity increased:
Activity 1.3.1: Capacity Building for Frontline Child Protection Service Providers: For the purpose of these Orange will be utilizing interagency/child protection sub-cluster training guidelines to deliver these trainings. 40 individuals (20 women and 20 men) 15 for child protection network and other service providers and 25 Project staff will receive this package of trainings during the project period.
Activity: 1.3.2: Establish a Child Protection Committees: These committees will meet regularly discussing the trends of child protections and advocating for proper response. Orange aims at capacitating 15 (8 women and 7 men) members of the community to be part of this child protection committee.
Activity 1.3.3: Provide Service mapping and referrals to other specialized services: During the project period, Orange will identify, and refer 200 children 100 boys and 100 girls during the project period.

Needs

Children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of killing and injury, recruitment and use in hostilities, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access by parties to the conflict. During the first 9 months of 2018, the UN verified 2,354 such violations against children, in 13 of 14 governorates of Syria (M4M 4Syria, December 2018), illustrating persistent trends of violence though not the full scope of conflict-related violence affecting children. This included more than 1,300 verified incidents of killing or injuring of children. Respondents in assessed communities reporting explosive hazard contamination in Syria identify adolescent boys and pre-adolescent boys as the groups most at risk of death or injury from accidents, with incidents reported while children were playing and going to school. (HNO,2019 DRAFT)The ongoing or recurrent conflict environment experienced by many boys and girls over the past seven years has profoundly affected their psychosocial well-being – weakening their sense of security, purpose, control and significance, or exercising social agency, and causing profound distress. Many are without civil documentation to prove their identity, access basic services and safeguard their rights. It is estimated that more than 2.6 million children remain displaced and separation from caregivers is a reported child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria putting children at risk of exploitation, abuse or neglect. Events leading to family separation indicate how related protection and child protection issues increase vulnerability of affected boys and girls, with death or disappearance of caregivers, disappearance of children, separation due to displacement or conflict, child marriage and child recruitment among cited reasons for family separations. Respondents in those assessed communities where discrimination in the delivery of humanitarian assistance emerged, identified unaccompanied girls as the most likely group to face such discrimination. (HNO,2019 DRAFT)Child labour interferes with children’s schooling by depriving them of the opportunity to attend school, obliging them to leave school prematurely, or requiring them to attempt to combine school attendance with excessively long and heavy work. (HNO,2019 DRAFT)Child marriage is reported in 12 out of 14 governorates mainly affecting adolescent girls, though adolescent boys are also at risk. Girls and boys are married early as a household negative coping strategy to “mitigate” protection risks and to respond to the worsening economic situation of families.Child marriage was discussed by men, women, adolescent boys and girls as a protection concern in 67% of FGDs, with female FGD participants mentioning it more than twice as many times. This was mirrored in the quantitative assessment, which found that in 66% of communities in Idleb, adolescent girls are affected by child marriage as well as 28% of girls below the age of 12. (Voices from Syria, 2018)

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. Mitigating Measure 1: Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed. Ensure consultation with communities and neighboring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for persons with visual/hearing impairments. Liaise with "neutral" parties as much as possible and triangulate all information from all sources prior to decision making. Ensure safety audits for access are conducted for each fixed site or mobile service location.Protection Risk 2: Girls, boys, women and men's physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed parties to the conflict; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receive services; lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure enroute to, during and directly after service provision.Mitigation Measure 2: Consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed site or mobile service provision. Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (route and location) by boys, girls, women and men, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical, visual/hearing deficits, psychological issues). Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community. Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community (to ensure diverse perspective and perceptions are considered). Ensure consultation and mobilisation of key persons in the community. Provide culturally adapted information on key child protection issues and responsibilities of different people in the growth and development of children. Ensure safety audits for access are conducted for each fixed site or mobile service location. Utilize sector agreed key messages to ensure consistency and inform sector of discrepancies or if new messaging is required. Protection Risk 3: Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialised child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality.Mitigation Measure 3: Ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules. Include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision. Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place. Child Protection messaging to non protection actors.Protection Risk 4: Risk to physical safety of child protection service providers if perceived to be interfering in family-based concerns.Mitigation Measure 4: 1. ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules. Include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Unforeseen Changes in the Conflict is such that organization cannot safely access locations, staff get besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost.Mitigation Measure 1: Continue to gather information from range of sources. Develop contingency plan to work in alternative location if conflict continues. Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space.Risk 2: Diversion of humanitarian aid is reoccurring event in many conflict or complex environments. Orange’s experience in Syria shows that it can happen on all sides of this conflict and patterns differ between location and local command. In most historical events, this has been small amounts for personal use. In limited cases, local leaders have attempted to policy demands. In some events, the loss of goods occurs when a storage space with commodities near a front line is taken.Mitigation Measure 2: Proper commodities/supply chain tracking, establish connection in the communities and ensure staff are aware of compliance and financial procedures. Staff are trained on anti-fraud practices.Risk 3: Low Quality of Programming includes limitations on direct monitoring, statistical data, percentages, samples and tight data related to gender, age or protection issues etc. This is the setting for a decline in program quality unless remote management tools are accurately employed.Mitigation Measure 3: Have monitoring and data collection staff from the same community and start the hiring process prior to kicking off the project implementation of activities. Deterioration of the security situation. Assumptions: Orange is maintaining a very good relationship with the community, relevant stakeholders, NGOs operating in the area. Orange has a bank of local potential staff who have the experience delivering similar activities in the same field. Orange has access to the community, responds to their feedback an ensure any necessary corrective decision is made during the life cycle of the project. Orange has been delivering similar programming, demonstrating its relevant experience in child protection programming. Orange is operating through policies, in line with donors’ rules and regulations including Human Resources, Finance, Procurement, Logistics, MEAL and project Management policies, through which Orange abides by donors’ and international standardized rules and regulations. Availability of all the resources, financial, human, material resources inside Syria, which will facilitate the implementation of the project activities.
