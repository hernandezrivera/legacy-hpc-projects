Project Summary

The North West and South West Regions of Cameroon has an estimated population of 4,358,867 inhabitants representing 18% of the estimated national population in 2017 that stands at 24,013,727 persons (BUCREP, 2019), of which 2,481,610 and 1,877,257 inhabitants are from the North West and South West, respectively. Since October 2016, the South-West and North-West regions of Cameroon are in a state of socio-political crisis, which started as claims of corporative groups demanding a better consideration of the English Judiciary and educational systems for teachers and lawyers. These strike actions rapidly turned into a political crisis. Unfortunately, the initial ideological and intellectual struggle gradually turned into an armed conflict between armed Non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and government forces and has led to loss of lives and destruction of properties. This was followed by acts of disobedience characterized by ghost towns and the shutting down of schools. Since 2018, the situation has further deteriorated with the invasions of NSAGs on the population such as kidnappings with ransom demands, executions, destruction of property and infrastructure, the destruction and burning down of schools and hospitals. This situation has therefore led to a massive displacement of people fleeing the armed conflict and famine towards neighbouring Nigeria and to safer areas.
According to September 2024 SITREP of OCHA NWSW regions, with more than 583 112 IDPs, 459 605 returnees and 73 481 Cameroonian refugees in Nigeria and increasing number of causalities on the military side, non-state actors and civilian end as well. These regions is characterized by continued violence against civilians and clashes between non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and State Security Forces (SSFs) with over 5,567 people (1,077 households) were reportedly displaced to nearby bushes, villages, and towns in the NWSW during the month. Manyu division in the SW and Mezam division in the NW were the most affected. Most of the displacements were pendular as civilians fled to safe areas during periods of clashes, violence or insecurity and returned to their homes when calm returned.
“Cadre Harmonisé” results conducted in Cameroon in October 2024 showed that 533 126 people in the North-West and South-West regions are food and nutritionally insecure (crisis to emergency phases). This represents 12.23% of the total population falls within the crisis to emergency phases. With continuous humanitarian aid, anticipated peace and next agricultural season running good, it is expected that the number of people suffering from food insecurity would have decreased by the end of the 2024 farming season but remains very worrying due to conflict and floods in certain areas has plunged the North West and South West regions into a drastic slowdown of economic activities in general, and agro pastoral production in particular. Due to the multiple killings, theft of livestock, destruction of crops and kidnappings perpetrated in rural and urban areas, and with all the trauma associated, many rural workers have left their homelands in search of more peaceful space.
This project priority is to build resilience for internally displaced people, returnees and host communities by setting up and monitoring highly productive micro-units of high-nutrient agro-pastoral products with high potential for financial revenue using Dimitra Clubs and Village Savings and Loan Association (VSLA known as AVEC in French) approach.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Main activities to be done throughout this project are as follows: 1. Technical capacity building: beneficiaries will be trained on human nutrition, mushroom production, sustainable farming and farm resource management using Dimitra and VSLA approaches ;2. Implementing market gardens made of rapid growth crops: targeted crops are potato, sweet potato, vegetables, dry vegetables, etc.3. Implementing sustainable poultry production units: providing veterinary products, animal and breeding materials, and follow-up on egg production4. Coordinating, monitoring, and evaluating, project activities 5. Cash-plus distribution to vulnerable households to protect seeds and livelihoods in general The main outputs expected from these activities are the following: 1) At least 6845 direct beneficiaries of the project trained on human nutrition and sustainable farm and resource management;2) Gross production of about 50000 tons of roots, tubers, vegetables, and legumes obtained from 5212 HHs;3) Gross production of about 8 000,000 table-eggs obtained from 2000HHs 4) At least 30 000kg of mushroom will be harvested from 1000HHs5) At least 6800 benefitting households targeted (including 60% of women headed households and 10% of PWD) ) sponsored 6) At least 150 local or community-based monitoring personnel mobilized and trained using Dimitra and VSLA approaches for follow-up of the beneficiaries
