Project Summary

This project is aimed at providing quality lifesaving child protection services to 11,404 children. Narrative Hub will increase its static response to hard to reach areas with protection monitoring and assessment, strengthening the Psycho-social Support Services and Protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging to the affected communities within the selected counties, increased capacity building program for staff and communities to support the children after the exit of NH and to continue improving the well-being of children that have gone through difficulties. Narrative Hub will adopt and multi-sectoral approach promoting a combination of delivery modalities and, mainstream CP and GBV into Education, WASH, Nutrition, Health, and FSL. Referral pathways will also be established to strengthen and support the implementation of case management and link children to other services and address the gaps in coordination within the targeted counties were Narrative Hub operates. Community engagement and accountability to affected population will be at the centre of the project activities. In all activities, health and security protocols and SOPs will be observed to avoid infections and its spread to both staff members and beneficiaries.
This project is linked to the allocation strategy and HNO 2024 in view of the timely and tailored response to target populations facing the greatest needs from the compound effects of armed conflict, displacement, hunger, disease and natural disasters such as floods. This project is aligned within the cluster strategy focusing on protection as the top need among the targeted counties. Especially on the provision of structured psychosocial support and life-skills interventions to conflict-affected children; provision of comprehensive case management; strengthen community-based networks including families to prevent and respond to violence against children, supporting vulnerable children through Case Management and supporting community-based mechanisms in Cuiebet, Manyo, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol, Nassir and Yei counties. Affected communities liaise with the area civil leaders to select, build and empower the Community-based Child Protection committee/Network, updating child protection referral pathways, training women and men (community leaders or humanitarian and government staff) on Child Protection in Emergencies, family tracing and reunification (FTR) services to vulnerable children. These interventions match NH’s organizational objectives and work that has been carried out for the last 3 years in child protection intervention.

1. Needs

According Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FAWS NET) report of September 2023, in the northern counties bordering Sudan, the influx of South Sudanese returnees continues to drive high humanitarian needs, putting additional burden on the host households who are sharing scarce resources with the newly arrived. Canal/Pigi and Fangak of northern Jonglei, and Fashoda and Panyikang of Upper Nile remain of high concern, where some households are likely still in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in the latter two counties due to multiple years of asset and livelihood erosion exacerbated by the arrival of South Sudanese returnees and conflict-related disruption to trade flow with Sudan. The acute food insecurity in South Sudan remains worrying according to the latest IPC report, with the latest data showing that 5.83 million people (46.3 percentage of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). An estimated 35,000 people are in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in Nyirol (3,000) county of Jonglei State, while 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). A further 1.64 million people are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). The most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 50 percent of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) are Jonglei State (61.3 percent), Upper Nile State (56.1 percent) and Lakes State (52.8 percent). The South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2024, attacks on civilians by armed cattle keepers, and cattle raids continued to displace people. Cattle raids were characterized by multiple forms of violence against civilians, including killing, injury, rape, abduction, looting and destruction of housing and property. Incidents have occurred in remote areas where victims of violence or survivors of sexual assault had little to no access to health services. Children were at the risk of recruitment into local defense groups and other forms of abuses, including abduction and possibly trafficking. In 2022, 5,862 schools were operating across the country, of which 560 were damaged due to flooding and 535 due to conflict. Safety and security incidents in and around education facilities as well as the destruction or occupation of facilities during conflict were reported to limit access for children to schools. These included displacement of teachers and children, killings of teachers and the risk of revenge killings of boys in schools. Other barriers include distance of schools from settlements and limited financial resources within communities to send their children to school. Girls face additional challenges, including cultural perceptions within communities that being educated would lead them to engage in criminal activities or risk becoming pregnant, which would in turn result in them fetching low dowries when they are married. According to the latest IRNA assessment report in Nassir of 18th to 20th August 2023, intense fighting in Sudan that began in mid-April 2023 and suspension of food aid in Ethiopian refugee camps has progressively led to deteriorating humanitarian situation in both countries. In all the assessments conducted in Nyirol, Nasir, Pigi/Canal, Manyo, Yei and Cuiebet, the refugee population found themselves exposed to difficult situations due to absence of basic services including food, health, education, protection and shelter/NFIs and forced to return. The IRNA assessment report in Manyo in October 2022 shows unaccompanied and separated children are not reunified with their family members. Over 20 UASC cases were estimated in Manyo County mostly boys and girls in areas of in Del-Ajak, Nyibor, Abwor, Kaka, Ayat, Peno, Akurwa and Athidhwoy around 13 cases of separated children. There were reported cases of missing children, more than 7 girls and boys have been missing in Manyo but not seen in the other areas.

2. Project activities

Objective 1: Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. presence/missions to field and deep field locations. Activities: • Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services including through mobile teams to children, adolescents and caregivers.• Strengthen Case Management approach including through cash-based assistance (including for UASC, GBV survivors, and CAAFAG). • Provision of family tracing and re-unification (FTR) services to Unaccompanied and Separated children. • Conduct awareness raising sessions with children and caregivers. • Conduct KI Interviews, at payam level, through the Protection Monitoring System Objective 2: Promoting nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, exacerbated by systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women and girls and persons with disabilities. Activity: • Community Based Child Protection (CBCP), capacity building and outreaches. • Strengthen/update child protection referral pathways • Train women and men (community leaders or humanitarian and government staff) on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE)
