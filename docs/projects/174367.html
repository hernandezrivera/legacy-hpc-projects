Project Summary

This project will target IDPs and returnees in three affected states, Yobe, Adamawa and Borno with provision of emergency shelters, Non-food Items (NFIs), transitional and shelter repair kits as protection live-saving assistance aligned with Shelter/ NFI/ CCCM Sector strategic objectives for the HRP 2019. In 2019 UNHCR continued to effectively respond to shelter needs and aims to target a total of 69,569 households, 347,846 individuals in formal and informal camps and newly accessible areas by the end of the year. As the number of returns and displacement increases due to military operations, there is need to provide shelter related assistance for returnees who are residing in their plots and as well as for host families whose resources have been overstretched. Subsequently acknowledging that the humanitarian situation is still unpredictable; there is need to provide shelters and NFIs for life-saving emergency assistance to promote the protection, safety and dignity of affected Persons of Concern (PoCs). Despite the achievements in the past, the continuous needs for IDPs, though shaped by very different dynamics, revealed that quite a significant majority of people are living in congested camps. The needs assessment conducted in 2019 by Shelter/ NFI/ CCCM sector indicated that as of end of June 2019, 3809 IDP households were living without shelter and NFI assistance since their arrival. In addition to that, 7593 households living in partitioned communal shelters needed NFIs and dignified emergency shelters. The ongoing and protracted displacement leaves the affected people in need of other humanitarian assistance such as Food, WASH, and Health among others. In 2020, UNHCR shall build upon progress being made in 2019 and proposes to respond to unmet needs in Adamawa (Mubi North, Mubi South, Madagali, Michika, Yola South and Yola north); Borno (Bama, Banki, Damasak, Damboa, Dikwa, Jere, konduga, Gwoza, Pulka, Maiduguri, Mobbar, Monguno, Rann and Ngala) and Yobe State (Damaturu and Gujba,) through provision of; - (1) 7,000 Emergency shelters (2) 52,129 NFIs kits to IDPs in both formal and informal camps (3) 10,440 Shelter repair kits to returnees with the provision of conditional CASH component of NGN 30,000 Naira adequate to cover for the cost labor and molding of mud bricks among other unforeseen needs. All the shelters are designed to cater for Women, Men, Girls and boys, persons with specific needs and partitioned to mitigate SGBV cases. The selection of beneficiaries will be based on needs assessment and vulnerability screening in the locations at the time of implementation. Identification and verification of beneficiaries are guided by UNHCR’s Protection principles, and endorsed by community leaders, NEMA, and SEMA officials at LGA areas. UNHCR will reinforce a transparent process of beneficiary selection and the prioritization of needs of vulnerable groups. The project targets the IDPs and Returnees households suffering from protracted displacement and returning home. UNHCR will work to ensure that potential beneficiaries are aware of their responsibilities during the project and of options available to them. It is important to recognize that not everyone can manage the construction/ repair of their own house and that their time might be better used to generate income. The families’ decision to participate in this programme will be achieved through sensitization meetings, detailed assessments, participatory planning and prioritizing the provision of technical training and capacity building. A community-based feedback mechanism through regular mass meetings and focus group discussions will be established to enable IDPs express their opinions on issues affecting their assistance need. The discussions will also provide beneficiaries the opportunity to understand the shelter selection and allocation process.

1 - Needs

With the intensification of military operations against non state armed groups (NSAG) in some LGA’s within Borno and surrounding states and the liberation of areas formerly under the control of Boko Haram, it is estimated that the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in Borno and affected states will reach up to 7.6 million in 2020. In responding to the shelter needs of IDPs, UNHCR needs to ensure sufficient and coordinated delivery of shelter and NFI assistance for new arrivals and /or suddenly displaced IDPs without proper shelters residing in camps and camp like settings. UNHCR also need to aim at contributing to broader government initiatives, facilitating access to durable solutions through provision of shelter repair kits in transition towards recovery and reintegration. The UNHCR shelter repair kit complements the IDP’s efforts in reconstruction and repair of their shelters. This will target vulnerable IDPs returning to their areas of origin whose shelters are either partially or completely damaged. This will provide sufficient materials for a substantial beginning, which can be built upon, including a small cash component to allow some flexibility to facilitate repairs of their shelters. UNHCR needs to advocate through the Shelter/NFI/CCCM Sector and the Protection sector for the identification of durable solutions targeting IDPs willing to settle permanently at their present location. This will be undertaken through advocacy to various stakeholders including community leaders, local, state and national authorities, host communities and the displaced population towards establishment of formal satellite villages, supporting the issuance of land title deeds, provision of support in reconstruction, incorporating proper urban planning principles as well as a provision for agriculture to support livelihood activities. In 2020, it’s expected that returns to Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states will continue to increase. UNHCR proposes to target 297,845 (59,569HH) IDPs and Returnees with 50,000 returnees expected from Cameroon and Niger. UNHCR needs to prioritize the improvement of reception conditions, through provision of emergency shelters and NFI along the border areas particularly through Banki, Ngala, Damasak in Borno state and Adamawa and Yobe states. As part of the repatriation package, UNHCR needs also prioritize the provision of the shelter repair kit and provision of technical guidance to returnees.

2 - Activities

Activities or outputs: Activity 1: Shelter beneficiary selection to determine vulnerability through registration and vulnerability screening. Activity 2: Undertake proper site selection and settlement planning. Activity 3: Emergency shelters response to respond to sudden displacement in formal and informal camps. Activity 4: Procurement and distribution of shelter repair kit materials to vulnerable families in area of return. Activity 5: Provision of build back safer capacity building training for beneficiaries of shelter repair kits. Activity 6: Procurement and distribution of NFIs displaced and returning IDP households Activity 7: Increased advocacy for establishment of formal satellite cities with proper urban planning, incorporating masterplan principles for IDP’s not planning to return to areas of origin. Activity 8: Increased advocacy on access to appropriate land, housing and tenure Activity 9: Monitoring and evaluation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection mainstreaming will be an important element of this project from the designed, implementation, monitoring ang reporting faces. This will be achieved through the following modalities; 1. The safety and dignity of our population of concern who are the right owners will be prioritized. All faces of the project must be analysed to prioritize and respect safety and dignity of persons of concern. 2. Also the project will provide ample access the rights owners through the project cycle. 3. Furthermore, there will be continues engagement with the right owners and setup of information centre at UNHCR protection desks to provide a channel for feedback to inform reassessment of needs to ensure the real needs of the people are addressed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to affected populations will be strengthened through the development of a community-based feedback mechanism; supporting partners to the inclusion of functional and accessible complaints and reporting mechanisms, convening frequent meetings with the community leaders, and beneficiaries and focus group discussions. UNHCR is an active member of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and has community-based structures known as Protection Action Groups (PAG) within Maiduguri and LGAs where UNHCR is present such as Bama, Banki, Damasak, Damboa, Damaturu, Dikwa, Gudjba, Gwoza, Jere, Gwoza, Madagali, Michika, MMC, Monguno, Ngala, Rann, Yola North, Yola South. Through these structures, UNHCR ensures that issues of concern to the community are brought to the attention of UNHCR and its partners for intervention. UNHCR shall also establish a feedback mechanism through its Protection Desk. In previous projects, some key concerns raised include beneficiary selection, the support provided, community representatives and payment process. All issues reported will be systematically investigated and followed up in a timely manner. Received complaints shall be categorized according to the level of sensitivity and severity and responded in accordance with protection principles.
