Project Summary

Internal displacement outside of camps remains one of the challenges in north-east displacement and has received increased attention from the humanitarian community over the past years. As displacement trends increased in complexity, becoming more urban and protracted nature, majority of displaced population also have access to wider range of settlement options. Unfortunately, this has not always translated to increase in access to assistance and protection. In order to improve access to information, protection and services, while ensuring representation, PRIDE plans to extend protection monitoring services to the displaced persons settled in out-of-camps in MMC and JERE

1 - Needs

In NE Nigeria, it is estimated that 60% of the IDPs live in the host community, with some residing in spontaneous makeshift structures in hazardous environments, rented apartments within the urban or peri-urban areas, particularly in informal settlements where state capacity to deliver services and infrastructure is weak, while others live with host families. It is acknowledged that there has been limited capacity and engagement from the humanitarian community to adequately address the need of the affected populations. Apart from suffering the effects of the insurgency, the displaced population and the host community are both exposed to serious risks including natural disaster, communicable diseases, food insecurity and marginalization.The large presence of displaced populations in urban or peri-urban areas has had significant impacts, placing additional pressure on local markets, as well as on the social, economic, cultural and administrative structures that are already weakened by effects of the insurgency. Their vulnerability is expounded as the host community has equally been affected by displacement. The disparity might cause tension and incite violence and scapegoating and especially during tension and changing phase of an emergency or during communicable disease outbreaks. As a result of displacement, they lost assets, housing, have limited social networks, separated from family members, lost documentation and poor access to available services, and are often undergoing trauma. IDPs living in urban areas are often exposed to exploitation, extortion, organised crime and antagonism from host communities. Frequently, IDPs become victims of forced evictions and expulsions. Ensuring security and protection of IDPs in urban areas therefore remains one of the most significant challenges facing the humanitarian community.

2 - Activities

The proposed Action has been designed according to the needs identified in Nigeria by the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), multi-sector needs assessment, and through communication with OCHA, sector leads and donors. This section of the proposal will be divided into the purposes outlined above: Purpose One: To mitigate and respond to the harm caused to persons who have suffered violence, coercion, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination, and to restore their capacity to live safe and dignified lives. Purpose Two: To strengthen targeted protection services to address the needs and vulnerability of persons at heightened risks of violence, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination; enhance their capacities and reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms. Purpose Three: To strengthen the protection environment through the identification, monitoring and strengthening of risk reduction strategies of the affected population, legal and political systems as well as the capacity of local actors to respond to protection concerns. The PRIDE will extend urgent need for support to the vulnerable groups among other activities. The application of protection services to the displaced persons in out of camps is greatly determined by access to the IDPs. In MMC and Jere insecurity is the major hindrance to access of humanitarian access, however for out Camp Protection Monitoring, in addition to insecurity, state government guidelines as well need to be considered. This strategy outlines the respective recommended implementation modalities on the basis of access to the IDPs: the activities include a. Remote Area-based approach: Needs of IDPs will be identified and addressed alongside those of host community residents, especially those in remote and marginalized areas. PRIDE teams will operationally designate geographical areas based on local government jurisdictions of the affected areas i.e., divisions and allocate community mobile monitoring teams per area in order to cover a wider region. It would also be necessary to establish remote protection monitoring structures through identification of the existing protection action group (PAG) leadership, train and orient them to take up and nurture community-self-protection monitoring from the onset. This approach can only be implemented in areas that are accessible. b. Mobile Team Approach: The mobile approach will be based on Mobile Outreach Teams (MoTs) visiting the sites on a regular basis, often multiple times a week. The MoTs are complemented by static community centers strategically located in walking distance from all the informal settlements to enable community members to access information and support without having to wait for an Outreach Team member to visit them in their site. The centers are staffed daily by community-based workers from the local neighborhood and the informal settlements as well as the mobile Outreach staff, who move between sites and centers. c. Neighborhood/Community-Based, OUTREACH CAMP: In urban, peri-urban, metropolis or Local Government Area (LGA), a neighborhood or community-based approach will be considered. In the first approach, needs of both the IDPs and host community are the same given their level of marginalization which begins from their geographical location. d. Helpdesk Safe Space: The helpdesk can develop into a Community Engagement Centre to create an opportunity for the displaced population and the host community to expand their capacity to manage difficulties, build self-resilience and strengthen community coping mechanisms. 4. CAPACITY BUILDING: PRIDE will ensure local authorities, community leaders and other community representatives are capacitated and are aware of what the Mobile Protection approach entails, the roles and responsibilities of the authorities, and what is expected.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Risks and security issues related to this program include possible child abuse, sexual harassment and exploitation of women and girls, acts of theft including defrauding of illiterate members or other forms or coercion of beneficiaries. PRIDE will ensure safe implementation of activities through strict adherence and commitment to uphold the principle of “Do No Harm” in its activities. Suitable sites will be chosen for any activities including meetings, focus group discussions PRIDE and key informant interviews of beneficiaries with community leaders. Secure sites that are near beneficiaries will be given priority to ensure that people can return home before dark to minimize risks. The program implementation team staff will be provided refresher training on child safeguarding, accountability, the Inter-Agency Standing Committee guidelines on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse of beneficiaries and Green Concern for Development’s Code of Conduct. All new PRIDE employees are oriented to and required to sign the PRIDE Child Safeguarding Policy and the Code of Conduct as part of their contractual package. PRIDE mainstreams protection across all programming. The protection team identifies unaccompanied and separated minors and identifies suitable caregivers. Caregivers are referred to the food assistance program as vulnerable households with minors. This reduces the burden for caregivers and the need for negative coping strategies such as begging and child labor (street hawking). The protection team conducts regular follow-up in cases of abuse and neglected children for proper referral pathway
