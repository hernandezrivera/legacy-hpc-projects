Project Summary

Non-food item assistance continues to be an essential and lifesaving need in Syria. In particular, NFIs become increasingly important during the cold weather months to ensure families in need for core relief items, such as winter clothes and thermal blankets, can live in safety and dignity. Thus, AAH will procure and distribute winter kits (pyjamas & thermal underwear) during the harsh winter weather and/or core and essential NFIs whenever needed (mattresses and thermal blankets) to help alleviate the suffering of up to 30,000 IDPs, returnees and host communities (women, men, boys and girls). Distributions will be timely and safe to avoid physical harm and ensure that all different target groups, including those with speciﬁc needs, have equal and easy access. AAH will prioritize the most severe locations based on the severity scores, PiN, and needs assessments, taking into account scenarios on displacement, return and emergency when planning the response.
Complaints mechanisms are incorporated in the response, and post distribution monitoring will be conducted to enable beneficiaries to share their feedback on quality, timeliness and usefulness of the aid, as well as to propose additional suggestions to help improve assistance.
The proposed project mainstreams and incorporates protection principles in the activities to promote meaningful access, safety and dignity. Despite the challenging environment, and thanks to its effective access strategy, AAH has full access to the targeted areas and has the capacity in place to start the operations. Particular attention will be paid to ensure activities implemented by AAH do not duplicate but complement activities undertaken by other actors.

4 - Needs

NFIs continue to be essential and lifesaving particularly for those newly displaced or forced to relocate, and returnees. The NFI PiN has declined by 22% from 2019 to an estimate 3.42 million people in 2020. This reflects both a continuing but slow improvement in market functionality, and the successful efforts of the sector to distribute assistance across the country. However, this progress is fragile as many communities suffer from high prices, unpredictable inflation, and low wages. NFIs become increasingly important during the cold weather months to ensure families in need for core relief items such as winter clothes and thermal blankets can live in safety and dignity. Following consideration of climatic conditions, underreached areas and proportion of different population groups, an estimated 338,000 families, nearly 1.7 million individuals are in need of winterization assistance across the 14 governorates within Syria. Overall, different NFIs needs are reported among different groups based on gender and age; however, hygiene kit and sanitary pads, baby and adult diapers, winter clothes, blankets, cooking tools and heating fuel are among the most reported NFIs needs across the country. NFIs contribute to improve the physical and mental well-being conditions of vulnerable women and children, and consequently help to prevent diseases. Indeed, NFIs are typically urgent needs for displaced people that have left behind home and their belongings and lack the means to sleep, cook, or clean themselves and their children, creating a source of distress particularly for the women, because of their gendered role in Syrian society as care-givers. They will be suffering the distress for as long as they are unable to conform to social rules of care and will probably resort to negative coping strategies if this capacity is not restored quickly.

5 - Implementation of activities

While market access appears to be gradually improving and NFIs are available in markets, many essential humanitarian lifesaving and life-sustaining basic items continue to be unaffordable for people. NFIs become increasingly important during the cold weather months to ensure families can live in safety and dignity. AAH will thus procure and distribute seasonal and supplementary NFIs (pyjamas & thermal underwear) and/or core and essential NFIs (mattresses and thermal blankets) to help alleviate the suffering of up to 30,000 IDPs, returnees and/or host communities and protect them from seasonally and adverse weather conditions. AAH has already ongoing framework agreements with qualified suppliers to cover needs across all of Syria and to fasten the procurement phase. Winter kits are designed to address the different needs of women, men, girls and boys and will include thermal underwear and pajamas (number and size of the items based on needs identified); distributions will be between October and November. The technical specifications of winter kits and core NFIs items are in accordance to sector standards and based on AAH previous experiences, taking into consideration beneficiaries’ feedback. The distributions will be done through SARC teams of volunteers, making sure that the distributions are timely and safe to avoid physical harm and ensure that all different target groups have equal and easy access. AAH will ensure that relevant age/sex groups are considered with all data sex disaggregated throughout the project cycle. Regular onsite monitoring will be carried out and Post Distribution Monitoring undertake to assess in quantity and quality of the inputs. Several sub-districts are selected as potential targets. The deﬁnitive areas of intervention will be deﬁned on a later stage based on needs assessments, according to which the support will target the most vulnerable conflict-affected populations selected based on vulnerability criteria.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Risk of increased community tension due to different assistance packages, unclear beneﬁciary selection criteria or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation: Sector meetings and coordination with partners to ensure consistency of assistance package. Changes to the package are thoroughly assessed and validated. Identify item/s particularly sensitive to the extent of increasing the risk of tension and exclude them. Beneﬁciary selection criteria are clearly communicated widely within targeted communities; Criteria are designed and selection of beneficiaries done based on needs and not status. Engage with community leaders taking into consideration gender balance in the committees to be selected to support in disseminating information. Field staff (both AAH and partners) and community ﬁeld monitors will be trained (from both genders) on conﬂict mitigation techniques. Protection risk: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety) Likelihood: medium, Impact: high Mitigation: Distribution points are organized to avoid overcrowding and long waiting period. Pre-consultation with communities is regularly done to agree on best distribution modality to ensure that all different target groups have equal and easy access. Staff involved (both AAH and partners staff) is trained in organizing and implementing distribution, including the monitoring during the distributions and PDM to identify best practices and constraints. Application of checklist at distribution sites. Protection risk: Abuse of authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion) Likelihood: high Impact: High Mitigation: AAH engages with community people (both men and women), to support in disseminating information in the communities. Feedback and complaint mechanism at community level allowing women to express their complaints.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: security situation allows safe response; access to target areas and beneficiaries is granted; partners are willing to collaborate on timely manner; availability of suppliers and items in the market, prices remain stable; no major displacements occur. Risk: security situation is unstable and/or worsens, AAH has no access; there are additional administrative procedures imposed on D-INGOs, and restrictions on access to beneﬁciaries; lengthy processes to obtain approvals. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: AAH coordinates with local authorities, Ministries, and stakeholders to monitor the situation, to maintain access to target areas and beneﬁciaries, to develop relationships and avoid or mitigate restrictions. Security rules and plans are in place and regularly reviewed. Re-planning of activities to recover eventual delays. Advocacy and coordination with the humanitarian community. Risk: significant devaluation of EURO/ USD against SYP; International bank transfers are not ﬂuid and timely. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: signiﬁcant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be monitored and, if materialized, communicated to donors and options discussed. Risk: aid diversion, financial fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: strict policies and procedures in place for procurement, financial management and risk management; internal controls set up. Monthly monitoring of financial spending. Risk: markets and services are not functional for planned deliveries; procurement of equipment/materials are delayed. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: diversify procurement according to AAH guidelines. Launch framework agreements or tenders for medium time frames. Risk: any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc.). Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: compulsory trainings on organizational ethical policies. AAH mission has a policy for PSEA and a reference person against harassment at country level.
