Project Summary

Most of the 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia live in informal and unplanned settlements where living conditions are poor and forced eviction is a common threat. Safety and security concerns remain a high priority for all affected populations, with displacement contributing to immediate and heightened exposure to violence, exploitation and other forms of abuse. Serious risks and threats to safety and security. The protection needs in Somalia changed and evolved over time. The current crisis affects individuals of all ages and genders, as well as from diverse social backgrounds, in different ways and scale. The most vulnerable groups include people with physical, sensorial, intellectual and mental disabilities, adolescent girls and boys, pregnant and lactating women and girls, female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children, persons from minority clans, older persons without caregivers, persons missing documentation, persons facing eviction, persons living in areas with armed conflict and violence. Women and adolescent girls from 18 – 50 years old (including those living with disabilities) are subjected to Intimate Partner Violence, rape, sexual exploitation, sexual harassment and abuse. Low reporting of GBV cases persist due to stigmatization, victim blaming, shame and interference by clan and religious leaders, slow access to justice and weak or lack of legal framework for the protection of women and girls from GBV. Persons with disabilities face various physical, attitudinal and institutional barriers to access food, health, shelter and protection on an equal basis with others. Persons with disabilities are estimated to be at least 15% of the population and the majority are among the poorest part of the population. They also face disability-based discrimination, which is exacerbated in situations of crisis, and when it intersects with other factors, such as poverty, gender or age. To mitigate the above mentioned challenge, SSWC will conduct meetings and consult with people living with disabilities throughout the project phases. Transportation plans for people living with disabilities will be made for easy access to our centres and our field monitors will ensure that they target households with people living with disabilities in their daily visits within the target areas. Up to 66 per cent of the population in need in Somalia are children who continue to be exposed to threats of recruitment, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation and violence. Hence, 1.88 million children including 10% of children with disabilities need immediate child protection services. Boys and girls of all ages in Somalia continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights including family separation, sexual and physical violence, killing and maiming, psycho social distress, worst forms of child labor, widespread recruitment and use of children, forced and protracted. SSWC wants to address GBV and child protection programming due to high number of Incidents of GBV this is as a result of reported cases, predominantly in IDP sites throughout the country. The objective of the project is to fill up the existing critical gaps in protection targeting 50,000 beneﬁciaries in Banadir district. The purpose of the intervention is to significantly reduce the incidents of GBV, promote awareness on prevention and response to GBV and provide child protection services to the community living in Banadir. Protection activities include provision of psychosocial support, Medical referrals, legal aid counselling, distribution of emergency social assistance (dignity kits), awareness and sensitization on GBV prevention and response, Capacity building for community structures on GBV prevention and response and comprehensive case management services at the Women Wellness centers and Safe houses.Establishment of Child friendly spaces, facilitation of psycho-social support for children including play therapy, art work and drawing.

Project Activities

The project will address the needs of the community through the following activities: Provision of Psycho social support. Survivors of GBV require appropriate and timely support to deal with the psychological and mental health consequences of violence – which may include depression, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), anxiety, and thoughts of suicide or self-harm – and the potential social consequences, including stigma, discrimination and rejection. Mental health and psychosocial support are essential components of the comprehensive package of care and aim to protect or promote psychosocial well-being and/or prevent or treat mental disorders among survivors of GBV. Psychosocial support, as part of a holistic and community-based intervention, can provide crucial assistance to survivors, as well as their families, social networks and communities, and help to prevent violence in the future. Strengthening of community based protection mechanisms in the IDP camps. SSWC will strengthen existing community structures on GBV prevention and response by training selected community members on community based protection mechanisms and also conducting awareness sessions at IDP sites to promote awareness on GBV prevention and response. Training and building capacity of community leaders and key gatekeepers is important so that they can be champions of change. Facilitate distribution of emergency social assistance (Dignity kits) to GBV survivors and vulnerable children in the IDP sites. The kits will contain hygiene and sanitary items explicitly tailored towards the local needs of women and girls of reproductive age. Dignity kits help women and girls maintain their dignity during humanitarian crises. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, which is important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence also assists them in providing care and protection to their children and reducing their risk to GBV. Carry out community based awareness sessions and forums at IDP sites to promote awareness on protection risks faced by women and children. SSWC will Involve the IDP communities through community forums and associations and initiate dialogues on GBV to change attitudes, perceptions and behaviors on a societal level. Develop and disseminate messages on GBV Prevention and Response through radio spots in local language. In most IDP Camps communication is by means of radio. SSWC will partner with a local radio station with wide coverage within the IDP camps and disseminate GBV radio spots at prime hours to reach masses with the messages. Provision of case management services to GBV survivors including children through Women wellness centers. SSWC manages two women wellness centers in Banadir district where survivors of GBV are attended to and receive comprehensive services including admission to safe houses for beneficiaries with heightened protection concerns, Clinical care which is done by trained nurses and medical referrals and legal aid counseling. Facilitate psychosocial support sessions for children at child friendly spaces (CFS). SSWC Will Construct child friendly spaces for children in the IDP Camps. Counselors and educators will facilitate psychosocial support sessions for children in CFSs. The Psychosocial sessions will target children in the IDP camps and host community who experience low to medium psychological distress. The sessions are aimed at providing children with a sense of normalcy, enhancing their ability to cope with the situation and fostering their long term emotional and social wellbeing which can mitigate long lasting psychological damage.

Mitigation Measures

During delivering assistance, exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance is possible and SSWC will prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm. Save Somali Women and Children will ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points of NFI kits and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. The distribution centers will be central and the distribution will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. SSWC has a protection project this will leverage the shelter project and help reduce the risks of GBV to beneficiaries. During the project implementation the Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents may be identified . SSWC will ensure that all selected beneficiaries are aware of all the mapped out referral services through its field monitors. The beneficiaries will also be encouraged to listen to radio programs sponsored by SSWC which highlight GBV issues and inform people on the available referral services in Somalia . The Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms may arise. SSWC will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints by calling the free toll number 2091 with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at distribution site, phone lines , setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff. Responding to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place and organizing awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. SSWC will also provide clear information to distribution actors and affected population explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for receiving services.

Please provide further explanation

The Project will focus on Strengthening existing community based Protection mechanisms and structures and linking them with local authorities who will closely work together with them in the IDP Camps even after the project closure. The service provision enters are locally owned and government provides oversight role in all its activities making it recognised legally. The project will also focus on building capacity for members of the community structures, training them on protection and equipping them with knowledge and skills that they can always use. The project encourages community ownership of the properties being used in supporting the vulnerable population. The sense of community belonging and acceptance will encourage community structures to continue providing protection services.
