Project Summary

Ikotos County in Eastern Equatoria state has a sizable number of returnees from neighboring Uganda having sought refuge in the height of 2016 armed unrest in South Sudan.With life increasingly becoming difficult in the camps in Uganda coupled with prevailing peace since the signing of the peace agreement in September 2018, around 60% of all the population initially fleeing have returned. Putting a lot of pressure on the existing resources and humanitarian intervention since they were not in plan for most of the 2019 humanitarian responses. This struggle for resources has heaped pressure to increase allocations for returnees in facilitating safe reintegration into the community. Through an earlier internal assessment by FOCOSS in January 2019 presented to UNFAO, UNFAO undertook to support 3000 households with emergency crop and vegetable seeds and farming hand tools for the main season from March 2019 through to January 2020.
In the Emergency Livelihood and Response Programme (ELRP) The project focuses on provision of crop seeds and training smallholder farmers on modern agronomic practices. To date FOCOSS has reached 3,000 beneficiaries with crop,vegetable seeds,farm hand tools and established 8 demonstration plots in Lomohidang North, Lomohidang South, Ikwoto, Hatire and Imatong Payams.
The proposed project will focus mainly on able bodied male and female (50% of all the targeted beneficiaries) adults from vulnerable households within the returnee population especially and hosts. Particular emphasis will be put to factor in minority groups and people with special needs. Child headed households and elderly persons as well as the sick.Principally, the project will contribute towards realization of FSL Cluster objectives 2 and 3 that focuses on emergency food production and strengthening household’s ability to absorb shocks. This will involve supporting needy smallholder farmers with farm inputs to enable them produce food so as to respond to emergency food needs of their families. The project will then embark on resilience building for the farmers through training on appropriate farming techniques focusing on Climate Resilient Sustainable Agriculture techniques including post-harvest management and food handling to reduce losses during and after harvests and improved health.
The county generally lacks diversified agriculture with most people cultivating maize, sorghum which are sources of starch only, often eaten with a single accompaniment of cow peas and local vegetable (kudhra) The project will through the training articulate on vegetable farming as a source of vital vitamins to alleviate malnutrition in children as well as a commercial venture that will provide income for the households. Both crop and vegetable farming combined will reduce protection risks associated with searching for food in the forest especially for women and children who may get injured, lose valuable family social time and school time .Instead children will be kept in school while the out of school youth will be actively engaged in farming for increased production. Being a family activity, all the members will be involved promoting cohesion and social well being. The men will do the heavier tasks of opening up arable through clearing and initial land preparation, while the women will be involved in lesser tasks like planting.

1 - Needs

According to FAO/WFP led Food Security Nutrition Monitoring Survey (FSNMS)and the subsequent integrated phase classification (IPC analysis August 2019), 80% of Households in Ikotos County practice crop farming totaling to about 19,291 households. This implies that crop farming is the main lifeline of communities in this County.However there is acute shortage of farming hand tools as well as crop seeds and vegetables. Consequently little land is opened up for farming. The farmers lack seeds for the next planting season owing to rampant poverty mostly, where they end up eating all the grain reserves. Lack of improved agricultural techniques play a big role in low crop production as well as prudent soil conservation and nutrient addition through addition of manure are not incorporated.Ikotos has a sizable number of returnees who are sharing the stocks harvested in the main season, putting a strain on the existing food reserves. Therefore early planning for the next season and availability of farming inputs will ensure sufficient food production. Lack of formal employment among the population is major contribution to the poverty levels and resilience building in crisis. With limited income sources there is continuous strain on family resources.Lack of post-harvest techniques and pest management contribute greatly to diminished yields coupled with losses to pests both in the farms and stores. The proposed project will address these problems associated with farmers in continuous manner by establishing project committees who are lead farmers for constant information and guidance. Poor record keeping has been attributed to poor tracking of crop pests thus hindering their management and control. Each payam will with the help of FOCOSS staff maintain a data of all crops and the particular pests associated with them at all times of the year for better control and management.Poor soil conservation methods is a major concern especially soil moisture conservation due to perennial dry conditions, through improved techniques such as mulching and cover crops as well as inter cropping will greatly address soil moisture/ nutrient deficiency for improved yields.

2 - Activities

1. Community engagement through mobilization,sensitization and information sharing on the upcoming intervention and criteria for targeting that will be done in close collaboration with FSL stakeholders, local authorities and various age groups and gender to articulate their unique needs.2. Identification, selection and registration of beneficiaries (50% female). Special focus on minority groups and people with special needs will be given opportunity. However the work involved being labor intensive, the able bodied members in their family or caregivers will be direct participants/beneficiaries to protect from strenuous work that may injure them. These will include the elderly, child headed households, the sick, pregnant and lactating mothers.3. Provide agricultural training to 2 organized groups in every payam to continually provide agricultural information and guidance. With the help of community leaders, demonstration gardens will be set at every payam headquarter for continuous reference and a payam project committee will be in charge of maintaining it. As motivation they be beneficiaries of all the harvest at the end of a season.4.Training of all the beneficiaries on pest management and control before planting,during crop growth,harvesting and in storage.Particular emphasis will be put on selection of planting seed for the next season to ensure purity.Special training and monitors will be selected to report of any outbreaks,trends and to spearhead preventive and curative measures. 5.Transportation,storage and distribution of crop,vegetable seeds and farming hand tools to 5000 beneficiaries in all the 6 payams. Emphasis will be put on accessibility and any protection risks especially when designating the distribution points to ensure safety.6.Selection and training of seed fair committees(1 in each pall scale business set up skills to better manage their savings and initiate new business increasing their sources of earnings while reducing risk on dependence from agricultural produce especially if rains fail or infestation by pests.Provide continuous information on market trends,supply,demand and prices while keeping a record for future planning.7.Establish community feed back/report desks in every payam headquarter to collect,document and relay to the head office complaints,compliments,protection concerns and the general well being of the project.Direct lines will be provided for those who can afford to directly call the head office and register their feed back.This information will be treated as confidential and used solely for the welfare of our beneficiaries. 8.Conduct detailed monitoring and evaluation before,during and after the project completion with 15%of the total beneficiaries taking part.They will be randomly selected from all the payams. While additional data will be obtained through focus group discussions especially the farmer groups for informed responses and future interventions.

The project will apply all principles of inclusivity in that all the affected populations including those are are displaced will benefit from the project so long as they are within the target area.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project has a capacity building component where the beneficiaries will be trained in varioues agricultural techniques. This will enable them to acquire skills needed for food production even beyond the project cycle. The demonstration plots to be initiated by this project will be linked to county department of agriculture for continuity. FOCOSS will try to continue building the capacity of ToTs in charge of the demo plots so that they transform the demo plots into integrated farmer field schools/ centers.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?
6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The working environment in Ikotos County is stable and safe as there no cases of insecurity witnessed for the last 6 months.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project has a hand over/ exit plan in place. FOCOSS will involve State and county government line ministries/ department, especially the ministry of Agriculture and Livestock from the onset of the project to the phase out in order to ensure sustainability of the project intervention. During the last quarter of the project, FOCOSS will embark of rolling out the phase out plan where the demonstration plots will be linked to the ministry for use as agricultural extension Centers at Payam level.
