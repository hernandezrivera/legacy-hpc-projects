Project Summary

IOM will undertake a wide range of activities that include transferrable and vocational skills development for Rohingyas to build their resilience and facilitate their sustainable reintegration in Myanmar when conditions become conducive. Such activities will help beneficiaries meet basic needs (food security, income generation). IOM will arrange in-kind asset support for agriculture and other activities to the Rohingya beneficiaries helping them to utilize the skills and gain a modest self-reliance.
The host community support will integrate disaster resilient and green entrepreneurship and livelihoods options into the transferable and vocational skills development activities to improve the resilience of the beneficiaries to external shocks, minimize environmental degradation and increase food security-income. The activities will include provision of trainings on disaster mitigation, disaster resilient crops-seeds, tools & methods for agriculture and other sectors, waste recycling, electronic refurbishment and other vocational and non-vocational skills that are not susceptible to disasters but relevant to markets and economic sectors (e.g. computer skills, tourism, customer service, business skills, plumbing, AC repair, secretary, call center, patient administration, catering).
The targeted vulnerable women beneficiaries who have technical skills but cannot or do not want to work outside their houses will receive Productive Asset Support (PAS) to gain a modest income. The activities include five days of basic business skills training through which beneficiaries prepare basic business plans. Then, the business plan is evaluated by a selection committee which will be comprised of both women and men. The business plans which demonstrate economic feasibility and chance of making a profit will be selected, and productive assets for starting the business may be purchased and delivered to beneficiaries by IOM or implementing partners.
Beneficiaries will also receive small business grants through Entrepreneurship Program to the existing Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) that can help them adapt and keep their businesses going through the current COVID-19 crisis or other potential shocks. Additionally, it can support to adapt and shift businesses into potential ecofriendly businesses (solar light production, eco-friendly retailing, organic catering, sustainable items for construction, eco-friendly landscaping, sustainable/eco-friendly event planning, bicycle repairing or refurbishing, handcrafted organic or natural products).
In addition to PAS and grant support, beneficiaries will receive assistance to develop their institutional capacities through the establishment of women's cooperatives (such as agriculture, livestock and handicrafts) empowering the beneficiaries to take joint business decisions, collective investments and profit sharing. The activity will benefit 2,300 host community beneficiaries.
IOM will help to expand market linkages with the private sector, including labor market, value chain, and unpaid care analysis for the host community that can enable small producers to sell their products to individuals and wholesalers. In addition, IOM will work on empowering young people by helping them find decent work through supporting life and vocational skills and job-matching livelihoods.

1 - Identified Needs

While previous investments contributed to the socio-economic development of Cox's Bazar District, COVID-19 and the heavy floods in July - August 2021 had a tremendous negative impact on the livelihoods of the local population. COVID-19 caused both temporary lockdown-induced job losses and permanent job losses. Temporary lockdown-induced job losses in Bangladesh were estimated to be between 12 and 17 million jobs. The shutdown of the major sectors of the economy created gaps as seven million jobs in the micro, small and medium enterprises (MSME) sector, five million jobs in the transport sector, three million jobs in the construction sector, and three million jobs in the manufacturing sector temporarily disappeared. In addition, ten million people have become jobless in the agriculture sector. According to J-MSNA 2021 for the host communities, regarding the Livelihoods, 89% of families reported negative coping mechanisms due to a lack of funds to meet their basic needs. Heavy floods over July-August 2021 destroyed almost 50% of the investments that beneficiaries had in livelihoods and self -reliance in Ukhiya and Teknaf, including loss of property, shelters, assets and even personal belongings. While the COVID-19 outbreak additionally disrupted agriculture, livestocks and fishery sectors in Cox’s Bazar. In addition, given the low rates of female labour force participation, women appear to be disproportionately affected by the structural economic inequalities and by the pandemic and have experienced higher job losses. In Cox’s Bazar, women have been more likely to look for work. Nearly all Rohingya refugees living in Cox's Bazar still depend on humanitarian services for survival. Humanitarian agencies provide the material resources needed for survival, such as food to meet basic nutritional and caloric needs and materials for constructing simple bamboo and tarpaulin shelters. However, despite this basic aid, ongoing livelihood challenges propel camp residents to pursue other options to fulfill needs. This situation creates an atmosphere in which hopelessness and worry lead to risk-taking and endangerment. Households are trapped in a cycle of hardship, poverty, and indebtedness, which impairs their ability to be resilient and overcome challenges. Although livelihoods support aims to improve beneficiaries' financial income, it may also contribute to empowerment and self-reliance. Vulnerable communities can decrease socio-economic vulnerabilities through developing their capacities for self-reliance and strengthening the sustainability of their livelihood strategies. IOM will focus on supporting: 1) Approaches which include identifying host communities existing skills and livelihood priorities and providing skills helpful to adapt to pandemics, disasters, or crises (human and social capital – social self-reliance). 2) Approaches which include supporting skills development/diversification and income generation to strengthen economic self-reliance (physical and financial capital, in addition to human and social) through improving capacity and supporting local communities to integrate into labour markets, whether through employability, entrepreneurship, green businesses or support to the affected markets (natural capital, in addition to other types of capital). Meanwhile, for Rohingya refugees this phased process will be implemented through a tailored social and economic self-reliance support based on self-defined needs- starting with life skills and vocational skills development. The intervention will also focus on maintaining the skills that may been lost during displacement in order to prevent skills erosion and to assist refugees in gaining transferable skills that can be used upon their return to Myanmar.
