Project Summary

The project will ensure provision of timely, well-coordinated and integrated humanitarian intervention to 10,000 IDPs and returnees in Aweil East and Nasir Counties to improve their well-being, their resilience and participation in decision-making processes. The delivery of CCCM activities in informal sites will be conducted through the deployment of mobile/roving teams. Narrative Hub, a women-led organization, is quite conversant with CCCM cluster strategy and methodology having been trained by the cluster from 2021 to 2023. NH has implemented and Emergency GBV project in Nasir County, constructing two (2) women and girls’ friendly spaces (one in Jikmir and another in Nasir Town), supported by IOM/RRF and now, by Diakonie, while also currently implementing a UNDP supported transitional justice project in Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State and an IOM supported GBV in emergency project in Aweil East, running a women and girls friendly space in Majokyinthiu in Madhol Payam, and according to local assessment reports and observation, CCCM support is needed. A multi-sector approach between CCCM, GBV, ES/SNFI and protection will be prioritized at the sites where needs will be commonly identified by the CCCM in coordination with ES/NFI and protection and all activities will be integrated during the implementation period to achieve the cluster set objectives in locations where needs are very highly identified. Narrative Hub has both static and mobile presence in the location providing protection services and therefore, the intervention will include services like support to GBV survivors, psychosocial support and raising awareness sessions for men, women, boys and girls, as well as mitigation measures. This will be done in the form of training. It will respond to emergency needs by empowering community and strengthening the capacities of local actors to have access to both essentials camp management service. Through CCCM Cluster support, the project will contribute significantly to service delivery in hard-to-reach areas of Nasir, and Aweil East counties, coordinating multi-sectorial assistance through organizing site level coordination meetings with community members from both displaced, returnees and host communities; conducting site maintenance and service mapping activities to support minimum living standard; and protection of people with special needs in coordination with actors; and strengthening community structure by training community members on CCCM and humanitarian response. Community engagement will be a priority to the project for purposes of accountability to the affected population (AAP) and strong complaint mechanisms will be established among others.

1. Needs

Nasir has been the center of two armed fights between government and Nuer Youths this year. Armed fighting between government and Nuer youth white army in Nasir first broke out in February, deteriorating the situation for women and girls. The conflict displaced about 29,500, some 15,000 of them women and girls, and more than half into Pandanyang (9,000), Torpuot (5,000), and Koat (8,000) alone. A 20th February 2024 tentative agreement reached at a meeting between the political leadership the community resolved that the government keep the army out of town for its own safety and for that of the population, allowing only soldiers’ wives out of the barracks. The resolution was regardless of the outcome of the community demand that the government relocates the barracks out of Nasir. But fighting again erupted in August 2024, affecting 40,000 households (Inter-Agency Rapid Needs Assessment to the Conflict Displaced andFlood Affected Population in Greater Nasir County, 2024). Women and girls lack hygiene kits. Reports of violence against women and girls are on the rise. Since the breakout of several conflicts in Nasir that resulted to displacement, women and girls’ vulnerability increased, according to the Rapid assessment which was conducted on 2nd may 2024, the findings shows high number of displacements, that has resulted to shortage of food, domestic violence at household level due to too much consumption of alcohol, drug abuse, lack of health facilities. The recent returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia due to war and recent flooding in Nasir has increased issues of mental and psychological challenges among women and girls. According to Humanitarian Situation Overview in Aweil East and Northern Bahr el Ghazal conducted by REACH South Sudan in October 2024, since April 2023, South Sudan has received more than 800,000 people displaced by fighting in Sudan. Although the large majority - roughly 80% - have crossed the border through a single town in Upper Nile State, intensified fighting in the neighboring Kordofan region of Sudan in 2024 has resulted in the emergence of another major entry area further west, in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State. The Majokyinthiu border crossing in Aweil East county is now the second most trafficked point of entry from Sudan nationwide, and Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State one of the most common settlement areas for new arrivals. Aid organizations have recorded more than 50,000 people crossing the border into Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State, while tens of thousands more have arrived from elsewhere in the country. The growing use of “informal” entry points has complicated efforts to track people crossing the border, as established routes are increasingly deemed too unsafe for travel. Findings suggest that gaps in monitoring along the Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State border have left many South Sudanese “returnees” - who comprise the large majority of arrivals - without humanitarian assistance since their initial displacement. Food, shelter, and healthcare were commonly identified as priority unmet needs. Large-scale arrivals from Sudan, steep price increases, and atypically severe flooding are exacerbating an already critical humanitarian situation. Available data indicate that, in parts of Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State, rates of acute malnutrition have increased significantly over the past 12 months, while severe acute food insecurity is widespread. This population, particularly is in urgent need of CCCM services. A number of protection concerns were noted in the settlement areas. Most of the families came without any household items. Many are sharing spaces without any privacy to women and girls.

2. Project activities

TThe following activities will be implemented under the four cluster objectives: CO1: Safe and dignified access to services at site or area level • Establish/facilitate functional site management teams • Provide CCCM services to beneficiaries by coordinating humanitarian response in camps and camp-like settings, monitoring service provision at site level and advocating on needs, and working with service providers to ensure an equitable and needs-based response. • Establish/facilitate static facilities providing specific services to vulnerable groups and PWSNs to ensure protection standards through regular site monitoring, community engagement, and site development • Provide case referral through the site/area level referral mechanism • Conduct safety audits in coordination with site level protection actors • Provide flood mitigation and preparedness works • Establish/facilitate/manage reception and transit centers CO2: Ensure and facilitate access to two-way communication pathways for displaced population • Establish functional feedback and complaints mechanisms at the sites and ensure the process of registering and receiving complaints are accessible and known to all site residents. • Conduct awareness raising campaigns to ensure that the displaced are aware of the assistance and services available in the camp and how to access them, and ensuring that the distribution points are accessible and the mechanisms for distribution has taken into consideration groups with specific needs to minimize chances of violence, abuse and exploitation within the camps • Set up and maintain a camp information management system including sex- and age-disaggregated data collection and sharing. CO3: Strengthen community self-organization and community cohesion • Establish functional and inclusive community committees and self-governance structures • Conduct trainings for community committees, site leadership and authorities • Provide cash for work to HHs from community-based projects aimed at site development, infrastructure improvement, site risk reduction or livelihood activities • Develop joint community-based environmental management plans. CO4: Support integrated approaches to develop phase-out/transition strategies, resilience, and durable solutions • Develop phase-out/ transition strategies together with other CCCM partners • Conduct joint intention surveys supported by CCCM partners • Conduct regular comprehensive site profiling exercises. • Support/facilitate peaceful coexistence and/or durable solutions community-led projects.
