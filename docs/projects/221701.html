Project Summary

It is estimated that approximately 1.17 million people will be affected by housing, land, and property (HLP) issues in 2025 across 39 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area. The situation is particularly challenging for 86,000 people in need, including 16,000 IDPs, 25,000 returnees, and 45,000 residents (SSD HNO 2024). However, with the ongoing influx of returnees and refugees from the Sudan crisis, HLP issues are likely to escalate.
Despite the guarantees in the RTCSS regarding women’s rights to land and property, South Sudanese women continue to face significant barriers to land ownership and have limited access rights, often dependent on their relationships with men. Women play a crucial role in agricultural production, and given the high number of female-headed households, it is essential to strengthen and secure their land rights to ensure livelihood, food and nutrition security, and the productive use of land for economic development (South Sudan Draft National Land Policy 2023, p.41).
Contributing factors to the increased risk of HLP issues include displacement history, the return of refugees from Sudan and Ethiopia, ongoing conflict, the proliferation of unauthorized weapons among criminal groups, secondary occupations, land grabbing, flooding, a lack of widespread knowledge of HLP rights, forced evictions, the loss of documentation and title deeds, disputes over customary land tenure, population growth, the transition of PoC sites to IDP camps, the increasing number of returnees from neighboring countries like Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia, and Sudan, and pandemics like COVID-19.
If these issues are not addressed through timely interventions, they are likely to lead to further displacement and conflicts, severely affecting families and children. As such, this HLP project will target 13,230 beneficiaries (6,880 women, 6,350 men) with HLP awareness-raising and information-sharing activities. CINA will conduct outreach to identify individuals in need, refer them to appropriate services, and inform communities about available HLP services. Legal aid and support, including non-cash and financial assistance, will be provided to achieve HLP outcomes. The project will also establish or strengthen dispute resolution mechanisms and train local authorities and relevant staff to participate in these processes.
Throughout all stages of implementation, CINA will comply with national laws and international HLP standards, adhere to the Code of Conduct, and implement a Community Complaint and Feedback Mechanism. The Do No Harm Principle and the centrality of protection will guide the project, ensuring accountability to affected populations before, during, and after implementation. The project will be implemented with technical assistance from the South Sudan HLP Area of Responsibility (AoR), in coordination with Protection Partners and relevant authorities, with an estimated budget of $500,000 to address the HLP needs of IDPs, returnees, and resident communities.

1. Needs

In South Sudan, Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) issues are both a consequence and a cause of conflict. The Sudan Crisis, climate change impacts, armed conflict between militias, and intercommunal violence have exacerbated many of the risks associated with HLP issues, particularly in areas such as Jonglei (Fangak, Uror, Pibor: Tier 1) and Upper Nile (Nasir/Luakpiny, Malakal, and Renk: Tier 1) (Protection Cluster SSD HNRP Locations Prioritization 2024). This is evidenced by the widespread destruction and looting of homes and properties, degradation of housing due to displacement, secondary occupation, forced evictions, loss of documentation and title deeds, and disputes over customary land tenure—features that characterize the ongoing humanitarian crisis in South Sudan (PC SSD HNRP 2024 - Guidance Note). The spontaneous return of IDPs and refugees from Sudan is increasing as the government works to accommodate them in various locations, resulting in a rise of HLP issues, particularly in areas of return. Illegal occupation and land grabbing of properties, such as houses and land, are expected to increase, potentially fueling subnational and localized violence and conflict along identity lines in some regions. There is also a lack of awareness regarding HLP rights among local authorities and affected populations. Displaced and returnee populations, especially women and child-headed households, face significant barriers in accessing legal assistance, limited court systems, or alternative dispute resolution mechanisms to claim their rights. Access to HLP, a critical issue during years of protracted conflict, remains a pressing concern as people are displaced further, the population grows, PoC sites begin to transition, and the number of South Sudanese refugees returning from neighboring countries like Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia, and Sudan increases. Additionally, the formalization of land laws and the creation of mechanisms for their enforcement remain gaps, obstructing people who wish to return. The lack of widespread knowledge of HLP laws and insufficient legal ownership documentation makes it difficult for many people to understand and effectively advocate for their rights. Worst of all, women, children, and people with disabilities disproportionately struggle to assert their rights to housing and land. Despite legal provisions recognizing women’s equal rights to land, the recognition and protection of these rights remain limited throughout South Sudan. Women’s land rights are often conditional, and dispossession of widows, daughters, and divorced women is common. HLP issues pose a major challenge to creating conditions for durable solutions for displaced populations and fostering peace within communities. In light of increasing land-related disputes, and the anticipated returns, relocations, and reintegration of IDPs and refugees, addressing HLP issues will be a critical priority for South Sudan in 2025 and beyond.

2. Project activities

Activity 1. CINA will conduct 13 sessions of HLP awareness and information-sharing activities aimedat identifying and timely referring individuals to appropriate services, as well as informing communitiesabout available services. These people include IDPs, returnees and Resident communities. The awareness and information sharing will center on the issues affecting the community social cohesion and what can bedone to avoid conflict in common places of social concerns. access to land and property is one of the main problems IDPs and returnees face in the area of returns. To facilitate HLP awareness raising activities, CINA will establish a HLP desk in the community center at each targeted locations (Fangak, Pibor, Uror, Nasir/Luakpiny, Malakal and Renk) managed by volunteers supervised by CINA HLP staff. They will conduct awareness and information sharing sessions on HLP and distribute the Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials for HLP issues, as well as identify and respond to cases. Particular focus will be given to women, who often do not have access to justice due to the lack of knowledge of their rights, availability of legal aid and associated payment fees. In addition, very few women are in a possession of land and even fewer who have documents confirming land ownership as highlighted in the National Draft Land Policy 2023. Activity 2: Provide legal Counselling to 666 people of concern. CINA will identify and prioritize HLP 666 beneficiaries to receive HLP support. Depending on the severity of the concern, the individual may receive more specialized support which might include additional legal advice or referrals to the authorities, whereas individuals who may not require case management will be referred to the HLP Help Desk. Through this component, beneficiaries will be supported to access both formal and informal justice mechanisms (individual cases follow up with law enforcement, courts). Where beneficiaries require referrals to external services, CINA will conduct direct payments to these services to ensure the beneficiaries are able to accessthis critical support. Activity 3. Provide HLP legal aid cash to 8,330 people of concern. Individualized legal assistance, pro bono services/case management will be provided for specific HLP cases. Activity 4. Provide HLP legal aid non-cash to 4,187 people of concern. Individualized legal assistance, pro bono services/case management will be provided for specific HLP cases. Activity 5. CINA will work with the community in IDP locations and areas of return to 16 establish alternative dispute resolution mechanisms/committees representative of IDPs, returnees and host communities. The 16 HLP ADR mechanisms will be set up in each location with the composition made up of local leaders,representatives of minority groups and people who have knowledge of disputes related to HLP after attending the awareness sessions. Activity 6. Conduct Capacity Building trainings to 320 Local authorities and relevant institution's staff on HLP, dispute resolution and ADR.
