Project Summary

International Medical Corps planned to provide a need-based emergency lifesaving intervention which is in line with HRP to the targeted 12 Woerdas of Oromia, Afar and Amhara Regions. The woredas selected based on a rapid assessment which confirmed the higher malnutrition prevalence, IDP settlement and being affected by recent shock which is conflict and drought.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) informs International Medical Corps’ accountability to affected populations (AAP) strategy and it is a full member of the CHS Alliance and is committed to upholding the nine commitments of the CHS. International Medical Corps Ethiopia will establish a strong community-based feedback and complaints mechanism (CBFRM) to ensure accountability to affected populations throughout the implementation of the project based on the IMC Ethiopia CBFRM guidelines and the experience International Medical Corps has had in different project locations.To ensure accountability and needs-based adaptations are considered in the proposed project, consultation meetings will be conducted with the affected communities and stakeholders including women, men, boys, girls, old age and PWD at the beginning of proposed project on the design, implementation, and during the regular review of the complaints-handling processes. An understanding of community preferences will ensure the establishment of appropriate mechanisms that can be accessed so that community members can safely use the mechanisms to submit feedback and complaints to International Medical Corps about program quality and staff behaviors and crisis- affected communities will be aware of complaints mechanisms established for their use. Information gathered through feedback and complaint mechanisms will be registered in a logbook and reviewed and responded to in a timely manner based on the sensitivity and urgency of the complaint or feedback. Realistic and agreed-upon action plans will be developed to ensure beneficiaries’ complaints and feedback are incorporated and correction will be made whenever necessary during the implementation period. The feedback information received will be used to improve performance, coverage, and effectiveness of the project activities and will help feed learning back into the organization on an ongoing basis. Complaints that do not fall within the scope of the organization will be referred to a relevant party in manner with consistent with good practices and local coordination agreements and referral pathways.To maintain confidentiality, information related to sensitive complaints is only shared on a strict need-to-know basis. All information related to a sensitive complaint is securely stored with controlled access limited to authorized individuals. IMC policy, procedures, and local CBFRM procedures for reporting sensitive complaints will be also employed in the proposed project areas. Staff will be routinely sensitized on how to handle sensitive complaints. Sensitive complaints including physical or sexual abuse will be referred to the applicable headquarter case management team through our Ethics Point web portal within 24 hours of receipt, and that victims of abuse have their immediate health and safety needs evaluated and addressed. In addition, IMC will communicate with affected populations in languages, and media that are easily understood, respectful, and culturally appropriate for different members of the community, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups.During the project period communities in the project area will be aware about the expected behavior of humanitarian staff, including organizational commitments made on the prevention of sexual exploitation.Throughout implementation and monitoring of the CBFRM, community consultations will be repeated periodically for on-going programs during implementation to validate if established CBFRM channels adequately meet the changing needs of the crisis-affected community. These consultations will include focus group discussions with communities and key informant interviews with key stakeholders.
