Project Summary

Globally, people are forced to flee their natural abode to settle temporarily in camps and smaller settlements as a result of one crises or the other. Organized camp or settlement become very important to make life easy for the settler. Camps and such settlement becomes a place where stranded persons’ gets in contact with humanitarian actors who helps to manage such camps and settlement to ensure that resources like food, water, sanitation, education, shelter, health services are provided and managed. This gives rise to Camp Coordination and Camp Managers (CCCM) whose overall goal is to improve living conditions during displacement while seeking and advocating for durable solutions to end camp-life and organize closure and phase-out of camps upon IDP return. CCCM is applied in camps (spontaneous and planned), camp-like settings, and communal settlements, but does not include host families. Though camps and communal settlements are temporary sites that should be established only as a last resort, it is important to note that they are becoming permanent features all and around the globe adorned with it numerous crises and challenges. Northeast Nigeria is not left out of the scene as people have been leaving in camp for more than a decade now and this call for proper camp coordination and camp management so that the intended purpose of making life easier for Persons of concerns (PoCs) can be achieved.
Currently, Over 1.9 million people are internally displaced with over 80% percent residing in Borno State. About 200,000 people are still in Cameroon, Chad and Niger, about 20,000 killed, thousands of women and girls abducted and children drafted as so-called "suicide" bombers into the insurgency as a result of insurgency occasion by boko haram activities in the north eastern part of Nigeria. (Humanitarian Need Overview 2019)
Since the security situation in the north-east is expected to remain fragile, setting up and management of camps to cater for IDP and refugees rank very high among humanitarian activities. Therefore, the continuous monitoring of camps, areas of return to ensure that issues arising from displacement are identified, and interventions planned and implemented in close collaboration with national authorities, other humanitarian stakeholders and the affected communities are inevitable. These camps that have been established need serious planning, coordination administration and management.
Being already present in all of the proposed areas of intervention, working closely alongside government authorities (NEMA, SEMA, LEMA), UNHCR and other partners, and the population of concerns covering protection, education, livelihood and GBV, FESYD is well placed to take the responsibility to provide the well needed camp coordination camp management needed to actually delivered a better living for the PoCs. To ensure this FESYDP has a dedicated camp management staff that can ensure, a more predictable and coordinated delivery of service. This can enhance participation, foster and accountability between the affected populations, aid providers and the governments, improve protective environment. FESYDP will ensure a structured Camp management that will be instrumental in empowering the affected populations to organize and mobilize their communities, make tangible contributions to the delivery of assistance and to make informed decisions on durable solutions.
