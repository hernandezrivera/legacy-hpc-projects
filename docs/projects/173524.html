Project Summary

The project has two components targeting Urban refugees and Asylm seekers in Khartoum and Umbada Localities and the new comers of the Tagray fleeing from the Ethiopian war in Um Rakoba Gadarif State.
The general humanitarian situation in Sudan is a complex mix of asylum seekers, refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), Sudanese refugee returnees and IDP returnees. Sudan is also a source, transit and destination country for mixed migratory population movements especially from their neighbors The general situation for refugees has a multi-dimensional of poverty that encompasses not just low income, but also vulnerability, insecurity, and inability to express their needs. Food accessibility is a challenge for almost all refugees due to lack of a sustainable source of income, poverty and vulnerability which resulted from them fleeing with no assets, food, money nor livestock. An estimated 30% of urban refugees in Khartoum are living in poverty specially during pandemic of COVID 19 and the economic impact. UNHCR Participatory Assessments indicate high unemployment among the refugee population with majority relying on humanitarian assistance. With just 1 per cent of refugees able to afford the local food basket, livelihood access gaps compound food insecurity for refugee communities. Sudan also applies the Arab/Islamic notions of asylum with respect to some refugee nationalities. Currently this is applied to Syrian, Yemeni, Iraqi and Palestinian nationals. Refugees of these nationalities are allowed to enter and remain in the country and are notionally treated as nationals in terms of freedom of movement, choice of residence and access to social amenities, including health and education. Access to employment is also substantively easier for refugees of these nationalities. Syrian and Yemeni refugees are slightly better off due to their relatively easy access to work permits. However, the majority of South Sudanese, Eritrean and Ethiopian refugees rely on informal labor opportunities facing challenges or high risk access work permits despite recent Government directive on refugee eligibility to work. The reasons behind this high rate of unemployment were not registered, physically unfit, and limited ability to speak Arabic, limited employment opportunities, lack of productive assets, and many do not have sufficient technical and entrepreneurial skills, recent arrival to Khartoum. Scale-up of targeted refugee livelihoods programming, is needed to improve household resilience and reduce need for financial assistance and other service assistance
In November more than 45,000 Tigray people flee to Sudan crossing the border are in desperate need of humanitarian assistance and services and the number is growing. They were transferred to camps with more infrastructure at UmRakoba to access safety and security The situation is critical and need urgent life-saving assistance including shelter, Blankets, Kitchen sets that will protect women from harassment, Dignity kits and mosquito nets

1 - Needs

The proposed project is designed to contribute in reducing the socioeconomic vulnerability and provide protection support awareness of 700 HH refugees of different populations (South Sudanese, Syrians, Yemenis, and EEC, 560HH refuges and 140 HH from host community Khartoum state and provide Emergency shelter support for Ethiopian refugees in Um Rakoba Gadarif stateMost urban refugees earn their living by informal economy, and they are badly affected by the impact of pandemic - COVID-19lock down especially refugee’s women lose their jobs and suffer from the severe GBV by their partners. Being in camps South Sudanese refugees are more affected by the pandemic economically and are unavailable of most effective interventions to protect themselves against COVID-19 as frequent hand washing, adherence to social-distancing guidelines, and wearing a mask, access of clean water or soap, and menstrual health and hygiene. Most women are suffering from the severe GBV by their partners.BPWVO will support and sustain good quality formal jobs, but also to explore how we can bring security and a decent job to those working in often inherently insecure sectors, helping them to take steps towards more formal work, by increasing resilience and self-reliance of refugees and host communities through access to secured, sustainable livelihood opportunities, as well as support refugees integration into local communities to develop viable business plan initiatives based on the market and labor market demands; and to access decent work which will bring an effective approach to livelihood strategies demonstration and validated development in selected areas through vocational training courses microcredit and other services, to facilitate and find opportunities to work and earn and improve family income and effective ways to rebuild their lives with dignity and in peace. This will be reflected on the financial inclusion of active vulnerable refugees to access quality livelihood straining for employment purpose services. Eritrean Refugees reported the difficulty of enrolling their children in school for the high prices of the fees and special hours for the eight elementary and the third secondary grades Education enrollment services BPWVO is planning to intervene through livelihood , objective is to contribute to build self-reliance and increase resilience and minimize vulnerabilities among refugees and host communities in target areas of Khartoum locality and Umbada , intended response include provision of vocation training support and startup kits, the projected intended to cover 700 POCs (60% female and 40% male, including adolescent). Livelihood intervention include vocational training on activities selected by the beneficiaries during focused group discussion conducted in May 2019 by BPWVO as follows: maintenance, Tailoring, carpentry, hairdressing and Henna, Food processing, general electricity, mechanics, Auto electricity. (120 females and 80 male) Also BPWO will support 500 Tigray refugees HH who flee from their region crossing the border with their children to Sudan leaving all their properties and assets behind They are in need of all the basic services to survive. BPWV will provide them life-saving assistance including shelter, Blanckets , Kitchen sets that will protect women from harassment, Dignity kits, and mosquito nets.
