Project Summary

Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria and Warrap States, like most parts of South Sudan experience perennial food insecurity that is fueled among other things by poor or lack of resilience to impacts of Climate Change on food production and protection of livelihoods and to some extent conflicts.
According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO, 2022), the magnitude and severity of food insecurity are anticipated to increase and worse in 2022/2023 compared to 2021/2022. An estimated 12,374 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity from December 2022 to July 2023. This represents a 33% increase from the 8.4 million people in need in 2022. The current economic crisis is impacting people’s ability to access markets, while conflict, flooding, drought and seasonal effects are limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services. Continued and increased sub-national violence reported in many states across the country, including Upper Nile and Warrap (particularly Greater Tonj), will continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade. This project will target 15% (39,250 individuals) of the total households under phase 4 (emergency).
The communities of Upper Nile and Warrap have not been spared of these effects. Maiwut, Ulang, Nasir Counties and in Greater Tonj have experienced combinations of year-by-year flooding and inter community conflicts. Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East and North) is experiencing drought. This has led to displacement, destruction of crops, livestock, shelters, and disruption of other livelihoods. Livestock die while those that survive are affected by waterborne diseases. Because livestock are the main livelihood for the pastoralist communities, vulnerability to food insecurity increases for them. Factors contributing to the situation include “a combination of shocks; floods, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19” and the effects of Russia/Ukraine war. These communities have also been affected by continued high food prices due to protracted macro-economic crisis which have heightened poverty & vulnerability brought about by the combination of the above factors.
Intercommunity conflicts have contributed to poor coexistence between communities and among the IDP, returnee and host populations. This has adversely affected women and children as displacements and movement pause direct effect to livelihoods and protection, particularly agricultural and fishing endeavors. Instability is not conducive to sustaining food security as food production activity is disrupted or threatened.
Adventist Development and Relief Agency (ADRA) will design consulted means of creating peace among communities through dialogues and support of community level peace building efforts. Adventist Development and Relief Agency (ADRA) will take the learning from the ‘Peace Infrastructure’ created in the community of Maiwut and Tonj South over the years to advocate for the returnee and IDP communities in the other proposed counties for peaceful core-existence with the host community. ADRA will also deliberately take action in form of supporting the existing government and community level structures in further capacity building them to ensure resilient programming is effective.
In project implementation the project will use a community- based approaches. These will be made up of participatory extension approaches that include the farmer field school; Lead farmer/Farmer/farmer approach innovation platforms, village-based demonstration sites, farmer market school & Exposure and learning visits underpinned by a layering approach. The project will build on previous interventions and experiences, which was successfully implemented by ADRA South Sudan. Farmers, pastoralists, fishermen and traders, have been empowered through various modern agriculture trainings.

1 - Needs

Conflict, climate shocks and economic instability in Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria and Greater Tonj have continued to exacerbate food insecurity in those regions and South Sudan. IPC Projections in Nasir, Ulang, Maiwut (UNS), Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North (Eastern Equatoria) and Tonj North, Tonj South, Tonj East (Warrap) ranged between 59%, 45% and 57% of crisis, emergency and humanitarian catastrophe respectively (December 2022 and March 2023). The situation is likely to worsen from April to July with Eastern Equatoria reaching 51%, Upper Nile 69.6% and Warrap 68.2%. Ensuing intercommunal conflicts and climate variabilities (drought in Kapoeta East & North and floods in Upper Nile & Warrap) has led to increased displacement in the counties, increasing food insecurity, and decreased levels of resilience making them part of the most vulnerable counties. The country is experiencing seasonal flooding and drought causing extreme destruction to lives, livestock and livelihoods of nearly 1,966,000 under emergency and castrophe phase (33000 in Jonglei State). The flood water in some locations destroyed homes, crops, displaced families, rendered basic services and markets non-functional, particularly in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and parts of Central Equatoria. Basic infrastructure has also been damaged, including boreholes now contaminated with tainted water. Preliminary assessments show that cultivated land has been damaged due to flooding in affected areas especially UNS and Warrap. There is need for immediate humanitarian response to these communities through distribution of food, vegetable seeds for the dry season and distribution of fishing kits, livestock vaccination and disaster risk reduction activities (DRR), (IRNA Report, 2-9 August 2022, Gogrial West). Conflict in South Sudan leads to a significant drop in economic growth, exacerbating poverty and food insecurity situation. UN Agencies call for a conceited effort to address root cause of malnutrition in South Sudan, Severe, multi-year flood events surging sub national violence and economic pressures are pushing more people into worsening levels of acute food insecurity. (South Sudan key context update 12-18 October 2022). As a result, the country has reported the largest number of refugees and internally displaced people, who experience greater levels of food insecurity. Food insecurity invariably is exacerbated when fighting drives large numbers of people from their homes and their sources of livelihoods. Conflicts have adverse effects on hunger, nutrition, sustainable development, peace and security. Yet, levels of crop and vegetable production remain low, and local food consumption is heavily dependent on imports. Many conflicts in Greater Tonj are because of competition for water for domestic use and livestock. In Tonj and UNS, water can allow agriculture to adapt along the ‘intensive margin’ of production—ADRA intensification pathway has been the conversion of rain fed flood prone areas to cropping adaptation and crop genetic improvement, partial irrigation areas. Water-harvesting intensification may be an option, ADRA will promote construction of multipurpose ponds, shallow wells and flood control dykes to decongest conflict prone water sources in these counties. However, in all instances, the environmental consequences should be considered. ADRA will promote a mixed crop–livestock farming system, incorporating stock feed supplements of fodder production for dominant pastoralists often cited as a threat to food security.

2 - Project activities

1. Distribution of inputs: vegetable, crop and fish inputs will be distributed to 49,350 individuals from the cluster target households falling under IPC phase 4 (emergency) in the eight counties. Of these households, 60% of the target group will be vulnerable women and men smallholder farmers including returnees. IDPs will constitute 20% of the total while young people also constitute 20%. 2. Demonstration sites: The project will establish a minimum of 12 crop and vegetable demonstration sites across each Payam. These will be centres for interaction for farmers, extension agents and private sector players (input suppliers, agro equipment suppliers) for knowledge sharing and field level testing of appropriate technologies. 3. Exposure and learning visits and events: The project will support field days, intra- and inter-county learning visits with the latter bringing together farmers who experience the same physical, climatic, social, economic and cultural characteristics. 4. Training in agronomic practices: One lead farmer will be selected from each group of 20 farmers formed through self-selection. The project will facilitate a Training of Trainers (ToT) for Agroecology and Farmer Field Schools (FFS) for four Agriculture Extension officers and a Crop Specialist at County level. 5. Promote Improved Post Harvest Management Practices/Value addition, preservation and storage: Famers in South Sudan loose about 30% of the harvested grain due to lack of appropriate post-harvest and storage facilities and knowledge. Farmers are forced to sell most of their grain soon after harvesting at very low prices, leaving them poor and vulnerable. ADRA will use its experience from previous interventions to implement the use of post-harvest equipment which includes solar driers and use of crop hermetic bags. 6. Pasture and Grazing Management (fodder banks/grazing land reinforcement) and Livestock Marketing Improved: The deaths due to limited grazing of cattle, overgrazed pastures and poor-quality pastures presents an opportunity to lure men into mixed farming strategy that can respond well to food insecurity that is affecting the region. Communities will conduct a participatory assessment of the grazing area to establish the state of the rangeland in order to come up with activities that address the immediate challenges. These challenges will be addressed through the FFS and demonstrations for pasture and grazing management. A TOT will be conducted for Veterinary and Agriculture extension staff on fodder production, management, harvesting and preservation. Training will include silage making, baling lablab leaves and feed formulations. 7. Monitoring and Evaluating Project Activities: The project will develop an MEAL Framework that includes an MEAL plan, indicator tracking, survey data where all the payams/ bomas will feed information. Key to sustainability is the farmers’ ability to monitor their own project, evaluate their performance and proffer solutions. Lead farmers/Farmer facilitators with the assistance of government extension agents at Payam level and ADRA will form community-based monitoring and evaluation (CBME) committees. These will be trained on basic participatory MEAL tools, data collection and the interpretation of collected data.

Upper Nile and Warrap States are dominated by pastoralist, the project will target livestock with vaccinations to curb diseases and achieve a healthy herd, so that communities are able to sustain their livelihoods through livestock, in the target area. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ADRA concede that most of the community members targeted by the project and throughout South Sudan have little or no formal education. The community based participatory planning acts as a substitute for project formal education, enabling communities to articulate their plans, experiences and to critically examine project issues and policies affecting their food security and lives. ADRA prioritizes the needs of the disadvantaged during assessments and project implementation of food security and livelihoods projects. Particularly people living with disabilities, child or female headed households and the vulnerable. To ensure that everyone's concerns (including people living with disabilities) are properly understood, ADRA will utilize project review meetings to get feedback directly from participants including providing mobile contact (hotlines). In all instances, a Project Management Committee will be established to handle complains or disputes and the local level. The Complains Mechanism forms and the suggestion box can also be used by illiterate or semi illiterate by way of using a trusted community member or Project Management Committee member. This will enable women and other aggrieved parties to speak their mind about the project, its implementation and concerns. Participants can identify a respected individuals in the community to whom they can lodge a complaint to ADRA. A Help Desk (stationary/ mobile) at every community gathering can be used. ADRA expanded the beneficiary feedback system through its hotline number which offers beneficiaries a platform for comments, complaints and feedback. The beneficiary feedback hotline allows project participants to communicate concerns directly but anonymously to ADRA Country Office

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ADRA prioritizes the needs of women and young children during assessments and general food distributions and other food security and livelihoods projects especially female headed households. Achieving gender balance among ADRA personnel and outsourced remains a challenge, despite the use of quotas as a condition in recruitment. To ensure women's concerns are properly understood, ADRA has put in place innovative measures, including providing mobile contact, Complains Mechanism forms and the suggestion box. This will enable women and other aggrieved parties to speak their mind about the project, its implementation and concerns. During implementation, participants can identify a respected woman in the community who can collect information to share with ADRA. In all instances, there is always a Help Desk (stationery/ mobile) at every gathering that is elected for that particular event. In order to inform beneficiaries about their entitlements, and to improve transparency and accountability, ADRA expanded the beneficiary feedback system through its hotline number which offers beneficiaries a platform for comments, complaints and feedback. The beneficiary feedback hotline allows women to communicate concerns directly but anonymously to ADRA Country Office.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The presence of a gender and protection officer has ensured that staff members in all ADRA Food Security& Livelihoods projects are trained on gender issues in the program cycle. ADRA has been in Protection Clusters and has experience with cultural and gender sensitive programming issues. All these measures are important, as in some cases men are opposed to food or economic entitlements being placed in the hands of women, and a level of sensitization is necessary. ADRA will always create awareness of PSEA issues through campaigns within the communities. At gathering part of the messaging will include Protection of all vulnerable groups, women, children and people living with disability. In the project design, community consultation will be done to ensure the needs of women, men, girls and boys and people living with disabilities are incorporated. The selection of the beneficiaries will be in accordance with the protection standards of ensuring a certain percentage of women and men are benefiting. The Project Management Committee (PMC) and the Community Volunteers (CVs) will include both males and females at community level and these will be trained on PSEA. ADRA will work with the committees to disseminate information during gatherings and within the communities. Complaint mechanisms will be established and all vulnerable groups will participate in the dialogue sessions on issues being raised on protection and other issues. ADRA will design IEC materials using infographics to put across protection messages and reporting mechanisms.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The programme team will share the information during monthly staff meetings so that all staff members benefit and learn from it. Information will also be shared with significant stakeholders at district and community levels. Information elicited from the evaluation will form the basis of our quarterly plans especially to close the gap established. The information shall provide the basis for further planning and plugging of gaps that may be envisaged. Good practices will also be shared and possibility of replication discussed. The practices will also be shared with other implementing agencies through the FSL Cluster for possible adoption and further support to existing project. Collected Most Significant Change Stories will be utilised to evaluate the quality of project services. In light of the fact that this is a community based project where implementation utilizes community systems and structures, it therefore implies that communities will take full charge of the project and ensure outcomes exceed expectations. The aforementioned is premised on the fact that the project will capacity-strengthen the communities to deal with issues affecting them for project ownership. ADRA has long experience in working with and supporting the targeted communities and that helps to meet our project objectives because of its presence at grass roots. The relationship and goodwill that has been created by both parties through the passage of time allows ADRA to freely and openly discuss the highs and lows of the project. If recourse is needed then the organisation will do so with compromise.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will endeavor to protect vulnerable groups through setting up different complaint mechanism options including hotline, messaging for those who have phones and good network. During distributions of inputs and NFI, information/complaint desks will be set up where people can express their issues. This desk will be manned by local leadership, stakeholders and ADRA representatives. Suggestion boxes will be placed at points where vulnerable groups can access without any intimidation. During registration of beneficiaries, to make sure that the vulnerable are targeted especially old people and those living with disability, home to home visits will be conducted. For protection of women during distribution and meetings or trainings, ADRA will always ensure that meetings are dismissed when people can still travel during day light. The post distribution monitoring report will also deliberately identify the vulnerable groups so that their opinions and decisions are captured. The risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in Upper Nile and Warrap States, has caused some of the conflicts between communities. In Warrap state, development partners recently conducted assessments in Tonj North and East in a bid to settle the land dispute issues. The project will ensure participation of all stakeholders during the project start-up process and during implementation to have an inclusive and transparent programming. Partners will also conduct joint monitoring to have same level of information on the project. During the baseline survey (with the participation of all stakeholders), the project will identify clearly areas of operation to avoid contested land. The local authorities and traditional chiefs are expected to take the lead during identification of areas of operation. Through projects implemented in the past and some ongoing ones, proposed project will utilise the existing social structures for peace building and conflict management to mitigate this risk in the event that land disputes arise.
