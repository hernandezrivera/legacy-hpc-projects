Project Summary

In line with the mentioned needs, HRP, Reach reports and SPHERE standards, Nasaem will provide timely heating fuel for the coming winter. Nasaem will provide diesel in urban areas. However, in the rural areas and in line with SPHERE’s guidance note “Identify and prioritize environmentally and economically sustainable sources of fuel or domestic energy”, Nasaem will not provide firewood in rural areas as it is severely influencing the environment as people are resorting to excessive logging to provide this. Rather, Nasaem will provide olive-pit fuel which is known in the area, and has increasingly been used by the target communities during the crisis. This is a bio-fuel, locally produced and ecofriendly. In addition, Nasaem will distribute/replenish 500 heaters with accessories to those households that have no heaters or have theirs worn.
Under the proposed project Nasaem will distribute heating materials for 2000 of the most vulnerable households (70% IDPs, 30% host community) in Ariha, Ehsem, Muhambal, Al Mara and Jisr Ash-Shugur sub-districts of Idleb province. Out of these, 700 households in urban areas will receive 360 liters of diesel distributed over two batches throughout winter season. The other 1300 households living in rural areas will receive 900 kg of olive pits distributed over two batches during winter. To achieve the goal of this project, Nasaem will distribute/replenish heating diesel/Olive pits stoves for 500 households out these 2000 households receiving heating materials.

Needs

Since the onset of the conflict in 2011, hundreds of thousands of people have been killed. In addition, there is a huge number of children are facing an incredibly harsh winter in each year without even the most basic supplies; moreover, vulnerable households have exhausted all their resources and are struggling to survive. Humanitarian needs overview (HNO 2019) has concluded there is significant overcrowding in Idleb and north-west Aleppo. The (Draft HNO 2019) has also reported that despite the gradual improvement of accessibility to market and NFI items are available in markets, many essential, humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining basic items continue to be unaffordable for many people, including clothing and fuel. Also, Unilateral coercive measures have affected the fuel supply in the country affecting the accessibility. HNO 2019 also mentioned that there are 4.4 million people require NFI assistance as well as the number of people in need of NFI assistance in Idleb has increased by over 600,000. However, within the situation in some areas, such as the northwest, is in fact deteriorating amidst and increase in people in need. Furthermore, needs remain substantial, both in terms of humanitarian life-saving needs, particularly those among the high numbers of newly displaced IDPs requiring core, supplementary and seasonal assistance. An additional risk factor is the seasonal fuel scarcity and the resulting increase in fuel prices which are expected during the winter. In addition, There is a need for increased fuel provision, storage, contingency stock and winterization fuel for IDPs shelters/medical centers for cooking, heating and other essential services. Fuel has been reported to be a priority need by most age and gender groups with the Elderly (60+ y) citing it as the second priority need. This humanitarian crisis has been exacerbated by the increase of all basic needs and services. For instance, prices of fuel oil increased 10-fold from 2011 to 2015. The 2019 HNO mentioned 113,825 displacements since September 2018 in north-west Syria. Of those, 80,605 displacements were to Idleb. Between November and December 2018, Nasaem team conducted a household surveys with 1275 households and 35 KIIs in many districts in Idleb governorate in order to prioritize the needs and to better understand the situation and preferences of the target communities. The assessment covered male and female respondents. Out of 1275 households, 830 households (about 65%) stated that they prefer heating fuel over any other NFI items like clothes, kitchen sets… etc. 1090 households responded that they have heaters and only 190 households (about 15%) said they do not have heaters. When asked about the type of fuel they prefer, urban areas preferred diesel while rural areas preferred firewood mostly. The assessment focused on the vulnerability of crisis-affected populations and has also shown that the IDPs, elderly, the disabled, person with chronic diseases, Pregnant/Lactating Women and some female-headed households are the most vulnerable and hence Nasaem is prioritizing these groups of beneficiaries in this project. Furthermore, the HNO 2019 stated that the access to NFIs has generally become more stable over the last year, the findings show a decreased need for standard/core NFI items and a significant increase in requests for items specific to the situation faced by certain communities or population groups. Such items include clothing, fuel and batteries. Populations in areas which experience cold conditions during winter continue to need seasonal support, with communities surveyed reporting heating-related shelter deficiencies. the availability and affordability of the heating fuel remains limited in the local markets, that mean it constitutes the most priorities of the most people in need and it should be addressed.

Implementation of activities

In line with the above needs, draft HNO 2019, the Shelter and NFI assessment and SPHERE standards, Nasaem will provide timely heating fuel for the coming winter. Nasaem will provide diesel to people who are at the greatest needs in urban town. However, in the rural areas and in line with SPHERE’s guidance note “Identify and prioritize environmentally and economically sustainable sources of fuel or domestic energy”. Nasaem will provide olive-pit fuel which is known in the area, and has increasingly been used by the target communities during the crisis. This is a bio-fuel, locally produced and eco-friendly. According to the result framework that issued by the S/NFI cluster, stated that the provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs aims to protect the targeted beneficiaries from seasonally harsh conditions . In addition, Nasaem will distribute/replenish heaters with accessories to those households that have no heaters or have theirs worn. - Nasaem will notify the targeted communities, local councils and other stakeholders about the project. Nasaem will explain the aim of the project, the number of households in each community, main activities, selection criteria, amounts of materials, and start and end dates of the project. Nasaem will strive for an impartial, non-discriminatory, transparent mechanism for targeting those who are most at risk and in greatest need. Once the items are available, Nasaem will also make sure that vulnerable individuals and households can access both information and the distributions.- Nasaem will focus on vulnerability of crisis-affected population and determine the most people in need using the following selection criteria (Single female headed household caring for children, Elderly people, women headed households, IDPs who undergo multiple movement, pregnant/lactating Women and person with disability, and household with no source of income.- Nasaem will announce for tendering, contracting and procurement of heating fuel, Jerry cans, olive-pits and heaters.- Nasaem will make the distributions in two batches. The first distribution in November 2019, the second in December 2019.- Furthermore, Nasaem will establish a process to address grievance issues raised by both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries or problems relating to access to information or final distribution points.- Conducting post-distribution monitoring. The gender-balanced M&E teams will check the qualities and quantities of items received from the suppliers, measure beneficiaries’ satisfaction and behavior of the staff.- Conducting final evaluation to measure the impact of the project and to evaluate the action's implementation and dynamics to ensure that results and indicators are achieved within the designated time frame.- Nasaem will train its staff on its code of conduct, child protection, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies as well as accountability to affected population and how to deal with all stakeholders with respect and dignity. Also, Nasaem’s female field staff will receive a training on referral of vulnerable cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to service providers.- Nasaem will provide awareness sessions in coordination with the local councils to all beneficiaries who will receive olive pit about using the olive-pit fuel.

Protection Risk Analysis

The project is designed to contribute to the activity number 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs. However, there is a risk of tensions between IDPs and host communities. To avoid creating tensions among host communities and IDPs and among new and existing IDPs as not all of them will receive assistance, Nasaem will discuss the project with the local councils and will also explain the project’s goal and vulnerability criteria to all communities in the target areas in addition to ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs. Nasaem will make efforts in cooperation with the S/NFI cluster to target % of vulnerable host residents especially for those who have greatly affected by the crisis.Furthermore, Nasaem will train its staff on its code of conduct, child protection, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies. Nasaem’s female field staff will receive a training on referral of vulnerable cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to service providers. Nasaem will provide awareness sessions in coordination with the local councils in the targeted areas to all beneficiaries about using the olive-pit fuel. Nasaem will print a sticker warning and guidance on each heater that will be distributed. Given the high number of IDPs in the suggested locations, the management in Nasaem Khair understands that services should be provided indiscriminately and all humanitarian standards are maintained.Nasaem will establish a robust feedback mechanism to submit complaints in case there is variance in the quantity or quality of items received. The feedback procedures will be communicated in an appropriate language and in a clear and simple format to allow the beneficiaries to know how to access and use the feedback mechanism. All Nasaem’s M&E staff members will be familiar with the feedback mechanism to handle and respond to complaints effectively. They know how to record complaints and refer them to the appropriate staff members. Also, conducting follow-up visits, post distribution monitoring (PDM) and FGDs with the targeted beneficiaries and local authorities to learn from the people we’re assisting and to identify problems/challenges early. It enables greater accountability and builds trust and facilitates communication between beneficiaries and the organization.Create equal opportunities to older people and people with disabilities and all person with vulnerability to enable them to participate and prevent them from being socially excluded or overly dependent on other and to reduce the risk of abuse and sexual exploitation. Nasaem will also hire male and female to participate equally in all phases of the project.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:Nasaem has a good rapport and coordination with the local councils in targeted communities and has been providing humanitarian aid in multiple sectors for many year ago at those areas, so Nasaem is acceptable in the community. Access to target locations remains possible.Risks:There is a risk of tensions among those receiving assistance and those who are not. Nasaem will present the project to the communities in close coordination with the local councils and will explain the selection criteria and ensure that all beneficiaries are informed about Nasaem’s What'sApp feedback mechanism to monitor and correct errors of inclusion and exclusion.Fire is another serious risk that happen as a result of this project, especially if households are using a new type of heater or a fuel that they have not used before. Nasaem will ensure that beneficiaries receive proper awareness raising sessions on risks of fire and training sessions on operating the heaters and fuel safety. Further, Nasaem has some heaters safety instructions and will print these on aluminum papers inArabic.Risks of theft will be reduced keeping minimum stock of items to be distributed through contracting suppliers to supply the fuel, olive-pits and heaters on certain times and to certain locations. The warehouse will be mainly used for short-term storage, with minimum amounts of returned item to for preparations that precede distributions to smaller villages where the supplier will not supply the materials.
