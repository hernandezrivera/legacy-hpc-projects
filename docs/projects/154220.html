Project Summary


This project is designed to target IDPs people which are affected by the crisis and live in HAA2 and Hajj Ali in Mosul in Ninawa Province. In this project, Public Aid Organization intends to provide Psychosocial activities integrated into learning community centers. Public Aid Organization will work in two camps; Haji Ali camp and HAA2 camp in Ninawa province. PAO will provide structured PSS support using different advanced tools developed by SCI, resilience programs, peer to peer, focus group discussion for children (10-17) years old. besides that, PAO provides learning package for (6-10) years old through (hand washing, how to use toilets, respect public equipment, respect parents and others, take care of your body, the importance of school, mine risk, environment and weather, discovering child skills, saving the environment). in additional to PAO conducts focus group discussions on the phenomenon like early marriage, child labor, drop out of school, to raise child voice among the community. PAO also will provide sessions for men and women about good parenting. PAO will use IRC’s manual.
PAO will strengthen child protection mechanize through child protection committee (CPC) by establishing child CPC in the camps and PAO will train them on key child protection issues. CPC will be chosen from the community leaders of the community like Mukhtar, sheik, and Mulla,
After that, CPC members will provide awareness sessions for parents and community members. The sessions will be conducted under the supervision of PAO. CPC will conduct an awareness session on key child protection issues like child neglect, abuse, exploitation, early marriage, and child labor.
in this project. PAO will identify and referral cases to service providers who work on case management in the camps and follow up the cases till the case will be recover and closed by both sides.

Needs

December 2017 marked the end of major military operations in Iraq against the so-called Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL). While more than 4 million returns have been recorded as of September 2018, almost 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) remain, of whom 71% reside outside of formal camps.1 Moreover, secondary displacement and new arrivals to formal camps2 signal the tenuous nature of some returns. Therefore, although recovery efforts in Iraq are underway, understanding the multifaceted and intersecting needs of all affected groups is critical to supporting durable returns, while maintaining services for those in protected displacement and addressing the unique vulnerabilities of populations who remained non-displaced during active conflict. A Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (MCNA) was conducted in July 2018 to provide this analysis and inform the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO). The MCNA was led by the Assessment Working Group and facilitated by REACH, in close collaboration with OCHA and the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG).13% of households with children showing signs of psychosocial distress, such as behavior change since the conflict began. 20% Households with at least one school-aged child outside of a learning environment (formal or non-formal).4% of households with at least one child aged 6-17 working during the 30 days prior to data collection.2% of households with at least one married child (aged 12-17).In additional to Public Aid Organization already conducted child protection needs assessment in Haji Ali camp and HAA2 camp. The assessment aimed at identifying child protection situation and existing child protection structure in the two camps. The needs assessment conducted through psychosocial focus group methodology with girls, boys, men, and women. PAO also interviewed community leaders and local authorities. After the focus group discussion and interviews, PAO collected and analyzed the data. Here are the findings of the needs assessment:• Although some child protection actors were available in the above-mentioned camps, the camps still need more child protection actors.• Many of child marriage were recorded, reasons being mostly to reduce the economic burden on the family (55%) or because of tradition (34%). For child labor, 157 children, or 4% of all children assessed, were working. For 43%of the children, their income represented the sole income of their family, while for 56%, their income was supplementary to the family’s one.• 26% of the school aged children assessed are not attending school. • Community and domestic violence were mentioned as a predominant risk for children. • Poor economic situation and limited finance capacity have been forcing IDPs families to send their children (boys and girls) to work in markets, street and other public locations. This situation expose children to risk of exploitation, abuse and dropping out from school.• Many children identified with psychological distress. • Many were subjected to human rights abuses and traumatic events, such as losing family members and witnessing extreme violence was prevalent among boys, girls, men, and women. • Conducting PSS activities through the community-based psycho-social programme is highly important to increase children, parents and community resilience.

Protection Risk Analysis

The security environment within HAA 2and Haji Ali camps are stable but surrounding areas have had incidents of violence and in June and October ISIS killed many civilians in Sharqat. This includes villages outside of HAA2, locations along the Tigris River to the North and the South, and the desert to the West of the main road between West Mosul and HAA. Although they have not carried out attacks against NGOs in or near HAA2 and Haji Ali camps, IS sleeping cells reportedly exist in several areas around HAA2. Several asymmetric attacks targeting Iraqi Security Forces have been reported in the past months. A pattern of attacks on houses has also been reported, mostly in areas outside of the HAA2 and Haji Ali camps but occasionally within it. These are usually attributed to IS attacking the homes of PMF militia members (in areas outside of the town), or to revenge attacks on houses of people who are supposedly family members of IS.

Mitigation Measures

In order to ensure the safety and the security of its teams, PAO has a Humanitarian Access & Security Advisor based in Erbil, as well as a Security Officer and a Liaison Officer based in Mosul to support the Field Coordinator. These staffs are based in Mosul but do regular visits to HAA and Haji Ali camps.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The proposed intervention has been designed taking into account the feedback of PAO’s beneficiaries, partners and Iraqi staff during PAO’s past and ongoing intervention in Iraq. During the implementation, PAO will directly involve its beneficiaries in the decisions related to them: - Households and individuals will give their informed consent to benefit from the project’s activities, including PAO’s direct services and referrals to other organizations. - PAO’s beneficiaries will always have the right to refuse the offered services- PAO ’s beneficiaries will have opportunities to provide feedback on PAO’s services.
