Project Summary

This project will be for one year and implemented in high priority areas of Manyo in Upper Nile State and Aweil East and North in Northern Bahr El Ghazal State. The project will target 20,301 direct learners, 240 teachers and 36 PTA/SMCs and county education stake holders
The Project aim to enhance access to safe, protective and inclusive quality education for children and youth affected by crises. Key actions include back-to-learning campaigns, mobilizing and tracking enrollment, distributing educational and hygiene kits, supporting children with disabilities, provision of psycho-social support and Mental Health (PSS & MHPSS) and fostering peer-to-peer and community-led learning. The project also aim at strengthening education management and resilience building in schools through training of teachers and PTAs/SMCs, coordination, and capacity building of SPEDP staff and local education actors to ensure effective and efficient coordination of education responses in emergencies for enhanced access to safe, protective and inclusive education.
The planned modality for the project intervention in the targeted locations will be a community-driven approach. This is to engage and empower local leadership such as PTAs/SMCs and education stakeholders at the community grassroots level. Their roles will be clearly spelled out to lead the mobilization efforts aimed at addressing the issue of Out-of-school children (OOSC) forced to work in cattle camps instead of attending school. SPEDP intends to establish Temporary Learning Spaces (TLSs), establish help desks and peace clubs within selected schools, train teachers on life skills, code of conduct and provision of emotional support for both teachers and learners.
The proposed project will align directly with national priorities and implemented in line with the Inter Agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE), Child safeguarding and other relevant standards such as EiE competency framework. The project will also align with the strategic goals outlined in the Republic of South Sudan Education Cluster Strategy 2023-2025. Refugees, IDPS, Host communities and returnees in the project counties of Aweil North and East of Northern Bahr El Ghazal and Manyo of Upper Nile State will be targeted. SPEDP will adhere to the International operational standards that include, “DO NO HARM" principles.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is deteriorating, impaired by factors such as conflict, inter-communal violence and the climate change. An estimated 2 million children, including 1,535,257 residents, 311,447 internally displaced, and 189,185 returnee children, along with nearly 41,000 teachers, all requiring Education related humanitarian aid. The education sector faces unique challenges, including a shortage of qualified teachers. Insufficient training for a third of teachers contributes to high pupil-teacher ratios, averaging 93:1. Additionally, there is a dire need to increase learning materials, especially for early childhood education, as textbook ratios for primary students are currently at 8:1, significantly below the recommended 1:1 guideline(south Sudan education cluster, 2024).Based on the above, South Sudan literacy rates are extremely low; only 34% (3rd lowest in the World, World Population Review Report, 2024). South Sudan still have strong cultures resulting in high discrimination against girls when choosing to send them to school. Schools also lack trained teachers, teaching materials, poor learning facilities, poor sanitation and hygiene and model female teachers, insecurity and flooding. Over 2.8 million children (53% girls) are out of school (MoGEI, 2020), Girls face many barriers that include; segregation, lack of access to resources, poverty, child marriage, cultural and religious norms, long distances to schools, absence of dignity kits and few female role model teachers.Upper Nile and Northern Bar El-Hazal States hosts the largest population of refugees and returnees from Sudan because of their proximity to Sudan. It’s estimated that 50.68% refugee children including host community are out of school in these States due to limited learning spaces. These States are known to face severe educational challenges such as high pupil-to-teacher ratios and low qualified teachers’ availability. Aweil North and East Counties in Northern Bahr- El Ghazal and Manyo County in Upper Nile where this project will be implemented are also affected by flooding. The flooding is likely to worsen with the continuing heavy down pours and rise of the Nile Waters, which will have direct impact on education. Besides, ongoing communal conflicts related to cattle raiding affect smooth learning. Another challenge is, the returnee/refugee and refugees children, having studied in Arabic, struggle to transition to English as the medium of instruction

2. Project activities

Through this intervention, SPEDP will address the most pressing educational needs in the selected locations to enable access to the Out of out-of-school children (OoSC) to schools in Aweil North, East and Manyo Counties through the implementation of the following activities; i. Construction and rehabilitation of 6 Temporary Learning Spaces (TLSs). This will be in line with the Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MOGEI) to enroll and accommodate 20,301 (40% girls) out-of-school children. ii. SPEDP will construct 8 (3 for boys and 5 for girls) Gender sensitive and inclusive ventilated improve Pit latrine stances in each of the schools constructed/rehabilitated. Changing rooms and ramps in the improved pit latrines will be provided iii. Hand washing facilities (water containers with taps, hand-washing soap) will be provided to each of the schools.iv. Rapid needs assessment of schools for construction/rehabilitation will be conducted.v. The project will conduct 6 (2 per county) Back to Learning (BTL) Campaigns in all the locations of the project as part of the enrollment drive for the out-of-school children. vi. To improve attendance and retention for girls, SPEDP will distribute dignity kits for adolescent girls between the ages of 13-17 years, and celebration of Girl Child Education Day on 7th July 2025 and 16 days of activism across the project locations (3 locations) will be carried. This is aimed at empowering the girl child prioritize education by engaging parents, teachers, community leaders, and local education stakeholders.vii. Improving quality of teaching will be prioritize in this intervention through training of 150 (60 female) teachers on EiE pedagogy and Mental Health and Psycho-social Support viii. Distribution of teaching and learning materials reaching to 17,415 (6967 Girls) learners, ECD materials reaching to 2,014 (806 Girls) ECD learners.ix. Psychosocial support programs will be provided to help children cope with trauma and stress from emergencies through training and distribution of recreational materials. x. To strengthen coordination mechanism and community engagement, SPEDP will provide training to 36 PTAs/SMCs Associations and 33 (11 per county) education stakeholders on school management, disaster preparedness, planning and response, and ensuring inclusive and culturally respectful participation in all stages of education activities during emergencies. xi. To enhance peaceful coexistence among the target population (returnees, refugees, IDPs and Host community), 90 school clubs (3 clubs per school) such as Peace Clubs, Debate clubs, Hygiene club, games and Sport club and environmental clubs will be formed to foster sense of peaceful coexistence. The club members will be trained on leadership skills and their roles to promote the agenda of schools being zones of peace.xii. The intervention will initiate the payment of volunteer teachers’ incentives (240 volunteer teachers). Conduct needs assessment to identify the gaps in the payment of teachers’ incentives. xiii. Distribution of Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials to create awareness of the project and its critical componentsxiv. Distribution of assistive devices to support enrolment, attendance and retention of children with disabilities.
