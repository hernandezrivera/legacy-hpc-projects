Project Summary

Through conflict-sensitive CCCM services, the Project aims at strengthening coordination of life-saving humanitarian assistance by deploying Acted global CCCM expertise, ensuring living minimum standards are met while communities develop self-reliance. Capacity building and close collaboration with local actors will promote pathways to durable solutions for IDPs, Returnees and Non-Displaced through an Area-Based-Approach (ABA). CCCM will be provided through on-site and mobile approaches to cover hard-to-reach areas with access to assistance and protection, ensuring adaptability to the evolving displacement movements.
The main objectives are as follows:
1.Enhance service coordination, monitoring, and information sharing to facilitate equitable and dignified access to cross-sectoral humanitarian assistance.
2.Strengthen community engagement and inclusive participation to ensure local ownership, self-reliance, and to promote a safe environment.
3.Improve the technical and operational capacities of local organizations to facilitate effective inter-agency CCCM, sites administration handover, and durable solutions for displacement.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The Project aims to respond with a comprehensive, need-based, conflict sensitive Camp Coordination & Camp Management intervention to enhance the well-being and protection for conflict affected communities (IDPs, Non-displaced, Returnees) in the districts of Macomia and Muidumbe, leading to durable solutions. This will be achieved through three main complementary components: 1.Service coordination, monitoring, and information sharing / 2.Community Engagement / 3.Capacity Building of local organizations. Acted will align to the CCCM Cluster “ABC strategy” targeting districts dedicated to “C - Creating cohesive communities”. Through this multi-sectoral Area Based Approach (ABA) Acted will deploy its global CCCM expertise to provide high quality coordination mechanisms [regular service providers – Site Management Committees (SMCs) meetings, rapid information management systems based on stakeholders-services-local capacities’ mapping, ...], improving access to humanitarian assistance and protection for affected people inside and outside settlements, ensuring hard-to-reach areas are served through mobile CCCM. Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) will be strengthened by inclusive Complaint & Response Mechanisms (CRMs) and by ensuring transparent, two-ways Communication with Communities (CwC) (awareness campaigns, community resource centers, referral pathways ...). As per Cluster strategy, Acted’s intervention will also promote disaster-resilient areas and integrate Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) in the broader capacity building provided to affected communities, adapting to the context a range of successful best practices implemented in other countries where Acted operates (Weather hazard and rainy seasons preparedness, Emergency response training for community volunteers, night disaster drills, ...) Gender-sensitive Community Engagement will be indeed pivotal for self-reliance and will encompass training on Protection principles, CCCM frameworks, and promote, where feasible, Quick Impact Projects (QIPs) proposed by the community, including women and youth led livelihood initiatives, and assessed against pre-defined criteria. The intervention will also build on Acted uninterrupted presence and consolidated community acceptance in Macomia district, complementing its blanket approach, in-kind food assistance and continuing its multi-sectoral efforts to coordinate with other humanitarian, development, and government actors. Acted’s ongoing collaboration with SDPI will smooth the localization process and allow a continuous exchange for gradual handover of administration, and for building pathways to durable solution with displaced people. Furthermore, the intervention will be implemented jointly by Acted’s local partner Pronanac, whose crucial presence in the targeted district will ensure the intervention builds on continuity of CCCM activities. Acted will also deploy its Grassroots Organization Capacity Assessment (GOCA) Tool, designed to ensure effective and sustainable capacity building, addressing technical/operational needs of local and governmental partners. Finally, ACTED will commit to strengthen the role of women, youth and people with disability in the community governance structures by ensuring balanced participation. Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) lenses will be applied through all programming to ensure fair and indiscriminate access to services and resources by youth, elderly, people with disabilities, women, girls, actively avoiding any obstacles to their empowerment and participation, as well as mitigating external risks as environmental, site and infrastructure related hazards.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1.Enhance service coordination, monitoring, and information sharing to facilitate equitable and dignified access to cross-sectoral humanitarian assistance: Facilitate regular meetings between service providers and Site Management Committees (SMCs) / Setup an Information management system for coordination, multi-sector service monitoring and Accountability to Affected Population (AAP): conduct multi-sectoral satisfaction survey, followed by regular multi-service monitoring, stakeholders mapping and service mapping including through 5W reports, surveys and community level information gathering on intentions-trends-returns-movements, demographic surveys, update site profiles, periodic publication of data / Facilitate multi sector first-line assistance for newly displaced populations through enhanced Reception Centers management / Implement Mobile CCCM modalities for hard to reach areas. 2.Strengthen community engagement and inclusive participation to ensure local ownership, self-reliance, and to promote a safe environment: AAP - Set up or enhance Complaint & Response Mechanisms (CRMs) that are accessible to all, timely follow-up on feedback by respecting a timeline communicated with beneficiaries / Transparent Communication with Communities (CwC) - Organize awareness campaigns through context-appropriate modalities (Radio Listening Groups, banners, ...) to inform the community on services available and access modalities / Create or facilitate existing referral pathways / Establish or maintain community resource centers in strategic locations to act as information hubs for communities and service providers / Conduct community governance capacity assessments to establish or reinforce SMCs, ensuring they are built on existing capacities. The SMCs will include host communities (HC) representatives to favour social cohesion / Organize community-level capacity building training on Protection (protection principles, GBV, PSEAH, psychological first aid, ...), and on CCCM frameworks to promote self-management / Facilitate regular recreational activities to enhance community cohesion and youth involvement / Promote a safe environment - Carry out participatory safety audits to inform risk mitigation plans including internal (protection, inclusion, ...) and external risks (environmental, infrastructure, ...) / Site and settlement infrastructure improvement and maintenance through community-led initiatives under a Cash for work (CfW) scheme, where possible / Conduct trainings on emergency preparedness, early action, disaster drills, evacuation procedures, and produce site adapted contingency plans 3.Improve the technical and operational capacities of local organizations to facilitate effective inter-agency CCCM, sites administration handover, and durable solutions for displacement:Deploy Acted’s flagship civil society organizational capacity assessment and development toolkit (GOCA) to: Analyze operational and technical capacities of local stakeholders (CSOs, NNGOs, Community Leaders, Government Authorities) / Conduct GOCA capacity building trainings on identified operational management gaps (information management, registration, reporting, two-ways-communication systems, DRR...) and on CCCM global standards (HAP, CAMP, SPHERE) / On-the-job mentoring including on-site monitoring and recommendations of activities, to facilitate integration of site-governance mechanism into the hosting villages, in cooperation with local authorities/ Support the implementation and monitoring of Quick Impact Projects (QIPs) proposed by communities through inclusive consultations. ACTED ad-hoc assessment will complement the pre-dentification of the QIPs sectors and sites, and the projects will be assessed against pre-defined criteria, including direct impact on community cohesion and feasibility assessments results./ Facilitate durable solutions by advocating with local authorities for access to land and promoting inter-community livelihood initiatives

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project proactively identifies potential risks and mitigates harm by conducting initial safety audits and protection risk assessments. These evaluations will be updated regularly to address emerging risks and adapt to the rapidly changing context. Protection principles, including protection for sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment, are embedded across all project stages. ACTED adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards SEAH. ACTED is committed to the prevention of SEAH both within the organization and within its programmes beneficiary populations. To this end, ACTED’s PSEAH Policy: ▪ Encourages prevention ▪ Promotes detection ▪ Sets out a clear investigation procedure ▪ Sets out a mechanism of sanctions ACTED conducts periodic training for staff and community workers on protection standards, PSEAH, and mainstreams GBV prevention and accessibility for vulnerable groups in its programs. Community consultations are central at all stages, including focus group discussions and the use of Acted Feedback Mechanism, and will guide culturally-gender sensitive and safe CCCM service delivery. Additionally, coordination with protection-focused actors and local authorities will strengthen early risk identification and foster a collaborative approach to ensure the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project ensures meaningful access to assistance and promotes active participation by incorporating gender and age perspectives in all phases. Community engagement will aim at inclusive decision-making, establishing trained, gender-balanced Site Management Committees and mainstreaming Women’s, Elderly, and Youth participation in community decision-making processes through dedicated platforms. Gender-age-disability disaggregated data collection will guide interventions, ensuring tailored responsiveness to the diversified needs. Additionally, Acted Feedback Mechanism will inform decisions and empower vulnerable groups to actively shapes the project outcomes.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will ensure people with disabilities are identified and supported through inclusive needs assessments and active engagement throughout the project cycle. Initial consultations, surveys and existing data will help identify individuals with disabilities and the specific barriers they face, directly shaping activities design. Planning solutions by coordinating with all stakeholders will allow individuals with disabilities to participate fully and access assistance safely without discrimination. Regular monitoring will ensure that project adaptations are responsive and inclusive.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

In line with ACTED’s 3Zero vision—Zero Exclusion, Zero Carbon, Zero Poverty—and Greening Strategy, this project will minimize environmental impact across all activities. Environmental impact assessments (NEAT+) data/ rapid environmental screening will be used to identify risks and shape mitigation strategies. The project will prioritize sustainable resource use by incorporating locally sourced, eco-friendly materials and applying a reduce, recycle, and reuse system. Engagement with local communities in awareness activities will emphasize responsible natural resource use to avoid environmental contamination and lower the risk of epidemic outbreaks. By integrating these measures, the project aligns with ACTED’s commitment to sustainable recovery that addresses the specific needs of affected communities while safeguarding the environment. Furthermore, compliance with national environmental regulations and coordination with local authorities will be maintained throughout the Project.
