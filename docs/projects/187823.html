Project Summary

The proposed action will enhance the food security of the most affected communities through integrated climate-smart agricultural interventions in NW Syria. The project philosophy is investing in agriculture to improve food self-sufficiency. Strengthening the resilience of rural and IDPs communities through improving their food security and income generation is the urgent need to face both food insecurity and climate change impacts as well. Therefore, the project is designed to deliver outputs relevant to local needs, build on local agricultural practices, integrate smart-climate agriculture, and apply regenerative agriculture. It is designed according to the best coping strategies that preserve assets, create new opportunities and provide the best options for FSL in both short and long term. The project includes the following main interventions:
1. Nursery site preparation to produce 40,000 of the most cultivated vegetable seedlings yearly to be disrupted to the beneficiaries of home and community gardens. The nursery itself will provide training opportunities and sustainable solution for quality vegetable seedlings.
2. Promoting food self-sufficiency and supporting the resilience of farmers and displaced communities applying a regenerative approach and local knowledge. 400 households (200 host communities and 200 IDPs) will be able to produce vegetables at home (home gardens/productive families). This intervention will allow households from host communities to use low-cost food production methods and use greywater and will provide access to the IDP farmers to cultivate land/spaces where they can produce their food and generate income by selling surplus products.
3. Supporting olive farmers. 400 olive farmers will be enhanced with agricultural inputs and the value chain to withstand low income and low marketing. Main agricultural inputs will be provided to the olive farmers, and 10 tons of the products will be purchased and distributed to 400 of the most vulnerable IDP households.
4. Rationalization of water use and enable farmers to use modern irrigation. 200 vegetable farmers will be supported with the required inputs to apply a drip irrigation system.
5. Strengthening the preservation of livestock. 400 livestock breeders will be supported by concentrated fodder (200 kg per farmer) and 50 bags of Azolla production.
6. Professional training on grafting and pruning methods for olive trees (30 trainees), home and community gardening applying regenerative approach (50 households), drip irrigation and Azolla production (50 livestock breeders).
7. Participation of stakeholders in the project and production of resources to raise agricultural knowledge.
The project will promote a participatory approach, sharing experiences and lessons learned. The project will promote the participatory approach in the local community as well as with interested actors and organizations. It will also document and publish the experiences learned from the project to be sources of learning and sustainable benefit beyond the project.

4 - Needs

SAE conducted a need assessment during 2021, it comprised several Semi-structured focus group discussions, in-depth interviews and participatory workshops with different stakeholders e.g. local councils, farmers, livestock breeders, agronomists etc. I addition, our field team are providing weekly feedbacks and observations. This project is designed according to these assessment in addition to the reports and needs reported from different resources. Some of the most challenges are lower yields, increase food insecurity and lack income opportunities, high prices of fodder, lack of income opportunities, and lack of skilled workforce and knowledge etc.Participants of both workshops in Sarmada and Azaz reported additional needs e.g. quality agricultural inputs especially pesticides and fertilisers; supporting transition to drip irrigation due to the lack of water resources and to increase production in the unit area; lack of agricultural extension and technical support; absence of any financial opportunity such as banks etc; also, they reports the lack of authority and farmers cooperatives etc [workshops report]. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The PRA could refer to some of the following risks:- Conflict-related to the beneficiary selection.- Unstable security situation.- Conflict with local authorities.- Beneficiaries are not satisfied with the interventions. The project will be conducted with a higher understanding of the context and needs in targeted areas. Our monthly beneficiary satisfaction surveys are disaggregated by gender and displacement status, which ensures the specific needs of different sexes and groups is factored into program design. All project beneficiaries will be selected according to clear criteria in participation and trenchancy with local communities at all stages of the project activities. SEA will ensure ‘Do No Harm’ principles are applied to identify and mitigate against potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the FSL sector strategy and related activities. SAE has built relationships of trust between the affected community and local authorities. SAE team will get written approvals from the local authorities to implement project activities according to Humanitarian Principles without any external interventions.SAE also will participate in sharing the access sites in OCHA access group to de-conflict humanitarian facilities and reports any access-relative incidents to the Access group immediately and ask for help if necessary.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All staff sign the SAE organization’s code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report abuse by staff or beneficiaries, or misuse of assets or funds. We have strict protocols against any sexual relations with minors or beneficiaries of any age and all staff have recently attended refresher training on SAE Child Protection and PSEA policies.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:The proposed locations became not safe or present high spread of Covid-19 cases.The households aren't motivated to cultivate vegetables in the home/community gardens.The olive farmers can't sell their products (green olives & olive oil) in the local markets.The households can’t join the training due to concerns related to transportation or Covid-19.There are no skilled media producers in Syria. Assumptions:The proposed locations are accessible without any concerns.The local authorities are cooperative and support the project. The agricultural inputs are available in the local markets. The households are able to attend the training.The households are motivated to use home/community gardens.The olive farmers can sell their products (green olives & olive oil) in the local markets.There are skilled staff in Syria to produce the podcast and the extension videos. Mitigation measures:SAE will monitor and assess the security situation in the field closely and share the risks.Unavailable agricultural inputs in the local markets will be purchased from Turkey.SAE team will explain to the benefits the importance of food production in the home/community gardens and will encourage them to decide what they want to grow and to work together. The project will support the olive value chain and purchase 10000 kg of green olives and olive oil from the farmers.SAE have strong experience and will contract with skilled Syrian media producers in Syria and Turkey.
