Project Summary

The project aims to response to gaps created by challenging situations in South Sudan resulted from wars, communal conflicts, deteriorating economy, COVID-19, and natural disasters such as flooding that displaced thousands of people and disrupted their livelihood in Yirol East, Twice East, Ayod, Yirol West and Awerial Counties. According to South Sudan Humanitarian need overview for 2023 that was projected in November 2022, it was estimated that 2.2 million people are displaced in the country since 2013 and more than 2.3 million people seek refuge in the neighboring countries with 9.4 million people to be in need of humanitarian assistance in South a figure that is higher than 8.9 million people in the year 2022.
The June 2023 – April 2024 famine and early warning system network predicted that acute food insecurity will remain high and many areas in South Sudan will be experiencing Emergency levels of food insecurity as this will be worsened by the continuous arrival of hundreds of thousands of displaced persons affected by the Sudan war.
The intersectoral analysis conducted using cluster people in needs in every county of the country that out of the 9.4 million, 56,325 people will experience catastrophe need in Panyikang county and there will be 66 counties out of the 78 counties will experience severe needs.
The lean season projection period from April – July 2023, predicted that 47 counties would be in emergency, (IPC 4) acute food insecurity, 29 would likely be in emergency (IPC 3) food insecurity and only one county will be stressed.
All the factors above are indications of the worse that is to befall the vulnerable communities of South Sudan in the year 2024 if there are no integrated humanitarian responses to address the challenges that are awaiting.
Help – Hilfe zur Selsthilfe Just like other Humanitarian Organizations that are trying their best to response to specific needs of the vulnerable people, has so far secured 33 months (1st July 2023 – 31st March 2026) project titled “Integrated WASH, Food Security and Nutrition services to conflict and disaster affected persons in Lakes and Jonglei States, South Sudan” with WASH, nutrition and FSL as the thematic focus areas. Under the Food Security and Livelihood thematic area looking into a more Sustainable Agriculture and Livelihoods Empowerment through Unconditional Cash/Food voucher, Food for Asset and distribution of seeds and tools to the Vulnerable Host and IDPs in Eastern Lakes and Western Jonglei States. Help- Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe has put more emphasis on provision of seeds and farm tools to 3000 vulnerable households, training of 3000 HHs to improve their traditional farming knowledge with modern production that are more productive, provision of fishing gears to 3000 households to diversify their livelihood sources and improve on their protein dietary intakes. The project also intends to increase access to food through unconditional food vouchers and food for asset (FFA). In all our interventions, our agricultural approaches are environmentally friendly, and this has been rejuvenated by an activity of Natural Resource Management that will be supported by Natural Resource Management committee. This committee will be trained and empowered to communicate the local community on the importance and negative impacts of management natural resources such as the lakes, rivers, forests, ponds and many more that are in the localities. There will be fruit tree nursery established in the three counties such that the afforestation is encourage.

1. Needs

With ongoing violence in some parts of South Sudan such as upper Nile and Equatoria regions marked by continuous displacements, flooding, that has disrupted markets and agricultural activities, droughts, coupled by deteriorating economy and effects of COVID-19, South Sudan is in a dire situation of the humanitarian needs. According to South Sudan Humanitarian need overview for 2023 that was projected in November 2022, it was estimated that 2.2 million people are displaced in the country since 2013 and more than 2.3 million people seek refuge in the neighboring countries. It was also projected that 9.4 million will be in need of humanitarian assistance in South a figure higher than 8.9 million of people in need in the year 2022. The June 2023 – April 2024 famine and early warning system network predicted that acute food insecurity will remain high and many areas in South Sudan will be experiencing Emergency levels of food insecurity as this will be worsened by the continuous arrival of hundreds of thousands of displaced persons affected by the Sudan war. IPC project for November – March 2023 put Yirol East in IPC 3 with possibility of sliding to Emergency IPC 4. At the moment, the county houses over 2000 IDPs households who share most of the livelihood opportunities with the host communities. If the number keeps growing, the humanitarian situation in Yirol East will deteriorate and many people will be affected. The previous IRNA reports indicated an increased vulnerability of people in Yirol East County. On 28th September – 6th October 2022 IRNA report for Yirol East and West found out that 6729 households (2002 HHs in Yirol East and 4727 HHs in Yirol West) so far were affected in which 13% of the total affected are under 5 children. The IRNA report analyzed that the food security situation would worsen from November 2022 – May 2023due to effects of flooding. The Multisectoral IRNA Assessment Report to IDPs Flood Affected from Unity state to Lakes State- 13th – 16th Dec. 2022 conducted in Yirol and Rumbek Centre showed that 1431 HHs (360 HHs in Rumbek East and 1071 HHs in Yirol East) arrived from Panyijar county of Unity state. According to the report, the total number of IDPs counted was 9747 (2700 individuals in Rumbek Centre and 7047 individuals in Yirol East County). The recommendations from the two (2) assessments indicated that there is a high need to respond to the situation by providing fishing gears, seeds and farming tools and additionally providing agronomic trainings through establishing demonstration gardens/sites mostly in our nutrition sites. Help is gearing towards achieving a more sustainable community that is food secure and malnutrition free under the two (2) project, Emergency Livelihood Response Programme (ELRP) and “Integrated WASH, Food Security and Nutrition services to conflict and disaster affected persons in Lakes and Jonglei States, South Sudan” All the above situations need a multifaceted response to rescue the worsening food security situation in the country and Help – Hilfe zur Seltshilfe response is geared towards providing food assistance to most vulnerable households to prevent famine and improve food consumption (In IPC 4 and 5), Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock, and Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. In the secured current project targets all genders (males and females), persons with disabilities, and all ages. The activities under this project include Unconditional food vouchers to 2700 HHs, Distribution of agricultural inputs and trainings to 3000 HHs, distribution of fishing gears to 3000 HHs, provide cash/Food for asset (FFA) to 3000 HHs, postharvest handling training to 3000 HHs, training and supporting 400 HHs with fruit tree seeds to environmental degradation cause by farming.

2. Project activities

Distribution of unconditional food to 2700 HHs most vulnerable households (female headed households, Child headed households and vulnerable males’ headed households, person with disabilities and IDPs. • Distribution of inputs (seeds and farm tools), to both males, females and children doing agriculture • Agronomic trainings to 3000 HHs that will receive seeds and farm tools • Distribution of fishing gears and training on netmaking, postharvest and fish hygiene to both males and females doing fishing. • Food for Asset (FFA) to 3000 HHs who will be engaged in doing farming works and rehabilitation and upgrade community roads to enable humanitarian support reach the vulnerable people. • Training on postharvest handling to 3000 HHs doing crop and vegetable production for males and females’ beneficiaries, and IDPs communities. • More fishery cooperatives/enterprises and support market functionality by supporting and training local Agro-dealers that will be able to supply fishery inputs (fishing gears) • Formation and strengthening of Natural Resource Management Committee (NRMC) through establishment of tree nurseries and distribution to help in maintenance of natural resources – water bodies, forests etc. • Safety Audit committees' formation in areas where we will be doing distribution and training of beneficiaries. These committees will be consisted with males and females to ease in communication in cases of GBV.
