Project Summary

ADRA have been working for more than 4 decades in Sudan and it is one of the oldest INGOs in this country. Our work is inspired in our main values “justice, compassion and love” to assist Sudanese people in need. Currently, we have projects in 5 states Khartoum, White Nile, Blue Nile, Central Darfur and West Darfur. All of them have faced hazard events such as flooding and intercommunal conflicts in different areas. This crisis directly affects the community, without exception of gender or age, triggering a dangerous and uncertain environment for the affected people, who suddenly loose their houses, jobs and, in some cases, their lives. ADRA looks for recovering their dignity through this project in terms of S/ NFI, complementing other WASH, FLS and Protection projects that have been developing in same areas.
Our purpose is to assist IDPs and returnees improving their currently emergency shelter through Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA). Additionally, we plan to provide shelter kits and NFI kits, for new IDPs, as we have been doing in other opportunities.
ADRA will be working in different localities of White Nile (Kosti, El Jabalian, El Salam, Rabak, and Tendalti), Blue Nile (all localities) and West Darfur (El Geneina, Jebel Moon & Kerinik). The emergency shelter kits will be provided to 5,000 households, benefiting more than 25,000 individuals. With the same number of beneficiaries, we are going to work for the NFI kits, benefiting 5,000 households as well. For those IDPs who needs to improve their emergency shelter, we plan to assist 2,000 households, benefiting around 10,000 individuals. Out of the all beneficiaries, ADRA aims at targeting 2000 H with emergency shelter kit through cash modality.

1 - Needs

The Project will be targeting the conflict or flood affected population as part of emergency response, as well, to improve the living conditions in West Darfur, White Nile State and Blue Nile State.According to the multisector need assessment 2021, over 50% of households were found to have unmet needs in the S/NFI sector in 15 out of 17 states (except River Nile and Northern states, where 49% of households had unmet needs in this sector). Indeed, a majority of households were found to live in shelters that did not meet agreed with technical and performance standards (i.e. had damage or structural problems), considering this situation as the most common need between IDP households (92%). Moreover, every year, Sudan expects torrential rains and Nile flooding combined. Historically overflow of the Nile River causes devastating damages across bordering states, reporting a loss of shelter, property, and livelihoods, especially those depending on agriculture. In addition, intercommunal conflicts are a threat for the communities in the affected areas. Destroying locality infrastructure and forces the movement of local people looking for protection and security in other states. As a consequence of these crises, families live in poor humanitarian conditions where protection risks are heightened as a result of loss of shelter and property. In both contexts, affected people require tens/shelter kits as an emergency need.ADRA will respond to the emerging need of those affected by current flooding or conflict through a community-led response, which meets the immediate needs of households affected by one of these crises. ADRA will provide shelter materials and NFI, as well as pilot cash assistance especially for urban vulnerable households and returnees to help in rehabilitating their shelters and rental subsidies.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Establishment of Community Committees: ADRA will work with community leaders and local authorities in forming community committees that will consist of both male and female members to ensure that cultural sensitivities regarding gender are taken into consideration. The project will also engage and mobilize ADRA’s currently trained community volunteers in leading the committees. The project will form a total of 10 committees. Prior to the commencement of distributions, committee members will be given a half-day training on vulnerability criteria, identification of beneficiaries, protection mainstreaming, and protection of data. 2. Verification of Households: ADRA Sudan will coordinate with HAC, community committees, and other humanitarian partners working in the area in compiling a beneficiary list for verification in the target areas. The list will then be shared with the community committees for verification. The project will conduct a verification of the lists for each target village. Committees will conduct household-level visits and fill out the established verification forms. 3. Distribution of Shelter-related and NFIs / Cash Assistance Shelter: In collaboration with local authorities in the target villages and with community committees, the ADRA team will select locations for the distribution. The distribution points will be selected based on their proximity to the targeted community as well as their security. ADRA will also ensure that distribution times and sites are suitable to the most vulnerable members of the community, particularly for females and disabled individuals.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ADRA Sudan will follow environment preservation approaches, providing environmentally friendly, durable, and locally sourced shelter solutions. The materials and construction methodology will be defined, selected, and adopted to prevent over-exploitation, pollution, and degradation of environmental conditions. The distribution of shelter materials will involve eco-friendly materials such as bamboo, reed mats, grass mats, with steel pillars for construction and rehabilitation of shelters. Bamboo, grass, and reed are growing in White Nile River. They are encouraged to be removed by the forest department because they are hindering boating and fishing activities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through this project, ADRA will maximize the positive impacts of shelter and NFI programs on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations access and enjoy their rights.ADRA will build on our existing relationships with communities and build rapport with new communities, to ensure that beneficiaries are comfortable in reaching out to them if and when issues arise to provide support to those who may not have any way to report issues; particularly surrounding GBV.ADRA uses its knowledge of the context in the project area, as well as consultations with local staff, in the design of gender-sensitive and tailored approaches. Both male and female community members will be consulted during the design of the project through focus groups as well as needs. During distributions, separate queues for women will be established, and females will be consulted on the most appropriate time and place for distributions. Tailored awareness methods will aim to be inclusive of different segments in the community. In order to successfully deliver the services in the area, ADRA will ensure that channels of communication continue to be open, regular, and accessible throughout the program cycle.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The camp feedback and complaint mechanism, which is co-led by UNHCR and SRC, will be used by ADRA as one of the main channels to counter fraud and aid diversion. In the event of families being assessed the Shelter/ NFI assistant and found to be lacking legal documents, cases will be referred to UNHCR. In the event of families having lost IDs (including IDs giving them access to basic service provision), cases will be referred to the respective issuing agencies. The current project design and geographic selection are coordinated closely with the cluster on the camp level and the block level with all partners involved. Multiple meetings will take place prior to the implementation of the action with all active partners in the field through the Refugee Consultation Forum (RCF) and bi-laterally to avoid duplication with other programs, such as UNHCR Shelter/NFI activities. ADRA Sudan is an active member of the Cash Working Group (CWG) at the national level ensuring regular representation in meetings. Moreover, ADRA will strengthen the coordination of cash and voucher programming in the camps and ensure that standard operational procedures are in place for all partners and that minimum standards endorsed by the CWG are followed to avoid confusion and tension in camp communities.
