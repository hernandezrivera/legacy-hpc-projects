Project Summary

Health situation in Somalia has been of alarming rate with several of the indicators and the prevalence of diseases being on the rise. Lower Juba is one of the areas in Somalia with chronic high levels of acute malnutrition beyond the emergency threshold. It is also a district where the IDPs report high prevalence acute malnutrition according to FSNAU surveys. Almost 1 in every 2 children suffering from a communicable disease, limited access to health facilities, very few women with access to Maternal and child care services including delivery services (Health cluster reports) , very high morbidity rates with child illness being highest , poor access to vaccination services and 1 in every 5 pregnant or lactating women being acutely malnourished. Prevalence of acute malnutrition and health needs affecting more than 1.6 million people. Therefore, WRRS in line with the national response plan will work in the proposed areas to ensure that in intergration with other sectors the excess mortality rates from crisis driven diseases and outbreaks among IDPs and non-displaced people are decreased by 5% by end of 2020 in our targeted districts. The focus will as well be to increase coverage of immunization among other factors to improve measles vaccinations and prevention and case management of AWD/cholera.
WRRS is currently having ongoing interventions in Afmadow, Bhadhade and kismayu that will be linked to the strategic response plan. This offers health care services integrated with nutrition aspects and WASH.
The project will therefore focus on; The management of complicated cases and mental health cases for treatment, too complicated cases will be refereed to the nearest Referral hospital for further management of the complicated cases, treatment of childhood illnesses, Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) services integrated together to the flood affected Households (HH) and other public health related concerned. These services will also not exclude people living with different disabilities, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and populations in Baidoa, Garowe and Lower Juba. We will also provide Training's to our team of nurses, mid-wives and community health workers comprehensive primary health care packages , Integrated management of childhood illness (IMCI), training of community health workers and volunteers on the promotion of hygiene, management and prevention of AWD/cholera cases.
The services will be targeting beneficiaries: men, women, boys and girls in mobile/ community and static health care centres the mobile services will entirely target far to reached Households (HH) while the static ones will be offering health care services for the nearby HH through treatment of illnesses and control of communicable diseases. Immunization will be offered to both pregnant and under 1 years to ease the burden of vaccine preventable diseases. Coordination with all stakeholders including the marginalized and the less minority groups will be adhered to for accountability.
WRRS will ensure that the coordination with other actors within the area of interventions is uphold, MOH from the targeted states will be involved and the clusters, which include, health Nutrition and WASH cluster among other clusters where relevant at that point in time.

Project Activities

Act 1.1.1 Provide Consultation services to an average of 40 consultations per day to offer treatment of common communicable diseases and appropriate follow ups to the affected population with an aim of reducing the disease burden. Act 1.1.2 Provision of intergrated health,nutrition and wash related services to manage cases of acute diarhoea or other AWD.. (In adherence to WHO standards.) NHHP services that WRRS intent to deliver based on the presentation of the climatic changes in the year, (rain or drought). Cases requiring Advanced/comprehensive emergency care will be referred referral hospital or in cases of outbreaks set up a CTC or CTU. Act 1.1.3 The project will provide Immunization services (EPI) as part of primary health services integrated with nutrition services (through referrals of the malnourished children and mothers to the out-patient therapeutic program) will be delivered by the vaccinators in the MCH. Children and pregnant women with malnutrition will be referred to nutrition centres in close proximity for nutrition care and support. Act 2.1.1 Provision of curative services. including management of childhood illnesses Act 2.1.2 Through the Health workers the project will provide services to mental health cases but refer emergency cases from the MCH into the major referral Hospital. sensitization services will include the need to have children immunized, early health seeking at the health facility for sick patients. Act 2.1.3 capacity building of staff Act 2.1.4 The project will equip the supported MCHs sites with essential drugs, vaccines, to deliver lifesaving health services from UNICEF and (WHO in cases of outbreaks like Cholera, measles and other haemorrhagic fevers) the procured medical supplies. Act 3.1.1Provision of surveillance services. On a weekly basis, the project will share Surveillance for epidemic diseases received at the MCHs including, AWD/Cholera, measles through case registration using DEWS tools with Health clusters, WASH cluster, sub-clusters and MoH for prompt response in case of an emergency outbreak. For public health concerned responsible chain of command to respond.

Mitigation Measures

WRRS through the project intends to involve the community both male and female, boys and girl of all ages in all stages of the program cycle, so that they understand the rationale behind decisions made.The project intends to use already existing community governance structures and use them as health committees. Health committees will be involved in recruitment of staff, community mobilization and monitoring of the project.Their presence will represent the interest and role of the community in successful completion of the project. The project will also have a feedback mechanism , from which complaints and comments from the community will be collected, and fed back to the same. This will be done in a highly confidential manner with very few staff in the management allowed to interact with this process. Complaints will be addressed immediately while compliments will also be shared with staff. Protection will be key in the project. We will ensure that girls and women that face abuse discovered in our centers get help. Our project will also treat all beneficiaries equally and protect the dignity of our beneficiaries.We will ensure that our project involves most community groups so as to demystify operations and avoid rumors of the projects' activities. We will also protect our staff by collecting security updates regularly and sharing with them in time so that they do not go to areas where their lives will be endangered.he level of conflict and catastrophic that have been experienced in Somalia have resulted in need to set measure to mitigate this including other risks. The ongoing conflict continue to cause displacement. It is estimated that the number of IDPs have surpassing two million. Displacement dynamics have consistently been directed mainly towards urban centers and merging with urbanization dynamics of increasing pressure on services in larger towns and cities. Humanitarian conditions in many of the informal settlements and public buildings occupied by the displaced throughout Somalia continue to be dire; and may be further aggravated for communities who are forcibly evicted from their location of settlement or continue reside with insecure tenure. In IDP settlements, many reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security provision. IDPs with their perceived links with non-state armed actors face increased exposure to security threats and discrimination in assistance provision from the host community. specific concern is the large number of drought and flood-affected IDP communities from conflict areas, who are often made up of separated families with men and youth remaining behind, increasing the exposure of women and girls to protection risks. WRRS will work closely with the local authorities to address some of the concerns encountered that may pose risk to the beneficiaries being targeted in the intervention.Displacement affects individuals of all gender, ages and sexes, as well as from diverse social backgrounds, in different ways and needs may evolve over time. Many IDPs, whether recently displaced or in a protracted displacement situation, are compelled by a lack of access to adequate assistance and livelihoods, to resort to negative coping mechanisms with serious protection repercussions. For instance, children are at a heightened risk for recruitment into fighting forces, child labor, early marriage and dropping out of school. This project targets beneficiaries with specific activities tailored to meet the needs of affected population with specially designed activities for each age group (e.g. children under 5, pregnant and lactating mothers, women of childbearing ages).
