Project Summary

The Community Engagement Strengthening Project (CESP) is a response to the growing number of community-based organizations (CBOs) and local non-governmental organizations (LNGOs) across Syria which have emerged largely in response to the Syrian crisis. In line with the global humanitarian community’s efforts around meaningful localization, CBOs and LNGOs are taking on prominent roles in humanitarian and early-recovery programming through design, targeting, leading consultation, and overseeing implementation. These community actors play an important role in restoring confidence, sustaining the momentum of recovery and transformation and bringing divided communities together around activities which represent mutual benefit.
The purpose of CESP is to strengthen community platforms that are inclusive, accountable, and responsive to community needs and aspirations. CESP builds on AKF’s early successful pilots through a three-step results-based implementation process of ‘Form, Build, and Action’. CESP will form 8 new CBOs in Salamieh District, build the capacity of 16 newly formed CBOs and existing LNGOs, and through a competitive process, provide 19 community small grants (CSGs) to CBOs and LNGOs to identify, design, implement and monitor community-based humanitarian and early recovery projects which meet immediate community needs.
AKF, through its Do No Harm approach, has cultivated a deeper understanding of prevailing authorities, power structures, and possible risks to beneficiaries as well as project staff. AKF will actively mitigate the unintended consequences of targeting strategies, sectarian fragilities, promote transparency and ensure wide community participation. CESP will ensure a gender-inclusive approach to these activities by ensuring that CBOs are formed with adequate representation of women and adolescent girls; organizational capacity assessments of CBOs/LNGOs are designed to assess the understanding, knowledge and skills that members have on gender equality and women’s empowerment; and community-based consulting groups for the award of CSGs have adequate representation of women and adolescent girls. CESP incorporates evidence-based design and implementation and continual beneficiary feedback loops. The project utilizes comprehensive evidence gathering mechanisms which prioritize primary evidence gathering to determine relevance, impact, safety and efficiency of project design and implementation.

4 - Needs

The severity of humanitarian needs within the Syrian crisis have been well documented with an unprecedented level of food insecurity, ongoing violations of international humanitarian law, breakdown of key social services and economic sectors, continued protection challenges, protracted displacement, and vast numbers of households reliant on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs. The international community has also rightly expressed concern over intangible, harder to measure challenges such as social cohesion, identity politics, sectarianism, and lack of governance. Research in the area of social capital finds that the ‘social capital index’ in Syria has decreased by 30% during the crisis (Ismail, Jebaie, Mehchy, & Nasser, 2017). There is a real risk that while communities may recover from a socio-economic perspective without deliberate efforts to foster an inclusive local society, Syrian communities are more likely to remain exclusive, fragmented and, in turn, unable to identify and inform their own humanitarian and development needs effectively. Women are often excluded in discussing their needs/priorities in their communities in the absence of community platforms that integrate their participation. The longer-term implications of divided communities present a number of concerns not only for Syria, but for the regional and international community.Today, the humanitarian landscape in Syria is considerably more diverse with LNGOs working alongside the UN system and INGOs throughout the country to address immediate and long-term challenges. These nascent organizations, while essential to meeting immediate humanitarian needs, are often connected in a sub-contracting relationship and unable to fully mature, particularly as donors pivot towards partnering with INGOs. Meaningful localization within a protracted crisis requires new ways of engaging and building up community platforms within Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

CESP adopts a three-step results-based implementation process of ‘Form, Build, and Action’: FORM – Increased formation of inclusive community-based organizations (CBOs): The formation process involves community context analysis, stakeholder consultations, establishment of a community consultation group, general awareness building, and voting/selection of a CBO leadership body, with particular emphasis placed on gender/age representation. A total of 8 CBOs will be formed with an inclusive gender and age sensitive leadership body of 13 individuals (30% F) per CBO; including IDPs, residents, women, youth, elderly and PwDs. BUILD – Strengthened governance and management capacity of local organizations. CESP supports a total of 16 organization (8 new CBOs and 8 LNGOs) through an organizational capacity building programme reaching 239 members (72F; 167M). Training topics include planning and implementing humanitarian and early recovery programming; participatory rural appraisal and rapid assessments; mobilizing and empowering communities; communications and conflict management/resolution; gender equality and women’s empowerment, organizational management; and inclusive governance. Organizational Capacity Assessments of CBOs and LNGOs, including gender equality, will be conducted to determine trainings needs.ACTION – Increased access to financial resources through Community Small Grants (CSGs). CBOs/LNGOs will mobilize communities to identify, design, implement, and monitor community-based humanitarian and early recovery projects to meet community needs. 19 CSGs will be provided to 19 CSOs through an open call for proposals. Community based consulting groups will be established to evaluate and select projects to receive CSG support. Example CSGs include the establishment of community parks, awareness campaigns around GBV prevention, community wide arts and culture activities, increasing street safety through solar lighting, and community psychosocial support events.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A. AKF staff and civil society organizations may face pressure or threats from some community members to get benefits and/or control over the project. likelihood: medium, impact: medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will establish strong linkage with local leaders who have influence on community members. AKF will also closely monitor the project implementation to identify and record potential threats, and take immediate response in case of staff or local community are exposed to harm. B. CBO/LNGO members might be criticized or accused of bias in selecting the beneficiaries or define the community needs. Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will oversee the selection process and ensure a precise implementation based on the selection criteria. Regular and confidential monitoring and feedback/compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of exploitations and take actions against it. AKF and the consultation groups will assess the proposals to ensure the relevance to community needs and ensure neutrality in selection the beneficiaries.C. AKF staff might be criticized or accused of favoritism or bias in selecting projects to receive CSGs. Likelihood: medium, Impact: low. Mitigation measures: AKF will ensure the inclusion of all stakeholders (women, men and youth, local leaders) in developing the selection criteria of selection.D. Gender related cultural and traditional norms and roles may discriminate/limit women’s participation in the CBO/LNGO or the small grants/ community initiatives. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will explain and ensure women representation in CBO/LNGOs and promote for their active involvement throughout formation and implementation phase (at least 30 % females). Women will be asked preference of specific work/task to ensure their comfort. AKF will facilitate gender equality awareness raising to facilitate positive interaction and appreciation of female participants.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement of staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors the regional and local security situation using a variety of primary and secondary sources. In the event that mobility of staff and beneficiaries in rural areas within Salamieh District is deemed a high risk, AKF will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is able to be reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Risk 2: High pressure from local communities to respond to their increasing needs with requests to expand small grants reach might lead to communities’ dissatisfaction. likelihood: medium, impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will openly communicate key messages on available funding to cultivate trust building between AKF, CBOs/LNGOs and local communities.Risk 3: Inflation and exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Likelihood: High, Impact: low. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the project and revise as needed. Inflation risk is mitigated by managing the amount of project liquid cash residing in the country. AKF manage reserve amounts by taking into consideration future cash flow needs to ensure cash on hand in the field is minimally exposed to inflation.Risk4: Lack of adherence on the part of MOSAL to deliver the required permits which results in either delayed or protracted approval processes for the legalization of CBO/LNGO projects. Mitigation: AKF will cooperate with CBOs/LNGOs to adjust implementation timelines till approvals are secured otherwise will have to allocate the fund for other priority projects of CSGs.
