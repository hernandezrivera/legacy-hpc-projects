Project Summary

As part of the proposed action, SIF will directly contribute to objectives 1 and 2 of the Education Sector’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Syria through providing non-formal education (NFE) to out-of-school children (OOSC) and children at-risk of dropping out both within and outside public educational institutions to provide a path to enter formal education. This proposed project will be part of an Education, Protection, WASH and Shelter integrated programming under 2020 HRP which will include rehabilitation of WASH facilities in the targeted 8 public schools and psychosocial support, including case identification and referral to specialized services in public schools and learning centers. Children will also be provided with awareness sessions on child protection concerns, including gender-based violence and risk education.
The response proposed by SIF is based on its 3-year experience in implementing UNICEF-funded education projects in Syria. SIF is thus intending to replicate its successes, taking into account the lessons learned, in locations showing high severity of needs. In 2019, SIF reached more than 960,000 direct beneficiaries with a budget of around USD 7,500,000.
Through the proposed intervention, SIF will rehabilitate 8 schools in Shafuniyeh (1), Qahtanieh (1), Karnaz (1), Mourek (1), Eastern Kwaires (2) and Jabel Saman (2) which will benefit approximately 3,200 children. SIF will also provide non-formal education (NFE) to a total of 2,500 children in Duma and Kafr Batna (Rural Damascus), and Khan Arnaba (Quneitra) in 3 public schools and in 4 learning centres in As-Saan and Karnaz (Hama Governorate), and Jebel Saman and Eastern Kweires (Aleppo Governorate) while 250 teachers and education personnel will benefit from professional development. By providing transportation, children with disabilities will benefit from a facilitated access to education facilities in learning centers. Moreover, boys and girls will be both encouraged to participate in the activities through Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns. These districts were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and SIF field assessments, with all the localities being rated over 4 out of 6 on the 2020 HNO severity scale.
The proposed intervention will include:
- Rehabilitate the buildings of 8 public schools and provide them with gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities, benefiting to 3,200 children;
- Establish 4 learning centers and provide them with furniture;
- Provide 2,500 OOSC and children at-risk of dropping out with NFE programs (self-learning programs (SLPs) and remedial education) in 4 learning spaces and 3 schools;
- Reach 2,500 children through BTL campaigns;
- Provide 2,500 students with school supplies in learning spaces and schools;
- Provide maintenance and running costs (such as cleaning material, stationery for school personnel or fuel for heating) in 4 learning spaces and 3 schools;
- Provide approximatively 1,000 children with transportation to 3 schools and 4 learning centers;
- Provide professional development to 250 teaching staff and education personnel (child-centered and protective pedagogy, active learning, self-learning, life skills, Curriculum B, New Curriculum, psychosocial support and referral mechanisms, Education in Emergencies and national/Interagency Network for Education in Emergencies minimum standards);
- Provide 250 teachers and education personnel with teaching resources, kits and guides in formal and non-formal settings;
- Provide textbooks to 2,500 children;
- Provide 2,500 children with supplementary learning materials.

4 - Needs

Throughout the past 9 years, access to education and teaching quality have significantly deteriorated as a result of the Syrian crisis. After a thorough needs assessment in December 2019, SIF found that a large percentage of educational facilities were either destroyed or used for purposes other than education in the targeted locations. As a result, most of the schools are congested and overcrowded. Moreover, most of the families reported unaffordable high costs of uniforms and school stationeries for their children. Coupled with scarcity and unaffordability of transportation, this is further dissuading parents from sending their children to school. Finally, other limiting factors, such as early marriage, child labour and violence both at school and home, are further limiting children access to education. In this context, girls and female adolescents are particularly subject to gender-based violence both on their way and inside schools. They also suffer from the absence of sex-disaggregated WASH facilities. Teaching quality has also drastically decreased as most of the teachers and education personnel are no longer in their teaching posts. Thus, reliance on unskilled and unspecialized teachers with limited financial remuneration is impeding learning outcomes of students in public schools. Finally, the lack of textbooks, teaching and learning materials impairs learning in the classrooms and demotivates children to continue education. As a consequence, most of the children have dropped out of school or at risk of dropping out in the targeted assessed areas. Please note that the project will not address education needs for youth as SIF’s mandate is focused on childhood in which the organization has a long and rich experience in its different countries of intervention.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project builds on SIF’s past experience in rehabilitating schools and providing NFE and protection assistance in learning centres and public schools with UNICEF and UNHCR. SIF has already established 5 learning centres in Rural Hama and Rural Homs and is intending to expand services to areas showing high severity of needs. The project will include the rehabilitation of 8 schools by contractors selected by SIF as part of a transparent and accountable tender process. SIF will closely coordinate with Ministry of Education and ensure that a period of guarantee is included in the contracts for the sustainability of the action. NFE will be directly implemented by SIF in both 4 learning centres and 3 public schools. In line with the Directorate of Education and Ministry of Education, SLPs for OOSC only will be conducted in public schools during the weekends and holidays while both SLPs and remedial classes for OOSC and children at-risk of dropping out will be implemented in SIF learning centres. Children with disabilities (CwD) or living in hard-to-reach areas will be provided with transportation to ensure their attendance to the activities. Learning and WASH facilities will also be adapted to CwD and gender-based specific needs. SIF will conduct pre-test, intermediate test and post-test with the children in order to evaluate their initial level and progress. The students will be grouped according to their level and age. The activities will be monitored on a daily basis by SIF’s team through field visits using data collection tools (Child Monitoring System and 4Ws). Through monthly reports and budget follow-up, SIF will ensure that standards of project implementation and fund resources are being effectively implemented. Participatory approach, through a complaints and feedback mechanism, will be undertaken to identify needs, implement and evaluate programs to ensure the views of all stakeholders are considered.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Teachers or other education personnel and students participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: establish a code of conduct in learning facilities; training and prevention in schools; professional development training on dealing with bullying, provide PSS activities in learning spaces. 2. Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: application of Child Safeguarding measures: recruitment of staff according to the safe recruitment process; training of all staff and associates who engage with children on child protection, identification of children victims of violence, first line support and safe referral pathways, safeguarding policies and procedures; signature of code of conduct also posted on walls; establishment of effective child-friendly complaint mechanism; awareness sessions on children's rights and GBV; sufficient number of adult women working in the learning environment to protect and reassure girls attending activities. 3. Children are exposed to UXOs on their way to schools or learning centers. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: thorough assessment will be conducted by SIF team in centers and schools’ surroundings prior to beginning the activities; children will be provided with RE as part of the Protection project.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Inflation rate increases prices. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: SIF has already included the inflation rate in the budget; in case of continuous inflation SIF will adjust the BoQs accordingly. 2. Overcrowded schools cannot accept new coming students who benefited from SLPs and have a negative impact on attendance and dropout rates. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigating: in last resort situations, children will be maintained in learning centres in order to provide them with continuous education activities; new students will be provided with remedial classes in learning centres in order to support their reinsertion into the formal education system. 3. Parents/caregivers discouraged from sending their children to school or attending awareness sessions. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: in case the parents would not support SLPs and would refuse to attend awareness sessions, BTL campaigns through door-to-door will be organized. A dedicated SIF team will visit the families and provide them with the necessary information about the importance of sending their children to school, and supporting them at home. 4. Unavailability of qualified manpower resources in the targeted locations. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: SIF will introduce campaigns and new methods of recruitment to reach quality staff from the same project locations. 5. Staff misbehaviour toward children. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: all of the persons working on this project will be trained on the code of conduct and will sign it; a feedback and complaints mechanism will be implemented and children sensitized on its use. 6. Learning facilities could be attacked. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: strengthen learning facilities; safety protocols in place; provide preparedness training.
