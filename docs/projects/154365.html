Project Summary

Tdh project will respond to the needs of internally displaced person (IDPs) living in informal sites and collective centers in Salah al Din through conducting Camp Coordination and Camp Management activities. These include creation and capacity building of the community structures, regular needs assessment, referrals to service providers as well as coordination among humanitarian actors. Returnee and IDP population living in Tal Afar District (Ninewa) will be supported by operating a Community Resource Centre (CRC). The CRC will serve as information hub for the community by collecting data on available services and employment opportunities. Furthermore the CRC community structures will allow the beneficiaries to monitor their needs in a participatory manner and submit them to humanitarian actors through CRC coordination structure.

Needs

The CCCM Cluster’s ongoing multi-sectoral site-level data analysis indicates 926,000 persons in need for the CCCM Cluster services, of which approximately 482,000 IDPs are in formal camps. IDPs that are continuously underserved, unable or unwilling to return and in protracted displacement; another 116,000 IDPs are in collective centers and informal sites which are severely under-served. Around three quarters of the noted population are in informal sites for which key informants report a lack of services in at least three sectors, with 97 per cent of individuals reporting no explicit intention to return in the next 12 months to their Area of Origin. Provision of basic services and assistance, as well as improved site management and coordination will be required in 2019. An estimated 28,000 individuals in host communities and around 300,000 returnees are estimated to in need as a result of not being adequately covered by the social protection floor and depending on assistance. In Salah al Din governorate, where Tdh is conducting CCCM activities, the majority of IDPs live either in unfinished or abandoned residential and public buildings and are unfavorably exposed to climatic conditions. In addition, many IDPs lack adequate access to basic services (i.e. drinking water and sanitation, healthcare and education) and livelihood opportunities - therefore exposing them to a greater degree of vulnerability (REACH Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (MCNA) Out-of-Camp IDPs Sept 18) . In complement, Tdh is also supporting the well-being and safety of IDPs residing in 28 neighborhoods in West Shirqat city by providing core CCCM activities (e.g. service mapping and coordinating humanitarian services, needs mapping). In August 2018, Tdh carried out an assessment of out-of-camp displaced population needs based on key informant interviews. The main findings of the assessment in Shirqat were that the top priorities for IDPs are food (86%), sanitation (40%) and clothes and footwear (40%), followed by education of children (34%), and employment and medical care (23% for both). There are many food related challenges faced by IDPs: low or no income to purchase sufficient amount of food, irregular and limited monthly food rations from the government; ration cards that are registered in another district and therefore inaccessible from Shirqat; and only a small number of humanitarian actors providing food kits to highly vulnerable families, either through case management or for newly arrived IDPs. Tdh is additionally supporting IDPs, and returnees with a Community Resource Centre in Tal Afar district in Ninewa. The governorate is the most severely affected governorate in Iraq in terms of people in need of assistance and proportion of returnees to host governorate population. In Tal Afar district, 53,379 families returned as of October 2018 and there are 2,268 IDP families (DTM round 106). In Tal Afar city, approximately 111,000 individuals have returned compared to the population of 200,000 individuals in 2014. The defeat of ISIS in the district took a heavy toll on the city. In August 2018, Tdh in collaboration with REACH and INTERSOS, conducted an Area Based Assessment (ABA) to inform evidence-based planning and prioritization of needs by actors implementing humanitarian and recovery interventions in the district. The main findings were that residents in the district need support with: Livelihoods: Only 33% of assessed adults were reportedly earning an income (median income being 335 USD per month) Education: 19% of school-aged children (6-17 years) in Tal Afar were not attending formal education during last school year. Many schools are overcrowded and structural damages impede the provision of basic services: most of the schools do not have water available in the toilets (pipes are either broken or blocked) or the electricity is not working. Health: The vast majority of households require access to free health care services as current service costs are the main barrier to receiving treatment. In addition, essential public infrastructure has been critically damaged in the city centre which impedes returnees’ access to basic services including,but not limited to, electricity and water, and solid waste disposal. This is further complicated by a significant reduction in the level of public services due to loss of government staff and expertise, resourcing, and difficulties obtaining public funding. Some returnees are also reluctant or unable to return due to protection and security issues stemming from the historical ethno-sectarian divisions. There is also widespread damage to markets, manufacturing and retail shops, and given that returnees are impoverished, their inability to obtain income generating activities further challenges the populations’ self-reliance. An estimated 5% of residential buildings are also destroyed and many more partially damaged. UXO contamination is also a challenge to returning households.

Activities

Tdh CCCM objectives, activities and indicators are aligned with the Iraq National CCCM Cluster Objectives 1 and 3 and activities 2018-2019, and the draft Iraq HRP 2019 Strategic Objective O1: “Increase access to basic services: for the most vulnerable conflict-affected people and ensure responsible transition to durable solutions for those with prolonged humanitarian needs”. CCCM activities in Tikrit and Shirqat, Salah al Din: Tdh will facilitate and coordinate the provision of multi-sectorial interventions to ensure a safe and dignified environment for IDPs in informal sites in both Shirqat, Samara and Tikrit districts and supporting the wider host communities in Salah Al Din. Tdh will achieve this by conducting the following activities and measuring their respective indicators: CCCM Cluster Objective 1: Facilitate and coordinate the provision of multi-sectorial interventions and conduct site risk reduction activities to ensure safe and dignified environment for internally displaced people in formal and informal sites including newly and secondary displaced. 1.2.1 Establish CCCM mobile teams for site management, monitoring and community engagement - community participation through theengagement and training of IDP representative committees, with focus on marginalised groups such as women, elderly and youth, and establish community complaint feedback mechanisms in each site. 1.2.2 Establish/update a master list of informal IDP sites at appropriate geographic level - developing and maintaining databases / site profiles for IDP populations in each geographic location by CCCM teams in Shirqat and Tikrit 1.2.3 Establish/update 3W’s of partners operating in informal IDP sites - CCCM teams update mapping of humanitarian partners’ services fortnightly (Who/What/Where/When) 1.2.4 Coordinate/ conduct intention surveys with relevant partners and clusters to ensure the intentions of populations are understood and supported - CCCM teams carry out intentions surveys twice a year to ascertain IDP’s intended movements 1.2.5 Establish or support existing governance structures and focal points for informal IDP sites - identify, establish and train community focal points to improve community participation and empowerment, enhance knowledge of needs, and share information with the community. 1.2.6 Together with other clusters and partners, as well as the authorities, coordinate the establishment/ efficient use of documented referral pathways for assistance/service delivery (including to persons with special needs). - identify and link beneficiaries in need of other forms of humanitarian assistance (including Protection), , and refer to appropriate actors while keeping a record of referrals. Community Resource Centre (CRC) activities in Tal Afar, Ninewa: Tdh will continue to support communities to identify and access essential services (including referral pathways), disseminate information, map services provision, support coordination among humanitarian partners and government authorities, and ultimately advocate for intervention by relevant actors in Tal Afar district, Ninewah. Funding for the extension of the CRC project beyond January 2018 (IHPF) has to be confirmed. National CCCM Cluster Objective 3: Facilitate sustainability of return and support to affected populations (Community Resource Centers) 3.1.1 Supporting the establishment or expansion of the CRC mechanism in high priority affected areas - establish a Community Resource Centre in Tal Afar in line with the concept of operation established by the CRC Steering Committee and JCMC. 3.1.2 Ensuring populations within the catchment area of a CRCs are able to access appropriate services (government, humanitarian, and development). - identify and link beneficiaries in need of other forms of humanitarian assistance (including Protection), government services or ‘recovery and resilience’ interventions, and refer to appropriate actors. Maintain a public database of available services in Tel Afar district. 3.1.3 Encouraging durable solutions to displacement, supporting the camp transition strategy as well as minimising the risk of secondary displacement, by ensuring appropriate linkages with humanitarian, transition / recovery and government actors - support durable solutions for IDPs and returnees by assisting them and local authorities to access humanitarian, and stabilization / rehabilitation efforts. In addition, Tdh will develop a road map within the first year of operation, in collaboration with the CRC Steering Committee, for the handover of the CRC to local authorities 3.1.4 Establishment of two-way communication mechanisms to affected population s in CRC catchment areas to ensure accountability of humanitarian and government actors providing services. - establish and promote the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism within the CRC

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks beneficiaries are exposed to during the implementation of project activities are multiple. Key protection risks are: 1) Marginalization of specific ethnic or religious groups due to partiality of the government actors and service providers. This applies to families perceived as IS affiliated and therefore excluded from access as a form of punishment. Members of religious group or clan which is different from that of local administration can often be marginalized. The priority is given to the population affiliated with the members of the local governance structures. The pressure to prioritize access of certain groups can further be extended to humanitarian actors. 2) Government pressure to obtain beneficiary data collected as a result of needs assessment and referrals 3) Under representation of the most-vulnerable participative structures. As a result, their needs and rights would be inadequately addressed and up held respectively. 4) IDPs might be subject to forced displacement by local authorities of Salah Al Din during the implementation period 5) Vulnerable groups may have limited access to services due to the constrained capacity of local authorities and limited presence of humanitarian actors. Vulnerable households (female, child or elderly-headed) with limited livelihood options might not be able to pay for the access to basic services which local authorities are unable to provide, water or sufficient food being only few examples. Furthermore these groups might also have limited voice to advocate for an improved access to services. 6) Operational access to area of intervention may be impeded due to deterioration of the security situation or lack of clearance from the local authorities

Mitigation Measures

Tdh will ensure that all groups, particularly women, are integrated and part of the community engagement teams of the different neighbourhoods. Tdh will advocate within the coordination structures for the needs of the vulnerable groups which were identified. Tdh will ensure that referral mechanisms are in place and functioning, and that in addition to assistance for the survivors, prevention and sensitization activities are implemented. Tdh will also ensure inclusion of age/gender disaggregated data in site population figures, and as possible collect indicators disaggregated by gender on the different activities and services provided. Tdh will also advocate to local authorities for service provision to be impartial, and advocate through appropriate bodies and mechanisms to address incidents of biased service provision. Finally, Tdh will ensure to use data and information responsibly in order to uphold the rights of the individuals, groups, and organizations with whom we work. In addition to technically securing data, Tdh will also safeguard the rights of people to be counted and heard; ensure their dignity, respect and privacy; and enable them to make informed decisions and not to be put at risk when providing data. The proposed project systematically incorporates protection principles in programming and implementation, and in particular: 1) Meaningful access is ensured by programming and implementing that promote a non-discriminatory access of beneficiaries, determined by needs and exposure to risk only. Particular attention will be given to proactively promote meaningful access for girls, women, persons with disabilities and particular vulnerabilities Consequently, Tdh focuses on involving equal input from women and men in the affected population (e.g. establishing women and youth representative committees), and thereby ensuring their participation and involvement in humanitarian coordination and planning. In addition, Tdh will promote and advocate for humanitarian principles (e.g. impartiality) to be observed by Tdh staff, government authorities and humanitarian partners. 2) Tdh will appropriately collate, analyze, and disseminate the information it collects to ensure participants’ right to be heard, especially within marginalized populations, and furthermore ensure that a participant’s personal dignity is maintained throughout data life cycle. Tdh will also ensure that participants are informed to make a decision about their participation in any data activity, and their consent is informed and voluntary. Where the process involves minors, Tdh will get both their parents / caregivers’ consent. Tdh will not discuss or share in any form, information gathered from participants with any unauthorized (by Tdh or beneficiaries) persons and store them securely to avoid exposing participants to any risk as a result of its data activity. 3) Accountability to the affected population will be ensured by the systematic set up of feedback mechanisms and complaint mechanisms, accessible, inclusive, adequate to sex and age. Authorities and communities are continuously involved at every stage of the project cycle to ensure voice and ownership. 4) Tdh will advocate returns to be conducted in a humane way and in adherence of the Principled Returns Framework. That is the returns are safe, informed, dignified and voluntary. 5) Where service gaps are identified, Tdh will advocate through appropriate coordination and cluster mechanisms at every level to promote a response to address the basic needs of the population in need.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Tdh is accountable the affected population which is recognized to be a dignified key actor to ensure meaningful participation and voice in all phases of the project cycle. Tdh is committed to the Accountability to Affected Population IASC framework, specifically by:- Promoting leadership and governance and ensuring that staff is aware of, and programs and approaches are in line with accountability to theaffected population principles. - A commitment to build on the capacities of local population and strengthen the resilience of people affected, whenever possible. - Collaboration and consultation with local actors in order to ensure implementation of activities that meet the needs and will encourage beneficiaries to actively participate.- Transparency of communication in order to help affected population to take informed decisions. - A focus on providing clear and transparentinformation on available services, consequences and risks will be given in particular to case management services’ beneficiaries in order to enable them to take informed decisions. At all project cycle steps, continuous and transparent communication on Tdh’s activities and objectives will be ensured with local authorities, as well as all other actors operating in the area. Tdh will also observe full coordination and information sharing with relevant cluster and sub-clusters both at national and governorate’s levels.-The establishment of feedback and complaint mechanisms that are child friendly and adequate to the age and the gender of beneficiaries. Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be designed to be inclusive, accessible, confidential when needed, and to not to be factor of exposure to further harm. Feedbacks and complaints will be systematically taken into account, FGDs will be regularly conducted with beneficiaries. This will allow to assess the level of satisfaction within the beneficiaries and adapt the design of activities and their implementation to the current needs and available approaches used for beneficiaries involvement. Tdh will take all necessary measures in order to ensure operationalization of Tdh’s Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP), including the identification of a Child Safeguarding (CS) focal point, set-up of safe and accessible reporting mechanisms, adoption of measures to ensure data protection, confidentiality and dignity. All Tdh employees, partners or visitors are carefully briefed on the document that must be read, understood and signed before starting any assignment, collaboration or visit.- Participation of beneficiaries to the principle of accountability is incorporated at all stage of the project cycle. A particular focus will be given to children participation and specifically children girls’ participation. During all activities children will be encouraged to participate and speak out. A wide range of participatory activities will be implemented in order to make sure that all opinions, particularly opinions of those moremarginalized, are taken into account. Community-based design activities are prioritized.- A participatory Design, Monitoring and evaluation of activities is ensured through ongoing consultation with beneficiaries. Focus groups discussion, bilateral and informal group meetings and other activities will constantly aim at improving the capacity of the project to better meet beneficiaries’ needs and collect best practices and recommendations.- Tdh will also take all necessary measures to ensure operationization of Tdh’s Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP) and PSEA policy, including the identification of a Child Safeguarding focal point, the setup of safe accessible reporting mechanism and the adoption of measures to ensure data protection and confidentiality as noted
