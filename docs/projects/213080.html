Project Summary

The project will directly contribute to support the local community by providing a safer environment for children. The project contributes to qualitative and integrated education services, also contributes towards maintaining and enhancing equitable access to education services to conflict and earthquake-affected children across Northwest Syria.
This project will be implemented in Northwest Syria, Idleb in Dana (89.43% Severity 4 / 10.01% Severity 5) and Ariha (72.81% Severity 4 ) subdistricts
Locations have been identified based on accessibility and Severity scale 22-23, however, this might change due to situation on the ground.
The education objective is to increase access and quality of education to IDP children and host communities. The project intends to operate in area with high severity ranking (Ariha 5 / Dana 4) and with high numbers with Children in need (Ariha 43,509 / Dana 457,971) around 4% of the PiN in the targeted areas are from CwDs The main goal of this project is to scale up safe and equitable access to formal\ non-formal education for schoolaged girls and boys between ages 5-17 by:
Activity 1: Provide children with Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE)
In Northwest Syria, specifically in Dana and Ariha subdistricts, we aim to enhance Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE). This involves training 150 teachers and education personnel, rehabilitating 92 classrooms to be inclusive, providing recreational materials for 5,140 children aged 3-5 years, furnishing 6 schools or learning spaces with necessary furniture, ensuring 6 WaSH blocks have gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive facilities, and supporting maintenance and running costs for 6 schools or learning spaces.
Activity 2: Support the provision of formal education to children and youth
We aim to provide incentives to 194 teachers and education personnel, supply teaching resources, kits, and guides to 150 educators, offer recreational materials to 5,140 children aged 6-17 years, facilitate enrollment of 5,140 children aged 5-17 years into formal general education, and ensure these children receive necessary school supplies.
Activity 3: Provide children and youth with non-formal education programs
We're committed to providing non-formal education programs, including psychosocial support and supplementary materials. We plan to offer psychosocial support to 5,140 children aged 6-17 years, provide supplementary materials to the same number of children in non-formal settings, supply teaching materials to 150 teachers/facilitators, and support the enrollment of 1,028 children aged 5-17 years in non-formal education through Catch-up Classes. 0 - Project Summary (extended)

In our endeavor to expand access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth in Northwest Syria, particularly in the Dana and Ariha subdistricts of Idleb, several key needs have been identified that directly influence the implementation strategy and location-specific interventions.One critical aspect is ensuring the enrollment of children aged 5-17, both girls and boys, in formal general education. This necessitates addressing barriers such as financial constraints, as evidenced by 33% of children being unable to afford schooling. Additionally, 28% of children are engaged in work to support their households, highlighting the need for targeted support to facilitate their attendance in school.To address these needs, our project will provide operational and running costs to targeted schools, covering expenses such as fuel for heating and salaries for teachers and administrative staff. This is particularly urgent as the school season has commenced, yet many schools lack coverage for essential expenses, potentially jeopardizing educational continuity.Moreover, our efforts extend to enhancing the quality of learning environments through maintenance and rehabilitation of classrooms. This includes ensuring gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive facilities to accommodate children with disabilities, a vital aspect for promoting inclusivity.Another pressing need identified is the shortage of essential teaching and learning materials in schools. To bridge this gap, we will provide education kits containing school bags and stationary for children, along with necessary learning materials such as pens and notebooks. Additionally, targeted schools will receive hygiene items and stationary for classrooms, ensuring a conducive learning environment.Furthermore, there is a recognized need to secure winterization requirements for schools and provide safety essentials like first aid kits. These provisions are essential for safeguarding the well-being of students and staff, particularly in crisis-affected areas.As for the implementation strategy, we will collaborate closely with local schools and educational authorities to ensure effective delivery of educational programs. Our team will work hand-in-hand with teachers to provide necessary support and resources, targeted schools will receive operational and running costs, hygiene items, and stationary to create a conducive learning environment.For non-formal education, we will adopt Curriculum B, which is specifically designed to cater to the needs of crisis-affected children and youth. This curriculum focuses on providing a holistic education experience, encompassing academic learning as well as life skills development.Overall, our implementation strategy prioritizes collaboration with local stakeholders, adoption of tailored curricula, and provision of necessary resources to ensure that all children have access to quality education in Northwest Syria.
