Project Summary

In 2024, the SCI will expand its multi-sector integrated approach to better prioritize the WASH-related acute vulnerabilities from a risk reduction perspective, through risk mitigation and case area targeted interventions, such as before the rainy season in the flood-prone areas and cholera hotspots. Considering the population movements and lessons learned regarding the cholera outbreak and historic floods, SCI will also reinforce the local capacities and prepositioning of the most cost-efficient common pipeline supplies. SCI will continue to mainstream protection and mitigate gender-based violence (GBV) to ensure that access to WASH services is framed after systematic consultations with women and girls from accountability for affected population (AAP) perspective at the top level beyond the feedback mechanisms, in particular women and children can do so in a safe manner. SCI will prioritize the following activities under the various categories and strategic objectives of the HRP:
• WATER: Support life-saving assistance through the provision of safe water at the household level by ensuring emergency and sustainable water sources are available for the affected population. In camps and hosting communities, support for health and nutrition centres that will present acute water shortages will be prioritized. The partners will increase responses in water quality through routine monitoring and chlorination (household and communal level). Additionally, the safety of beneficiaries to access water points will be increased by ensuring the functionality of water points, and improved ownership by the community and technical designs to reduce incidences of theft and vandalism of submissible pumps and equipment as reported in 2023.
• SANITATION: Improve access to sanitation, by ensuring the availability of gender-segregated sanitation facilities to the affected population, desludging to compliant dumping site, and by the introduction of eco-friendly sanitation solutions for fecal sludge management (FSM). Additional support will be prioritized to Health, Nutrition centres and Temporary Learning Spaces which will be closely coordinated with respective sectors. Before the rainy season, the sector will prioritize vector control campaigns in camps and scale up preparedness plans, including dislodging capacity support.
• HYGIENE: Promote in local languages culturally appropriate hygiene messages for social behaviour change, all along the key WASH NFIs distribution and monitoring by the WASH partners to all target populations, namely the households with SAM children and/or cholera cases. Prevention and awareness messages will be aimed at breaking the transmission cycle of communicable diseases (especially cholera) which will improve food intake and health of children especially those under 5, significantly contributing to health and nutrition objectives. Priority will be given in Borno State to Bama, Damboa, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Kala/Balge, Konduga, Mafa, Magumeri, Maiduguri, Mobbar, Monguno and Ngala LGAs, in Yobe State to Fika, Geidam, Gujba, Gulani and Jakusko, and in Adamawa State to (Girei, Madagali, Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South, Shelleng, Yola North, Yola South. Further, the sector will target women and girls with menstrual hygiene awareness items and messaging including improving the capacity to locally prepare produced menstrual hygiene items.
In addition, SC will tailor the program through its active local partners to ensure Integration into its existing nutrition program to address issues of malnutrition, the outbreak of communicable diseases and improved access to schools by ensuring water and sanitation access for TLSs (temporary learning spaces), in line with the centrality of protection with guidance from Protection Sector.
Localization will be prioritized and improved capacity of response for national organizations through capacity building and promoting partnerships.

1 - Needs

Five million people have been identified in need of humanitarian WASH assistance across the BAY States with a significant deterioration of the severity compared to last year, from 27 to 34 LGAs in critical situation (phase 4). Women and girls accounting for 52% of the population in need, often are tasked with fetching water far away, potentially exposing them to protection issues. Children accounting for 60% of population in need are highly impacted making them susceptible to WASH related diseases and malnutrition. Persons with disability require adaptive WASH infrastructures-water supply and sanitation, through which their dignity and hygiene are upheld. Access to safe and sufficient quantity of water remain limited in nearly all of the BAY states affecting the population groups in the IDP/informal settlements, returnees and host communities, with 17% of assessed households reported having less than SPHERE standard of 15l/p/d with 5% of assessed households having to walk for more than 1 hour round trip to access water. Overall Borno has 9 LGAs with critical/severe water needs (Severity between 3 and 5), while Adamawa has 12 LGAs with critical/sever water needs (severity between 3 and 5) with Yobe having 4 LGAs with critical/severe water needs (Severity between 3 and 5), total 25, LGAs with critical/sever water needs (severity b/w 3 and 5) in three BAY states (Borno, Adamawa & Yobe). Sanitation across the IDP camps remain a lingering challenge and major need arising from difficulty in FSM reducing useability and access to latrine with 71% of assessed camps having latrine to person ratio above 1:50 beyond SPHERE not reaching standards. Borno has 18 LGAs with critical/severe sanitation needs (Severity between 3 and 5), while Adamawa has 17 LGAs with critical/sever sanitation needs (severity between 3 and 5) with Yobe having 17 LGAs with critical/severe sanitation needs (Severity between 3 and 5), total 52, LGAs with critical/sever sanitation needs (severity b/w 3 and 5) in three BAY states.1

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will work with different community groups, including organizations of persons with disabilities and community-based rehabilitation centres (where they exist), to sustain sensitization and awareness activities targeted at men, women, boys and girls about prevention of SGBV at household and community levels. Frontline SC staff will also have their capacities strengthened on disability inclusion and inter-agency SGBV referral pathways and how to receive disclosures and make safe, timely and ethical referrals for SGBV survivors.
