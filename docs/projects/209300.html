Project Summary

Chad hosts the largest number of people fleeing violence in Sudan, including 496,834 newly arrived Sudanese refugees as of December 2023, and 93,700 Chadian migrant returnees according to the International Organisation for Migration (IOM), in Ouaddaï, Wadi Firas, Sila and Ennedi Est provinces.
New arrivals – mostly women and children – often arrive in poor health, malnourished, traumatized and wounded. New arrivals first settle in overcrowded, spontaneous sites along the border, where they sleep in makeshift shelters. They need protection and assistance, including psychosocial support, food, shelter, water and sanitation services, and core relief items such as mattresses, soap and cooking utensils. Given the high proportion of those who have been physically assaulted, survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) and women and children among new arrivals, mental health and psychosocial support and child protection are critical concerns. Due to insecurity in areas along the borders, relocating newly arriving refugees to settlements remains a top priority.
The new arrivals come mostly from Darfur, one of Sudan’s regions most affected by violence and where the escalation of conflict, including attacks, indiscriminate violence, lootings, and intense fighting, continues to force thousands of people to flee. Some 500,000 people were displaced to Chad by the end of 2023. An additional 250,000 refugees and 60,000 Chadian migrant returnees could arrive in Chad in 2024.
New refugees continue to arrive in an area that was already hosting 407,000 Sudanese refugees. In this region, where natural resources are scarce, infrastructure, essential services, and livelihood activities are limited, the most vulnerable host, refugee and returnee families already face incredible hardship, with refugee families hit the hardest.
The latest food security and nutrition surveys conducted in 2023 showed high food insecurity and malnutrition rates. More than 577,990 people are severely food insecure and cannot meet their most basic food needs without external assistance. Food insecurity affected an additional 536,852 persons during the lean season, the period between harvest when food is most difficult to access. Food stocks were depleted, and people did not have enough money to buy food.
Refugee families are hit the hardest. More than 80 per cent of refugees have a poor or borderline consumption level, with food insecurity impacting almost 83 per cent of new arrivals compared to 73 per cent of the existing refugee population. Refugee families with specific needs are among the most food insecure. In these regions, 10 per cent to 16 per cent of families resort to negative coping strategies to survive. These include selling household assets, reducing the quantity and nutritional quality of meals, begging, survival sex, borrowing from traders, and taking children out of school for daily work to be able to buy food. Levels of malnutrition are also extremely concerning, with the prevalence of global acute malnutrition exceeding 10 per cent in all three provinces.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

The UNHCR and CNARR are actively pre-registering and conducting biometric registration for refugees arriving at the Chad-Sudan border. As of December, 136,649 individuals have been registered, emphasizing collaboration with government entities for screening, separation of combatants, and community policing. The project spans three phases: head counts and pre-registration, setting up new settlements and relocation, and establishing/strengthening protection services. Key sectors include Child Protection, Gender-Based Violence (GBV), Community Mobilization, Education, Food Security, Public Health & Nutrition, Livelihoods & Economic Inclusion, Settlement & Shelter, Water Hygiene & Sanitation, Logistics, Supply & Telecommunications, and Environment & Energy.In Child Protection, services include identifying and supporting at-risk children, family tracing, alternative care, and community-based initiatives. GBV efforts focus on prevention, survivor support, and economic empowerment. Community Mobilization is crucial for multisectoral response, ensuring community engagement, and promoting social cohesion. The Education sector aims to enroll 250,000 school-aged children, construct classrooms, provide school kits, and enhance teacher training. Food Security addresses immediate needs, nutritional assistance, and long-term agricultural initiatives. Public Health & Nutrition involves healthcare support, facility construction, and health worker recruitment.Livelihoods & Economic Inclusion prioritizes agropastoral value chains, SMEs, and empowering vulnerable groups. Settlement & Shelter focuses on constructing shelters, rehabilitating existing ones, and providing essential items. Water Hygiene & Sanitation plans water supply infrastructure, boreholes, sanitation facilities, and hygiene services. Logistics, Supply & Telecommunications ensure efficient transport and pre-positioning of relief items. Environment & Energy initiatives include tree planting, alternative energy promotion, and community engagement for environmental protection.The extensive use of cash assistance is emphasized for dignified support and economic empowerment. Ongoing conflict in Sudan necessitates reinforced coordination, collaboration with authorities and affected populations, risk mitigation, and mainstreaming cash-based interventions.The project aims to address immediate needs while fostering long-term resilience and inclusion of refugees in national systems. Coordination, collaboration, and community involvement are key themes throughout the multifaceted response.
