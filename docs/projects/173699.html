Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen the resilience of 20,000 conflict-affected children and adolescents by providing equitable access to quality and conflict-sensitive educational services. In order to respond to the cumulative disruptions triggered by insurgency, seasonal natural disasters and COVID19 pandemic on the educational systems of Borno and Yobe States, the proposed intervention seeks to ensure the continuation of basic education by targeting out-of-school IDPs, returnees and host community children/youth in the following target areas: Maiduguri (Borno State), Damaturu and Geidam LGAs (Yobe State).
To achieve the abovementioned goal, COOPI proposes a holistic intervention which converges the provision of formal/informal educational programs and child protection services with the construction/rehabilitation of learning spaces and WASH facilities. More specifically, the project will focus on: I. improving children’s access to alternative education programs to enable them to reintegrate in the formal school system; II. training teachers on risk education and psychosocial first aid in order to recognize signs of distress and other protection concerns in children to refer them to appropriate professionals; III. training youth on vocational/entrepreneurship skills and providing start-up kits to enhance their future employment opportunities; IV. construction/rehabilitation/equipment of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS)/Classrooms with provision of didactic/recreational kits, schools’ furniture and WASH facilities, with a special focus on the needs of girls and children with disabilities; V. capacity-building sessions for communities, School-based Management Committees (SBMCs) and Government Ministries and Agencies on school management and risk reduction planning.
The proposed activities are meant to be carried out through a proper mainstreaming of cross-cutting issues of gender, protection and inclusion within the EiE. A child and youth protection-safeguarding approach will be prioritized to protect their rights from abuse and exploitation. An integrated approach to EiE and CP will be promoted in all areas of operation, providing psychosocial support for most vulnerable children as well as COVID19 awareness campaigns, including the disinfection of schools. The purpose is to create a safe, inclusive and protective learning environment able to ensure the physic-mental wellbeing of learners and educational personnel in a manner that does not contravene the social distancing protocol and COVID19 protection measures.
In order to ensure more harmonized and impactful outputs, the response will be implemented in close coordination with the ERL, WASH, Child Protection (including GBV) sectors, meaningfully engaging with the Ministry of Education, State Universal Basic Education (SUBEB) and State Ministry of Youth, Sport and Social Development. Equally relevant, the action will build on the best practices and learnt lessons from COOPI’s previous EiE/CP projects funded by AICS and OFDA in 2017 in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC), Borno State.
In light of the above, the proposed intervention is deeply aligned with the 2020 Nigeria HRP Specific Objective 1.2 (to ensure access to quality basic services, such as education and health), the Yobe State Socio-Economic Reform Agenda (YOSERA) as well as with Borno State’s 25 Year Development Framework. In line with the Grand Bargain, the program is designed to be delivered through the engagement of local entities, thus enhancing the localization approach, the government educational systems’ strengthening and overall communal psychosocial wellbeing.

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian crisis in the North-East BAY States (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) has left 7.9M people (23% women, 57% children) in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020 (HRP Nigeria 2020). An estimated 1M people (98% children, 15% people with disability) are in need of educational services assistance. There are 0.8M out of school conflict-affected children aged 3-17 who are facing critical problems related to living standards. Equally alarming, there are 935 schools closed and 1.4M out-of-school girls and 1.3M boys facing uncertain future without education (HNO 2020). The 33rd round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTMR33) estimated the number of IDPs and returnees to be 2,118,550 (78% women and children, 28% children under 6y) and 1,714,682 (82% women and children, 18% children under 6y) respectively. Out of the number of displaced people, 1,566,011 IDPs are in Borno and 142,425 in Yobe, while 708,527 returnees are in Borno and 187,007 in Yobe. In sites where IDPs were residing with host communities, less than 25% of children were attending school in 16% of sites in Borno and 17% in Yobe State. The unavailability of educational facilities in the areas of return amounted to 47% (Yobe) and 49% (Borno). The frequent conflicts, aggravating climatic conditions and COVID-19 pandemic are negatively affecting the Nigerian education system, with many schools and learning facilities closed/damaged, thus hindering children and adolescents’ access to basic education. As a result, the deprivation of educational services is further fuelling inter-communal tensions between IDPs, returnees and host communities, prompting children to adopt negative coping mechanisms to meet their basic needs (fetching firewood, sexual transactions etc.) and highly exposing them to recruitment into non-state armed groups. Having endured traumatic experiences (deaths of family members, home destruction etc.) and lacking access to education, there is an urgent need to provide protection and education services to support the resilience and well-being of children. This is particularly pertinent since, despite a decrease in the number of security incidents targeting education structures since 2017, the risk of violent attacks remains a real threat. Although proved to be a necessary measure to slow Covid19 spread, the closure of schools adversely affects the improvements achieved in the education sector by causing negative psychosocial and security challenges for children, education personnel and the whole communities (HRP 2020, Covid19 Addendum). Schools do represent more than a place of learning: they ensure social protection, health, nutrition services and emotional support for children. Indeed, the long-lasting closure of schools has increased the risk of learning loss and exacerbated the inequalities, spurring children to carry out alternative tasks for their families and disproportionally exposing girls to the risk of exploitation and sexual harassment. Further aggravating the pre-existing scarcity of school personnel and facilities, the schools’ closure has also increased the risk of losing many voluntary teachers (due to lack of salary) and entailed an alarming deterioration of facilities for the delivery of quality education. The current critical situation is challenging children’ enjoyment of their fundamental right to a safe and inclusive education, hence hampering the realization of the Sustainable Development Goal 4 (quality education). In order to avert the COVID19 pandemic to further aggravate the already weakened Nigerian education sector, it is of utmost urgency to mitigate the immediate pandemic’s effects through a development perspective able to strengthen the resilience of the national education sector against similar occurrences in the long-term. To this purpose, the proposed action aims to ensure the continuity of academic activities without placing the health and security of learners and education personnel at risk.

2 - Activities

In light of the above, COOPI’s intervention aims to carry out the following activities: Specific Objective 1: To provide quality basic education and vocational skills training for conflict-affected children and adolescents (3-17 years) in a protective and inclusive learning environment. Activity 1.1: Community sensitisation through “back-to-school” campaigns to address parents’ reluctance to send their children to school, most of all for girls;Activity 1.2: Conduction of hygiene promotion sessions and Covid19 awareness-raising campaigns in local language to inform students, education personnel, parents and the whole communities on the prevention measures (ex. disinfection, safe distancing, use of masks);Activity 1.3: Provision of conditional cash transfers to households to support for school fees and learning materials;Activity 1.4: Trainings for teachers on improved teaching techniques, risk education and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) for teachers; Activity 1.5: Provision of both formal and alternative basic education programs (ex. integrated Qur’anic Education and accelerated learning models); Activity 1.6: Provision of psychosocial counselling to address stigmatization and discrimination for both individuals/groups within schools and other child friendly spaces;Activity 1.7: Supply of didactic and recreational materials, including ECD kits, for scholars and teachers; Activity 1.8: Supply of hygiene kits, masks and other non-food items to ease the suffering during the pandemic. Specific Objective 2: To construct, rehabilitate and equip educational infrastructures with WASH services. Activity 2.1: Construction and/or rehabilitation of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) and Classrooms;Activity 2.2: Construction and/or restoration of adequate WASH facilities within the schools, disaggregated by gender and age, according to the SPHERE Standards;Activity 2.3: Provision of handwashing water points with soap, sanitizers and disinfestation of the classrooms; Activity 2.4: Supply of basic school materials, equipment and furniture (ex. chairs, desks, registers). Specific Objective 3: To reinforce communities’ resilience through participation on school development and risk reduction planning. Activity 3.1: Creation and/or reactivation of School-based Management Committees (SBMCs);Activity 3.2: Capacity-building training sessions to communities’ members, SBMCs, social workers and Government Ministries, Departments and Agencies (Ministry of Education, Borno State Universal Basic Education Board – SUBEB) on educational infrastructures’ management and the main child protection issues/psychosocial needs/referral system;Activity 3.3: Establishment of a community/school COVID-19 Referral System (protocols and procedures to follow).

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The complexity of the current protracted humanitarian crisis has led the international community to realize that a silos approach is inefficient to respond to the needs of conflict-affected populations/children. As emphasized at the 2016 World Humanitarian Summit (WHS), it is of utmost urgency to mainstream humanitarian, development and peace realms into an integrated action capitalizing on their respective comparative advantages to successfully achieve the 2030 Agenda - Sustainable Development Goals, especially the SDG 4 (inclusive education). Indeed, there is worldwide growing recognition that there can be no peace without sustainable development and no sustainable development without peace. COOPI fully embraces this "New Way of Working" and, in alignment with the HDP Nexus, it is proposing a project with a cross-cutting and inter-sectoral perspective which synergically combines life-saving humanitarian assistance with long-term development activities and peace-building sensitizations at community level. This is based on the premise that to achieve long-lasting peaceful coexistence between IDPs, returnees and host communities, it is paramount to address immediate basic educational needs through a development lens which does not only ensure early recovery but aims to strengthen children and other beneficiaries' resilience to conflict-driven and climate stressors in the long-run. As a proof, besides promoting the recovery for the access to formal/informal education and the rehabilitation of educational infrastructures (including WASH), COOPI simultaneously aims to provide skills-training for youth and capacity-building sessions to LGAs departments (State Ministries of Education, State Universal Basic Education Boards (SUBEBs) and Local Government Education Authorities) as well as to School-Based Management Committees (SBMCs). By doing so, COOPI's goal complimentarily encompasses a strong contribution to social cohesion and economic development through a localization approach and state system's strengthening: both of them ensuring the sustainability of the action and thus an enduring peace consolidation. To this purpose, COOPI relies on a broad variety of partners, widely ranging from LGAs departments and International Organizations to local associations and community leaders, all across the three interlinked sectors of humanitarian assistance, development and peace building.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

COOPI places the community at the heart of its intervention. The community is regarded as the recipient of the action but also as a driver of change to improve living conditions and increase local resilience. For this reason, COOPI organizes in a structured way awareness raising campaigns, and promotes local partnerships and capacity building activities to ensure ownership of the program by the community itself. COOPI considers that mobilization and community engagement are essential to ensure that wherever possible, the population is encouraged to take charge and to be the key element for promoting sustainability. Furthermore, COOPI sets up from the start of the programs a clear strategy for mobilization to enable communities to respond appropriately to their own protection needs, to strengthen their coping mechanisms and to manage positively conflicts, especially in the future. COOPI’s main approach consists of facilitating the re-establishment of community structures and mechanisms especially governance and representation structures, or organizations of women and young people so as to reconstruct, to the extent possible, previous community groups, such as the SBMCs.Gender and age will be taken into strong consideration in the selection of the beneficiaries and in the whole implementation and monitoring of the project. Youth will be provided skills-vocational trainings to enhance their future employment opportunities. The SBMCs will have a strong women representation, fostering a more important role in the decision making among the households. The strong presence of women/children/youth in the project will indulge COOPI to ensure that the project respects the safety, the dignity and the integrity of the women, men, girls and boys receiving the assistance. Prioritization on queues and easy access to distribution sites for beneficiaries with disabilities will be ensured. In addition, COOPI will commit not to engage children in the management of the distribution sites. COOPI monitoring team will ensure that the rights of all beneficiaries are fully respected and prevent gender and age oppressive stereotypes.Furthermore, COOPI has a Code of Ethics, Protection Policy and PSEA Policy whose core pillars and mechanisms will be instructed to both beneficiaries and implementing staff/partners through specific in-depth training sessions. Last but not least, Protection Officers and CFM assistants will be available to gather and answer to the questions of the members of the communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COOPI will put into place mechanisms whereby beneficiaries can express their grievances, seek redress, share feedbacks and recommendations in order to adjust the project accordingly. A great emphasis will be put on children participation throughout the project cycle, especially during needs assessment, program design and implementation. COOPI will have dedicated CRM Officers whose responsibility will be collecting and responding to complaints and feedbacks. Where the nature or the subject of the complaint exceeds his/her jurisdiction, the complaint will be referred to a designated member of COOPI’s Senior Management Team (SMT) to manage the complaint. COOPI will institute the following methods for complaints and feedbacks: A) Complaint Desk: to be set up in the community during crucial activities and distributions to receive complaints/feedbacks from beneficiaries; B) Community feedback sessions and field consultation: to be undertaken by a community social worker in collaboration with the SBMCs; C) Hotline Telephone contacts of the focal person (CRM Officer): to be available to community members to enable them to present complaints and/or feedbacks.
