Project Summary

The proposed project relates to health cluster objective #1 and to activities #2, #3 and #8. Medical Aid for Palestinians will work in partnership with Islah Charitable Society to run two mobile clinics serving Bedouin communities in the Jordan Valley. Each clinic, run by a general physician, nurse and 3 community health workers from two vehicles equipped with basic equipment and medicines will visit 12 communities once a week delivering basic primary healthcare, maternal and child healthcare, health awareness sessions, first
aid classes and hygiene kits.
The communities served by the clinic are isolated, located in Area C of the West Bank where new buildings are prohibited by Israel and their shelters and buildings are often subject to demolition. The mobile clinics therefore will serve as a lifeline to these communities who otherwise would have little access to healthcare.

1 - Needs

Bedouin communities in the Jordan Valley continue to be isolated from government services in part due to their need to live in rural areas within Area C to preserve lifestyle and cultural traditions. There are no permanent healthcare facilities as the Palestinian Authority is not allowed to build permanent structures and there is no regular public transport to take residents to access healthcare. UNRWA faced significant funding cuts and their support for mobile clinics in the WB was being compromised by MAP. The goalof the proposed project is to improve the health status of Bedouin communities in the Jordan Valley by providing equitable, high quality Primary Health Care and Maternal/Child Health services through two mobile health clinics. One of MAP's two mobile clinics has been running for over 12 years in partnership with Islah Charitable Society(ICS) and has developed trust with the communities. We maximize the impact and avoid duplication of services through close coordination with Health Cluster partners and the mobile clinic working group. Added value is provided by our continued advocacy on behalf of all Bedouin communities in Area C and planned legal support and our long-term support for the development of family practice in the WB in partnership with MoH and UNRWA. MAP will also be committed to serve all registered refugees in our targeted communities whom UNRWA stopped serving. Currently the mobile clinic visits 12 Bedouin communities (16,850 Bedouins,93% refugees) at least twice a month with the most vulnerable visited weekly. Limited funding has prevented weekly visits for all communities but this is the target following the recent purchase of the second mobile clinic and improved funding opportunities. During the past year, focus group discussions and knowledge/satisfaction surveys were conducted with Bedouin women, men and children. These showed that services are effective and highly valued. A recent evaluation of the project validated these findings with the results informing the design of the new project phase. Women and children comprise 80% of the target population and are the most vulnerable. Researchthrough the Lancet Palestinian Health Alliance showed significant maternal/child mental health needs and expected reproductive health/communicable disease needs. The Community Health Workers(CHWs) attached to the mobile clinic have unique access to women and children and can conduct screening mental health assessments referring on to the clinic doctor. They will also facilitate disease data collection and enhancing GBV detection and child protection linking these communities with the national referral system.Palestinian National Institute of Public Health research in 2016 also revealed a rise in levels of stunting in Bedouin children under the age of 5. Mobile clinic staff can detect early signs of malnutrition and prevent stunting which is irreversible leaving children unable to achieve their full social, academic and health potential. A male CHW will improve access for men in the community; a particularly hard group to reach. People with disabilities, the most marginalized within these communities will also receive equitable access to health services. The health education and first aid training components of the project improve knowledge and behavior on a range of health issues building greater resilience within communities. ICS is the only mobile clinic providing these services and the demand for them is high. The project is urgently needed to support essential health care services, protect marginalized communities, promote greater resilience within these communities, preserve dignity and avoid causing harm to any Bedouin.Through ouradvocacy work and capacity building activities,MAP promotes the voices of members of these communities, encourages them to provide feedback and complain if needed without fear of adverse consequences strengthening their capacities and improving their sense of well-being.

2 - Activities

MAP working with ICS with close and direct supervision of MAP staff seeks to improve the health status of these communities through improving access to quality primary healthcare services as well as improving health resilience among the population. This is achieved through the provision of two well-equipped mobile clinic which travels weekly to each of the 12 communities to provide PHC services, maternal and child health care for pregnant women, mothers and children up to the age of 5, treatment and monitoring of patients with chronic illnesses as well as hygiene kit distribution for everyone in the community. The project also seeks to increase awareness of community members and change attitudes and behavior to help empower beneficiaries and increase communities’ resilience. Through the male CHW,we hope to see an improvement in male health seeking behavior among the communities we target. In total, 6 CHWs with great knowledge of Area C and experience working with Bedouin communities are selected to build the capacity of the local communities who are isolated and vulnerable.The clinic staff also organise and run day trips for women to nearby cities,summer camps for children and celebrations to mark International Women’s Day and International Day of PWDs.The project coordinator employed by our partner will have daily direct contact with MAP team to ensure project is running as per action plan and budget. MAP staff will be directly involved in all procurement details and complete adherence of partner to MAP’s strict due diligence policies.The MAP team will also support ICS M&E follow up and progress of the project by improving current assessment tools to better measure impact on targeted population. Community participation in carrying out the project monitoring and evaluation is vital as it would highly develop the ownership of project amongst the targeted communities.Accordingly, MAP and ICS will encourage involvement of project beneficiaries to take part in the monitoring and evaluation process through on-going recording of case studies, conducting interviews and providing feedback.The activities include: Activity 1.1. - Weekly clinics are provided to Bedouin women, men, boys and girls in 12 communities according to schedule and adhere to MCH and chronic diseases protocols, provide the needed treatment, proper physical examination, follow up chronic diseases cases, refer patients for follow up and specialist treatment at secondary healthcare clinics, - conduct outreach home visits for PWDs Activity 1.2 Procurement of drugs and disposables Activity 1.3. Provision of medications, supplements and disposables to patients of all age groups in need Activity 1.4 Provision of hygiene kits to families Activity 1.5 Cooperation with MoH to ensure a regular vaccination programme in all 12 communities Activity 1.6 Quarterly household health surveying Activity 2.1- Health and hygiene education sessions for the target community including sessions on reproductive health for Bedouin women and girls Activity, - ensuring awareness raising and health education is in an accessible format for PWDs 2.2 First Aid training courses for Bedouin women and men of the target communities Activity 2.3 First Aid refresher courses Activity 2.4 Procurement of first aid kits and refill supplies Activity 2.5 Community mobilisation activities; trips, summer camps, international women’s day, purchase of toys for kindergartens Activity 3.1 A clinic user survey is conducted to gauge satisfaction with the services provided by the clinic Activity 3.2 Focus group discussions with users of the clinic every 6 months Activity 3.3 Clinical training by MAP staff for ICS staff and CHWs for improved service delivery in line with cluster guidelines Activity 3.4 Mapping of local GBV, mental health and disability services providers and training for CHWs to conduct basic assessments for GBV, mental health and disability issues and referral to services and/or specialized NGOs.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Given our long-standing relationship with our implementing partner Islah Charitable Society and their strong presence in the communities we target, MAP UK and our partner have strongly encouraged beneficiaries’, especially women and youth, active involvement in the project design and implementation. In the first phases of implementation, MAP and ICS conducted key communitymeetings with heads of Bedouin families (elderly) and other active members in the Bedouin communities including women to discuss the project approach, services, number of visits and other details and ideas. These focus group discussions formed the basis for our project design and implementation. We, along with ICS, are open to new ideas and activities suggested by our targetcommunities. Results from various forms of data collection such as household surveys, satisfaction surveys, evaluation sheets and focus group discussions will be reviewed periodically with the ICS Project Coordinator and MAP programme staff to see where possible improvements in the project delivery can be made. The learning from the community feedback will be reported in the quarterly monitoring reports submitted by ICS to MAP. The learnings will also be considered during the MAP Programmes Advisory Panel meetings which take place quarterly as a sub-committee of Board meetings, where any significant changes to the project will be discussed. Increasing MAP’s presence in each targeted community (once a week) means that beneficiaries have full access to both partner staff and MAP staff where they are encouraged to provide feedback through discussions and one-to-one interviews done by the community worker from our partner ICS.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

With the continuation of current political and socio-economic conditions, it was imperative to develop a system of healthcare that is responsive to the needs of such communities with the commitment to agreed Islamic values, humanitarian principles and international standards. MAP ensures the participation of vulnerable affected populations, particularly women and children in every stage of the project cycle. MAP also respects all beneficiaries’ Islamic values and culture. For example, experienced female health workers deal with women and an experienced male health worker deal with men. There are also separate focus group discussions held for men and women and confidentiality and privacy are always respected in any form of consultation andcounseling. Further, the project provides support to all vulnerable people (including people with reduced mobility and non-mobility related disability) in the 12 communities without any discrimination as they all receive the service based on theirneeds. The free of charge medical check-ups and medications provided by the clinic significantly reduces the financial burden on communities. The project focuses on a ‘do no harm’ approach and we track unintended consequences of out interventions to ensure this. The mobile clinics always give their services away from actual or potential threats and are designed to preserve the safety and dignity of its uses. The communities are highly appreciative to ICS and MAP’s efforts. Not only that the mobile clinic is offering much needed care to residents of Bedouin communities, they feel that this clinic provides an essential link with the outside world. Other activities as the first aid training, hygiene supplies and women open days are seen as a good opportunity for women to debrief and share their priorities and concerns. We maintain rigorous financial controls, complaints mechanisms and procurement procedures to ensure we are managing our financial resources effectively, and our regular independent auditsconfirm this. MAP aims to be as transparent as possible and we make publicly available our annual accounts, policies and strategies as well as reports on our work. We have an ongoing programme of due diligence when working with local partners, which includes providing capacity building support to them where needed. we also have a safeguarding policy for monitoring and reporting instances of abuse,exploitation and any protection concerns.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The target population is the total population of Bedouins residing in the 12 communities which the mobile clinic visits in the Jordan Valley and the E1 area with a total population of 16,850 in 936 households as per our most recent household survey conducted in June 2019. 93% of these are registered refugees and 20 cases have been identified as PWDs. All these communities have been identified and targeted given their lack of access to primary health services due to the unavailability of service as well as the physical accessibility challenges. OCHA VPP profiling study clearly show the vulnerability of those residing in Area C ingeneral and in the E1 area. Beneficiaries include People with Disabilities and the mobile clinic services target these individuals within the Bedouin communities, recognising that their access to healthcare is even more reduced than the rest of the community. As a result, outreach home visits are conducted for those who cannot reach the clinics and a regular follow up is being done. The project will particularly: 1. Improve access to key components of reproductive healthcare for women 2. Improve identification and management ofmalnutrition in young children 3. Improve communicable and non-communicable disease control 4. Improve hygiene standards and 5. Improve health “self-help” capacity. Its aim and focus also fits within national vision of a future Palestinian state that “promotes equality between men & women” in hopes of full adoption of CEDAW and the UN Sustainable Development Goals gender equality target aiming to “Provide women and girls with equal access to… health care… [which] will fuel sustainable economies and benefit societies and humanity at large”.
