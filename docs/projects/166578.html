Project Summary

UNICEF and other partners are actively engaged in the national coordination and preparedness planning processes supporting the national Public Health Emergency Operations Centre (PHEOC) in scaling up preparedness and response efforts and implementation of related recommendations suggested by International Health Regulations (IHR) emergency committee. Currently, Zimbabwe is facing multiple hazards driven by drought, economic crisis and the residual impact of the Cyclone Idai, where the humanitarian situation is deteriorating with 6.7M people in need of acute humanitarian assistance and services. COVID-19 is an additional burden further stretching the fragile social systems in place, especially with regards to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services.
The UNICEF WASH COVID-19 response focuses on the immediate measures that must be undertaken with due priority to ensure preparedness and response actions to mitigate the COVID-19 outbreak in Zimbabwe. The plan builds on the Government-led Preparedness and Response Plan for the 2019 Novel Coronavirus and considers lessons learned, established inter-agency partnerships, collaboration with the national Government and civil society partners through leveraging existing platforms and systems at all levels. The response strategy will also build on and expand the significant investments which have been made over the past years in supporting WASH Service delivery, especially for the most vulnerable communities or those who are identified to be as high-risk groups. UNICEF WASH is supporting 3 of the 8 national COVID-19 response plan; namely WASH Infection prevention and control (IPC); (2) Risk communication and community engagement (RCCE); and (3) Coordination, planning and monitoring. The response strategies include:
• Strengthening effective coordination and surveillance mechanisms at national and sub-national level, with links to Health Cluster coordination arrangements on critical inter-sectoral issues.
• Supporting the scaling up of the Case Area Targeted Interventions (CATI’s) with the Environmental Health Rapid Response teams from National to District level in COVID-19 affected areas and to reduce the prevailing high risk of co-morbidity of cholera and typhoid.
• Improving awareness among the population on the importance of handwashing with soap and ‘respiratory hygiene’ with a focus on community engagement, participatory health and hygiene education (PHHE) to educate and to debunk myths and rumors.
• Providing access to critical handwashing facilities including soap and alcohol-based sanitizers, WASH-related hygiene kits, with a focus on the ports of entry, including schools with a focus on pre-positioning of emergency WASH supplies in critical districts.
• Supporting the health system to reduce the risk of hospital-associated infections and enhance infection, prevention and control (IPC) through WASH related support to isolation centers and health care facilities
• Support urban water supply through the provision of limited duration support on emergency water treatment chemicals to urban local authorities.
• Support emergency rehabilitation of water points in urban and rural communities to increase water availability
