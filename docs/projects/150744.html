Project Summary

The proposed project herein targets a very specific children group particularly and directly affected by conflict related violence: children between 9 and 17 years old who have come into contact with the Israeli Security Forces (ISF): those children detained, arrested, under home arrest, injured, recent ex-detainees in addition to conflict affected children at high risk of being in conflict with the ISF. They are exposed to harsh violence, severe child abuse and violations of their rights, including physical and emotional ill-treatment as well as denial of legal rights in the process. Lack of specialized emergency child protection services for them and their families have immediate and mid- and long-term psychosocial consequences for their development, safety and well-being.
This intervention aims to promptly respond to the above-mentioned children’s target group needs as well as to strengthen their resilience and that of their caregivers and communities in the face of chronic exposure to conflict-related violence in addition to reinforce the professional support networks and their capacities to better take care of and respond to the needs of children in distress by providing: (i) specialized legal aid (counselling and representation) in all stages: from injury, arrest to release and reintegration, including caregivers support services throughout all stages, (ii) structured and non-structured psychosocial services in safe, protective and child-friendly spaces to both children and families so as to activate and ensure an intra-family and social safety net around children, (iii) case management support services to children and families that require well-assessed intake (child individual care plans), referral to other services and individual follow up to safeguard and monitor the reintegration of children in their communities and social environments, (iv) tailor-made training on Child Protection Minimum Standards on Humanitarian Action (CPMS) to child protection workers including those involved in the case management networks/system as well as specific sessions to beneficiaries and affected communities focused on community-based actions and top priority resilience needs, and (v) dissemination on the project’s impact on the lives of beneficiaries and affected communities, their participation throughout the lifespan of the project as well as the remaining humanitarian needs and accountability for violations through a info-graphic product that will be used for evidence-based advocacy actions.
The outputs are designed to meet HRP 2018- 2020 Protection Cluster Strategic Objectives (1) “Increased respect for International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL) and accountability for violations”, and (2) “Protection response mechanisms are in place and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence".
Terre des hommes Lausanne Foundation (Tdh) and its project partner the Palestinian Counselling Center (PCC) will target most vulnerable children affected by conflict-related violence, their families and communities in Jerusalem (Silwan, Shuafat Refugee Camp, Jabal al Mukabber, Essawiya) and Area C of the West Bank (communities of Fayasel, Zbeidat and Al Oujah). The locations under this intervention have been selected based on consultations and assessed needs, previous and current access and trustworthy relationship with them and complementary expertise of the project partners as well as based on the protection risks and threats, vulnerability profile of beneficiaries and their protracted exposure to violence and abuse.
By implementing an integrated and comprehensive protection response specialized on the very particular group of children included within the intervention, Tdh and PCC aim at providing critical emergency services in a coherent and holistic way according to the needs as well as to upgrade the capacities and knowledge of the communities and child protection workers to better cope with protracted threats and conflict-related shocks, violence and daily adversity.

Needs

Palestinian children have been affected disproportionately by five decades of chronic humanitarian crisis, dispossession, forced displacement, conflict and military occupation; a situation brought about by a lack of compliance of IHL and IHRL that is progressively deteriorating. Children continue to be exposed to high levels of violence, are denied their fundamental rights, and are unable to live in safety and dignity. Recurring violence, forced expulsions, arrests and detentions, as well as the high risk of direct conflict exposure with Israeli Security Forces and settlers have a harsh impact on their psychological well-being and safe development. The daily impact of living in hot spot settings implies enormous levels of fear and anxiety affecting adult caregivers, child protection workers, leading to negative coping mechanism and reduced levels of resilience. The selected areas have been particularly reported to constantly being exposed to chronic episodes of occupation-related violence, where children are notably targeted by ISF violence. While the particular situation in East-Jerusalem have traumatic legal and psychosocial impacts on children, Silwan, Shuafat Refugee Camp, Jabal al Mukabber, Essawiya areas have been identified to suffer service delivery gaps pertaining to child protection emergency responses (UNICEF, 2018 – Humanitarian SitRep and “Safeguarding Children´s Rights In East Jerusalem”). Likewise, communities in the Area C WB (communities of Fayasel, Zbeidat and Al Oujah) are continuously affected by critical incidents related to the use of force by the occupying power: physical assaults, verbal harassment, live ammunition use, demolitions, settler violence (UNRWA, 2018).The proposed intervention is focused on a very particular children group who is high need to receive emergency child protection response services (yet very specialized according to their concrete situation and needs), precisely as identified in the HNO 2018-20: specialized legal aid, PSS support and case management. Following 2017 data of the Commission of Detainees and Ex-Detainees, 1467 children were arrested/detained by ISF – 55% from Jerusalem, 37% from Area C communities. Physical and emotional ill-treatment as well as denial of legal rights have been extensively documented both in civil/penal courts within the Israeli justice system (Jerusalem) and military courts (Area C) (cases documented CAACWG 2017, 2018). The child target group included in the proposed intervention will be mostly boys because they are the ones more exposed to contact with ISF forces given, among others, the enactment of manhood and masculinity concepts to confront the occupying power (they are stigmatized as heroes when they are released, leading to higher chances to go back to arrest/detention). However, girls are also exposed to conflict-related violence in a different way i.e. harassment, dealing differently with it and linked as well with concepts of safety and honour assigned to female. This is a reflection of the gender roles learnt at home and in the communities. Therefore, activities foreseen in the project integrate in their delivery the analysed gender differences. Current coverage of services is fragmented, not holistic neither specific or coordinated. Within the project, coordination with other child protection actors/agencies (MdM Swiss, SCI and YMCA, among others including existing legal networks) is ensured at different levels: there is an already existing coordination mechanism that will be applied while implementing the project activities: referral pathways, case management, multi-sectoral meetings to discuss about cases that may require a different type of services.Therefore, complementarity is ensured as opposed to duplication. Affected communities and beneficiaries been consulted as part of the design of this project (individual and FGDs). Activities

Activity 1: Provision of specialised legal aid (legal counselling and/or representation) to targeted children affected by conflict-related violence vis-a-vis Israeli Security Forces as well as to their family members directly affected (caregivers support interventions focused on legal aid). Activity 2: Structured and non-structured PSS specialized counselling and safe child-friendly protection activities to targeted children affected by conflict-related violence vis-a-vis Israeli Security ForcesActivity 3: Structured and non-structured PSS services to targeted children’s families (caregivers support interventions focused on PSS). Activity 4: Case management support services to children and families that require well-assessed intake (child individual care plans), referral to other services and individual follow up.Activity 5: Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action (CPMS) training for child protection workers and CBOs’ staff including those involved in the case management networks/system. Activity 6: Awareness specialised sessions (legal, PSS, case management) to targeted communities and affected families and children focused on community-based actions and top priority resilience needs (Community Engagement related activity including both approaches: Communication with Communities-CwC and Accountability to Affected Populations-AAP).Activity 7: Production of an advocacy info- graphic of the intervention which will gather project’s impact on the lives of beneficiaries and affected communities, their participation throughout the lifespan of the project as well as the remaining humanitarian needs and accountability for violations. The design and implementation of the abovementioned activities will be carried out ensuring age, gender and cultural appropriate standards.Tdh will apply at all times its Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP) as well as its Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will ensure partners are applying them while carrying out all project activities.Concerning intervention’s M&E approach, Tdh counts with the Monitoring, Evaluation, Transparency, Quality and Accountability Policy with includes a particular M&E Framework adjusted per project taking into account the timeline, results to be achieved, work plan and indicators. The M&E framework plans the processes for data collection and it is based on the logical framework and indicators of the project. The tools included in the M&E Framework systematically analyse on a regular basis the extent to which implemented activities and their results impact on the achievement of the defined objectives. The methodology includes participatory processes for information exchange as well and feedback methodologies to ensure the inclusion of the beneficiaries’ within the M&E framework (Focus Discussion Groups, FDG; Post Service Monitoring Surveys, PSMS; child-friendly complaints and grievances mechanisms; Information meetings with affected population and community representatives to provide accessible and timely inputs on organizational procedures, types of project activities, objectives, calendar for sessions/event depending on availability and on-site monitoring through field visits). An informed decision-making process that considers the elements shared by all the participants in the project also contributes to a progressive and constant learning, allowing adjustments as necessary for the achievement of results and objectives.

Indicators

210 of children (175 boys, 35 girls) provided with specialised legal aid (legal counselling and/or representation). 210 families (40% male and 60% female) who received caregivers support services focused on the legal aid provided to their children. 350 of children (301 boys, 49 girls) affected by conflict related violence benefiting from structured and non-structured PSS services and child protection activities. 350 of families (40% men, 60% women) who received structured and non-structured PSS services. 65% families reported feeling better equipped to deal with protection risk and vulnerability threats affecting their children and able to apply child-friendly copying mechanisms. 210 of children (175 boys, 35 girls) benefiting from individual case management multi-sectoral services (including intake, referral and follow-up). 35 child protection workers trained on Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action (CPMS). 85% of child protection workers trained on Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action (CPMS) who gained knowledge and perceive that they are better equipped to deal appropriately with children. 175 community members who attended awareness specialised sessions (legal, PSS, case management) focused on community-based action and resilience needs. 75% of the community members (including targeted families and children) who attended awareness specialised sessions (legal, PSS, case management) reported increased quality information, improved participation on project implementation and decision-making based on needs and enhanced practical resilience self-abilities on child protection (reduced psychosocial distress and strengthened coping mechanisms at the community level).
