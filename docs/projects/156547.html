Project Summary

the project aims to Support livelihoods on both households and communities levels through creating income generating activities, supporting the restoring and rehabilitating of assets, and building capacities of youth and enabling them to start their own businesses. In addition to rehabilitating productive infrastructures and contributing to fixing the production cycle of the one-to-two strategic industry or crop through Value Chain methodology. Additionally, Violet aims through this project to improve the sustainability of the health system in Idleb, Aleppo and Hama governorates through installing PVs system to 20 health facilities in order to reduce the operational costs of the facilities and also to ensure the health services.

Needs

Despite a reduced level of hostilities and violence in many key densely populated areas of the country by the end of 2018, millions of Syrians continue to face huge challenges in accessing basic services, due to widespread infrastructure destruction, erosion of the productive base, scarce resources, and massive internal displacement. The social fabric of the country has been severely damaged. Inside Syria, some 11.7 million people are currently in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. An estimated 64-to-84 per cent of the population is living below the poverty line. The erosion of living conditions has severely increased vulnerability and undermined the ability of affected people to cope with the consequences of the existing crisis as well as future shocks. The after-effects will likely continue for many years, especially for women and girls due to the entrenched complexity of the issues they continue to face. With an increasing proportion of the country now moving towards de facto post-conflict conditions, the acute life-saving and protection needs still remain the paramount focus of the HRP. The response, however, must evolve to better take into account longer-term drivers of vulnerability and capitalize on opportunities to rejuvenate social and economic dynamics, while continuing to focus on humanitarian needs. Moreover, the anticipated spontaneous returns significantly increased numbers of IDPs and refugees in 2019 would add to the socio-economic stress in the near future. Ensuring safe, dignified and voluntary return and sustainable reintegration of displaced people will depend on enhanced availability of basic and social infrastructure and services, a secure environment, and viable livelihood opportunities. All activities addressing the longer-term vulnerabilities of the affected people will be guided by humanitarian principles and would be distinct from broader reconstruction efforts which lies outside the spectrum of humanitarian action. Resilience oriented programming and the early recovery approach are integrated and mainstreamed in the 2019 HRP. To this end, a “resilience tag” has been introduced to determine to what extent interventions ultimately support the self-reliance and early recovery efforts of the beneficiary households and communities. The ERL Sector will, therefore, in implementing its multi-faceted community-focused strategy, coordinate closely with other sectors, as detailed below, to promote synergies and avoid overlap and duplication.

Implementation of activities

1. Sustainable livelihoods opportunities for households and communities provided for 750 householders (short-term work for 3 months) as a result of their participation in rehabilitating the basic infrastructures of their communities, so their resilience is improved against the negative coping strategies and the basic services of the host communities are improved by operating-or-rehabilitating the main services/infrastructure. 2. 100 beneficiaries (50% are male and the 50% are female mainly from the youth age-segment, where 10% are with disabilities) are obtained a three months vocational training directly in vendors locations in addition to the basic of business management. 3. 25 beneficiaries (50% are male and the 50% are female mainly from the youth age-segment) out of those graduated from the vocational training programme are obtain Bussiness-Cash-Grants and the required support to start up their small-scale businesses so most of them (75% out of them) are succeed in obtaining a sustained income source. 4. At least 3750 directly benefited households had access to the services of basic-or-productive infrastructure through rehabilitating (or even constructing) 5 facilities. 5. 20 primary health facilities have increased electricity resilience through the provision and installation of PVs systems
