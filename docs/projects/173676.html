Project Summary

LETSAI is a female, youth led organization, which was established to empower youths, adolescent and vulnerable groups in Northeast Nigeria with employability skills, peacebuilding and gender based violence response and reduction. The Boko Haram and ISWAP insurgence in Northeast Nigeria currently at its 10th year have left over 8 million people in need of humanitarian assistance (UNOCHA, 2020). While the youths and children are the targets for recruitment and radicalization by the insurgent groups, the women and young girls are not left out as they are being kidnaped and coerced into marriage and weaponised with bombs. Therefore living families with trauma, conflicting families’ especially if the son of those families are known Boko haram members. Sports have always being an instrument of unity, Football, Horse racing, Dembe (boxing), volleyball amongst others. Due to the insurgence, this unifying games have ended abruptly and everyone is now confined to their various homes. The project seeks to empower youths in Borno state, Nigeria with volleyball and footballs skills as a tool to promote peace, participation, inclusion, human values, acceptance of rules, discipline, health promotion, non-violence, tolerance, gender equality, teamwork, among others. The project will include partnership with the Nigerian sports federation, the Government, Civil Society Organization and community based organization. Our targets will be 450 adolescent boys and girls and youths between the ages of 15 -25, while the community will be secondary beneficiaries with an approximate figure of 1200. . They will also undergo entrepreneurial training with provision of startups, the project duration will be 12 months with continued coaching and mentorship for 6 months after project completion.

1 - Needs

The insurgence in the Northeast Nigeria leading lack of economic opportunities has serious social implications, as the inability to gain financial autonomy affects young people’s dignity and ability to achieve their goal. While unemployment rates are high among the more educated women, the very vast majority of young people suffering from a lack of economic opportunities are the low-skilled (69% of all women have less than a college degree, and 20% are illiterate). Girls are particularly vulnerable, with a staggering 82% of those not in school being out of the labor force either for family reasons. Moreover, even among those young people who are in employment, over 50% are underpaid, majority working in the informal sector, often under precarious conditions. Despite this scenario, most policy interventions in Borno state up to date have focused on a minority of high-skilled unemployed women, while ill serving the less educated majority. In order to enhance economic opportunities and inclusion, promoting self-employment through hand-on skills development, civil engagement and enhancing academic attitude and knowledge among women will greatly minimize the key drivers to extremism and sexual exploitation and abuse. Therefore, in order to enhance economic and social inclusion, supporting vulnerable women in the informal sector and tailoring the design and delivery of training and enterprise support towards the particular needs of young informal workers has emerged as a key priority that is currently unmet particularly in the insurgent affected states of north east Nigeria.

2 - Activities

The key target beneficiaries will be youths aged 20-35, numbering 2500 with minimal vocational skills and education who are internally displaced by the Boko Haram insurgence in Gubio, Rann and chibok local government areas of Borno State. The activities to be undertaken include vocational skills development (mainly craftsmanship), placement of some women in apprenticeship programs, formal entrepreneurship training business planning and financial management. Critical to the success of this program our already well developed beneficiary graduation model that starts from targeting, skills development, civil engagement to after graduation coaching and mentorship. The current model also supports in providing a locally relevant and market-driven suite of urban enterprise options, connecting participants to basic services, developing collective bargaining systems through informal community committees and building deeper community ties in informal settlements where social capital of the ultra-poor is weakest away from their networks of family. The key expected medium results include: • Readiness and employability attitudes and skills enhanced • Civic engagement attitudes enhanced • Interpersonal skills increased • Academic attitudes and knowledge increased
