Project Summary

The ongoing project aims to promote eco-friendly micro-entrepreneurship and alternative livelihood opportunities for marginalized populations, especially women and other excluded groups, in Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilas of Cox’s Bazar district. The initiative supports 1,000 households (HH) in host communities and 600 HH in camps, prioritizing women (70% of host community beneficiaries) and individuals with disabilities (7%), with a particular focus on female-headed households and vulnerable groups aged 18–45.
Participants, selected through ACF’s vulnerability scoring tool and participatory rural appraisal techniques (e.g., wealth ranking, community consultations), will engage in on-farm income-generating activities (IGAs) such as crop farming, gardening, poultry, livestock, fisheries, and aquaculture. Climate-smart agricultural technologies will be introduced to help beneficiaries adapt to multi-hazards like tidal surges, floods, salinity, and drought. Training, inputs, and market linkage support will enable beneficiaries to achieve sustainable livelihoods while enhancing self-reliance and well-being.
Incorporating disaster risk reduction (DRR), the project builds community resilience to climate and disaster risks. Since 2009, ACF has implemented DRR activities in Cox’s Bazar and continues to facilitate the development and review of Community Risk Assessments (CRA) and Risk Reduction Action Plans (RRAP) at the union level. Small-scale mitigation measures identified through CRA and RRAP include training Union and Ward Disaster Management Committees (UDMCs and WDMCs) as per the updated Standing Orders on Disaster (SoD). DRR taskforces focusing on early warning, search and rescue, evacuation, and first aid are being developed with Cyclone Preparedness Program (CPP) support. Community and fire drills, along with first aid training, are organized for enhanced preparedness.
The project will establish Women-Friendly and Inclusive Spaces (WFIS) as hubs for voluntary community actions. These include awareness campaigns on early warning, search and rescue, hygiene, environmental cleanliness, tree plantation, and anti-drug initiatives. WFIS members, particularly female CPP youth volunteers, will assist with DRR efforts and link with CPP structures to ensure comprehensive community engagement. Beneficiaries will receive training on disaster preparedness and climate change mitigation topics, such as vertical gardening, composting, bio-pesticides, and pheromone traps, promoting eco-friendly practices at the household level.
To ensure transparency and community engagement, the project includes a robust complaints and feedback mechanism. ACF’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) team will track progress through surveys, focus groups, and participatory reviews. Contingency plans are in place to address emergencies, enabling staff to respond within 72 hours of a crisis.
This holistic approach promotes social cohesion between host and refugee communities while equipping marginalized populations with sustainable livelihood opportunities and DRR skills, contributing to long-term stability and resilience in the region.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Bangladesh hosts around one million FDMNs, most of whom fled for their lives from Myanmar in 2017. The country set an admirable example of solidarity with the refugees despite its resource constraints. Over the years, solidarity with the plight of FDMNs evolved because of economic instability because of the wage fall and price hike, unequal access to humanitarian aid and uneven distribution of resource opportunities created through substantial donor support to the FDMNs. Because of the disrupted economy that comes with hosting so many refugees, the tension between host communities and refugees increase. To address these tensions, creating increased and sustainable income opportunities of host communities, which will ultimately contribute to peaceful conflict resolution. According to RIMA round 2 (October 2023), half of the households from the refugee community were found having inadequate food consumption in 2022. 68% of the households from the Rohingya community adopted at least one consumption-based coping strategy to cope with the food crisis. Dependency on less preferred or less expensive food is the most used coping strategy in Rohingya (66%) communities; one-third of the households from the Rohingya community followed at least one crisis or emergency strategy and 42% followed a stress strategy of coping. Additionally, 34% of the households from host community are suffering from inadequate food consumption (borderline and poor). According to the Refugee-Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA) round 7 (July 2024), this is the first time since 2017, a significant number of Rohingya households could not afford an acceptable diet. The survey revealed that 70% of households were experiencing poor or borderline consumption, highlighting alarming nutritional consequences. According to the IPC April 2024 projection, Cox’s Bazar FDMN is estimated to have 20% of its population is classified as IPC Phase 4 and 40% as IPC phase 3. Similarly, the host communities are expected to have 5% of their population in IPC Phase 4 with 35% in IPC Phase 3. Key impacts of influx on the host communities, identified through a recent study conducted by ACF (May 2024) in the adjacent host communities include increased prices of goods (83% respondent), decreased rates of wage labor (81% respondent), reduced agricultural land (67%respondent), increased theft and insecurity (63% respondent). There were discrepancies in food consumption between female and male-headed households among Rohingya Refugees, despite of standardized assistance from WFP. A higher proportion of women-headed households reported inadequate and poor food consumption compared to male-headed households (49% versus 41%, respectively). Similarly, in the host community, food consumption discrepancies were evident (41% versus 33%, respectively). REVA -7 revealed that the Minimum Dietary Diversity for the women of reproductive age population in both community is very alarming. More than 80% in both the camps and more than 70% in the Bangladeshi community could not access nutritious food. The above information and context analysis conclude that Rohingya refugee populations and the host communities are facing a severe food security crisis characterized by inadequate food consumption, nutritional deficiencies, and limited access to basic necessities. This crisis is exacerbated by factors such as, gender inequality, tension between Rohingya and host communities and natural disaster and economic shocks. To address this crisis, there is an urgent need for diversified livelihood opportunities to reduce dependency on aid, increased food production and income, improved market environment and supply systems to ensure adequate supply of nutritious food.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

ACF is a member of PSEA network and have an assigned focal person. ACF will conduct PSEA training for all staffs and volunteers involved in project implementation. The project is committed to fostering inclusive participation, specifically targeting diverse groups, including individuals with disabilities, while steadfastly adhering to the "Do No Harm" principle. ACF will establish Women-friendly and Inclusive Spaces (WFIS) as an integral component of this project. The construction of WFIS will prioritize the safety, security, and unique needs of women, person with disability and persons with special needs. These spaces will serve as venues for meetings, awareness-raising sessions, and other activities aligned with the project's objectives. ACF will install complaint boxes at the community level within WFIS. Additionally, ACF will ensure the dissemination of a hotline number through leaflets, stickers, beneficiary passbooks, and other mediums at various project sites. A conflict-sensitive approach will be consistently applied throughout, incorporating risk and security assessments to mitigate potential harm. This proactive strategy aims to prevent interventions from escalating tensions or compromising the safety of both staff and beneficiaries.

2.3 - Age & Disability

ACF maintain Sex, Age and diversity Disaggregated Data (SADDD) and Washington Group Questions (WGQ) for targeting beneficiary in order to address the specific needs of the each beneficiary group. ACF abide by the local laws and by applicable international instruments, including the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child and International Labor Organization conventions. The activities will be enabled for age group 18+ under the project. ACF provides priority for person with disability in selection process and implementation of activities. ACF will provide further training for staffs on SADDD and WGQ. ACF will ensure referral mechanism to ensure rehabilitation services for every person with disabilities and establish facilities after conducting accessibility assessment.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project beneficiaries will be actively engaged in the assessments conducted by ACF like post distribution monitoring (PDM), food security surveillance survey (FSS). The beneficiary selection will be done by community consultations and the affected people will decide to select beneficiaries as per criteria of project interventions. Household with PLW, person with disability, children under 5 and elderly are prioritized in the criteria of selection of beneficiaries. Communities also will provide their feedback on ACF led project activities formally through the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) using hotline number, complaint box and email. And informally through meetings and individual interactions based on which actions will be taken to rectify the project activities if needed. Apart from this, ACF will prepared a Project Monitoring Committee (PMC) by the local community, who will provide feedback and learnings.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

ACF is committed to the mainstreaming of the PSEA principles and has own policy. This entails embedding a robust framework that prioritizes the safety, dignity, and well-being of all participants, particularly those who may be vulnerable or at-risk. ACF will take initiative to aware community people, staffs, beneficiary and all stakeholders involved, equipping them with the knowledge and skills to recognize signs of vulnerability and report any incidents promptly. ACF have clear reporting mechanisms, including a confidential hotline, complaint box and email to encourage open communication and facilitate immediate response to any concerns raised. Additionally, ongoing monitoring and evaluation will be conducted through a PSEA lens, ensuring that interventions remain aligned with best practices and uphold the highest standards of protection for all individuals involved in the program.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, ACF have been implementing disaster risk reduction activities in Cox’s Bazar since 2009. ACF facilitating to develop and review of CRA (Community Risk Assessment) and RRAP (Risk Reduction Action Plan) of Union Parishad. ACF always take initiatives on small scale mitigation measures based on CRA and RRAP. ACF developed DRR taskforce on early warning, search & rescue, evacuation and first aid by the support of Cyclone Preparedness Program (CPP). ACF organized disaster dill by CPP and fire drill by fire Service and Civil Defense as per community sensitization. Capacitated UDMC and WDMC members based on updated SoD. Through this program, ACF will facilitate for organizing community development voluntary action by Women Friendly and Inclusive Spaces (WFIS) through campaigns such as awareness on early warning, search & rescue and evacuation, hygiene practice, tree plantation, cleanness of area, anti-use of polythene, anti-drug and livestock vaccination as per identified problems by the community. WFIS will be oriented and linked with CPP volunteers for assisting DRR activities. Youth female CPP volunteers will get priority as a member of WFIS. ACF will develop and review contingency plans to ensure the safety and well-being of project participants in the event of a crisis. All the participants under the training activities will receive disaster preparedness environ and climate change mitigation topics. This project will introduce climate smart agriculture for homestead gardening such as vertical garden, sac/bag/hanging gardening, use of compost pit for organic manure by using household waste etc. This project will introduce pheromone trap, bio-pesticides at beneficiary level.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project is designed to enhance self-reliance and resilience among refugees through targeted agricultural support. By assisting 600 refugee households with homestead gardening initiatives, ACF aims to empower families to grow their own nutritious food and improve food security. The support includes providing seeds, various inputs for garden structures, and technical training on sustainable gardening practices, enabling households to adapt to their environment and enhance their livelihoods.
