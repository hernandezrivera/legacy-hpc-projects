Project Summary

The locations targeted continue to face inter-sectoral needs in the thematic areas of WASH, Nutrition, Food Security, Health, and Protection. WASH Cluster severity indices and Integrated Phase Classification reports placing the counties targeted at the highest level of severity (Severity 4 and 5, and IPC 3,4 and 5)
High GAM rates, disease burdens, and SGBV cases are prevalent in areas with inadequate WASH services, hence through an integrated approach, the project seeks to reduce WASH-related morbidity and mortality, safe equitable, and dignified access to WASH services while building the resilience of communities.
The project will seek to Integrate WASH response with Nutrition/(Food Security); Reduce the prevalence of WASH-related diseases and risk of an outbreak by transmission contexts upon epidemiological investigation; Increase access to WASH basic services; mitigate WASH-related Gender-Based Violence and build WASH resilience while mitigating the spread of COVID 19 in the areas targeted. The aims of this intervention are in line with cluster objectives That is WASH integrated intervention starting at nutrition facilities targeting the couple “mother/care-taker – severely malnourished child” until HH level aiming at breaking WASH-related diseases transmission cycle. The second most important objective is to improve access to safe water and promote safe hygiene practices in communities showing a high prevalence of GAM and high WASH vulnerabilities level 5. This intervention is expected to reach a total of 32,000 affected population in the areas respectively, The priorities of our intervention include WASH interventions at household and community levels for displaced population/host community in the areas with high malnutrition rates (GAM) coupled with the highest famine risks (IPC5& 4), WASH interventions at the community level for displaced population/host community in the areas at highest risk of protections issues within Nutrition and WASH interventions and cholera, etc. transmission contexts levels for displaced population/host community in the areas at highest risk/rate of cholera outbreaks/WASH-related diseases. CAO will integrate all the four sectors that include WASH and Nutrition training and supervision for treating acute malnutrition, along with distribution of soaps, aqua tabs of minimum package. In OTPs and in the communities, screening for SAM combined with information, education, and communication on hygiene promotion and household water treatment. CAO WASH and health partners will focus on cholera preparedness and response in these hotspots areas that can contribute to the decrease of the transmission rate in case of a cholera outbreak.
Women and girls are affected by WASH need more than men and boys, as women and girls are the primary water collectors, as well as caretakers in the home. In South Sudan, the primary water collector is a female. Increasing safe water access, and thereby reducing collection time, can increase the time spent on livelihood activities. Where possible WASH interventions will seek to increase the access to basic WASH services taking into account safety considerations of water points and latrines that can place women and girls in vulnerable locations that posted a very high risk to the women and girls in Fangak where CAO water and Sanitation are submerged by flooding. The analysis presented reflects people’s needs in all the 78 Counties of South Sudan. COVID-19 mitigating measures limited primary data collection and delayed the usual assessment of the needs during the lean season when people’s needs are highest. Remote methods, including key informant interviews, were used to safely collect the information on humanitarian needs. This integrated WASH project will ensure people with disabilities needs are addressed through consultation and assessing their needs and collect disability dis-aggregated data, addressing barriers to access to WASH Facilities by people with disabilities.

1 - Needs

High levels of needs and vulnerabilities are evident in the 6 counties of Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Warrap state due to poor standards of living and well-being of mostly multiply internally displaced persons, returnees, and vulnerable host communities attributed to poor WASH standards amongst these communities. Chronic inadequacy of access to clean water, improved sanitation and hygiene practices due to limited physical infrastructure, localized violence, erratic weather patterns culminating into floods, displacement, and dysfunctional markets, have played a major role in exacerbating the humanitarian needs in these communities. The most recent Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) release paints a grim picture of the food security, nutrition, and health situation of persons in the counties targeted. The Counties of Akobo, Fangak, Pigi/Canal (Jonglei State), Panyikang, Nasir (Upper Nile State), and Twic County (Warrap State) are all classified under IPC 4 and 5, with 15,000 persons estimated to be under phase 4 and 17,000 under phase 5. the direct beneficiaries are 25,000 and the Indirect beneficiaries 7,000. There’s a direct correlation between poor WASH, malnutrition, food insecurity, and high disease burdens, especially amongst women and children. In Upper Nile and Jonglei, 0.072% of the HHS have access to an improved water source under 30 minutes of collection, with 0.384% of the populations relying on the surface, unsafe water for both human consumption and domestic use. A similar trend is observed in Warrap state, where access to safe water is below acceptable thresholds. Dysfunctional markets make it impossible for communities to purchase household water purification remedies such as chlorine tablets, not to mention a drop in household purchasing power due to economic and livelihood distress. In terms of sanitation access, 0.102% of HHs in the counties of Akobo, Fangak, Pigi/Canal (Jonglei State), Panyikang, Nasir (Upper Nile State) and Twic County (Warrap State) own a latrine within their compounds, 0.024% use a shared latrine, 0.012% use communal latrines, while 46.2% practice open defecation due to lack of adequate, gender-appropriate latrines, as well as socio-cultural tendencies. 0.017% of HHs have access to a dignified sanitation facility and rightly practicing correct handwashing. Seasonal and torrential floods have over the last 8 months undermined efforts by partners and community members to provide adequate WASH services to the most vulnerable, with the observed collapse of latrine facilities, contamination of water sources, destruction of livelihoods, displacement, and hindering person’s livelihoods. High GAM rates observed in Akobo, Fangak, Pigi/Canal (Jonglei State), Panyikang, Nasir (Upper Nile State), and Twic County (Warrap State) are strongly attributed to poor WASH standards, affecting mostly <5s with mothers and caretakers of malnourished children lacking the basic knowledge of acceptable WASH practices. Acute watery and bloody diarrheas have been on the increase in locations targeted, as a result of inadequate WASH services, which in turn contribute to high levels of <5 malnutrition and other morbidities. Since women bear the rigor of water collection for domestic use, many are exposed to risks of harassment, due to distant water points, with 0.246% of women requiring more than 30 minutes’ walk to access a water point. Many in turn resort to consumption of surface water, which is unsafe exposing households to water-related diseases. Protraction of localized insecurity has bred a cluster of persons with disabilities due to gunshot wounds, amputations, with data indicating up to 0.09% of HHs having a member of the family with a disability. PWDs have an increased risk of vulnerability, hence requiring special consideration in WASH service provision. The same trend applies to elderly persons amongst the different targeted sets of internally displaced persons, returnees, and host communities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The major core principle of CAO is the protection mainstreaming as a foundation for all other protection work. CAO has integrated proactive measures to minimize all SEA risks through continuous staff capacity development related to PSEA and Child safeguarding. As a policy, all new CAO staff complete a mandatory induction which includes the code of conduct, PSEA, and Child safeguarding policies, and all employees are trained on PSEA at the beginning of their assignment, and once in a year as a refreshment training. CAO has child protection and safeguarding focal point to address any allegations of SEA on adult population and harm on children, and beneficiaries and community members receive information about CAO zero tolerance on SEA/Child abuse, and information on how and to whom to report any breach of PSEA/Child safeguarding policy. GBV is widespread in South Sudan, and women and girls as main users of water points face significant threats to be exposed to GBV incidents. Feedback received through FGD and Community Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism are acted upon during scale-ups and shared as lessons learned, the project identifies specific areas of risk which include safety and dignity, humanitarian access to crisis-affected populations, populations access to aid, beneficiary privacy, individuals with special needs/risks, social relations, fraud and diversion with protection implications and market impacts with protection implications. The project will include affected communities as participants in all phases of the program cycle to ensure that affected populations identify their own protection risks, benefits, and self-protection mechanisms. CAO through M&E Officers will ensure data on people with disabilities and elderly are collected and the feedback is made to the community ensuring that the project is own by them.Suggestions box can be set up and FGD can be conducted to strengthen the flow of the feedback mechanism including the collection of the success stories. Beneficiaries will be inform through Feedback mechanism with CAO M&E team will ensure complains are collected ,analysis and the feedback is share with the beneficiaries.
