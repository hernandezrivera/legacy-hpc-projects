Project Summary

The humanitarian context of North East Nigerian especially BAY states is volatile. According to the humanitarian needs overview (OCHA, JUNE-SEPTEMBER 2021), about 8.47 million people needed humanitarian assistance due to ongoing conflict in the region. Thefore, GSF shall focus on WASH issues across Yobe state. The wellbeing and living standards of an estimated 3.03 million people will be adversely affected by shortage of clean water and poor sanitation and hygiene practices in 2022.
GSF target locations are host communities. Therefore, this note shall particularly assess the WASH needs in Giedam and Yusufari LGAs as our proposed area of responsibility. Generally, in Yobe state communities hosting IDPs families continue to report additional pressures on critical infrastructures, particularly WASH facilities. In contrast to camp/camp-like settings where emergency latrine are provided, IDPs in host communities are often able to build household latrines. Although, in some locations, partners have also constructed emergency latrines.
However, housing, land and property (HLP) issues prevent adequate coverage, leading to rampant open defecation. While markets are more accessible outside of home latrines, the ability of househods to bear the costs of key NFI (soap, chloine tablet and menstrual hygiene kit for girls and women) has left a huge gap in 2023. Even with an increased willingness to adopt proper and safe hygiene practices, many households will simply be unable to afford to regularly wash their hands with soap.
Moreover, returnees and vulnerable host communities were nearly 2 million across BAY state and they are in acute need of clean water and sanitation support. While 50% of returnees are provided with sanitation facilities, it is likely that some major gaps will remain in may of the returned areas.
Giedm and Yusufari have very low access latrine coverage, partly due to lack of HLP and the costs associated with latrines construction due to economic impact and aftermath of climate changes including cashless poliy that were being introduced by Nigerian Governement, and some of the latrines in schools and health centers were dilapidated structure according to the resident in Geidam and Yusufari LGA. Gender sensitivity of sanitation facilities remained a critical gap in 2023, and agencies are working to address it. HLP issues, particularly the refusal of landowners to grant permission to construct latrines (both emergency and household) has resulted in major WASH gaps, especially for IDPs in informal settlements.

1 - Needs

The convergence of armed conflict, displacement, flooding, food insecurity, and cholera outbreaks in Northeast Nigeria, particularly in the Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states, has significantly amplified the WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) humanitarian needs. The armed conflict has led to mass displacement, with many IDPs living in makeshift camps that often lack adequate WASH facilities. Overcrowding in these camps strain existing water sources and sanitation infrastructures. Seasonal and yearly floods results in temporary displacement potentially contaminating water sources (where not properly protected and resilient), making them unsafe for consumption. Floods also compromise sanitation by inundating latrines or causing them to overflow, leading to the spread of fecal matter and heightening the risk of waterborne diseases such as cholera. Malnutrition from food insecurity weaken immune systems, rendering people especially children under 5 years are more susceptible to diseases arising from poor WASH conditions. Outbreaks of waterborne diseases such as cholera are often a direct result of inadequate WASH facilities coupled with other risk factors such as flooding and poor hygiene practices. Without clean water, proper sanitation, and hygiene education, the spread of these diseases becomes rapid, especially in crowded IDP camps. 2 - Activities

1. Water supply development: This involves the construction and rehabilitation of water supply systems, such as wells, boreholes, hand pumps, and piped water systems.2. Sanitation facilities construction: This involves the construction of toilets, latrines, and other sanitation facilities to improve access to safe and hygienic sanitation.3. Hygiene promotion: This involves the promotion of good hygiene practices such as handwashing, food hygiene, and personal hygiene, to prevent the spread of diseases.4. Community-led Total Sanitation (CLTS): This is an approach that aims to mobilize communities to end open defecation by using participatory methods to raise awareness of the health risks associated with open defecation.5. Behaviour Change Communication (BCC): This involves using communication techniques to encourage people to adopt positive behaviours, such as handwashing and proper sanitation practices.6. Water Safety Planning (WSP): This is a risk management approach that focuses on ensuring the safety of drinking water by identifying and addressing potential hazards in the water supply chain.7. Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM): This involves providing access to menstrual hygiene products and facilities, promoting positive attitudes towards menstruation, and creating a supportive environment for women and girls during menstruation.8. Emergency WASH: This involves providing immediate assistance in the form of water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities during and after emergencies such as natural disasters or conflicts. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming and integration throughout the project activities, the project team will conduct risk analysis exercises prior to activities and periodically thereafter to understand the vulnerabilities, capacities, threats, power holders, gender analysis and service availability. When the risk is identified, either per-existing or potentially due to GSF activities, specific mitigation measures are assigned (time-bound with a responsible person identified), and the risk itself is measured by the community against the positives of the activity proposed. The proposed project will take into account strict adherence to gender equality and equity throughout the design and implementation phase and the specific needs of children and women survivors are based on age and gender, ensuring for instance that adolescent females are assigned a female staff or volunteer, and those young children are assigned a staff who has experience with, and can easily interact with children of their age. The staff and volunteers engaged in the project will be gender-balanced to help ensure that both girls and boys are able to relate to and talk with appropriate persons. The leadership of the community structures will include females and GSF will facilitate participation from all members, irrespective of their age and gender. The use of women to contribute to awareness creation on GBV and CP directly is a context specific opportunity to advance women’s participation. The majority of the religious and community leaders are males with only a few female representatives at the community level. During the training and meetings with the community leaders, the project will aim to engage all female leaders to ensure their representation and may if appropriate, include other well-respected women in the community who are vocal about GBV and CP protection Concerns. Supported by the Community Based mechanism, GSF will monitor the overall protection situation in camps, informal settings, and host communities on a regular basis and develop networks and contacts with relevant stakeholders to identify incidents and protection concerns, such as access to humanitarian assistance by communities in general and by persons with special needs, UASC, GBV survivors, gaps in service delivery but also information related to education, WASH, food, healthcare, shelter as well as safety and security including any related incidents. Concerns on reported/alleged GBV/violations/protection issues will be documented, recorded, verified and analyzed together to support the identification of priority key GBV issues to respond to either by providing direct advice or assistance or by referring beneficiaries to appropriate service providers (in the case of incidents). The methodology for protection mainstreaming will include spot observations, focus group discussions (FGDs) with key informants and monitored populations, individual/household interviews, and area assessments as well as testimonies and complaints collected through the complaint and response mechanism. In addition, as part of the overall mainstreaming strategy, GSF will conduct two-way information sharing with the target population throughout all of its activities, thus providing information to IDPs on return conditions, rights and entitlements while seeking information from IDPs on GBV incidents and protection concerns in camps, host communities and in their areas of implementation.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GSF is committed to the core values of IASC framework on AAP by ensuring participation and inclusion, communication and transparency, feedback and response, organizational learning and adoption. We would be accountable to all our beneficiaries and stakeholders from the onset of the project. This requires GSF to establish Child Help Desk, conduct child friendly CRM, KIIs and FGDs to collect complaints and provide feedback. This systematic involvement and participation of the beneficiaries and stakeholders would ensure accountability and quality response to the affected people. Information sharing about the project activities through meetings with community based stakeholders, gender-balanced staff speaking local languages and message dissemination through community leaders, will ensure that GSF interventions have clear, relevant and timely information required to make informed decision and choices. GSF shall conduct location based focused group discussion with influential community based stakeholders such as youth leaders, women leaders, religious and traditional leaders to understand the appropriate means of collecting, disseminating feedback from each project site. GSF would do that by recommending some of the existing feedback and complaint and accountability mechanism such as the complaint box which has overtime proven to be non-effective as advised by other partners, GSF CFM lines to receive direct calls from beneficiaries and focus sessions with community groups to understand what the issues are and concerted, practical steps of resolving the bottlenecks so as to make the project better and more accountable for the persons that we serve. Community based facilitators will support to generate the data needed to ensure responsiveness and accountability in programming through documenting integrated GBV concerns including instances/trends in violations, gaps in service provision, lack of information, and other barriers to service access.
