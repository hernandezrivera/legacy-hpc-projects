Project Summary

The overall objective of the project will contribute to education cluster objective 2 of the HRP strategic objective , to ensure effective humanitarian coordination among education sector actors including the Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MoEHE) and government authorities in the occupied Palestinian territories (oPt) through the Education Sub-Cluster in Gaza and the Education in Emergencies Working Group (EIE WG) in the West Bank and to provide humanitarian assistance through the promotion of humanitarian principles and the rights of Palestinians living under occupation.
Specifically, the project will ensure uninterrupted implementation of the core cluster coordination functions including:
1. Supporting service delivery and coordination
2. Informing strategic decision-making of the HC/HCT for the humanitarian response
3. Planning and strategy development
4. Advocacy
5. Monitoring and reporting
6. Capacity building in contingency/preparedness planning
In the context of oPt, authorities lack national coordination mechanisms for addressing the enormous challenges facing the education system - each education system, in East Jerusalem, Area C, and Gaza has different duty bearers and face different significant protection challenges. The Education Cluster and the EiE WG, will support the relevant duty bearers, in particular the MoEHE, to ensure that children (age 8-12) 50% girls, youth (13-18) and teachers in the most vulnerable communities can safely access schools that meet minimum humanitarian standards and provide a protective environment in the context of protracted conflict. All interventions planned through the 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) will be closely coordinated with local communities, and meet the requirement of the strong protection mainstreaming and community engagement especially in the needs assessment as well as the implementation of activities.
The EIE WG/Sub Cluster is also planning to strengthen the ministerial level engagement on gender through joint cluster needs assessments on gender specific needs in the field, joint HRP mid review and related gender analysis, strengthening MoEHE capacity on data collection and reporting of disaggregated data related to special needs of women, girls, boys or men. Partners will jointly identify the cluster priority interventions and responses based on the gender disaggregated needs assessments, reports and testimonies. The Cluster will assign the Cluster Gender Focal point in Gaza and WB who will support the cluster coordinators in mainstreaming gender in the humanitarian planning cycle (Cluster HNO/HRP/Projects and reporting), support capacity of cluster partners on gender mainstreaming, facilitate participation of women’s organizations in cluster and identify entry points to address gender gaps in needs identification (including assessments), response and advocacy.

Needs

The Education Cluster estimates that 505,285 (249,327 male, 255,958 female) children across the oPt face challenges in accessing quality education in a safe, child-friendly environment. Some 13,973 (5,942 male, 8,031 female) teachers are in need of support. In the Gaza Strip, recurrent conflict, the deteriorating humanitarian situation, the chronic electricity deficit and ongoing restrictions on the movement of people and goods, have affected 480,013 basic, secondary and kindergarten (KG) students and teachers. In the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, access to education is heavily compromised by an increase in education-related violations, affecting 39,245 students and teachers. This includes 26,387 in Area C, 11,481 in East Jerusalem and 1,377 in other areas of the West Bank. Challenges children face in accessing quality education affect learning outcomes and may lead to increase in school drop-outIn Gaza, deteriorating living conditions, unpaid teacher salaries and lack of school budgets call into question the ability of the education system to deliver adequate education to half a million students. Some 70 per cent of UNRWA schools and 63 per cent of those run by the Ministry of Education already operate on a double- or triple-shift system, reducing instructional hours on core subjects and foundation learning. This is compounded by UNRWA’s financial crisis, and uncertainty regarding whether the Agency can continue operating its schools through the current school year and maintain the delivery of other core services. The vast majority of UNRWA students come from poor and vulnerable families. The deterioration in the humanitarian situation has pushed more families into poverty undermining their ability to afford basic education supplies for their children, and increasing the risk of drop out. In the West Bank, children continue to face violence and challenges in accessing schools. Tear gas and rubber bullets are frequently fired at students, and Israeli forces make regular incursions onto school grounds. Frequently, children must pass through one of 61 checkpoints, where they are subjected to bag and/or body searches, and at times harassment and detention. Students also risk confrontation, intimidation and violence while walking past settlements or buildings occupied by settlers. These children can be exposed to conflict-related violence and delays, which can lead to higher school drop-out rates. According to the April 2018 Cluster assessment, there are 44 schools with demolition orders in Area C and EJ, majority in the Bedouin areas that already suffer from the lack of school infrastructure. According to OCHA 2017 assessment, 36% of residential areas in Area C of the West Bank (189 out of 532) lack a primary school. Against the unique contextual backdrop of the oPt, there is a continued need for coordinated humanitarian response and advocacy. There is a high demand within the local humanitarian community, as well as in the broader international community, for support to develop a robust, detailed, evidence-based common situational analysis; for the coordination of advocacy and operational humanitarian responses; for the management and monitoring of country-based pooled funds; and for the development and monitoring of strategic needs analyses and response plans. With over 40 implementing partners having participated in the 2019 HRP, and financial requirements reaching over USD 15 million targeting 0.4 million people in 2019, there is a clear strategic need for continued coordination of humanitarian action in oPt to maximize achievements, while reducing gaps and duplication in the provision of humanitarian assistance. OCHA’s unique role in facilitating and coordinating these streams of humanitarian work continues therefore to be of critical importance.

Activities

1. Coordinate meetings, at country and operational levels, to ensure a common situation awareness and timely, appropriate and effective humanitarian response.2. Strengthen needs analysis through improved trends analysis and reduced information gaps, including gender sensitive assessments and monitoring, data collection and analysis of key vulnerabilities.3. Working through ICCG West Bank and Gaza and participating in development of joint response plans, promote cross cutting interventions in the humanitarian response including WASH, health and child protection. The 2019 Education cluster response is built around different inter-sectoral linkages. Schools are identified across clusters as hubs for multi-sectoral interventions, so that partners can support children with a more holistic response. The linkage with the Protection Cluster includes working within schools to provide child protection referrals and school-based psychosocial support to students, teachers and parents. Legal aid and protection advocacy are also done through inter-cluster coordination. The Education Cluster will also work with the Protection Cluster to link schools with protection service providers to address protection needs in the most vulnerable schools.4. Implement joint advocacy events among Cluster partners as required. 5. Support service delivery for acute vulnerabilities of populations in Gaza and West Bank according to humanitarian caseload through coordination of education partners including government authorities and development actors.6. Support strategic decision making of the HC and HCT for the humanitarian response, while strengthening Sub-National Education Cluster Coordination in Gaza.7. Contribute to planning and strategy development of the humanitarian response, including in particular the development of the HRP as well as updating Joint Advocacy and Protection Strategy developed by MOEHE. The Cluster will make sure that these documents and strategies take into consideration different needs of girls and boys including children with disabilities and responses are planned accordingly. 8. Strengthen national capacity of MoEHE in contingency planning and emergency preparedness, humanitarian coordination as well as in integrating EIE in education sector plan and budget. These efforts are integral to the transition process, whereby the national Education Cluster transitioned to an EiE WG that is accountable to the Education Sector Working Group. UNICEF and Save the Children continue to be accountable as the Cluster Co-lead agencies for the core functions of the cluster.9. Conduct joint monitoring and evaluation on the general education humanitarian response. 10. Maintain proper information management structures and ensure sufficient information sharing between cluster members and interested actors.11. Promote Resource Mobilization through a) periodic information sessions with local donors on the intervention needs and Cluster projects and strategies; b) updates to partners in the Education Sector Working Group; and c) completion of the Education Cannot Wait (ECW) programme proposal.
