Project Summary

The proposal calls for mobilization of substantial increases in WASH interventions in
support of the Government’s COVID-19 and Natural disaster response and as part of
accelerated action to achieve SDG 6. This step change in approach is necessary not just
as a response to the pandemic, which requires rigorous and regular handwashing as a key
preventative measure, but also to address the steep challenges facing the water sector
more broadly in schools and communities of Killa Saifullah, Killa Abdullah and Quetta
The objectives and targets shaping the behavior of school children around health and
hygiene include providing latrines and adequate access to water (whereby not available,
for drinking and sanitation). Muslim Aid has presence in all three proposed districts (Killa
Abdullah, Killa Saifullah and Quetta) through ongoing (ECHO and NDRMF Project) those
are completing on August 2021. So, Keeping in view the presence of the organization,
project will easily be implemented in government institutions for the provision of WASH
Services for COVID and Natural disaster affectees
The strategies to achieve the objectives and targets are 1) Creating awareness amongst
children, teachers, and community about health, hygiene, and sanitation; 2) Constructing
WASH facilities in schools and health facilities; 3) Providing safe drinking water water
through either repair/rehabilitation of non-functional water supply schemes or installation
of new schemes in 30 Government institutions in three districts of Balochistan; 4)
Ensuring operation and maintenance of Government Institution WASH facilities; 5)
Addressing cross-cutting issues, such as the needs of persons with disabilities through
provision of inclusive sanitation facilities and of marginalized girls and women by
distributing Menstrual Hygiene Kits; and,6) Hygiene promotion for the vulnerable
population, including the messages on the importance of handwashing at critical times
and the use of various household water treatment options (IEC material development,
printing and dissemination, Radio Jingles, TVCs, Static media campaign, infotainment
events, Risk Communication activities) to mitigate the risk of COVID-19 in patients and
healthcare workers.
UNICEF, the situation analysis report indicates a very alarming situation in Balochistan
province where latrine facilitate are not available in 44% of male primary schools, 38% of
female primary schools, 29% of male middle schools, and 20% of female middle schools.
Improving hygiene behavior must go along with toilet construction and the provision of safe water and washing facilities in schools. The report also highlights that 17% male
high schools and 10% female high schools do not have latrines and toilets for students.
Similarly, drinking water facilities are limited in these facilities with 50% male primary
schools and 37% female primary schools; 36% male middle schools and 23% female
middle schools; and 26% male high schools and 17% female high schools, without any
drinking water facilities. not Hence, overall, the situation regarding WASH in Balochistan
public schools is not encouraging.
Pakistan Social and Living Standards Measurement (PSLM) 2018-19 reports that
“Balochistan latrines facilities include people having ush at 41.03%, non-ush 41.6% and
open defecation at 17.25%.” The same report highlights that the source of drinking water
in rural areas is 19% tap water, 8% hand pump, 29% motorized pump, 11% dug well, 12%
tanker/truck , 0, and 20% water from spring, river, canals, and streams. The report also
signicantly quotes the role of different sources and means as to who installed water
delivery mechanism in their respective areas and the results were local government
installed water source delivery in 22% of rural areas, non-government * NGOs, etc. 41% in
rural, household it self *Through own means at33%. The PSLM report notes distance of
drink water source. 51% of respondents reported use of tap water, 5% hand pump
