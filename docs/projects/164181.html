Project Summary

Since the beginning of May until the 13th of June 2019, some 330,000 people were displaced from northern Hama and southern Idleb governorates. A small number of recently displaced individuals have headed to northern Aleppo governorate, whereas the vast majority have been displaced within the Idleb governorate. This puts humanitarian operations that are already at or above capacity under considerable strain.
Less than a quarter of HHs in 91% of villages can fulfil their basic needs, while less than a quarter of HHs in 85% of camps are able to fulfil their basic needs.
The dangerous advance of the Syrian government towards the North has provoked a massive displacement from Idlib area – latest data from the CMMM cluster reports 72,033 individuals’ displacements in NW Syria.
According to the latest HNO, Al Bab area has a major need of FSL intervention, due mainly to the significant influx of IDPS returnees as well as the presence of the newly displaced due to the military campaign in Idlib. Despite the area of Aleppo is considered stable and has been designated as a de-escalation zone, price stabilization and food security remain volatile because of the high number of IDPs. Moreover, although minimum adequate food consumption was assessed, people are unable to afford essential non-food expenditures without facing coping strategies. In this sense, action to provide food assistance is required.
In this regard, White Hats is committed to use its previous experience in the distribution of kits as well as use the staff expertise in the area to the benefit of the project. Despite having established itself more has a stabilization actor through civil society support and education and protection activities, the organization started as an initiative in Syria during the war and has therefore contributed to the emergency response through different interventions. Between them, the distribution of food in different areas of Syria.
White Hats will receive the beneficiary lists from the local council. The MEAL team will conduct 100% verification field visits in each community for the beneficiaries in order to be sure that these beneficiaries. Moreover, it will conduct field visits after the distribution process to know whether the social and economic situation of the beneficiaries improved.
White Hats will distribute the food rations to all the beneficiaries inside the targeted communities, prioritizing women, elderly, children, and people with disability.
At the beginning of the project White Hats will share project information and will conduct focus group discussions (FGDs) with the community and stakeholders. In addition to designing and printing brochures to ensure all the people receive information about the project beneficiaries will also be provided with complaint mechanism information. White Hats will put complaints box in each distribution point, and will set a hot line, WhatsApp and email reporting mechanism in addition to the field visits and observation. In order to mainstream protection, a gender and protection officer and accountability officer will be responsible to make sure that all the beneficiaries are familiar “gender, sexual abuse, complaint mechanism and the confidently of the provided information”.

5 - Implementation of activities

White Hats Organization WHOSD will implement the activities under two scopes of activities; distribute food baskets to the IDPs and distribute RTE to the newly displaced people in emergency cases and massive displacement settings. WHOSD will target 2695 HHs for the first activity and aim at supporting 500 HHs (2500 IDPs) in cases of displacement.The selection of beneficiaries will be according to WHOSD guidelines in coordination with the FSL working group. The list of target communities households will be obtained from the local council and the list will be verified with a third party if possible. The selection process will be relevant to the vulnerability criteria that will be confirmed with the FSL working group in advance. The criteria will include the presence of displaced HH members, women-headed HHs, malnutrition cases, and other criteria.WHOSD will recruit staff for distribution that will receive the required training sessions.The M&E activities will be implemented according to a pre-set plan and the engaged staff will be determined.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Do not harm: The beneficiaries will have improved access to services at their places and houses. In other words, people will not head to specific centres or facilities to get the planned services. Therefore, the harm of accessing the planned services will be at its minimum. However, WHOSD will assess the potential harm due to this program to develop a plan to mitigate it.2- Meaningful access: Attention to vulnerable people was paid during the design of the project, and will be paid throughout the implementation phase and the M&E activities. The most vulnerable groups will be identified, and the M&E teams will collect data about access from these groups to be separately analysed in order to amend the intervention according to the findings. The access will be measured on the level of the individual rather than the level of the household to ensure that the results represent the entire target community.3- Accountability: WHOSD is developing its policies and guidelines related to the accountability to the affected population. The current feedback mechanism is through the complaint boxes, direct interviews, and WhatsApp numbers shared with the target population. WHOSD will communicate with the local council to verify that all listed HHs are matching the existing ones. The compliant system will cover the entire target communities to make sure that the catchment population has access to the compliant mechanism4- Participation and Empowerment: WHOSD will support the empowerment of the target communities through the capacity building component for the local council

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk Mitigation: • White Hats will consider a safe place for selecting the warehouse. In addition, and in case any security incident took place, emergency measures will take place and limit staff movement as necessary. • Proper conditions are available for storing of products;• Quality check of sample from each lot will be performed before the distribution;• RTERs will be prepositioned in the warehouse and in case of emergency will be dispatched in 72h;• Specific triggers of potential displacement will be set and checked to forecast the need of RTERs• Rapid need assessments will be carried out to register the population;• Selection of beneficiaries will keep into account vulnerability criteria and gender sensitivity.
