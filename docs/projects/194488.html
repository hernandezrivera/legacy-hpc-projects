Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan continues to deteriorate ten years after the country gained its independence in 2011 because of an escalation in armed conflict, subnational violence, devastating climatic shocks, economic instability and high levels of food insecurity. Recurring climatic shocks persist with permanent flooding for the fourth year in a row reaching the largest observed levels in 2022 and at a scale comparable to that of the mid-1960’s. Drought on the other hand continues to affect the South-eastern part of the country which has led to significant delays in the onset of favourable conditions for planting and early crop development, hence reducing cultivated areas and extending the hunger gap.

As of end of October 2022, over 924,000 people4 were reportedly affected by flooding in 31 counties across South Sudan and in the southern part of the Abyei Administrative Area. Humanitarian physical access to flood-affected communities is hampered by flooded roads, rising flood waters, insecurity, and poor road infrastructures whereby less than two percent of the country’s estimated 17,000 km of roads are paved.

The already dire situation in South Sudan is compounded by the significant increase in food, commodity, and fuel prices due to the Ukraine crisis and an economic downfall triggered by US dollar/ South Sudanese Pound exchange rate fluctuations. The prices of staple foods have doubled, and the currency has depreciated by over 30 percent since February 2022, further reducing purchasing power of the most vulnerable and resulting in further deterioration of food security and increased vulnerability. Additionally, the increase in fuel prices triggered a rise in transportation costs in Juba and across other urban counties. Over the first half of 2022, fuel prices increased by 90 percent which is expected to lead to approximately 25 percent rise in the primary transport cost. 

The October 2022 Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) results indicated that 7.76 million people – over 63 percent of the population of South Sudan – will likely face high acute food insecurity levels at the peak of the May-July lean season5, with around 43,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5. This represents a slight increase over the 7.74 million people in need during the 2022 lean season and is the highest number recorded since independence.
Additionally, based on the IPC findings, an estimated 1.4 million children under 5 years are malnourished and in need of lifesaving nutrition interventions. Around 738,000 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and new mothers are acutely malnourished and in need of treatment. This is driven by deteriorating food insecurity, poor diet, high prevalence of diseases, sub-optimal mother and child care practices and limited access to basic health and nutrition services.

In response to the dire nutrition needs in South Sudan, WFP's project will facilitate the provision of nutrition prevention and treatment services to populations affected by conflict, displacement, climate change and insecurity. The project will target residents, IDPs, refugees and returnees (for treatment only) to prevent malnutrition related morbidity and mortality.
WFP will continue to work with cooperating partners to support an emergency nutrition response through a preventive supplementary feeding programme and treatment services for moderate acute malnutrition targeting children aged 6-24 months (and up to 59 months in IPC 5 locations), pregnant & lactating women, and people living with HIV/TB across all states of South Sudan.
In hard-to-reach areas, WFP will conduct direct delivery of life-saving nutrition services through the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The project implementation will be coordinated closely with the Nutrition cluster and use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (health, education, FSL, WASH).

1 - Needs

The project aims to address the nutritional needs of vulnerable women and children in populations affected by food and nutrition insecurity resulting from conflict, insecurity, climate change and displacement. The Food Security Monitoring System Round 28 showed that the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) was at 16 percent while Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was 5.9 percent which are above emergency levels. The 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that over 2.1 million people (host community, returned and IDPs) will suffer from acute malnutrition. Of this, over 1.4 million alone will be children under five years of age and over 737,000 pregnant and breastfeeding mothers. In addition to this, over 26,500 children and women refugees will also be acutely malnourished. These figures present an increase of 7% from 2022. The main drivers of malnutrition in South Sudan continue to be climatic, conflict & economic shocks: floods and dry spells, high prevalence of diseases, poor infant and maternal care practises, high food price rises, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence. A total of 31 counties in four states (Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, and Upper Nile) have Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 percent (very high acute malnutrition level- WHO classification). The most affected counties include Rubkona, Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Mayom, Guit, Panyijiar, Uror, Duk, and Akobo, with GAM levels of 20% or above. From the 2022 Integrated Phase Classification for Acute Malnutrition analysis (IPC- AM), 15 counties are classified in phase 3(Serious) and 30 classified in phase 4 (critical). Projection for 2023 lean season indicates a worsening situation with a total of 59 counties projected to be in phase 4. This represents the highest number classified in IPC phase 4 for the past 3 similar seasons. Additionally, food insecurity remains high across the same counties and states affected by high malnutrition rates (classified using the IPC for acute food insecurity – IPC -AFI Phase 4). The IPC AFI projections suggest a worsening situation in 2023 with more states (7) and counties (48) projected to be in IPC 4. The nutrition situation is also expected to deteriorate as we go into the lean season in 2023 with 59 counties projected to be in IPC 4, which is the highest number of counties in the last 3 rounds. The key causes of malnutrition continue to remain dire, especially the worsening food security, WASH, increased displacements due to flooding and violence. Children under 5 and pregnant and nursing mothers will continue to be at the highest risks especially in IDPs, refugee and returnee populations. WFP plans to cover all areas of high malnutrition needs across all ten states in South Sudan.

2 - Project activities

In collaboration with cooperating partners, WFP will: 1. Provide children aged 6-59 months, PLHIV/TB and PLW screened in communities and identified as moderately malnourished with an appropriate treatment protocol, inclusive of specialized nutritious food (SNF), through the Targeted Supplementary Feeding programme (TSFP) under the CMAM approach. The expanded criteria will be used as strategy in some areas when WFP/UNICEF /Nutrition Cluster jointly agree. 2. Provide preventive ration of specialized nutritious food to children U2 and PLW in counties with GAM rates above 10% through a preventive Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme in the aim of preventing acute malnutrition. The Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme will be implemented in combination with the General Food Distribution in areas of high food insecurity. In areas classified as IPC 5, the BSFP target group will be expanded to children under 5 years of age. 3. Support an outreach network of over 7000 community nutrition volunteers to provide nutrition and health message, outreach services, actively screen and identify those at risk or with acute malnutrition and ensure proper referral to health and nutrition services. 4. Ensure the project implementation will use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (Health, FSL, Education, WASH, Protection, NFIs). 5. Ensure revised delivery modalities will allow for prepositioning in locations with irregular access year-round, in strategic hubs with the aim of reducing pipeline shortfalls during the rainy season. River deliveries and airlifts are conducted to Upper Nile, Unity and Jonglei States to the final distribution point where access is impossible by road to ensure the programmes are not disrupted. 6. Monitor and provide joint supervision through a network of field monitors, programme and nutrition staff to ensure implementing partners activities and stocks are meeting the high quality and reaching the most nutritionally vulnerable individuals. 7. Contribute to enhancing the nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information through supporting the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System. WFP undertakes the FSNMS twice a year, once in the lean season and once post-harvest and collects a range of food security and nutrition indicators to inform the situation in country and thereby programmatic adjustments as needed.

The success of the project will be measured through programme performance indicators and through output indicators. The programme performance indicators will comprise of the following: Cure rate; Defaulter rate; Non-respondent rate; and Death rate. These performance indicators will be measured against the SPHERE standards. The output indicators measured will include: a. The number of boys and girls under 5 years of age reached with preventative and curative nutritional assistance; b. The number of pregnant and lactating women reached with preventative and curative nutritional assistance; c. The metric tonnes of food distributed by commodity type for the preventative and curative interventions.On the Food Security and Nutrition monitoring: a. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

WFP monitoring and data systems have been adjusted to include the Washington Group Questions to increase visibility and response to persons with disability. With regards to disability inclusion, WFP is finalizing guidelines on disability inclusion that can be used by WFP and its cooperating partners. The document will also list Food Security and livelihood activities that Persons living with disability can be actively engaged in line with WFP’s Policies. The guidelines will also provide a pool of indicators that Programme units can adopt to measure progress.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To strengthen community engagement, WFP will continue to support the formation and engagement of functioning of project management committees (PMC), comprising women, men, youth, persons with disabilities, and other individuals identified as vulnerable, thereby ensuring that the PMC is representative and inclusive, and empowering the most vulnerable to have their voice heard. The committees facilitate engagement, problem resolution, and communication with WFP to facilitate effective delivery of life-saving food and nutrition assistance. Before and during distributions, WFP and its partners provide information to the affected community on programme objective, activity, eligibility criteria, entitlement, and where and how to lodge grievances and suggestions. This information is disseminated through various communication channels, including radio broadcasts in the IDP camps and POCs; and through community meetings, help desks, hotlines and appointed community mobilizers in remote locations.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

WFP is cognizant of its potential to advancing protection outcomes for the population of South Sudan due to the breadth of WFP access to populations, extensive size and coverage through operations and programmes and, the highly valuable commodity of food and cash assistance. WFP conducts regular protection assessments to capture information on WFP’s current and possible impact on dignity, integrity, safety, AAP, conflict (and Peacebuilding) and other key information. This is then utilized to inform activity design, adjustments and implementation that reflects community realities and preferences (as much as possible). WFP operates with the assumption that GBV is ongoing in all locations of assistance as a product of gender inequality and the social, political and economic environments in which it is operating. As such, WFP applies a Do No Harm approach through participation in interagency GBV Safety Audits and checklists to identify areas requiring attention and mitigation for GBV harm minimization. WFP has increased its investment in researching, partnering and focus on conflict sensitive programming.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Certain aspects of project assistance such as proximity of nutrition sites, availability of shelter, inclusion of particular topics during awareness raising, are informed by community needs/situation. Feedback from beneficiaries will be considered and where feasible changes are made.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

FP will mitigate contextual risks by monitoring the political situation and updating its business continuity plan accordingly. Conflict, protection analysis and rapid food insecurity analysis will help ensure that WFP's activities do not exacerbate conflict or tensions and do not have unintended negative effects on people living in conflict areas. Supply chain management and innovative programme modalities will provide flexibility to respond to changes in conditions. Advance financing, procurement through the Global Commodity Management Facility and pre-positioning will help to ensure that food and nutrition products are available as needs and transport conditions change. Transport capacities will be augmented and means of delivery adapted as needed. WFP will lobby for more functional corridors. Inadequate funding could prevent WFP from fully meeting the needs of vulnerable people. Operational shortfalls and pipeline breaks may damage WFP's reputation and its relationships with communities. To mitigate these risks, the country office has been continuously engaging with partners highlighting needs and emphasizing the importance of allocation of resources early in the year. A refined prioritization strategy will also serve as a risk mitigation measure in case of resourcing shortfalls. To mitigate programmatic risks and facilitate the timely delivery of food and nutrition supplies to people in need, WFP carries out regular conflict and protection analysis. Through its dedicated access team it negotiates with local authorities to ensure access to affected populations and staff security and works with local cooperating partners to ensure access to affected populations and to improve targeting. Risk assessments for transport, storage and delivery help to ensure that operations continue with minimal disruptions and food assistance is timely delivered
