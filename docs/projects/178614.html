Project Summary

Based on HNO 2021. Overall, 13.4 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance.
As reported in the 2021 Mine Action Needs, 10.3 million people live in areas affected by explosive ordnance.
Explosive ordnance contamination remains a major protection concern, severely affecting the lives and livelihoods of the population and further amplifying the social and economic crisis aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Despite a decrease in the number of explosive incidents recorded in 2020, localized hostilities and widespread explosive ordnance contamination continues to threaten lives and hamper access to basic services including medical facilities, as well as the delivery of humanitarian assistance and early recovery of the community.
It is with this in mind that NAS is planning to implement this 12-month project to reduce as much as possible the dangers and injuries that civilians regularly encounter with land mines and explosives, particularly among children in addtion to support and empowering people with amputations directly through prosthetic supports (Provision of 100 prosthesis),The beneficiaries will be selected from through referral channels.
NAS will conduct 12 months awareness raising activities on Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) in Idleb and the countryside of Idlib and Aleppo through 4 teams, to combat the lack of knowledge that children have of the dangers of landmines and explosive remnants of war present in their communities. To Ensure that no overlapping of activities with other actors in Idlib, NAS made use of 4WS info and Forward planning (Mine S/cluster) tool. NAS chose these communities in these areas because they are contaminated with unexploded war remnants and they were frontline areas.
NAS will mitigate the risk of ERW on the children, their caregivers, and humanitarian actors, and enhance the capacity of community and humanitarian actors on delivering awareness about the dangers and signs of explosives and mines. By conducting risk awareness sessions, disseminating RE material to children in and out schools in the most affected communities and conduct training for community focal points (key and most effective people) to help contribute towards the Mine Action Cluster' objectives.
Regarding the pandemic, COVID-19 protective measures will be taken into consideration during implementation for all activities. NAS has good experience and knowledge to modify and tailor all activities to deliver in safe and active manner during COVID 19, and to target those groups aggravated by COVID-19 by appropriate modality.
NAS will focus its response on:
A-Delivering tailored explosive ordnance risk awareness activities, including through remote and alternative.
under COVID 19 effects, through alternative/online/indirect modalities such as :
1- Conduct home visits to implement the sessions in beneficiaries home taking into consideration all safety steps
2- deliver direct and focus awareness sentences through speakers, in this way we can reach a lot of beneficiaries without any direct contact
3- conduct the activities remotely (create WhatsApp groups each group contain the same people who will attend our sessions and record videos from our animators contain all the required information, social media platforms, online meetings platforms, radio streaming)
4- distribute brochures and send videos about the selected topics through communication channels.
B-Transferring and reinforcing local skills by training community members in the delivery of explosive risk awareness and education within their own Communities', which increases the community’s response capacity and their self-sustainability, ultimately impacting communities, resilience. NAS will Train 80 ERW community focal points in Idlib and Aleppo.
C- Enhancing referral mechanism, seeking to support and expand the availability, access, and provision of specialized services for persons with disabilities, including survivors of explosive ordnance and their families.

4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies characterized by ongoing hostilities which have killed hundreds of thousands of people,Today, Today, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance – a 21 per cent increase compared to 2020 .Explosive ordnance contamination remains a major protection concern, severely affecting the lives and livelihoods of the population and further amplifying the social and economic crisis aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Despite a decrease in the number of explosive incidents recorded in 2020, localized hostilities and widespread explosive ordnance contamination continues to threaten lives and hamper access to basic services including medical facilities, as well as the delivery of humanitarian assistance and early recovery of thecommunity.It is with this in mind that NAS is planning to implement this 12-month project to reduce as much as possible the dangers and injuries that civilians regularly encounter with land mines and explosives, particularly among children. NAS will respond to the issues raised above through the following activities based on the Humanitarian Response Plan 2021 : A) Conduct tailored risk education and risk awareness for at-risk groups, including IDPs. b) Train humanitarian workers, community focal points, and other people of concern to conduct risk education to improve the integration of risk education throughout all other sectors responses in addition ,NAS will Enhance referral mechanism, seeking to support and expand the availability, access, and provision of specialized services for persons with disabilities, including survivors of explosive ordnance and their families.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: The political and security environment will remain conducive to the implementation process.Assumption: services are provided in safe and healthy environment.Assumption: Local authorities do not interfere with the project. Risk: Weakness of interaction of the beneﬁciaries of the activities. Mitigation: Preparing participatory activities. Risks: The targeted beneﬁciaries are not aware of the importance of the activities being provided. Mitigation: We will be announced in cooperation between mobile teams and local councils within the targeted villages. Risks: Airstrikes and bombed cars might happen in Idleb area. Mitigation: 1.security staff will do a daily, weekly and monthly assessment of the security situation to ensure the security of staff and beneﬁciaries. Activities may be delayed when situation is unsafe 2.Choose response centres, which they are far from military locations. 3. Coordination and continuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authorities to ensure the safety of staff and beneﬁciaries. Risks: Cultural background and norms could be sometimes as challenges against the achievement of the outputs. Mitigation: NAS team will conduct focus group discussion with all beneﬁciaries to explain that the activities of this project are not against the norms and culture of the target communities.Risk: Local authorities decide to ban the implementation of the project. Mitigation: ongoing IP coordination and sensitization with the local councils.Risk: COVID -19 outbreak:Mitigation: Adopting Plan-B to move to distance modality. protective measures (medical masks, sterilizers, gloves by the field team during distribution keeping social distance 1-2 m.
