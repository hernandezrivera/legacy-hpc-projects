Project Summary

IMC plan to continue provision of comprehensive PHC services through its 3 static clinics (Masakeh Barzeh is operational since 2008, Jaramana and Sanamayn since 2013) and 6 mobile facilities deployed in various areas of Damascus and Rural Damascus. Funding for the mobile clinics is secured until June 2021.
Furthermore, IMC is supporting public PHC facilities in Hama governorate, through capacity building and technical support, provision of supplies (including COVID 19 supplies) and medical equipment aimed at enhancing service provision and quality of healthcare. Two mobile facilities are also planned to be supported in the most underserved areas. Specific services vary based on the facility; IMC provides preventive and curative healthcare for communicable and non-communicable diseases, specifically: internal medicine, pediatrics, gynecology, ENT, ophthalmology, provision of medications, basic lab services, health education and malnutrition screening. Some of the facilities also provide case management services for Mental Health (in addition to Protection- GBV and Child protection), and related advanced PSS. Through private service providers, IMC support referrals to advanced care (consultations, surgeries, specialized diagnostic services) and provides mobility/disability NFIs to targeted vulnerable categories of the population. On average among all facilities, female represent 60 to 70% of the total beneficiaries served; children below 5 years of age represent 15% and beneficiaries below 18 years of age about 30%. Consultations to people with disability amount to about 2% of total consultations. Approximately 65% of the consultations are related to NCDs, highlighting the increased needs for uninterrupted treatment and support for medications.
Areas of intervention are selected based on assessed needs and gaps. IMC clinics are open to all population and provide services and medications free of charge. Specific services require selection of the beneficiaries. Selection criteria for the referrals include: socio-economic vulnerability; necessity and duration of the suggested treatment; concomitant diseases and age; feasibility and evidence base of the treatment plan; prognosis. For provision of NFIs, criteria vary based on the item to be provided, but all require IMC doctor to examine the patient.
IMC is part of the health cluster as well as the MHPSS sub-group and shares regularly 4Ws regarding implemented activities both to Health and Nutrition clusters as well as IMCI monthly reports. IMC also coordinates with the MoH regarding EWARS, aimed at strengthening the national surveillance system and early detect epidemic threats. The proposed response is in line with the sector priorities for 2021, overall focusing on sustaining and expanding quality healthcare services and addressing barriers related to cost of care, through mobile facilities, strengthening of the public system, and collaboration with private service providers.

4 - Needs

As detailed in the HNO 2021, the main gaps continue to be related to damages to the facilities, severe shortages of staff, medical supplies, equipment and pharmaceuticals. The COVID 19 pandemic has not only taken a heavy toll on the health workers, but has also further strained an already weak system as it has required to redirect available resources. Although the number of confirmed cases remain relatively low, it is clear that this is linked to the limited testing capacities and does not reflect the real extent of the pandemic. The economic crisis and increased food insecurity among the majority of the population means that access to health services and medication is even more challenging than before. Additional constraints related to distance, cost of transportation and lack of documentation represent further barriers to access basic healthcare services. EWARS reports indicate that influenza-like illness, acute diarrhea, cutaneous leishmaniosis and suspected hepatitis are the most common morbidities; NCDs are estimated to account for 45% of the mortality. Among the most affected categories of the population are women of reproductive age, who face specific challenges in accessing essential sexual and reproductive health services and account for 3.3 million out of the 12.4 million estimated to be in need. Health education, prevention and treatment services offered at PHC level are essential to ensure continuity of care and to detect infectious diseases including COVID 19. PHC health facilities also constitute a crucial entry point for advanced/secondary health care, reducing the risk of deterioration of the health conditions and ultimately contributing in decreasing morbidity, mortality and permanent disabilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

IMC will continue providing PHC preventive and curative services at its existing static and mobile clinics, including nutrition and COVID 19 related screenings and provision of essential medications. IMC will also continue support advanced health care services through private service providers and provision of disability/mobility NFIs, including hearing aids. MH/GBV Case management will continue to be available in Barzeh and Jaramana, with specialized MH support through psychologists and psychiatrists, and through mobile teams. Capacity building and supportive supervision activities will continue be provided to both IMC and MOH health workers, for a variety of topics selected based on capacity assessments. These might include others: chronic diseases in children, mhGAP, IPC, CMR, Rational Use of Drugs, medical triage and COVID 19 related trainings. IMC will also continue supporting the public system at PHC level, through provision of supplies and equipment based on observed gaps. Through its health and MEAL staff, IMC will ensure close monitoring of its facilities and public centers supported, including among others: regular checks of diagnosis and related treatments, monthly monitoring of medication and supplies consumption, close supervision to ensure adherence to technical standards. Patient satisfaction and PDM surveys are also conducted regularly to inform programming adaption and identify any areas for improvement and emerging needs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1. Low quality services expose beneficiaries to harm and deterioration of their health conditions Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1) Regular supportive supervision and monitoring on quality of health care provided at IMC and MOH facilities; 2) capacity building activities; 3) Effective feedback and complaints mechanisms in place. Risk 2: Violence against health care. Likelihood: Low/Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1) close collaboration and information sharing with UNDSS and other humanitarian partners security focal points; 2) temporary suspension of services in case of increased likelihood of security related incidents. Risk 3: inequitable access to health care, including for PWDs. Likelihood: low. Impact: Medium/High Mitigation measures: 1) facilities open to everyone and services provided free of charge; 2) removal of physical barriers for PWDs to access healthcare; 3) deployment of mobile teams to reach population unable to travel to static health facilities; 4) gender balanced staffing operating in static and mobile clinics.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK 1: Delayed/lack of approvals for dispatch of commodities and monitoring visits. MITIGATION 1: Regular follow up with relevant authorities; remote management system in place; buffer stock maintained at clinics. RISK 2: Deterioration of the security situation, requiring suspension of services, disrupting dispatch/staff movements, or causing loss/damage of equipment and supplies. MITIGATION 2: adequate security/surveillance at warehouses, facilities, offices; minimize value/quantity of items while ensuring balance with programmatic needs; monitoring and info sharing with UNDSS.RISK 3: Unavailability of qualified medical staff. MITIGATION 3: Maintain a roster of qualified staff; offer attractive benefits package; invest in learning and development activities.RISK 4: Unavailability of quality medical supplies and pharmaceuticals in the local market. MITIGATION 4: ongoing pre-qualification process to expand suppliers; maintain long term agreements; regular market surveys and update of vendors’ catalogue; info sharing with other humanitarian partners.RISK 5: Sanctions delaying bank transfers. MITIGATION 5: Maintain bank accounts with at least two banks; advocate on waiving of international sanctions; info sharing with other INGOs to identify solutions. RISK 6: COVID 19 (lockdowns and infection among health workers) causing services to be suspended. MITIGATION 6: request waiver to allow movement of staff and mobile facilities; adherence to preventive and isolation measures as needed.
