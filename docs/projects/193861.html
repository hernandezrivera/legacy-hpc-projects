Project Summary

This project will ensure continuity of health and nutrition services to Ethiopian refugees and host population of Village 8 and its transit refugee camp, in cooperation with the Ministry of Health (MoH), COR and other partners.
The project aims to support directly the refugees hosted in Village 8 transit site through the provision of health and nutrition services. The number of refugees in Village 8 may vary during the course of the year, but are estimated to 3,137 according to UNHCR figures published the 8th of December 2021.
The health services supported will also be available to the host community of Village 8, estimated to 9,000 Sudanese.
The project also entails the distribution of hot meals to new arrival refugees and people with specific needs in Village 8 transit site. The number of beneficiaries of this assistance is expected to be 164.
PUI will maintain an essential presence in the Welcoming Center in Village 8, providing triaging for refugees, and basic consultation by two clinical officers, this is also were PSS support is provided by PSS providers. If the patient requires further treatment or observation by a doctor or midwife, they are referred to the PHCC, a few hundred meters away from the Welcoming Center. Children under the age of five years will also be screened for malnutrition and sent to the PHC if they need treatment and follow up. Consultation of patients who do not need observation or further investigation and treatment at the health facility will be treated by the clinical officers at the reception center.
PUI intervention under this project is well in line with the UNHCR 2022 result framework described as Persons of Concern (PoCs) have access to relevant humanitarian services and their basic needs met through access to multi-sector assistance services. As such, the activities by output are as follow.
PUI will ensure two main outputs, that 1. Primary healthcare services provided and 2. Nutrition services provided. Under output 1, PUI will:
• Ensure provision of primary health care package and psychosocial care,
• Support preventive health services
• Provide capacity building and financial support for the MOH health staff
• Strengthen SRH services including ANC/PNC, FP, CMR and delivery
• Strengthen and maintain prompt and effective referral system to secondary/tertiary health care, including follow up of the patients
• Strengthen emergency preparedness and response capacity
And under Output 2, PUI will ensure:
• Screening and referral of malnourished children for treatment
• Community awareness raising on nutrition
• Support malnutrition treatment services
• Provision of hot meal for new arrivals and people with specific needs at reception center in Village 8
PUI is closely working with different stakeholders, both governmental and non-governmental in achieving its goals to provide aid to populations affected by natural disasters, economic crises and conflicts. As such, the organization is active member of coordination platforms for humanitarian responses to people of concern, including the health, nutrition, logistics clusters and the PSEA network and SRH working group.

1 - Needs

Needs are envisaged from the Health and Nutrition Sector discussions and humanitarian needs overview:Health:The protracted humanitarian crisis in Sudan continues to impact the already fragile health system, reducing the capacity to provide basic health services and respond to multiple emergencies affecting the country. The annual cycles of floods, disease outbreaks, civil unrest, localised conflicts, and ongoing economic crisis affect the accessibility to health services. According to the 2022 MSNA, 62 per cent of the population had to pay in full for the medical consultation, 63 per cent paid in full for laboratory diagnostics, and 94 per cent paid for medicines. The availability of essential medicines was 30 per cent in the private sector and 31 per cent in the public sector. Higher vulnerabilities such as children, women and elderly are most affected; in addition gender issues play a role in increasing the needs for females. Moreover, people with disability will have higher needs compared to the rest of the population. Refugees show higher vulnerability, when compared to host community, due to different reasons, such as status, less opportunities in livelihoods and different context. Deteriorating living conditions affect people’s health, who become susceptible to epidemics and diseases, which could otherwise be avoided. The lack of knowledge around good hygienic, nutritional and sanitary practices contributes to worsening the already poor health conditions of the most vulnerable individuals. Concerns over maternal health issues remain high given limited access to essential obstetric and maternal care services. Access to lifesaving emergency obstetric and neonatal care (EmONC) remains challenging as the coverage is 32 per cent, the coverage with clinical management of rape (CMR) services coverage remained low, with less than 20 per cent of facilities having them.The public sector struggles to provide a complete basic healthcare package, with an increased burden on the health system due to influx of refugees, migrants and asylum seekers. NUTAcute and chronic malnutrition have been problems of public health significance in Sudan for the past several decades. This is the case, if not worse, for displaced people, whether IDPs or refugees. The drivers of malnutrition are multi-sectoral that include poverty, poor WASH conditions leading to epidemics, limited access to health services, illiteracy and high food prices contributing to inadequate food intake and dietary diversity at household level. Inappropriate feeding practices and cultural norms that affect children’s growth and development from an early age. humanitarian crisis tend to increase the nutrition related protections risks especially among young children that might engage in child labour, early child marriage that increase risks of being malnourished. Despite the ongoing scaling up treatment and preventive nutrition responses the prevalence of risk factors (Health, WASH, Food security, feeding practices) show no signs of significant improvements. For example, over one third of acutely malnourished children live in extreme and catastrophic severity scale for WASH and heath sector expositing them to heighted morbidity and mortality risks. Thus, the overall nutrition sector people in need (PIN) remain at elevated levels of 3.9M as it was in 2022, under-five children accounting for over 76% and 23% pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in need of life-saving treatment and preventive nutrition responses. With respect to refugees, over 67,000 of which 66% are under-five and 34% are pregnant and lactating women are in urgent need of life saving nutrition interventions. Nutrition needs have significant impact on physical, mental and wellbeing of children, pregnant and lactating women, and the community in general.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Output 1: Primary healthcare services provided Activity 1: Ensuring provision of primary health care package and psychosocial care Both the refugee and the host population will be provided with primary health care packages and psychosocial support under this project. The services provided at the PHCC include OPD consultation, routine immunization, screening and treatment of children for malnutrition, SRH services (including 24/7 maternity services, ANC, PNC and FP), and health awareness. PUI will also continue to support the provision of PSS at the Welcome Center. Activity 2: Support preventive health services To prevent potential outbreaks of preventable diseases, PUI will support the health facility to effectively undertake the routine immunizations according to the Sudan MoH immunization protocol. Activity 3: Provide capacity building and financial support for the MOH health staffCapacity building for the health care providers will be ensured through formal trainings and on-job trainings and supervision of the health workers. In collaboration with the State ministry of health, capacity building training will be organized for the staff different topics.Activity 4: Strengthen SRH services including ANC/PNC, FP, CMR and deliveryApart from capacity building training for the staff, there should be adequate support to provide quality and safe SRH services. SRH team will be trained on CMR (clinical management of rape) to enable them provide the necessary services to the survivors of the rape without any delay. Activity 5: Strengthen and maintain prompt and effective referral system to secondary/tertiary health care, including follow up of the patients Referrals will be carried out based on defined referral criteria and SOP: obstetric emergencies, SAM children with medical complications, patient with severe complicated malaria and other life threatening medical and surgical emergencies will be covered by this referral service.Activity 6: Strengthen emergency preparedness and response capacitySupport the preparedness plan of the state Output 2: Nutrition services providedActivity 1: Screening and referral of malnourished children for treatment PUI will use the community volunteers’ network, to screen children for malnutrition and refer them for appropriate treatment on time. This will be applied to both refugees and host populationActivity 2: Community awareness raising on nutritionThrough mother care groups, mass awareness raising activities and during home visits by the community volunteers. Activity 3: Support malnutrition treatment services PUI will work closely with the MoH to provide technical support such as training of staff on CMAM protocols and provide supportive supervision jointly with the MoH technical person in addition to the daily support and follow up by the dedicated PUI health facility supervisor. Supplies will be transported to the point of use and proper storage and stock management will also be ensured.Activity 4: Provide hot meal for new arrivals and people with specific needs at reception center in Village 8Refugees who arrive at the reception center after walking for many days need food, PUI in collaboration with the UNHCR will provide them with hot meals to new arrivals for the first 72 hours. This will support the refugees to recover from the hunger and prevent further deterioration into malnutrition for children and PLW who are at high risk. The hot meal will also be distributed to people with specific needs. This activity will be carried out following UNHCR specific SOP.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

PUI activities revolve around an integrated approach, focusing on Protection as a cross-cutting theme. As per guidelines of the Global Protection Cluster, PUI will incorporate protection principle and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity in all activities.The following elements will be taken into account:1. Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks2. Meaningful Access: arrange for people’s access to assistance and services -in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 3. Accountability: set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints4. Participation and empowerment: support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to claim their rightsTo prevent causing harm and ensure the safety of the beneficiaries, PUI will conduct sessions on safety measures to adopt during the repairs and after the work. These will also be developed with the aim to reinforce the beneficiaries’ autonomy and capacity to protect themselves in case of endangerment. PUI will ensure creating a safe and dignified environment. PUI aims at identifying the most vulnerable individuals or groups when leading assessments and surveys, to be able to appropriately analyze their needs and feedbacks, and PUI will develop strong accountability mechanisms as well. PUI will develop efficient accountability mechanisms, with the local authorities and community leaders. PUI will apply the “Do No Harm” principle across all its activities. PUI will also ensure that the supported health facilities are safely accessible, particularly for the vulnerable groups. An entrance accessible for people with disability, and a safe waiting area will be dedicated to women. Across all its intervention at health care facility level, the health and nutrition staff will able to identify unattended cases in need of PSS and base protection assistance such as child protection related issues, GBV cases, and severely traumatic experiences. PUI will activate the identified and relevant referral system when necessary.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

During assessments and project design and implementation, local stakeholders and the community as well as beneficiaries are consulted to ensure maximum understanding of their needs. Activities are designed according to their needs and our minimum quality standards. These are monitored, commented on and redirected as necessary, according to feedback from the field throughout the course of work.PUI will ensure that the field staff share with the community PUI’s commitment to accountability to the affected population on issues such as staff recruitment and training, code of conduct, information sharing and transparency. Partners will be included in the monitoring and evaluation mechanisms. The beneficiary population will also be informed of the project implementation modalities and the selection procedure, if any, as mentioned above.PUI will set up a complaint and feedback mechanism adapted to the local context to collect the views and complaints of a wide range of stakeholders (affected populations, communities, local civil society actors, international humanitarian actors, local authorities or national governments and donors) and take appropriate action.A complaint response mechanism (CRM) will be designed in line with the protection principle of Do No Harm, in consultation with partners and affected communities, taking also into account available internal resources (financial and human) and with the objective of gradual scaling up. PUI thus aims to guarantee the availability of appropriate Feedback/Complaints channels, while ensuring effective complaints handling, in order to avoid creating frustration or mistrust with regard to the proposed mechanisms.Depending on the context, this mechanism consist of:• Community Mobilizers, who would be an important messengers conveying feedbacks and complaints from the population, themselves being part of the community,• Complaint boxes, especially in health facilities,• Regular consultations with community focal points / community committees,• Focus group discussions led by PUI teams. Whatever the mechanism(s) chosen, PUI will:• Inform the population of the existence and functioning of the complaint and feedback mechanism. This information will be disseminated through several channels (i.e. through community relays, during surveys etc.) in order to ensure that the targeted community can make use of it as soon as the first activities are launched.• Inform the population in the intervention area of the anti-fraud, anti-corruption and PSEA principles
