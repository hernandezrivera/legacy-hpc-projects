Project Summary

Action Against Hunger will provide emergency WASH support to the most vulnerable host and displaced communities affected by undernutrition, WASH related disease outbreaks and/or sudden-onset disasters. Action against Hunger will deliver emergency temporary services to address life-saving needs and reduce WASH-related mortality/morbidity. The next level of response will involve delivery of sustainable WASH solutions to progressively improve the WASH situation and health status of the targeted communities. In so doing, Action Against Hunger will contribute to health, nutrition and education outcomes by ensuring access to safe water supply, adequate sanitation and hygiene promotion in health facilities of all types (cholera, nutrition, primary health care, etc.) and schools by employing an integrated approach. To reduce the risk of violence against women, children and men when accessing WASH facilities, Action Against Hunger will apply a strong protection lens in construction of WASH infrastructure and delivery of services. The capacity of the beneficiary communities will be strengthened through regular trainings of WASH governance groups and recruitment of WASH staff from the beneficiary communities.

Needs

Access to sufficient amounts of safe water is a major challenge across the Somalia, with 66 percent of the existing water points in the targeted project areas in need of rehabilitation. In Bakool, for instance, a severity ranking study conducted by REACH in two of the five districts in the region (El Barde and Huddur) show critical levels of WASH services among IDPs, which indicates need for urgent and sustained level of humanitarian support. Similarly, the Somalia WASH cluster ranks the region as extremely vulnerable in terms of WASH services.In the targeted areas, women and children bear the burden of water collection and have to walk long distances to the remaining functional water points where they queue for several hours compromising childcare practices. Assessments conducted by Action against Hunger in 2018 indicated that 55% of the population spent more than one hour queuing at water points. Majority of the respondents (78%) in the surveys felt that their main water source for drinking water was unsafe while only 9% treated their drinking water at household level. Latrine coverage stands at 50 percent; with 81 percent of the shared latrines being unclean thereby reducing use by women and girls. In addition, 40 percent of the caregivers of children under 5 reported disposing children faeces in the open. This coupled with poor handwashing practices has contributed to the frequent diarrheal disease outbreaks in the targeted areas (cholera in 2017 in Bakool and Banadir and AWD in 2018). To increase utilization of WASH facilities by women and girls at all times, a stronger protection lens will be applied to ensure communal latrines and bathing facilities in IDP settlements and communal areas are gender separated and provided with lockable doors. Further, the distance to water point will be reduced to ensure the vulnerable communities access water within 30 minutes.

Activities

Emergency and sustainable WASH services will be delivered, the former during acute emergency to address life-saving needs aimed at reducing WASH-related mortality/morbidity, particularly among children. This will include emergency water trucking through vouchers for newly displaced households, provision of household water treatment and/or point of use water treatment, bucket chlorination, safe water storage at HH level, closely followed by rehabilitation of water sources, construction of emergency latrines with handwashing points and hygiene promotion. Hygiene promotion will be strengthened at Health/Nutrition facility and schools, HH and community level through a network of community volunteers. The targeting of beneficiaries will be based on nutritional vulnerability where groups with the highest undernutrition rates, those most vulnerable to undernutrition (children under 5, pregnant and lactating women) and populations facing shocks related to nutrition security, AWD/Cholera and drought.To increase the ownership of outputs, Community health workers (CHWs) will be recruited from the targeted villages, trained by ACF and MoH and provided with materials to conduct health education and hygiene promotion activities. In addition, gender balanced water resource management committees will be established for each targeted water point and trained. Follow up training will be conducted and the governance teams supported to establish VSLAs as a kitty for village level development initiatives including water point breakdown maintenance. Action Against Hunger will also improve access to water sustainably through construction of new boreholes, equipped with elevated water tanks and distribution systems to communal water kiosks. The use of solar powered water pumping systems will be promoted for both rehabilitated and new water points. Sustainable sanitation will be delivered through the CLTS approach. Response quality will be ensured through ACF national & global expertise

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed intervention is aligned to the WASH, Food Security and livelihoods and Health and Nutrition cluster HRP priorities to focus on basic life-saving activities and community resilience-building activities in all high GAM/SAM and IDP areas. The regions where the interventions will be implemented are characterized by enormous environmental challenges related to deforestation, land degradation, increasing aridity, overgrazing and water scarcity. By increasing access to water through rehabilitation of essential water sources and construction of new ones, the project reduces abnormal migration of human and livestock to other regions where they inflict significant environmental damages through over grazing and exerting added pressure on scarce water sources. In the absence of latrines, in the new areas of displacement, open defecation would increase posing serious risks to the environmental wellbeing. Portions of land are usually left open to be used by the displaced population for human waste disposal. This poses serious health risks due to the spread of faecal matter, especially during the rainy season when there is seepage into water wells used for drinking water. This has been partly responsible for the periodic outbreaks of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) in IDPs and villages in Somalia. This proposed project is foreseen to impose minimal risks to the surrounding environment. With the planned rehabilitation of key water infrastructure and establishment of effective community based water sources management mechanism and provision of more latrines both at household and institutional level, the project reduces negative pressure on the environment and significantly contributes the health and wellbeing of the habitat. Activities aimed at increasing access to safe water for the vulnerable population will focus on rehabilitation of existing wells, which are not expected to pose any additional environmental risk. Moreover, pprovision of latrines with hand washing facilities will ease the level of public health risk posed by providing safe places and consequently check the spread of human waste. The location of latrines will also be far from the water wells to check the risks of contamination. Public health will be ensured through proper disposal and management of medical equipment, supplies and waste, including the use of incinerators. A component of the hygiene training will also include lessons on environmental awareness with the objective of providing skills to the beneficiaries on better waste management, which doesn’t endanger their immediate environment. The project will jointly with line ministry representatives support the establishment and capacity building of facility management committees (Water Resource Management Committees, Community Health Committees, Natural Resource Management Committees) to ensure resilient community systems are in place. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main target population for the project are internally displaced people and the surrounding host communities. Displaced populations, youth, women and the elderly, most notable those in camps have poor access to basic services such as water, sanitation and hygiene services. This exposes them, especially women and young girls to sex and Gender based violence. Moreover, access to basic services and opportunities is further compounded by the pervasive marginalization and exclusion of IDPs and certain sections of the host communities mostly attributed to clan dynamics. IDP and host community families who are members of weaker clans or minorities face extra vulnerabilities and protection threats including economic exploitation, gender-based violence, trafficking and discrimination. To mitigate against the risks identified, ACF will ensure protection sensitive data collection and analysis that seeks to identify the specific protection challenges of the target population at project implementation stage. Targeting of project locations and beneficiaries will be guided strictly by need with specific attention to households that may be marginalized due to family and clan factors. Engagement at community level will emphasize representation of all groups/clans in the target locations. ACF staff are already trained and sensitizes on PSEA and have signed a code of conduct that commits them to strictly adhere to humanitarian principles when providing services. There also exists a clear reporting framework for PSEA related issues. This is expected to curtail any cases of humanitarian staff exploiting the beneficiaries. In addition, community leaders will be sensitized on the importance of including male and females in all WASH interventions; targeted beneficiaries will be registered, profiled and data disaggregated by gender and age, which will be used for assessing specific needs of women and other disadvantaged groups.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Main risks include: lack of WASH facilities that are gender sensitive to the needs of both men and women, distance to facilities, for example water points, that over burden women and expose them to SGBV, lack of involvement in decision making regarding the delivery of services, especially for men and women from weaker sections of the community. In order to address the identified challenges, wash NFIs and water vouchers will be given directly to women which will promote a greater role for women in decision-making within targeted households. Men and women will be consulted on the most appropriate collection points for water trucking and rehabilitation of essential water points based on needs and priorities, which will reduce the amount of time women spend collecting water. This will allow enough time for women to engage in other roles and will promote health seeking behavior by women and U5s, positive nutrition practices as well as reduced women and girls’ exposure to related SGBV risks. Moreover, construction of latrines will ensure that the beneficiaries, especially women and girls, have access to safe places to protect their dignity and reduce their exposure to risk. ACF will also distribute hygiene kits to the most vulnerable people, prioritizing women and girls. ACF will maintain close contact with the beneficiaries through the established governance structures at community level and seek feedback from beneficiaries through PDMs, HH level visits and FGDs with women, men boys and girls. The beneficiary communities will be informed and involved in project activities through the established committees and joint activity monitoring conducted with the committees and local authorities. ACF will make deliberate efforts to listen to and respond to communities’ concerns.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

ACF works in areas where there is active conflict between state and non-state armed actors or between clans. The key risks include Service delivery being appropriated by different sides to the conflict against the others, aid services being diverted to the most powerful groups, retaliatory attacks or victimization of those who seek services by hostile groups, attack on beneficiary on their way to service delivery points and attacks on humanitarian staff among. ACF applies conflict sensitivity approaches in its project implementation and this will be mainstreamed in the project. All volunteers engaged in this action will be sensitize on the implication of this engagement in line with power dynamics at the household and community level. Deliberate action will be taken to discuss also the implication of this action on individual beneficiaries at the community level and if they are concerns, proper mitigation measures will be instituted. ACF local teams are well trained and experienced in handling sensitive community issues, especially if related to power dynamics and protection of all involved in the successful completion of this project. Additionally, beneficiary information will be protected and treated as confidential with consent being sort if any information such as pictures are to be shared. ACF will also be strictly guided by humanitarian principles, key among these principals being neutrality when providing services. Strict security protocols will be employed to ensure staff are not put in harm’s way, for example by ensuring that staff are back from the field stations early enough. Mobile outreach services will also target the remote but accessible areas, regardless of the community dynamics.
