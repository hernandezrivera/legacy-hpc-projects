Project Summary

Lebanon is currently facing economic, political, monetary, and social instability that puts that educational sector directly in danger. Due to the economic crisis and Covid-19 shutdown, many vulnerable children did not meet the necessary educational needs. As such, LOST will address the unmet educational needs of the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant school-aged children who are in enrolled in schools. In order to offer quality education, an inclusive and adapted catch-up learning support programs addressing learning losses, based on needs of 1000 vulnerable children. This program will target children enrolled in public schools and are at risk of dropping out due to their poor academic performance and achievement results. It will focus on supporting these children in all subjects that they need help with especially the major ones of the Lebanese curriculum (Arabic, Foreign language, Mathematics and Science). This will be done through 4 cycles, 250 students per cycle over 2.5 months providing 200 educational remedial sessions per student.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Overall Goal: Improve the education reality in Baalback-Hermel and Bekaa Governorates. Outcome 1: Support vulnerable students to receive an all inclusive quality education Output 1.1: Offer 1000 vulnerable households whose children are in public education with Remedial support Activity 1.1.1: Provide Remedial support targeting 1000 vulnerable Lebanese and migrant students to enable retention in education The activity will focus on targeting students in the Baalbeck Hermel and Bekaa Governorates in grades 7 through 12 into a remedial program to cover the gap created by the public school continous strike and ensure that these students do not lose an educational year. The training will focus on making up missing input and will be designed to plug gaps created by the strikes.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

LOST will ensure that all staff involved in this program are aware of the special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and are implementing them throughout the project. This is done through explaining the internal LOST PSEA policy and holding all staff accountable towards it. The HR will make sure the all staff has signed it as a standard procedure and will monitor its application at all times. In addition, hotline numbers for complaints will be distributed to the beneficiaries and will be posted at all LOST centers. All staff will attend training sessions on the PSEA policy and will cascade the information to the beneficiaries in order for them to know and understand their rights. Referral mechanism will be also taught to the staff for them to refer any case to the suitable agency/program including case management.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

LOST holds the commitment towards Accountability for Affected Population very seriously. LOST pledges to deliver aid to vulnerable/marginalized individuals while upholding human rights and dignity. As part of AAP, LOST will inform, involve and engage with the affected population as to make them part of the decision-making process that will affect them. This will be through setting up a complaint and feedback mechanism such as pre-surveys and focus-group discussions to improve quality of program design and delivery as to strengthen current accountability mechanisms. LOST acknowledges that there is a power differential in the course of interaction between the staff and affected populations and will address that by empowering all members of the affected population regardless of their sex, age, or disability to prevent power abuse such as sexual exploitation and abuse. LOST is committed to enforcing and integrating these AAP approaches to achieve consistent application of accountability.
