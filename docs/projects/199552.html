Project Summary

Chad faces a combination of rapid-onset and protracted humanitarian crises that have been exacerbated by the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic. Some 6.1 million people, including 3 million children,2 will require humanitarian assistance in 2023. Armed conflict and intercommunal violence have led to increased population displacements, primarily of women and children, both within the country and from neighboring countries. Chad remains highly vulnerable to epidemics and climatic events, including floods and droughts. It also faces a protracted food and nutritional crisis. Access to essential services remains limited.
UNICEF will provide a timely, coordinated, lifesaving multisectoral humanitarian response in provinces facing recurrent population displacement and other crises.
This project aims at ensuring adequate response for 125,600 floods affected and internally displaced persons (15,933 household NFI kit and 5,000 household through HCT) . The project will be implemented in the most affected zones across the country.
The necessary budget to carry out the activities is $5,273,100 Without sufficient and timely funding, UNICEF will not be unable to support the national response to the country’s continuing crises.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

The humanitarian situation in Chad can be described as a prolonged multidimensional crisis caused by continued population displacements due to conflict, natural disasters, persistent food insecurity, high malnutrition, economic crisis and political instability. Chad ranks 190 out of 191 countries on the Human Development Index, and an estimated 6.4 million Chadians live in poverty. In 2022, there has been an increase in people fleeing conflict in the Lake Chad Basin and seeking refuge in neighboring countries. Nearly 592,769 refugees reside in Chad and 381,289 Chadians are internally displaced. Armed conflict in the Lake Chad Basin is also limiting humanitarian actors’ ability to reach affected populations. The nutritional situation remains alarming. The prevalence of global acute malnutrition in children under five is 10.9 per cent, including 2 per cent who suffer from severe wasting. It is estimated that 1.9 million children aged 6-59 months who are wasted will require treatment. The government declared a state of food, nutrition and floods emergency in 2022.The project aims to assist 125,600 people through the distribution of non-food items (NFI), and cash transfers. The cash transfer approach will help empower beneficiaries to take responsibility for their own needs by giving them the opportunity to make choices for themselves. The participation of the beneficiary populations in this project remains a priority for UNICEF, which will place particular emphasis on accountability to the beneficiary populations. This approach will be reflected in the continuous monitoring of the implementation of the accountability strategy, in particular through the organization of beneficiary satisfaction surveys and the strengthening of communication mechanisms with the populations of the targeted areas. To reduce vulnerabilities and tension factors, implementing partners will work in synergy with administrative, political and religious/traditional leaders, and local women's/youth associations to strengthen social cohesion and promote peace. These actors will be involved in all phases of implementation as key stakeholders. Stakeholder feedback will be taken into account throughout the implementation process.People with disabilities, especially women and girls, will be given priority in the selection of beneficiaries. Community mechanisms are opportunities to identify children with disabilities and refer them for care. Sensitization of parents and leaders on the inclusive participation of children without any other consideration will be promoted to ensure that the principles of non-discrimination, the best interests of the child and the right to participation of children prevail.The project is in line with the principles of "Do no harm" and conflict sensitivity. UNICEF, as a humanitarian organization, acts and requires its partners to act in accordance with the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality and neutrality. The strict application of these principles already guarantees compliance with the principle of "Do no harm" in the sense of never harming the dignity of people or their physical or moral integrity.Cash transfers will be made through a cell phone operator. The NGO will identify the most vulnerable households that have agreed to receive the transfers. The preliminary implementation phase of the project will consist of consulting vulnerable populations to identify their expectations, through a U-Report survey and a rapid diagnostic to assess the protection risks related to the distribution of cash transfers and take appropriate mitigation measures. Following the distributions, a second consultation via U-Report will be organized to assess the degree of satisfaction of the beneficiaries.Project objective:vulnerable households receive quality assistance in non-food items and HCTIndicators:1.
