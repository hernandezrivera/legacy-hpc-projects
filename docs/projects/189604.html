Project Summary

The project focuses on provision of Life-saving intervention and disaster mitigation through health services that includes integrated outreaches covering Child health and MISP provisions. The target locations are Ibba and Maridi Counties reaching Nabanga, Mazengi, Yosia, Madebe, Bamaani in Ibba; and Madebe and DukuduOlo in Maridi, where displaced persons are hosted. Implementation shall take an integrated approach to address WASH, Nutrition, and Protection needs through other Amref projects and working with other cluster partners. Project implementation team shall include local personnel and national staff that will support technical delivery of the assistance. Regular Community meetings and awareness will be held to ensure beneficiaries are informed of the assistance and their role. Volunteers will be selected among beneficiaries to support guide implementation and report any community concerns.

1 - Needs

Ibba has been experiencing repeated incursions by cattle nomads from Central Africa and Lakes states, which led to tension in communities causing displacements in Tanamuko (since 2021 to date) and in Nabanga Payams in 2022.Total people affected in Ibba is projected to be 5,090 by Ibba SSRRC (2,840 in Tanamuko area and 2,250 in Nabanga payam). The people of Tanamuko are being hosted in Mangezi and Yosia area, while the people of Nabanga are displaced towards Bamaani, Madebe. And Ibba. The Umbororo people are about 2500 who are also accessing services from the same health facilities in the locations of Nabanga, Bamaani, Madebe, and Tanamuko. Amref through HPF has been supporting 11 Health facilities in Ibba county, and 23 in Maridi County. With challenge of reduction in HPF facility support, there will 59% reduction in facilities to be supported in 2022-2023 with only 6 facilities in Ibba and 8 in Maridi county. This will significantly affect support to these displaced communities for which can be supplemented by emergency response activities at such critical time. In Maridi County, the skirmishes in Landili Payam (RasOlo) and Ngamunde Payam since 2019-2021 has caused displacement to areas of Mambe and Maridi payams. These have never been responded to as most population got assimilated in host communities, and 96 HHs displaced to Mambe with no assistance (SSRRC Maridi 2022). With increased challenge of access to health care, especially in RasOlo area, support has only been possible during UNMISS operations to the area and when AMREF convoy is cleared to deliver basic items. This emergency fund is critically needed to facilitate basic outreaches to support women and children affected in that hard to reach payam. The most common problem among the displaced persons in Ibba and their Umburoro counterpart is Malaria, Soft-tissue injuries, and UTIs. Miscarriages/abortions are most common among women in of the pastoral communities. The pressure on health care system is high. The need for integrated outreaches, basic WASH supplies, and MISP supplies will improve the situation and reduce risks of worse health problems. On the other hand, people from displaced communities of Maridi were treated on Malaria, ARI, and Diarrhea. Reaching RasOlo area with COVID-19 and EVD messages and vaccines had been an immense challenge. Maternal referrals are serious night mares in the far remote area near the border to Yei county and DRC. The emergency fund is very critical in bring the much needed hope to the mothers, children and health care workers in this challenging location.In 2021, GBV reported cases at facilities were 122 (6Children and 116 in above 5yo) in Ibba and 4 cases in Maridi (among adults); DPT3 achievement in Ibba was 2104, and 5592 children in Maridi; Measles vaccination coverage remains low with 10 Measles cases reported in Ibba, and 1 in Maridi in 2021. (DHSIS2 2022). Suspected cases of meningitis were reported in Jan 2022, and sample under investigation, and 15 cases and 6 deaths have been reported so far. (WHO, Epidemiological Bulletin Week 07, 2022).In Feb 2022, bush fire caused displacement and affected 319 HH were affected with 1914 people in Mambe and Maridi Payams (SRRC Maridi). Though the emergency had minimal health priorities the displacement puts women, and children (girls and boys) at risk of SGBV, malaria, and other problems

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback shall be collected, compiled and analyzed for effective response to beneficiaries. The feedback will be useful for taking disciplinary measures; adjusting schedules of activities; effective representations of implementation workers (gender, vulnerability, age, etc. representations) as may be appropriate

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The care takers are critical in providing feedback from those with speech/hearing impairment, while those with physical impairments can communicate their feedback in meetings, interview sessions or written. Communications shall be in local language and translated for record. Privacy shall be provided for sensitive communications as appropriate and confidentiality guaranteed. Each project location shall have a PSEA/Safeguarding focal persons, who links up with women leaders, youth groups and disable persons.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Amref Health Africa has whistle blower contacts and links that will be shared and displayed at work places. Safeguarding posters will also be provided in addition to PSEA briefing and whistle blowing contact sharing during start-up meeting and other subsequent review meetings.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project start-up meeting will involve beneficiaries (women and young people) with focus to debrief them on key activities and their role in the project. The youth leaders, women leaders from the implementation areas will have summary copy of the project plan, activities and budget. Quarterly project review meetings will be held to share project progress. Contacts of project focal person and County authority will be shared to guarantee transparency and ensure community confidence in the process and services. The project shall have a focal Project Manager, Health Officer (technical lead) and Clinical, Midwifery, and vaccination staff at the selected sites (fixed and outreach) to support

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Women, girls and boys may be vulnerable due to traditional practices of early marriages in the area of the humanitarian assistance. Long distance to service centers in creases the vulnerability further. Outreach is a critical component of this assistance to take services closer to people. This will also enable disabled persons reach with ease. Sensitization on early child abuse, abuse of women, and Safeguarding policies will be held throughout the implementation period. Referral services exists but are only at central levels of the counties. This makes the distances far for survivors. The GBV services will be part of the services to be supported closer to the IDP locations. Liaison with community focal persons for safeguarding issues will reduce access burden in getting quick support. The legal support however remains weak, though efforts will be made to liaise work with police protection units Use of local staff may pause challenge of confidentiality. However, confidentiality will be a clause for the hire of the local staff to increase caution. Files and sensitive document on GBV, FP, or other critical matters will be kept under key and lock Land disputes is a sensitive matter in context of displacements who are at mercy of a host. An unclear settlement will bring land wrangles in host communities. The local authorities of the host community and County will be engaged in supporting allocation of land for IDPs and channels of reporting complaint for affected IDPs streamlined.
