Project Summary

Advance Africa Initiative (ADAFIN), a women led national NGO, proposes actions that will target Aweil East County of former Northern Bahr el Ghazal. Proposed action will build, maintain current implementation momentum and strengthen the ongoing current UNFPA funded ADAFIN’s ‘’Ending Child Marriage in Aweil East County’’ project.
Proposed project will address Gender-based violence (GBV), which has been identified as one of the most critical threats that continue to affect protection and well being of women and children in Aweil East. Despite recent ADAFIN’s efforts in current programming which have culminated in the banning of early child marriages in Aweil East, a key milestone among vulnerable women and girls; GBV is still rampant particularly in the current conflict context, which is exacerbated by unprecedented food insecurity levels and current mass flooding further alleviating women and children vulnerabilities (SSRC Assessment, Sept 2019).
Moreover, sexual violence at household level had been rampant through intimate partner violence which remains as a common form of GBV, often perpetrated by family members. A recent study by IOM South Sudan found that 75% of women reported having experienced some form of physical, sexual or psychological violence by their intimate partners (IOM GBV KAPs survey, 2017) which calls for more concerted efforts in addressing the vice at the household level. In addition, rampant cases of early and forced marriages continue unabated, with no legal frameworks for redress and protection (UNICEF Situation Reports, 2019).
Proposed activities will target 6000 people (1000 men, 1000 women, 2000 girls and 2000 boys) and will entail GBV protection awareness/ messaging, legal assistance and counselling; psychosocial support provision including referrals, GBV service provision through women and girl friendly spaces including putting in place appropriate preparedness and protection risk mitigation.
ADAFIN’s current UNFPA Protection funding linkages, ground presence through Aweil and Malualkon coordination offices and ability to cost effectively and rapidly respond to the priority GBV needs. ADAFIN will in addition ensure protection mainstreaming through ongoing UNFPA funded Protection/GBV project; acknowledging that women, girls, men and boys with diverse ages, disabilities and backgrounds have very different needs, risk and coping strategies, by paying particular attention to protection and inclusion of vulnerable groups based and on gender and diversity analysis in addition to ensuring prevention of incidences of Gender Based Violence, sexual exploitation and abuse and other protection concerns, ensuring activities ensure Do No Harm, and environmental protection; ensuring Community Engagement and Accountability to Affected Populations is mainstreamed throughout the intervention to guarantee maximum and meaningful participation of the affected men, women, girls and boys. A feedback and complaint desk will be put in place for recipients of GBV services and inputs and trainings to provide direct feedback on implementation. Multi-sectoral synergies with ADAFIN’s current programming and inter-cluster synergies through working closely with Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management to coordinate relief and humanitarian efforts, Ministry of Gender, Children and Social Welfare among other stakeholders.

1 - Needs

Recent political violence and clashes in Aweil East County in addition to the county grappling with extreme environmental impacts of mass flooding coupled further by compounding deteriorating access to food among vulnerable populations, with worse affected women and children (SSRC/ ADAFIN September 2019 Assessment Reports). The brunt of the conflict weighs heavily on children (boys and girls) including women and men who face increased violence, gender based cases, rape, forced marriages among others (Protection cluster Updates 2019). This has been compounded by unprecedented levels of food insecurity, malnutrition with an estimated 59% of the population in Northern Bahr el Ghazal experiencing IPC Phase 3 or worse acute food insecurity which is projected to deteriorate further early 2020(IPC September 2019 Update). These events have been forcing women and girls who have to walk for longer distances in search of food among other amenities, further exposing them to exploitation, sexual and gender based violence while girls have been forced into early marriages, engaging in transactional sex that exposes them to negative reproductive health consequences. The protection needs especially amongst women and girls is exacerbated by conflict induced family separation, destruction of community assets, disruption of livelihoods, basic services and community based networks that provide support services to women and children’s protection needs. Displacements and loss of livelihoods has particularly rendered men and women, the caregivers of children vulnerable and incapable of providing adequate and appropriate care. The 2019 Humanitarian Response and Protection/Child Protection Cluster Updates have identified a number of gaps in GBV services, including: rampant early child and forced marriages, physical and emotional abuse, sexual violence including rape, lack of access to psychosocial support services and weak community-based protection mechanisms and mechanisms of redress including existing patriarchal traditional norms. Gender based Violence including rape, assault, harassment, domestic violence; early and forced marriages and survival sex continue to persist in Aweil East among other counties in South Sudan (UNICEF, 2019). In addition, sexual violence at household level had been rampant through intimate partner violence which remains as a common form of GBV, often perpetrated by family members. A recent study by IOM South Sudan found that 75% of women reported having experienced some form of physical, sexual or psychological violence by their intimate partners (IOM GBV KAPs survey, 2017) which calls for more concerted efforts in addressing the vice at the household level. In addition, rampant cases of early and forced marriages continue unabated, with no legal frameworks for redress and protection (UNICEF Situation Reports, 2019). GBV survivors often fear associated stigma and often do not report, further compounded by a weak legal system. Advance Africa Initiative (ADAFIN), a women led national NGO, proposes actions that are in line with Protection/ GBV SubCluster Cluster 2020 HRP strategy. ADAFIN is currently implementing a one year UNFPA funded GBV program and will link this with protection mainstreaming. ADAFIN will ensure Do No Harm, and environmental protection.. ADAFIN will in addition ensure Community Engagement and AAP is mainstreamed throughout the intervention to guarantee maximum and meaningful participation of the affected men, women, girls and boys. In addition, ADAFIN’s recent and current programming in the targeted counties will complement the proposed project to ensure an effective and rapid support among the most vulnerable populations. However, complementarities and synergies will be explored by working with other agencies such as RRC, and partners in Health, WASH, FSL, Protection among others.

2 - Activities

• Conduct GBV protection awareness raising campaigns and prevention messaging among targeted 6,000 people (1000 men, 1000 women, 2000 girls and 2000 boys)• Provision of GBV services among targeted girls and women via existing women and girl friendly spaces in Aweil East• Support and provide case management among 100 women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns including providing appropriate referral services• Conduct cash based individual assistance among 50 men, women, girls and boys as part of case management including referrals provision among relevant sectors• Support CBP preparedness and protection risk mitigation measures in Aweil East• Provide psychosocial support services including specialized PSS and related activities including recreational events among identified groups/ victims• Support legal awareness, counselling and aid among 25 • Conduct training of 50 staff/ community front line workers on case management/ PSS• Conduct GBV prevention initiatives among targeted beneficiaries• A total of 500 people engaged through EMAP and SASA initiatives• Provide dignity kits among 250 girls and 250 women as part of addressing differential needs and GBV risk mitigation• A total of 50 community mobilisers trained/strengthened on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Beneficiaries’ rights to access services will be ensured by sharing information in advance of the various support packages the project will be offering. 6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

ADAFIN will prioritize and continue to work with internally displaced populations including minority groups who will be prioritized through the project interventions. These will be registered based on their status and implementation will ensure their views and dignity and incorporated into programming.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

ADAFIN will ensure community and beneficiary linkages through consultations including involving key stakeholders on the project through organized public forums during implementation and monitoring. The project will as well be linked to the RRC and the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare. Relief and Rehabilitation Commission will be key in ensuring unhindered access to targeted beneficiaries and locations in addition to soliciting beneficiary support by various local counterparts and leaders and coordination of relief efforts. The project will also be in line with South Sudan strategy of ending early child marriage by 2030.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

ADAFIN’s proposed project is aligned to Cluster Strategic Objectives for 2020 HRP which aim at GBV protection awareness/ messaging, legal assistance and counselling; psychosocial support provision including referrals, GBV service provision through women and girl friendly spaces including putting in place appropriate preparedness and protection risk mitigation. In addition to working closely with the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare in addition to cluster framework for inter-sectoral integration initiatives in other sectors that will promote synergies and achievement of holistic outcomes.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Despite Northern Bahr el Ghazal being relatively stable, there have been rampant instability and protection concerns in the recent past due to political violence. Proposed project will pay particular attention to protection and inclusion of vulnerable groups based and on gender and diversity analysis. Gender roles will be considered when setting up activities including all stages of implementation with appropriate and culturally acceptable mitigation measures. Registration of beneficiaries and implementation times, durations and sites will be organized by consulting with women, men, girls and boys; conducted during daylight hours allow safe travel home. Protection will as well include actions on staff awareness and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, gender-based violence, including putting in place reporting mechanisms and referrals of victims. This is in addition to carrying out regular protection and risk analysis and taking into consideration specific needs of men, women, girls, boys, youth, people with special needs, elderly etc, and ensure key protection concerns are mitigated. ADAFIN will in addition carry our regular protection barriers and access analysis in consultation with key stakeholders and formulate mitigation measures. In addition, ADAFIN will carry our regular analysis of inequalities/ protection threats in consultation with key stakeholders and formulate mitigation measures.ADAFIN will ensure impartial access to assistance as it adheres to the Code of Conduct of Non-Governmental Organizations in Disaster Relief and the Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Response (Sphere) in delivering assistance to the most vulnerable.Mechanisms put in place will involve project management committees which will be supported to provide support and capacity building to take ownership of prioritization responsibilities including self-protection capacities by walking in groups and offering assistance to people with special needs.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

ADAFIN will closely work with targeted beneficiaries and key stakeholders including the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare. These will be engaged throughout the phases of implementation to promote ownership and sustainability of the action. Line ministries are an essential technical work force, although constrained by resources, technical backstopping of the project will be leverage by the ministry personnel in supporting GBV activities. Beneficiaries and key stakeholders will own the project and implementation will continue even beyond the project life span. At the end of the operation, lessons learnt workshop will be organized to involve key stakeholders such as line ministries and RRC to reflect on implementation and capture key sustainability components. This workshop will allow for informed planning and adequate community and line ministry support.
