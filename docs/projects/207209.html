Project Summary

For more than seven years now, NW and SW regions have been the center of violence that has resulted in the displacement of over 630,000 individuals (UNICEF, July 2023). The conflict in the NW is highly characterized by violent clashes between government forces and Non-State Armed groups (NSAGs) and inter-communal violence, that leads to civilians’ killings, kidnapping for ransom, arbitrary arrests, destruction of properties and the disruption of markets food and nutrition services due to violence. The conflict has aggravated the humanitarian situation with needs ranging from immediate food, protection, shelter/NFI, water and sanitation as well as access to health and education. The forced displacements and loss of livelihoods that has resulted in poor food habits that has had negative impact especially on children.
The October 2019 Emergency Food Security Assessment (EFSA) showed proxy GAM rates of 7.1 % and 5.1% for SW and NW regions respectively. The SAM prevalence rates were reported at 3.0% and 2.0% for SW and NW regions respectively. The high SAM prevalence necessitated continuation and scaling up of SAM treatment programs in NWSW regions. From January 2023 until date COMINSUD in partnership with WFP screened about 55,578 (29341 girls and 26237 boys) children 6-59 months for malnutrition in Wum, Benakuma and Fundong Health Districts in the Northwest Region. Out of the total screened, 49 children (0.08%) were identified with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and 195 children (0.35%) were identified with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) showing increasing rates of malnutrition. Like 2023, a key concern in 2024, will be the availability of resources and the ability of partners like COMINSUD to reach out to more children and pregnant and lactating women especially in hard-to-reach areas. These challenges will constitute 2024 priorities with a focus on SAM treatment, IYCF awareness, deworming prophylaxis, use of mosquito nets and micronutrient supplementation targeting children, adolescent, and pregnant women.
The objective of this nutrition response aims at addressing critical gaps in maintaining good coverage of critical lifesaving interventions for treatment of acute malnutrition and prevention of under nutrition in North West and South West Regions.
In this project, COMINSUD intends to work closely with UNICEF and WFP to ensure the prevention-treatment-prevention continuum of malnutrition through the establishment of effective screening and referral systems between blanket supplementary feeding interventions and UNICEF supported treatment activities and the promotion of infant and young child feeding in the context of emergency in NW region. Currrently, COMINSUD has an ongoing response with WFP on BSFP in the targeted locations targeting the same beneficiaries.
Activities will include active screening of acute malnutrition & referall of children for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) for treatment; Sensitization of Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies (IYCF-E), creation of mother support groups for preganat and lactating women and capacity building to the community i.e community health workers (CHWs) and caregivers. Caregivers will be trained on Family MUAC/Mother MUAC approach to enhance early identification of SAM. Involving mothers in nutrition screening activities recognizes the fact that they are best placed to identify early signs of malnutrition and reinforces their role in protecting and promoting their child’s health. By integrating nutrition services, WFP and UNICEF will avoid duplication of activities, such as the screening of beneficiaries and training of the CHWs .
