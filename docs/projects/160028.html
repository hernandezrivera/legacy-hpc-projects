Project Summary

Somalia remains in a state of chronic catastrophe characterized by a complex political environment, extreme poverty, food insecurity, conflict and instability. This has led to poor access to basic health services, recurrent disease outbreaks, food insecurity and high malnutrition rates, a lack of safe water supplies, and significant rates of population displacement.
Furthermore, the humanitarian needs have increased drastically due to limited rain, large-scale displacement, lack of access to basic services and, at its root, ongoing conflict and significant displacement from rural to urban areas, because of the above-mentioned condition this project responds to the current health needs in the drought and conflicts affected populations in the targeted regions.
Provision of quality essential lifesaving and life sustaining health services in Middle Shabelle, Bay and Bakool regions with focus on women, children, neonates and other vulnerable groups and strengthen the local capacity to deliver evidence-based and cost effective services based on the Integrated Primary Health Care Approach with BEmONC services and enhanced referral system for severe medical conditions and Obstetric complication in the targeted area of intervention.
The health sector in Somalia especially the targeted districts is in critical condition with very poor Health indicators. The high-risk groups of the direct beneficiaries are U5 (55,492) Under 1 yr (72,125), Pregnant women (13,492) in the targeted areas of intervention.
The project aims at reducing morbidity and mortality of undeserved communities of Bay, Bakool, Middle Shabelle and Banadir regions (Baidoa and Dinsoor in Bay, Wajid and Hudur in Bakool and Jowhar and Bal’ad in Middle Shabelle) with focus on children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating women through the provision of life saving emergency Health services by supporting Health Facilities and Mobile clinics supported by a network of Community Volunteers (CVs). The proposal will follow on the priorities set by the Humanitarian Technical Clusters and Somalia Humanitarian Fund (SHF) for emergency interventions by supportingﬁxed health facility per district (Baidoa, Diinsoor, Waajid, Hudur, Jowhar and Bal’ad) and mobile teams with a network of CVs. The ﬁxed facilities will provide PHC with integrated BEmONC and further referral for secondary health services. More so, INTERSOS will engage 20 CVs in every district of implementation. The Primary Health Care Package includes Curative consultations, Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH), Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses, Immunization, screening of under ﬁve children and PLWs immunization (for both routine and supplementary vaccines), provision of Medical supply, Health Education massage for 104,832 in the target location.
The project will deploy Basic Life Support (BLS) ambulances equipped with basic medical supplies to all targeted locations. The BLS ambulance will provide referral services from the outreach to the ﬁxed facilities and those requiring secondary health care will be referred to secondary healthcare providers ensuring rapid transportation of critical cases (priority will be given to under 5 children and pregnant women with Obstetric complications). The project will establish an eﬃcient outreach and referral mechanism from community level to primary health services and for primary to secondary health services, through a network of CVs and the BLS capacity for the transport/stabilization of critical patient. The community volunteers will do screening, assessing and identifying the sick patient and severe ill person to refer to the hospital for further diagnosis and treatment. This will ensure to fasten the referral process and provide efficient and effective basic life support during the referral process to the hospital in which will provide a trained community volunteers and in support with the nurses to maintain and secure the life of the ill patient before the referral is done.

Mitigation Measures

Matigation measures of exclusion of Women in decision making: INTERSOS strongly believe in the importance of gender equality in humanitarian assistance, and recognize that men, boys, girls and women have specic needs and constraints. In Somalia, Women and Girls have been particularly exposed to violence. The needs of women and young girls’ remains unmet and INTERSOS will continue involving the vulnerable groups at all stages of the project cycle. INTERSOS put into consideration recruitment, training and deployment both male and female staff with varying age group, to create a free interaction between a health care provider and the clients. Participatory sessions will be used to access and incorporate gender equality and age perspectives in all aspects of the project cycle, with a specific focus on gender during the implementation phases, with community discussion and assessments mandatorily conducted with groups of men and groups of women. As well, protection mainstreaming among the partners will be ensured throughout the intervention. The implementation of this project is aligned with cluster objectives of saving lives and the desire to help mitigate and respond to public health shocks through provision of quality health services in emergency. INTERSOS using the mobile teams will reach out to the IDPs and the marginalized communities to provide basic health care and refer cases whenever necessary.

Please provide further explanation

INTERSOS works to minimize any negative effect that the intervention might have on the affected population, ensuring respect for their dignity and safety with particular attention to hidden vulnerabilities (such as age and physical/mental disability) to provide services to all categories of people equitably. INTERSOS work to build the skills and knowledge of local social constructs, such as the community health committees and community health volunteers (CHV) as well as the local health staff, with a desire to instill capacity for prevention and preparedness for future emergencies and build resilience. INTERSOS create sustainability within its intervention and therefore pushes its programs to be participative and inclusive of community and existing local structures. Engagement of the communities is also ensured by training and mentoring different community groups, to increase their knowledge and capacity to continue developing their skills throughout the interventions with the aim of sustaining quality service delivery when the security situation (among others) will allow the population to return to normality. In addition, to strengthen sustainability, INTERSOS engages the ministries representatives (especially the State Ministry of Health and the State Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development) and will continue to do so, not only for them to endorse its activities, but also to receive and grant them support by strengthening existing health system.
