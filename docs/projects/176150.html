Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to expand on existing WASH infrastructure and services in 3 underserved counties. Access to safe drinking water will be realized through rehabilitation 60 broken-down boreholes and drilling of 10 new ones which is expected to benefit approximately 24,000 individuals. WASH outcomes will be further strengthened by provision of WASH NFIs to 8,400 households and distribution of 5,000 menstrual hygiene kits to girls and women of reproductive age. All activities will be accompanied by parallel hygiene sensitization focused on household food hygiene, prevention of waterborne diseases and management of excreta. Sustainability of water points will be ensured through formation and capacity building of water user committees and training of pump attendants. The project will also address the spread of COVI-19 by installation of handwashing facilities in selected nutrition centers and risk awareness messaging to the public.
The project will target areas underserved areas with high prevalence of GAM and SAM as well as those affected by recent flooding and those in IPC 3 or worse levels of food insecurity. In line with the Cluster strategic objectives, Nutrition centers and areas with high incidence of diseases will be targeted for rehabilitation of water points, hygiene promotion and NFI distribution so that linkage to health and nutrition is ensured.

1 - Needs

The WASH sector in South Sudan has been severely affected by years of neglect occasioned by the protracted political conflict and resulting weakened service provision. According to the inter-cluster HNO for 2021, Living standards and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices. Nationally 5.9 million people have WASH related needs,54% of whom are children, 50% are women and 15 % are disabled. The HNO analysis further indicates that 65% of households have no access to safe water,77% have no access to toilets. The analysis also indicates high prevalence of of water protection issues including GBV and water related conflicts and huge gaps in access to NFIs across the country .According to WASH vulnerability mapping for 2021,WASH indicators in the larger Northern Bar el Ghazal and Unity states where the proposed interventions will be focused is no different: only 32% and 39% of households in Norther Bar el Ghazal and unity , respectfully , have access to safe water, only 8% and 24% of households in Unity and Northern Bar el Ghazal have access to WASH NFIs while prevalence of vector and waterborne diseases in Northern Bar el Ghazal is 71% with Unity having a prevalence of 74%.The WASH situation in the 3 counties (Aweil South, Aweil East and Panijiar is further worsened by displacement due to intermitted conflict and flooding which have forced people in to IDP camps and institutions which are often overcrowded and underserved. Floods and conflict often result in destruction or vandalism of water facilities, with people NFIs as they move. Conflict and flooding additionally create barriers to implementation of WASH measures.The HRP of 2021 stresses the importance of addressing the interface between WASH and Nutrition and Food security. According to the analysis, poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs. Mothers and care takers of malnourished children lack basic hygiene knowledge and access to safe water which jeopardizing the benefits gained in nutrition programs. It is also important to consider the increase of WASH needs owing to COVID-19 which is expected to continue in to 2021.Given that the project can only address some of the needs in the most deserving locations, focus will be on water supply, distribution of NFis and hygiene sensitization. Geographical focus will be in Aweil South, Aweil East and Panijiar counties all of which are projected to be in IPC4 or worse and have compelling WASH needs. Crosscutting issues like gender protection and inclusivity have also been considered in the design of the project. All population sectors including marginalized groups like the elderly and the disabled will be given an opportunity to influence project design and give feedback on project methodology and content. Deliberate measures will be in place to ensure inclusion of women elderly and the disabled of all ages and gender in project target with close follow up to ensure they receive endowments. The project will adopt a conflict sensitive approach to all actions: community water facilities will have a provision for livestock with site selection for all activities being decided through community participation. crosscutting issues, the project will adopt conflict sensitive approach as well as gender, protection mainstreaming in all activities: Benefiting households and project sites will be selected through consultative processes and agreement of all interested parties. Water points shall include a provision for both human beings and cattle and shall be in open safe places identified through consultation with local populations.

2 - Project activities

Rehabilitation of broken down waterpoints: Welthungerhilfe will repair/rehabilitate 60 broken down water points (serving a minimum 4,200 households, approximately 21,000 individuals). The activity aims at improving nutrition and health outcomes in underserved locations experiencing IPC 3-5 levels of food insecurity and high prevalence of GAM. Priority will be given to water points in nutrition centers (24 to be targeted) with the remaining 36 evenly between low coverage locations with high incidence of diseases (18) and those in which communities experiencing high GAM prevalence (18 to be targeted). Water user committee capacity building: Repairs will be accompanied by a mandatory (re)constitution and training of water user committees and at least 2 community-based pump attendants at each site to ensure sustainability. Water Committees will be trained in the technical maintenance of the boreholes, hygiene, and safety issues as well as in basic bookkeeping skills as well as in methods to setting up a user fee system to recover the costs of maintenance from the community through water / user fees. Distribution of WASH NFI kits: activity will focus on households hosting SAM children and their care givers. NFI kits composition will be in line with WASH cluster standard guidelines:(1 bucket with lid and tap for storage, 1 bucket/water jerrycan with lid and no tap for transportation, HWTs for 3 months, Soap for 3 months and filter cloth). 4,800 households in 24 nutrition centers will be targeted. Additional 3,600 households selected from health facilities and catchment areas of the 36 community waterpoints will also benefit from WASH hygiene kits. Hygiene promotion/sensitization: NFI distributions will be accompanied by hygiene promotion sessions to be organized by (105) pre-trained CNVs who shall also be facilitated to carryout household visits to ensure compliance to hygiene and nutrition guidelines. Sensitization package cover food hygiene, prevention of waterborne diseases, excreta disposal, hand washing with soap and COVID-19 prevention. Similar sensitization sessions will also be held for an additional 2,520 households in the catchment of health facility and community water points mentioned above. Drilling of 10 new boreholes to serve minimum 700 households, 3,500 individuals; Borehole sites will be selected in Aweil South which is projected to be experiencing IPC 4 level of food insecurity and is just recovering from recent flooding and Aweil East which is equally highly underserved. Drilling to be carried out by professional contractors. Fencing and flood mitigation measures. Boreholes to be fitted with locally used handpumps. Water user committees and 2 hand pump mechanics to be trained and equipped with basic tools for maintenance.700 household representatives from the catchment areas to be sensitized on food hygiene and prevention of waterborne diseases and COVID-19. Distribution of MHM kits: The project will source menstrual hygiene kits from cluster pipeline and distribute to deserving women and girls from the targeted households. A total 5000 kits are planned but number to depend on availability. Hand washing facilities installation in 24 nutrition centers.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All WHH staff are contractually bound to appreciate and disseminating the organizations policy on PSEA. Refresher trainings for staff will be held at project inception and staff prepared to disseminate to communities and report on violations. Communities will be informed during project inception meetings and all contact sessions with project staff. Moreover, reporting on PSEA will been Integral part of community trainings on the feedback and complaint mechanism.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Multiple channels of communication including hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint desks at all project activity sites will be availed to communities. The project will additionally organize regular feedback meetings for special interest groups at community level. Project staff will be trained on how to handle special interest groups’ complaints with staffing for complaints desks and hotlines being made gender and age sensitive

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Project inception meetings to be attended by communities and stakeholders will be held at all locations. Issues to be covered in the meetings will include explanation of project objectives, methodology, activities and beneficiary endowments, responsibilities, and the rights thereof. Participants will additionally be sensitized on the project complaints and feedback mechanism and its application. Contacts details and reporting tools for the mechanism will be shared and their use explained. Regular reminders on the importance of the complaints and feedback mechanism will be communicated during activity implementation and field monitoring visits. Project communication including adverts and visibility materials will have messaging on CFRM as well as the reporting hotline contacts.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback will be collected through the feedback mechanisms and directly during deliberately organized feedback meetings at community level. Normally, all Welthungerhilfe projects and locations have a feedback analysis committee chaired by respective project managers. The committee analyses feedback immediately it is received and decides on responses. Responses are then directed to the responsible units for implementation which includes adjustment of approaches and communication to affected parties. The project manager follows up to ensure implementation and refers issues to higher levels and third parties as may be deemed necessary. The response mechanism has clear guidelines of protecting information sources
