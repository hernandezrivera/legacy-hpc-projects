Project Summary

This project’s objective is to contribute to improving the living conditions of the conflict-affected population in Borno State, Northeast Nigeria. Specifically, the project will improve the access to food for particularly vulnerable persons in Gwoza, Monguno and Maiduguri LGAs. The beneficiaries of the project will be selected among IDPs and Host community based on their vulnerability to food insecurity. Caregivers of children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), persons with high risk of falling victims of Protection concerns such as Gender-based Violence (GBV) and child labour due to their financial vulnerability will be targeted in priority. Other selection criteria will include elderly persons with no source of income other than begging, female-headed household with no source of income or a high number of dependents. This project will complement other interventions Premiere Urgence Internationale implements that aim at preventing or treating acute malnutrition, providing primary health care and preventing and responding to GBV.
PUI will provide food assistance to 5,390 vulnerable individual beneficiaries on a monthly basis for 12 months. The food assistance will be provided as food e-vouchers or e-cash redeemable at partner vendors operating within the targeted area. The monthly disbursement will cover 70% of the basic food needs of the beneficiaries and the cost of cooking fuel. PUI will conduct regular price monitoring of food items in order to update the transfer value in line with market prices when necessary. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to purchase items belonging to different food groups to have a diverse diet.

1 - Needs

Borno State accounts for 76% of the total IDPs of the Northeast, with 1,865,175 Internally Displaced Persons, and for half of the 8.4 million people in need in the region, 80% of whom are women and children. The LGAs of Maiduguri M.C, Monguno and Gwoza are respectively the second, third and fourth LGA with the highest numbers of IDPs in the whole area: 240,580 in MMC, 160,239 in Monguno, and 159,584 IDPs in Gwoza, where Pulka is located, who are experiencing high level of vulnerability. With 60% of them living in host communities, access to already limited essential services and resources is further constrained.According to the United Nations, 1.9 million people were in need of food assistance in Borno State in 2022, and according to the food security sector, in October 2022, 34% of the needs were not covered. In Maiduguri MC, almost 50% of the needs were still to be met, a bit more than 20% in Gwoza LGA.Difficulties to access an adequate food diet, in sufficient quantities is a cause of high prevalence of malnutrition in Borno State in general, and in the hard-to-reach areas, such as Monguno and Pulka. These two LGAs are currently in IPC AMN Phase 4 or “critical” phase (IPC, November 2022). PUI’s SMART survey conducted in August-September 2022 indicated Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates of 24.4% and 12.9% in Monguno and Pulka town respectively, Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rates respectively were 10% and 2.9%. In Maiduguri, GAM rate was 14.4% and SAM rate 3.8%. These rates are above WHO thresholds and very concerning. The overall prevalence of acute malnutrition is generally highest among young children (6-23 months) compared to older age groups (24-59 months); in Borno’s hard-to-reach areas, GAM rates in September 2022 among young children ranged between 22.4% and 25.7%, whereas the range was 14.3% to 17% among older age groups, as highlighted in the Humanitarian Situation Monitoring (HSM) Bulletin of September 2022. Food consumption deficit and limited diversity diet are the main direct causes of this high prevalence of acute malnutrition. 45.4% of households in hard-to-reach areas have a poor or borderline food consumption score (HSM, September 2022).

2 - Activities

PUI intends to provide cash and voucher assistance (CVA) to 5,390 individual beneficiaries in Pulka (Gwoza LGA), Monguno and Maiduguri, to help the targeted beneficiaries increase their purchasing power. For 12 months, every month, beneficiaries will receive e-cash and e-voucher redeemable at partner vendor shops operating within the targeted areas.Beneficiaries will be selected based on vulnerability criteria jointly defined by the project team, local leaders, and community representatives. These criteria will be reviewed at the beginning of the project and could include:Households• with malnourished kid(s)• with member(s) who suffer from a chronicle disease• with persons with disabilities• with a high dependency ratio (defined as total number of household members/ total number of household members of working age)• with limited productive assets (e.g. : households who do not own an IGA equipment), or limited and unstable source of income (e.g.: households relying on begging or petty trade)• headed by single mother or an elderly person with no source of income / a child• living in a low standard shelter (tent, makeshift or squatting),Persons • at high risk of falling victim of GBV referred by the Protection team. Potential beneficiaries will be pre-identified by representatives of different socioeconomic groups (IDPs, women, youth, ethnic minorities, Persons with Disabilities, etc.) within the community. PUI project team will conduct a systematic door-to-door verification to assess the eligibility of each pre-identified beneficiary. PUI MEAL will then conduct an additional control on a sample drawn from the validated list after the door-to-door verification process. The beneficiaries will be finally validated if the MEAL results confirm that less than 10% of the sampled pre-identified beneficiaries are non-eligible.Households with victims or members deemed to be at high risk of falling victim to various forms of child abuse, exploitation, neglect, violence, or gender-based violence (GBV) will be identified by the Protection department according to existing PUI SOP and referred for potential integration in the food assistance support. The proposed total transfer value of the e-cash and e-voucher per household was calculated to cover 70% of the cost of the food basket as defined in the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB), which is based on a 2,100-kilocalorie ration/person/day and the cost of cooking fuel (2000 NGN per household). Targeted beneficiaries will contribute for the remaining 30% as per the Food Security Sector (FSS) recommendation in Borno state. Using VAM market prices monitoring in Maiduguri as of October 2022, 70% of the MEB cost for a 5-members household is 44,463 NGN. Following FSS guidance, the household's transfer value was calculated according to the household's size (per capita approach), and the different thresholds. For a HouseHold (HH) with a size between 1-3 people, 3 persons ration (regardless of the actual number of household's members), with a Transfer Value worth (NGN) 20,669For a HH size of 4-7 members, the number of rations will be equal the actual number of persons in the household, with a Transfer Value comprised between 26,892 – 45,461 NGNHH size of 8 members +,8 persons ration (regardless of the actual number of household's members), value 51,784 NGNThe total transfer value received by the household will be divided into two equal parts, 50% provided to beneficiaries as unrestricted cash and 50% as value e-voucher. The beneficiaries will redeem their cash and e-voucher at partner vendors operating in their neighborhood. There is no restriction on the use of cash, whereas, for the e-voucher, beneficiaries can choose from a limited list of 12 food groups, each inclduing a variety of several food items: cereals, roots and tubers, vegetables, fruits, meat/poultry and offal, eggs, fish and seafood, pulses/legumes/nuts, Milk and milk products, oil/fats, sugar/honey, miscellaneous.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

PUI will ensure regular analysis of the local protection environment to ensure that the distribution of e-voucher does not put beneficiaries at greater risk of violence, abduction, or abuse. Moreover, PUI will ensure the absence of potential threats and harm to vulnerable people by implementing various measures, such as making sure that the location of both e-vouchers distribution sites and vendors' stands as well as routes to access them are away from actual or potential threats, with a particular concern to the risk or threat linked to sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), and attacks from armed groups. PUI works with a holistic approach, guaranteeing the centrality of protection and ensuring that cross-cutting protection and gender are integrated across all activties in all stages of implementation and monitoring. Using this approach, PUI ensures accountability to beneficiaries, guarantees equal access, and considers gender needs. To achieve these objectives, PUI performs analyses according to a risk approach as defined by the international standards IASC and GPC (risks = threats *vulnerability / capabilities). PUI has field teams trained on protection principles of protection. PUI will ensure that e-vouchers and other Food Security-related responses reduce the threats against the affected population, and that program activities do not inadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other undesirable actors. PUI will ensure that necessary arrangements are made for all older people, persons living with disability, pregnant women receive their e-vouchers, and that beneficiaries know their right to safe and equitable food and/or livelihoods assistance and how to obtain it. Information sharing and awareness-raising in the community will target both host community and IDPs, especially new arrivals in displacement settings. PUI will ensure that all community groups (e.g., gender, age, ethnicity, socio-economic groups, etc.) are represented. Targeted measures will be included to ensure the effective and meaningful participation of representatives of all groups in the community. Besides, all Food Security teams / members will be trained on Protection Mainstreaming principles so that they can be able to identify issues related to exclusion discrimination and service diversion. PUI will regularly assess and monitor access to food security activities by collecting disaggregated data by age, gender, disability, location, or status. This data will be used to identify whether there is discrimination or if service is being diverted, and in this case, PUI will take appropriate measures to ensure assistance reaches the most vulnerable.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Premiere Urgence Internationale's commitment is to make sure that communities, beneficiaries, and among them the most vulnerable members, can provide feedback, make suggestions or express, file complaints if required. PUI has developed a community-based feedback system that consists of multiple entry points such as the helpline, which is free of charge for the beneficiaries and functional at any time during the working hours/days, the mobile helpdesk, community volunteers' networks, and monthly Community Committee Meetings. These are meetings with beneficiary representatives. PUI also regularly conducts key informant interviews (KII) and FGDs to understand the evolving context of its intervention areas as part of the monitoring strategy. Besides, PUI works alongside community leaders and representatives to gather inputs that influence the program design and implementation, the targeting methodology, the gaps, and unaddressed needs analysis. These channels are reviewed yearly following beneficiary feedback to ensure the mechanism is inclusive, context-specific, and accessible safely and confidentially by women, girls, boys, and men. Accountability teams are composed of both male and female members who speak the most popular language of the area of intervention to allow beneficiaries to easily express their concerns. To ensure a consistent feedback management process across the intervention areas, SOPs emanating from the organizational accountability field guide are in place to track and respond to complaints and feedback. The SOPs describe steps for managing both non-sensitive and sensitive cases (e.g, fraud, corruption, PSEA, child protection). A well-trained accountability team is responsible for collecting, documenting, and categorizing the complaints and feedback from the different channels in a dedicated secured database.
