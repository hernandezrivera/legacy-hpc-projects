Project Summary

GVC will intervene in Deir-Ez-Zor and Aleppo Governorates providing light rehabilitation and repair of water supply services in schools and at community level. In both Governorates, the Action will target Hard to Reach (HTR) areas where the INGO is present with running programs in WASH and Education in Emergency sectors (all ranked 3 to 5 as per WASH severity scale). The Action aims to provide sufficient and safe water to around 76,000 people (25,080 women, 20,520 men, 13,680 boys and 16,720 girls) living in Deir-Ez-Zor city and rural Aleppo areas (sub-districts of Al-Khafsa, Membij, Jebel Saman, Maskane). The Action will ensure access to water to communities and improve the capacity to cope with the increasing number of returnees and/or IDPs. In particular, the following activities are foreseen to guarantee enough quantity and good quality of water for the population: 1) rehabilitation of 1 water pumping station in Jebel Saman sub-district (Handarat); 2) rehabilitation of 1 water reservoir in Al Jafra neighbourhood (Deir-Ez-Zor city); 3) light rehabilitation of existing water networks in case of emergencies as rapid response mechanism within the sub-districts of intervention, based on needs identified case-by-case in coordination with local Water Boards. In addition, the rehabilitation of WASH infrastructures in 12 schools (5 in Deir-Ez-Zor city, 7 in rural Aleppo) will guarantee safe and dignified access to girls and boys thanks to gender-oriented WASH facilities and dedicated toilets for children with disability, thus favouring school attendance for all students. The emergency distribution of family hygiene kits at household level, including jerry cans, and community WASH items, such as water tanks and latrines, will include Hygiene Promotion sessions to the targeted population in the selected areas -tailored on age and gender when feasible- to boost the adoption of correct hygiene practices and consequently reduce the incidence of waterborne diseases.

4 - Needs

GVC run an assessment through KI interviews in Nov2019 with MoWR and local WBs in the sub-districts of Jebel Saman, Membji, Al Khafsa, Maskana and Deir-Ez-Zor, selected according to: severity scale 3-5,returnees flow,GVC presence. Focus on:access to/quality of WASH services;infrastructure status on site by GVC engineers;secondary data (HNO2019;INSO;REACH).Urgent needs identified:1)Rehabilitate water pumping stations to ensure access to adequate quantity/quality water from controlled sources, decreasing dependency on others and reducing economic burden on vulnerable families;2)Rehabilitate water reservoirs to provide storage capacity and ensure water network functioning;3)Light rehabilitation of water networks to restore system efficiency;4)Increase storage capacity at HH level;5)Promote hygiene practices. In Deir-Ez-Zor city, the main problems are production of quality water and intermittence of service. Water of municipal water network is drawn from Euphrates river with poor treatment as many stations are out of service/partially functional due to power outages/lack of fuel for generators.KIs reported that perceived daily quantity of water is 5-25 litres/person.Even though quantity of water is sufficient to fulfil HHs need, water from the network is received 3-4d/week for 5-12h. Health problems are still reported (diarrhoea, skin rushes) for all ages, likely linked to poor hygiene practices.In Al Jafra neighbourhood water supply relies on inefficient electricity network’s availability and generators.In Aleppo sub-districts, 100%KIs reported access to insufficient water as main problem (5-25 litres/person), confirming critical quantities with consequences on hygiene conditions, not being able to afford hygiene products.85%KIs rely on water trucking, boreholes and water network to cope with water insufficiency.In addition, 12 schools were identified (5 Deir-Ez-Zor, 7 rural Aleppo) in need of rehabilitation/WASH facilities to ensure safe access to boys, girls and PWD.

5 - Implementation of activities

1.1 A)Rehabilitation of 1 pumping station (Handarat in JebelSaman-38,500 ind) to access water at reduced cost compared to water trucking thanks to direct supply through water network. Priority works:rehab/reconstruct building; pumps installation; connection to electricity network for lighting/safety; installation of disinfection system. B)Rehabilitation of 100m3-reservoir in Al-Jafra (DEZ city-15,000ind) to store/ensure water through distribution network when pumps are not working due to electricity outages; ensure pressure in distribution line; improve fire fighting and irrigation systems. C)Light rehabilitation of water networks in DEZ city, Al-Khafsa, Maskane, Membij sub-districts as rapid response mechanism to emergencies. Exact locations to be defined according to needs reported by WBs. Framework agreements with local contractors to provide items and execute works to re-establish network functioning(2,000ind). 1.2 A)Management activities performed by WBs in DEZ/Aleppo in parallel to activities under 1.1, including capacity development of WB staff.Water Safety Plan:map of distribution system; identify hazards; monitor control measures; verify effectiveness/compliance of WSP Manager,WB and NGO. O&M Plan:activities needed to provide adequate/reliable supply of water i.e. monitor system state; enforce policies/procedures; repair/replacement of system components(53,500ind). 2.1 A)Distribution of 3,000 family hygiene kits during HP campaign with focus on women/children needs, compliant with WASH cluster standard (15,000ind especially IDPs). B)HPC through SARC volunteers, community-based campaign aiming at improving knowledge, attitude and practices on WASH, emphasizing the importance of personal hygiene(15,000ind). C)Emergency provision of community WASH items (latrines, water tanks, ...) for 15,000ind mainly IDPs from conflict zones done by SARC in coordination with GVC/UN. 4.1 A)Rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 12 schools in rural Aleppo and DEZ (5,500ind).

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS:a)Stable security in targeted areas b)Safe access for staff/contractors to conduct activities c)Access to infrastructure to be repaired guaranteed by WBs d)MoWR and WBs maintain planned priorities in the areas e)Internal return of people sensibly increases because of improved political and safety situation f)Collaboration of Authorities to set up criteria for beneficiaries' selection and monitor distribution of HK and community WASH items g)Local communities and other agencies are positive towards services newly restored.RISKS:1)Security situation deteriorates up to prevent the implementation of some activities 2)Shrinking of humanitarian and operational space in some areas due to military operation 3)Major change in demographics due to population movements requiring strategy modification 4)Major change in attitude of authorities and local partners towards planned interventions 5)GBV/PSEA.RISK MITIGATION STRATEGY:1)GVC ensures continuous coordination with SARC and UNDSS to monitor the situation.GVC adopts security plan and SOP in case of any major accident 2)Policy environment is monitored through attendance to Clusters, WGs and coordination mechanisms 3)GVC monitors demography and liaises with other agencies 4)GVC ensures good relationship with authorities and transparent/accountable way of working, informing on planned activities.Plans of intervention are shared and discussed in order to increase acceptance 5)GVC directly ensures M&E of activities 5)Compliance with GVC code of conduct.Feedback mechanism in place.CONTINGENCY MEASURES:1)Security alert system prevents risks and different routes/timing are adopted to ensure the service 2)If access is impossible, remote management through SARC or change of intervention area will be studied 3)Any unpredictable decision of MoWR on priorities will be discussed in close consultation with WASH Sector Coordination platform, finding solutions to improve beneficiaries' conditions according to project's objective.
