Project Summary

The project appeal is in support of integrated emergency response to enhance community resilience for an inclusive healthy living and peaceful co-existence in South Sudan. The project will inclusively benefit the neediest host communities, IDPs and returnees in Morobo, Terekeka, rural Juba and Yei Counties of Central Equatoria State. The humanitarian situation in the project locations is wanting and according to the IPC projection (April – July 2021), 31,000 people are in catastrophe and 2,467,000 people are in emergency. The WASH cluster 2021 severity mapping has placed Morobo, Yei and Terekeka Counties at severity level 5 and Juba County at severity level 4. The severity mapping levels imply that there is urgent need to drill 14 new boreholes, rehabilitate the 50 dilapidated non functional boreholes, support 2,300 vulnerable women, persons with disabilities and elderly people with torches, conduct 64 community trainings on guidance and counseling for GBV survivours, conduct 64 COVID-19 and climate information services awareness campaigns, distribute NFI kits, distribute household water treatment products, support 1,000 school girls with dignity kits, conduct training for 64 water source and sanitation committees (60% representation will be women), train 26 water pump technicians, distribute WASH kit items, distribute facemasks after GBV risk analysis with women, girls persons with disabilities and elderly persons and provide 20 community-hand washing facilities. The project will significantly contribute to; increased access to safe water, improved food security and livelihood, increased levels of community awareness on water borne diseases, climate information services and COVID-19, enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence and increased school enrolment and participation of the girl child at family and community activities. This project will also enhance inclusive healthy learning and living environment for children when schools reopen in South Sudan. In Lirya Payam of Juba County, there have been 22 non functional boreholes of which Seven 07 boreholes are for schools while in Terekeka County, there have been 28 non-functional boreholes of which 08 are for schools. The project implementation approach will be; integrated, participatory and inclusively rights based.

1 - Needs

The current humanitarian situation is distressing. According to the IPC projection (April – July 2021), 31,000 people are in catastrophe and 2,467,000 people are in emergency. The WASH cluster 2021 severity mapping has placed Morobo, Yei and Terekeka counties at severity level 5 and Juba County at severity level 4. Water crises, increased GBV, high levels of school dropouts and acute food insecurity in Morobo, Yei, Terekeka and rural Juba are driven by recurrent flooding, escalation of inter-communal conflicts, insecurity, population displacements, declined food production, pests and diseases, poor macro-economic conditions, economic impacts of COVID-19, limited access to basic services and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. This project is aimed at enhancing community resilience of the neediest population at the grassroots level for an inclusive healthy living and peaceful co-existence. A total of 19,200 people in need will be supported with humanitarian services of which 24% of the affected population are women, 54% are children, 15% persons with disabilities and 7% elderly people. Last year (2020), CAD drilled 11 boreholes in Terekeka, Kapoeta East, Juba and Kassangor Boma of Greater Pibor Administrative Area (10 boreholes installed with Indian II hand pump and 01 borehole installed with submersible pump) and based on the community feedback during our age, gender, and diversity risk assessment conducted in January 2021 in Terekaka, Yei, Morobo and areas of rural Juba, we have learned that lack of enough potable water results into increased water born diseases and GBV as women walk long distances in search of water. Lack of dignity kits affect women and girls from smoothly conducting their livelihood activities and from attending school. This creates GBV risks, as men do not understand the challenges women go through during menstruation. Recurrent inter-community conflicts increase especially during the dry season as some communities refuse their neighbours from accessing water points in their villages. For example, the payams of Gwor, Nyori, Tombek, Muni and Tindillo are generally dry and arid. During the dry season, both people and animals struggle to access water. This often results in competition over water and grazing land which often results in conflict and thereafter affect inter-communal relations. Since May 2020, several clashes occurred between cattle herders and communities in Terekeka, rural Juba and Lainya. The inter-communal violence and insecurity forced thousands of people to flee their homes in Terekeka and seek shelter on the islands of Gemeiza, Khorshomba, Kanya-wai, Gulubach, Gori, Yeki, Malang, Legger and Lainya County, as well as to the north in Bor South County. The IDPs due to the inter-communal conflicts in Terekeka County have been sheltering out in the open, drinking untreated water and practicing open defecation. In addition, the recurrent flooding affected 1,302 households in Terekeka County and 3,189 households in Juba County especially in Lado Payam. According to WHO situation report (July 2020), a total of 6,208 new IDPs in Lirya Payam of Juba County have also been internally displaced as a result of fighting between opposition forces (SSPDF and NAS) in Langabu locality. According to REACH report (April-September 2020), there are 2,223 refugee returnees in Morobo County and conflict-induced displacements in Yei that need urgent humanitarian services. This project is looking at providing potable water through drilling of 18 new boreholes and rehabilitation of 50 existing dilapidated non-functional boreholes in 43 villages in support of 7,800 people in Gwor, Nyori, Muni, Gemeiza, Tombek and Tindilo payams of Terekeka County, 4,800 people in Liriya payam of Juba County, 3,000 people in Gulumbi, Kimba and Panyume payams of Morobo County and 3,600 people in Mugwo, Yei,, Otogo and Lasu payams of Yei County.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CAD has PSEA policy and procedures. All staff members will receive refresher training on PSEA, code of conduct/ best practices and Do-No-Harm. CAD through community awareness creation and integrating training of WASH committee member on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse will provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse . Beneficiaries should know that Sexual exploitation and sexual abuse perpetrated against any community member constitute serious misconduct and are therefore grounds for disciplinary action and or punishment by law. Additionally, sexual activity with persons under the age of 18 is prohibited by law. The project will sensitize the project beneficiaries that provision of humanitarian services is for free and a human right and therefore sexual favours is prohibited and condemned in the strongest terms.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Women and girls often bear the responsibility for water collection. Women and girls also have special needs in terms of sanitary facilities. Travelling long distances to water points and unsafe sanitary facilities can increase the risk of GBV and other at-risk groups such as older people and persons with disabilities. This project assistance is tailored to tap women and girls source of knowledge regarding cultural WASH practices. Ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable people is taken into consideration the location of the project WASH facilities will be inclusively agreed upon so that it can favour older persons, persons with disabilities, women/ girls and other minority group of persons. The design of WASH facilities will be age and disability friendly. Women, older people and persons with disabilities will be given leadership roles in WASH facilities so that their feedback will be received easily. Incorporating feedback from the most vulnerable people will help safeguard hygiene and the right to water for older people, persons with disabilities, women, girls, men and boys. It will also reduce public health risks, build safer communities, promotes dignity for all through the inclusive consultation and feedback mechanisms and it will also promote ownership and sustainability of WASH facilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will have an established Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs). This project will provide means for affected people to voice complaints and provide feedback on areas relevant to operations in a safe and dignified manner. The CFM system will include established procedures for recording, referring, taking action and providing feedback to the complainant. Valid complaints and useful feedback will be taken into account to ensure improved programming. The project will sensitize the populations about referral pathways for survivors of GBV. Awareness raising of SGBV, COVID-19, climate information services and availability of services via community communication channels

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project has a built-in feedback and or complaint mechanisms. This will ensure that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with complaints about the project assistance and its sustainability. The project is designed to benefit populations in emergency and crises and this means women, men, persons with disabilities, older persons, displacement-affected communities, host communities and returnees will have access/ benefit from the project activities. For instance, this project will inclusively benefit 19,200 neediest population affected by recurrent flooding, escalation of inter-communal conflicts, insecurity, population displacements, declined food production, pests and diseases, poor macro-economic conditions, the economic impacts of COVID-19, limited access to basic services and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. Cross cutting issues such as community awareness on COVID-19 pandemic and climate information services have been incorporated in the project design.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project will mitigate the four top risks as follows;1. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection will be mitigated through community mobilization and sensitization about the referral services for survivors of protection incidences. The communities will be told about the existing referral pathways in their areas. The police protection desk is of great importance in situations of abuse. 2. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes will be mitigated at the project design phase. The project will be age, sex and disability friendly meaning there will be inclusive decision-making during the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. There will be inclusive leadership for the community WASH facilities.3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms can be addressed through the establishment of Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs). This project will provide means for affected people to voice complaints and provide feedback on areas that affect them. In addition, inclusion of women, older people and persons with disabilities in WASH and community leadership will make it easy for these vulnerable groups of persons to report any complain. CAD has an established complain and feedback desk which helps address such risks. 4. Risk of interference from external stakeholders will be mitigated through inclusive formation of Community Project Management Committees. The local authorities and chiefs will be sensitized on the benefits of collective efforts in enhancing easy delivery of humanitarian assistance to their communities. In addition, there will be impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries to avoid external interference from stakeholders. CAD adheres to its inclusive selection criteria of beneficiaries.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

This project will contribute to quality, timely and inclusive protection services by providing practical ways to engage with all members of the community, respond to their differing needs and draw on their capacities in the most non-discriminatory and effective way. It will incorporate all emergency preparedness, prevention, response, reconstruction, recovery efforts, monitoring, evaluation and reporting to build inclusive and resilient societies in South Sudan.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

This project will decrease proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5 by replenishing household assets. This can be done by providing inclusive access to safe water which will help women and young girls reduce their long walk in search of water hence leaving behind the elderly people and children and their vital socio-economic activities. Provision of assorted tools, seeds and agriculture extension services will improve community productive capacities and there will also be counseling services to GBV survivours to help uplift their self-esteems

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will inclusively adopt both gender-sensitive and gender-transformative approaches based on the findings of a comprehensive gender analysis completed by HAS through previous programs in the target areas. The project will leverage all aspects of community engagement to promote gender equity, ensuring inclusive community consultation, building skills and expertise, creating spaces for leadership and decision-making and engaging women, men, persons with disabilities and the elderly persons in dialogues that advocate for positive social change that promotes greater equity, opportunities and respect for all persons. The project will strengthen women and persons with disabilities and empowerment by actively supporting their participation in leadership positions through the project. For example, at least 60% of water source and sanitation committees and lead farmers trained by the project will be women and persons with disabilities must be included.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Nearly half of all deaths in children under 5 are attributable to malrnutrition. Malrnutrition puts children at greater risk of dying from common infections, increases the frequency and severity of such infections, and delays recovery. This project will strengthen the coping mechanisms of the beneficiaries hence enabling them to provide vitamin, mineral and micronutrient supplementation to their children under 5. Through community-based nutrition education, the beneficiaries will be able to delay cord clamping after birth, provide kangaroo mother care, initiate early breastfeeding, provide dietary diversity, fortify staple foods and support school feeding programmes
