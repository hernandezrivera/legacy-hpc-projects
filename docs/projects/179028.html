Project Summary

The project will take place in Harim, Al-Dana, Jisr-Alsahogour districts – Idleb governate and Al-Atareb districts - Aleppo governate
Cost per student: 149 $

Estimated number of beneficiaries: 2043 (1036 Girls - 914 Boys - 58 CwDs) 54 Men - 39 women
(1050 formal education – 900 nonformal education - 93 schools’ staff)
This project will support the educational activities in Harim, Al-Dana, Jisr-Alsahogour districts – Idleb governate and Al-Atareb districts - Aleppo governate by establishing new 5 temporary Education Centers (TECs) considering the access of children with special needs besides the cultural appropriateness of the context in order to ensure that girls have safe and equitable access providing children with the chance to continue their studies and providing access to quality formal and non-formal education is ensured by providing incentives and capacity building for teachers and education personnel.
The project contributes to the Education Sector’s HRB objectives through:
1- Project will scale up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education in Harim, Al-Dana, Jisr-Alsahogour districts – Idleb governate and Al-Atareb districts - Aleppo governate age (6- 17) by establishing and operating 5 temporary Education Centers.
2- Enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth (aged 6-17 years) within a protective environment and raising the capacity of teachers and education personnel through several needed training and coordination with the education directorate.
The project targeted the camps area within Harim, Jisr-Alsahogour, and Al-Dana districts in Idleb and Al-Atareb districts - Aleppo governate through the following activities:
1- 5 Temporary Education Centers (TECs) will be established and supported by providing supplies, equipment, staff salaries, and heating fuel
2- 1050 students will receive formal education
3- 900 students will receive non-formal education (catch up & remedial classes)
4- 93 school staff will be provided with incentive and capacity building
The project will increase the boys’ and girls’ beneficiaries by providing the activities of formal and non-formal education and both will access these activities for children and youth (6 – 14)
The project will increase the boys’ and girls’ beneficiaries with disabilities by providing the Education Centers (TECs) with the necessary tools and equipment’s to be appropriate with disabilities
The project fund is not secured and we are looking for a donor to cover all the activities in the project

4 - Needs

The crisis accompanied by COVID-19 had a major impact on the education sector whereas one-third of the schools in Syria are completely or partially destroyed, while other schools are used for purposes other than education as shelters for the displaced people. Children especially in the IDP community who are able to attend school often learn in overcrowded classrooms, without adequate water, sanitation, electricity, heating, and ventilation facilities. the COVID-19 pandemic’s curfews and lockdown have prevented continuous access to learning and recreation for children. Although the few available psychical learning spaces re-opened past lockdowns, many families still struggle to send their children to school due to financial barriers.And according to the HNO 2020 report, issued by the OCHA: there is 6 million children in Syria who need education, 2.4 million of whom are out of school, in addition to another 1.3 million children who are at risk of dropping out of schoolThe project is based on the needs of the targeted community, to increase access to education and provision of formal and non-formal education. Conflicts cause several negative impacts and it is mostly affecting children in many aspects. Children, who are out of school for a long period are unlikely to re-attend or enrolled in the schools and subject to the lost generationWar creates chaos. From a child psychology perspective, it is important that all children have some routine in their lives. If children living in a warzone cannot go to school, the routine disappears, and they will have trouble recovering from the crisis. SDI's intervention contribution provides chances to children and youth to have their routine back, in a safe environment.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Beneficiaries are at risk on being exposed to any sexual misconduct by the humanitarian workers and they might not report the misconduct due to the following reason: 1. lack of knowledge of reporting mechanisms, especially for people with disabilities and women living in far away areas. 2. feeling afraid of retaliation. 3. fear of stigma. 4. fear of deprivation of assistance. 5. being unable to understand written banners for being illiterate and/or for having a visual disability. 6. for children, language might be complicated To mitigate these risks, SDI will :1. aim at clearly announcing the hotline number and email specialized for PSEA of SDI and UN numbers. through many means, like, banners, social media, and messages to beneficiaries phones through telegram channel to ensure reaching to the biggest number of beneficiaries including women living in far away areas and people with disabilities. 2. Conduct PSEA training for all staff members and contractors/suppliers dealing with beneficiaries and make them read and sign CoC that state Zero Tolerance towards SEA violations. 3. Conduct awareness raising sessions on PSEA for beneficiaries to encourage them to report any violations and stress on the confidentiality and on eliminating any other fears that they might have.4. Conduct awareness raising sessions for children in an appropriate language. 5. complaint boxes6. Using PSEA field and organizational checklists tools.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: The spread of the COVID-19 19 virus within the project campsLikelihood: Medium - Impact: Low Risk management strategy: All preventive measures will be taken during the period of the project, but in the event that the virus spreads significantly, physical education will be suspended and the transition to distance education, as SDI has good experience in managing distance education during the last periodRisk 2: Bombardment/bombing on schools in targeted areasLikelihood: Low - Impact: Medium Risk management strategy: The areas of the project were selected to be far from frontline and near borderline. Moreover, the teachers and student will be trained on evacuation and other action during emergencies. Moreover, if the project areas become unsafe, we will relocate to other safe areas.
