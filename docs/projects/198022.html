Project Summary

The project will address quality and other barriers to accessing quality basic education and vocational skills opportunities by enhancing the capacity of teachers and systems strengthening (School Based Management Committees, Local and State officials in charge of Basic education) to effectively support and monitor service delivery and provide continuous professional development to teachers in Kikan Primary School - Numan LGA, Daware Primary School -Fufore LGA, Tudun-Wad Primary School - Michika LGA, Thawalchina Primary School - Madagali LGA and Madalu Primary School - Girei LGA of Adamawa State. JRS will build on the lessons learned from other projects in creating linkages to support the vocational skills development and livelihood support (Income Generating Activities - IGA) of targeted learners and parents respectively. This is expected to help the children and their respective households to steadily increase their income while the children remain in school. The target beneficiary parents/households will be further supported to start Village Savings and Loan Association (VSLA) to ensure sustainability of their IGA.

1 - Needs

In the Northeast, 1.9 million and 3.3 million people need education in 2022 . Out of these, 1.2 million people cluster through the humanitarian response plan, with 21 partners on the ground with limited funding (Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022). The conflict has led to massive displacement, thus, exerting more pressure on the already weak system in place for prevention and responding to education issues. As of July 2022, the DTM identified 2,455,190 IDPs in 501,758 households and 1,983,130 returnees in 323,277 households. The IDP population included IDPs in camps and camp-like settings (988,428 individuals or 40%) and IDPs residing in host communities (1,466,762 individuals or 60%). The returnee population included IDP returnees (1,822,932 individuals or 92%) and returnees from abroad (323,277 individuals or 8%) , all requiring protection and education support. About 19,000 teachers have been displaced since 2009, 2,295 killed and approximately 1,500 schools damaged or destroyed (https://protectingeducation.org). This equally destroyed people’s means of livelihood and hence a majority are unable to provide basic needs and social services for their families. The selected LGA for this project are among those most affected by the insurgency directly/ indirectly and of people facing severe unmet needs, host households were extremely vulnerable, with limited social, physical and financial capital. The report of the essential needs analysis conducted by World Food Programme in 2021, showed that 57.4% of households have expenditures below the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB), which means they do not have the sufficient economic capacity to meet their essential needs and are thus considered monetary poor. 42% of all households have expenditures below the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) or the food-only MEB, suggesting household monthly budgets are not enough to meet even the most basic food needs. The situation is further compounded by chronic poverty, the decline in food production, and the depreciation of the Nigerian Naira, which has increased prices of local and imported foods. These, in turn, and many other factors have decreased the chances of children to be in school as many parents send their children to farm or hawking, especially the girls are most affected. Most vulnerable IDPs and host community households, are adopting negative coping strategies such as removing children from school, sending children to do casual work or to beg on the street. The combination of current conflict and pre-existing chronic poverty has pushed many pupils out of school. In addition to economic barriers to education, evidence shows regional and gender variations in enrolment and completion rates for girls and boys. While primary completion rates in the Southern states of Nigeria is very high, completion rates in the Northeast are low since the cases of abduction of students started in 2014 with the Chibok girls. Owing to the fact that attendance and retention in school is low, both learners and teachers, teachers lack the minimum competency to facilitate learning and most SBMCs are inactive among other factors. The project will target the 250 households and 500 formally out of school children (40% male and 60% female) with accelerated learning program, 100 teachers, 85 members of the School Based Management Committees and 10 State and Local Government Education officials from the social mobilization department. Of the total target, 40% will be returnees, 20% IDPS and 40% host communities in Adamawa State. The project will be implemented in 5 LGAs where Jesuit Refugee Service already has existing ongoing assistance programs. More learning space will be created through renovation and rehabilitation of classrooms by another project to make the structures safe for both learners and teachers. Furniture needed will also be provided.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1-Train 5 School Based Management Committees (SBMC) members to effectively play their roles: Members of the SBMC play a crucial role in monitoring what occurs in the classroom. They have the ability to regularly check on teachers' attendance and determine whether or not those who are in the classroom are teaching effectively because they are in the community and closer to the school than any other LGEA or SUBEB actors. In addition, SBMCs should take the lead in creating the school development plan and guaranteeing that the classroom atmosphere is favorable for both teachers and students. They will also support community mobilization and awareness raising events.Activity 1.2 - Accelerated Learning Program (ALP): Accelerated Learning After School program which will target both in and out of school children, further allowing them to use their instructional skills and increase their earning potential. The accelerated learning program (ALP) is designed to allow teachers and students to acquire the identified necessary knowledge and skills in a shorter timeframe; it serves as a catch-up program to children who may have missed schooling or are currently unable to satisfactorily perform academically at their current grade level. This program will be conducted three days a week for 2.5 hours per day. The schedule will include both academic and socio recreation activities to encourage a holistic approach to child development, with a focus on improving literacy and numeracy skills. Other activities include: Activity 1.3: Conduct baseline and end line assessment of pupils on Literacy and numeracyActivity 1.4: Support the school with reference, syllabus and curriculum books for teachers and text books for pupilsActivity 1.5: Provision of a voucher based learning pack including sandals, school bag or uniform to 500 children based on their needs.Activity 1.6: Conduct a 5 days teacher training on literacy and numeracyActivity 1.7: Conduct a 5 days teacher training on child centered teaching and learning methods:Activity 1.8: Conduct school based coaching and mentoring through joint monitoring and support supervision with the SUBEB and LGEA quality assurance departments and Master trainers from the college of EducationActivity 1.9: Support school led total sanitation and promotion of hygieneActivity 2.1: Support vocational skills development through arts and crafts, knitting, sewing and beading for learnersActivity 2.2: Conduct refresher training for the selected households on business planning, record keeping and micro enterprise managementActivity 2.3 Support the formation of Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs)

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

JRS has been strengthening the financial capacities of vulnerable parents in the Northeast since 2017 to gain or increase income and meet the cost of educating their children. These households were trained and provided with start-up kits to start income generating activities based on their priorities and the findings of the market assessments that was conducted in the last phase. The beneficiaries received business start-up kits along with financial and business management training. JRS worked with the Ministry of Agriculture and consultants who conducts study and trainings. These inputs were provided through voucher systems. This project will build on the lessons learned so far and strengthen the beneficiaries livelihoods through livelihood trainings, coaching and creating linkages. One of the lessons leant was that the income from beneficiaries existing businesses was quite low, an average of N5, 000 per month, mainly due to limited business skills and low capital which limits the scope of their businesses. This would be addressed by providing training on the required skills and by providing IGA kits, based on the recommendations of the market study from other projects. Some of the training needs identified and addressed include: idea generation and innovation, marketing, business trends, financial literacy, value chain integration and diversification, recording keeping, credit terms and conditions and profit and loss determination. JRS will profile common factors responsible for business failures and emphasize on competency areas to enable beneficiaries mitigate these failures in their businesses. This is expected to help the households to steadily increase their income, build long-term food security and maintain their children in school.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

JRS Nigeria has a Child Safeguarding policy in place, as well as reporting procedures. There is a designated National Child Safeguarding Officer, and each project location has a Child Safeguarding officer on ground. All staff are inducted into the organization, which includes a presentation on the Child Safeguarding Policy and the principles behind it. Topics explored include boundary setting, different types of abuse, duty to report and reporting mechanisms. All staff are made aware of the Child Safeguarding Officers in their organization are walked through how to make a report. In addition to the Child Safeguarding Policy and Procedures, JRS Nigeria also has a code of conduct and a policy for images and media use. All of these documents are presented and signed by JRS staff. The Child Safeguarding Policy must also be signed by all JRS staff. The Child Safeguarding Policy and procedures lay out very clearly, including the repercussion for child abuse and exploitation, the role of the Child Safeguarding Officer, procedures for responding to complaints, how to prevent child abuse and the Dos and Don'ts when it comes to interacting with children as a staff of JRS - this applies to both personal and professional life. The policy clearly lays out the steps for reporting a case, which must immediately be brought to the Child Safeguarding Officer for follow up. A flow chart guides the process, covering topics such as when to report to authorities and ensuring JRS internal procedures are adhered to. Emphasis in placed on acceptance of what the child says, taking allegations seriously, acting quickly and clear communication with the child on next steps.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback from the community will be given priority during monitoring activities and they will also participate in the monthly reflection and learning meetings to ensure that their voices feed into programming. In addition, qualitative data will be routinely collected through monitoring visits and meetings with other stakeholders, members of local committees, families, community leaders – and most importantly, children – to provide real-time feedback on the quality and effectiveness of program activities. Moreover, Accountability will be demonstrated through participation of beneficiaries, leadership, in preparing activity plans for their locality. The project plan will include both activity milestones and a connected monitoring schedule to get feedback from beneficiaries.
