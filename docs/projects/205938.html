Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to assist 1,100,000 people in vulnerable communities with humanitarian food assistance, Emergency livelihood support and resilience sustenance in Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, Central Equatoria and Greater Bahr el Ghazal. Project interventions to be implemented will prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and improve coping strategies for the target beneficiaries in IPC 5, IPC 4 and IPC 3 locations. Moreover, the project seeks to enhance emergency food production and contribute to the reduction in dependency on food and agricultural inputs. World Vision will adopt a combination of intervention approaches including unconditional and General Food Distribution, cash transfers, complementary livelihood support, capacity building (e.g., modern agronomic practices, Integrated Pest Management, Natural Resource Management, etc.), and resilience building at the household level. Due to limited resources and for greater impact WV will implement complementary interventions (health, nutrition, WASH, and protection) from different donors and in the targeted locations, while maintaining coordination and complementarity with the efforts of other humanitarian actors and in alignment with line ministries. In order to reach the target beneficiaries with the prioritized cluster objectives, World Vision seeks to raise a total of $9,900,000 for the 2024 project budget.

1. Needs

According to the IPC analysis (October 2023) food insecurity remains high and severe, with a projected 8.5 million people likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of 2024. Food insecurity is highest in Jonglei where 68% of the population will likely face severe acute food insecurity, followed by Unity (66%), Upper Nile (66%), Lakes (59%) and Northern Bahr El Ghazal (66%) with the highest levels of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) at the peak of the lean season 2024. Climate change continues to impact different sectors, in particular, agriculture and livestock which is one of the most climate sensitive economic sectors. The abnormal rain pattern in 2023 affected crop performance in most parts of the country in general and in WV target locations in particular. The key factors including long dry spells and floods are likely to continue driving high levels of food insecurity into the foreseeable future e.g., access to markets affected by the economic crisis, protracted conflict and flooding limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services; sub-national violence is expected to continue in many locations and abate in others until the peace process and governance becomes truly inclusive; a fourth year of atypical flooding is becoming the new ‘normal’ causing displacement, damage to homes and affecting agricultural livelihoods and households ‘ability to produce their own food with the added effect of water logging and water borne disease on livestock and human health; other locations experience periodic dry spells(localized drought) at critical times of crop growth and requiring livestock to move further from the homestead (with a detrimental impact on child malnutrition); persistent high food prices despite the fiscal reforms continue to threaten people’s food security across the urban locations, former POCs and small towns where growing numbers of the population are becoming market dependent; and seasonality resulting in ‘normal ‘heightened food insecurity and poor nutritional status at times in the agro ecological calendar. WV aims to reach the most vulnerable populations in Warrap, Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, Central Equatoria, and Greater Bahr el Ghazal states with food assistance, emergency food production through support of agricultural and fisheries inputs, and to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. In the current analysis period of September to November 2023, an estimated 5.8 million people (46% of the population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 1.6 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. In the lean season projection period of April to July 2024, an estimated 7.1 million people (56% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, during this period, an estimated 2.34 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Moreover, an estimated 79,000 people (0.6%) people are likely to face catastrophic (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity including Norther Bhar el Ghazel where WV has strong presence in the state. Hence, WV in partnership with different donors including WFP, FAO, BHA, FCDO, SDC, ECHO, GAC will continue implementing Food, Livelihood support, and Cash programming in the target locations.

2. Project activities

In addressing the priority humanitarian needs in South Sudan, WV will align implementation to FSL cluster objectives: -Cluster Objective (CO1): Emergency Food Assistance: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations. WV will implement: General food distribution; Cash and voucher distribution (Conditional and unconditional); Conditional food transfer (i.e., FFA) and GFD+; Humanitarian livelihood support; Focus on seasonal food assistance with other multi sector integrated actions (Protection, WASH, Nutrition & Health actors); Post distribution monitoring (PDM) Cluster Objective (CO2): Emergency livelihood support: This includes emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support. WV will implement the following activities: Emergency food production; Main season agriculture seeds and tool distribution; fish, vegetable and crop kits; Emergency food & livelihood support; Fishing kits/ vegetable kits; Livestock vaccination; disease prevention &treatment; Vaccination campaign support; Small ruminants restocking; CAHWs refresher training and vet kits support. Cluster Objective (CO3): Resilience building initiative: This includes activities that attempts to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs by supporting and strengthening households’ ability to absorb stressors: WV will implement the following activities: Organize seed fairs using locally sourced seed; Support market functionality and support value chain addition; Training &capacity building; Adaptive and absorptive capacities; On-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots); Saving groups, Income generation (business skills), vocational training; Multisectoral models; Livelihoods, nutrition sensitive food security, hygiene, sanitation reducing pest and crop disease; Post-harvest losses management including introduction of appropriate technologies; Village saving & loan schemes & micro finance; Peace building Activities; Establish, train & support peace committees; Enable conflict related meetings to strengthen local peace structures WV uses a result-based management approach to deliver project objectives. The project results will be monitored through a monitoring and evaluation plan which consist of Indicator Tracking Tables (ITT), Detailed Implementation Plan (DIP), and project tracker. This tracking is done on regular bases (monthly, quarterly, and annually) with results measured and quantified. The following key indicators, among others, will be closely monitored: - # of beneficiaries receiving food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers - # of people receiving seeds and tools- # of people receiving fi shing kits- # of people trained in improved agronomic practices - # of livestock vaccinated and treated - Preportion of people with improved dietary diversity - % increase in food consumption/income - Preportion of people with low Food Insecurity Experience Scale (FIES)During reporting, the project outputs and outcomes [PDM and Annual Outcome Monitoring (AOM)] will be measured based on the above indicators whose values will be tracked on a rolling basis.
