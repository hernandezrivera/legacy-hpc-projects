Project Summary

• This proposed project for Bama, Dikwa, magumeri, Maiduguri, Ngala and Konduga LGAs will lead to reduction in the mortality and morbidity amongst under 5, pregnant and lactating women and amongst the most vulnerable people within locations. This intervention will support health service delivery hampered by the breakdown of health facilities infrastructure and attacks by the insurgency on the available and functioning health facilities in addition to the high risk of epidemic outbreaks especially cholera, meningitis, measles, yellow fever. This intervention will support referral to secondary health care and referral services in remote areas is where there are significantly limited or not available. The intervention will also address the serious shortage of skilled health care workers, particularly doctors, nurses and midwives, many of whom remain reluctant to work in inaccessible areas because of ongoing armed conflict. Continuous population displacements and influx of returnees and/or refugees due to camp closure and economic issues related to inflation and exchange rate has put strain on already existing facilities. Flooding in over 27 LGAs and displacement many households which counting still continue as we have recorded over 60,000 affected individuals. Some of the settlements and communities are not accessible at the moment due to flooding. The aftermath effect of flooding will be spilling over to the year 2023 and these need to be estimated for and planned for as well.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in the BAY states continues to persist despite several interventions and is expected to persist unabated in 2023: the continuing conflict will still severely affect millions of people in 2023, leading to more uncertainties on the displacement patterns and the unpredictable attacks. With the continued recent closure of camps by the state authorities leaving PiN to settle in informal shelters and unsecure areas. It is estimated that more than 2.2 million people including women, children, and those living with disabilities will be affected. The continued multiple displacements and confinement of people in camps, coupled with restricted movements of host communities is crippling local capacities to meet essential survival needs in health, nutrition, WaSH, protection, and livelihoods without external humanitarian support. Whereas the prospects for displaced people’s safe return to their areas of origin are perceived as possible with some areas of origin looking conducive for return or could be made conducive for return, the major challenge remains the unpredictability of the OAGs.According to the HNO released by UNOCHA on March, 2022, about 8.4 million people are affected with 8.4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance across the BAY states. Of these 3.5 million people are food insecure with the same number targeted by humanitarian actors. The situation is not the same for other sectors like health ( 4.6million), WASH (3.0, million), Nutrition ( 3.0 million), Protection ( 5 million). Disease outbreaks such as cholera, measles continue to wreak havoc in the communities with more than 12,000 cases of Cholera reported and case fatality rate above 3%. The burden of COVID-19 continues to affect the communities especially economically by surge in prices and spiraling cost of living.Recent IPC-AM and Cadre Harmonise data showed an increase in GAM rate during the lean period and the situation for 2023 is not expected to improve given the impact of flooding on livelihoods and possible political instability.The timely assistance has contributed to addressing the unmet needs of the affected populations and at the same time made an immense contribution to the alleviation of the suffering of the most vulnerable populations including children under five years, pregnant and lactating women people living with disabilities (PLWD), internally displaced person IDPs, returnees, and host community in the areas of interventions. For instance, under the current BHA grant INTERSOS is reaching, INTERSOS is pleased to have made a substantial contribution to the response to the gaps through USAID/BHA support. INTERSOS will build on these previous achievements, incorporate the lessons learned, and exercise a high level of flexibility and modifications in responding to the rapidly evolving needs of the vulnerable beneficiaries and local security contexts so that we are able to continue bridging prevailing gaps in unmet needs through USAID/BHA funding. The implementation of this project is aligned with USAID/BHA’s mandate of saving lives and alleviating suffering in disaster situations and their desire to help mitigate the current ongoing crisis in Northeast Nigeria through quality emergency response and treatment by NGOs and UN agencies.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

All groups including women, children and elderly are engaged in gathering data on their specific WASH needs. Concerns of all affected people, including vulnerable groups are factored in to the programme design ensuring that activities proposed are specifically designed to meet the needs of different age and gender groups. The programmes include estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the need’s assessment. For latrines, gender segregated sanitation location, design, safety and appropriateness and convenience of facilities will be decided in consultation with the users, particularly women, adolescent girls and any persons with disabilities to ensure the protection of affected people. All sanitation facilities, including latrines, will be constructed near residential units to ensure the privacy and security of women and children and easy access for elderly. Project will ensure that separate lockable, well-lit latrines are constructed for women and children and the criteria and details of activities will be explained in the community for better understanding. Project will ensure women's participation while selecting the sites for sanitation facilities, water points and timing of hygiene promotion sessions and WASH committee meetings. A close supervision that is also adaptable to the evolving needs of the target beneficiaries will be carried out by INTERSOS to support the community members to maximize their knowledge and promote their engagement to prevent and address child protection concerns. First, the training modules will be adapted to the needs of the audience and will use interactive activities, visual material and as far as possible key actors working with children will be invited to stimulate beneficiaries’ understanding, participation and interests. Before and after each session group play activities will test participants’ understanding on specific issues, if needed, topics will be re discussed. After the training cycle is completed, INTERSOS staffers initially will facilitate regular meetings with the community members and the subsequent design of an action work plan and afterwards to follow up on the action plan and its results. INTERSOS has a strong reputation on protection and GBV prevention and risk mitigation, as supported by the international protection coordinator and GBV/PSS specialist. In addition, INTERSOS protection staff work alongside the health, WASH and nutrition teams to ensure mainstreaming of protection and GBV issues across all activities. Furthermore, all INTERSOS staff is bounded to INTERSOS Code of Ethics, an official document that sets the rights, duties and responsibilities for all INTERSOS operators, collaborators, partners, advisers and members of the statutory bodies to which it is addressed. It also reaffirms the observance of values and ethical principles common to every human being, such as honesty, loyalty, probity and those pertaining to its nature as a humanitarian organization: humanitarian relief, neutrality, impartiality, independence, non-discrimination, transparency. Moreover, protection will be mainstreamed in this project as per the following consolidated procedures:- Prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding causing harm, by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of activities;- Ensuring access, by arranging for beneficiaries’ easy access to assistance and services, and paying special attention to individuals who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services;- Participation and empowerment, by supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting beneficiaries to claim their rights through their full involvement in activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

INTERSOS embraces participatory community-led approaches, that involve target beneficiaries, |Project Management Committees (PMCs), and key local leaders and influential persons to understand the project targeting criteria as well as expected contributions from the community, for instance social capital and indigenous knowledge. Community entry messaging include information like source of funding (donor), selection criteria, targeting criteria and expected humanitarian support among others. As part of the CHS commitments, INTERSOS establishes functional complaint and feedback mechanisms in areas of project interventions. A compliant help desks are set up in INTERSOS supported safe space (funded by OFDA) where people can report their complaints or give feedback about the project. Beneficiaries feedback will be gathered through several sources, including: face to face with field staff, WhatsApp, suggestion boxes, help desk, and email. Feedback received from adults and children in the community is gathered and organized by project and sector. This information is sent to the INTERSOS office in Maiduguri, where it is added to the Complaints Response Mechanism Register and referred to the most relevant person. In addition to the aforementioned set-up, the community-based approach, through active involvement of men, women, boys and girls, is at the base of the project design. Since the onset of INTERSOS action in the region, it has become well-known and respected within the targeted communities, positively engaging with both the hosting and IDP communities. INTERSOS conducts regular focus group discussions with target beneficiaries to improve its programming, taking into consideration the suggestions coming directly from the affected communities.
