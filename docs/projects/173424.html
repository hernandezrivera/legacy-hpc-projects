Project Summary

BRAC Child Protection sector has been implementing child protection interventions since 2017 both in camp and host community with a significant contribution to strengthen child protection mechanism in close collaboration with the community people through providing psychosocial support, case management, life skills based session etc. BRAC is highly committed to follow the BRAC Safeguarding Policy, Child Protection Code of Conduct, CPMS while implementing the interventions to ensure “Do no Harm” for the children and adolescents. Through recruiting volunteers from the Rohingya and Host community, and engage the adolescents in various activities through skills development, dialogue within the community etc. BRAC intends to promote empowerment in the communities that it works in. Heading towards 2021, BRAC Child Protection is aiming to strengthen the child protection system and services across the camps and host community ensuring the continuation of existing services e.g. play-based curriculum, structured PSS and adolescent engagement activities etc. with an intensive focus on integrated service to children with disabilities. BRAC will continue the strong coordination it maintains with CPSS, CMTWG along with MHPSS Working Group, ECD Working Group and Youth Working Group through active participation and contribution. Amid the COVID-19 effect, introduction of pashe Achhi Model (Beside You) turned as a remarkable stand for the Child protection services through telecommunication as an alternative modality. BRAC will revisit the existing implementation plan considering overall COVID-19 safety measures when the time for facilities reopening comes. Regular assessment of the facilities and taking precaution to address the DRR issues and complaints response mechanism will be in general focus for all the CP interventions BRAC will implement.
The upcoming JRP will target 28,140 beneficiaries with composition of children, adolescent, community people and child protection staff in 32 camps (camp 1E-camp 27, excluding the register camps) and host community e.g. Teknaf and Ukhiya.

1 - Identified Needs

Child Protection services comprise a huge number of target population as boys and girls represent 51% of all Rohingya population (UNHCR Population factsheet, 30 September 2020) and there are a significant number of children exposed to child labor, child trafficking, child marriage, child separation as well as violence towards children in the camps. The Rohingya have adopted negative copying mechanism such as early marriage of their girls to increase food supply as well as to the host community in order to obtain legal status. According to J-MSNA 2020 study report, about 3% of household reported the presence of at least one child is engaged in child labor. It is also mentioned that child protection concern is increasing, i.e. 9% household said child marriage has been increased (ibid). This is also a great concern in the host community as a significant number of working children are involved in hazardous works. Unaccompanied and Separated children also need special attention as they are more likely to be exposed to different forms of protection concern that remain still urgent. According to population fact sheet by UNHCR (31st October 2020), 1% of total population is person with disabilities whom are most vulnerable. It is also remarkable that inclusion of children with disabilities is less than the other programme. It is Moreover, the situation of the camps is likely to generate anxiety, aggression, depression and various sorts of psychosocial stress which need to be addressed and treated properly through a more comprehensive and structured Mental Health and Psycho-social support (MHPSS). Ensuring social cohesion will need to be addressed to decrease different forms of tension in the host community people raised as an impact of entrance of Rohingya people into their lands. Child protection sector will consider the matter of recreational space and child safety. The project will, therefore, adopt a comprehensive approach catering to 0-18 years old children and adolescents, both boys and girls in different camps and host community. Through play-based curriculum BRAC will ensure holistic development and resilience of young children in CFSs as well home-based pockets, promote adolescents’ resilience, leadership and psycho-social well-being. The Multi-Purpose Child and Adolescent Centre under this project will serve as one stop centre where a range of CP services will be available including case management, structured psycho-social support, life skills, pre-vocational skills and service provision for children with disabilities. It will also engage community people and other important stakeholders to strengthen the overall child protection mechanism.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Provide comprehensive case management and referral services to the children at risk including unaccompanied and separated children, children with specific needs including disability and responding to children exposed to SGBV in collaboration with the GBV sector partners. Case Management support will be provided for the same target group in host community as well.2. Play-based curriculum including Pashe Achhi Model to adapt with the COVID-19 pandemic situation will be continued in the Child Protection facilities with a harmonized and holistic services for children in the host community and the Rohingya community with special regards to age, gender and diversity. Curriculum as well as the facility will be assessed through the lens of inclusion to ensure the service to be inclusive. Consultation with community people at every stage will be ensured.3. Provide structured psychosocial support for boys and girls, including children with disabilities in the camps who are affected by memories of violence and who had been exposed to violence as well as trauma. Psycho-social support will be provided for the same age group in host community as well.4. Provide life skills based education, leadership skills, pre-vocational skills and psychosocial wellbeing through centre and home based group for both Rohingya and host community adolescents where participation of adolescents will be ensured in designing the service and safety mapping to address their age, gender and context specific needs.5. Mainstream Child Protection activities in other sectors through capacity building on PSEA and CPSS guideline on child labor, combating child marriage and violence against children to ensure children safety while accessing the services and work with this sectors in monitoring risks that the children are facing.6. Strengthen and enhance protective environment for children in the community through Community Based Child Protection mechanism e.g. CBCPC who will monitor CP risks and develop community response as well.7. Integrating DRR approach in child protection services through strengthening the Emergency Child Care information Centre (ECCC) to act as a centre for the information. Also the project will provide knowledge on disaster risk reduction & tree plantation to the communities. 8. Project will ensure social cohesion through different sorts of activities, e.g. different day observation, arranging sports, awareness raising meeting or events etc. in collaboration with Government and local organizations.
