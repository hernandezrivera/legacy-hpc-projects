Project Summary

According to HNO 2023 report and the primary need assessment that conducted by Women Support Association (WSA) in conjunction in Idleb, Jarablus sub-districts and Azaz.
this project is developed to:
Firstly: support Wheat Value Chain (WVC) in 1200 hectares, which is expected to benefit 2400 smallholder households (12000 individuals. 3240 B, 3360 G, 3500 W, and 1900 M) in 2023, 50% of targeted populations are female head households (FHH), and 30% are IDPs, will be supported with nitrogen fertilizers (such as urea 46%), fuel for supplementary irrigation, and harvesting cost support, for the 2023 season.
2- Purchase seeds from farmers the expected amount to be collected is 400 MT for grinding in the summer of 2023, price per ton 480 $
Secondly, contribute towards addressing critical food insecurity aspects through enhancing access to bread in 2023.
1-WSA will conduct a detailed food security need assessment at the household level to identify 2400 families and 12000 individuals. 3240 B, 3360 G, 3500 W, and 1900 M) (50 % host community and 50% IDPs) with focusing on the most vulnerable segments of the population including( Female-HH caring for children, elderly, pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities, family with no source of income, as well as families with the largest number of women and girl.
2-Daily free bread distribution for a period of 5 months, targeting 2400 of the most vulnerable household 1 kg/day/HH. This activity will be materialized through supporting one bakery in Idlib and one in Jurablus with 60 MT flour/month and associated materials for bread production (yeast, salt), as well as covering the operational cost (fuel and labor).
the project will adhere to a Do No Harm approach (DNH), and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) to ensure quality livelihood services without causing any harm to the existing social system. WSA proposed program is cost-effective, and leverages existing operations and program resources, to maximize efficiency. As part of an exit strategy, WSA will inform all relevant stakeholders of the exact project dates including closure from the onset of the project. WSA M&E department will complete an end-line survey to gauge the outcome and output level results.

3 - Needs

Syria still suffers from multiple and complex socio-economic challenges. The protracted crisis since more than12 year exacerbated food security and livelihood needs for many families across the country, which further eroding the resilience and recovery prospects. The overall estimated Sector PIN for 2022 – of food insecurity and those at risk of food insecurity – is estimated at 13.9 million, with 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The acute food insecure figure includes 1.9 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 percent food insecure. The FSA sector foresees an increase in the number of food-insecure households in 2023. As the global economy was coming to terms with the food systems disruption caused by COVID-19 pandemic, the outbreak of the Ukraine crisis in late February 2022 sent shockwaves in the commodity markets. Energy and food prices have risen considerably since then, and as the crisis drags on, the elevated price levels are likely to linger much longer due to heightened uncertainty. Syria has not been spared from this economic tumor. For instance, The current uncertainties surrounding the UN-backed deal on grain and vegetable oil exports through the Black Sea ports, and the continued depreciation of the local currency for a food-import-dependent country, are likely to keep prices elevated. 4 - Implementation of activities

WSA will consult men, women, boys, girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities to understand their needs and preferences for location, design, and methodology of assistance. This project will be directly implemented by WSA in coordination and cooperation with the local authorities of the selected target districts. The community mobilizers will be in charge of beneficiary selection from both IDPs and the host communities, based on the following criteria: most vulnerable households, households headed by women, HHs with elderly, disabled, and HHs with sick care takers. WSA will implement a full market assessment and identify traders and evaluate their capacity and reputation, to sign the MOU with selected traders. many awareness sessions will implement with traders and beneficiaries to identify vouchers and how benefices redeem them. The voucher will provide with many information (ID, code, singe of .. etc) to avoid fraud. The beneficiaries will be verified and integrated IDPs with the host community through the establishment of clear mechanisms in cooperation with the local councils to distribute bread and identify the HHs and the establishment of mechanisms for monitoring and supervision by coordination with local authorities. The project will be managed by a qualified FSL technical adviser and oversee the local staff by providing technical advice and guidance. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Based on WSA needs assessment conducted within the targeted locations, WSA will prioritize the most vulnerable persons to receive services during this project. However, they may face risks of exploitation by service providers or by another actor in the community and WSA will work on mitigation measures through the following: 1. Ensure participants know services were to be disclose if violence occurs 2. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and how to report 3. Establish good relations with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles, and stick to them. 4. ensure the compliant and feedback mechanisms are accessible to all. 5. train all staff and contractors on PSEA CoD. Also, the project is designed to enable equal and impartial access through community consultation and participation across age, gender, and diversity in how to reach the most vulnerable groups, not to exclude any groups, and provide the identified services with dignity and impartiality. Starting with the project's first needs assessments and during the whole project period, WSA will focus on using the "Do No Harm" approach to integrate conflict sensitivity wherever the feedback (complaint) mechanisms will be in place and all beneficiaries must be aware of them. It will be confirmed that the service provision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against beneficiaries. Apply the principle of “different needs equal opportunities.”


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The military factions' interventions in humanitarian action are possible, so WSA always mitigates this risk through community acceptance. WSA will make a continuous assessments of the accessible safe routes.. Diversion of support by the belligerent parties,. WSA has good networks with local communities and stakeholders. The existence of many checkpoints inside Syria, may limit access to the target area. WSA will coordinate with local partners and local committees who will help in facilitating the movement of the items to the target areas. The abduction of humanitarian workers is a possibility and part of the risk management is the commitment to humanitarian principles, especially neutrality towards the parties to the conflict in addition to the movement clearance for field staff. As part of the Security of Information management, the field team daily basis scans the financial documents and sends them to the main office in Turkey. The money will be transferred through t post offices in Turkey (Ptt) to Ptt offices in NWS.WSA will make market assessments to determine the probable vendors, prices of materials, and the material kinds; that will ensure to chose the materials have the best quality and acceptable prices. Additionally, WSA will develop a mechanism for remediation vouchers that allow beneficiaries to choose their materials by themselves from the shops of vendors. All contracts will be concluded in US dollars to avoid any fluctuations in the Turkish lira prices
