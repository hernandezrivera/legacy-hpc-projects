Project Summary

Summary It is widely recognized that it is vital for recipients of humanitarian assistance to feel that they have a voice and agency in determining the assistance that is needed. In addition, it is also recognized that with strong community engagement strategies, recipients of humanitarian assistance can provide relevant and timely feedback to improve the humanitarian response. However, it is also true that when the local population does not feel heard, its reaction can hinder an effective humanitarian response. A feasible mitigation strategy is to effectively and efficiently engage the community-at-large in conversations and discussions about their needs.
As a core member of the Camp Management and Camp Coordination Cluster, Internews proposes this project to support the humanitarian community to engage with displaced populations inside and outside camps to:
a) Strengthen inclusive community participation to ensure local ownership, self- governance and self-reliance, by informing, consulting, mobilizing and supporting community structures, such as camp leadership or women’s groups. Through this process the project will address barriers to participation, particularly for underrepresented groups, to allow them to participate in the decision-making processes of the camp lifecycle.
b) Improve engagement with vulnerable population with priority on addressing protection gaps and building resilience and to ensure access to assistance, protection and services in camps and camp-like settings.
This proposal presents a comprehensive package of innovative Community Communications and Engagement (CCE) activities, led by the development of both a physical and virtual community engagement structures, called town hall meetings, involving host and IDP communities discussing important issues with humanitarian agency representatives, and broadcasted on the radio. Through this coordinated

engagement strategy with all parties, moderated and frank conversations are used to provide accountability, while also mitigating risks of conflict between different groups. This project builts upon Internews’ expertise in CCE and Communication with Communities (CwC), particularly the pre-existing networks of listening groups, focus groups and broadcasting systems (both BBTT and radio). The project will be implemented in the existing Internews projects in Malakal, Juba, Wau and/or Bentiu and their surrounding collective sites. Once implemented, these systems will provide clear avenues for all humanitarians in these locations to further engage and communicate with the communities they serve.

1 - Needs

Approximately 6.35 million people – 54 per cent of the South Sudanese population – were severely food insecure in August 2019, according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification analysis for August 2019 to April 2020. Armed clashes were reported in Lainya, Central Equatoria; Torit, Eastern Equatoria; Maiwut, Upper Nile; and Tonj, Warrap, with civilian casualties, displacements and suspension of humanitarian activities. Cattle- raiding in Tonj South displaced thousands to Tonj North. An estimated 46,000 people were displaced in Maiwut (33,000) and Tonj South (13,000). During August, seasonal floods have affected more than 364,600 people in different locations including more than 31,600 people in Akobo, Pibor and Uror. This goes to add to the already existing 7.2 PiN that the humanitarian community is trying to serve in the country. The overpopulation of the camps, and the increasingly lack of security outside the camps, compounded with a consistent under-funding of the response, and therefore a lack of resources to support the response, have increased discontent and frustration within the local, IDPs’ and refugees’ communities. The result has been, throughout 2018 and 2019, a rise in the occurrences of protests in camps – often exacerbated by miscommunication with humanitarian responders. Attacks to humanitarian workers are increasing while IDPs and local communities feel that don’t have many options to resort to, to access services. The response from the humanitarian community has not been effective so far. A strong over reliance on “elected” camp leadership for engagement does not consider their actual representative value, and often has increased chances of corruption or discontent in between the rest of the population. With a total PiN of 1.3 million, the CCCM cluster is at the forefront of the relationship with local communities and the often-problematic relationship with camp leadership is now becoming often an obstacle rather than a facilitation. In order to bring leadership back to the community, the heart of this project is the creation of structured Town Hall meetings that allow all stakeholders, from elected representative to selected members of different sections of the community, along with humanitarian responders and UNMISS, to take part in the discussion. These meetings will be a structured and facilitated dialogue with the primary purpose of these meetings will be to make sure that information is shared between all sides rather than a place for decision-making. They are designed to supplement and consolidate community engagement rather than be any form of decision-making process. By serving as an effective and direct way to communicate and receive feedback from the community, women’s groups, religious leaders, teachers, persons with disabilities, etc., will be actively engaged in the conversations, thus minimizing the risks of information gate-keeping. In order to promote community-ownership of sites, the Town Hall meeting will act as an open forum for Camp Management and humanitarian partners to engage directly with the community and discuss contentious or important issues. One of the important issues identify by the AAP IASC framework is the need to close the feedback loop and provide relevant and timely data to affected populations. Internews will do so by providing information back about reactions and feelings within each location, leveraging its existing feedback collection mechanism and listening group meetings. This system will ensure that conversations within the town hall meetings truly reflects the needs of the affected population. Town hall meetings will also be held with trained stakeholders in locations outside of the camp. By engaging with more diverse sections of the affected populations and allowing communities (especially marginalized groups) to provide input in a constructive manner, CCCM partners will have more reliable and accurate information to tailor the response.

2 - Activities

A. Activities aimed at the target beneficiaries in static and mobile responses Activity A1: Town Hall Trainings All participants to the Town Hall are trained before taking part in the discussion and each discussion always has moderators and is prepared in advance. These trainings will focus on how to represent your own communities or groups at the town hall meetings, and how to develop constructive relationships with humanitarian organizations. For Rapid response the trainings will be shorter and tailored to the communities addressed. Activity A2: Town Hall Meetings Town Hall meetings will be organized at each static location. In effect, the primary purpose of these meetings is to make sure that information is shared between all sides rather than a place for decision-making. In mobile response, the Town Hall meetings will have as attendants all the representatives of vulnerable groups, women, children, elders, persons with disabilities and community leaders.Activity A3: Town Hall Radio programming and distribution The Town Hall meetings will also be recorded so that information can be disseminated to a wider audience through Internews’ humanitarian radio stations or/and Boda Boda Talk Talk (BBTT) network of mobile speakers This will further ensure that accountability to affected populations is met, by engaging people that have not had the chance to participate directly to the meetings. The recordings and broadcasting of the Town Hall meetings will also give birth to specialized follow-up radio programs and live shows. The same system will be used for Mobile Responses.Activity A4: Radio Distribution Internews will procure, buy and distribute 2,000 radio sets. Priority will be given to places where there the cluster will conduct mobile responses. B. Activities in support of the CCCM cluster and partners to improve camp management operations. Activity B1: NGOs and CCCM cluster trainingsEight trainings in CCE methodologies will be conducted, each for 20 NGO staff working inside and outside the PoCs with a focus on how NGO workers at all levels can fully participate in CCE activities including in the town hall meetings.Activity B2: Feedback and Rumors Tracking Reports for the CCCM clusterIn addition to issues surfaced during Town Hall meetings, the collection of feedback will also be done through the continue collection of listening group data. Internews will ensure that the feedback loop is closed by soliciting responses from the cluster members. Activity B3: Rumors Tracking ResponseBased on Internews’ existing experience with rumor tracking, Internews will track dangerous information spreading within the camps and collective centers that can have detrimental effects for both humanitarian delivery and workers, IDPs and local communities. If rumors are deemed by camp management to potentially have a detrimental effect on their work, Internews will design immediate responses to be broadcasted using its existing radios and BBTT projects to dispel the rumors and provide reliable information on the issue. Activity B4: Coordination with the CCCM cluster at the state levelInternews will work with the cluster members to develop programming that will respond to feedback and show to residents how camp management is addressing issues that are important to them.Activity B5: Dedicated Mobile Responses support Based on activity A6 above, Internews project will support the CCCM partners through rapid information needs assessments, dedicated radio programs for the mobile responses to be broadcasted on mobile speakers (with a focus on information related to protection, PSEA, the role of the CCCM cluster and on responding to the information needs of returnees, IDPs, host communities in spontaneous sites and camp-like settlements, feedback collection that will then be used to feed back into the cluster members, including the NAWG, to close the feedback loop.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Information will be given in real-time in response to public questions in a public forum. In POC sites, such as Malakal, Internews has been working with other agencies that focus on youth and women’s programming to help alleviate the barriers to access to information experienced by these groups. Internews will rely on its established relationships with existing organizations working with adolescents and women in the POC and harness its existing youth and women listening groups to discuss who and how people should be engaged in the project. The project will use its dedicated women staff to address issues related to girls and women in the camps and make sure that their voices are heard. Regular Round table discussion in the Listening Groups are produced, where efforts are made to bring youth and women to the table to put their concerns, issues and question on difficult issues directly to the respective NGO. These round tables in particular work as ways of 'by-passing' the leadership structures and giving voice to a broader range of people. Through our Information Needs assessments we collect and analyze gender-disaggregated data to than analyze the impact of the crisis on girls, boys, women and men and what this entails in terms of information access. We work to make sure that women, men, boys, and girls can access information equally. Through its presence both at the field level clusters and in Juba, Internews works to ensure coordination and gender mainstreaming in all areas of our humanitarian operations.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Internews has led in the area of AAP through the implementation of itsHumanitarian Information Services set up specifically following this conflict inSouth Sudan. The HIS has set up an independent communications feedbackloop using more than 100 listening groups across 4 POCs, thus providing avoice for communities and a platform for discussion between all actors. On ayearly basis Internews collects more than 4,000 complaints and feedback, usingalmost 100% of them to inform agencies about services adjustments, or toprovide more information to IDPs about specific issues. On average around 60% of the feedback reported to humanitarian organizations by Internews is resolved within a month. The services actively support agencies to deliver with broader impact; reaching those without access to information and allowing them to know what services are available, who is providing them, and, in the absence of these services or a shortfall in standards, who to talk to. This mechanism has significantly increased trust between agencies and affected communities. It is the intention of Internews that the project being proposed would use this approach to support a better response at the macro-level.
