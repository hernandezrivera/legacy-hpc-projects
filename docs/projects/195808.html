Project Summary

SI is proposing to provide multisectoral life-saving assistance (WASH, shelter/NFI and
food) to IDPs and vulenrable host communities.
Firstly, life-saving WASH assistance will be provided to newly displaced people and
affected host-community through emergency water trucking, installation of water
treatment plants and distribution systems and repair of damaged water points to ensure
access to safe water, construction or rehabilitation of emergency/semi-permanent latrines
to mitigate the risk of epidemic outbreak and distribution of hygiene kits coupled with
emergency hygiene promotion.
Access to dignified shelter will then be ensured through the distribution of emergency
shelter kits in areas where local communities cannot host displaced people. Affected
people will also receive NFI kits with basic items such as buckets, mosquito nets, cooking
sets.
Finally, affected populations will receive cash transfers to cover their basic needs. Cash
can be used for vulnerable households to cover their basic needs in places where the
markets are functional. At this point it is expected that most of the MPCA will be used on
food items. In places where the markets are not yet functional the in-kind approach will be
implemented in order to support households in accessing basic food commodities.
The intervention will also look at establishing partnerships with civil society organization
and local NGOs to enhance response capacity based on their areas of expertise and
geographical locations positioning to support affected population.

1 - Needs

Clashes between the Sudanese armed forces (SAF) and the Rapid response forces (RSF)that erupted on 15 April 2023 have plummeted Sudan in an unprecedented humanitariancrisis while hampering ongoing humanitarian efforts. Fighting between SAF and RSFresulted in at least 705 deaths and 5,287 injured, according to the latest data shared bythe Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH). Over 1 million people are internally displacedwithin Sudan, according to the International Organization for Migration’s DisplacementTracking Matrix (IOM DTM) as of 21 May. Nearly 70% of the recent IDPs are coming fromKhartoum state, where displacements are triggered by active fighting and severeshortages in water and electricity. The situation West Darfur’s capital Geneina is verychallenging after all gathering points burned down leaving more than 90,000 Individualswithout access to shelter, food, water, and health and nutrition services. All the gatheringpoints in the city were completely burned down and all IDPs have fled to differentlocations, some are using governmental offices and humanitarian organization premisesas shelter. There is no precise number on the people in need for shelter and NFIs availablebut it is estimated that more than 194,593 are now displaced and in urgent need of WASH,shelter and NFIs support. WASH needs in conflict areas and areas hosting IDPs haveincreased significantly. Many WASH infrastructures have been destroyed or are inoperabledue to fuel shortages and lack of supplies. With the price of fuel skyrocketing (when it isavailable), water trucking and operational costs for fuel reliant water supply systems havequadrupled in cost. In conflict areas, access to communal water points is furtherhampered by the presence of snipers and unexploded ordnance. People fleeing theviolence often find themselves in crowded gathering places or with host families, withoutadequate sanitation facilities and access to safe drinking water. Increased pressure on thealready insufficient water and sanitation infrastructure has led to increased breakdown;long queues at the remaining functional waterpoints have led to scramble and violence atwater points, with the potential of increasing intercommunal tensions and conflict. ManyIDPs have left their homes with little more than the clothes on their back and a few basicsupplies. Lack of safe drinking water and resources for healthy hygiene practicesincreases the risk of water-borne diseases (including cholera) and vector borne diseases(dengue fever and malaria), especially during the upcoming rainy season. Furthermore,IDPs currently without shelter are in need of sanitation facilities; many families lack basichygiene items such as soap and girls and women do not have access to dignity kits..Acute food insecurity in Sudan is escalating rapidly and worsening the already highburden of food insecurity before the conflict. According to FEWSNET, the ongoing conflicthas caused a swift and unanticipated disruption to trade and market functionality,household mobility, humanitarian assistance, and basic service provision, includinghealthcare, banking, electricity, transport, and communication, has left millions of peoplefacing critical shortages of food, water, and basic supplies, including in dense urban areasand in greater Darfur, which hosts a large share of displaced and acutely food insecurepeople. The number of acutely food insecure is expected to rise to 19 million acrossSudan in the next 3-6 months. Availability of food items in the market is a majorchallenge, especially in the hotspot urban areas such as Khartoum and parts of Darfur,given disruption of the supply markets and closure of markets and limited access tomarkets that are functional.

2 - Activities/Outputs

SI plans to respond in areas affected by active conflict and those considered safe wherepeople have sought refuge. With support of global and regional logistics team,procurement through Dubai and Paris in ongoing with supplies expected to arrive in thenext few days (pending import approvals and paperwork). Considering SI’s currentoperational capacities, the initial geographical focus will be on Port Sudan and Gedaref,scaling up to include Medani, Khartoum and West Darfur once the security context allows.SI’s multisectoral intervention strategy in all areas will focus on WASH, Shelter NFI andFSL, with cash interventions as a preferred methodology for activities related to (shelter)and food security. The immediate response aims to provide life-saving services, includingwater supply, surface water treatment and distribution of emergency WASH and sheltersupplies and cash for food (or MPCA) in areas not covered by food distributions by otheractors. Considering the volatile nature of the security context, SI incorporates flexibilitywithin its programming, upon close consultation and collaboration with otherhumanitarian actors and the sector leads. The precise timeline of interventions dependson organizational capacity in each of the proposed intervention areas, administrativeapprovals and functionality of telecommunications and banking systems. Flexibility ofprogramming will also extend to programmatic considerations, adapting to needs whenthey arise, linked to the development of the conflict and population movements. SI hasbeen able to operate in Gedaref since the start of the hostilities and is able to scale upquickly thanks to ongoing grants. HR and logistics are in place for its current intervention,with a larger program team on standby. In Geneina, West Darfur, the situation is morecomplex as much of the violence is fed by intercommunal tensions and movement isrestricted based on tribal allegiance. As of 25 May, the SI guesthouse and office remainaccessible and have not been looted. Most staff remain at home, while the securitycontext is monitored on a day to day basis. Starting on 26th May, SI will ensure four dailytrips with a 30m3 truck to deliver water in accessible areas in the north and east ofGeneina. Activities as planned, include: WASH • Water trucking to newly establishedgathering points • Water point repair and rehabilitation • Water point upgrade (installationof solar panels to limit fuel dependency) • Emergency latrine construction • Hygiene kitdistribution Shelter • Distribution of emergency shelter kits (content as per sectorrecommendation) • Cash for rent (or MPCA) FSL Cash for food in priority areas not covered by food distributions. The overall implementation strategy is based on addressinglifesaving needs; delivery mechanism will be adapted to local needs and opportunities.Implementation will be scaled up progressively as the context develops, starting in PortSudan once supplies arrive, moving towards Gedaref, Medani (al Jazeera state) andKhartoum. Entry point for SI operations in new areas is water supply (treatment) andsanitation. Activities in Geneina will be managed from Chad initially and will start withwater supply through water trucking, to be complemented with WASH and Shelter NFI kits(or cash).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

SI aims to increase compliance with the PSEA Policy through offering the PSEA online training and embedding aspects related to PSEA in partnership toolkits and post-delivery monitoring (PDMs) exercises, such as questions on whether beneficiaries had to exchange anything for the service. SI staff and non-staff (e.g. daily workers) are inducted on the Code of Conduct and new successful candidates are vetted through staff in the field. Within the framework of this action, SI considers protection and GBV concerns through -among others- the design of latrines with a focus on PWD and women.SI reiterates that aid is delivered free of costs.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Complaints, Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM): The mechanism enables the target communities to provide regular feedbacks, ensuring transparency and accountability in the performed activities.The project will ensure that all beneficiaries, mindful of all types of vulnerability, are reached with the same level of information. To ensure this SI will uses different means of communication adapted to beneficiaries’ differing literacy abilities/language, i.e., group discussions, information sessions, banner and festoon. Information will be designed and translated in accurate, clear, relevant, engaging, timely, targeted and practical manner. All banners/festoon will be in English and Arabic and explaining information about complaint and feedback mechanisms. Moreover, in consultation with community SI will install complaint boxes in IDP sites where necessary All the complaint and Feedback will be analyzed regularly by the Project staff and will be used to inform for monitoring of implementation (quality, progress, outcomes), to adapt activities/approaches as needed to feedback/needs, and to respond to any complaints/breaches of policy/conduct. Within IDP site, site leaders and committee will be engaged to support this mechanism. These tools will enable SI to regularly assess the relevance and quality of services, while establishing trust and communication with the targeted communities.During the distribution, SI will set up a complaint desk for targeted community to share their feedback and complaints. The desk will be managed by SI staff who will be able to register complaints and if possible, to provide immediate answers. If not possible, SI staff will come back on later days to ensure feedback is being shared with the community on topics raised.
