Project Summary

Silk Road's WASH intervention through this project is focused on the immediate, scalable and sustained provision of WASH services to the affected population in different locations in Aleppo and Idlib governorates, where Silk Road aims to improve the overall condition of most affected people with a special focus on newly IDPs and the already existed IDPs in the targeted communities through implementing of planned activities aimed at filling the gaps and reducing the needs between the displaced and the newly displaced as follows:
1-Support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems (Targeting both Newly IDPs and host communities)
2- Distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion
3- Conducting Hygiene promotion campaign (Targeting Newly IDPs)
4- Establishment of the cost recovery system and safety plans as well. To ensure the sustainability of operation of wells after handing over the project where we will prepare and review plans and provided required training to the local councils during the period of operation.
5- Provide water testing devices, which includes portable chlorine testers, sterile sampling containers and microbiological analysis for the water authority /local council. This considers as the acritical need for us/ operators and the local councils and will allow us the control the quality of the water pumped through the water network (Targeting both Newly IDPs and host communities).
6- Support and strengthen the waste management system, which helps in the sustainability of the project after hand over to the local councils (Targeting both Newly IDPs and host communities).
7- Installation of waste bins (Targeting both Newly IDPs and host communities).
8-Distribution of water tanks 1 cubic meter and water trucking for the newly IDPs.
By implementing these main WASH activities, that will be the best solution for the current situation and in this context that can allow newly IDPs and the already existed IDPs have access to safe water through restored water systems to reduce vulnerability to preventable illnesses and improve resilience and IDPs and other vulnerable groups (disabled, elderly) have access to life-saving assistance to reduce the risk of WASH-related morbidity and mortality. Within the current situation of the targeted people in the communities and the scope of the intervention, the treatment of the pumped water will be the most effective method to secure access to safe water in sufficient quantity and quality and can ensure equitable and unrestricted access to all members of the community also Silk Road
The hygiene promotion campaigns will focus on awareness of water-borne diseases, basic guidance for personal hygiene, environmental cleanliness and the specific activities and inputs of the overall project and will aim to encourage communities to adopt safer hygiene practices while also providing the necessary hygiene items to reduce the risk of occurrence of health threats. Good hygiene in a community is achieved through the promotion of hygiene messages in complementarity with the provision of basic hygiene items to enable the communities to practice good hygiene. The proposed rehabilitation of infrastructure in this proposal will provide the best solutions to the suffering population ( host and IDPs together) because this activity will ensure that the provision of water through the rehabilitated network to everyone will be fair and there will be no discrimination against the beneficiaries. Also, we will ensure that they do not create additional risks for the affected population, also Silk Road will ensure that the health impact in this emergency WASH intervention will reduce disease risk and reduced transmission risk and it adhering to internationally recognized standards and best practices, ensuring that services are provided in line with the Do No Harm framework, Sphere standards, and humanitarian principles and that services are of the highest quality.

4 - Needs

Based on WASH assessments were conducted in June-August 2019 to inform the 2020 HNO there are an estimated 10.7 million people who require WASH assistance, where the assessments revealed that, despite the overall slight improvement of WASH conditions in Syria, vast WASH-related needs continue to exist in many parts of Syria, affecting various population groups. This includes demand for domestic water, solid waste management, community latrines, wastewater management, hygiene kits and jerry cans. WASH services and supplies to at least 500,000 IDPs accommodated within host communities – are needed. According to OCHA, from 1 December 2019 to 2 February 2020, some 586,000 people fled from their homes in northwest Syria as a result of ongoing hostilities.According to the need assessment conducted by Silk Road, people in the targeted communities live now on unsafe water trucking or closed wells to provide their water needs which are unsafe, unclean and is not regulated as well. Chlorine is not added to the tanks before sold to people and this can cause diseases, especially for kids. Many families, especially Newly IDPs, have to face difficulties in reaching the needed water where there is one private source with a high price and not available at all times. This situation seems difficult for people like widows, divorcees and people with special needs because all people depend on this source (water trucking).In the targeted locations the Displaced people, especially the newly displaced, are dispersed in abandoned houses or in rented houses and in scattered tents around the town. (They have to face difficulties in reaching the needed water and this situation seems difficult for people like widows, divorcees and people with special needs).The waste management sector in the targeted towns needs support in order to reduce the prevalence of epidemics and diseases due to the suspension of the garbage removing beside the limited number of waste metal bins and workers available

5 - Implementation of activities

The main activities of the project as follows:1 Project preparation key activities including announcing the project for the targeted community. 2 Rehabilitate of the water networks and water systems in the targeted communities (This include water pipes and high water tanks for 8 water systems) 3 Distribution of the 1200 Water tanks for the 1200 newly IDPs family. 4 Supply diesel fuel to operate the generators in the targeted locations for six months ( 11 water stations).we can operate the water stations using Alternative / renewable energy while it could be a good solution to face challenges related to supplying fuel.5 Supply engine oil and spare parts for periodic maintenance of the generators in the targeted locations for six months.6 Support water stations and water systems with needed Sodium Hypochlorite for disinfecting water for six months.7 Conducting regular water quality testing and FRC at the household level.8 Develop the cost-recovery mechanisms in the targeted communities in preparation for applying after the handing over of the project. 9 Twelve workshops each one for 2 days, technical, management, operational and Re-establish a cost-recovery system to support sustainability in the targeted communities.Conducting 10 hygiene promotion campaigns in the targeted community ( Two campaigns in each location).10 Distribution of 3000 Hygiene kits for one-off during the project.11 Support the municipality and waste management system in the targeted locations for 6 months through providing needed diesel fuel and daily workers and maintenance of dumpsites.12 Distribute of 300 new metal waste bins for the targeted location according to the need in order to ensure support the municipality and waste management systems.13 Field monitoring visits by the field team. Its purpose is to monitor progress and the works to ensure that all activities are under continuous monitoring and they are a part of the Silk Road monitoring system.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Discrimination against beneficiaries or /and sexual exploitation and abuse /Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls Mitigation measures:Staff training and use non-discrimination policies/code of conduct and involve Local authoritative bodies.Establish a strong feedback mechanismAll staff contracts to include PSEAEstablish a confidential complaint mechanism/ Address any SEA complaints2- Inappropriate or poorly planned distribution of cash and/or vouchers might expose beneficiaries to the risk of exploitation and/or anti-social use of the provided resources non-humanitarian purposes.Mitigation measures:Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set-up, postponed or cancelledEnsure that the distribution is completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylightConsider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate riskEstablish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as the disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating womenSet-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs3-Potential exposure to the explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/ rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure (water stations, towers, distribution networks).Mitigating Measure:Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared;Conduct safety training with staff and contractors.4- Unequal distribution of services inadvertently empowers or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors.Mitigating Measure:Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Organise joint planning culture and agree on the minimum standard; Include community and authorities in targeting; Ensure sensitive information is handled with care.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/materials such as generators and construction materials and chemicals such as chlorine (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities.Mitigating Measure:-MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities.Promote cost recovery systems for longer-term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership. Work closely with all partners on the ground;Promote distribution stock between multiple warehouses in the target regions (for large quantities of stock).2- Improper storage of hazardous supplies (chlorine and other) materials) - Possibility of the explosion, human casualty and asset destruction.Mitigating Measure:- Ensure proper storage of supplies with the clear provision of care and safety consideration. Make sure that clear instruction for use is available for chlorine products primarily for household use.3- Movements of garbage trucks in congested and Reliability of services. (Its probability is low and its impact is medium)Mitigating Measure:- Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding SWM services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedule; Provide household level solid waste containers/plastic bags to ease the disposal of waste from H/H to the public SW collection points. Establish clear routes and schedule for garbage trucks that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; - Potential exposure to the explosive hazard in case of support to municipal solid waste removal;4- Market system disruption. - Silk Road will monitor inflation and prices and include this risk in its contracts with service providers. If inflation rises, they will work with the donor to re-alignthe budget and program accordingly. - We will Consider using fixed prices and placing this risk in the contractor's contracts.
