Project Summary

The "Employability and Livelihoods Enhancement (ELE) Project" is a comprehensive initiative designed to address the multifaceted challenges faced by Rohingya refugees/FDMNs and the vulnerable host community in Cox’s Bazar. With three objectives, the project aims to create a significant impact by supporting skills and capacity building through non-formal technical training, and healthy manage livelihood options. Addressing the two objectives of LSDS within the FDMN, the project aims to be implemented at Camp-17. The project will assist in developing a small-scale livelihood opportunity through non-formal technical training, focusing on tailoring, and repairing gas stoves. Approximately 310 FDMN individuals will be targeted, with a specific emphasis on achieving at least 50% female participation rate. With the support and concern of camp management, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will utilize the existing CB manage facilities of WaSH at Camp-17 as a center for non-formal technical training. During the project participant selection process, CB will adhere to the LSDS guidelines, conduct community consultations and needs assessments, and seek input from camp management. Following the LSDS guidelines for CBT, approximately 20 individuals will be enrolled in each batch, with two instructors deployed for each batch. Each batch will continue for 120 hours (around 3-4 hours per day). Trainees will receive a stipend of 150 BDT per day. The project will further ensure skill development through follow-up, and referral activities. Potentially providing equipment or in-kind support to the trained beneficiaries, enabling them to pursue self-employment opportunities.
In addressing Objective-3, three types of activities will be implemented in the host community of 5 unions under the Ukhiya Upazila. As per the activities groups, the proposed activities as Investment Grant, Handicraft/Off-farm opportunities, Cash Stipend, and MPCG for Income Generating Activities. Approximately 300 vulnerable households will have the opportunity to participate, while majority percent will be women. To reduce household level vulnerabilities through enhancing household capacity is also a more important component of the project. Due to the Rohingya influx, the surrounding host communities were facing a problem of reducing their livelihood options and it was a key cause of increasing dissatisfaction and tension. So the livelihood activities directly contribute to increasing their regular income and that contributes to reducing the tension between FDMN and Bangladeshi people. Under the objective#3, the selected vulnerable HH will be encouraged to diversify their income sources and livelihood options by taking up one or more Income Generating Activities (IGA) along with accumulating monthly savings. CB will conduct the area/cluster-based community consultation meetings and conduct an assessment in the targeted area to select the most vulnerable households in need of livelihood support, particularly for vulnerable women’s headed families or single women/female youth, will be prioritized.
In both working areas, CB will address cross-cutting issues, including Gender-Based Violence (GBV), other inclusion-related factors such as disabilities, DRR, and relevant issues. In order to ensure a dignified working environment, CB will deploy both women and men staff and manage separate and accessible toilets for women, men, and people with disabilities. In applicable cases, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will set-up a child corner for the children of project participants who have no alternative option for care. Project participants will also be made aware of these issues. In anticipation of potential consequences, the project aims to bring about lasting positive change for both Rohingya and host communities in Cox's Bazar.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The living conditions of Rohingya households in the Cox's Bazar camps have shown some improvement, with 22 percent of households now able to put food on the table without resorting to negative livelihood coping strategies. This is a significant improvement from the 10 percent recorded in 2019. However, there is still a substantial portion, approximately one-third (33%), that relies on crisis or emergency strategies to meet their basic needs.In contrast, the Bangladeshi community has seen an increase in the proportion of households (29%) not using any coping strategies to secure food, reflecting an 11-percentage point rise from the previous year. Despite this improvement, it remains 10 percentage points lower than the base year in 2019. The use of stress-coping strategies has decreased from 43 to 38 percent between 2021 and 2022, indicating a slow recovery from the economic and livelihood challenges posed by the pandemic.Examining individual coping strategies, both Rohingya and Bangladeshi households commonly resort to borrowing money, with 57 and 58 percent, respectively, using this method to meet essential needs. Additionally, a significant percentage buys food on credit. One-third of households in both communities reduce non-food expenses, such as education and health, to cover food needs. Notably, a small percentage withdraws children from school, more prevalent among Rohingya households (2%) compared to Bangladeshi community households (5%).The primary driver for resorting to livelihood coping strategies for both groups remains the need for food, followed by health, shelter, and energy needs. Despite the improvements observed, the reliance on coping strategies underscores ongoing challenges.In 2022, 72 percent of Rohingya and 60 percent of Bangladeshi households resorted to borrowing money for essential needs like food, healthcare, medication, and school-related fees, as well as transportation. While this represents a slight decrease from the peak in 2021, the prevalence of debt remains high, indicating persistent difficulties in meeting basic needs.Notably, in the camps, there has been a drastic increase in households taking on debt to cover health needs, growing from 52 to 70 percent between 2021 and 2022. Similarly, the proportion of households going into debt to meet food needs has escalated dramatically from 42 to 72 percent. In the Bangladeshi community, this proportion increased from 36 to 60 percent. These findings highlight an urgent need for external assistance to help these communities meet their basic needs and suggest a critical role for interventions focusing on sustainable livelihoods and skills development.Based the above data of MSJN and REVE-6, to address these challenges, a comprehensive strategy should be devised, encompassing three key objectives. Firstly, there is a need to support the skills and capacity building of Rohingya refugees and FDMNs to facilitate their sustainable reintegration into Myanmar. Secondly, efforts should be directed at supporting the utilization of gained transferable vocational skills, non-formal technical training within existing camps and aligning them with humanitarian sector response needs. Lastly, there is a crucial need to support vulnerable host communities in diversifying vocational skills development including non-formal technical training and creating a livelihood options. By targeting these objectives, a holistic approach can be taken to address the multifaceted challenges faced by these communities and pave the way for sustainable and dignified living conditions.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) stands as one of the initial responders, actively engaged in humanitarian efforts within the camps and the surrounding host community since the Rohingya influx in Bangladesh began in 2017. CB is dedicated to delivering quality services in both the camps and host communities through effective communication and collaboration. The organization consistently communicates and coordinates with camp-level sectors, CiC offices, and the UNO to prevent duplication and resolve issues through mutual understanding. CB has been an integral part of the Joint Response Plan (JRP) for Shelter, Site Improvement & NFI, WASH, Protection, Child protection, Education and GBV from the beginning. Additionally, CB is an active member of the PSEA Network, with designated PSEA Focal and Safeguarding Focal roles within the organization.The proposed intervention seamlessly integrates into the overall response in Cox’s Bazar, where Caritas Bangladesh is already involved in various interventions within both the Rohingya and host communities. This intervention is slated for implementation at Camp-17, strategically positioned near the camp location. In these camps, CB has a robust presence in WaSH sector, further optimizing operational efficiency. With the support and concern of camp management, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will utilize the existing CB manage facilities of WaSH at Camp-17 as a center for non-formal technical training. Operating through camps will contribute to a reduction in relevant operational costs.As part of the project implementation, Caritas will ensure that project staff and volunteers receive training in the basic standards of humanitarian assistance, with a specific emphasis on the ""do-no-harm"" principle. Through these strategic initiatives, Caritas Bangladesh not only addresses the immediate needs of the affected populations but also contributes to the overall resilience and well-being of the communities it serves.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Caritas Bangladesh will actively screen and identify individuals with disabilities, including children, utilizing the Washington Group Questions (WGQ)-Enhanced Set and the Child Functioning Module (CFM). This comprehensive approach ensures that we are attuned to the diverse needs within our target population. A key aspect of our commitment is to target around 12% of persons with disabilities in the Camp and 7% in HC within the total target for the project. In order to ensure a dignified working environment, CB will deploy both women and men staff and manage separate and accessible toilets for women, men, and people with disabilities. In applicable cases, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will set-up a child corner for the children of project participants who have no alternative option for care. This intentional focus aims to promote equitable access and participation, fostering a more inclusive project environment. Recognizing the significance of inclusive programming, we are dedicated to providing sector-specific training on inclusive practices for our staff and volunteers. This training will enhance their understanding of the unique challenges faced by individuals with disabilities and empower them to contribute effectively to our inclusive initiatives.Through these commitments, our project not only aims to meet the standards of inclusivity but also strives to go beyond, actively contributing to the empowerment and well-being of individuals of all ages and abilities within our target communities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The foundation of proposal rests upon the 2023 Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (JMSNA) and other pertinent participatory assessment findings. This ensures that our project is directly aligned with the needs and perspectives of the communities we aim to serve, promoting transparency and responsiveness. CB will follow the community led approach to identifying the proper project participants as a selected dignified criterion. CB is dedicated to fostering community participation in the evaluation process. To achieve this, we will develop a comprehensive plan that actively involves community members in shaping evaluation methodologies. Moreover, the results of these evaluations will be shared with the communities in an accessible format, ensuring transparency and accountability in our project outcomes.CB will organize minimum bi-yearly consultation meetings to evaluate the project's progress. These sessions will serve as valuable opportunities for community feedback and engagement. The insights gathered during these consultations will be integral in shaping the lessons learned workshop at the working level, creating a continuous feedback loop that informs and enhances our project implementation.For AAP Effective complain and feedback mechanism is in place through setting Opinion & Complaint box in all offices, camp facilities, distribution points and majhi blocks, dedicated Hotline and e-mail account. CB conducts PDM and beneficiaries feedback survey regularly. These feedbacks are processed, and regularly analysed by the Safeguarding committee and M&E team and shared with higher authority to action & program team to re-design the programming.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

CB also follows a comprehensive Safeguarding Policy to this humanitarian context. In the unwavering commitment to the principles of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), our project places paramount importance on the safety and dignity of all individuals involved. To achieve this, we pledge to conduct mandatory PSEA training for 100% of our staff, including volunteers, ensuring they are equipped with the necessary knowledge to prevent and respond to instances of exploitation and abuse. Furthermore, our project is dedicated to fostering awareness among beneficiaries through the prominent display of PSEA materials, such as posters, fliers, and videos, incorporating key messages and information about accessible Complaint Feedback Mechanisms. These mechanisms, designed to be confidential, culturally appropriate, and accessible, will provide a secure channel for reporting concerns and feedback, thereby creating an environment where the well-being of all individuals is prioritized and safeguarded.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The programme will not have environmental issues but will ensure special attention to upholding the environmental sustainability. The project participants will get three types of awareness sessions on Household preparedness to reduce disaster risk, Early Warning Systems & Community’s responsibilities and How to protect productive livelihood assets during emergencies. The community people will also be motivated to protect the soil erosion through roadside plantation, keep their surrounding clean with waste management and reduce use of single use plastic for their daily chores. They will also be made knowledgeable and motivated not to cut trees indiscriminately and to plant trees at their homesteads. CB also closely coordinates with Energy and Environment Technical Working Group (EETWG) under ISCG in order to address all the issues related to environment and receive guidance on current and new approaches. CB will give priority to preventing environmental pollution in the camp and HC.
