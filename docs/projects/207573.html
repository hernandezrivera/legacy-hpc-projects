Project Summary

UNHCR’s strategic vision is to provide services following the public health approach through the integration of health and nutrition services to foster access and a continuum of care for beneficiaries as per UNHCR’s public health strategy and nutrition sector strategy. UNHCR through its partners in collaboration with UNICEF and WFP will implement comprehensive nutrition programmes focusing on treatment and prevention services with the overall objective of reducing the impact of acute malnutrition amongst Rohingya refugees and improving the nutrition wellbeing of children and women. These services will be implemented in 19 integrated nutrition centers across 14 camps and two stabilization centers which will be integrated with primary health care facilities. Treatment programmes will be implemented following the community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) which entails community outreach programmes to ensure timely identification, referral to the respective nutrition programes such as stabilization centre (SC) outpatient therapeutic feeding programme (OTP) and targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP) .Preventive programmes will be strengthened through the blanket supplementary feeding programmes, growth monitoring and promotion (GMP) and infant and young child feeding (IYCF) programmes through group sessions and individual counselling. Additionally, UNHCR will scale up focused support to children aged under five months and their mothers through the community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM). Based on monitoring and staff capacity assessments, continued capacity building and mentoring of partner staff on key nutrition programmes, as well cross cutting issues, are required to enhance mainstreaming of nutrition programmes with other sectors. In terms of community engagement, partners will continue to strengthen community participation through community dialogues with block leaders and mahjis, as well as fathers of children supported through various nutrition programmes. To achieve the overall objective of improving the nutrition situation among children under five years, collaboration with other sectors (protection, health, WASH, food security and livelihoods and education) will take place to enhance collaborative efforts in the mainstreaming of humanitarian components and ensure a multi-sectoral approach in addressing the high malnutrition levels. UNHCR will lead the implementation of the annual nutrition surveys following the Standard Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) methodology and support other nutrition sector assessments. In 2024, UNHCR and partners will strengthen programme monitoring and make relevant adjustments to ensure the implementation of high-quality programmes and accountability to the affected population. An increased focus on the implementation of nutrition-sensitive programmes, disability inclusion and AGDM approach in all nutrition centres will be facilitated in collaboration with other relevant sectors. UNHCR continues to support the Government in strengthening its localization strategy through the engagement of a national NGO and is working towards building its capacity to implement comprehensive nutrition programmes and increase their coverage in the future. The Cox’s Bazar camps are regularly affected by monsoon rains, cyclone threats and fire outbreaks. To ensure the uninterrupted provision of life-saving nutrition services, UNHCR will coordinate with the Nutrition Sector and partner on emergency response plans, ensure timely establishment of mobile nutrition teams (MNTs) and provision of adequate supplies and funding for partners to rehabilitate nutrition centers.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The malnutrition situation in the Rohingya refugee camps remains very high as per the UNICEF/WHO classification of acute malnutrition. As per the 2022 Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) the overall prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) among children aged 6-59 months comprised 12.3%, while prevalence of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) comprised 0.7%. Despite the reduction compared to the previous year, a significant caseload remains of children suffering from moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), which requires attention to prevent a further deterioration to the severe categories. Chronic malnutrition (stunting) comprised 41.2% which is above the 30% critical range, with anaemia remaining above the public health threshold among children aged under five years. Anaemia prevalence among children aged 6-59 months was above the critical public health threshold of >40% and was remarkably high among children aged 6-23 months with a prevalence of 72% in Cox’s Bazar. Some of the factors contributing to the high levels of malnutrition include poor sanitation, vulnerability to infections and poor maternal and childcare givers. As per the most recent Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) assessment, 28.7% met the acceptable minimum diets, while only 63% of children aged 0-5 months were exclusively breast fed. There have been significant developments in the UNHCR Bangladesh since the beginning of 2023, ranging from the impact of natural disasters (cyclone Mocha in May) and a reduction in funding across different sectors. The funding gap is foreseen to have an impact on the humanitarian services provided to the refugees as well host communities living near the camps. The food security sector is the most severely impacted, leading to a reduction in food vouchers from 12 USD to 10 USD implemented from March onwards, with a further reduction to 8 USD at the beginning of June. This is predicted to significantly impact the food security and nutrition status of refugees, as well as their level of general wellbeing. Programmes to improve the nutritional situation among children under five years will be strengthened by partners, as well as through increased collaboration with other sectors to address high malnutrition levels. The nutritional needs of older adults and other vulnerable categories, such as adolescent girls, medical cases and persons with other specific needs will be considered and supported as per sector and UNHCR guidelines. UNHCR will ensure funding and technical capacity to support the capacity training of nutrition staff and volunteers to ensure implementation of nutrition services as per existing protocols in order to achieve a high quality of care and overall programme outcomes. UNHCR and its partners will strengthen programme monitoring, making adjustments as necessary to ensure that the implementation of high-quality programmes and accountability to the affected population is maintained. There will be an increased focus on the implementation of nutrition-sensitive programmes, disability inclusion and age, gender and diversity mainstreaming (AGDM) approach in all nutrition centres. UNHCR will continue to support the Government in strengthening its localization strategy through the engagement of one national NGO by expanding coverage. A total estimated 148,681 beneficiaries (66,545 children aged under five years, 30,962 adolescent girls and 17,479 pregnant and lactating women) will be supported in the UNHCR-funded nutrition programmes as per the sectoral needs analysis.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UNHCR is a member of the SAG and nutrition technical working group meetings where the staff actively particpate in the coordination and support in developement of sector and development of guidelines. Nutrition programmes have been planned as per the outcome of the 2020 nutrition sector rationalization of nutrition programmes. UNHCR will continue to strengthen the integration of health and nutrition programmes through one partner implementing both services in the camps where feasible, whlie also ensuring the increased collaboration of health and nutrition partners at the camp level to provide a continuum of health and nutrition services to refugees. In 2024, further rationalization will take place in the stabilization centres, shifting from standalone centres to integration in primary health care facilities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection partners implementing UNHCR-funded programmes will ensure protection mainstreaming in all components to ensure the "Do No Harm" principle is achieved in service delivery to all beneficiaries receiving support through the integrated nutrition centres. Assessments of the partner’s understanding of key protection principles and mainstreaming will be jointly implemented by UNHCR's protection and nutrition units. Training on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), gender-based violence (GBV), child protection and referral pathways between nutrition and protection in the camps will be carried out to ensure the necessary staff capacity to support the refugees to access services in a dignified manner. In addition, other components identified as potential protection risks will be monitored and mitigation strategies identified. Regular monitoring of protection-related issues will be ensured throughout project implementation, as well as necessary actions to prevent GBV experienced by women, girls, boys or men receiving assistance through nutrition programmes.

3.3 - Age & Disability

UNHCR is committed to preventing child abuse and exploitation of refugees in its nutrition programmes. All partner staff will be trained on child safeguarding principles in order to understand the standards and conduct expected from all staff and volunteers working in the nutrition centres. Messages to improve the prevention of any form of abuse among the beneficiaries of all ages, boys and girls aged under five years, adolescent girls and women of reproductive age will be included in health education and community engagement sessions in order to increase awareness.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to the affected population will be ensured throughout the project cycle, and participation of the refugees in the different stages of programming through consultations will be implemented. UNHCR will ensure that partners establish feedback and complaints mechanisms in all the centres with a clear plan to address any issues that arise in an integrated manner. This will also include the provision of different methods of communication with refugees, who will be engaged in awareness raising efforts with regard to the different programs, new programmes or any changes that may have an impact on their access to services and the overall nutrition status of the beneficiaries supported in the nutrition programmes. UNHCR will monitor the feedback and complaints mechanism put in place by the partner to ensure that they adhere to the acceptable UNHCR standards, including capacity enhancement of all INF focal points to keep the AAP mainstreaming as a continuous monitoring point (with support from AAP focal point in UNHCR).

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. UNHCR is committed to reducing the negative impact of some elements of its nutrition programmes on the environment through sensitization and training of nutrition staff and volunteers on climate change principles. UNHCR will emphasize the need for proper disposal of nutrition packaging for the purpose of reducing pollution and improper disposal in the camps. In addition, monitoring of the non-use of polythene bags in line with the directives of the Office of The Refugee Relief and Repatriation (RRRC) will be ensured, while all partners will be supported with adequate funds to procure jute bags or alternatives to carry or store nutrition supplies. In 2023, UNHCR solarized 10 out of 19 nutrition centers and will continue this initiative in 2024 in line with funding available.
