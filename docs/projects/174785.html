Project Summary

According to Humanitarian Response Plan, 2020 an estimated 3 million people have been affected among which 679,000 IDPs and 204,000 returnees. Currently, IDPs live in crowded, unhygienic and deplorable conditions in communities which have relative peace thereby further aggravating their already deteriorated physical and mental state. Many have lost their means of livelihood, official documents such as birth certificates, school certificates, passports, identity cards and others either in property destructions or hastened fleeing from confrontations which heighten the risk of arrest, limit their movement, and possibility to engage in gainful operations. There is also an observed increase of abuse towards women and children in the conflict regions. More children are born from rape cases and are not registered in the civil status and this has long term consequences. Also, vulnerable persons such as PWDs, especially women and girls face a triple jeopardy as a consequence of their gender, economic situation and disability. According to crisis reports, persons with disabilities are up to four times more likely to suffer in times of disaster while children with disabilities face higher risk of abuse and neglect, and women with disabilities are at increased risk of sexual violence. UN Security Council’s Resolution recognizes the serious concern regarding the disproportionate impact of armed conflict on persons with disabilities. In the same vein, older people constitute a significant and growing number of those affected by humanitarian crises. Generally, they remain in crisis zones and most often are forgotten in programing and implementation of humanitarian interventions.
While there are target killings affecting both older and younger men, women and children are directly affected as there remains an increase in the number of women and child-headed households. The crisis has broken the protection niche in most families.
In response to the huge needs in these regions, in collaboration with religious bodies, community leaders, Disabled persons Organizations (DPOs) and Community-based organizations (grassroots structures), CBCHS will address protection needs of most vulnerable persons in the affected regions. Through this project, the CBCHS will engage relevant government and local council structures to facilitate the acquisition of civil documentations, ensure the protection and economic empowerment of individuals. Using advocacy strategies, CBCHS will cause local authorities to speed up the procedure for the establishment of civil documentations, and commit to the respect of the rights and dignity of people. Through awareness raising in communities, members will be more informed and victims of various abuses will be more willing and assisted to access justice. This project will also ensure that most vulnerable persons identified receive protection assistance such as food and non-food items. These will include old people, pregnant and lactating mothers, and people with disabilities who have been left behind. To ensure long term impact, the project will provide sustainable economic empowerment opportunities to PwDs especially women and girls by training and providing them with start-up capital. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to save for business expansions through the effective and evidenced based Village Saving and Loans model. Communities will be trained to adopt and use the C-ban concept (Village Saving and Loan schemes). These schemes are evidence based to promote financial freedom to survivors and simultaneously enhance social cohesion. A project monitoring, evaluation and learning component has been integrated; using Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) and targets including Accountability to Affected Populations. This will provide a rich source of evidence, successes and lessons which will be analyzed and used to inform future initiatives, nationwide advocacy for greater recognition and better support to conflict victim

1 - Besoins / Needs

Humanitarian needs have steeply increased since the beginning of the Anglophone crisis. This has led to a gap between the needs on ground and the available response. In order to bridge this gap, humanitarian actors have developed a humanitarian needs overview (HNO) to back up specific needs of different groups affected by the crisis. While there remains immense needs of displaced persons, the needs of vulnerable groups such as PwDs, the old, pregnant/nursing mothers and children become enormous. PwDs especially women and girls with disabilities are unable to evacuate crisis zones to safer towns and villages because of bodily, environmental and systemic limitations. With limited or no economic activities, most of them have lost their means of livelihood and access to basic necessities including food, water and shelter. These may culminate in death for some of these vulnerable persons while women and girls suffer from rape, end up with unwanted pregnancies.In light of the above, the project will deliver the following interventions bearing in mind the principles of humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence. Sustainable livelihoods measures in emergencies are critical in responding to the quality of life of displaced persons. Many of the communities affected in both project area are largely comprised of farmers. Life becomes challenging for these people in host communities as they have to start afresh from barely nothing. Many IDPs who were engaged in economic activities have also lost their capital and even assets thereby increasing their vulnerability to engage in illegal activities to sustain them. Some of them especially young men have engaged in armed activities, kidnapping for ransom as a means of livelihood. On the other hand, vulnerable persons left behind, barely have means of sustenance. This has led to malnutrition and health hazards causing girls to succumb to survival sex.In order to handle this, the project in collaboration with community-based structures will:• Identify and assess needs through community agents and religious groups. • Do a proper need assessment to support eligible beneficiaries. • For livelihood sustainability, beneficiaries shall receive cash vouchers after training. They will be introduced to C-Ban concept. This approach has been tested in some communities in the NW, West and Adamawa regions with amazing outcomes. • Identify and empower vulnerable women, men, girls, PWDs, aging, returnees, pregnant and lactating mothers on livelihood skills • Select and train local religious authorities and community volunteers to identify and refer those in need of official documentations. • Carry out mass awareness raising campaigns on the importance of civil documentation through mass media, social media and print, churches, schools and other public gatherings. • Engage and strengthen the CBC prison ministry for timely identification of persons arbitrarily arrested and or detained and link them up our legal services and partners to enable them access justice. .• Support those who have been evidently abused to access legal and justice services. • 120- Religious leaders trained on the identification of those in need of documentation services within the communities. • 30 awareness campaigns carried out on the importance of civil documentation • 44 Community volunteers trained, are identifying and / facilitating the re-establishment and establishment of civil documents. 2500 vulnerable persons (old people, retirees, PwDs, children born without birth registration; women, men, boys and girls who have lost their official documents)• 500 vulnerable persons received vocational training• 100 detainees arbitrarily arrested accessed justice.
