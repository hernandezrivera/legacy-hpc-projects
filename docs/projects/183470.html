Project Summary

This proposal is in line with the objectives and the framework of the OPTI country 2022 HRP strategy under objective 1: The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL), while duty-bearers are increasingly held to account, objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL and objective 3: The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome the protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
Furthermore, this project is aligned to the WASH cluster HRP Objective 1: In accordance with international humanitarian law (IHL) and international human rights law (IHRL), Palestinians living under occupation access safe and adequate WASH services with dignity, objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinian and objective 3: Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks.
The project's overall objective is to contribute to strengthening the capacity of essential WASH services for the vulnerable population affected by the ongoing crisis in the Gaza Strip, while the specific objective of the project is: To provide an appropriate, integrated WASH response for vulnerable populations in Gaza Strip to enhance sufficient access to WASH services provided to women, children, girls and boys in fragile and critically under-served communities. The objectives are in line with the needs identified in the humanitarian need overview (HNO) and Oxfam's recent assessments.

1 - Needs

WASH vulnerabilities in the Gaza Strip have been driven by the ongoing blockade, Israeli restrictions, the internal Palestinian divide, the COVID-19 and the May 2021 escalation, along with the fiscal and socioeconomic consequences of these factors. Due to the limited capacities of the local authorities and the Israeli restrictions on the entry of materials, the WASH service providers are still struggling to fully restore and maintain the functionality of WASH services affected during the May 2021 escalation in the Gaza Strip. The WASH Cluster partners’ assessments and consultations identified four main key issues generating people’s need for WASH assistance: insufficient water supply to households, poor sanitation and solid waste services, limited WASH services to public facilities, and risk of flooding.According to HNO 2022, there are significant shortages of adequate sanitation facilities at the household level. More than 6,000 households in Gaza are suffering from limited access to adequate household sanitation facilities. They are adopting several coping mechanisms, including sharing latrines with other families and using shelter spaces, other than bathrooms, for handwashing or bathing. Such practices generate privacy and protection concerns, particularly for women and girls.With regard to institutions, at least 162 schools and health care units in Gaza Strip suffer from insufficient WASH facilities to meet the standards set up by the Ministries of Education and Health. This results in overcrowding the existing facilities and undermining hygiene practices, all of which raise particular concern due to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. Almost 94,000 Palestinians reside in low-lying areas at risk of flooding events during winter due to heavy rains and overflow of storm water facilities and sewage pumping stations. And recently, due to climate change, flooding has become more frequent and have caused significant losses of assets, disrupted access to livelihoods and services, and exacerbated the risk of waterborne diseases. Women bear the brunt of the hardship in flooded homes, as they are expected to continue fulfilling their domestic duties under these adverse conditions.Based on Oxfam's WASH strategy 2021-2025, Oxfam's gender analysis (July 2021), Oxfam's rapid needs assessment carried out in September 2021, WASH cluster Vulnerability Map updated in August 2021 and coordination efforts with the WASH cluster areas focal point, PWA, CMWU, Ministry of Health (MoH), Ministry of Education (MoE) and Oxfam's local partners, WASH interventions at community, HHs and institutions levels were identified under Oxfam's 2022 HRP plan along with flood mitigation and prevention actions as per below: 1- Community-level: CMWU and Oxfam agreed to prioritise 15 critical public WASH facilities (9 wells, 3 reservoirs and 3 wastewater pumping stations) that serve eight vulnerable communities in the Gaza strip (Qizan 'abu rashwan, Jafarawi, El Sakka, Aslan, Irmilah, Al Hara Shrqiea, Al Salam and blocks 6,7) with average domestic water supply less than 70 (l/c/d). Accordingly, PEF carried out a technical field assessment in September 2021 to identify the specific needs of each facility. In addition, 8 FGDs (4 women and 4 Men) were conducted in the targeted communities with 64 participants (21 women and 43 men), confirmed their need to rehabilitee the prioritised WASH facilities to ensure adequate access to WASH services which would improve care responsibilities and house chores for women and children as they bear the brunt of having access to WASH services at the level of their HHs. 2- HHs level: Following a discussion between Oxfam and MoSD, PWA, AAH, WeWorld-GVC, IR, and Save the Children in Sep 2021. Households with outdated sanitation facilities in Aslan community in Bait Lahia El-Zaytoun in Gaza were identified to be targeted under this intervention. Accordingly, Oxfam, YEC and PEF conducted a rapid assessment on 18th August 2021 where 5 FGDs (3 women and 2 Men) were carried out in the targeted communities with 32 participants (19 women and 13 men) and 2 KIIs conducted with the local community committees representative. Out of the 5 FGDs, 3 FGDs were conducted with Women Headed HHs, PWDs and Down's syndrome people (2 females and 1 male). The assessment highlighted the need to improve access to adequate water and sanitation services at HH level for 250 families in the targeted communities, 40% are Women Headed HHs, PWDs and Down's syndrome people. The assessment also found that the majority of the population have poor hygiene practices, especially in terms of water handling, which increases the risk of COVID-19 infection. Moreover, Oxfam's gender analysis (July 2021) indicated that women are mostly affected by the lack of access to safe water and sufficient sanitation services, reporting serious concerns about their children's hygiene, health and safety. WASH needs were prioritised to improve adequate water and sanitation services at HHs, including distribution of HKs and Hygiene promotion awareness.3- Institutions level (Health care facilities and Schools): Considering the WASH in HCFs Vulnerability Mapping and WASH in schools assessment conducted by the WASH cluster during the 3rd quarter of 2021. In August 2021, Oxfam, PEF and YEC, in coordination with the MoH and MoE, conducted in-depth assessments for Seven health care centres and four schools serving nine vulnerable communities with insufficient and inadequate WASH facilities. The assessment illustrated the need to intervene in the seven identified Health care centres and the four schools by maintaining domestic and drinking water networks and sanitation items, rehabilitation of desalination units, adaptation work to meet the needs of females and PWDs relevant and rehabilitation/upgrading of toilets.

2 - Activities

This project will respond to the needs of targeted male & female beneficiaries in the targeted areas, including children, the elderly and people with disabilities as the rehabilitation and maintenance work for the public WASH facilities, the rehabilitation and upgrading of WASH facilities at HHs, schools and HCFs will improve the access of targeted people to sufficient WASH services and will contribute to alleviating the burden on women and children. The complementary hygiene promotion and public awareness campaigns will enhance gender and age responsiveness by enhancing women's knowledge and awareness, including COVID-19. The floods mitigation and prevention actions will contribute to strengthening WASH preparedness and response capacity. While developing the policy brief on access restrictions and needs of the WASH sector in the Gaza Strip will promote access to safe and adequate WASH services with dignity. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle. This is particularly relevant in the Gaza context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative protection norms. Explicit plans to reduce risks and competently mitigate and respond to any incident, including safeguarding training (i.e. on sexual harassment, discrimination and exploitation, and child protection) will be elaborated by Oxfam and monitored, adjusted, refreshed if the context changes. Oxfam will be responsible for the implementation of the action in partnership with PEF, YEC and relevant stakeholders to ensure access to systems, procedures and standards to achieve effective and high-quality delivery. Oxfam's particular added value will be in its ability to bring together multiple stakeholders, local authorities, end-users through its active participation in the WASH cluster and engagement with related stakeholders, ensure action delivery in accordance with the agreed action plan and budget in compliance with the donor programmatic, visibility, reporting, financial and procurement guidelines. Oxfam will be responsible for the development and dissemination of the policy brief related to access restrictions and needs of the WASH sector and will provide advice and support on gender and protection-sensitive project implementation and gender mainstreaming, support PEF and YEC in designing and implementing the gender and hygiene promotion plan. PEF and YEC will lead the implementation of the community mobilisation actions, the beneficiaries' selection process, the technical engineering assessment, and announce the tenders in local newspapers in compliance with the donor, Oxfam and their organisation's procurement regulations and guidelines.YEC and PEF work closely with vulnerable communities in diverse locations, including farmers, women, children and youth. They have extensive experience in WASH and access to sustainable and safe water, sanitation services in the Gaza strip. YEC and PEF were fully engaged in the project's design, needs assessment and community consultations. The two partners will implement the project in coordination with Oxfam with joint responsibility for M&E activities and stakeholder engagement. Also, PEF will be engaged in the implementation of outputs 1.1, 3.1 and 4.1. In contrast, YEC will be engaged in the implementation of output 2.1. Output 5.1 will be implemented directly by Oxfam. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing Oxfam's humanitarian interventions. Oxfam premised its project design on the 'do no harm approach' ensuring that project activities or any resulting consequences do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential WASH needs, which would have put them at risk, had it not been available, and by conducting ongoing monitoring and collecting beneficiaries' feedback to be aware of any potential harm and work on prevention measures. The action responds to Oxfam's and PWA's needs analysis where women highlighted the need to be engaged in community committees and municipality activities due to their severe lack of representation in public life. Community mobilisation sessions on gender, water and sanitation will be implemented, encouraging participation of men and women community leaders and mentors. These sessions will also serve as an awareness-raising tool to sensitise households and the community to accept women participation. This will contribute to mitigating any risk/ harm that women could face as a result of this newfound participation in public life. Gender will be mainstreamed, ensuring that the needs of men and women, girls and boys, are addressed in line with Oxfam's gender in emergencies minimum standards and gender mainstreaming strategies. This action's response is grounded on the fact that all women and men's lives and dignity, including people with disabilities, youth, children and elderly, both direct and indirect, must be protected. Key humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle, particularly important in the Gaza context, where social and political instability is evident in many areas with conservative gender norms. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks or exclusion at the community level. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured by conducting orientation sessions to inform and consult with targeted communities about services that will be provided and safety measures and access modalities. It is worth mentioning that preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence and coping with stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women's self-esteem and confidence will be positively reflected and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in the overall improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As part of the main principles of protection mainstreaming in all of Oxfam's humanitarian work, special attention is paid to achieve equal and impartial access to aid. This will be done throughout the different stages of the project; the selection of targeted communities is premised on concrete and updated needs assessments confirming the intervention's vulnerability and need. Similarly, selecting the beneficiaries follows a clear process with selection criteria and presents an equal opportunity for all eligible to benefit. Gender and age are also taken into consideration, meaning that women, men, youth, elderly people, people with disabilities and children will be able to benefit from the intervention as fit for their needs without any discrimination based on their gender, age or status. Using a combination of community outreach approach, virtual platforms for information sharing, and actual work in the field will also address any obstacles related to the spread of COVID 19 that could hinder access to aid. Having a feedback mechanism available and accessible for all the targeted community will facilitate knowing people's concerns and comments that could highlight obstacles of access, whether physical or related to the processes of implementing the intervention and would inform the response to ensure equality and impartiality.
