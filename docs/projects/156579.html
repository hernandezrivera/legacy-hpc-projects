Needs

While approaching the ninth year of the crises in Syria, the estimated number of people in need of humanitarian assistance reached 11.7 million people including 5 million in acute need as of the end of 2018 . The number of internally displaced is 6.2 million. Near 1.4 million of people returned to their homes. According to UN estimate half of the population have food insecurity. The protracted crises continued to shed its negative impact on the socio economic conditions in Syria. According to ESCWA estimate more than 80 per cent of the population is living below the poverty line . Millions are deprived of their basic rights including access to proper shelter, food, health services and livelihood opportunities. All that increased the risk of engagement in harmful coping mechanism. The most recent UN population estimate shows that 33 per cent of the total population is between 10 and 24 years representing 6,037,216 million young people, out of which 4.1 million are between the ages of 10 and 19 years .The situation for Syria’s youth population is dire. According to the 2018 ERL severity scale analysis half of the population is estimated to be in acute need of access to basic and social services and to livelihoods and income generating opportunities. 55 percent of Syrians have no jobs and 73 per cent of young Syrians aged 15-24 are unemployed. In the absence of decent job and livelihood opportunities, more people living in Syria are resorting to harmful coping mechanisms including gender based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse and early and forced marriage. It is estimated that over 15 percent of the active Syrians are now engaged in the illegal sector and violence economy for a living, such as smuggling and trafficking. The crises has led to a significant change in the role of women in society, many women became the only bread winners in their families. UNFPA is committed to invest in youth, and emphasizes the rights of adolescents and the need to increase the potential of adolescents and youth to contribute to positive social transformation. UNFPA is mandated to address the needs of young people that are considered lifesavings for their future wellbeing, it is imperative to develop projects that will contribute to empower young people and keep them away from harmful practices that affect their decisions, health and future. Through the proposed project, UNFPA will invest to empower youth, especially young girls aiming to enhance their resilience, livelihood opportunities and social participation through two main interventions: building the capacity of young people to take the lead and the responsibility to identify and prioritize their needs as well as the community needs; and through building the capacity of young people, more specifically youth female headed households on business startups and provision of seed funding to support them in leading entrepreneurship projects. The project will be implemented in Dara’a, Hama, Aleppo, Latakia, Der Ezzor and Rural Damascus. The communities selected were based mainly on the severity scale and on the fact that they are priority return destination, they are de escalation zones and accessible to our IPs. The project will be implemented through partnership with local NGOs.

Implementation of activities

Civic engagement Carry out 16 training workshops to develop the capacity of 400 young people on needs assessment techniques and prioritization in the field of civic engagement. Provision of financial assistance to support 160 youth led initiatives  Coaching and mentoring through field visits to monitor the project in the various geographical location Monitoring the project through the provision of support to personnel mainly one part time project coordinator and one part time finance assistant. Entrepreneurship Carry out 16 training workshops to develop the capacity of 400 young people on business startups Provision of seed funding to support 160 entrepreneurship projects. Coaching and mentoring through field visits to monitor the project in the various geographical location Monitoring the project through the provision of support to personnel mainly part time project coordinator and part time finance assistant

Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow the Do No Harm and Do Good approaches. Agreement will be made with the implementing partners (IPs), that the project will have to be initiated ensuring that young people will be selected in a transparent process with the involvement of the concerned authorities from the initial kick of phase of the project till its evaluation. More so, it will also be agreed, that in case of any signs of escalation, both UNFPA and the IPs will be consulting with the local communities on how to proceed further with a convenient response. Based on the results of the consultation, the project activities will be shifted to meet the new requirements. In what concerns the limitation in availability of resources and response capacities, the project will closely involve the local communities, will indeed follow mitigating measures in terms of 1) assessing the needs, 2) doing prioritization and 3) ensuring equal access to those in needs. Such results will be discussed and reviewed by the project committee specially formed for this purpose. Finally, the IP will have to share on regular basis the achievements of the activities and report on them.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The major risks might be encountered during the project implementation:• Denial of access to the selected project site due to sudden security situation. The implementation will be shifted to another area. • Interventions might contribute to increase tensions between host and displaced communities. The project design will take into consideration that participants are selected covering IDP, returnees and hosting community.
