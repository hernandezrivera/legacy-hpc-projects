Needs

According to OCHA, The total number of people estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in Sudan in 2018 is 5.5 million, an increase of 0.7 million compared to 2017. The figure for 2019 is still not released but, People to receive aid in Sudan for 2019 according to the GHO) may exceed 4.4 million.Access to WASH in Sudan suffers chronic underdevelopment throughout the country and is further exacerbated to the extent of survival for people in the areas affected by conflicts. In Darfur, intermittent conflict and inter communal tensions continue to impact civilians, notably women, children and Elders, and also prevent the return of displaced people to their areas of origin. In some areas access has improved as in BN, access to other areas, including where there is active conflict, remains restricted.According to the 2018 HNO, about 5.5 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance in Darfur. About 36,500 South Sudanese refugees arrived in Darfur in 1st quarter of 2017, bringing their total number in the region since December 2013 to about 121,300 people. South Kordofan state has been at the centre of an armed conflict between GOS and the SPLM N since June 2011. The ongoing conflict drove thousands of civilians from their homes which put additional pressure on host communities to share limited resources and increase the vulnerability of the affected girls, boys, men and women. Gender and protection will be a priority for this project. All activities will be gauged against the 'Do No Harm' criteria to ensure they do not exacerbate vulnerabilities of communities and particularly of women, girls and boys. This project will follow the SHF 'Accountability to Affected Populations' guidelines for protection. The project targets 29,404 women, 23,012 men, 38,354 girls and 37,075 boys with safe water, sanitation and hygiene promotion services. Project targets beneficiaries who are clearly defined in terms of gender and age (women, men, boys and girls) ,the project will contribute to gender equality in the following ways:- Promoting and strengthening the role of women in the management of water and sanitation facilities. Previously low proportions of women were meaningfully involved in the water management committees, yet the water burden is mostly shouldered by women due to the fact that in Sudan, culturally, it is the role of women to fetch household water. - Taking deliberate step not only to ensure inclusion of at least 50% of women in the water committees, but also empower and encourage them to meaningfully participate in decision making processes on issues pertaining to WASH in their communities.- Lack of adequate sanitation facilities affected women and girls. Women and girls also face risks of GBV when defecating in the open or having to walk long distances to latrines. WV will consult with women and girls in the project area, and support construction of latrines at locations that are secure for women and girls.

Activities

1.Improved water quantity: Construction/rehabilitation of water infrastructures: Construct/rehabilitate water points. Location of the water points decided in consultation with girls, boys, women and men.2. Sustainability of water service: Water related training & O&M of water facilities: Establish/strengthen WASH committees to oversee water resources in their areas. Ensure inclusion of at least 50% of women in the water committees also empower and encourage them to participate in decision making on issues pertaining to WASH in their communities. Each of the areas targeted for new water points/rehabilitation of existing water points will have a committee established/refresher trained on management of water sources & systems within their areas. Train hand pump mechanics to repair hand pumps3. Improved defecation facilities: CATS / CLTS will help the targeted communities understand that negative impact of OD and the contribution of OD to morbidity particularly the spread of AWD and the need for a collective approach to stop OD. The project will give highest priority in targeting the female and children headed households4.Sustainability of defecation facilities: Sanitation related training5. Improved environmental health: Solid waste management: Developing collection and disposal system & building landfills. Consulting & educating users (girls, boys, women and men)6.Behavioral change through hygiene promotion: PHAST, CHAST, Hygiene related training: Carry out hygiene & sanitation behavioral change campaigns (PHAST, CHAST & CLTS) at targeted areas by CLTS & CATS using Designing for Behavioral Change methodology. The campaigns will happen parallel to the CLTS & CATS. It will be facilitated by the Hygiene Promoter & community volunteers & coordinated with WASH committees. Menstrual hygiene management & menstrual hygiene waste disposal to increase access to absorbents & encourage safe handling & disposal of used absorbents supporting adolescent girls’ health & dignity.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Different environmental care activities will be implemented according to the type of intervention, whether it is an emergency, rehabilitation or development context. In emergency contexts, mitigation and preservation measures will be prioritized over recovery ones, unless the population is at risk of disaster linked to their local or neighboring environment. • Environmental Preservation activities will be implemented whenever: - WASH activities may negatively impact on the environment and mitigation activities would not prevent or delay more urgent life-saving activities. - An initial Environmental Impact Assessment points out short or mid-term risks of negative environmental impacts on human life, worse than risks supposed to be tackled by usual WASH activities. • Environmental Protection and Recovery activities will be implemented whenever: - The population is at risk of disaster linked to their local or neighboring environment.
