Project Summary

The increasing requirements for long term livelihood support cannot be overemphasized in North East Nigeria. According to the Humanitarian Response Strategy, 2019-2021, the sustained humanitarian assistance and service delivery,, many communities have been stabilized and number of people are gradually returning to some of the communities declared safe. With their return to destroyed communities, there are expectations on their part for support to rebuild their lives.
In many areas across affected states, trade routes and market have been cut off with negative impacts on the people. The high rate of unemployment in Nigeria is also not helping matters.
To further maintain the efforts made at sustaining relative peace and ensure that the situation does not deteriorate and people do not slip back into crisis, this project seeks to identify the opportunities available in communities and build the skills to adequately explore those opportunities. This is to help the returnees, IDPs and host communities develop potentials and be self reliant. The project targets 2615 community members between the ages of 15-35 for various skill acquisition in Borno(Monguno and Hawul LGA), Yobe (Nangere and Fune LGA) and Adamawa (Michika and Hong LGA) communities of North East Nigeria.

1 - Needs

A number of people have lost their sources of livelihood since the outbreak of violence by the Boko Haram insurgents in the North East and largely depend on aid from humanitarian actors. Beneficiaries of such aid most times use very little of what is received and then sell some of the items to get money for some which meets their needs.It is gradually becoming a routing to depend heavily on this easy way of life. The skill acquisition is meant to equip them to build themselves and gradually reduce high dependency on aid. The project also seeks to equip the youth who are the target of the project with an alternative to enrolling into Boko Haram or some other violent group, it therefore seeks to reduce idleness to the barest minimum. Emergency support has been going on for quite some time and sustained, gradual sustainable interventions in communities is needed to allow the IDPs and host communities cater for themselves . Trainings received can never be lost. The skills will provide an alternative to business or trade that cannot be allowed to go on due to security situations in some communities such farming. The skill acquisition will promote entrepreneurship will give room for employment which will alleviate poverty.

2 - Activities

1. Needs Assessment/Baseline Survey will conducted to ascertain the training needs of the target beneficiaries, how the needs will be addressed and prioritize the needs to enable adequate response. The baseline survey will be conducted to determine the situation of the target before during and after intervention to be able to assess the impact of the intervention. This activity will be conducted at the beginning of the project by a commissioned consultant. 2. Community entry, this will address sensitization/awareness, identification and selection of beneficiaries and Volunteers, Planning meetings. This activity will be carried out through meetings by the staff of the organization to create awareness on the project to the key stakeholders on the communities selected, this will help point JDPH in the right direction on identification and selection of beneficiaries and the volunteers that will assist with implementation after being trained. 3. Identification and engagement of Trainers. This will be done through expression of interest which will be assessed for selection and then engagement following the procurement policy of JDPH 4. Training of Volunteers. Trainings on monitoring tools and methods of distribution, registration of beneficiaries, will be offered all our Volunteers before they can help in the implementation. 5. Skill acquisition trainings. This will be done in batches. There will be 3 batches of trainings for 3 weeks for the short term skill acquisition, while the long term skills will take 3 months for each batch. The training centres will be in the communities to avoid adding burden of movement to the beneficiaries. 6. Supply and distribution of items. After the trainings, well performing beneficiaries will be supported with machinery, tools, kits and take of grants. This will be done in a small ceremony with key stakeholders present. 6. Follow up and Monitoring. This will be done during the project each month to ensure project objectives are being achieved based on indicators, manage feedback from beneficiaries and effect necessary changes to ensure a successful intervention. 7. Soft Trainings: This will be a 5 day training a batch of beneficiaries on skill acquisition each day on book keeping, leadership and saving/lending schemes This is to equip them with basic knowledge on how to manage a successful business and how they could through that build others up. In all these trainings, JDPH'S best practice of mainstreaming gender and peacebuilding into all projects will also be done to ensure beneficiaries are properly equiped to allow room for gender balance and manage conflict within and outside their business/communities well. 8. Post Distribution Monitoring: This will be carried out by staff and volunteers of the organization to determine outputs and outcome of the intervention. 9. LGA Coordination/Sector meetings. There will be sharing, of experiences and learning at these meetings with other NGOs, INGOs, donors and the public to help improve project output and outcome. This will be done monthly by staff on the project. 9. There will be Auditing of the project .
