Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve access to safe water and sanitation services for the most vulnerable communities and families, including children, in Area C in the West Bank. The interventions will contribute to reducing the targeted communities WASH vulnerability with a focus on children and persons with disabilities (PWD), especially females, as they are considered the most vulnerable and more in need of these critical services. Also, the interventions will target unserved and underserved communities with essential WASH servicers protecting the most vulnerable families under threat of forcible transfer. The interventions will focus on improving the communities’ access to 1) adequate and safely managed water services; 2) sanitation and sewer services and 3) WASH facilities in schools and health facilities; 4) safe and adequate water in underserved communities, including schools and health facilities; 5) increase the storage capacity of the unconnected households. All interventions will be implemented in parallel with the provision of hygiene materials and hygiene awareness sessions in schools, health centres and communities.
The project interventions were developed based on WASH cluster eligible actions for HRP 2022 and targeting the most vulnerable communities according to WASH 2020/2021 vulnerability mapping.
UNICEF and its partners will adopt several implementation methodologies that consider the targeted communities’ locations, proposed response technical standards, and UNICEF’s accessibility and partners to these communities. Therefore, UNICEF and the partners will adopt and consider beneficiary/community implementation mechanisms for household interventions such as construction/rehabilitation of household WASH facilities and cisterns. Implementation through suppliers/contractors will be adopted for WASH facilities in schools , health care centers and water community water infrastructures activity.
Moreover, the proposed interventions will consider the targeted groups’ socioeconomic, protection and COVID-19-related vulnerabilities. UNICEF will target the communities under the risk of demolition and forcible transfer in Area C, the families under the poverty line, and the communities highly affected by the COVID-19 outbreak. The proposed interventions will improve the targeted groups’ capacities and resilience to deal with the aforementioned challenges.
UNICEF and its partners will carefully consider the special and persistent needs of women, children, elders and PWD. Thus, these vulnerable groups will be consulted during the planning, design, and implementation of the interventions. Also proposing sufficient response of these groups that consider the different social and traditional characteristics in the targeted communities. UNICEF and its partners have developed a complain system and tools. UNICEF is strengthening Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in program by systematizing commitments in agreements with all implementing partners. UNICEF supports the partner in strengthening and mainstreaming systems for Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and AAP in their strategies. It provides training on PSEA and AAP approaches, particularly on community engagement, complaints system, and feedback mechanisms. UNICEF has also initiated a new project to digitalize AAP-PSEA mechanisms in the country office. As part of the UNICEF framework on AAP &PSEA the office had designated two complaints handlers. The complaint email address, in addition to the Interagency Hotline (managed by WFP), will be promoted as community feedback channels to reflect the views of marginalized populations, including stakeholders and partners throughout the program design cycle to feed the information received from beneficiaries back into programme and ultimately encourage community engagement .

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO, more than 1.35 million Palestinians suffer from limited access to WASH services, of which 690,000 are living in vulnerable communities in the West Bank. Most of them also suffer from other protection, social and economic challenges such as restrictions in Area C, high poverty rates, and the increased number of COVID 19 cases. In the West Bank, the inadequate water resources and water infrastructures affect the access to water for many Palestinian communities. The water losses on the water infrastructure in the West Bank reach more than 40%. According to WASH cluster vulnerability mapping, more than 690,000 persons in the West Bank suffer from a low water consumption of less than 50 I/c/d. These families are obliged to adopt several negative coping mechanisms to satisfy their water needs, such as purchasing expensive water resources. Considering that access constraints in Area C pose challenges for the water trucks to reach remote localities, this is usually coupled with a subsequent increase in the final cost of water up to 40 NIS/m3. UNICEF and its partners have conducted WASH vulnerability analysis in 24 communities and 17 schools, and 34 health facilities. These schools, health facilities, and communities are suffering from limited access to WASH services, about 408,670 persons, including 9,788 students within the age of 7-17 years old, and 386,605 persons visiting the clinics are either not connected to water networks, their water consumption is less than 50 I/c/d or using inadequate WASH facilities.WASH services in schools and health facilities are key components to provide a healthy and conducive learning environment and eliminate disease transmission from health facilities. In coordination with the WASH cluster partners, the Ministry of Education and Ministry of Health targeted around 250 schools and 55 clinics with WASH services. Yet, the need is still high. This results in students overcrowding around the available facilities and the associated protection, privacy, dignity complications. The low water consumption affects the daily hygiene practices, and the lack of sufficient sanitation facilities affects the students’ willingness to attend schools. Women and girls are also suffering in two dimensions: 1) low quality of life due to restricted access to drinking water and subsequent obstacles to adopting hygiene practices and safe menstrual hygiene management, especially with the existing threats related to the COVID-19 pandemic, 2) lack of sense of safety due to the long distance between households and water sources, where they might be exposed to harassment and violence. The situation was worsened by the frequent movement restrictions in response to the COVID 19 pandemic. In Area C, many communities are affected by the restricted Israeli construction regime in the area. More than 33 incidents occurred against 51 WASH infrastructures in Area C during 2021, which affected access to water and sanitation for many households who depended on these infrastructures.

2 - Activities

The proposed project will deal with the source of WASH vulnerability in communities, such as the unavailability of water and water storage capacity and inadequate WASH facilities in households, schools, and health centres. UNICEF will keep coordinating with relevant authorities at all phases of this project to ensure that the communities and families are targeted based on actual needs and vulnerabilities as a result of multisectoral approaches together with other key sectors and following the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and other official assessments. UNICEF and its partners will organize meetings with stakeholders to coordinate the activities and the locations to avoid overlap. UNICEF and partners will also ensure acceptance of interventions by the beneficiaries and the satisfaction of quality of works implemented in the areas of interventions. Local councils in the targeted locations have been identified as partners for data collection related to the baseline and post-project. UNICEF will lead the advocacy efforts towards key stakeholders and the International Community, linked with the provision of the following WASH intervention implemented by partners in full coordination with relevant authorities:- The rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities. This activity will include three components 1) rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities in Area C. 2) provision of household latrines for two communities in Area C 3) promoting hygiene and sanitation practices with gender and protection mainstreaming integrated. Through this activity, it is expected to reach 493 persons (Men 149, Women 143, Boys 75, Girls 72, adults males 70, adults females 67, elderly males 5, elderly females 5, people under poverty line 196, PWD 5)- Hygiene promotion awareness campaigns in all the targeted communities reaching 12,000 persons. The themes addressed will be based on assessing the commujity practices, HH hygiene, and how to maintain household hygiene practices. - The construction /rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 17 schools for 9,788 students in Hebron and Bethlehem governorates. This will include construction/rehabilitation of new sanitation facilities, latrines, and water fountains facilities in targeted schools and/ or co-educational schools for students aged between 7-17 years old based on Ministry of Education standards for school water and sanitation facilities. - Hygiene awareness campaigns in the 17 targeted schools. Through campaigns and sessions, handwashing, MHM, waste management, and other themes will be addressed to the students in coordination with the MoE scope of work and public health program for schools.- The construction /rehabilitation of WASH facilities (handwashing, latrines, sewer system) in 34 HCFs for around 386,605 persons (men 196,554, Women 89,065, Boys 98,773, Girls 94,666, Adults males 92,465, Adults females 88,170, elderly males 5,316, Elderly females 6,229, people under poverty line 74,459, PWD 6,341) This will include construction and/or rehabilitation of new sanitation facilities, latrines and water facilities in the targeted health facilities, this will have a reflection on the level of services and reducing the risk of disease transmission, keep the dignity of the health team and the patients visiting these health centers. - The provision of environmental and personal cleaning kits for the targeted34 HFC to improve the personal and environmental hygiene status in the health care facilities. - Installation/ rehabilitation of water transmission pipelines, networks, and HH connections in 11 communities benefiting 26,870 persons in Hebron and Bethlehem governorates serving (men, 5111, women 4,904, boys 2,650, girls 2,539, adults males 2,335, adults females 2,225, elderly males 126, elderly females 140, people under poverty line 1982, PWD 152). The water networks will be connected to the municipal water distribution system. Therefore, the targeted communities will improve their access to water.- The rehabilitation of 3,000 sewer collection networks in the Hebron H2 Area serving 1200 people ( 612 men, 587 women, 320 boys, 306 girls, 31 elders, 18 PWDs). This will be implemented by contractors or municipalities. - Construction/ rehabilitation of household water cisterns for 21 households in Hebron and Ramallah governorates cisterns will be considered for vulnerable families with special attention to female-headed households and families with disabled members in the targeted Area with a focus on Area C. A socio-economic baseline survey will be conducted including female-headed households. This will be implemented by contractors or village council members and beneficiaries and will build upon their knowledge of the Area and the potential risks involved. It is expected through this activity to serve 158 persons (80 men, 77 women, 41 boys, 39 girls, 37 adults males, 36 adults females, elderly 2, elderly female 1, people under poverty line 55, PWD1)- Conducting technical capacity building training for teams in schools, clinics, municipalities, and village councils in addition to service providers.

3 - Indicators

UNICEF and its implementing partners will monitor the works progress and quality during implementation. This will be ensured through regular field visits to the sites, report tracking, evaluation and if needed assessments. Staff will monitor progress against agreed indicators and verify the validity of reported data through review of means of verification. Focus group discussions will ensure separation of women and men to create an open environment where women are free to discuss key issues. field visits and water analysis will be conducted to supervise, monitor, and evaluate the activities. The programmatic visits will also assess bottlenecks, barriers, challenges, and lessons learned. Also, UNICEF will follow what PSEA claiming methods been used by the partners and its effectiveness. This is in accordance with UNICEF’s requirement for Harmonized Approach to Cash Transfer (HACT). Financial spot checks will also be conducted in line with HACT requirements. The proposed intervention is expected to improve the overall protection of the targeted communities through improving their access to basic WASH services to sustain livelihoods and avoid/respond to displacement, support the WASH advocacy strategy, and improve the coordination platform. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through the establishment of a beneficiary feedback mechanism. 9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNICEF is strengthening Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in programmes by systematizing commitments in agreements with all implementing partners. All UNICEF partners have been assessed on their risk to SEA and given access to a capacity-building program, which is continuous this year. UNICEF is implementing a project to strengthen the capacity building of 12 national NGOs on AAP & PSEA. The work includes the development of AAP & PSEA Context Analysis, capacity building, and AAP/ PSEA system strengthening. UNICEF SoP has also initiated a new project to digitalize AAP-PSEA mechanisms in the country office. UNICEF has expanded support to the SAWA hotline to be able to handle SEA claims at the interagency level, which includes referral of claims among agencies. Awareness and communication actions have started to progressively publicize the PSEA system and the right of affected populations to the complaint.Specifically for this project, UNICEF and partners dedicated hotline for complaints will be accessible by direct and indirect beneficiaries throughout the duration of the project and up to 6 months after the end of the project. All complaints/feedback are reported to the Monitoring and Coordination manager by field staff, project managers, and the appointed focal points. If the complaint requires follow-up, the complainant will receive an answer within the given time. Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the monitoring, and coordination manager analyses complaints and categorize each of them according to the internal classification. Particular attention will be given to vulnerable groups. They will be informed on the complaints mechanisms. Teams are obliged to inform and explain to all direct and indirect beneficiaries, stakeholders on the presence of complain Response Mechanism (CRM) and referral pathway/ mechanism for their complaints.
