Project Summary

The MPCA Program will give individuals and families the freedom to make autonomous decisions in prioritizing and meeting their immediate needs, which vary from one family to the next. The MPCA Program will contribute to the attainment of outputs 2.2 of pillar 3 of the UN Strategic Framework 2022- 2025, in addition to the Strategic Objective 11 of the Humanitarian Response Plan 2023. As the beneficiaries need to have safe and meaningful access to the market and financial service, areas with active conflicts, areas with movement restriction such as camps, and extremely remote areas will not be considered for MPCA Programme and will only receive in-kind assistance. In determining the targeting criteria, UNHCR proposes criteria that are known to be correlated to socioeconomic vulnerability, widely accepted by the population, culturally appropriate, clearly defined, and objectively verifiable. Capitalizing UNHCR and partners’ years of experiences and investment in multi-sectoral assistances centered around Community Centers, UNHCR tap into families registered at Community Centers whose vulnerability has been assessed by trained partner staff. Partners will analyze their database to generate a “long list” of potential beneficiaries based on the above-mentioned criteria.
In areas far from the Community Centers, UNHCR and partner will reach out to communities with severe socio-economic vulnerability and apply the same criteria to obtain a “long list” in consultation with the community members and the local authorities.

PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Strategy
UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria to establish a framework that emphasizes the "do no harm" principle. PSEA is a key focus for UNHCR, with an emphasis on staff training and partner assessments. Failure to meet standards may result in the discontinuation of partnerships. UNHCR remains committed to safeguarding children's welfare, prioritizing child protection through assessments, referrals, and partnerships with child protection entities. Collaborating with UNICEF, UNHCR ensures the provision of essential services for children through a coordinated referral system.
Targeting
[NWS]
Vulnerable individuals are identified using a score card approach which takes an intersectional view of vulnerability, giving weight to various needs of particular groups, such as female headed households and disabled individuals, and of varying conditions, such as shelter type and length of displacement.

MPC will help them address their basic needs to mitigate their protection risks that vulnerable households face. UNHCR’s Community Centers have a repository of vulnerable households in the community. In the absence of multi-sectoral needs assessment, UNHCR will apply categorical targeting, using age, gender and disability specific needs criteria such as households with persons with disability, female headed households, and elderly at risks.

Transfer Value, payment modality, and market information [NWS]The MPCA program in NWS targets vulnerable IDP and host community households in Azzaz sub-district in northern Aleppo. In accordance with the guidance from the NWS Cash Working Group, identified recipients will receive $100 for eight months, after which an assessment will be done to determine whether payments should continue. Cash will be delivered in hand at designated distribution sites following identity checks. The MPCA pilot conducted in 2023 and early 2024 validated the functioning of markets and ability of recipients to access necessary goods to meet their basic needs. [GoS]The MPCA program in GoS targets vulnerable IDP, returnee and host community households in all GoS governorates except Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa. Beneficiary identification will be mainly done through UNHCR’s Community Centers in each area. Identified recipients will receive two instalments of 360 – 720 USD depending on the household size (an estimated average of 500 USD) with a 3-month interval, considering the large number of beneficiaries and capacity of the financial service providers. The transfer value is set below the CWG’s recommendation taking into account the feedbacks on the ground from the recent earthquake response that we should prioritize reaching more households than to assist the same households with more substantial amount, in view of the widespread poverty and potential tension in the community. UNHCR will closely communicate with the CWG co-chairs and members particularly when the updated transfer value recommendations are determined. Cash transfers will be made through a money transfer company contracted by UNHCR. Through desk review of available information including UNHCR’s CBI PDM, catchment areas of UNHCR’s Community Centers seem to have functional markets. However, UNHCR will continue to monitor the market information as well as liquidity and access to financial service throughout the implementation process given the volatile situation and fragile market. Monitoring and communicating with the communities. For both areas, monitoring will be conducted by on site visits by UNHCR followed by post-distribution monitoring. It is also important to note from monitoring and risk management perspective, that UNHCR has directly contracted the financial service provider responsible for deliver of the cash, while another partner is responsible for the identification and communication process. UNHCR also validates all beneficiary and payment details throughout the process. Complaints and feedback mechanisms such as hotlines, outreach volunteers, complaints boxes, social media, among others, will also be used for monitoring purposes.
