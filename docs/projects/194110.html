1 - Needs

Acute and chronic malnutrition are problems of public health significance in Sudan for the past several decades. The drivers of malnutrition are multi-sectoral that include high income poverty, poor WASH conditions leading to epidemics, limited access to health services, illiteracy and high food prices contributing to inadequate food intake and dietary diversity at household level. The number of people in need (PiN) of humanitarian assistance has increased from 15.8 million, estimated in November 2022, to 24.7 million in May 2023, representing a 57 per cent increaseDue to Clashes between SAF and RSF (have been ongoing since 15 April), particularly in and around residential areas in Khartoum. Several cities have faced heavy bombardments and clashes, and associated unexploded ordnance, causing hundreds of civilian deaths and casualties, as well as damage to critical water, health care and power infrastructure. The fighting has also reached West Darfur, South Darfur, North Darfur, South Kordofan, North Kordofan, Blue Nile, Kassala and the Red Sea. Since 24 April, hostilities between SAF and RSF have ignited intercommunal violence in West Darfur, particularly in the state capital of Ag Geneina, resulting in numerous casualties and displacements, and widespread looting. The indiscriminate attacks on civilian infrastructure and the looting of homes are clear violations of International Humanitarian Law (IHL) , in addition to inappropriate feeding practices and cultural norms affect children’s growth and development from an early age. Displacement either due to floods or inter-communal clashes continue to be one of the main drivers of malnutrition as it disrupts affected communities’, access to health and wash services and therefore increasing risks to disease outbreaks and food insecurity in limited access to farming land, availability, and utilizations of food. The ongoing currency inflation has reduced purchasing power implying increased families with food insecurity and therefore malnutrition risks. Moreover, Humanitarian crisis tend to increase the nutrition related protections risks especially among young children that might engage in child labor, early child marriage that increase risks of being malnourished besides, funding constraints may cause temporary suspension of over 52% of the TSFP programs. In addition, the 18% increase in number of localities in severity scale 4, will likely contribute worsening of acute malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies among the under-fives, pregnant and lactating women in Sudan. The humanitarian nutritional needs in Sudan are projected to remain at elevated high levels or worsen further in 2023, making Sudan one among the top counties in the World with highest estimated acute malnutrition caseload. While the overall Global acute malnutrition (GAM) remains at 13.6%, the prevalence of GAM varies significantly across the localities with very high situation (GAM above 15% and above as per WHO classification) reported in 64 localities. These localities include four where 2022 SMART surveys conducted. Five of the 64 localities have GAM prevalence above 30%, classifying the situation as catastrophic (HNO 2023).To mitigate and prevent morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition, AMVO will targeting 52,250 severe acute malnourished under five without medical complications out of 275,200 in need to reach with other sector partner over 83% of the total over 690,000 i ne will target also 69,863 moderates acute malnutrition five out of 2.44 million in need (HRP2023). Severe malnourished children prioritized since they have 11 times higher estimated mortality risk compared to their well-nourished peers. The immediate life-saving nutrition needs that Include Outpatient treatment and in-patient treatment of severe acute malnutrition, treatment of moderate acute malnourished under-fives and global acute malnutrition among PLWs) in prioritized localities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated through the entire project management cycle. Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated in the activities to ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. Key environmental concerns will be built into the project in order to reduce deforestation in Sudan. Almanar will attempt to switch away from fired bricks to alternative construction methods such as stabilized soil blocks (SSB) or concrete blocks (CSB) depending on the prerequisites of the region. Environmental mainstreaming will be included during monitoring and evaluation of the project outcomes of the response to ensure that the effects sought was indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time in order to adjust activities accordingly. It is a necessary part of ensuring that the principle of do no harm is fulfilled. Furthermore, to help identify best practices and lessons learnt that offer valuable insight for making improvements in future aid efforts. Almanar will work with local NGOs, CSOs and communities including working with the national and state governmental authorities, since Community consultation and participation is a key part of environmental management.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Almanar through coordination with the local government authorities and humanitarian partners will incorporate protection principles with DO NO HARM and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity in the implementation of this project by avoiding causing harm preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the project activities which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Protection mainstreaming is one of the most significant factors, which is increasingly recognized as an integral component of UN programming, is the gender equality and protection of girls and women. Inequalities between the sexes typically reflect, and can increase, vulnerabilities of pregnant and lactating women and girls, may face different protection risks, have different needs and choices, and possess different skills, knowledge and coping strategies. The project activities, all throughout the execution, will aim to ensure community participation through the project cycle, capacity and vulnerability analysis, reduce the level of exploitation to women and promote protection of girls in communities and families with the support of legal and traditional protection systems. The project will use and advocate for Minimum Standards of Protection package amongst the targeted communities all through the project implementation. This project through the referral pathway between the two targeted sectors will ensure that women, girls and boys victims of S/GBV, elderly, disables and their families and guardians are fully aware of the project interventions in 4 targeted States. Almanar will ensure the served locations are safe and accessible to all without discrimination, and ensure that the beneficiaries receiving services in dignity and confidentiality.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In the all stages of the project the communities are kept engaged and involved through community dialogue meetings, project dissemination. Accountability will be ensured across the activities in this project through establishment of complaints and response mechanisms at project level aimed at ensuring that the voices of the affected populations will be heard. The established nutrition and health community committees and community leaders networks including volunteers will facilitate community engagement at all levels of the project including planning, management, monitoring and evaluation.These committees are expected to be gender and ability sensitive with the inclusion of both males, females, elderly and people with disabilities to ensure that all voices, including those of the marginalized are heard, equality and equity are realized with regards to access to services that will be provided by the project. In addition, AMVO will ensure that all staff/volunteers are trained on PSEA policies and procedures which are in line with UN's protocols. The Complaints handling mechanisms will be established in the targeted area and fully inform the community about the complaint mechanisms, their purposes and use, and what constitutes a complaint to allow people confidentially report their concerns and with developing monthly tracker to ensure that communities complaints/feedback are comprehensively analyzed and responded to with the beneficiaries getting feedback on a monthly basis. Project staff will ensure adequate consultation with the communities to ensure that existing complaints handling mechanisms are incorporated into the complaints and response mechanisms to be established by the project. I.e.(complain box, a complaint committee or direct complaints to project staff members trained to act as “focal points,” receiving complaints sensitively and without judgment, and ensuring the complainant’s safety.
