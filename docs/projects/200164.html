Project Summary

Lebanon has been grappling with the worst economic crisis in the country’s recent history since late 2019. Poverty has dramatically increased, prices have skyrocketed, services are becoming increasingly unavailable. There is a huge number of people in need of assistance across all population groups. However, compared to the Lebanese nationals, migrants are in an especially vulnerable position. According to IOM report issued in October 2022, there are an estimated 135,499 migrants in Lebanon, majority of them female. The vast majority of them reside in Beirut/Mount Lebanon. Out of the total number of migrants currently in Lebanon, 60% are considered in urgent need of specialized protection assistance. This includes shelter support, access to documentation, legal aid, psychosocial support and/or basic household expenditures. Within this project, Insan will provide migrants with awareness sessions on legal and other services, legal assistance, case management (including MHPSS), case management, other types of limited assistance and referrals, cash assistance and NFIs and offer temporary shelter. Furthermore, Insan will provide awareness raising for service providers. By providing awareness sessions and legal assistance, the migrants will be more aware about the Kafala system, less exploited by employers, able to advocate for their rights and more knowledgeable about services available to them. By providing cash and NFIs Insan will help them live in dignity. By providing emergency shelter they will be less prone to be abused and to be engaged in illegal activities. By providing case management, limited amount of other assistance services, MHPSS and referrals to other service providers, they will be more resilient to adverse shocks, have improved mental health and self-esteem which will lead to improved social cohesion. By training the service providers, they will become more aware of the migrants issues and hopefully more tolerant and outspoken for their rights in Lebanon, as well as increase access to services for migrants. The proposed project thus responds directly to these most pressing needs of the migrants residing in Lebanon and will therefore be a life saving intervention until a more sustainable solution becomes available. The total number of migrants who will benefit from this project is 1,490. Additionally the project will train 50 service providers. Insan has been working with migrants since it was established in 1998 and has been providing them with holistic solutions through case management combining education (primarily for the children of migrants who are unable to attend official school due to the lack of residency documents), awareness raising (mostly on migrants' rights), psychosocial support, legal assistance (counselling, legal mitigation and court representation), medical care, repatriation (in most severe cases) as well as temporary shelter, cash assistance and NFIs. Insan also participates regularly in the UN migrant sector meetings in order to stay up to date with the latest developments in the field and provide necessary input. Moreover, Insan conducts advocacy and lobbying for migrants' rights in Lebanon and in the wider MENA region through the Cross Regional Center for Refugees and Migrants (CCRM) that it established in 2017 and where it currently holds the secretariat. This years long experience of Insan in the migrant sector will provide an added value to the project beyond doubt and help to guarantee that the services provided will bring along the best possible outcomes for the migrants.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Insan has a PSEA policy that all the staff members, consultants and volunteers have to read, agree and sign before starting their employment. Insan also holds frequent PSEA refresher trainings for the staff and has PSEA awareness banners in all its offices and centers that serve both the staff and the beneficiaries. Insan is an equal opportunities employer, meaning that it employs its staff members without discrimination of race, nationality, gender, religion or other such characteristics. Insan also assists its beneficiaries based on their vulnerabilities and doesn't discriminate based on ethnicity, nationality, gender, social origins, religion and political or other affiliations. However, Insan does put a greater focus on assisting women and children as they are generally in a more vulnerable position compared to men.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

All Insan's staff members, consultants and volunteers have to abide by the organisation's policies that help to ensure accountability. The project design relies heavily on beneficiaries’ needs. Insan has been working with migrants for over 20 years and is always in close contact with the communities. Throughout the project all beneficiaries are encouraged to provide honest feedback to Insan. They can do so by speaking to the staff members directly, sending a message through social media channels or by leaving their comments anonymously in the feedback boxes available at Insan's centers. At the end of the project, a feedback survey/focus group discussions will be conducted as well. Beneficiaries can also file complaints, in which case the HR department will carry out an investigation and determine an appropriate solution.
