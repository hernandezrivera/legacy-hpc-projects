Project Summary

As per DRM-ATF update, agriculture was an important sector in Tigrai before the outbreak of the conflict last year as more than 85 percent of the population were living in rural areas and depend directly or indirectly on agriculture for their livelihoods. The current conflict has negatively affected Tigray rural communities. Farm families have been displaced; food stores and animals have been lost, looted or destroyed. In Oromia and SNNP region the significant yield failure in targeted districts has been linked to poor and erratic rain patterns during previous seasons as well as insecurity problem. This in turn and has negatively impacted the food security of the target population, increasing malnutrition among children under five and pregnant and lactating women. A rise in the prices of staple foods, deterioration of livestock body condition, a decline in livestock prices as well as unusual migration by the population between districts have left the population in a difficult situation.
This emergency support project is a 6-months response to assist Ahsea, Edaga Arbi, Keyeh Tekli, Kola Temben and Ahferom woredas in Central zone , tigray region. In addition MCMDO will provide the six months response assist for Oromia and SNNP region. The intervention aims to ensure that farmers affected by the conflict suffering is reduced through the provision of agricultural inputs (seeds) in the targeted areas in order to enhance the food production, animal health, IGA support and cash transfer interventions to the most vulnerable population of the Tigray, Oromia and SNNP regions.
The objective of this 6 months intervention is to contribute to the restoration of resilient livelihoods for 30,000 HHs in Tigray five targeted Woredas and 9,000HH in Oromia and SNNP region. This submission is part of the 2022 HRP to address the recovery needs of farmers affected by the war/conflict and drought.
MCMDO proposed interventions are in line with the cluster objectives that focuses on humanitarian assistance with elements of recovery, specifically through the provision of inputs such as seeds, animal health, cash and IGA supports.
In collaboration with key figures of the community, MCMDO will develop the beneficiaries' selection criteria. The selection criteria will be verified and approved by the community. Agriculture and nutrition are integrated since strengthening agricultural productivity will improve household nutrition and essential for reducing food insecurity. Agriculture and nutrition share a common entry point. Food is also key outcome of agriculture activities. Veterinary drug will be distributed to each beneficiary and will help to improve survival of core breeding animals from current drought and support animal health for vulnerable HHs.
MCMDO has practical demonstrated experience in emergency seed, animal health, cash and IGA support projects in similar situations. It has implemented the same project in oromia and SNNP Regions West Guji and Gedeo zones respectively with the support of OCHA and FAO. MCMDO has got different lessons and learning from previous similar projects which help to implement this project successfully. Moreover, MCMDO is operating in Tigray Region targeted woredas implementing emergency nutrition and health projects for conflict-affected communities which can be considered as an opportunity to integrate among the projects. It has well-established and strong partnerships with community representatives. This is also a major input for the successful execution of the project activities. It doesn't 'harm the climate and is used to promote natural resource.
MCMDO has a regional office in Mekelle, Tigray, and field offices in ten woredas of Tigray region. MCMDO has a long-standing partnership with the regional and Woreda government offices. Currently MCMDO is implementing similar project supporting farmers to get emergency seeds with the support of OCHA and FAO.

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

As there is an acute shortage of cash in Tigray, Oromia and SNNP region cash transfer will be provided and that will save lives and enhance food security and local natural resources to vulnerable households. CVA is deemed the preferred modality by communities and will contribute to recovery of local markets, mitigate negative coping strategies, and improve income for vulnerable HHs. Thus, PLWs, PwD and other vulnerable groups will be provided with multi-purpose cash transfer as they do not have land, working power to plough the land as well as to get improved crop or vegetable seeds. Unconditional cash assistance will be mainly used for expenditure related to food-staple and fresh food consumption items, and enable access to either health facilities, or basic non-food items, whilst also smoothening household food consumption and cover cash needs and contributing to the protection of household assets.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Response are designed considering the real needs at the community level. MCMDO conducts quick assessments to identify the scope and types of humanitarian responses needs. The assessment is a joint assessment which involves the local government, sector offices, the community representatives, affected communities and other key stakeholders. MCMDO assessment teams use standardized templates to capture the existing response needs. Key informant interviews, Focus Group discussions, document review and site visits are some of the basic tools used to capture the key information to design the response. MCMDO also conducts a discussion before the design of the response with the sector offices to discuss and agree on the strategies, activities and targeting. Using these inputs, MCMDO compiles the information and produces assessment reports which are used as a sole information to design the responses for resource mobilization. As resources are committed by donors, MCMDO submit the response to the regional or zonal stakeholders for appraisal. The stakeholders review and sign the project agreement as the project is designed jointly and reflects the primary needs of the affected communities. The response is aligned and harmonized with the existing government and other actors plans to avoid duplication of efforts and realize complementarity to meet the wider need of the communities. MCMDO advocates the stakeholders and the community to contribute resources including resources in cash, in kind, technical support or other forms of contributions. This helps the response to be sustainable and the beneficiaries and the stakeholders to ensure the ownership of the response outcomes. During the implementation of the projects, the stakeholders, community representatives and other actors engaged during the design stage will be part and parcel of the implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages of the response. MCMDO invites the government counter parts to periodically review the performance and achievements of the response, provide feedbacks and capitalize the best practices. The beneficiaries and community representatives lead the implementation of the activities at community level. They are engaged in Beneficiaries selection & verification, delivery of response/support, modalities, efficiency, effectiveness and relevance of the response. Moreover, MCMDO will design and implement compliant and feedback mechanisms at grassroots levels which helps to ensure the accountability to the affected Populations (AAP). Such systems will be reviewed periodically and assess their effectiveness as well as relevance to the targeted communities. Thus, the targeted beneficiaries and the local stakeholders will make meaningful participations and lead the response to contribute for the realization of the planned objectives and achieve wider impacts on the lives of the beneficiaries.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Beneficiary accountability and feedback mechanisms is one of MCMDO quality standards reflected as a cross-cutting issue throughout its programmes. MCMDO has engaged the targeted beneficiaries and local actors in all stages of the project. In this response, there is a witnessed challenge to conduct an assessment and engage the affected communities due to the humanitarian challenges. The project will establish robust feedback mechanisms to get feedback, complaints and other information from the community. This will be confidential and appropriate channel. Since the communities served by the project have different levels of understanding and awareness, multiple feedback mechanisms will be established. Suggestion boxes, color codes, free hotline and email accounts will be established and beneficiaries will be informed about theses mechanisms to deliver their grievances/compliant, comments/feedbacks. Each challenge will have its own focal person or committee to review the feedbacks and provide appropriate response which includes correction measures, provision of additional information or continuous discussions as needed. Continuous discussions will be held to address the issues raised from the community in a way that addresses their concerns. On the other hand, local governments and community representatives will be part of the committees regarding accountability and compliant mechanisms. This helps to improve the quality of the project implementation.
