Project Summary

Due to the ongoing protracted crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon over 44,000 schools remain closed with more than 600,000 children out of school, with over 270.000 in need of psychosocial support services.
As the conflict persists, these children are increasingly exposed to so much harm - more cases of sexual violence, harassment and forced early marriages accompanied by unwanted pregnancies are being reported. Majority of those girls are pregnant and/or HIV positive, while they do not have access to medical care, including pre-natal care, due to the lack/loss of civil documentation (i.e. ID card or birth certificate) to go through checkpoints to access health facilities/services. On the other hand more cases of adolescent boys have been exposed to recruitment into non state armed groups and child labour.
Family separation is one of the major concerns identified in this crisis, with at least 2500 children either unaccompanied or separated from their parent(s), most of whom are reported to be living with other members of their communities or left alone in child-headed households in deplorable conditions in rural, peri-urban and urban where they are involved in various forms of child abuses especially as the crisis persist and get worse in rural communities in the NWR where schools remain closed since 2017.
Proir to the commencement of the ongoing academic year the Government of Cameroon embarked on a ‘’Back to school’’ campaign, which involved education key stakeholders, elites in government, administration, CSOs, Faith Based Organisations and international organisations/INGO. This gave parents/guardians hopes that schools would resume. Ironically all these campaigns were inadequate to counter persistent separatists’ school boycot/closure enforcement. Presently, in the NWR formal education is accessible only Bamenda central town where security is tense and Misaje/Nkambe Towns in Donga Mantung Division, while all schools remain closed elsewhere in the region.
While communities’ needs/interest for implementation of various humanitarian services have received attention, despite low and slow, the campaign for children’s education remains zero positive in most parts of the North West and South West regions since 2017. And, while the communities remain volatile children/adolescents continue to be the most affected and hopeless.
Challenged by this humanitarian context on education of children and adolescents Green Partners Association (GPA), Bamenda, launched a Non-Formal Education programme last July 2019, targeting out of school and unschooled children 5-17 years living in conflict affected hard-to-reach communities in the NWR. Even with limited resources we now run 48 centers with over 6000 children, in 13 sub-divisions.
Our successes are based on our relations/partnerships with church denominations (Full Gospel Mission, Apostolic Church, Baptist Church, Presbyterian Church), which have put at our disposal their Sunday School Halls for classes and pastors to supervise the centers and provide Moral and Spiritual support to the children.
Remarkably UNICEF/CRF (Children Radio Foundation) are already partnering with us for the implementation of the Radio Education Programme for 5,000 conflict affected children.
While our original focus was to provide only alternative learning opportunities we have been challenged by new experiences and empirical evidence from the field that despite operating in non-formal setting the programme cannot create reasonable impact without providing psychosocial support, WASH, basic health care, school feeding, infrastructure, birth certificates and support to volunteers. Unfortunately while our limited resources are exhausted our hope to reach at least 10,000 children with all these services in 2020 remains sustained.

Besoins / Needs

Due to the ongoing protracted crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon over 44,000 schools remain closed with more than 600,000 children out of school, in need of Learning opportunities accompanied by psychosocial support, WASH, health care, civil status registration documents and nutritional support. As the conflict persists, schools remain closed in most parts of the North West region.Proir to the commencement of the ongoing academic year the Government embarked on ‘’Back to school’’ campaign, which involved education key stakeholders, elites in government, administration, CSOs, Faith Based Organisations and international organisations/INGO. This gave parents/guardians hopes that schools would resume. Ironically all these campaigns were inadequate to counter persistent separatists’ school boycot/closure enforcement. Presently, in the NWR formal education is accessible only Bamenda central town where security is tense and Misaje/Nkambe Towns in Donga Mantung Division, while all schools remain closed elsewhere in the region.While communities’ needs/interest for implementation of various humanitarian services have received attention, despite low and slow, the request for children’s education remains zero in most parts of the NWR since 2017. It should be noted that our non-formal learning programmes are located in Sunday School halls, which were not designed for that purpose and therefore such infrastructure cannot accommodate the numerous children as would be a formal school. Besides, these children are unable to afford learning material as well as GPA is unable to provide adequate teaching material.Furthermore, the water system and toilet facilities were designed and usually made available only for Sunday services and not accessible for the non-formal learning classes.Psychosocial support services remain challenging as facilitators have not received any formal training for that.Most of the children have lost their parents and even those with parents suffer nutritional challenges especially as the major source of livelihood (agriculture) has been handicapped by the persistent war and in most cases NSAGs have occupied farm lands or farmers are afraid of stray bullets on their ways to farms.Most children have either lost birth certificates during displacement or burnt homes or the fact that for the past 3 years those services are unavailable in the communities.While health systems in most of the conflict affected hard-to-reach communities are non-functional community health workers are required to provide primary health care to children.
