Project Summary

The protection of IDPs , returnees and vulnerable people in South Darfur is challenged by conflict, political instability, economic decline and climate change. Therefore, the project plans to address the critical humanitarian conditions facing the most needy groups, especially in areas that suffer from security, social and humanitarian deterioration, which can be summarized as follows:
1- Address the immediate and acute protection risks and needs of vulnerable IDPs, displaced persons and vulnerable host communities affected by the crisis through the provision of just and timely life-saving services
2- Strengthen the protection environment through robust protection monitoring and analysis, identifying protection risks, human rights violations, and gaps in service delivery and advocacy;
3- Strengthening community protection mechanisms to meet protection needs, enhance preparedness and early warning capabilities, and prevent or mitigate protection risks.
The target group of the project is to reach 4,982 beneficiaries of IDPs, returnees and the host community, including children, women and people with special needs who face a major challenge in accessing general protection services in South Darfur, especially those of IDPs and returnees. . Need legal aid, civil documentation, mental health support, psychological support, housing compensation, land and property rights, special protective accommodations for women and girls, and multi-purpose cash assistance.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project contributes to covering the shortage in basic services because the deteriorating socio-economic situation has had dire consequences on people's physical and mental well-being, living standards, and long-term resilience. Therefore, the project targeted 4,982 people, including 2,700 women, 1,050 children, 300 disabled people, and 932 elderly people.Vulnerable groups – including women, children, the elderly, people with disabilities, the chronically ill and the marginalized – are faced with a set of challenges unique to their individual circumstances, which are exacerbated by their displacement and non-functioning or overstretched family/community support networks. Because these groups and others suffer disproportionately from various protection risks, including violence, sexual and other forms of exploitation and abuse, and human rights violations, they are in need of specialized protection services. For women and girls, the risks of gender-based violence remain high, particularly when they are on the move or working in the fields collecting water or firewood.The project aims to address the abuses to which boys and men in particular continue to be subjected to physical violence, injury, death and arbitrary arrest, as they are often seen as supporting armed opposition groups. Displaced youth lack livelihood activities, and are burdened with sporadic and incomplete education. Young people are also reported to be increasingly resorting to violence to resolve differences with community leaders. People with disabilities continue to be marginalized and neglected, with few governments or human services targeting their unique needs. Access to healthcare, particularly for the elderly and the chronically ill, remains limited.The project works to access birth registration, issuance of documents and identity cards, legalize agricultural and residential lands, remove mines and raise awareness of their risks. These problems are a challenge in most locations, which increases the risk of statelessness and the inability to access basic services. IDPs and vulnerable residents living outside government-controlled areas have access to birth registration, which prevents their children from attending government schools. Non-registration of births also affects freedom of movement and access to basic services.

The project will support the development of self-protection capacities and aims to improve the daily lives of beneficiaries by improving formal and informal protection mechanisms. This is considered the most effective way to address gender inequality as the root cause of gender-based violence.
