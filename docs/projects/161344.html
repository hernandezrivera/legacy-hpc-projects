Project Summary

The project will target 18,500 beneficiaries (11,100 Females and 7,400 Males) in both Ikwotos and Lafon Counties of Eastern Equatoria State.
The project outcomes include; diversification of livelihoods for farming Communities, Climate Resilient Agriculture, and livestock health and production.
Conducting training of participants and local leaders on the CMDRR process in the project are: Ark for Humanity shall identify participants and local leaders for the training in development of Community Based Disaster Risk Reduction (CMDRR) process. Ark for Humanity will undertake the process of mobilizing communities in a systematic way towards achieving a common disaster risk reduction objective in the target areas of Lafon and Ikwotos Counties. The process will involves a participatory disaster risk assessment and risk analysis followed by the development of a community action plan.
Form and train Natural Resources Management Committees (NRMC) for regular community dialogue on natural resources and conflict resolution: Ark for Humanity (AFH) shall form the Natural Resource Management Committee for regular community dialogue on natural resources and conflict resolution.
AFH will facilitate the identification of DRR priorities that will be developed in to CMDRR action plan. The action plan will be developed in active participation of the target communities and facilitated by participatory methodologies.
Organize training of farmer groups on crop diversity in order to formulate business plans and provide appropriate extension support on Climate Smart Agriculture: AFH shall identify existing farmers group from the project area and organize training on crop diversity. AFH will formulate business plans for the farmers groups. AFH has been working in the target project areas for over 10 years and have established various farming groups and cooperatives which are functional todate. Selected farmer groups shall be trained on Climate smart Agriculture.
Set up demonstration/group demonstration on short-term drought tolerant varieties: Ark for Humanity shall identify existing farmers group and identify the potential drought tolerant crops in the project area. AFH shall establish demonstration Plots in the selected project area in both Lafon and Ikwotos Counties and group demonstration on the short term drought tolerant varieties and technologies. AFH will support the season long comparative experimentation and learning process in the existing field Schools/APFS as well as technical guidance on the demonstration plots.
Establishment of Ten (10) Agropastotal Field School (APFS) in the project areas: Ark for Humanity shall identify and support training of at least 10 Farmers or community Agro-pastoral field school facilitators within the target communities. To maximise the outreach of this activity, selected farmers, as Community FFS facilitators, will undergo a Training of Trainers (ToT) course on FFS Methodology organized by AFH. The facilitators will apply what they have learned in the ToT and conduct weekly second-level trainings for FFS members throughout the project period.
APFS members shall be trained on animal health and production. However the project does not plan to procure any animals to be kept at the APFS.
Distribute foundation seeds, tools and other inputs to APFS and initiate seed multiplication activities: AFH shall procure 10MT of assorted foundation seeds (Sorghum, maize, Sesame, Ground Nuts, Green peas, Cow peas and assorted vegetable varieties) and distribute to the APFS for planting in the demonstration plots established. AFH shall target all the 10 APFS established for Seed Production activities.
AFH shall mainstream GBV in the FSL project by Conduct awareness on GBV targeting specific groups and conduct safety audits/advocacy (including in markets andwater points). AFH shall Train selected local women groups on basic PSS/PFA and referral pathway.

1 - Needs

Despite the decline in conflict following the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) and expected slight improvement in the food security situation, many households have lost key livelihood assets and strategies to cope with food insecurity as a result of the persistent conflict. The loss of key assets and livelihood options has continued to drive high levels of acute food insecurity, and a risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) and unprecedented levels of malnutrition will continue to persist in South Sudan. A number of factors have compromised households’ ability to obtain adequate food consistently over the last six years they include insecurity, unfavourable weather conditions such as prolonged floods (that have led to temporary displacement and loss of assets and food stocks in Ikotos and Lafon Counties) and dry spells; instability and diminished activity in the markets as a result of degraded infrastructure and poor market economy. This situation is even made worse by the sky rocketing inflation in the country with the farming communities having very low purchasing power. Limited employment opportunities and income generating activities in the current context of economic collapse has also limited households’ consistent ability to access adequate food. Food insecurity in Ikotos and Lafon Counties has had a great effect in Childrens' education and general wellbeing of vulnerable groups among which include but not limited to; girl child, Pregnant and Lactating Mothers and elderly, blind and Children under the age of 5years. With the dividends of peace been seen in Eastern Equatoria State, a lot of returnees from the neighbouring countries of Uganda and Kenya have started returning home. This has exerted tremendous pressure on the limited food stocks of the host communities communities in Lafon and Ikwotos counties. According to IPC projections for Jan 2020 to April 2020, an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) are likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in South Sudan. And in Eastern Equatoria State where Ikotos falls under, 400,000 people shall be in stressed (IPC phase 2), 300,000 people shall be in crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 100,000 people in Emergency (IPC Phase 4).This therefore calls for the implementation of Humanitarian Response Programme (HRP) activities in Ikotos and Lafon to meet immediate food needs while helping households develop resilience against future shocks and stresses. The prolonged civil war has destroyed the social fabric and livelihoods of most communities across South Sudan with no exception to Ikotos in Eastern Equatoria. The implementation of recovery and resilience building activities are essential to help rebuild livelihoods and strengthen local capacities to manage shocks in Ikotos and Lafon Counties in 2020.
