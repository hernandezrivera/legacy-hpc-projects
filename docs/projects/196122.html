Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to contribute to the improvement of the living conditions and access to critically needed WASH services for the Rohingya Refugee and vulnerable Host Communities in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh:
Regular, sufficient, equitable and dignified access to safe water for drinking and domestic needs.
Adequate, appropriate, and acceptable sanitation facilities (including household waste).
Change of potentially dangerous behaviours partly through innovative hygiene promotion (including hygiene items).

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Joint Response Plan 2022 estimates that 1.46 million people (918,841 RRs and 541,021 HCs) affected by the crisis are in urgent need of WASH support. The needs range from access to safe drinking water and water for domestic needs, adequate sanitation facilities including solid waste management and access to hygiene services need to improve. The proposed project focuses on Block A & C in Camp 27 and Block E in Camp 24, where many HHs have high needs in all these four areas simultaneously. Project will support around 32,206 Rohingya refugees and adjunct host communities directly (6,710 HHs) in the targeted camps and locations. Indirectly around 105,133 inhabitants of camps 24, 27 and Nhila union will benefit from the project. Twenty-five percent of the project provisions will be allocated for the host community (as specified by the government). Project will give priority to the extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs), persons with disabilities (PWD), girls, women and children. Adolescents and women will be specifically targeted for the hygiene behavioral change, promotion and capacity building activities. Teknaf is an area of water scarcity and in dry season there is always water crisis happen. In this project we will supply safe drinking water from borehole to the Rohingya refugee and adjacent host community. Also ensure the access to the wash facility with certain gender segregation at wash intervention. Beside, regular maintenance & operation of FSM and SWM. Change the behavior towards hygiene through awareness raising. Also focus on user group formation to improve their capacity and develop ownership.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will be implemented in an integrated approach where an implementation plan will be developed and followed coordinating with camp level WASH sector, relevant agencies, CICs and other govt agencies and stakeholders. For service delivery to the to the refugees and host community participation of the project's participants will be ensured. In the project, there's an opportunity to promote hygiene for the beneficiaries and raise awareness to maintain proper hygiene practices. If the community maintain properly, it simultaneously reduces health risks and decreases diseases. To ensure safe drinking water for the target beneficiaries, use rental boreholes from the host community and borehole owners to benefit from the project and develop their socio-economic condition. Also, we have an existing solid waste management plant that will have produced the compost which will be used in the agricultural land we may link with the agricultural development project or agricultural department which will also benefit from the project.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection strategies in WASH will ensure that all groups of people facing additional vulnerabilities including children, adolescence boys & girls, women, older people, and people with disabilities, have adequate and safe access to water collection and use; functional latrines and bathing units with safe and accessible pathways, solid doors, locks and other inclusive features such as screening and lighting; and safe spaces for practising Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM). Latrine or bathing cubicles that are used by the females are distinguished by fencing around the facilities. Two seperate queues are made for male & female while collecting water. The WASH Sector coordinates with the Protection Sector, the GBV Sub-Sector to improve the responsiveness of WASH actors in relation to protection-related issues. The WASH partners pursue full implementation of the Global WASH Cluster’s five minimum commitments for safety and dignity.

3.2 - Gender

Gender is not just about the biological differences between men and women but refers to their different roles, rights, and responsibilities, and the relations between them. It is generally associated with unequal power and access to resources because, in many societies and cultures, men tend to dominate and consider women to be subordinate. Gender mainstreaming is a process that aims to address this imbalance. Gender equality can result in participation in programme work being representative of all water users, managers and stakeholders. Hence, project objectives and activities is set considering gender equality and sustainability of the community. Gender mainstreaming is the process of ensuring that women and men have equal access and control over resources, development benefits and decision-making, at all stages of the development process including projects, programs and policy. Diversity refers to the different social backgrounds and identities that make up populations. 3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

For the sake of transparency and accountability, the necessary project information will be shared with the community members to raise their awareness, community consultation sessions need to be conducted to ensure the participation of the community and to collect their opinions and feedback. Under the project, community complaints will be received through a number of channels: complaint boxes, use managed hotline, FGDs, community meetings and routine monitoring, field staff, observation distribution monitoring (ODM) and post-distribution monitoring (PDM). All complaints will be recorded in the CRM register book with a unique number in order to easily identify each feedback and complaint. After sorting and classification, complaints will be forwarded to the relevant technical person to be addressed and resolved.
