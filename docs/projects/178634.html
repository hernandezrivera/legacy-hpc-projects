Project Summary

Project aims to contribute to achieving HRP objectives that targets school age children aged, teachers, and education personnel. and focuses on: increasing the availability of and access to safe and equitable formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth; enhancing the quality of formal and non-formal education services.
Balad has access to targeted sites through its team on ground, and has the necessary expertise due to implementation of several educational projects (2 projects funded by HF, 2 projects funded by UNICEF, and others), the number of beneficiaries of Balad' projects within several sectors in 2020 was more than 170,000 BNFs, with a total funding of $ 1,845,000.
Project intends to enhance access to formal and non-formal education services and promoting child safeguarding in learning facilities by supporting 4 community development centers and 6 schools.
Project will provide operational costs for 4 community development centers in regions, these centers are already established at the Al-Bab (severity 5), Jarablus (severity 4), Afrin (severity 4), Salqin (severity 4).
The centers are equipped with basic equipment and furnishings, in addition to securing the rent, utilities (electricity, water and internet bills). The 4 centers need to be provided by operational costs during project period.
These centers provide educational services free of charge, for children aged 6-17 also supporting children with special needs.
Safety and security plans will be established in community development centers which will be provided with safety equipment.
During the recent crisis, salaries for teachers have generally not stable and decreased. Salaries will be secured for teachers in 4 school Al-Atareb (severity 5), to ensuring that they are sufficiently compensated to ensure the livelihoods of their own families, are key to advancing child rights, wellbeing teachers do not receive any salaries from any other organization, and they work as volunteers in schools, and this is what causes teachers to Displaced to other areas in search of work and this leads to the suspension of school, so the project will support 4 formal schools, as operational costs and Incentives for teachers, in Ataerb community , so that students can continue their studies.
As well Minor rehabilitation 2 Schools in Jarablus (severity 4) to expand enhance education infrastructure to ensure safe and conducive learning environments through inclusive and integrated light repairs.
Recreational area and sanitary facilities for the school to be suitable for girls and boys in the school.
2 blocks of 2 gender-sensitive and disability-friendly toilets will be constructed in each school.
Project will provide these activities:
- Providing Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) for children and Children with Disability aged between (6-17) (grades 1-9).
- Providing (Catch-up Education) for students from the age of 6 to 17 years.
- Providing languages courses (English, Turkish) for student from (6-17) years.
- Running recreational, psychosocial and resilience activities in centers and schools.
- Conducting awareness raising activities for children, parents, humanitarian workers and community members, focusing on following topics (Education related issues, Child Labor, Basic CP, Child Rights).
- Conducting awareness campaigns about COVID-19.
- Supporting schools and centers with hygiene supplies to support prevention of COVID-19 infection.
- Providing Incentives for 141teachers, in both centers and schools.
- Providing Minor rehabilitation 2 Schools in Jarablus.
- Providing transportation insurance services to solve the problem of not sending their female children out of fear for them, so that parents can feel safe to send their female children to education.
Project targets 7280 students and their parents (2912 boys, 4368 girls. 339 teachers (women, and men) (145 of them with disabilities).

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, "despite tremendous efforts by humanitarian partners, the education system in Syria faced formidable and mounting challenges in 2020.In 2021, investments must improve the availability of, and access to, learning opportunities to avoid learning loss, reduced future productive capacity and lessen the multiple adverse socio-emotional and other threats related to disrupting a child’s education.An estimated 6.9 million people (97% children) need humanitarian education assistance.The sector is not able to provide a 2021 estimate of out of school children, however, in 2020 an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out.Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out, almost certain to increase.Hostilities, the use of schools for non-educational purposes and other safety concerns continue to impact the safe use and availability of education services—particularly in the north. In 2020 at least, 42 children were killed and 38 children were injured by 61 verified attacks on education and 31 verified instances of military use of schools. In addition to the terrible fear this instils in students and school personnel, attacks on education also reduce the availability of education infrastructure, contributing to overcrowding and result in unsafe school buildings being used by students."

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

To prevent any PSEA issue from occurring during the implementation period of the project activities, especially the activities in which the project staff and service providers are in direct contact with the beneficiaries, all field activities have been reviewed and assessed for risk factors for PSEA with the use of the PSEA Network Field and Organizational Mitigation and Prevention Checklists and the following prevention and mitigation measures will be taken:1- Balad will make sure that All staff, contractor, and volunteers understand and sign the Code of Conduct.2- Field staff with direct contact with beneficiaries will receive PSEA training.3- Balad will review internal reporting mechanisms and ensure all staff know about reporting mechanisms.4- female staff will always be present with the field team.5- Beneficiaries will be given information that aid is free6- Contractors and their workers will not have direct contact with beneficiaries7- PSEA messaging and awareness raising will be done though pasting PSEA posters and placing PSEA banners at the distribution site or other service place.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption A:Qualified teachers (females/males) will be hired to launch the education process.Risk 1: Lack of qualified teachers (females/males) for non-formal education.Mitigation 1: Training teachers (females/males) on non-formal education modality and curricula Risk 2: Lack of qualified trainers.Mitigation 2: Providing training online and sending trainers from Turkey to Syria.Assumption B:School supplies are delivered to beneficiaries (girls/boys) on timely manner.Risk 3: Closing the border-gates between Syria and Turkey hindered the education supplies to be delivered on timely manner.Mitigation 3: Procure the supplies and print textbooks and brochures in Syria.Risk 4: Sudden economic changes influencing currency prices Mitigation 4: Rational management of available resources and adoption of early storage and rationalize the process of converting currencies from foreign to local. Assumption C:Project will not cause increasing of COVID-19 cases.Risk 5: Increased number of cases infected with COVID in NWMitigation 5: Providing sterilization (sterilization + masks), instructions brochures, and awareness sessions.
