Project Summary

This program intends to provide an integrated emergency and sustainable WASH intervention package to host communities and IDPs in the communities and the camps and informal settlements in northern Syria.
The program formulation has been based on needs identified by the WASH cluster, supported by Rapid Need Assessment conducted by White Hands (WH) in Nov 2022 and firsthand consultations with camps management and local authorities/stakeholders.
WH’s WASH program recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men, and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group across the range of WASH interventions.
Considering their vulnerability, IDPs living in IDP sites/camps, female-headed HHs, PWDs, and elderly persons at risk will form the priority groups of WH’s WASH program, particularly among the high numbers of new IDPs & returnees requiring core WASH assistance.
1. In-camp, where residents rely heavily on humanitarian assistance for their water needs, WH’s intervention will focus on life-saving WASH support and delivery of basic services for 20,635 IDPs residing in 20 camps across Idleb, Harim, Ariha, and Jisr-Ash-Shugur districts. This will include:
- Supply safe and adequate water through trucking. This intervention will secure safe access to clean, sufficient, and adequate water for drinking and hygiene purposes. Each individual will receive 40 liters per day.
- Water quality monitoring through testing at three levels: water sources, trucks, and households to ensure water is according to standards.
- Expansion of water distribution networks and sewerage systems in the targeted camps where applicable, including installing a renewable energy system (PV Solar energy systems) to minimize the water system operating costs & to ensure the sustainability & long-term impact of the project.
- Installation of water tanks to address a current shortage, and women will be consulted in selecting the most appropriate location for the water tanks.
- Regular emptying of septic tanks in the 20 targeted camps.
- Solid waste removal will be organized from each of the 20 camps. All waste will be disposed into a communal landfill after the consent of the respective LC.
- Provision of garbage bins to improve the hygienic conditions in the targeted camps.
- Addressing the gap in latrines numbers in the targeted camps by constructing new latrine blocks, making them gender-sensitive, and addressing the needs of the elderly & PWDs.
- Consumable hygiene kits and Cholera Kits will be distributed to displaced families in all target locations, in line with the Cluster recommendations regarding the amount of soap and cleaning materials to ensure the continuation of the hygiene practices necessary to prevent Covid-19 and Cholera.
- Hygiene awareness: WH will hold segregated sessions for men, women, and children to raise personal and environmental hygiene awareness.
2. Off-camp, WH’s intervention will integrate a more sustainable component, with a focus on long-term improvement of access to safe water sources, through:
- Rehabilitation of 4 water stations & supporting them with the operational costs, including (fuel, oil, chlorine, spare parts & salaries) for 6 months located in Afrin district to serve 56,058 BNFs.
- Rehabilitation of 4 sewage systems in 8 communities in Afrin district to serve 46,754 BNFs.
- Develop tariff-based system management in line with the WASH cluster recommendations and in coordination with relevant stakeholders: fees will be collected monthly from beneficiaries under the supervision of LC to secure operation and maintenance costs required to sustain the project.
The program will seek feedback from both male & female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access & equal opportunities.
This intervention will benefit a total of approximately 123,447 individuals (28,059 men, 31,790 women, 30,864 boys, and 32,734 girls) across NS.

3 - Needs

According to the 2023 Syria HNO WASH Sector, 13.6M PiN of WASH assistance. Of this, 6.6M (49%) are female & 2.7M (20%) are PWDs. The 2022 outbreak of cholera in Syria, first Syria has seen in decades, is a direct result of the deteriorating water situation.Moreover, according to the MSNA conducted by WH in Dec 2022 in NWS:- WASH was reported among the top three priority needs in more than a third of assessed camps.- Most commonly reported reasons IDPs cannot access sufficient water were high prices of water trucking (89%) and the main network is completely or partially damaged (51%).- Most commonly reported public sanitation issues were no sewage system in the camps (56%), garbage on the site (36%), and sewage flowing onto the site (33%).- Most commonly reported problems with access to latrines were not enough latrines (78%), no available latrines for PWDs (51%), and latrines having no privacy (62%).- In 69% of assessed camps, HHs reportedly had no access to a functioning main water network, leading to informal water trucking being the most commonly reported source of drinking water (87%).- Most commonly reported coping strategies for lack of water used by HHs in the month before data collection are modifying hygiene practices (43%), relying on personal water stocks (38%), drinking less (30%), and drinking water used for other purposes (14%).- Limited water availability is likely to impact COVID-19 prevention if there are insufficient handwashing facilities in HHs.

4 - Implementation of activities

Restoring water stations and distribution networks intervention is based on detailed BoQs and needs assessments of the water systems by providing the required mechanical rehabilitation and the operations and maintenance costs for 6 months as the efforts will be made to establish/re-activate a tariff collection and accounting system, and the sustainability of the water system in these communities. The work will include the establishment of a Services Unit (SU) per water station to manage the timely operation, maintenance, and repairs for the long-term sustainability of water systems. The rehabilitation of 4 sewage systems will include the installation of concrete pipes with excavation & backfilling, cleaning and unblocking sewage pipes and manholes, an extension of sewage networks, and repairing the concrete in existing drainage & manholes. Emergency services will be done through the provision of adequate drinking water via water trucking (40l/p/d) while constantly assuring the quality of the water through chlorination and testing, storage water tanks, solid waste removal, provision of waste containers, constructing latrines blocks (each1 cell serve 25 people), desludging latrine septic tanks. Hygiene and Cholera kits & hygiene promotion awareness. Beneficiary selection and verification. - Procurement phase. - Distribution phase. - M&E and closure - Monthly monitoring report. The project activities will be implemented in line with the COVID-19 guidance of the WASH Cluster.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

•Movements of water trucks in congested, camp-like settings L:H, I: L Mitigation: Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points, delivery routes & its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes & schedule for water trucking that use wider access routes, •Long distance to water collection points & route to be traveled not safe (exposure to GBV risk, UXO/ERW hazard). L:L, I: H Mitigation: Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards (200-500m) & closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women & men, including older people & PWDs in the assessment, •Toilets present GBV and/or health, safety risk; Access to toilet is not available to all 24/7; Access to toilets not available for all; L:H, I: H Mitigation: Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these provisions/aspects during implementation. Ensure women and adolescent girls are consulted during design, implementation & monitoring phase. Accountability mechanisms are put in place and received feedback from users is followed up. Lessons from previous experience are shared and learned.•Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups & used in conflict activities. L:L, I: H Mitigation: MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts& Address any SEA complaints.Risk2: The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. MS: Advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided. We will include women, girls, and other at-risk groups to determine such things as if systems/criteria are in place to determine how shelters are being allocated; if they are built for safety and privacy, Consultations with women, girls, boys, and men should be organized &community safety audits.Risk3: Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender-segregated sanitation facilities can lead to an increase in GBV and sexual exploitation as well as communicable diseases. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: provision of gender-segregated sanitation and hygiene facilities sufficiently close to dwellings, provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities, and work closely with protection and other sectors. Specific attention concerning complaints/feedback mechanisms (CFMs) will be given to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Following the PSEA Network recommendations, WH's focal points will ensure the availability of information to the beneficiaries concerning CFMs through informative sessions.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: WH has full access to the targeted communities & the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the project. Risk: Escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in the targeted area affecting WH’s ability to operate and access it.Likelihood: M/Impact: H MS: WH will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities.Assumption2:The activities can be completed without delay. Risk: -Adverse weather conditions will affect the implementation of rehabilitation works. Probability: H/Impact: M MS: A fast track approach shall be followed to maximize the implementation of the rehabilitation works period, so there will be a flexible schedule. The rehabilitation works will be divided into 3 parts to be performed simultaneously by WH, hence ensuring fast delivery before winter arrives around November.Risk3: Spread of COVID-19 pandemic cases in NWS. Probability:M/Impact: M MS3: Follow strict preventive measures, building on the WASH guidelines and recommendations. Those will be reflected in each activity, All project teams and staff will receive sensitization on COVID-19 precautions. Risk4: Aid diversion by armed groups, including looting of warehouses. Probability: L/Impact: M MS4: Invest in sound security measures at the warehouses, Train the staff on how to deal with aid diversion, Establish timely reporting mechanisms & info sharing with other actors on the ground, donors, and clusters.
