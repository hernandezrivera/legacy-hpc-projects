Project Summary

In this project, CMC aims to provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water as well as improving knowledge of most vulnerable population on hygiene to reduce morbidity and mortality resulting from WASH-related diseases and malnutrition.
CMC will implement integrated lifesaving WASH and nutrition activities mainstreaming protection, accountability to affected population and strengthening the capacity of affected community on copping mechanism and resilience through training of different groups of people in the community such as pump mechanics, Water management committee, women groups, community leaders among others The project will target communities affected with conflict and natural disasters with limited access to safe water supply, unsafe hygiene and sanitation practices and food insecurity with support to institutions like nutrition/health centers and schools with WASH facilities and hygiene promotion.
Under this project, CMC will implement life-saving WASH activities targeting 15000 Direct Beneficiaries and 10000 indirect beneficiaries suffering from inadequate access to safe drinking water and poor hygiene conditions. CMC intends to target vulnerable and crisis affected population with a structured, effective preparedness and response programme aimed at improving access to safe water, enhancing community awareness on safe hygiene and sanitation practices in order to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases.
CMC will coordinate with nutrition, education and FSL partners to reduce malnutrition level through supporting nutrition centers and schools with WASH facilities and enhancing knowledge of mothers who are the primary care givers on safe hygiene and sanitation practices especially in areas of new displacement. This will contribute to the reduction of risk of WASH related diseases including malnutrition hence making health, nutrition and education centers safe for users.
To enhance sustainability and resilience, CMC will implement capacity building of the community to prepare for and cope with current shocks and sustain livelihoods, linkages with development action to support resilience
Protection and accountability to affected people will be at the central point in this project, putting communities at the centre of the response. CMC will ensure that services delivered are gender-sensitive and will consider the needs of older people, children (boys and girls) and people living with disabilities. Where necessary cash programming will be incorporated through voucher for WASH NFIs like soap provided to health/nutrition centers at risk of contamination and malnutrition. Key hygiene and nutrition messaging at these institutions will be accompanied.
Before response, initial needs assessments (either IRNA or CMC’s own assessment) will be carried out in consultation with the national WASH cluster and WASH states focal persons for the targeted locations. Activities will include will include safe water supply through water point construction repair and rehabilitation, provision of sanitation facilities at institution levels especially health/nutrition centers and schools, hygiene promotion focusing on key messages (hand washing, safe water chain and safe excreta disposal) Hygiene and sanitation campaigns in communities and institutions, capacity-building of community through trainings (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, school's hygiene clubs), and distribution of WASH NFIs and Menstrual hygiene Management Materials to women and girls of reproductive age.
To ensure accountability to affected population, indicators will be put in place to track cooperation with communities by attending to complaints and carrying out feedback sessions with the community to address GBV threats in WASH, and ensuring services provided meet the needs of the beneficiaries. Post intervention monitoring will be conducted to measure the appropriateness, coverage and effectiveness. Clear WASH NFI beneficiary selection criteria will be put in place and clearly presented to the community before and during distribution.
The continuing deteriorating access to humanitarian support services for the Internally Displaced People and Returnees contributed negatively to the affected population with serious traumatic life experiences in the sites of displacement and return, through lessons learnt by other partners in current and most recent projects, (CMC) has acknowledged the rapid increasing cases of protection and Sexual Gender Based Violence which stand as a prove that protection and Sexual Gender Based Violence is a cross cutting thematic issue that need to be addressed across all projects and this project will contribute in addressing the gaps through inter-sector response.

Needs

CMC activities will target populations faced with crisis of limited access to adequate safe water, unsafe hygiene and sanitation practices, high malnutrition rates resulting from conflicts and other natural disasters. High priority will be places with influx of newly displaced populations due to conflict and natural disasters leaving in remote hard to reach areas, protracted IDPs in collective centers, returnees and vulnerable communities hosting IDPs with limited WASH facilities. CMC project seeks to implement integrated WASH interventions to vulnerable community, GBV prone and IPC 3, 4 areas in places with needs. CMC recognizes the need for increased WASH interventions at household and institutional levels for displaced population, host community in the areas with high level of food insecurity coupled with malnutrition (GAM) (IPC 4), areas at high risk of protections issues especially GBV that are WASH related.Protracted conflict in South Sudan has caused erosion of physical and social infrastructure, death and displacement of hundreds of thousands of people in Central Equatoria, Jonglei states and other states in South Sudan. The conflicts echoed to tribal clashes, cattle raiding among others. Serimon, Kuda, Rokon and Terekeka in Central Equatoria, Pibor, Likuangole, Pochalla, Lokormach, Gumuruk and Boma counties in Jonglei State are among the most affected with recurrent attacks often causing more displacements, loss of lives and destruction of infrastructure. Coupling, the national financial crises have worsened the situation. According to the World Bank report 2017-2018, over half (51%) of the 12.3 million South Sudanese live below the national consumption poverty line. This implies that majority of the population in the country cannot easily afford basic needs including water, soap, among others. This situation continued to worsen where more people are displaced, livelihoods destroyed and more fighting being reported. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) ‘Key IPC findings of January-July 2018 and September-October 2018, report highlighted Pibor and its neighbouring counties in Jonglei state as one of the most food insecure counties in South Sudan. Additionally, the October 2017 Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) survey indicated the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) was 26.8% and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) at 8%.2 3 Cattle raids, which previous REACH studies have linked to food insecurity, have become progressively more frequent, far ranging, and violent letting to displacement and subsequent WASH gaps.Different needs assessments shows that, in most parts of the country, people are faced with immense lack of access to safe adequate water, poor hygiene practices and sanitation conditions, food insecurity exposing people to the risk of WASH related diseases and malnutrition especially among children under-fives. This is attributed to several reasons the main one being displacement due to conflict. In recent reports, Several civilians were reported to have been displaced from Minyori, of which, about 629 IDPs have been registered by partners in Yei town between 12 and 15 October 2018, and several were still waiting to be registered. (Humanitarian key context updates 22 October 2018). Partners provided some humanitarian assistance to the IDPs, but food remains the main gap. Fact finding mission report of Kuda, Sirimon and Rokon in central Equatoria on form 4-6 October, show that IDPs are suffering from lack of safe adequate water, basic hygiene and sanitation facilities which is exposing people to risk of WASH related diseases. Hunger was also reported in the region which is resulting from the ongoing insecurity and limited rainfall in 2018. REACH conducted a rapid assessment in Pibor, Gumuruk, Verteth and Lekuangole Payams between 6/4/2018 indicating displaced population having no access to safe water, sanitation, hygiene facilities, and NFIs

Activities

Activity 1: Conducting needs assessments in areas of crisis prior to any response to determine the exact needs of the community and establish relevant recommendations. CMC will participate in IRNA assessment and conduct its own assessment and share reports with the WASH cluster and other partners. Activity 2: Providing timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations through repair and rehabilitations of water points, provision of water purifiers (PUR and aqua tabs), building capacities of local pump mechanics and water management committee to ensure sustainability and resilience, water quality analysis at water points, Households and public facilities (Health/Nutrition centres, Schools etc.). Consideration will be made concerning the location of the water point through involving women in deciding for the area where the water point can be constructed to ensure secure access to women and girls. Activity 3: Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. Hygiene promotion activities like HH visits by trained Community Hygiene Promoters targeting key messages like hand washing at critical times, safe water chain, food hygiene and safe excreta disposal. CMC will also target schools and nutrition/health centers focusing on mothers who are taking care of children with malnutrition. Menstrual Hygiene Management will also be an area of focus considering the fact that MHM is still misunderstood in most communities. Activity 4: Enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services. At institution level like health/nutrition centers and schools, latrines will be constructed to cater for boys and girls, men and women separately. For the girls and latrines will be equipped with water disposal bins for disposing used sanitary pads and adequate space for changing and washing. Additional HH latrines will also be constructed as samples using local available materials for community to learn from. Activity 5: Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities. CMC will complement other sectoral responses through integration and mainstreaming methodologies.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Prior to any engagement in the community, CMC assessment/response team will meet with the community leaders and various groups in the community including man, women and youths in focus group discussions and key informant interview explain the scope of the project and the TOR. During response/intervention, the team will meet frequently with community leaders and relevant stakeholders providing activity progress and attending to complaints and feedbacks from the community.Team will continue to conduct sensitization to the beneficiaries on protection concerns, correct use of WASH NFIs received, water quality analysis results, targeting criteria during interventions. Banners, posters and flayers will be used to conduct this messaging with some printed in the local languages used in the project locations. Such sensitizations shall be done collectively through the focus group discussions, the public outreaches, orientations during the WASH NFI distributions and during the assessments.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CMC will give messages according to the context based on the needs. In some situations, gender specific sessions will be conducted to men and women separately for example, issues of menstrual hygiene management will be approached differently to men and women. Some messages like household hygiene will be general however, more focus will be given to women since they are the more involved in hygiene of the household and children. CMC will carry out sessions for children in schools and in the community with messages that are child friendly.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CMC intervention team will comprise of protection personnel, whose role is to make sure CMC staffs committed to the CMC code of conduct and zero tolerant to engagement in sexual exploitation and abuse. CMC will ensure all contractors and casual staffs like community hygiene promoters, enumerators and pump mechanics in the field level signs commitment document of no sexual abuse of any form to the beneficiaries. CMC shall establish the complaints and suggestion box to report any case of sexual exploitations and abuses. CMC will work together with other protection partners to ensure protections issues are addressed right from designing and execution of the activities proposed. Banners, posters and flayers will be used to conduct this messaging with some printed in the local languages used in the project locations

Apart from the community leaders, CMC go ahead and meet with women, men and youths separately to get their ideas and interest in the projects in Focus Group Discussions and household visits during assessments and response. Such sensitizations shall be done collectively through the focus group discussions, the public outreaches, orientations during the distributions and during the assessments.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

CMC response team comprising of M&E staff will continue to carryout beneficiaries’ satisfactory check at household level using the focus group discussions and rom key informants. Additional suggestion/complain box shall be stationed in all the activities locations, beneficiaries shall be given orientations on how they can report concerns. All complains and feedbacks will be documented for references and lessons learned and where necessary reported to the cluster and relevant partners. The intervention teams will inform beneficiaries about how and where they can provide complaints and receive feedback on the work of CMC and the items distributed (the complains boxes, the AAP/M&E focal persons). The Rapid monitoring activities will help carry feedback from the beneficiaries throughout the intervention and such feedback will help inform the intervention team on how best they can incorporate realistic ideas to improve the project impact. The team shall be inclusive of the beneficiaries representatives, the local authorities and CMC M&E team.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Beneficiaries will be involved in all stages of the project from the design, formulation activities and implementation to ensure project ownership and sustainability by the community. During intervention, the team will hold regular meetings with the community to measure progress and in at the end of the project, evaluation will be done together with the community. Committees from the beneficiaries with full gender representation shall be formed to work together with CMC monitoring and evaluation team during and after intervention.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Protection will be mainstreamed in every activity of the project for example in construction of sanitation facilities in institutions, CMC will ensure that there are separate latrines for boys and girls, men and women equipped with lockers form inside for privacy. During distribution of WASH NFIs, the team will ensure that the vulnerable and people living with disabilities are served fast so that they don’t cue for long and also where new water points will be constructed, consideration will be made through consultation with women on the distance and the location to ensure that women are safe in the process of collecting water. Protection will be at the central point in this project, putting communities at the canter of the response. CMC will ensure that services delivered are gender-sensitive and will consider the needs of older people, children (boys and girls) and people living with disabilities. CMC will ensure that the “Do no harm” principle is adhered.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CMC community entry process will be inclusive where all the beneficiaries have involvement in (men, women, boys, girls, disabled etc.). To reach the areas of intervention, CMC plans to use regular UNHAS flights as well as the air-assets of Log Cluster. If the latter is impossible, CMC has budgeted for chartered flights. On the ground, CMC mostly intends to cover the necessary distances on foot. If a possibility arises, a car/tractor/boat will be hired for the local transportation in the field. All access constrains faced by CMC will be reported to OCHA access unit at both state and national level with knowledge of the SFP and Cluster coordinators. In these targeted locations, CMC through the assessments conducted prior to the intervention continues to best inform CMC on the barriers of access. CMC will work with the locally recruited staff from the targeted locations and channel the supplies where accessible to all groups within the beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will be fully engaged in the procedures to do selection of vulnerable groups, WASH NFIs distribution sites. Cross-cutting issues will be mainstreamed.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CMC will ensure equal participation of men and women in the project for example CMC’s Hygiene promoters and pump mechanics will comprise of both men and women and during selection of these groups, the selection criteria will be made clear for example, pregnant mothers will not be used as pump mechanics. Protection threats will be considered in every activity for example, separate latrines for male and females will be made for institutions and clearly marked for beneficiaries to identify, locations for WASH NFI distribution will be decided through consultation with women, activity time will be agreed upon between CMC response team and locally hired staffs.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CMC will ensure gender inclusivity in the project and all staffs including field staffs who will have attachment with the community will be made to sign the CMC code of conduct which strongly prohibits violence, exploitation, deprivation or neglect. Beneficiaries will be sensitized on their rights to report every case of violence, coercion and exploitation to relevant authorities or CMC protection officer. Protection will be at the canter of CMC’s response.
