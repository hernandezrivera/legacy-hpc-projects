Project Summary

As the coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic sweeps across the world, it is causing widespread concern, fear and stress, all of which are natural and normal reactions to the changing and uncertain situation that everyone finds themselves. SCI will continue to;
• Provide CP services; to children and their caregivers, Case management including alternative care support
• Strengthen capacities of community based mechanisms and government capacity including COVID – 19 guidelines and adaptations,
• Prevention of, and response to GBV and
• Provision of psychosocial support (PSS) & specialized services eg (MHPSS) to children and their caregivers.
Considering the impact of COVID-19, on people’s mental health, SC will be monitoring and responding to protection needs of children and families together with national authorities, thus SC will be providing information and guidance to staff and the beneficiaries we serve. SC will continue to mainstream CP across all sectors and all phases of project implementation in save the children to maximize the benefits of save the children intervention and reduce possible risks against boys and girls.
After the implementation of the above mentioned intervention, about 20,000 people will be reached children (5,000 girls; 7,000 boys) and (1000 men; 6000 women) will be reached by 2021. As part of the implementation, SC common approaches such as parenting without violence (PWV) and Steps to protect children in conflict. In the phase of implementation, COVID-19 adaptation for child protection case management that must have both child protection and safety at the core.

1 - Needs

As at 28 April 2020, Nigeria has recorded one thousand five hundred and thirty-two (1532) cases of COVID-19 across 34 states in Nigeria including FCT Abuja. Borno state has recorded 53 cases with three deaths. Factors that may contribute to the spread of COVID-19 are a weakened health system, multiple disease outbreaks, high population concentration in specific urban centers, lack of access to safe potable water and sanitation infrastructure, inadequate awareness on preventive measures, and traditional practices. This is particularly true in the conflict-affected states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY states), which have been affected by over ten years of violent conflict. The impact of the humanitarian crisis in the conflict-affected BAY States presents one of the most significant vulnerabilities to the spread of COVID-19. The situation is a complex, protracted crisis with huge developmental needs, damaged infrastructure, lack of governance, and the rule of law outside Maiduguri and major garrison towns. The most vulnerable population groups are children, women, the elderly, and people with chronic medical conditions. The biggest fear is the effects on the decreased immunity associated with diseases and risks typical of humanitarian contexts such as malnutrition, measles, malaria, HIV. Thousands of children are displaced and in need of protection intervention, the protection risks and GBV risks children face have been heightened by the COVID – 19 pandemic. These include child-headed-households, unaccompanied and separated children, and adolescent boys and girls are particularly at risk of SGBV, child recruitment, and human trafficking.SC seeks to improve impartial access to services for children to their basic rights and strengthen the overall protection environment. SC will be responding by providing interventions such as psychosocial support through building the capacity of caregivers to provide PSS at home, house to house visits while maintaining IPC guidelines in the coming 2-4 months where CFS might not be open and establishment of CFS post COVID – 19. SC will conduct a risk analysis and review child friendly spaces activities to meet the PSS needs of children at both individual and family level, alternative care arrangement/placement or provision of foster parents, strengthening community based protection structures, as well as prevention of and response to GBV and further provision of more advance mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). Most CFS activities would be at the initial/preparatory phase due to COVID – 19, proper CFS activities will resume post COVID -19

2 - Activities

Case management Support (CP/GBV)Identification and provision of case management service to UASC and other children at risk including child headed household, survivors of SGBV, children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG) etc. If CAAFAGs are released, they will be placed under SC trained standby foster parents in line with their best interest. This will be implemented as follows:i. Provision of personal protection equipment (PPEs) for field staffsii. Face to face home visits and case management support for only high risk cases (level 1) or those who are otherwise out of contact (The use of PPEs is mandatory)iii. Remote case management support for other level 2&3 cases. Caseworkers will be provided with recharge cards to facilitate their remote follow ups in order to minimize person to person contact. However, if no means of remote communication are available, community support Eg neighbor’s/ community volunteers, child protection committee members with access to phones and PPE kits are used to communicate to the cases safely and maintaining the confidentiality and dignity of the cases/ beneficiaries. iv. Remote Identification, training and support of standby and actual foster parents including COVID – 19 IPC guidelines. Trainings will be done in small batches whenever the COVID – 19 lockdown measures are relaxed. v. Sensitization and awareness rising session to populates on child protection key child friendly messages including gender base violence and COVID – 19 IPC guidelinesvi. Mentoring and coaching of caseworkers on IPC measures and COVID – 19 case management guidelines and adaptations including supervision on the cases they are supporting remotely virtually through non-contact methods, phones and at the field while maintaining social distancing.vii. Update the referral pathways in the respective locations through desk reviews using non-contact methods in line with COVID – 19 guidelines and referral of vulnerable children to access inter-sectoral and other specialized services.PSS/MHPSSi. Support caregivers and provide PSS especially to those without access to safe infection control materials for their home, resources for safe and healthy quarantine and/or access to information on how to care, protect and talk to their children. This will be done face to face through community volunteers and also remotely where necessary while maintaining physical distancing and IPC guidelines.ii. Provision of face to face and remote MHPSS specialized services to children showing complex signs of distress/mental health while maintaining IPC guidelines.iii. Provide facts about COVID-19 and give clear child-friendly information about how to reduce the risk of infection and stay safe in words that children can understand (e.g. demonstrate to children how they can keep themselves safe, show them effective handwashing)iv.Provide psychoeducation services to caregivers and children through non-contact methods e.g Anger mnagementv. Provision of PFA and PSS through remote and non-contact methods e.g Through phone calls.vi. Training of frontline workers on PSS/PFA, identification and reporting of CP concerns.vii. Re-opening of child friendly space post Covid – 19 with age appropriate and culturally sensitive orientated activities for boys and girls while aligning to IPC guidelines and physical distancing and or train caregivers/foster parents on signs of distress to look out for in children under their care and how they can provide PSS at home using SC HEART and PWV modules during this COVID – 19 pandemic. OTHER ACTIVITIES• Capacity building for community based structures including community stakeholders and child protection related topics and COVID-19 prevention.• Advocacy to the government on Child protection COVID – 19 related issues and continued collaboration with the MWASD on case management and other CP services provision to children and caregivers.• Protection and MHPSS mainstreaming in other Sectors
