Project Summary

The Gaza Protection Consortium (GPC) integrates cash assistance delivered by the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) and Mercy Corps, protection assistance delivered by NRC and Médecins du Monde France (MdM) and heath assistance delivered by MdM and Humanity & Inclusion (HI) across Gaza, in collaboration with local partners. The Consortium delivers humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable population affected by the humanitarian crisis and violations of their basic rights. Between October 2020 and March 2022, the GPC provided a lifeline to 8,689 of the most vulnerable households in Gaza affected by deep poverty and/or emergencies.
The GPC follows a ‘cash plus’ approach, whereby protection and health beneficiaries are identified from the cash caseload to provide integrated and comprehensive support to households and better safeguard protection outcomes. This beneficiary identification pathway is distinct from the pathways used for standalone NRC, MdM and HI projects outside of the Consortium, meaning that, while activities are similar, there is no overlap in the support provided. The GPC aims to contribute to learning and evidence regarding how an integrated ‘cash plus’ approach, after the identification of needs through a unified household level baseline assessment, can result in stronger protection outcomes compared to cash alone.
This project will support approximately 12,000 of the most vulnerable individuals across Gaza by providing multi-purpose cash assistance, protection case management, multi-disciplinary rehabilitation and assistive devices, and mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS). The project will train 60 humanitarian staff in protection mainstreaming, 342 emergency volunteers training in emergency rehabilitation and 114 healthcare providers in MHPSS, including mhGAP and GBV.
Specifically, the project aims to:
(1) Enhance the ability of households to meet their critical basic needs by providing five rounds of MPCA, with protection mainstreamed throughout and aligned with Cash Working Group (CWG) standards.
(2) Provide shock-affected households with emergency MPCA within 72 hours to restore access to urgent needs during an escalation or weather-related emergency through a Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM).
(3) Increase the ability of vulnerable individuals, with particular attention to women and girls facing violence, to address the protection risks they face by providing general protection case management and community protection mobilization activities with a focus on protection and prevention of violence and abuse.
(4) Improve the availability, accessibility, and quality of MHPSS services at the community and secondary healthcare level.
(5) Improve the independence of women, men, girls and boys with disabilities through the provision of multidisciplinary rehabilitation (MDR) services and assistive devices.

The project supports vulnerable households in Gaza living under blockade to meet their basic needs and protects their rights. The project supports strengthening of resilience among the poorest and most vulnerable strata of the Gaza population, improves access to services and restores more dignified living conditions. It protects the most vulnerable Palestinians through a protection-oriented, multi-sectoral approach that encompasses emergency relief, community-based protection, and MHPSS. Finally, the project complements the efforts of the Palestinian National Cash Transfer Program (PNCTP), run by Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and co-financed by the European Union, to support households living in deep poverty.

1 - Needs

a) Unemployment, Poverty and Social Protection: Gaza continues to have one of the highest unemployment levels in the region; over the past four years PCBS reported an average unemployment rate between 44% and 52%. Jobs are scarce and the PNCTP is failing to deliver steady and predictable cash, in part due to the delay of donor funding to the Palestinian Authority. The impact of the May 2021 escalation, COVID-19 and lack of viable livelihood in Gaza increased the number of households registered on the waiting list. Low incomes and high rates of unemployment lead to extremely high rates of poverty, with over half living in poverty, of which one third are living in ‘deep poverty’ (expenditure below ILS 1,974 per month) which makes Gaza among the poorest in the region. Households hold significant debt, taken on primarily to meet basic needs, with 83% reporting outstanding debt in the latest MSNA. b) Food Security: According to the latest MSNA, 63% of households in Gaza are experiencing severe or moderate food insecurity. Food insecurity is high among women due to more significant difficulties in joining the labour market; 64% of women and 34% of families headed by women are food insecure. The primary reason for food insecurity is insufficient financial resources. The current crisis in Ukraine has exacerbated an already precarious situation; since February 2022, wheat flour prices in Palestine have increased by around 23%. Price increases add another layer to the challenges faced by the Gaza population and food insecurity will continue to pose a challenge. c) Protection: The socio-economic vulnerabilities faced by Gazans are compounding and a catalyst to increasing protection risks, including persistent insecurity, physical violence, prolonged exposure to severe psychosocial distress. Decades of Israeli occupation and the ongoing conflict exacerbate risks of violence and discrimination that women and children face, including domestic violence, sexual abuse and forced marriage. GBV is still dominant, but the actual numbers are not captured correctly. Women and girls face widespread and varied forms of GBV, underpinned by high levels of gender inequality, supported and promoted by a strong patriarchal culture. The Protection Cluster has identified 990,000 people in need of specialised protection services in Gaza; 50% women and girls. The number of Palestinians at risk of protection threats increased over 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic and resulting lockdowns and the May 2021 escalation; these events also significantly decreased the preventive and responsive capacity of protection actors, authorities, and communities. d) Disability: Persons with disabilities are exposed to heightened risks and remain amongst the most likely to be left out of humanitarian assistance, due to environmental, institutional and attitudinal barriers. According to the latest MSNA, 21% of households in Gaza include at least one member with a disability, compared to 9.7% in the West Bank, and 9.3% of households have at least one child (age 5-17) with a disability. 17.8% reported that a member of their household had experienced difficulties in accessing one or more services (e.g., education, health clinics, etc.) due to mental or physical difficulty. e) MHPSS: There is a high need for MHPSS integration in the healthcare system in Gaza. A 2021 MdM Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice Survey among Primary Health Care service providers in Middle Area reflected limited integration of MH and cross-cutting topics such as GBV and persons with disabilities. The survey revealed that only 7% of doctors expressed confidence in their MH skills (screening, diagnosis, and management of mental disorders). More than 50% of doctors and nurses had never received capacity building in MH. A study conducted by Save the Children in June 2022 found that, since 2018, the psychosocial well-being of children, young people, and their caregivers has declined to alarming levels due to a combination of chronic instability and protracted social and economic hardship. According to the latest MSNA, 40% of households reported at least one member experiencing signs of psychosocial distress or trauma in the past year; this figure increases dramatically to 84.2% when considering only adult members of the household (over 18 years). f) Shocks and Emergencies: Gaza is at risk of small, medium and large-scale emergency situations related primarily to military escalations and bad weather. Between 10 and 21 May 2021 Gaza experienced the most serious escalation since 2014; 253 Palestinians were killed, including 66 children and 38 women. Year-on-year bad weather places strain on run-down infrastructure, and power supplies, especially for vulnerable communities living in areas prone to flooding and densely populated. The blockade continues to result in a chronic electricity deficit and an inability to import critical building supplies, leading to damaged sewage and drainage networks. g) Gender: Palestine is 119th of 188 countries on the UN Gender Equality Index. Gender plays a key role in vulnerabilities and exposure to risk in Gaza in particular. Women are more affected and burdened by the lack of income and employment opportunities, which undermines their access to. Women, in all their intersecting identities, in particular age and disability status, also suffer the consequences of violence, poverty, and deprivation of basic rights. Female headed households account for 11% of all households in the oPt. These households are under stress with unpaid care of vulnerable family members and lack of livelihood and child education opportunities. The political and legal fragmentation between the West Bank, Gaza, and East Jerusalem poses a major obstacle to the realization of women’s rights and protection of women and girls. At present, no laws specifically criminalize GBV such as femicide, domestic violence, and sexual harassment, abuse, and assault.

2 - Activities

R1 (MPCA, NRC and MC): Five rounds of MPCA, aligned with CWG standards:1.1 Nomination of 1,000 unserved households from PNCTP waiting list and CBOs, prioritising female headed, elderly, HHs with member with disability, chronic illness, or many dependents, and living in deep poverty. Conduct household baseline assessment (UVAT with integration of Washington Group Questions and MHPSS questions).1.2 Distribution of MPCA using Bank of Palestine ATM cards or PalPay, using CWG transfer value (60% of the minimum expenditure basket (MEB), 1,081 NIS) and providing five instalments (based on GPC evaluation).1.3 After the third and last transfer, conduct PDM to observe and analyse how the cash was spent and what needs were met. Best practices and lessons learned shared through CWG.1.4 Exit strategy: Referral of individuals able join labour market to job creation programs led by development actors. One nexus referral platform is set up with UNDP. GPC is seeking additional opportunities and piloting income generation activities with MPCA beneficiaries. GPC is a member of the SP CVA TWG and engages with MoSD to strengthen the national system. R2 (MPCA, NRC): Emergency MPCA to shock-affected households through a RRM:2.1 Analysis of trigger and development of response plan. Selection of beneficiaries from lists of affected households from MoSD, MoPWH and CBOs. Close coordination with relevant clusters, OCHA, CWG and local stakeholders to ensure no overlap.2.2 Distribution of MPCA up to 72 hours after shock using PalPay and CWG transfer value (60% of the survival MEB, 754 NIS).2.3 Referral to specialized services through existing referral pathways e.g., shelter cluster. Up-to-date mapping to facilitate this.2.4 During an escalation, use communication and advocacy channels to highlight the situation and impact, including the need to respect IHL and IHRL.2.5 PDM to observe and analyse how the cash was spent and what needs were met. R3 (protection, NRC): General protection case management and community protection mobilization activities with a focus on protection and prevention of violence and abuse:3.1 Referral of individuals in need of protection services from cash caseload.3.2 Protection case management by trained NRC Protection Case Workers as per SoPs, including assisted referrals to services and tailored psychosocial support (level three of MHPSS pyramid). Strengthening of existing referral pathways through cluster. Cases in need of child protection and GBV promptly referred to specialized actors and if appropriate, case management handed over.3.3 Protection community engagement activities by Ma’an to support and strengthen community based protective mechanisms and build a protective layer for individuals, based on agreed SoPs (level two of MHPSS pyramid).3.4 Capacity building activities on protection related topics for staff, local partners, local authorities and CBOs. R4 (protection and health, MdM): Availability, accessibility and quality of MHPSS services:4.1 Referral of individuals in need of MHPSS services from cash caseload.4.2 Community: MdM, in partnership with CFTA (City, Khan Younis, Rafah) and Al Atta (North, Khan Younis, Rafah), will provide structured community-level support as per agreed SoPs (level three of MHPSS pyramid), including detection, assessment, psychoeducation, supportive counselling, problem management sessions, trauma recovery sessions, and family-caregiver counselling.4.3 Hospital, North: MdM will increase the capacity of Awda staff to deal with common mental, neurological and substance use disorders through establishment of a MH Unit and training medical teams in identification, emergency response, management and referral (level four of MHPSS pyramid).4.4 Hospital, City: MdM will establish an MHPSS Unit at Al Shifa Hospital in partnership with the MoH. Activities will be led by MoH MHPSS team (psychologists) and MdM will provide mhGAP training. MdM will procure psychotropic drugs at the MoH Central Drug Store (level four of MHPSS pyramid). R5 (health, HI): MDR services and assistive devices:5.1 Referral of persons with disabilities in need of MDR from cash caseload.5.2 Assessment of needs by technical team (physiotherapist, nurse, occupational therapist and psychologist), involving caregivers and family, and development of individual treatment plan.5.2 Provision of MDR, including physiotherapy, psychosocial support, occupational therapy, nursing, speech therapy, wound dressing kits, and assistive devices. Outreach services will be operated in specific cases.5.3 Replenishment of five contingency assistive devices stocks across all governorates and capacity building for emergency volunteers on emergency rehabilitation.5.4 Referral of beneficiaries to additional relevant services and update the services available in line with the needs identified in the Local Referral Network. The referral mechanism from the cash caseload to protection and health services is managed by NRC data and MEAL colleagues. A SoP has been developed to outline the criteria and method for the referral. Data protection agreements are in place and information is shared through protected methods. GPC is developing a unified database to assist with this process. Where beneficiaries need services that GPC cannot provide, partners use existing referral pathways through the cluster system. NRC, HI and MdM can receive referral cases from clusters and partners are working with cluster leads to ensure criteria are clear. NRC, MdM and HI are committed to providing support to local partners at the programmatic, technical, and operational level to build capacity and ensure sustainability. This is outlined in partnership agreements between respective organisations and demonstrated by the longstanding nature of the partnerships across multiple projects, e.g., HI has been working with PACF since 2009.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The Gaza Protection Consortium recognizes, based on its analysis (baseline, midline, endline), that most households have multi-sectoral needs. In particular, they owe substantial debt, require seasonal items such as clothes, transportation, education and medical items as well as food and other basic needs which cannot be covered with the in-kind or voucher modality. Multi-purpose cash allows households the dignity to choose what their most pressing needs are at any given time. However, cash alone cannot address the non-financial and structural barriers that the Gaza population face, meaning that there are certain services which are unavailable or unattainable in the Gaza Strip, regardless of income. GPC’s strategy is therefore to complement cash assistance with relevant protection and health services, prioritizing those families with protection vulnerabilities in order to alleviate the burden that cannot otherwise be addressed. This approach, GPC’s “Cash Plus”, is a comprehensive integrated approach providing MPCA and otherwise unavailable services to beneficiaries with specific needs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Beneficiaries are selected primarily from the MoSD waiting list from the PNCTP, as well as through CBOs to ensure vulnerable households do not fall through the gaps of the system. A household baseline assessment is conducted by a team of enumerators using an adapted version of UVAT, with integration of Washington Group Questions and MHPSS questions. To ensure the quality of data collection and to mitigate any risk of hardm, GPC provided a two-day training for enumerators on data collection and field work, protection principles, safeguarding, PSEA, feedback and complaints, disability and inclusion and mental health and psychosocial integration. All households selected for the program are unserved, live in poverty or deep poverty and suffer from multiple protection vulnerabilities. Each household receives a standard MPCA transfer based on CWG recommendation. Each protection and health vulnerability that can be addressed by the Consortium is addressed while all out-of-scope protection and health vulnerabilities are referred out to existing service providers via cluster pathways. Cash is transferred to beneficiary’s accounts and can be withdrawn using a Bank of Palestine ATM card. GPC recognises that there is a risk that access to assistance could be unequal through which discriminatory practices would be reinforced, as well as a negative impact on HH dynamics and assistance functioning as a pull factor for harmful practices. To mitigate this, the GPC will remain mindful of the equal access to assistance for all beneficiaries. The GPC will continue to conduct studies and learning into how assistance impacts HH dynamics, positively or negatively, especially any gendered impacts. Moreover, the GPC will monitor and measure the impact of the implemented inputs. Any negative impact will be picked up and analyzed, followed by corrective measures. In 2022, NRC conducted a country-wide protection mainstreaming self-assessment that included a significant component of Do No Harm. Based on that self-assessment and subsequent document review, the country office created a mainstreaming/DNH action plan. The Action Plan is intended to ensure all activities uphold NRC’s protection mainstreaming minimum standards and are rooted in the centrality of protection. Specifically in this project, all protection specialized Staff with solid background in social-work, psychosocial support and community-based activities have been hired and are supported by a Protection PDM and regional case management adviser. A Protection risk analysis matrix has been developed to assess risks and mitigation measures developed including: a solid capacity building plan in place on Protection in humanitarian action, referrals, case management and psychosocial support and on NRC internal Protection SOPs and Global Protection standards and guidance; establishment of supervision mechanisms; update of the existing referral pathways, SOPs and relevant MoUs. There is a risk throughout programming that actual or perceived fraud will occur. To mitigate against this, each partner has detailed and well-established administrative, accountancy, financial and logistic procedures which are respected and followed by staff and implementing partners. Internal and external audits are regularly conducted to provide a high level of safety in the management of funds. For cash assistance, the GPC bases its selection process to the most vulnerable households on clear vulnerability criteria and conducts a 100% verification process through home visits. Mercy Corps has carefully selected Bank of Palestine and PalPay as the primary financial service providers. The above measures safeguard do no harm by ensuring that beneficiaries are aware why they have been selected, and what assistance they should expect. The assistance provided does not put them at risk, instead, it allows them to better sustain themselves and access basic needs and services.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project uses existing structures to determine its target group. The Consortium works with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) waiting list of households, which are unserved by the National Cash Transfer Program (NCTP) despite being eligible and living in poverty, as determined by the national threshold. The MoSD sends a list, prioritising those with specific needs, including people with disabilities, chronically ill, female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women and those with a high number of dependents/elderly. The GPC then conducts a baseline assessment, using an adapted version of the CWG Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool. The assessment uses the Proxy Means Test Formula to assess poverty and includes questions to assess wider vulnerability in terms of income, shelter, access to services, coping strategies and food security. For the 2022 assessment, given the expansion of Consortium activities, the GPC’s vulnerability tool has been updated to include questions to assess mental health and psychosocial concerns within households and the Washington Group questions have been integrated to capture the prevalence of functional disability among the household. Households with most prevailing protection and socio-economic vulnerabilities are selected. Every household that matches the eligibility criteria (has the highest protection + socio-economic vulnerability) has an equal likelihood of being selected.
