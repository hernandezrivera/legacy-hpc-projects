Project Summary

The Impact Health Organization (IHO) is proposing a project titled "Integrated Primary Health Services for Vulnerable Populations in Kapoeta East and Aweil North Counties" under the South Sudan Humanitarian Plan 2024.
The project aims to improve access to and utilization of essential healthcare services for all people in need, thereby reducing excess morbidity and mortality. This is in response to the Interagency and IHO needs assessments in Kapoeta East, Eastern Equatoria, and Aweil North County, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, South Sudan. The health systems in these locations are challenged with limited access to human resources, medicine, other supplies, blood transfusion services, obstetric care service, mental health services, WASH services, and a hampered referral system due to poor roads. The communities also have limited access to health education and low immunization. The leading causes of morbidity include Malaria, Pneumonia, Watery Diarrhea among children, HIV/AIDS, and Tuberculosis (IHO 2023; WHO 2023; UNICEF 2023).
The project seeks to decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from illnesses such as malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine-preventable illnesses like measles. This will be achieved through the management of illnesses including malaria RDT screening and treatment, as well as a measles vaccination campaign. The project will also provide access to life-saving essential healthcare, including mental health to women, men, girls, and boys, including ability-challenged persons.
To reduce the vulnerability of women, men, girls, and boys at risk of mortality, morbidity, and protection incidents/threats in priority areas, the project will undertake protection monitoring, advocacy, awareness raising, and risk mitigation. The project will integrate water, sanitation, hygiene, nutrition with health, including sexual and reproductive health to improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality healthcare services.
To reduce the suffering of girls, boys, women, men, older persons, persons with disabilities, and other persons with specific needs at risk of, or who experienced violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect, including gender-based violence, the project will increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders, and the elderly.
To strengthen the feedback mechanism, the project will consult the population groups targeted to receive assistance throughout the entire cycle of the response by establishing a community feedback mechanism. Lastly, the project will strengthen coordination and contextual analysis of needs by working closely with the state ministry of health and county health departments to meet the emergency needs of 22611 Individuals (Boys 6865 Girls 6734, Men 4750 Women 5262) 22% from Kapoeta East (9444 Individuals) and 78 Percent from Aweil North (14167) as per needs.

1. Needs

The health needs in South Sudan are significant and multifaceted. Decades of protracted conflicts, seasonal flooding, economic and political instability continue to negatively impact access to essential health services (WHO 2022; UNICEF 2022; WHO 2023). Recurrent disease outbreaks, poor immunization structures, inadequate qualified health personnel, and high malnutrition rates contribute to high morbidity and mortality rates reported for children under 5 years across the country (WHO 2022; UNICEF 2022; WHO 2023). In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19 (WHO 2022; UNICEF 2022; WHO 2023). According to the 18 August 2023 Integrated Food security phase classification (IPC), the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that 9.4 million people need humanitarian assistance in 2023, a staggering 76 per cent of South Sudan’s population2. This is an increase of 5% (500,000 people) from 2022. In 2022, 7.74 million people were in Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or above) reaching the highest in the seven-year history of the GRFC during the April–July 2022 lean season2. IPC projection (April – July 2023) puts Aweil North (IPC phase 4), and Kapoeta East also in the IPC phase signaling both counties are in an emergency state among the counties that are most likely to be affected (UNICEF 2022). These continuous and emergent crises have contributed to South Sudan’s poor health indicators including an immunization coverage rate of less than 50%, under five mortality rate is 90.7 per 1000 live births, and a maternal mortality rate of 789 deaths per 1000 persons (WHO 2022; UNICEF 2022; WHO 2023). The majority of the population (about 56%) reside more than a 5km walking distance from the nearest health facility (WHO 2022; IHO 2023; UNICEF 2022; WHO 2023). This is further coupled with inadequately qualified healthcare practitioners, inadequate access to supplies and medical equipment, and poor health workers' competencies and morale (WHO 2022; IHO 2023; UNICEF 2022; WHO 2023). Sociocultural barriers have also limited the uptake and utilization of primary health care services (WHO 2022; IHO 2023; UNICEF 2022; WHO 2023).These targeted counties are inhabited by an extremely vulnerable and traumatized community majority being women, girls, boys, and elderly people, persons with disabilities who are physically and psychologically exposed to a multitude of stressors (floods, conflicts, disease outbreaks, etc) (WHO 2022; IHO 2023; UNICEF 2022; WHO 2023). This poses a great gap in terms of health services. In order to prevent further escalation of disease outbreaks and loss of lives, IHO will ensure the provision of lifesaving quality health service to 92825 individuals (boys 25976, girls 25551, Men 19196, and Women 22102)123.

2. Project activities

The Impact Health Organization (IHO) plans to undertake the following key activities based on the indicators:1. Percentage of Health Care Facilities meeting all WHO IPC Minimum Requirements targets: IHO will work to improve infection prevention and control (IPC) measures in 4 health facilities (2 PHCU and 2 PHCC).2. Coverage of PENTA3 in <One-year-old by administrative unit: IHO will strengthen routine immunization services to increase the coverage of PENTA3 among children under one year of age.3. Number of community health workers per 1,000 people: IHO will recruit and train additional community health workers to increase their density per 1,000 people.4. Number of persons with disability receiving rehabilitation services disaggregated by age and sex: IHO will provide rehabilitation services to persons with disabilities.5. Number of persons receiving MHPSS services disaggregated by sex, age, and disability: IHO will provide mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services to those in need.6. Number of consultations per person per year disaggregated by sex, age, and disability: IHO will ensure that all individuals, regardless of sex, age, or disability, have access to health consultations.7. Percentage of deliveries assisted by a skilled birth attendant: IHO will work to increase the percentage of deliveries assisted by a skilled birth attendant.8. Case fatality rate for malaria by age and gender: IHO will implement malaria control measures to reduce the case fatality rate.9. Percentage of reported alerts being verified within 24 hours: IHO will strengthen disease surveillance systems to ensure that reported alerts are verified within 24 hours.10. Coverage of reactive measles vaccination of boys and girls between 6 months – 15 years: IHO will conduct reactive measles vaccination campaigns in response to measles outbreaks.11. Number of health facilities supported with Interagency Emergency Health kits (IEHK): IHO will provide Interagency Emergency Health Kits (IEHK) to health facilities (2 PHCU and 2 PHCC).12. Number of SGBV survivors receiving CMR services by age and sex and disability: IHO will provide clinical management of rape (CMR) services to survivors of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV).13. Number of community health events conducted for behavioral change communication: IHO will conduct community health events to promote behavior change.14. Number of meetings conducted with the County Health Department: IHO will regularly engage with the County Health Department to coordinate activities, share information, and address challenges.15. Percentage of facilities with community feedback mechanisms: IHO will establish community feedback mechanisms in health facilities.15. Number of community consultative meetings conducted to identify needs, participate in response, and reduce the impact on their lives disaggregated by men, women, boys and girls and disability: IHO will conduct community consultative meetings to involve community members in the identification of needs, planning, and implementation of responses, and monitoring and evaluation of activities.These activities will be implemented in a phased manner, with regular monitoring and evaluation to ensure that they are achieving the desired outcomes. IHO will also work closely with the government, other NGOs, and community leaders to ensure the success of these activities.
