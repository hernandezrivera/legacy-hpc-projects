Project Summary

This project will involve life-saving interventions, including infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and malnutrition activities, responding to the needs of the most vulnerable communities residing in Lebanon, with a focus on pregnant, lactating women (PLW) and children under five. The proposed activities will align with the nutrition sector’s priorities, building on existing capacities and experiences and complementing ongoing projects implemented by IOCC and others.
Through this project, IOCC will provide malnutrition prevention and treatment services to the most vulnerable population in Lebanon, thus decreasing risks of morbidity and mortality. The proposed interventions are to:
- Provide skilled nutrition counselling on optimal maternal nutrition, IYCF, healthy nutrition and ECD targeting PLW and the caretakers of children under 5 at the community, primary health care (PHC), and hospital levels
- Provide awareness raising activities on optimal maternal nutrition, IYCF, healthy nutrition and ECD to PLW, caregivers of children under 5, and the community at large.
-Establish integrated nutrition and early childhood development corners in all governorates
- Establish mother support groups to empower the community and encourage positive behavior change in IYCF practices
- Refer the community to available IYCF services and follow up on service provision
-Refer beneficiaries to other services (cash, food, WASH..) as needed and follow up on service provision
- Monitor, follow up on, and address violations of the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes, and enforce law 47/2008
- Provide capacity building sessions to organizations providing breastmilk substitutes to ensure safe provision of artificial feeding support, in line with national and international recommendations
- Provide PLW and children 6-59 months with micronutrient supplements, including multiple micronutrient supplements (MMS), micronutrient powder (MNP), or other nutritional supplements
- Screen for acute malnutrition at the community level and scale up the family Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) approach, strengthening referrals to primary health care centers (PHCCs) for screening and treatment of malnutrition
- Support PHCCs to provide malnutrition services through on-the-job coaching and guidance, and subsidize malnutrition treatment consultations and transportation for acute malnutrition cases
- Cover the cost of malnutrition treatment services at the hospital level

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

IOCC mainstreams Protection in the different phases of the project. IOCC follows a needs-based approach and target those with highest levels of vulnerability with appropriate assistance. FGDs and assessments conducted at the beginning of the project with men and women include protection. IOCC ensures that services are within safe and easy reach and are culturally relevant and socially acceptable. IOCC developed consent forms to ensure that neither stories nor photos are written or taken without the written consent. All staff sign a code of conduct that includes PSEA commitments, and regular protection trainings are provided. IOCC has a PSEA focal point to coordinate the implementation of IOCC PSEA policy and practices. IOCC’s CRM was updated with a focus on sensitive allegations, and CRM policy and procedures and communication materials were developed. Activities are selected in a way to ensure that both women and men can participate actively and can benefit their families.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

FGDs, satisfactory questionnaires, and observational monitoring visits are developed to ensure that beneficiaries are not at any risk and to share feedback and address concerns and complaints. IOCC set up a safe line for beneficiaries in case of any complaint regarding the assistance provided. Awareness sessions are disseminated to introduce the CRMs and inform them of their ability to direct complaints to IOCC’s safe line. Participation starts with the collection of data for surveys that helps develop the awareness sessions strategy in each area. The results of those surveys are discussed through FGDs which include beneficiaries from the host and refugee communities who will together agree on the best way to address hygiene among them, mentioning the topics that might interest them most and leave a greater impact on their daily lives. IOCC team develop participatory activities that apply various communication techniques adapted to both gender and different age groups.
