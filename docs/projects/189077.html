Project Summary

This proposed project will address emergency shelter and non-food items needs of vulnerable populations affected by conflict, flooding or in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) acute food insecurity both displaced and in secondary displacement. The project will target 55,000 individuals (Men 8800, Women 9900, Boys 13200, Girls 23100) in situations of emergency or transition both newly displaced and protracted IDPs and host communities, in Lakes (Rumbek North), Northern Bahr el Ghazel (Aweil South) and Warrap (Tonj North and Tonj South). Those to be targeted are most vulnerable populations in need (after an assessment). The project will reach 15% of the target population (8250 individuals) as People with Special Needs. 100% of the target population will receive Gift In-Kind assistance which will be prepositioned from the Cluster Core Pipeline. After an assessment has been done, and an analysis report submitted through the SFP to the Cluster at the National level, a pipeline request will be submitted. After approval, items will be transported to respective CCOSS storage facilities and subsequent distributions. CCOSS will coordinate will other cluster partners in respective locations for effective response. According to South Sudan Joint Market Monitoring Imitative (JMMI) report of November 7, 2021, there are no functional markets in our target locations. In locations such as Aweil South (Maleek Alel) where there is a market, it has reduced functionality, thus not able to sustain a CVA program.
Crucial information gaps remain a challenge, with poor access to many parts of Lakes, Northern Bahr el Ghazel and Warrap due to insecurity and inadequate infrastructure. Lakes, Warrap, and parts of Northern Bahr el Ghazel states have continued to remain one of the most difficult locations especially for humanitarian agencies to operate. This is due to drivers and multipliers of the crisis that have remained present over time. These include insecurity and violence, local and inter-communal violence (IVC), ongoing displacement, sparse basic services, disease, climate shocks, flooding, economic instability, and insecure access to food and livelihoods.
Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) will ensure that there is proper coordination especially in locations where there is more than one implementing partner. County cluster meetings will be held weekly to ensure proper coordination, avoid double-counting, and as well reduce duplication of efforts. Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) is a key member of other clusters working within the same locality as well as a State Co-lead for Lakes and Warrap state.
Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) will use humanitarian hubs for coordination meetings and other related humanitarian activities. Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) will be working with other partners who are operating in the same areas or locations on the issues affecting vulnerable populations in hard to reached areas through mobile response in collaboration with other partners on the ground. Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) will also work with other partners on area-based approaches through the open forums during cluster meetings where partners would highlight activities being implemented and locations of operation to avoid duplication activities. This would be achieved through coordination of the cluster and support of CCCM, coordinating the activities of partners to avoid overlaps of the same activities in one location.
General Operation & other direct costs include costs associated with providing direct management & other support functions at the Main office in Juba. This includes management staff salaries, communication, stationery, rents, etc. The Indirect project costs include bank charges, staff recruitment & servicing costs, auditing and consultancy costs.

1 - Needs

In the last quarter of 2021, shocks related to conflict and insecurity have likely driven multi-sectoral needs across Warrap State. Findings indicate that the effects of these high levels of conflict have primarily been seen in Greater Tonj, seemingly leading to displacement on an intra- and inter-county level, and driving a deterioration of shelter conditions for already food insecure IDP populations. Moreover, there are indications of particularly severe levels of food insecurity and vulnerability to further shocks in Greater Tonj, primarily related to low levels of harvested food stocks, displacement and decreased livelihood access and diversity Inter-communal violence has also been a major driver of insecurity and displacement in Rumbek North County. There are longstanding tensions that have resulted in violence and cattle raids. On February 21, more than 15 people were reported in a revenge related attacks between clans from Rumbek North and Tonj East. Many people have been displaced as a result of these attacks. IDPs and Host Communities in Rumbek North, Tonj North, and Tonj South, and Aweil South have the highest severity of needs. Within these population groups, some of the vulnerable groups that may have specific needs include children, women at risk, the elderly, people with disabilities, single-headed household members, and the extremely poor. Being unable to support themselves through communal connections and without coping mechanisms from other resources, people who are outside camps have very limited or no access to essential life-saving shelter or NFIs. In both protracted and new displacements, people are forced to leave behind basic household items and shelter materials as a result of conflict compounded by food insecurity and economic deterioration. Those to be targeted include women and children will remain especially vulnerable due to their roles in society and the protection risks inherent in being without shelter or social support networks. A small number of host community members do not have sufficient emergency shelter or non-food items (ES/NFIs) as they have used their already limited resources to provide for the displaced. People’s ES/NFI needs are primarily linked to conflict induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration and disease outbreaks. While people outside of camp-like settings have better access to shelter materials, their overall ES/NFI needs are traditionally broader due to the absence of functional and accessible markets and lack of communal connections or coping mechanisms from other resources. This is particularly the case for IDPs who have been subjected to multiple displacements. CCOSS will focus its interventions on communities in crisis and emergency in the four Counties namely; Rumbek North, Tonj North, Tonj South and Aweil South. The beneficiaries will be populations in need and will be targeted using the cluster targeting criteria: • Households with children under 5; • Pregnant or lactating women (PLW); • Unaccompanied elderly (>60 years); • Persons living with disability (PLWD); • Female headed households; • Child headed households and/or unaccompanied minors; • Chronically ill people; • Persons with no effective community linkages. CCOSS will use participatory approaches to identify the needs of women, men, boys and girls. In this proposed project, barriers to participation of women and other vulnerable groups will be discussed and taken into account in both assessment and implementation stages. Gender and age considerations will be included in needs assessments prior to implementation. CCOSS will also train staff, community leaders and community members on AAP methodology. This will ensure that AAP/CCE will be incorporated in all the stages of the project cycle.

2 - Project activities

The following activities will be implemented. The needs will be identified after conducting a needs and verification assessment. Shelter items will include tarpaulins, plastic sheets, ropes, bamboo, and, local poles, while lifesaving NFIs will include mosquito nets, blankets, kanga, and kitchen sets. These activities will protect displaced persons from harsh weather conditions and protection risks as well as enhance access to other basic services. Some of the targets will assume an overlap between Shelter and NFI as some people are likely be reached with both 1) Engage the community in a way that stimulates active participation and promotes accountability to the affected populations. This will be through focus group discussions; setting up complaint and feedback mechanisms (feedback desks in the community) prior to project implementation; formation of committees. This will enhance the community’s dignity while increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and build on existing skills. 2) Provide assistance with an in-kind standard emergency shelter to 2400 displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Rumbek North, Tonj North, Tonj South, and Aweil South. 3) Provide assistance with an in-kind light emergency shelter to 3500 displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Rumbek North, Tonj North, Tonj South, and Aweil South. The emergency shelter will include plastic sheets. 4) Provide assistance with in-kind standard lifesaving NFIs to 2400 displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Rumbek North, Tonj North, Tonj South, and Aweil South. 5) Provide assistance with in-kind light NFI kits to 3500 displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Rumbek North, Tonj North, Tonj South, and Aweil South. 6) Provide assistance with in-kind flood kits to 22,300 displaced populations displaced by floods in Rumbek North, Tonj North, Tonj South, and Aweil South. 7) Provide assistance with in-kind individual Shelter/NFIs to 4200 displaced populations in situations of emergency or transition in Rumbek North, Tonj North, Tonj South, and Aweil South. 8) Provide assistance with essential emergency shelter upgrades to 14,800 highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non-displaced in Rumbek North, Tonj North, Tonj South, and Aweil South. 9) Provide assistance with essential NFI replacements to 7,800 highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non-displaced in Rumbek North, Tonj North, Tonj South, and Aweil South. 10) Monitoring of quality service provision – PDM will be conducted two weeks after the distribution is finalized. The format for the PDM will include conducting focus group discussions, random sampling for interviews. This activity will ensure a fast, lifesaving response to the right people, enhance the ability to use S/NFIs for supporting results-based health, protection, livelihood, and hygiene needs, and reduce overlap to reach the truly vulnerable. 11) Mainstream HLP AAP/CCE and conflict-sensitive approaches throughout all stages of S/NFI response through conducting conflict analysis, sensitizing local chiefs on women rights, especially about land, avoiding manipulation of distribution of S/NFIs by remaining neutral and practicing the “do-no-harm” principle. This activity will reduce harm to communities and build resilience by strengthening existing systems and community dynamics. 12) Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response – this will be done through conducting gender safety audits, ensuring that distributions are done in an open space, during the day, and close to the camps. This will ensure gender and protection risks are addressed directly, mitigating the chances of exacerbated vulnerabilities

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The community participation in the planning implementation and monitoring of the interventions will also be solicited. Care for Children& Old Age in South Sudan will set up desks in the community manned by community volunteer representatives who will be trained on how to get the complaints and feedback. The Project Officer will on a regular basis go through the feedback received and act on the information accordingly. The feedback will inform the project on the exact needs of the beneficiaries. For instance, beneficiaries will be asked which items are of priority, and ranked before any distribution is done.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Complaint and Feedback desks will be set in strategic places in the community. Some of these desks will be manned by some community members who will assist other community members about the issues they want to raise. Illiterate community members will be assisted with those who are able to read and right by documenting their complaints and feedback. Households with members with disabilities will be visited and their complaints and feedback received. CCOSS will also train staff, community leaders and community members on AAP methodology. This will ensure that AAP/CCE will be incorporated in all the stages of the project cycle.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Shelter/NFI and protection responses are closely intertwined. Therefore, protection considerations, notably the principles of Do-No-Harm and non-discrimination will prevail in the assessments, program design, while implementing as well as when monitoring and evaluating. CCOSS will thus prioritize (1) Safety & security, including HLP land tenure security, and dignity; (2) Meaningful access, including for women, children, persons with disabilities or other persons with special needs; (3) Accountability, for example through community-based feedback mechanism; and (4) Information, consultation, participation and community empowerment. The project will train the Relief Committees and community volunteers on PSEA. The CFM desks in the community will also provide information to the community and beneficiaries about PSEA

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Complaint /feedback mechanism will be agreed upon and established prior to commencement of project. Monitoring of the complaints and remedial action will be documented during the project implementation cycle. This will be done with the goal of promoting community engagement and accountability. The community participation in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the post distribution will also be solicited. For instance, the community will be involved in the identification and selection of community volunteers to help in distribution among others. After the completion of the distribution, a monitoring team will be sent to visit selected samples of beneficiaries to confirm the actual quantity received and the quality of the relief commodities, as well as to collect further feedback from beneficiaries. Once complaints are received, CCOSS will look for the most appropriate way to respond to the complaints
