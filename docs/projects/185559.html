Project Summary

Cameroon has enjoyed relative peace since independence. The Anglophone Crisis that started in November 2016 as a result of a strike action orchestrated by lawyers and teachers generated violence in the North-west and South-west Regions of Cameroon. The violence that is still continuing has led to the loss of several lives, family separation, displacement of persons, loss of property, means of livelihood, destruction of villages, burning of schools and houses, destruction of basic social amenities and disruption of services. Most of the existing structures in the communities that provided basic social services are totally disrupted, this leads to massive human right violation, increase gender based violence and child abuses. According to a report by OCHA published in November 3th 2021, the north west and south west regions in Cameroon have 2.2 million people affected by the crisis, 1.6 million targeted for assistance, 573,900 internally displaced persons with 383600 returnees in these two regions, 68600 Cameroonian refugees in Nigerian. These figures have increased in the last days with increasing attacks taking place in some yet to burn down villages.
In a report by ‘New Humanitarian’ News Agency, Education became a victim of war since 2017 with a call for school boy cut and according to UNICEF and educational authorities despite an increase in the enrolment of children in communities; in the north west, out of 76,478 children enrolled in primary school at the time of lockdown only 23428 representing 31 per cent had access to education during the multiple lockdowns. At the level of the secondary education, out of 41626 students, only 11649 students had access to education representing a 28 per cent of its population. These figures are far less than the figures the NW had recorded before the crisis. The quality of teaching has been abandon in the hands of untrained professionals who are more involve into money making than providing a quality education to the children who are in dire need to be educated. The non-formal learning or alternative form of education within communities though in some communities and partners have yielded fruits, it remain inaccessible to many especially the underprivileged, physically challenge, and children who are the most vulnerable set of the population. There has been an increase attacks on schools and education as many grave violations on human rights have been recorded in the past weeks like the killing of a pupil on her way to school in Buea, the killing of Tawtaw a pupil of 8 years in Bamenda and the recent one witness in Ekondo Titi where 3 students were killed and a teacher. From statistics two out of three schools in the NW remain close keeping more than 700,000 children and adolescent been affected by school closure.. Though some communities in the last academic year have seen their schools resume with a great increase in the number of pupils and students, some communities have rather remained a shadow of themselves at the educational level. More than 70% of schools have been destroyed, while students, teachers and other school personnel are subjected to routine violence and intimidation if they refuse to toe the boycott line. Rampant kidnapping of students and teachers, molesting and paying of ransom for release has further made things difficult to many education stakeholders in the North West and south west.
There is no gain saying that targeting the education system is “putting the future of an entire generation of children at risk, children who with the right support and opportunities can build a more stable and prosperous future”, (UNICEF spokesman Toby Fricker). We often say, failing to educate a generation is not only jeopardizing its future but its present as well. Through this project, the Centre for Human Rights and Peace Advocacy(CHRAPA) as the lead organisation with its grassroots organisations operating in the field on day to day bases like ANYEA

1 - Besoins / Needs

Arising from the urgent need to harness and coordinate school resumption activities in the 2021/2022 school year in Belo municipality, a municipality that pioneered secondary education in Boyo Division and prior to November 2016 had one of the best secondary general and technical institutions in Cameroon, some concerned and affected stakeholders coming from various works of life came together in a platform made up of CHRAPA as the lead, NKEF as consultant and ANYEA a grass root CSOs and education cluster member to chart the way forward through advocacy and the encouragement of community participation initiatives. This action was necessitated by the reality that for the past three years, attempts by some concerned elite and individuals to encourage school resumption were uncoordinated and hence yielded negative results. Since many community education stakeholders, children and teachers had been internally displaced, harassed or even murdered (Mr Chia Kuo Emmanuel-then Vice Principal of GBHS Belo, Miss Akong Chia Renata former teacher GTHS Njinikejem),a Think Tank had to be formed to encourage the education of the less privileged who remained in the municipality at the apex of the crises. This think tank in their pioneer meeting resolved to advocate for the formation of a general assembly of lovers of education in the municipality via a whatsapp chart group; for now the think tank executes group resolutions pending the holding of a general assembly meeting in Belo when the security situation shall improve.The aforementioned activities of the platform encouraged by voluntary donations of 650,000 FRS by members due to the scarcity of funders in Education and underfunded nature of the crisis in the two English region of Cameroon were used to advocate for the opening of public and private educational spaces in the four zones of the municipality. Regrettably as we present this proposal, only private spaces are functional as attested by resumption statistics obtained from official sources .This progress in our opinion represents a good beginning. Funds from the humanitarian response plan (HRP) shall ensure that many children in dire need also receive quality and inclusive education with minimum expenditure. Execution of this project by a Coalition of Stakeholders that began the initiative without external funding facilitates project implementation. The platform with a good knowledge of the terrain is an incentive for more work to be done and sacrifices to be made mindful of the risks of moving to enslaved villages like MBessa where there are functional private secondary and primary educational spaces..The people are basically peasant farmers. Coffee is the main cash crop. It is subsidized by beans, maize, bananas and Irish potatoes from the rich volcanic slopes of the Ijim mountain. Cattle rearing is a prominent activity of the Fulani in the municipality. The Cultivation of tea is gradually gaining grounds in Ndawara area where the Ndawara Ranch is located. These peasantry activities, however, still leave most of the Communities of large families below the poverty line. It is worthwhile mentioning that some of the people do carving, hunting, weaving and timber exploitation. The interplay of the relief, settlement pattern and the socio-economic life of the people have their obvious influence on the general trend of education in the Sub Division.The ongoing crises have made it difficult for these subsistence farmers to sell their produce; an activity that really helped them to pay for the education of their children mostly in public educational spaces where fees are relatively cheap compared to private educational spaces. The collapse of the local economy is the main reason why parents who were unable to relocate their children to relatively safe zones of the country really find it difficult to pay school fees. Regrettably, any armed non state actors are more comfortable with children study in rural areas
