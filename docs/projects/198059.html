Project Summary

To contribute to the overall WASH objective and ensure populations affected by displacement have reduced risk of exposure to WASH related diseases, NRC will strengthen populations’ capacity to engage in safe practices to promote better hygiene behaviours to limit health risks associated with poor WASH practices. NRC will use a range of technical approaches to provide the hardware required for access to safe water, improved hygiene and safe sanitation.
Target group
NRC targets the most vulnerable individuals and communities affected by crisis including IDPs and vulnerable host communities, in both urban, peri urban hard to reach areas including bushes and rural areas NRC WASH interventions in the Far North region aims to reach 15,000 persons (7,650 females and 7,350 males) in 2143 Households. Specifically, the following beneficiaries are targeted:
1. Internally Displaced Persons
2. Returnees (includes refugee returnees)
3. Vulnerable Host Communities
4. People living in areas currently not accessible
5. Persons living with disabilities and those with special needs
Beneficiary selection:
The identification of beneficiaries is based on the findings of needs assessment conducted by NRC and guided by gaps on the current 5W matrix at cluster level and guided by the Emergency tracking tool updated and shared by IOM every month. The final selection of beneficiaries will be done during focus group discussions with the community in a beneficiary validation process involving NRC’s CFM staff. NRC prioritizes vulnerable households headed by women and children who will benefit from WASH kits and construction of latrines. when households headed by men are identified as part of the most vulnerable, NRC will ensure that women in those male-headed households are the focal points to receive the assistance for the household, while making sure that this respects the principle of do no harm. NRC promotes women’s active participation to ensure community ownership and gender inclusion. Community engagement
NRC ensures adequate community engagement throughout the programme to strengthen an integrated, coordinated, gender-responsive water, hygiene and sanitation programme. Engaging with the users/ residents/ refugees/ displaced people helps them be active participants in the response rather than passive beneficiaries. It should make interventions more effective, efficient, and sustainable. The WASH intervention community engagement covers all four areas: i) community participation, ii) community mobilisation including behaviour change interventions, iii) community management and iv) accountability. For the strategic period, it is necessary to focus on strengthening the capacity in community engagement through improving community participations and behaviour changes techniques. A minimum number of staff need to be able to use and analysis participatory and qualitative methods such as observation and focus group discussions whenever relevant.
Gender inclusion in WASH: A particular focus is placed on women-headed households, girl children, persons living with disabilities and the elderly. Within the socio-economic context of Cameroon, women and girl children carry much of the domestic chores and other time-consuming burdens. As such, by bringing water points as well as hygiene and sanitation services closer to populations, NRC considers that beneficiaries, and in particular women and girl children, will be less exposed to protection risks and will be better able to engage time and energy elsewhere, potentially in income generating activities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Social services and WASH infrastructure in the Far North region are insufficient to meet the needs of the displaced populations (IDPs, refugees and returnees) due to either having been destroyed during conflict or poorly maintained. Due to insecurity and community conflict, the affected population have left their homes seeking refuge, living in informal settings and traveling long distances in search of water. Host communities have also seen their livelihoods and access to basic services including water supply severely impacted by the current crisis and thus have little capacity to support the IDPs. According to the multisectoral need assessment (MSNA), about 34 per cent of the population still collect drinking water directly from stream and rivers whose quality is limited. This is due to the insufficient number of improved water points and the existing ones are mostly non-functional. The operation and maintenance of water points is managed at community level through water management committees however, the fact that women seldom participate in community level decisions with little representation in WASH committees doesn’t allow addressing their specific water and hygiene related needs. In addition, access to improved water points remains a challenge for people with disabilities and generally the distance from households to water point is too long and incidences of gender-based violence remains an issue of importance. The trend of unimproved and non-functional water points is observed throughout the region with four and out of five divisions in the region having less than 40 per cent of their population accessing an improved water point. This puts the need for safe drinking water third on the region’s priority needs for affected population (IDPs and Hosts) . Furthermore, sanitation practices are still lacking among target populations such that regardless of the existence of latrines in the communities, the population practice open defecation. This is attributed to the poor state of latrines that limit privacy and offers poor security disposing users to gender-based violence. Therefore, there is an urgent need not only to increase access to sanitation facilities but also improve the quality of sanitation services offered to affected population in Far North region.
