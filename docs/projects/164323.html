4 - Needs

As the Syria crisis enters its ninth year, the humanitarian situation for people in northwest Syria remains alarming. The hostilities in northwest Syria continue to impact heavily on civilians who have endured nine years of crisis, violence, multiple displacements and economic downturn. From 1 December 2019 to 14 January 2020, almost 350,000 people were displaced in northwest Syria, mostly from southern parts of Idleb area, 80 percent of whom are women and children. Many fled their homes within the space of hours without any belongings while others managed take with them what they needed to survive, believing that they would not be coming back. The ongoing conflict has forced families from their homes, with not even the most basic of necessities available to them. Of the four million people in northwest Syria, an estimated 2.7 million are in need of humanitarian assistance. The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2019) has concluded that there are 4.4 million people require NFI assistance as well as the number of people in need of NFI assistance in Idleb has increased by over 600,000. However, within the situation in some areas, such as the northwest, is in fact deteriorating amidst and increase in people in need in addition to significant overcrowding in Idleb and north-west Aleppo. Furthermore, needs remain substantial, both in terms of humanitarian life-saving needs, particularly those among the high numbers of newly displaced IDPs requiring core, supplementary and seasonal assistance. the most recent escalation of hostilities compounded an already dire humanitarian situation in northwest Syria, where an estimated two million people are internally displaced. Following an increase in violence that began in late April, more than 400,000 people have fled their homes in search of safety and to access to basic services. Entire communities of people fled north mostly to areas near the Syrian Turkish border that are already densely-populated.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: A breach of ethics. Mitigation: Nasaem will train the staff on the code of conduct, child safeguarding, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, and ask them to sign them. Risk: Staff may find sensitive protection cases. Mitigation: Nasaem’s field staff will receive training on the referral of cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to the available service providers in the area based on the updated service map.For the establishment of the new camps, Nasaem will ensure that the sites of the camps are safe as much as we can and shouldn't be proximal to frontline areas.It might be a risk of inter-community tension between the old and recently arrival IDPs due to varied assistance packages across persons, Nasaem will promote the effective contribution of communities actors in the camps and will coordinate closely with the cluster and use the CCCM cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content to avoid the tension that may arise.If different to recommend standard, we will identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. Nasaem will ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. Nasaem will make its best to inform all the targeted households by multiple tools.There might be a risk of occurring fire in the camps, Nasaem will ensure to set adequate fire fighting points in the targeted camps as well as all the required procedures are taken place to mitigate the risks in case of emergency cases.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Risk of tensions between IDPs and host communities. Mitigation measure: To avoid creating tensions among host communities and IDPs as not all of them will receive assistance, Nasaem will discuss the project with the local councils, camps' management and will also explain the project’s goal and vulnerability criteria to all communities, in addition, to ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs. Nasaem will make efforts in cooperation with the CCCM cluster to target % of vulnerable host residents especially for those who have greatly affected by the crisis. Risk: Complaints may arise from the beneficiaries. Mitigation: Nasaem will establish a feedback mechanism to submit complaints in case there is variance in the quantity or quality of items received. The feedback procedures will be communicated in an appropriate language and in a clear format. Nasaem will hire male and female to participate equally in all project phases. Risk: Risk of fire. Mitigation: Nasaem will provide awareness sessions in coordination with the Camps' managers and local councils to all beneficiaries. Nasaem will install a fire ponits to prevent the rsiks of firing in the proposed camps. Assumptions: Nasaem has a good rapport and coordination with the local councils and camps' management in targeted camps and communities and has been providing humanitarian aid in multiple sectors for many years ago at those areas, so Nasaem is acceptable in the community. Access to target locations remains possible. Risks of theft will be reduced keeping minimum the stock of items to be distributed through contracting suppliers to supply the fuel, olive pits and heaters at certain times and to certain locations. Mitigation measure: The warehouse will be mainly used for short-term storage, with minimum amounts of the returned item for preparations that precede distributions to smaller villages where the supplier will not supply the materials.
