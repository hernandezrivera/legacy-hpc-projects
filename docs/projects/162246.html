Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

NeedsChad hosts more than 460,000 refugees, among those more than 340,000 Sudanese in the East and 105,000 Central Africans in the South who have fled their country due to the violence in their respective countries. Since 2015, the Lake region has been severely affected by the Nigerian crisis. Attacks by non-state armed groups and military operations in borders with Nigeria and Niger have led to a massive population movement. These attacks continue to affect villages around KaigaKindjiria and Ngouboua with limited access for humanitarian assistance. In 2019, more than 5,000 new refugees were registered and Lake and relocated in Dar Es Salam refugee camp. To date, more than 100,000 Chadians from Nigeria have settled in Lake province. Those displacements people exert an increased pressure on existing structures in a context of low access to essential basic services (health, education, water). The nutritional situation remains worrying in view of the high rate of malnutrition in the country. The results from the national nutrition survey conducted in August 2019 shown that the prevalence of wasting is alarming, with the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) 12.9% [12.1 - 13.7] and severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate of 2.9% [2.6 - 3.4]. According to the results of surveys in refugee camps, 143 refugees and 3,562 returned children will be affected by severe malnutrition in 2020 in the Lake province. SAM children (both male and female) will be admitted for treatment regardless their sex. The only acceptable criteria is the nutrition status of the child. For more impact, their mothers will benefit from keys messages to prevent malnutrition.Activities1. Procurement and distribution of 3,705 RUTF boxes and others nutrition supplies, materials and equipment to health facilities 2. Ensure training and refresher training for 40 community health workers on the prevention and management of SAM 3. Provide training to 30 health workers on IYCF practices4. Setup 30 support groups to promote Exclusive breastfeeding and good IYCF practices5. Conduct mass screening and active case finding of acute malnutrition among children under fives in the targeted areas6. Ensure regular provincial coordination meeting 7. Conduct field monitoring visits and joint supervision to health facilities 8. Provide technical assistance to front-line staff Indicators1.) 3,705 children treated for SAM, disaggregated by sex and age 2.) 30 health care providers trained in the prevention and management of SAM, disaggregated by sex and age3.) One (01) quarterly joint supervisions organized4.) 3,562 men/women (caregivers) sensitized on essential nutrition actions5.) 4,038 children screened for SAM
