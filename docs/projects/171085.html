Project Summary

The project’s goal is to strengthen the comprehensive protection mechanisms of the women living in remote Area C areas in the Hebron governorate, especially victims of GBV. The project will be doing this through providing them with psychosocial, legal and economic support, as well as raising their awareness towards their rights, violence against women and sexual and reproductive health. The project will target mainly women survivors of GBV, women with disabilities or have a family member with a disability, and women who are heads of their households.
Activities to be conducted include MHPSS and psychosocial support, Legal consultation and representation, economic assistance, and awareness raising. The activities will be implemented in Area C remote villages in the Hebron governorate over the course of one year.
The goal of the project is to provide the beforementioned services in order to help empower and assist the target groups, enable them to attain their human rights and enhance their mental health as well as legal and economic status.
The project will directly target around 2000 women in these areas, spread over 31 villages. Remote services will also be provided in accordance with COVID-19 situation and restrictions as to be able to reach the largest number of beneficiaries possible.
The UPWC has previous experience in working with these communities, as well as having connections and relations with local CBOs in these areas which will enable us to reach a high number of beneficiaries who are known to be in need of assistance.
The UPWC has six community centers in the targeted areas, where most of the activities will be held, which enable us to be in constant direct contact with the communities. The UPWC also has a branch office in the city of Hebron that can make monitoring and implementation much smoother.
This project was planned through creating and working with focus groups in these areas to assess their needs, and we also conducted a study on the impact of COVID-19 on Palestinian women in the period between May 5th and June 4th of 2020, and one of the findings was the need for increased psychosocial and legal support for women in general and especially for GBV survivors, as Area C residents are subject to high shapes of oppression, especially with the halt of coordination between the Palestinian Government and the Israeli Occupation, their situation has become much more dire.
The approach adopted throughout this project will be participatory, whereas woman beneficiaries will be part of planning the implementation of the project and activities.
In relation to sustainable development, the UPWC builds its strategic plans in alignment with SDGs for the promotion of a Palestinian community that supports sustainable development. Our work revolves around SDGs 3, 5 and 10 (Good Health and Well-Being, Gender Equality and Reduced Inequalities).

1 - Needs

In an area with some of the most complex and long-standing conflicts with human rights violations, a dire economic and social situation is always of huge concern, an exclusion and deprivation of many rights are witnessed and stressed in other marginalization acts linked to social class, gender, geographic location and community. In this sense it is of a concern that one most marginalized group in the Palestinian society are poor women living in remote villages especially women who are heads of their household.Not only the outbreak of COVID-19 and its related movement restrictions, school closures, job losses and socio-economic distress have caused serious concerns about protection in oPt but additional challenges have arose (especially in area C) due to that halt of coordination between the Palestinian government and Israel resulting in economic deficiency of the government, and lack or even in some cases unavailable services provided to citizens living in Area C. An assessment by SAWA organization found that “The pandemic-related restrictions have exacerbated the prevalence of GBV, including domestic violence, sexual abuse and forced marriage.”29% of Palestinian women have been subjected to one type or another of GBV, it is triggered by many factors in the Palestinian society including: Patriarchal society and social Stigma: women are afraid to talk about it or seek any services. Whilst the society encourages the inequalities between women and men, boys and girls. The Israeli policies in general, especially those that concerns freedom of movement, home demolitions, arrest and Jerusalemite ID issues. GBV is promptly linked to poverty, its profoundly obvious that poverty increases GBV within Palestinian families, especially during the loss of income during Covid-19 breakdown (UPWC-other stats) Hebron is one of the most marginalized governorates in the West Bank due to the existence of many settlements and checkpoint in the city, settler violence, and the prevailing patriarchal society in this area, increasing GBV and lack of access to services especially in Area C which is neglected and deprived of all services, due to the urgent need of interventions in these areas, and our experience working with these communities, 31 communities have been selected. Most of them are Area C and one is a Bedouin community called Al-Ramadeen. The most vulnerable sectors in these areas are women especially heads of household, people with disabilities, and women elderlies. Sikka, one of the targeted communities, show that there are 25 women who are sole supporter of their children and family, supporting 56 children and adults. also, after contacting the local council of Ithna, one of the targeted communities, it was found that PwD are 6% of the community which is double the percentage in the West Bank, Sikka also shows that there are 24 people with disabilities in the community 9 of them are women, also that there are 18 elderlies women living alone with no support. 29% of the Palestinian women have been subjected to one type or another of GBV, Gender Based Violence is triggered by many factors in the Palestinian society including: Patriarchal society and social Stigma: whereas women are afraid to talk about it or seek any services. Whilst the society encourages the inequalities between women and men, boys and girls  The Israeli policies in general, especially those that concerns freedom of movement, home demolitions, arrest and Jerusalemite ID issues. GBV is promptly linked to poverty, its profoundly obvious that poverty increases GBV within Palestinian families, especially during the loss of income during Covid-19 breakdown, due to a study made by UPWC in May and June, 37% of Palestinian women sought economic support from organizations. Due to all those concerns and the urgent need of intervention for the sake of protection of marginalized women, Union of Palestinian Women Committees have implemented a study and need assessment through an online survey to study the social, psychosocial and economic effects of Covid-19 on the Palestinian society, afterwards focus groups have been implemented with youth and adult females to prioritize the needs as following:  GBV survivors are in urgent need of psychosocial aid due to the prevailing stress and anxiety that have increased during Covid-19 pandemic.  Legal aid (consultation and representation), Palestinian women suffering from GBV lack legal services that supports them especially in C areas where is there no law enforcement and remote services making it an urgent need to provide such services inside the communities itself. Raising awareness of GBV and Reproductive and sexual health for youth females, it’s very important to teach women from young ages about issues they might face and how and where they can seek help, since survivors in Palestine “prefer not to file a complaint or seek professional help, often due to cultural beliefs and stigma, the survivor’s fear of being exposed, or fear of being stigmatized by their community.” Especially in remote areas and Area C in which women are not provided of such awareness. A study made by UNFPA found that many women who attend to local NGOs seeking financial help and job opportunities and after that have attended training and workshops, only then they realized that they have been subjected to GBV. On the other hand, schools in Palestinian usually don’t tend to give classes on GBV and on reproductive and sexual health. It is profoundly clear that in order to achieve protection outcome, economic support is a must. Most of the GBV survivors come from poor families that have been badly affected by the Covid-19 loss of income, meaning economic support for families headed by females, and females with disabilities will make them at less risk of GBV.

2 - Activities

The planned activities in this proposed project mainly focuses on the results mentioned below, in order to achieve these results, the activities planned will be as following: * first number is number of results as above and second number is the number of activities.Result 1: Enhanced MHPSS and access to legal services including consultation and representation to women living in targeted areas.A.1.1: Provision of Individual Psychosocial Support for GBV Survivors in Targeted Areas: Two psychosocial specialists will be hired to provide psychosocial support for 100 GBV survivors in targeted areas in around 400 sessions (since one survivor might need up to 10 sessions). The session might be given in person, or remotely this will be decided upon the lockdown procedures and the urgency of the case regarding COVID-19. The telephone number of the specialist will be published on our website and Facebook page and through CBOs in the areas.A.1.2: Provision of Collective Psychosocial Support Sessions for GBV Survivors in Targeted Area: the same specialists hired for A.1.1 will provide collective psychosocial support in around 240 group sessions for 200 beneficiaries from the targeted area. The psychologist will determine the need of each group through a first session of need assessment and then plan the subjects that will be considered in each session. The beneficiaries will be women regardless of age or any other consideration but with priorities to families with PwD.A.1.3: (3) Recreational Activities for Stress Relief for GBV Survivors in Targeted Areas: 3 recreational activities will be implemented in the targeted areas that will enhance the well-being of women, such as trips, festival on women day etc. No. of beneficiaries are 100 women.A.1.4: Provision of Legal Individual Consultation for GBV Survivors in Targeted Areas: A Lawyer will be hired to provide legal consultation for 240 GBV survivors in targeted areas. The session might be given in person, or remotely this will be decided upon the lockdown procedures and the urgency of the case. The telephone number of the lawyer will be published on our website and Facebook page and through CBO’s in the areas.A.1.5: Provision of Legal Group Consultation Sessions for GBV Survivors in Targeted Areas: the same lawyer hired for A.1.4 will provide collective legal support in around 120 group sessions for 100 beneficiaries from the targeted area. The lawyer will determine the need of each group through a first session of need assessment and then plan the subjects that will be considered in each session. Subjects may include: inheritance, early marriage, rights in marriage, Personal and criminal law etc. A.1.6 Provision of Legal Representation for GBV Survivors in Targeted Areas: A lawyer will be contracted to represent 15 cases of women in courts. The beneficiaries will be targeted through our psychosocial and individual support in A.1.1 and A.1.4. A.1.7 Capacity building for front line psychologist and lawyers on how to use remote technologies for sessions: the idea of this activity is to support frontline workers so they are able to give the services they provide usually face to face in a remote digital environment. Beneficiaries are the coordinators, lawyers and psychologists No. of beneficiaries 5. A.1.8 Psychosocial first aid and referral system, and protection mainstreaming for humanitarian staff and volunteers: volunteers and staff must have basic knowledge of how to treat and engage of GBV survivors, and where and how the referral system works, the beneficiaries of activities are the staff and volunteers of UPWC working in this area which is 10. Result 2: Awareness of GBV and good reproductive and sexual health is raised in targeted communities especially for youth women. A.2.1 Raising Awareness Workshops Among Young Female Adults Regarding GBV and Reproductive and Sexual Health: women especially adults are not provided with awareness about GBV and how to react to it, and usually are afraid to seek help and support. 140 workshops will be given to 700 beneficiaries. A.2.2 Digital Campaign about GBV: A campaign that targets the community specially men and young males about GBV and its effects on the society will be implemented through UPWC on social media. Result 3: 1000 women are economically supported through the project, with special focus on elderlies and PwD.A.3.1: Provision of Provision and food Parcels for Female-Headed Households and Families with members with PwD: 1000 beneficiaries of women with special consideration to families with members with disabilities, elderlies and head of household females. Based on the priorities of the need’s parcels might include non-food items such as Diapers for children, milk for children, medicine for sick elderlies. Our volunteers and staff in coordination with local councils and CBOs in these areas will determine the poor families in need in each area and what exactly are their needs through field visit then the parcels will be prepared and distributed. * UPWC will implement most of the activities in its community centers.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

At UPWC we adopt the DO NO HARM approach where we believe in equal and impartial access to aid, and we understand that our work must have no negative impacts on the context and culture of the area. To ensure no conflict is inadvertently exacerbated by our actions, we implement a deep analysis and understanding of the context following the Do No Harm process (DNH) which requires steps such as: 1- Analysis of the context and actors.2- Identifications of connectors and dividers of the community we are working with and how the project will interact with them in a way that enhances connectors and cooperation.3- Study all possible consequences of our work.The relationship with beneficiaries is also important as we ensure proved accountability towards beneficiaries, and a good relationship. As a local NGO we already have built accountability with beneficiaries as we believe they are not only beneficiaries of our work but also key players in it.We make sure not to enhance exclusion and discrimination through our activities, especially with the power distribution. The safety and security of beneficiaries is a must in a work since we work with survivors of GBV. We also believe that some humanitarian aid can have negative effect on the resilience of communities and that is why our interventions are always accompanied by raising awareness so that one day those communities would be able to support themselves with no or less aid interventions. Safeguarding policies UPWC holds the obligation that in all and any circumstances and in any and all times to not put threat or jeopardize the life of beneficiaries including their physical and psychosocial safety and well-being. We do this through studying the context and the risks that may be caused and to exercise good judgement, caution and sensitivity in interactions.Due to our sensitive work with victims of GBV it’s important to us to ensure that any contact with GBV survivors of women and children wouldn’t not place them at risk in result of our contact, thus when women contact us we establish a safe method of communication such as what time is better (for example when the husband is at work, they can call us), and the method or tool of communication (for example) especially today with remote services through platforms such as zoom, which in some cases has dangers on confidentiality in such cases we usually explain to the beneficiaries in legal and psychosocial aid. Safeguarding children and adults is our main priority. Ensuring that everyone comes in contact with our work is at safe.Access to beneficiaries through remote services Due to covid-19 psychosocial and legal aid is mostly implemented through remote service and platforms such as Zoom and phone. On July our frontline worker, psychologist and legal aid providers have undergone a training on using remote services for providing services with Alianza por la solidaridad (Security guidelines for GBV remote service delivery) , the discussion of safety and confidentiality has been risen in these training, with the internet today it’s hard to guarantee 100% confidentiality, therefore our teams are to follow these procedure:1- Make beneficiaries aware of the risks of Phone or internet services on secrecy.2- Implement the services depending on the circumstance od each beneficiary with flexibility in timing.3- When beneficiaries are at immediate risk to contact responsible offical bodies such as (police, health care ect)In order to access women we have a network of volunteers in targeted areas, and we have a good experience working with local councils and local organizations.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The targeted communities in general can be classified as oppressed and suffering from inequalities, targeting them creates a base for ensuring equal and impartial access to aid. As we have specified before, GBV survivors, poor women, PwD and the elderly (Especially during COVID-19 circumstances) are more subjected to these inequalities, this is why we are targeting our project towards providing them with services and assistance. UPWC social workers will be in contact with local councils and CBOs to find the most in need and prioritize them, but no aid will be declined for those seeking it.Equal access to aid for children UPWC code of conducts ratifies non discriminative approach in services, aid and protection and equal access to aid for all people with no regard to age, race, gender and class. Our team is trained to work with children, and provide equal access to services, education and support. to guarantee equal access s to aid we will promote the services provdided to children through schools kindergartens and other service providers in the community. we will also guarantee access to most families in targeted areas especially those with children and PwD, so they will be aware of the services provided. through mechanisms such as field visits and provide transportation and means to access services if they are unable to do so. Disability mainstreaming UPWC have experience in working with children with disability especially through our child program and kindergartens whereas we have 50 certified teachers qualified to work with children with disabilities, the kindergarten are all equipped to adapt to the needs of PwD.UPWC follows the guidelines of IASC on the inclusion of persons with disabilities in humanitarian actions, and all staff must be aware of these guidelines, especially concerning these points:1- All protection programs are designed and adapted to ensure the inclusion of and accessible to everyone, including persons with disabilities. 2- Protection programs accommodate the individual needs of persons with disabilities by providing reasonable accommodations. For example, extension programs reach out to persons with disabilities who are isolated or distant and support their participation in decisions that are relevant to them.3- Develop outreach activities, including community-based outreach, to reach individuals who are isolated in their homes or institutions4- Take steps to assist persons with disabilities to obtain personal documentation. Publicize the importance of marriage and birth registration; organize mobile registration for refugees and other displaced populations, including persons with disabilities; make legal case management available to persons with disabilities who lack access to civil documentation.5- Monitor and report on violations of the rights of persons with disabilities. Include targeted violence, forced medical treatment, disability-related discrimination and barriers to accessing protection services. Follow cases up and remove barriers that impede or deter persons with disabilities from accessing protection services or reporting violations6- Design and implement protection interventions for persons with disabilities that assessments have found to be at risk. (Assessments need to be gender and age sensitive.)
