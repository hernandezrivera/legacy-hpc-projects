Project Summary

The overall objective is: “To improve the health of vulnerable mothers and children in Area C in Hebron and Nablus through community preventive health and nutrition intervention services. “The specific objectives: • Vulnerable children <5 , neonates , pregnant and lactating women have access to life sustaining interventions Mental health and psychosocial services are provided to vulnerable mothers and to vulnerable children at schools. The project responds to the First cluster objective “Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza and the West Bank /EJ including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building.” The selected interventions will be implemented at three levels; households, schools and community; using a multidisciplinary health approach. WV community health workers, and MOH service providers will join their efforts for optimal outcomes. At community level, WV CHW will work with mothers to change behaviors and practices mainly in infant care such as behavior communication change on breast feeding practices, complementary feeding, supplement intake, recognition of danger signs and providing psychosocial support to mothers showing signs of distress. In partnership with MoH, child friendly facility approach for infant and young child caring and feeding practices will be integrated at the field level at the clinic and/or village council premises to provide further technical support to mothers and children including growth monitoring, promotion activities, awareness sessions, support group meetings and so on. This project will complement the services provided by the mobile clinics service providers, the provided resources will improve the quality of services , WV community health workers will target mothers and their children with responsive and ongoing support , they will reach to those in the hard to reach areas , they will be the linkage between their communities and the service provides ( MOH,PMRS) to ensure proper service utilization ,ensure referral when needed to the appropriate channels, support services providers and provide individual support to those in need (ex :support mother who are unable to breastfed).At school level, psychological first aid- PFA- interventions will be done through training of teachers and counselors to provide assistance to affected children and refer cases, including GBV to the appropriate channels. PFA related extracurricular activities will be done at school level to overcome psychological stress of affected children. The project will ensure proper utilization of health and mental health services provided by different partners, mainly the MOH. Building in WV experience in the health sector ,we are proposing new expanded services , part of the proposed approach within project was evaluated by WV (specifically the targeted interventions to the mothers) ,results showed significant changes among mother not only in regards to their knowledge but also critical lifesaving behaviors such as illness management ,feeding practices ,service utilization and supplementation , ( evaluation results are available to be shared ,data was shared recently at the global nutrition cluster meeting ) ,those changes were reflected on mothers and children’s health.

1 - Needs

Needs for this project were identified through referring to the following sources:• WV health and nutrition assessment for 25 area C communities in Nablus and Hebron , a quantitative assessment was conducted through household survey for a sample of 427 on; Mothers Knowledge, attitudes and practices on issues related to maternal, newborn and child health and nutrition critical issues, and on Mental health for mothers. Support on data collection and analysis was provided from consultants form Al Quds University, the collected data included the communities that we will target under this project. • Database from WV health interventions in the targeted communities in Area C and data collected from WV trained community health workers• Review data available at MoH on topics including anemia prevalence among mothers and children, supplementation availability and others. • WV school assessment in cooperation with the MOE ( September 2019 ) it was conducted for area C communities in Hebron, that included data on number of affected students, availability of counselors, students with disabilities, protection issues and violations . The selected areas are specified as the most in need due to limited interventions from organizations and PA. Number of these villages, mainly in Hebron, are either adjacent to settlements or by bass roads that makes access to health services difficult mainly during political escalation, closure of roads, demolition orders and other Israeli regimes, Access to essential health services is very limited, the 17 selected communities that will be covered under this project have no access to structured health facilities, they only receive limited services through mobile clinics. Such limitation contributes little knowledge and poor practices on issues related to mother care, poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and new born caring practices. In Hebron; exclusive breastfeeding is practiced by only 3.4%, only 8.1% are practicing the correct management during diarrhea, 47% of the mothers are not giving their children iron supplements on regular basis and 54.4% are not giving Vit A,D regularly , only 26.8% of mothers are taking iron on regular basis . In Nablus communities 45.6% of mothers are not taking folic acid, only 4.8 % followed proper correct management during diarrhea among children. In Nablus communities only 5% of mother are practicing exclusive breastfeeding, only 8.8% are practicing diarrhea correct management, 37% of mother are not taking iron, folate on regular basis. Anemia prevalence among mothers and children in all the targeted areas is high, among children it’s 40.7% in Hebron and 48.3% % in Nablus whereas among mothers it’s 39% in Hebron and 29.6% in Nablus (WV assessment,2019). The majority of the interviewed mothers (95%) mentioned that they were not aware of maternal and infants danger signs and don’t seek any care once these signs appear .They also mentioned introducing unappropriated complementary meals and at very early age (WV assessment,2018). According to WV recent research regarding mental health, the data showed that the prevalence rate of postpartum depression in Nablus communities is around 20% (WV assessment,2018). Based on OCHA vulnerable school mapping and recent WV assessment a number of schools are frequently being affected by different kinds of violations , those schools were selected and prioritized they will be provided with psychosocial support ( this has been coordinated with the protection cluster )

2 - Activities

The proposed activities are designed to respond to the high needs of the selected communities, knowing that current WV services cover only 20% of those needs, additional support is still needed to achieve better health outcomes. Output 1.1. Pregnant and lactating women and children <5 receive targeted health and nutrition interventions. In cooperation with MoH, enrichment of infant and young child caring and feeding practices will be strengthened at community level through establishing Child friendly facilities. Facilities will be used by both health service providers and CHW to conduct activities related to child and mother health including growth monitoring, promotion activities, awareness sessions, support groups meetings and others. Facilities at health clinics and/or village councils will be rehabilitated and supported with needed tools and supplies to enhance the services offered especially CWDs. MoH field staff will utilize those spaces to provide the services supported by WV trained CHWs. CHWs (21), will target 500 mother and their children at household level using a social and behavioral communication change approach. Trained CHWs will visit mothers and address issues mainly related to infant care and feeding including behavior communication change on breast feeding, complementary feeding practices, assuring the supplement intake, recognition of any danger signs, pre-post natal care. CHW will be the linkages between their communities and service providers to ensure proper utilization and timely referral of mothers and children attending child friendly spaces and needed further support not provided in partnership with MoH , all the identified CWDs will be prioritized (30 CWD out of the 500). 1.1.1 21 CHWs will conduct home visits: (2 visits /month /households) 1.1.2 Establish 17 child friendly facilities and support them with tools/supplies 1.1.3 Rehabilitate health facilities to be accessed by CWDs; 1.1.4 Conduct advanced training for 38 CHWs and health providers on special nutritional and caring needs for CWD including first aid. Output1.2: Teachers, children and mothers’ mental health psychosocial status is enhanced Psychological first aid (PFA) training and activities follow up will be done jointly in partnership with Juzoor a specialized in this field. The training will be provided to school counselors, teachers and community health workers. Activity is to equip them with skills needed to deal with affected children including GBV and refer cases when needed. 9 teachers will receive an intensive related training. Extracurricular activities will be designed to help provide students with essential skills to increase their resilience. Counselors and Community health workers will be equipped with techniques and comprehensive tool kit to sustain interventions. For critical cases, including GBV, it will be referred through the appropriate channels 1.2.1 Train 9 counselors/teachers (4M/5F) on PFA.1.2.2 Conduct extracurricular PFA related activities in 9 targeted schools for 1940 children (880M/1059 F &23 CWDs) 1.2.3 Train 38 CHW and service providers on mental health and PFA 1.2.4 Insure referral mechanisms are in place Coordination with the protection cluster in regards to MHPSS took place. PFA training supported with basic tools will be provided to volunteers in each of the targeted communities. Active community participation and engagement in implementation and monitoring will be ensured and maintained as a way to ensure quality of work and draw lesson learnt for future interventions. Needs were discussed with communities, recommendations received from communities and partners were considered. Satisfaction surveys and FG discussions will be considered taken into consideration while preparing the second year of intervention. Services will be accessed by all while ensuring safety, mothers with neonates will be reached at households and CHWs will be from the same communities to avoid travel risk.

3 - Indicators

The project will target as direct beneficiaries: 21 female CHWs will be provided with advanced skills and will target mothers at the household level 500 mothers and 500 children boys and girls <5 (priority will be given to CWD) will be provided with consultations on prenatal, postnatal care, infant and young child feeding, caring practices at the household level 1000 visits /month (2 visits for each household /month) will be conducted on monthly basis by the trained CHWs. 17 health facilities /spaces will be provided with the needed tools and equipment to initiate and activate mother child friendly facilities ( equipment’s will be provided according to MoH/nutrition department standards ) 30 (4male and 26 female) of school counselors and community health workers who were trained on mental health and have improved skills on psychosocial support 1940 students (males 880–females 1095) including at least 23 with disabilities ( data collected from the MoE including number of disabilities ), those children are located in the of the most vulnerable 9 schools . The action will establish strong monitoring and learning mechanisms to assess and track progress against program objectives. The monitoring of the action will be conducted in accordance with existing WV JWG monitoring systems under the local responsibility of the field team in close liaison with national office and partners. To improve the monitoring process at the results' level, a Detailed Monitoring Plan (DMP) will be developed, which details all monitoring tools and monitoring time frames. Monitoring tools will be developed taking into consideration age and gender aspects. Monitoring will be done at 3 levels: among trainees, beneficiaries and facilities structures level. Tools will include clinics observation report including growth monitoring tracking and available services, baseline and end line of beneficiaries to measure changes in knowledge and practices including feeding practices, recognizing danger signs, supplement use, attendance sheets and capacities development of trainees, tracking of referred cases ( mothers, children and students). The action monitoring approach will follow key principals: 1 Joint monitoring practices through collaboration with the implementing partner, communities and MoH, MoE. WV has strong relationships with the action's stakeholders creating a foundation for shared responsibilities and accountability in M&E tasks.2 World Vision field team and in coordination with community members and project partners will be oriented on using the monitoring tools. It’s worth mentioning some of the action monitoring tools builds on existing monitoring mechanisms aiming at strengthening them and achieving better impact.3 Progress reports will be regularly collected on weekly basis and shared with key stakeholders including communities. 4 WV team will follow up with the training and extracurricular program in cooperation with implementing partner and MoH to ensure quality implementation of all activities. 5 End of project report will be shared with cluster and partners. Targets in relations to the Indicators: 1. # of < 5 children receiving targeted health and nutrition interventions (1000 child); 2. # of women in reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women receiving quality maternal and nutrition services (500 mothers); 3. # of healthcare workers trained on neonate interventions and SRH topic and management of Malnutrition (17 CHW); 4. # of people directly benefiting from awareness sessions and health education; 5. # of women received SRH services (500 mothers); # of health workers trained on different SRH topics (38 males & females); 6. # of people directly benefiting from awareness sessions and health / nutrition education (1000 males & females); 7. # patients with access to MHPSS services (500 males & females); 8. # of people directly benefiting from awareness session and mental health promotion activities ( 3500 males & females).
