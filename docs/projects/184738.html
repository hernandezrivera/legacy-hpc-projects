Project Summary

A delayed start, early end, and erratic rainfall distribution characterized the April to June 2021 Gu rainfall season. As a result, cumulative rainfall was below the 40-year average across much of the country, especially in central and southern Somalia. The poor rains led to below-average Gu crop production in southern Somalia and poor crop harvest prospects in agropastoral livelihood zones in the Northwest. Although heavy rains between late April and mid-May partially replenished pasture and water resources, these resources are inadequate to support normal livestock production until the start of Deyr season rainfall in October. Furthermore, floods caused further population displacement and damaged crops and farmland in riverine areas of Hiiraan, Shabelle, and Juba regions.
Based on the results of household surveys and field assessments conducted in June and July 2021, more than 2.2 million people face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes in the presence of planned and likely humanitarian assistance during the July to September 2021 period. An additional 3.4 million people are Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people experiencing acute food insecurity to 5.6 million. Humanitarian assistance for food security and nutrition, as well as government support, reached more than 1.6 million per month on average between January and June 2021. This assistance likely prevented the worsening of food security and nutrition outcomes across many parts of Somalia. Available humanitarian assistance plans indicate a similar number of people will likely receive assistance through September 2021.
From October to December 2021, food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr (October-December) season rainfall, continued insecurity and other food security related risk factors, including rising food prices and cost of living, declining availability of milk for both consumption and sale, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season. Without sustained humanitarian food assistance, 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes between October and December 2021. An additional 3.7 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 7.2 million. The October-December 2021 projections do not factor in the potential impact of food assistance, as information on planned and funded food and cash assistance through December 2021 was not available at the time of the analysis. Humanitarian assistance must be sustained through December 2021 to prevent Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes for 3.5 million people. Livelihoods support is also required for people that are Stressed (IPC Phase 2) or worse.
According to the results of 35 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2021, the overall median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Somalia remained Serious (11.1% weight-for-height z-score). The drivers of acute malnutrition include high morbidity, low immunization, low vitamin-A supplementation, reduced access to milk, and food insecurity. Urgent treatment and nutrition support are required for approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden), who will likely face acute malnutrition between August 2021 and July 2022, including 213 400 who are likely to be severely malnourished. Integrated interventions should be provided to support recovery and prevent deterioration in the nutrition situation.
Population groups classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, eradicating acute malnutrition, saving lives, and protecting and saving livelihoods.

1. - Projects activities

Nutrition: Activity 1.1.1: To enhance early detection, timely referral and treatment outcomes, 60 community nutrition volunteers (30 male and 30 Female ) that will be recruited from the respective targeted villages. The community nutrition volunteers (CNV) will be will work with Nutrition team and conducted community mobilization and community based screening malnourished children and PLW using MUAC tape and oedema checking as part of activities for early treatment of cases before deteriorating to severe cases with complications. Nutrition: Activity 1.1.2: This proposed project will support 8 OTP and 8TSFP sites that will provide treatment for 133,73 SAM cases in Qansax, Dinsoor Tayeglow and Elberde districts . All the centers will adopt Somalia IMAM guideline for the treatment of acute malnutrition. The OTP will be integrated with TSFP in both outreach and static sites. Activities will be done according to the Somalia Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM) guidelines for treatment of moderately malnourished children 6 to 59 months and PLW Nutrition: Activity 1.1.3: Treatment of 24,633 children (6-59 months) and 5,547 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with moderate acute malnutrition Qansax , Dinsoor Tayeglow and Elberde: The TSFP will be integrated with OTP in both outreach and static sites. The project will support 18 sites in the 4 districts of Qansax, Dinsoor Tayeglow and Elberde , each with 2 Mobile and 2 Fixed, and activities will be done according to the Somalia Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM) guidelines for treatment of moderately malnourished children 6 to 59 months and PLW Nutrition: Activity 1.2.4: 18,937 of girls and boys aged 06-23 months receiving specialized nutritious foods Nutrition: Activity 1.2.5 8,522 of pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will be receiving specialized nutritious foods. Activity 1.2.6: 25,000 Pregnant women and mothers with children under two years old will be screened for IYCF practices in Qansax, Dinsoor Tayeglow and Elberde by trained counsellors with nutrition team during the OTPs/TSFP or communities level. Activity 1.2.7:. 24,633 of girls and boys aged 6-59 months will be received vitamin A supplement in semester 1 Nutrition: Activity 1.2.8: 60 Community nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) will be trained on early detection and screening using Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) tape referral and home visits. The CNVs Training will also cover key nutrition, and sanitation and hygiene messages to support and reinforce key messages during home visits. Nutrition: Activity 1.2.9: Multiple micronutrient supplementation will be provided for 8,521 PLWs. Micronutrient are only needed in very small quantities but are essential for normal physiological function.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Golweyne relief and rehabilitation NGO has comprehensive safety and security guidelines with specific recommendations for teams operating in Somalia and has a robust Safety and Security Plan to safeguard GRRN beneficiaries, staff, and property, and undertaken a risk assessment and implemented mitigating measures. These guidelines are updated regularly in order to ensure appropriateness to the prevailing context. Weekly staff meetings are held at field level which include updating all staff about security incidents, how these impact our programmes and staff, and the decisions/actions taken. The safety and security team work closely with the regional security forums as well national security bodies such as the NSP (National Security Protection), (NGO security Programme), UNDSS (UN department for safety and security) and the local security authorities to access security information which often serves the early warning and early action purpose. Golweyne relief and rehabilitation NGO is committed to minimizing safety and security risks to staff and ensuring staff are given training, support, and information to reduce their risk exposure while maximizing the impact of our programmes for children and families. Golweyne relief and rehabilitation NGO seeks to provide safe and healthy working conditions for all staff, mindful that each individual staff member also has responsibility for achieving this. All Golweyne relief and rehabilitation NGO staff will have access to, and orientate themselves on, the Safety and Security policy, standards, procedures and supporting tools and guidelines. Protection Mainstreaming in Sector Activities: GRRN will ensure its staff have the awareness and capacity to identify any harm to children resulting from our programs, including sexual exploitation and abuse. Each staff, upon recruitment, and on an annual basis, is trained in child safeguarding on an annual basis, and signs the policy. A child safeguarding focal point is appointed and specially trained to deal sensitively and confidentially with any cases that arise. Accountability mechanisms ensure that community members understand how they can report child safeguarding concerns, to whom.Gender Analysis and Mainstreaming: In Somalia, gender dynamics are extremely complex and deeply rooted in the culture and religion, and particularly for the situation of women, who generally have limited access to education, healthcare, and economic opportunities. Pregnant and lactating women are clearly vulnerable to malnutrition. This program targets pregnant and lactating women of infants 0-23 months as well as community members such as husbands, grandmothers, religious leaders, and traditional birth attendants to create a conducive environment and support for mothers to adopt optimal IYCF practices. Mothers and caregivers with disabilities shall be attended to by the IYCF counsellors and follow-ups for those with special needs shall be done in the community by the same counsellors. GRRN will ensure equitable gender participation during training, committee formation, and feedback. GRRN will be working closely with pregnant and lactating women and women caretakers, and will ensure suitability of project activities to this target group. The project will collaborate with available mechanisms in the region to prevent any occurrence of gender-based violence, including FGC. This will be fully mainstreamed in nutrition and health education sessions. GRRN has not seen any evidence of a gendered dimension to children’s vulnerability to malnutrition in proposed regions, but will ensure that trends are analyzed on a consistent basis. To this end, beneficiary data will be disaggregated by age and sex. In order to work with women beneficiaries, GRRN strives to achieve gender balance in recruitment during emergency nutrition responses, though it faces major challenges to do so in Bay region. In the short term, GRRN advertises roles in a wide range of media.
