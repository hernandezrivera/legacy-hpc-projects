Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to prevent nutrition related morbidity and mortality caused by the negative impacts of the prolonged drought, economic hardships and the Covid19 pandemic. A set of integrated lifesaving nutrition interventions will be implemented in six priority districts (Seke, Gokwe North, Harare, Zvishavane, and Zvishavane Urban, Gweru rural) whose GAM severity is 4 and are experiencing high food insecurity. Pregnant and lactating women and children 0-59 months will be the main target groups. The targeted districts have nutrition structures at all levels though poorly resourced and lack capacity to sustainably drive nutrition-relevant action forward especially in the face of drought and difficult economic conditions. Leveraging on its global and local context experiences on health, food & nutrition, availability of technical expertise and physical presence in the target districts, ADRA will work through strengthening of local structures (including duty bearers) to improve delivery of health and extension services and integrate nutrition education and counselling at all levels. Health workers will be trained on (IMAM, cIYCF-e and Growth standards) in order for them to provide lifesaving nutrition services. Community volunteers will be trained on screening, cIYCF-e and reporting using the Rapid pro platform. Health workers (Nurses, EHTs, Ward Nutrition Coordinators) will be active in community engagement, through mobilization, follow-ups and facilitation of AAP activities. Scheduled provincial and district mentorship visits will be done to health facilities. Using the care group model, VHWs will be supported to work with the groups in disseminating cIYCF –e messages and conducting child caring demonstrations. They will then be supported in ensuring that they conduct screening fortnightly to children in their catchment area including the distant outreach points and submit data weekly using the rapid pro platform. To inform programming, SMART survey and data management training will be conducted in all the districts. Monthly (VHWs, HCC, FNC) meetings and reports will be submitted at the health facility. Where applicable, households hosting malnourished children will be referred to existing livelihood or safety net programs (SFP, LSA, Cash voucher) and or care groups for continuum of care. The MoHCC is overall in charge of implementation and monitoring of health and nutrition interventions at district level while ADRA will complement them with resources for enhanced monitoring, mentoring and timely accurate reporting. Cross cutting themes such as gender, environment, protection and AAP will be dealt with at all levels.

1 - Activities

- Supporting District Food and Nutrition Coordination Meetings in all districts.- Training and mentoring health workers (nurses, EHTs, Ward Nutrition Coordinators, nurse aides) on management acute malnutrition (prevention, identification, referral, treatment and discharge) - Training and mentoring village health workers (VHWs) on active case finding, referral and defaulter tracing and data management while reinforcing family led MUAC screening for children under five years.- Training health workers on community Infant and Young Child Feeding in emergencies and point of use supplementation while intensifying and cascading IYCF-e messages through the care group model in all districts. - Intensifying nutrition surveillance for Pregnant and lactating women and U5 children through supporting Health and nutrition outreach activities and ensuring increased coverage of micro nutrient supplementation for the different target groups. - Mainstreaming gender, nutrition sensitive agriculture, social protection through the care group model. - Conducting cooking demonstrations during the care group sessions- Data collection, reporting and documentation of best practices and lessons learnt.- Supporting monitoring, evaluation and learning activities which include but not limited to district and provincial mentorship visits routine surveys and SMART survey in all project districts. - Supply chain management for emergency nutrition products. - Supporting Community dialogues and community mobilisation towards increasing access and uptake of the health and nutrition services- Messaging, production and distribution of IEC materials (messaging for IYCF, MNPs health and hygiene and IMAM).-Resilience building and linkages to existing and emerging livelihood and resilience activities. •

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?
