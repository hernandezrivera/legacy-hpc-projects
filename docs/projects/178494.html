Project Summary

The project aims to deliver sustained assistance to the most chronically food insecure. It will focus primarily in Raqqa city, informal IDP settlements in multiple governorates while also increasing the provision of assistance to conflict affected areas along the Euphrates River in Deir-ez-Zor Governorate. This will be done in coordination with other food security partners in NES. The project will consist primarily of two main modalities of food security assistance dry food rations and food vouchers and will also include a complimentary activity comprising the provision of agricultural vouchers. A small stock of RTEs will also be pre-positioned in case of any new displacements or emergencies.

4 - Needs

After a decade of conflict, humanitarian needs across Syria remain extensive and severe. A rapidly deteriorating economy, the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and new and localized conflict have severely exacerbated vulnerabilities with an increasing number of people now reliant on humanitarian assistance. According to the 2021 Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 13 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, of whom 4.7 million people are in acute need. 6.7 million people remain internally displaced with many families living in overcrowded camps and collective shelters. There are 631,451 IDPs residing in communities across North East Syria (NES). Of these, 138,171 IDPs (22%) are residing in informal camps and settlements where access to basic services is limited and humanitarian needs are acute. Levels of food insecurity in NES have reached a critical level with underserved and hard-to-reach communities most severely impacted. The average wage for an unskilled laborer is far below what a household needs to survive. REACH reported that the average day laborer would need to work 64 days to earn the monthly cost of the basic Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) items. As households struggle to access employment opportunities, more and more Syrians are dependent on humanitarian aid to survive. This project will support the most vulnerable and at-risk households to support their basic food needs through a comprehensive unconditional food assistance program comprising food vouchers and in-kind assistance

5 - Implementation of activities

Improving food security for vulnerable households and IDPs through two food assistance modalities: food vouchers and general food rations (GFRs). We will also distribute to mulit-purpose cash (MPC) to the most vulnerable households with extensive needs. In addition, agricultural inputs will be provided using voucher modality and through technical trainings for farmers to improve their crop management techniques. Food rations will be distributed to both newly displaced IDPs, and extended IDPs residing in hard to reach areas where a voucher modality cannot be used. We will aim to support IDPs in informal settlements for 8 months, while newly displaced IDPs who are still moving will receive the same for one month. IDP and returnee households in areas with access to functional markets will be supported with a minimum of eight months’ food voucher distributions. This intervention will primarily be implemented in Deir-Ez-Zor and Ar-Raqqa Governorates. Support may also be continued in Aleppo governorate particularly in the instance of new displacement. We will target IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities using our extensively tested food voucher household assessment tool; 100% of households receiving assistance will be assessed. The NGO uses a multi-vulnerability assessment, in line with guidance of the FSS. The assessment tool uses a points-based vulnerability scoring system (examining household size, gender of head of household, income sources, number of young children, disability, food consumption scores, negative coping strategies, amount of productive assets.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Based on the analysis of the FSS sector of the numerous risks and potential threats linked to the implementation of the sector strategy, the NGO similarly identifies two key protection risks around distributions themselves and the possibility of causing tension between IDP and host communities. Mitigation measures are spelled out below: In order to mitigate the security/protection risks for all beneficiaries during distribution, they will be carried out early in the day to ensure beneficiaries can come and go with daylight. Women and men have separate lines at distribution points that are staffed by male and female field workers. In camp settings, multiple distribution points are established to help keep the number of people at one point to a minimum and to reduce waiting times. For out of camp distributions, the duration between assessment and distribution is kept to a minimum to avoid large distributions and to help deliver assistance to beneficiaries as soon as possible. Voucher distributions take place by mixed-gender teams at the household level. Whenever possible and appropriate staff from the area will carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive informational networks and good community acceptance. It ensures conflict sensitivity as our staff have an awareness to the social, cultural and political dynamics of the area and are representative of the program participants. The NGO also holds consultations between host and IDP communities prior to activities. Additionally CRM is in place and reporting methods communicated clearly with beneficiaries at all distributions and in all activities. Participants in all our activities can directly provide feedback or complaints about projects

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: - Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence in Deir ez Zour with extension of attacks and activities to surrounding areas -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. - Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations - COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the programme accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. • Flexibly shifting modalities as required between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. • Full engagement in, and the development of robust, continuity of services plans to minimise the potential humanitarian consequences of any disruption in services to target area • COVID-19 SOPs for general IPC in place as well as activity level SOPs.
