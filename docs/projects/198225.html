Project Summary

The situation of armed conflict that, since 20217, has plagued the Province of Cabo Delgado has made more than 900,000 internally displaced people. These people, who, in addition to being forced to leave their homes in search of a safer environment, have experienced traumatic situations of danger, deprivation and loss, are now in a situation of great vulnerability.
In line with MdM's strategic operational plan in the country, the proposed project is based on a three-pronged strategy that aims to streght intersectorial action focus on MHPSS and GBV service using health services as an entry point. Health services are often the first – and sometimes, the only – point of contact for survivors seeking assistance for gender-based violence. The project will provide MHPSS direct assistance to beneficiaries in need; conducting awareness-raising activities on MHPSS and GBV within the communities and capacity-building of local actors (governmental and non-governmental) in order to establish a lasting protection response capacity in Montepuez, Balama and Nangade. The intervention will contribute to the following cluster objectives:
Obj.1. Provide quality life-saving services to address inclusive protection immediate risks and needs (including CP & GBV) of most vulnerable populations in priority areas
Obj.3. Strengthen the protection environment and enhance the protection of affected populations – including related to GBV and child protection, and through collective and intersectoral action

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The conflict in Cabo Delgado occurs in a geographic area that has already been experiencing developmental and structural challenges and is one of the province of Mozambique with the highest rates of poverty in the country. The continued insecurity and the recent attacks in areas that until recently were deemed to be safe resulted in additional constraints for the response. As displaced, returnees and host communities continue to experience severe needs in all sectors, the situation in some of the inaccessible areas in the north of the province as Nangade is of highest concern. With displacement becoming increasing protracted, the initial positive response from host communities starts to fade with increasing dispute over resources increasingly on humanitarian assistance. This also contributes to IDPs moving to other locations or back to districts that might not yet be safe. Displacement puts men, women, girls and boys at risk and vulnerable to various forms of violence, including Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and MHPSS in host communities and relocation sites. Addressing GBV in fragile and conflict affected situations requires a strong understanding of the added complexities of people on the move or in transient camps that may end up becoming long-term living situations. The challenges, and consequently solutions, in different situations are obviously varied, but the health sector plays a pivotal role in managing and preventing GBV across all settings.Investing in health and social services that address GBV is therefore critical to improving the health and well-being of refugee and displaced populations. While the program focus on increased availability and accessibility of women, girls and youth to health to end preventable maternal deaths and unmet need for family planning, efforts will be done to strengthen health facility-based GBV and MHPSS response through survivor-centered and gender transformative approaches that improve access to services, strength coordination and referral pathways and challenge social norms and practices that perpetuate GBV and harmful practices. The intervention will frame and contribute also to increase access to MHPSS services to resettlmenet sites and host communities.MHPSS programmes must consider the specific needs of women, men, boys and girls in order to understand how the experiences, strengths, needs and vulnerabilities may differ and how these differences affect their coping strategies, help seeking behaviour and access to supportive resources. Stereotypes related to masculinity and feminity disproportionately affect men who carry the burden of bringing money to the family and women who disproportionately carry the burden of caregiving for children, older persons and people with disabilities. Social conceptions of masculinity can fuel child labour, violence, disengagement from school and recruitment into armed groups.Social norms and limited access to services result in some of the highest teenage pregnancy, child marriage, and growing HIV and STD prevalence rates. In Cabo Delgado, more precisely, the rate of use of contraception is one of the four lowest in the country, where only about two women in ten use modern contraceptive methods and poor quality of services and limited access to information is also a factor that undermine women’s abilities to prevent pregnancy and the ability to access lifesaving interventions. With the aim to provide quality life-saving services to address inclusive protection immediate risks and needs of most vulnerable populations in priority areas, community and right awareness-rasing and sensitization activities will be conducted tailoring the needs and concers of the population dissagregated by gender, aged groups. The intervention will also ensure inclusion of people with disabilities as particularly women and girls, as in their case their vulnerability is even greater based in discrimination and exclusion.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Project key activities on general protection:• Individual protection assistance - psychosocial services/MHPSS:a) MHPSS support provided for affected persons.b) Awareness raising activities on MHPSS.• Rights awareness-raising and sensitization including RCCE:a) Activities to sensitize or train local authorities and civil society on GBV prevention and response and international standards (gender equality, CRSV, Women's rights, PWD inclusion, etc.).Project key-activities on GBV and PSEA• Specialized health and PSS to survivor:a) Capacity building of clinical and community personnelb) Clinical management of rape,c) Individual PSS to survivorsd) GBV capacity building initiatives.• Risk and safety mitigation:a) Safety and risk mitigation activities, referral pathways and assessments.b) GBV awareness raising initiatives (small group sessions of a maximum of 20 persons). Large mass sessions organized during mass awareness raising events such as IWD and 16 days of activism. Activities will be implemented in Montepuez (Namanhumbir, Namuetho, Mapoupoulu, Nropa, Mirate), Balama (Balama sede, Kwekwe, Impire, Ntete, Muripa) and Nangade (Ntamba, Muadi, Mualela and Ntoli).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The programme uses participatory discussions, designed to engage the community, as part of it’s evaluation of basic needs and risks to establish the most suitable forms of assistance. The discussions with the affected population will assist the programme design, though the identification their basic needs, associated protection risks, how to address them, and how to access channels of information. The resulting priority will enable the affected populations, with different needs to access and use the feedback mechanism. At the same, time, clear SOPs, and referral pathways will be developed, including managing critical for sensitive cases, that will be registered on a separate database. This database will have limited access, and will be answered within 24 hours. Mdm will follow the protection guide on data management.To minimise the risk of violence, due to the distances of activities implementation settings, there will be additional practical measures incorporated into the activity design. In addition, heath workers not directly envolved in project implementation, will receive training on GBV and mental health topics to be also able assist protection needs that MdM has identified in the communities, while carrying out their daily work in the health facilities

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

There are certain key-actions that are especially important and that should be considered during the project assessment/design:- Ensure that the locations where the activities will take place and the routes to reach them are away from real or potential threats, especially with regard to GBV risks. Also ensure that these places are accessible to people with disabilities and the elderly.- Ensure respect for the confidentiality and privacy of beneficiaries, by selecting locations that are well separated from public spaces. In the case of health units, for example, ensure appropriate rooms that allow privacy.- Supporting women to use services by ensuring convenient opening hours.- Ensure female professionals with skills and experience working with women, children and the elderly.- Establish guidelines and mechanisms to monitor and report cases of abuse and exploitation.- Ensure that all beneficiaries receive information about the right to health and protection care and services.- Identify and establish contact with community leaders, essential for project activities to reach the community as a whole and bring widespread benefits. And, although most community leaders are men, it is important to identify any female leaders such as midwives, elders or traditional healers to promote women's leadership and representation.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will be focused on: • Identify and address the heightened risks faced by people with disabilities and elderly persons in humanitarian contexts having in consideration the intersectionality of age, gender, socio-economic factors and other diversities.• Identify, address and monitor environmental, attitudinal and institutional barriers and identify enablers to participation for people with disabilities and elderly persons. • The involvement of people with disabilities, elderly persons and/or their representative organizations during project design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

As significant health determinants, climate crisis and environmental pollution are among MdM’s main topics to address in the protection of human health. MdM will follow its environmental policy and best practices, aiming, on the one hand, to incorporate an environmental focus in all interventions and, on the other hand, to contribute to environmental sustainability by reducing the impact (“zero footprint”) of its activities. An environmental pre-diagnosis was carried out by MdM in Mozambique in 2022, in order to identify appropriate actions and approaches to be applied to all its interventions in the country.Furthermore, a specific protocol entitled "COVID-19 and the environment" has been defined to complement the above-mentioned policy, providing specific guidance to ensure the environmental approach to COVID-19 related measures, services, and actions. It should be noted that MdM also follows WHO recommendation “Operational Framework for Building Climate-Resilient Health Systems” to integrate the environmental dimension in the supported health units
