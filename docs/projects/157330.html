Project Summary

The project has been developed based on assessments and analyses from phase I of the integrated response planning framework to promote sustainable return under the RRR sector under which UPO in phase II will aim to: 1 Increase access to basic lifesaving services to the extremely affected population returnees underserved areas through establishment of basic services and enhancing the community capacity to contribute actively to rehabilitation and reconstruction of basic services. 2 to improve the vulnerable affected households' livelihood through increased access to short term employments, vocational trainings and income generation activities. . 3 to enhance government institution capacity on recovery, return and local reintegrationUPO will continue working with communities to improve their capacity to maintain and rehabilitate basic services. UPO will integrate GBV risk reduction strategies into the implementation of the activities through the consultation of women in locating resources and services, as well as including them as members of the decision-making committees that govern these resources and services. In order to reduce the potential for intercommunal violence and build trust and communication between members of the community, UPO will incorporate peacebuilding messages and diversity integration strategies into the provision of communal services. In an effort to meet strategic outcome three, UPO will work in coordination with relevant actors in the field to ensure that activities target HHs with children with severe and moderate acute malnutrition, as well those headed by females, focusing on lactating mothers. UPO continue working with other RRR partners working in the same areas and Sudan to coordinate and create synergy. UPO will ensure all the approach, area-base, multi-sectorial, multi-year and inclusivity is adopted throughout its intervention as well as the key principle ( women right, gender equality , leave no one behind, sustainability and resilience and participation and accountability)

Needs

Umdukhun, Golo and Um Baru localities are some of the most severely underserved locations with both IDPs and returnees. According to an interagency assessment conducted in the 18 25 January 2018, the total number of returnees registered to date in Um Dukhun locality is 80,387, with the Commissioner expecting 5,000 more to return this year from Chad. Over 50% of the returnees in Um Dukhun are women, with the largest number of vulnerable households belonging to lactating women. Their identified needs include the lack of access to basic services, as well as general poor health and nutrition status and a significant lack of livelihood opportunities. Umdafug and Shattaya localities in SD have received large influx of returnees according to HAC SD report and therefore severely under served and need RRR sector response. Kornoi in North Darfur also received large number of refugees returnees in the last 3 years with only few assistance provided, big gaps in Health, Water , livelihood and education fields were identified by the inter agency assessment conducted in 2016. The influx of these populations have exerted pressure on already inadequate lifesaving services given that old IDPs and the hosting communities are also suffering from these limited services. There are also major concerns about intercommunal violence, between the returnees, IDPs and host communities. The continued insecurity has negatively impacted the returnees in underserved areas, not allowing them to put down roots. The majority of women’s main source of income remains collecting fire, which exposes them to many dangers, hazards and risks of floods and exploitation. This poses significant risks, particularly for women and girls, who often bear the brunt of abuse and physical and/or sexual violence given their vulnerability to armed groups and male aggressors when traveling to and from remote sources of water and firewood. UPO plans to provide IGAs and livelihood training and starting kits for the most vulnerable women, boys and girls to enable them restore the hope of life and dignity. In order to reduce environmental degradation through providing of training on alternative sources of income to charcoal, as well as training in SSBs, in order to provide alternative materials to be used for building construction. UPO will continue working with communities to improve their capacity to maintain and rehabilitate basic services. UPO will integrate GBV risk reduction strategies into the implementation of the activities through the consultation of women in locating resources and services, as well as including them as members of the decision-making committees that govern these resources and services. In order to reduce the potential for inter communal violence and build trust and communication between members of the community, UPO will incorporate peacebuilding messages and diversity integration strategies into the provision of communal services.

Activities

1 Providing training on IGAs and livelihood activities (provision of donkey carts, small business start up materials etc.) for 600 (350 female, 250 male) vulnerable individuals from protracted IDPs, returnees and host communities. ( Golo, Umbaro, Kornoi) to help them improve their livelihood situation and increase self-dependency 2. Conducting vocational skills trainings for 900 most affected youth (600 male, 300 female) targeting IDPs, returnees and most extremely vulnerable host populations ( Kornoi, Umbaro, central Jebel Marra) 3. Providing Cash for work and short term employment for 700 most extremely vulnerable youth ( 500 male, 200 women) to generate income and increase self reliance in all target locations 4. Conducting 4 training for 150 community members (100 male, 50 female) to identify, manage and reduce risks related to climate related hazards (floods, droughts) and develop prevention action plans to mitigate potential risks and hazard. 5. Rehabilitation of 2 basic schools and one health center in remote affected rural areas with extremely vulnerable returnees as basic lifesaving services by using environment friendly blocks SSB, the rehabilitated basic infrastructure is expected to serve 16,000 severely affected persons (5,000 men, 7,000 women, 2,000 boys and 2,000 girls) 6. Rehabilitation of 20 water pumps and a water yard expected to serve 15,000 (4,000 men, 6,000 women, 2,500 girls and 2,500 boys) 7. Strengthen the capacity of the government related institution to increase services and address the needs of the most vulnerable in return site through capacity building targeting 500 individuals ( 300 male and 200 females) 8. Conducting trainings for 200 community members (80 female and 120 male) to enable them develop community security plans, prepare adequate reconciliation and peace building efforts and improve peaceful coexistence among the communities (IDPs, returnees and host communities). 9. in close coordination with UNEP, Ministry of agriculture
