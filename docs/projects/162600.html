Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen the resilience and coping capacities of vulnerable migrants such as IDPs and returnees and their host community to enhance food and livelihood security as well as to mitigate the risk of malnutrition. This will be achieved through delivering agricultural, livestock interventions and alternative income generating activities. The planned activities will promote the synergy between agricultural production and nutrition requirements. The interventions will target the most vulnerable small scale farmers, IDPs, returnees and local communities with special attention to youths and women. IOM will apply a right based, community centered approach to promote community initiative to respond to humanitarian needs and to ensure that planned interventions are met based on needs and rights of the vulnerable migrants and community to access to improved economic opportunities.
The project will focus on eight localities and this includes: Umm Dukhun locality, Central Darfur, El Fasher locality, North Darfur, Geneina and Krenik localities, West Darfur, Rahad El Berdi and Um Dafug localities, South Darfur, Ghubaysh localitiy, West Kordofan State, and Doudeb locality, Red Sea State. Returnees, IDPs and local communities in these localities are vulnerable to food insecurity and limited livelihood opportunities due to limited purchasing power resulting from inﬂation and high food price. This contributes towards food insecurity alongside chronic and severe malnutrition. The identiﬁed localities suffered from the high prevalence in Integrated Food Security Phase Classiﬁcation (IPC) phase 3 (Crisis) and/or phase 4 (Emergency) further highlighting the need for timely and adequate livelihood interventions to address food insecurity, strengthen community resilience to protect lives, and move towards economic self-suﬃciency.
Under HRP 2020, this project ﬁts with the overall Outcomes 2. The planned interventions will include: (1) capacity training and inputs on agriculture for better local production addressing climate adaptation technique: (3) support sustainable livestock husbandry practice and veterinary services (2) small business support for youth and women on agriculture and other livelihood skills such as food processing activities. IOM has already established a good access to the most of target localities (Dourdeb, Krenik, Geneina, Um Dafug, El Fasher, Umm Dukhun) implementing community stabilization and durable solution initiative activities under UNDAF outcomes. The planned interventions will be delivered in a way to complement past and on-going activities implemented, thereby will support strengthening links between the HRP Outcome and UNDAF outcome.

1 - Needs

The ongoing economic crisis along with continued drought and environmental degradation has exacerbated the livelihood situation of vulnerable communities in Sudan. The high price of agricultural inputs and food has undermined food security and livelihood situations. The crisis has led to a drop in people’s purchasing power, and reduced their ability to purchase enough food and essential basic services. This is particularly relevant to small scale farmers, IDPs, returnees and local communities affected by human displacement. Poor food security and livelihood opportunities, together with inappropriate feeding practices and lack of knowledge on nutrition drive acute and chronic malnutrition among the most vulnerable group especially pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and their children. Growing constraints on access to sustainable income generating activities have added to the high-level of vulnerability in the targeted localities. The influx of returnee and IDP movements and season migrants is adding further pressure on the usage of local natural resources of host communities. Since 2018, IOM began working in return and displacement areas of Umm Dukhun, El Fasher, Geneina and Krenik localities and other affected areas such as Dourdeb and Um Dafug localities. IOM’s need assessments informed that community members in these areas highlighted that most of the communities rely heavily on agriculture and livestock as a source of food and income while many of them are selling local agricultural and livestock for a short term income gain to acquire enough foods. Despite this self-identified importance, local production of crops and livestock remain low and limited to self-subsistence due to relatively low rainfall, deteriorating grazing lands and poor farming and livestock practice that do not meet the local market demands. Post-harvest losses are also a major constraint on farmers’ profitability due to their inappropriate handling of harvest, limited farming tools and mold infestation and contamination of grants, etc. Many of women headed households in the target localities still rely on unstable income sources such as ﬁrewood collection and charcoal making which have a potential of environmental damage, with limited ownership of common and productive assets. They also adapt food based negative coping strategy- such as selling the last remaining female animals to acquire enough food, which leading to irreversible loss of livelihoods. Such negative coping strategy along with the communities’ minimal or absent awareness of the importance of food nutrition is the leading cause of malnutrition among PLWs and children. The project will respond to the needs by delivering immediate agricultural assistance while ensuring sustainable agricultural practices through resilience-based agricultural and livestock interventions. To enhance skills and knowledge for better agricultural production and nutrition sensitive, climate adaptive techniques, agricultural training and inputs will be implemented by having technical assistance of Sudanese agricultural expert from abroad. IOM will strengthen the existing partnership with the Sudanese diaspora to improve traditional agricultural activities and to better absorb shocks to climate change, whilst at the same time carry out trainings for the local authorities to increase their capacity. Small business support on agriculture (e.g. an establishment of groundnut shelling machine and oil pressing machines with business training) and food processing and conservation addressing food insecurity and malnutrition will be delivered targeting vulnerable youths and women.

2 - Activities / Outputs

All proposed activities will be coordinated with the relevant government authorities and the sector leading agencies and other livelihood partners working in the same areas. IOM will support each locality equally based on their needs. In addition, in South Darfur, IOM will attempt to reach the sector target of Um Dafug locality (a total 582 under Outcome 2-Output 2.2) through the planned livelihood activities while more number of beneficiaries in Rahad El Berdi locality will be supported. Due to geographic location, the residents in both localities are interacting for trading. Hence, there will be a potential that communities in two localities could share the existing resources and the project inputs for a mutual benefit.Activities: 1. Agricultural and livestock support: 1) Conduct needs assessments per locality. 2) Set-up a community agricultural committee for each locality (eight committees: 10-20 members for each committee). 3) Organize and conduct Training of Trainers for the established committee members (targeting a total of 160 trainees) and this will be done by the Sudanese agricultural expert. 4) Distribute improved seeds and farming tools for a total 20,000 beneficiaries (400- 500 HHs (approximately 2000-2500 individuals from each locality) and other agricultural inputs such as small water pumps for irrigation farm, motor cycles (tuktuk) to support transportation of crop production to local market as needed. 5) Conduct agricultural training (5-7 days) for the selected beneficiaries by the trained committee members. 6) Establish an irrigation system for three selected farms (target localities may include: Krenik, EL Fasher and Umm Dukhun) through having the technical support of a Sudanese agricultural expert: The irrigation systems will be established, having a potential to develop an integrated farm that will allow target communities to nutrition sensitive vegetable, livestock and fruits. The interventions will include the distribution of vegetable seeds, fruit seeds, farming tools. Motorcycles (tuktuk) for each farm will be provided to support transportation of crop production to local market. The project inputs will be shared between target migrants and local communities. 7) Provide upgraded veterinary care equipment and refresher training targeting a total 80 Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) (5-10 CAHWs from each locality). 8) Provide vaccinated healthy local livestock (goats) with training on better husbandry practice and livestock health for female headed households (HHs)- A total of 800 HHs (at least 80-100 households per locality). 2. Small business and income generating supports 1) Identify vulnerable youths and establish a total of 16 groups of youths (two groups per locality and 10-20 youths per group). 2) Provide small business supports through an establishment of groundnut shelling and oil pressing machines for each group of youths. The locations to install machines will be determined based on community consultation process. Training on machine maintenance and business training will further be provided. 3) Provide food processing and conservation training based on local agricultural productions and addressing key components of nutritional care for women (PLWs) and children: The interventions will target a total of 800 female headed HHs with small children (80-100 HHs from each locality). 4) Provide training on alternative income generating activities based on household livestock products for the beneﬁciaries who supported through the provision of goats. 3. Monitoring and Evaluation 1) Conduct post assessment and share key outputs and challenges including lessons learnt and good practices to sector lead, relevant state authorities, local Non-Governmental Organization(NGOs/ Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) as well as target communities.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Planned agricultural training will be organized and addressed by a Sudanese agricultural expert and training will focus on irrigation farming system, proper use and maintenance of farming equipment and seeds, land preparation, fertilizer application, use of organic manure and composing, integrated pest management, post harvesting technique by promoting natural resource management strategies. In addition, the irrigation system will be established to have a potential to create an integrated farm in future which will allow vulnerable farmers to have nutrition sensitive vegetable, fruits and livelihoods. The planned food processing activities and livestock supports will focus on supporting women-especially PLWs and their children. It will also be designed and implemented by strengthening the synergy between nutritional necessities and the local agricultural and livestock production addressing natural resource management.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will employ a right based approach to promote the protection and human rights of migrants. The project will pay special attention to returnees, IDPs in vulnerable situations including, youths and women as well as local/hosting communities who do not have adequate access to basic services and vulnerable to food and livelihood security. The project will empower these groups by involving them in the decision-making processes through a people-centred approach. The project will focus on community consultation process during the project design and implementation and this will ensure that the proposed/delivered livelihood activities are met with the needs of target beneficiaries. A broad range of mobility issues will be discussed during the community consultative process, ranging from protection threats faced by vulnerable returnees, IDPs, women and youths, for example what causes their displacement and vulnerabilities and economic disparities.

7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with group consultations with aid workers

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase -immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected migrants/returnees to the implementation of the interventions. The proposed project is built around a community-centred and participatory approach to ensure that the communities themselves are enablers and drivers of their own resilience, recovery and development. Accountability will also be promoted through capacity development activities for relevant government stakeholders, community leaders and leaders from women and youth groups, etc. As part of capacity development, the project will establish the community management committees or re-activate the existing committees and the key responsibility of committee will be to coordinate and monitor overall project implementation as well as obtain feedback from the project beneficiaries. Women’s representativeness will be assured within the committees, and female committee members will be encouraged to report to IOM’s focal point if protection threats such as harassment sexual exploitation and abuse, targeted women and girls are reported by target beneficiaries. If needed, IOM will provide direct assistance through psychosocial support, counselling and medical assistance to such victim and this will be done in strong coordination with other partners in protection sector.
