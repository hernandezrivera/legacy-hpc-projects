Project Summary

The project aims to bolster the resilience of internally displaced persons (IDP) households forced to flee their homes due to escalating security threats. Additionally, it targets vulnerable host communities identified through specific criteria set by IYD.
The project will distribute Multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) of $100 per month per household for 3,000 households over eight rounds, significantly improving their ability to meet basic needs and enhance household food security
Besides, One-off MPCA valued at $150 will be provided to 5,000 vulnerable households within project-targeted sub-districts.
The IDPs are entirely reliant on humanitarian assistance for their daily needs, exacerbating the strain on host communities in provinces like Aleppo and Idleb, which have limited financial and livelihood resources. To address this, the project will implement activities aimed at improving food security through MPCA, and this will enable BNFs to prioritize their needs, maintain their dignity as they won't have to wait in queues to receive in-kind rations, and improve their living conditions. Furthermore, the MPCA assistance will have a positive impact on the market as it will inject money into it, thus reviving it.
Needs:
The severity of the humanitarian crisis in Syria remains significant, as a result of the continued hostilities in localized areas, protracted displacement, and harsh weather conditions. According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview Summary published on 21 December 2023, the number of people in need increased by 1.4 million reaching 16.7 million compared to 2023, 5.5 million of them are internally displaced people. Whilst there has been a decrease of hostilities in Idleb governorate over the current year, the number of people in need had increased significantly, the impact of hostilities on civilians remains the principal driver of humanitarian needs in Syria.
Implementation of Activities:
Implementation of the project activities will be comprehensive, involving registration and verification of beneficiaries, contracting of service providers, conducting market assessments, and coordinating with local authorities for distribution. Regular post-distribution monitoring using harmonized PDM tool, registration monitoring, distribution monitoring, price monitoring, baseline, and endline will ensure adherence to project and donor policies
Protection Risk Analysis:
Several risks have been identified, including exclusion and discrimination in service provision and potential harm to vulnerable groups during project activities. Mitigation measures include clarifying selection criteria, establishing robust feedback mechanisms, and conducting constant assessments to ensure the safety and dignity of beneficiaries.
PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Strategy:
IYD has adopted a comprehensive PSEA strategy, including adherence to the inter-agency Code of Conduct, active participation in PSEA networks, and establishment of internal reporting and investigative mechanisms. Completion of PSEA checklists and ongoing monitoring will ensure adherence to standards and prompt action against violations.
Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures:
Various risks, including institutional, operational, social, and security risks, have been identified. Mitigation measures involve maintaining strong collaboration with stakeholders, ensuring service provider commitment, addressing social concerns, and coordinating with local leaders and security teams to mitigate security risks. Regular monitoring and updates will be conducted to ensure the safety and security of project implementation. Potential risks of multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) include aid diversion, causing price rises or shortages, and exacerbating tensions in households or communities. Mitigation involves involving recipients in design, managing expectations, and ensuring aid is used appropriately

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Multi-purpose cash assistance will be provided to 8000 households (HHs) divided as MPCA) of $100 per month per household for 3,000 households over eight rounds with each HH receiving $150, aimed at enhancing their purchasing power for daily needs and One-off MPCA valued at $150 will be provided to 5,000 vulnerable households within project-targeted sub-districts. The assistance targets both internally displaced persons (IDPs) and families of host communities in Aleppo and Idleb governorates, with a particular focus on recently displaced individuals and those affected by recent natural disasters like floods, wind, heavy rain, and snowstorms. Selection criteria for beneficiaries include households with limited or no regular income, those not previously targeted by similar assistance, and families with vulnerabilities such as disabled members, elderly individuals, and single-headed households. Coordination with relevant clusters (CCCM, CWG, SNFI) and local authorities is crucial for tracking displaced populations, selecting intervention locations based on updated mapping, and conducting rapid needs assessments to identify priorities and response gaps. Comprehensive market and price assessments will be conducted using tools like the Comprehensive Market Assessment tool to ensure that the intervention does not negatively affect market prices or competitiveness. Beneficiary identification involves signing Memorandums of Understanding (MoUs) with local authorities, conducting door-to-door household registration using the IYD's tools, and verification processes to ensure eligibility criteria are met. Printed coupons will be distributed to selected beneficiaries at predetermined distribution sites, coordinated with local authorities for security and logistical purposes. Information about distributions will be communicated through local councils, banners, and IYD local staff. Redemption of coupons will be verified against distribution records by IYD's field team, with invoices and payment requests reviewed for accuracy. The distribution team will also be responsible for site safety, monitoring, and addressing beneficiary complaints. Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted after 7-15 days, with data cleaning, analysis, and reporting carried out by the MEAL team. Reports will be shared with relevant stakeholders on a monthly basis. Payments will be processed after verification and audit, primarily through contracted Financial Service Providers (FSPs). Vendors/service providers will be paid in cash through the FSPs. A feedback/complaints mechanism will be established, and PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) awareness sessions will be conducted alongside cash distribution activities to raise awareness among beneficiaries, including girls and boys.
