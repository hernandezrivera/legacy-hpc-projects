Project Summary

Most South Sudanese lack access to essential health services, safe water, and sanitation. One in every ten children dies before they turn five, and the rate that new mothers pass away from pregnancy complications is one of the highest in the world: roughly 1,223 per 100,000 live births, according to the World Bank. The main causes of morbidity remain communicable diseases; malaria is the top cause of morbidity (64%) and mortality (45%) among outpatients, followed by pneumonia and diarrhea. Several Counties report malaria cases above the threshold perennially, especially during the rainy seasons, affecting mainly children under five years. The last malaria indicator survey (2023) estimated malaria prevalence of 32%, 34%, and 18% among children under five, protection of civilian sites, and internally displaced persons, respectively. HIV/AIDS and measles continue to threaten lives across the country. The ongoing conflict has intensified weaknesses in the health system, with massive destruction of healthcare infrastructure. South Sudan is also facing acute food insecurity; integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projected that over half of South Sudan’s population experienced high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC phase 3 or worse) according to the projection for April to July 2024, including 2.366 million people in IPC phase 4 (emergency) and 79 000 people in IPC phase 5 (catastrophe). These numbers were expected to hit 7.8 million during April-July 2023.

1. Needs

Since May 2024, flooding has affected more than 735,000 people across 38 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area in South Sudan. The floods reportedly destroyed homes, crops, and infrastructure, disrupting health and education services and heightening disease risks. In August 2024, OCHA reported an estimated 338,470 persons (Fangak-88,700, Ayod-87,140, Uror-60,820, Bor South-58,060, Duk-40,880, and Pochalla-2,870) were displaced in Jonglei State. The flood crisis forced thousands of people to abandon their homes due to rising water levels that submerged most villages, leaving only the payam headquarters accessible as a refuge for hundreds of displaced residents. Efforts to support the affected communities were hindered by limited resources and ongoing isolation. Local youth mobilized to reinforce broken dykes, resulting in the rehabilitation of about 4 km of the perimeter. In Bor South County, heavy rains flooded several residential areas in Bor town, displacing some 2,600 people.Healthcare facilities were rendered inaccessible and are struggling with inadequate drug supply, equipment and understaffing. Mental health and psychosocial support services are unmet and vector control measures are inadequate further heightening the risk of mosquito-borne diseases. The disease surveillance systems have been weakened hence reducing the ability of the County to effectively monitor, report, and provide early warnings for epidemic-prone diseases, while insufficient immunization coverage exacerbates vulnerability to preventable illnesses such as measles and cholera.Compounding these issues is limited access to clean water, exposing the IDPs to waterborne illnesses. Poor hygiene practices stemming from insufficient education contribute to preventable health risks. Humanitarian actors are engaged in an emergency response despite inadequate funding. Survivors of gender-based violence, particularly women and girls, children, and persons with disabilities, are facing significant barriers in accessing clinical management and targeted support services. High rates of malnutrition among children and adults are further aggravated by inadequate screening, treatment, and therapeutic nutrition programs. A shortage of trained community health workers is limiting healthcare access. All these challenges call for an urgent response mechanism in order to alleviate suffering of the IDPs and host communities in the County.

2. Project activities

1: Establish 1 Mobile Health Clinic to provide essential health services to IDPs and underserved communities in remote areas. 2: Strengthen Disease Surveillance Systems: Improve reporting, monitoring, and early warning systems for epidemic-prone diseases. 3: Expand Vaccination Campaigns: Conduct 4 mass immunizations for measles, cholera, and other preventable diseases. 4: Enhance Health Facility Capacity: Supply assorted medicines, medical equipment, and train 20 healthcare personnel to handle increased patient loads. 5: Provide Psychosocial Support Services: Offer daily counseling and mental health services for conflict-affected populations. 6: Implement Vector Control Programs: Distribute 10,000 insecticide-treated nets and conduct community-based mosquito eradication activities. 7: Improve Access to Clean Water: Distribute 10,000 water purification tablets to mitigate waterborne diseases. 8: Promote Hygiene and Sanitation Practices: Organize 24 community education campaigns on handwashing, safe waste disposal, and personal hygiene. 9: Develop Emergency Preparedness Plans: Equip 4 health facilities and communities to respond to floods, droughts, and disease outbreaks. 10: GBV response services: Provide all survivors with clinical management of rape services for targeted support for women, children, and persons with disabilities. 11: Conduct Malnutrition Screening and Treatment: Support nutrition feeding programs and provide therapeutic nutrition for malnourished children and adults. 12: Enhance Community Health Worker Programs: Train and deploy 15 health workers to improve healthcare access at the community level. 13: Coordinate with Humanitarian Partners: Strengthen collaboration among health, WASH, and protection actors to ensure a holistic response. 14: Strengthen Referral Systems: Create pathways for timely access to secondary and tertiary healthcare for severe cases. 15: Conduct Public Health Awareness Campaigns: Educate communities on disease prevention, recognizing symptoms, and when to seek care.
