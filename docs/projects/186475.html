Project Summary

Since 2010, the armed conflict in northeastern Nigeria has heavily affected the populations of the Borno State. According to the HRP, 36,000 people have been killed in the northeast of the country since 2009 (OCHA, 2021). In addition, there are frequent epidemics, including the impact of COVID-19 and extreme climate events with devastating consequences on the economy and lives of the population. As violences has caused many people to flee their homes, today, the number of internally displaced people (IDP) in the northeast exceeds 2 million. In 2021, the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance has reached 8.9 million, including 1.2 million in areas that are difficult to reach or inaccessible to humanitarian actors. This is an 11% increase from 7.9 million people at the beginning of 2020 (OCHA, 2021). The crisis has implicated growing needs for Livelihoods, Health, Nutrition and Protection services, as presented hereunder.
According to IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM round 37 updated May 2021), Monguno has 152,191 IDPs in need of humanitarian assistant. PUI seeks, therefore, to improve the protection environment and living conditions of both IDP’s and host community members in Monguno LGA, Borno state by strengthening the provision of a comprehensive multi sectoral assistance through health, nutrition and protection intervention. The project will target children under 5 years, adolescent girls, women of reproductive age and men who are affected by the armed conflict. The needs this proposal aims to improve aligns with NHF strategies and various sector priorities such as right violation, negative coping strategy, increased protection risks as a result of lack of access to basic services.

1 - Needs

Due to the consequences of the protracted conflict in Borno State and in Monguno LGA, PUI identified a lack of adequate access to quality primary health care services. Following the increase of Internally Displaced persons (IDP) and returnees in Monguno LGA, the health needs increased, necessitating the scaling up of health activities in this LGA. According to the Northeast health sector bulletin, out of the 5.3 million people targeted for health assistance in 2021, only 2.07 million (39% of the target) have been reached as of September 2021. Mostly, unreached people are from hard-to-reach areas including Monguno. According to last 6 months consultations, an average of 2207 OPD consultations were recorded per month, 110 consultation per day for 2 clinician with a caseload of 55 consultation per day against WHO standard of 20-30 patients per day per clinician.Further, PUI identified a lack of access to adequate services to treat malnutrition though a SMART survey carried out by ACF indicated a worrisome situation. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate was 12% and the Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rate of 2% in November 2020. Similarly, the IPC Acute malnutrition analysis September 2020-August 2021 issued in March 2021 indicated Monguno as one of the 7 LGAs in Borno under serious classification. The situation is always expected to deteriorate during the lean season (June/September). PUI aims, therefore, to improve access to adequate and quality services in order to treat malnutrition through increasing the capacity of its SC (Stabilization Center) already overstretched. Additionally, the widespread of right violation and lack of access to basic services have increased protection concerns. According to protection analysis report June 2021, Data from the Armed Conflict Location and Event Data project (ACLED) shows that over 300 security incidents with about 2,096 fatalities occurred in northeast Nigeria over during the reporting period. This is further substantiated by information received from protection monitoring reports which estimate that over 150, 000 people were adversely affected by 453 reported protection incidents. Protection incidents typology ranged from GBV incidents, attacks by NSAGs, physical assault, abduction, fire outbreaks and destruction of property to looting, communal clashes, and forced evictions. IDPs as well as host communities were disproportionately affected by these incidents in Monguno, with over 60% of the victims being women and girls. A multi-sectorial needs assessment conducted by PUI in March 2020 stated that the lack of access to essential services is the main protection concern recognized by 62% of the households as a significant stress factor. As these services are unavailable or financially inaccessible, communities are likely to resort to potentially harmful coping mechanisms, further increasing vulnerability for individuals of concern.

2 - Activities

PUI aims to complement the current health activities in Waterboard IDPs camp to include basic BEmNOC (Basic Emergency Neonatal and Obstetric care) by providing antenatal care, post-natal care, clinical management of rape, family planning and provide outpatient consultation for communicable and non-communicable diseases in the already established health post in the Camp. Additionally, PUI aims to increase the capacity of the health center to meet up with the influx of patients due to recurrent displacement. PUI will scale up inpatient management of acute malnutrition in stabilization center in Monguno in term of increasing bed capacity as this is the only stabilization center in Monguno LGA. Since PUI reopen the Stabilization center in mid-March 2021, it has experience high admission an average of 92 admission for SAM with medical complication per month with 21 bed capacity. Ensure psychosocial support activities are integrated into stabilization center services by providing child stimulation activities. Moreover, PUI aims to implement general protection activities and a harmonized sector protection monitoring especially in Gana Ali and Bakassi Shuwari locations. The lack of actors in this area provokes gaps in terms of right violations identification and understanding protection concerns evolution. This service gap exposes vulnerable communities to more risks and lack of access to basic services.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

PUI strives to ensure mainstreaming of protection principles and respect for the safety and dignity of people in all its programming and sectors of intervention, in order to minimize unintended adverse effects of its interventions. All interventions will be based on a thorough analysis of the needs and gaps identified through participatory assessments conducted with different groups of the targeted populations. The targeted populations will be consulted in all phases of the project cycle (design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation) to ensure that the response is adapted to the needs and capacities of the affected populations. In this process, different groups from diverse backgrounds will be engaged to ensure meaningful access to services, participation and empowerment of the communities. Moreover, PUI closely works with the community and local leaders to select sites and ensure the beneficiaries' day-to-day safety. In order to facilitate meaningful access, PUI will adopt multiple approaches, including sharing information about the available services with the populations of concern and ensuring the disability-friendliness of the facilities and activities. With regards to disability inclusion, PUI will also diversify the communication and information-sharing channels to improve communication with people suffering from different types of disability (e.g. pictorial aid, mega phones, etc.). PUI will train its staff in protection mainstreaming and raise their awareness of the protection principles, CoC, PSEA, etc. to ensure compliance with safeguarding principles and the principle of “do no harm”. PUI will pay special attention to specific vulnerabilities in order to reduce barriers to health-care access by implementing an active and efficient triage of patients to ensure the most vulnerable are given priority. In all interventions, gender dynamics/norms will be thoroughly analyzed and mainstreamed in the activities (e.g. separated waiting areas (male/female) when feasible; gender-balanced teams to cater for the different needs of both males and females, etc.). PUI attaches high importance to the safety and confidentiality of the beneficiaries and ensures that all the staff are aware of these principles, specifically data protection protocols. In addition to GBVIMS information sharing protocol, PUI has developed its internal data protection protocol, signed and adhered to by all relevant staff.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The current community-based feedback system consists of multiple entry points: the helpline, themobile helpdesk, the community volunteers' network, the satisfaction surveys, and the monthlycommunity committees’ meetings. These channels are reviewed yearly following beneficiaryfeedbacks to ensure the mechanism is inclusive, context-specific, and accessible safely andconfidentially by women, girls, boys, and men.Further, accountability refresher training ensures that field staff and volunteers know the feedbackmechanisms, procedures, and accountability principles. Accountability teams are composed ofboth male and female members who speak the most spoken language of the area of interventionto allow beneficiaries to express their concerns easily. When attending the helpdesks in the healthfacilities, accountability team ensures to provide awareness to beneficiaries and non-beneficiariesregarding the various channels available for complaints and feedbacks.
