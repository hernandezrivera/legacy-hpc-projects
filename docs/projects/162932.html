Project Summary

United Mission on Relief and Development(UMORD) proposes a project aiming at improving access to WASH ,Livelihood and quality and safe primary education to conflict affected children of refugees, IDPs, returnee and host communities population in Adila ,Abu Jabra and Bahar Alarab Locality of East Darfur state. UMCOR will work with UMORD NNGO partner and the State Ministry of Education, WES and Ministry of Agriculture.
The project will focus on rehabilitation of primary schools dilapidated due to long years of service and the poor quality of the materials they were built. The project will construct 8 semi-permanent classrooms, teachers’ office, and gender sensitive VIP latrines with 8 drop-holes, having drinking and hand washing facilities per each of the three targeted locality. The newly constructed classrooms will reduce overcrowding and increase intake capacity of the school to absorb returnee, IDP and refugee children. In addition a pre-school with 2 classrooms and an office will be constructed and supplied with the standard learning and teaching materials per locality. The new pre and primary school classrooms will be provided with seating desks and fences erected for the safety of the children and the properties, assets and facilities in the premises.
UMORD will also scale up its ongoing FSL activities in East Darfur coupling provision of agricultural inputs and marketing training with micro-finance through VSLA for cultivation of groundnuts and support with IGA activities
To ensure access to WASH Services, the project rehabilitated three water points each per locality,train WASH Committees and support in Operation and Maintenance and good governance Noting that there are some areas with Haffirs as main water source, The project hopes to rehabilitate one improved Haffir in Abujabra Locality and life straw filters to households using water from haffir.UMORD will also work with state Ministry to trigger the community to construct their own latrines through CLTS Approach. A total of 30 ToT Natural leaders and 15 Natural leaders will be trained to drive this process
UMCOR is requesting US $ 1,026,623 to successfully implement the above proposed WASH, Livelihood and Education intervention to benefit the South Sudanese refugees living with the host communities in Adila,Abujabra and Bahar Alarab Localities of East Darfur

1 - Needs

East Darfur hosts 108,299 refugees with 14,806 residing in Abu Jabra,9613 in Adila and 39,191 in Bahr El Arab. The rapid influx of refugees in the recent years has further strained the capacity of host communities and social services to absorb the new arrivals and address their needs.The state WASH Cluster and field data shows the dire need for a range of WASH infrastructure, sanitation and hygiene promotion services. As per UMCOR rapid WASH assessment in March 2019 the main sources of water for human consumption in the target localities are water yards and Haffir that are in very poor and contributing to the lack of safe water. In water yards, frequent breakdown of the generators results in long wait times. Leakages in the pipeline system cause waste and create mud pits. The storage capacity of Haffir in the areas of Abu Jabra locality is significantly reduced due to heavy silts and blockages Access to clean water is limited in part because water infrastructure including pumps, wells, and generators are exposed to improper usage and management. Without access to reliable sources of safe water, communities are compelled to access unsafe water sources and are at risk of parasitic infection and AWD. Regarding food security and livelihoods, insufficient capital is identified as one of the major challenges in the vocational sector and there is a strong demand for financing among women. Due to the protracted displacement, refugees and Host communities in East Darfur share similar economic challenges related to limited access to food, income and employment. The conflict and economic crisis have had a significant impact on education in East Darfur which hosts 9% of out-of-school-children in Sudan (approximately 200,000 children). Access to basic education is limited as a result of inadequate learning spaces, lack of qualified teachers and community involvement, and insufficient learning materials. The conflict has left many schools destroyed, burned, looted and deserted, while other schools are overcrowded, exerting considerable the existing system. School infrastructure is well below standard and insufficient to support refugee and host communities. Most school buildings have not been repaired for years and the GoS capacity to construct schools is limited

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

To mitigate on the impacts of the project on the environment,The following strategies will be used: • AWARENESS RAISING Include environmental messaging to beneficiaries, to raise awareness and also to support sustainable use and maintenance of WASH facilities. • PROMOTE INNOVATIVE TECHNIQUES Promote innovative technologies; alternative energy sources such as solar panels for water pumps and lighting for safer use of latrines and showers; ideas for water management and water conservation such as rainwater harvesting, grey water reuse for small plantations, and eco sanitation • ENVIRONMENT AND HYGIENE CLUBS: Include Environmental issues in School Hygiene Clubs, provide schools and CFSs with cleaning kit and combine with cleaning campaigns. • AGE ADAPTED WASH FACILITIES Ensure WASH Facilities in schools are adapted to the child’s age to guarantee proper use of facilities, and reduce environmental risks due to standing water in connection to hand-washing stations, or open defecation • WORK WITH THE NATIONAL AUTHORITIES Work together with and support WES for continuous monitoring of ground water levels through Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM).• 3R’s: REDUCE, REUSE, RECYCLE Support schools, CFSs, communities and camps to implement a system for Solid Waste Management. Reduce packaging material and discourage use of plastic bags; Reuse old construction material when possible; recycle plastic sheeting to weave bags. Bio-degradable material, when properly sorted, can be sold or used directly as soil enrichment in a household or school vegetable garden. Reuse of grey water from handwashing facilities for extra water needs.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Traditionally, women and girls are responsible for collecting water and the risks associated with this responsibility are felt dis proportionally by women and girls who face potential sexual violence as they go about their daily task of collecting water from outside the camps or their living areas. Women and girls face similar risk also when they venture outside their living premises to relive themselves due to lack of appropriate sanitation infrastructures. In addition to the risk of sexual violence, women and girls spend a significant amount of their time either traveling long distances or queuing for hours to collect water. UMCOR will mitigate such risks by ensuring that the water points are within 500m and latrines at least 50m from households and they are located at a well-lit place at night to ease the burden on women and girls and ensure they live in safety, dignity and respect. To ensure equal access to services, several arrangements will be put into place by the program. For example: during the implementation, community members such as women, girls, old people and people with disabilities will consulted separately on the appropriateness of selected activities. Latrine construction sites will be chosen based on accessibility, safety and privacy for women and other vulnerable groups. In the design of emergency latrines, special attention will be given to people with disabilities so that the design fits their special needs. To ensure sustainability and ownership, the targeted beneficiaries will be engaged in all the project process from the beginning to the end.All staff will be trained on referral procedures and mechanisms, including the child-specific referral pathway in schools, linking patients directly to experts from other agencies and government in already supporting the health sector at nearby hospitals and other secondary facilities.Awareness-raising during community mobilization on health and nutrition, education a key component that UMORD has put in place for better understanding among the beneficiaries for self-protection on various programmatic matters.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

UMORD is accountable to the women, men, boys and girls whose lives it aims to improve and places this responsibility is at the core of its Humanitarian policy.
