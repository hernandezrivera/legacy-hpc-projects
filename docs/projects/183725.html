Project Summary

The project is a multi-sectorial protection and education intervention targeting vulnerable children and their families in 10 locations in 5 areas in all Gaza governorates that were most affected by the recent attacks. Geographical Areas targeted are: Rafah (Tal al sultan-Mawasi); Khanyounis (Hay Al amal and Khanyounis Camp / Al batn al sameen/ Japanese neighbourhood); Middle area (Nusairat and Buraij)- Gaza North (Jabaila- Beit Hanoun)
The project will provide 1) holistic and multi-layered MHPSS services to vulnerable children and caregivers, including group-based PSS interventions and Child protection prevention and awareness activities (level 1 and 2); focused PSS intervention to manage trauma symptoms for children and individual Child protection and GBV case management services (level 3) to the most vulnerable children and women including budget for multi-sectoral needs, such as medical care, education needs, etc to support the case management services; and referral to specialized MH services (Level 4); 3) remedial education and learning equipment for 1st to 4th grade children affected by the lack of access to quality education and online learning during COVID-19; 4) referrals of vulnerable children and families to relevant services within CPAOR, including referral to specialized MH services (Level 4) to Gaza Community Mental Health Programme (GCMH).
The project will be implemented by War Child and 3 Palestinian partners – CFTA, CTCCM and TCC - and will build upon the partners' strong expertise and complementarities: War Child will utilize its expertise in MHPSS and established network of CBOs and CBOs volunteers in Gaza, as well as its coordination with relevant clusters (education, CP, MHPSS), while CFTA, CTCCM and TCC will further capitalize on their extensive Child Protection, MHPSS, GBV and education experience, reach in Gaza and on its direct and strong relationship with the relevant Ministries.
The project will be implemented in coordination with MOSD and with active members of CPAOR, specifically ones providing CP and will align with Standards SOP for CP. All project staff will be trained on PSEA by War Child's PSEA trainer, and awareness raising on PSEA and complaints and referral pathways will also be integrated in all project activities.

1 - Needs

The May 2021 escalation of hostilities exacerbated the already severe protection and education needs throughout the oPt and in particular in Gaza. Protection and MHPSS: According to the HNO 2022 (draft) and Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CP AoR) there are considerable needs for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) interventions including specialized case management, and approaches targeting adolescents and caregivers, particularly males, with an estimated 678,000 children (345,800 boys ad 332,200 girls) across the oPt in need of child protection and MHPSS services, including 122,000 in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services. In Gaza more than half (53%) of all children are in need, and 137,000 caregivers are also in need of various MHPSS services. War Child and Save the Children’s Needs Assessment in Gaza (September 2021) confirmed these findings: 78.2% of respondents stated they experience signs of distress and observable changes in their mood or behaviour, including insomnia, struggling with relationships, agitation, social withdrawal, anxiety, fear, panic attacks, and sadness, with only 6.7% of respondents receiving MHPSS services. Furthermore, 79% of the respondents’ children showed signs of distress and observable changes in behaviour, with a strong correlation between caregivers’ distress and children’s psychosocial difficulties, and only 9.4% of these children had been provided with MHPSS services. Lack of access to adequately and timely MHPS support can result in people developing serious more persistent and/or more complex mental health issues without addressing it or even getting it in records. Even the available MPHSS service providers are struggling with limited resources and shortage of trained personnel. Notably, the limited MHPSS is not equally distributed across the five governorates; secondly, the social stigma and misconceptions associated with mental health issues and seeking PSS, the lack of awareness and knowledge about the symptoms and signs of mental health issues also impact their ability to receive any MHPSS. Furthermore, people are still not aware of the available MHPSS service providers, type of the service available and the commitment related to it- that these services require. Women and girls: 19% households in Gaza are concerned about the safety and security of girls, while 17% are concerned about the safety and security of women (oPt multi sectoral needs assessment 2021). COVID-19 and the May 2021 escalation resulted in an increase in reported GBV cases. Women and girls in communities impacted by conflict and displacement, i.e. Gaza, Area C and East Jerusalem, tend to suffer from higher incidents of GBV, including higher incidence of intimate partner violence, sexual abuse and forced marriage. These communities also tend to be characterized by limited availability and access to multi-sectoral protection services. The areas with the lowest % of households reporting availability of PSS services in case of GBV were East Jerusalem (8,9%), Gaza (31,6%) and Area C (38,8%). Women and girls with disabilities face additional barriers that limits their access to services that puts them in additional risk. In Gaza, 58% of persons with disabilities lack Information about GBV prevention and response, including how and where to access relevant assistance and services during emergency. Access to GBV services – already compromised throughout the oPt by the occupation and COVID19 restrictions - has been weakened by the recent escalation, particularly in Gaza where GBV in-person services, such as legal aid, PSS, GBV case management, and other community education/awareness raising activities were suspended during the hostilities. (HNO 2022 draft) War Child and Save the Children Needs Assessment in Gaza (September 2021) confirmed these trends with 19% of respondents stating that women and girls feel unsafe when they are outside their home, and 25% that there are areas that women and girls avoid because they feel unsafe and fear violence. Education: the escalation intensified the already existing education structural problems, damaging 149 public and private schools and 37 UNRWA schools. Another 63 UNRWA schools were used as Designated Emergency Shelters (DES) during the escalation to host IDPs and were partially damaged and in need of minor rehabilitation. In addition, 80 Kindergartens (KGs) were also assessed and reported damages due to the escalation. (Education Cluster Report on damage in educational facilities in Gaza, July 2021) Due to COVID-19 and public health measures taken to slow the transmission of the virus, schools in the Gaza Strip had been closed since 8 April and education was provided through distance learning approaches. During the escalation and after the ceasefire, all schools remained closed in Gaza, and both MoE and UNRWA had to terminate the academic year shorter than expected due to the different challenges following the latest escalation of violence, including frequent electricity and internet cuts, damage to schools’ infrastructure and difficulty in accessing schools due to damaged streets and infrastructure. Learning was interrupted for approximately 600,000 school-age children in Gaza, including about 285,000 who attend UNRWA schools. Key Informants interviewed within War Child and Save the Children Needs Assessment in Gaza recommended to prioritize children's health, psychosocial support and education, by enhancing children’s life skills and providing psychological and social support to promote their resilience, providing child friendly and safe spaces, and strengthening the protective environment around the child by working with parents, teachers, counsellors and those who care for children.

2 - Activities

The project will be implemented by War Child and 3 local partners well established in the targeted communities and with long standing expertise in the sector they will lead: CTCCM on PSS, CFTA on CP and GBV and TCC on education. CTCCM will work through 7 CBOs, established partners of War Child and whose CP and PSS capacity was developed through extensive training and coaching. Local partners are able to quickly access vulnerable populations, are well recognized by the communities and can easily negotiate access/permits with authorities, thus ensuring a short time frame operational implementation, localization and sustainability of the response. War Child will support the partners through technical and management specialists in country and in the region and will provide grant and project management, technical support on MHPSS/CP methodologies, child participation and accountability to affected populations, inclusion and gender mainstreaming. Specifically, War Child will provide Supervision/capacity building for partners providing CM services (initially CFTA, with potential expansion to other CP CM actors). Activities will achieve the Protection Cluster objective 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence through: Standard activity CHILD PROTECTION and MHPSS: Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support and programmes aimed at supporting vulnerable children and families who have been affected by conflict related violence and violations and violence in households. (1) CFTA will provide child protection case management services for 120 children (50% boys and 50% girls including 10% of children with disability) under the age of 18. Children are assessed and registered based on the vulnerability matrix of the Gaza CM SOPs. Case management services will also include emergency cash (100$ per case), managed by the caseworker, for targeted material and in-kind support like NFIs that can be used to provide support to extremely vulnerable families and children in order to reduce the risks and vulnerabilities of the child. The needs per case will be decided based on an assessment by the caseworker. (2) CTCCM will provide focused psychosocial support services that include 7 cycles of the Teaching Trauma Recovery Intervention (TRT) for 84 children between the age of 8-18 years (50% boys and 50% girls), and 35 cycles of other structured PSS interventions such as Team UP, DEALS structured PSS for 525 affected children (60% girls and 40% boys). Standard activity CHILD PROTECTION: capacity building for CP service providers. War Child will provide regular supervision and coaching sessions to CFTA (5 female case managers including 1 supervisor) using supervision and coaching practices. WCH will work to strengthen the case supervision structure, starting with direct supervision, along with ensuring adherence to child protection minimum standards, available case management SoPs and related data protection and information sharing protocols. War Child has extensive experience on case management in oPt, regionally and globally, and supports localization efforts by transferring knowledge and skills to local national organizations to take on more ownership of case management and be prepared when emergencies arise, thus enhancing sustainability. War Child will train other CP actors on Case Management supervision and coaching. A comprehensive 5 day training package will be designed based on two types of assessments: 1. organizational readiness assessment of CP actors which focuses supervision and coaching, information management, child safeguarding, PSEA and specific alignment to the child protection minimum standards in humanitarian action (CPMS). 2. An individual capacity assessment for the social workers that reviews knowledge, skills and attitudes on child protection and case management to be supported through supervision and coaching. Standard activity CHILD PROTECTION: CFTA will provide awareness sessions to 450 adults and children on CP including risks, case management services, how caregivers can refer their children to CP and GBV services, PSEA and the referral pathways.Standard activity MHPSS: Provision of MHPSS for adults (including caregivers). CTCCM will provide 21 cycles of WCH's Caregiver Support Intervention (CSI) - PSS support for 315 caregivers between the age of 18 to 60 years (60% female and 40% male including 5% PWD). The intervention is designed to help caregivers manage their own stress in order to better support children, and reduce harsh parenting practices. CSI is research informed, and designed based on the identification of common elements of evidence-based stress management programs. It will be delivered by community facilitators and para-professionals trained and supervised by War Child. Standard activities GBV: CFTA will provide MHPSS services, Emergency health care and legal aid within their GBV case management response for 120 women in need of urgent protection needs and referral to needed services, including 5% of women with disability. This service will also include awareness sessions for women on PSEA and reporting channels. Being a multi sectorial Protection end education project, the project will also contribute to the Education Cluster Objective 2: Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children through: Standard education activity: provision of remedial education and catch up learning support to vulnerable school aged children through face to face or remote support. TCC will provide remedial education and learning equipment for 400 children in the 1st-4th grade affected by the lack of access to quality education and online learning during COVID-19 over a period of 10 months (50% boys and 50% girls including 10% CWD, 200 each school semester).

3 - Indicators

War Child has an established global monitoring and reporting framework and uses standardized and globally validated means of verification to assess program effects on the wellbeing of participating children and families, and participant satisfaction. Through WCH’s monitoring and evaluation methodology, quality implementation of programming and delivery of care will be ensured. Consortium partners will integrate monitoring at all levels (activities, outputs, and outcomes) in their daily work and mechanisms will be put in place to facilitate early detection of any negative unintended results of the project. Regular field visits to and supervision of all activity sites will be conducted, not only for accountability purposes but will also serve learning and support adaptive programming. Activities and interventions will be further refined and adapted to meet the specific needs of the communities through engagement of CBOs and community facilitators who will regularly assess needs and participant satisfaction through quarterly focus group discussions and using WCH tools for measuring participant satisfaction and through the establishment of a complaints and feedback mechanism with support from WCH. Routine project progress meetings and reviews will be held to ensure that the project is reaching its intended targets, address any unexpected results, reﬂect new priorities, or changes to the context. Learning will be embedded in the project at multiple levels: at the project level, the monitoring system including the participatory feedback mechanism - will gather evidence against the indicators in the results framework and will inform project level decision-making and adaptation. An end-of-project evaluation with participants through focus group discussions will also give children, caregivers, and community volunteers the opportunity to evaluate the various activities. Progress and lessons learnt will be shared on a regular basis with other stakeholders including Cluster partners. The project includes HRP standard indicators: # 609 (272 boys, 337 girls) children affected by conflict related violence and violence in households benefiting from structured psychosocial services (level 3); # 120 (60 boys, 60 girls) children affected by conflict related violence benefiting from specialised individual case management (level 3); # 5 CP workers (women and men) trained and supported in remote service delivery and CP minimum standards; # 300 (150 women), 150 children (75 boys, 75 girls) persons (women, girls, men and boys) participating in child protection awareness raising sessions on CP issues (Level 2) # 315 (100 men,215 women) vulnerable men and women benefiting from structured psychosocial services (level 3) # 80 GBV survivors (women, girls, men and boys) receiving MHPSS services # 30 GBV survivors (women, girls, men and boys) receiving emergency health care # 5 GBV survivors (women, girls, men and boys) receiving legal representation # 5 GBV survivors (women, girls, men and boys) receiving legal consultation # 400 (200 boys; 200 girls) students benefiting from remedial education services and learning support In addition the project includes the following benefit indicators 1. % of children participating in targeted psychosocial interventions who show improved psychosocial well-being. Globally validated tools including the short Warwick Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale are used to measure this indicator. The Short Warwick Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale includes seven validated items and two additions from WCH. The items cover the feeling and functioning dimensions of mental wellbeing. 2. % of caregivers with improved mental wellbeing score. The short Warwick Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale is used to measure this indicator 3. Participant satisfaction - Children, young people and adults including those with disability have a right to provide feedback about how satisfied they are with program activities. This indicator gathers feedback from participants about how they feel about a program activity, and how they think it can be improved. The group exercise gathers both qualitative and quantitative information.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Through this project, War Child and partners will provide Child Protection Case Management, MHPSS, and GBV services. While the need for child protection, GBV and psychosocial support is increasing, the current care system is overstretched and cannot respond to the protection and psychosocial needs of girls, boys and their caregivers. War Child’s assessments show that there is a significant gap between needs and the provision of MHPSS services. Likewise, the 2022 HNO highlights the needs for multi-sectoral GBV case management, and increased access for women and girls and to MHPSS services. The budget includes assistance for multi-sectoral needs, such as medical care, education needs, to support the case management services.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Action based learning and accountability: M&E information and lessons learned, including non-confidential information collected through the feedback and complaint mechanism, will be shared and discussed internally with partners and staff to support management in planning, decision making, actions for quality improvement and changes in programming.

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

a - Generic email,c - Complaint box,d - Satisfaction survey,e - Field visit

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

Community-based and child-friendly complaints and feedback mechanisms will be established for all services, and participant satisfaction assessments using WCH tools and through quarterly focus group discussions with women, men, girls and boys will regularly inform the project of what’s working and what needs improvement and to be further tailored. War Child and partner team members will every month analyze and reflect upon feedback and complaints received to ensure regular reflection on the issues raised, solutions proposed and who reported them help us to address problems, and feed into strategic decisions and help to the program as needed. An end-of-project evaluation with participants through focus group discussions will also give children, caregivers, and community volunteers the opportunity to evaluate the various activities.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

For adults WC email and the phone number of a child safeguarding/complaint focal point are shared with project participants at the inception phase. For children War Child conducts an introductory session before they start participating in the activities. In the session they are briefed about the CP and Child safeguarding risks and made aware about CS focal points and his/her contact number. WC specific complaint boxes are placed in an easily reached area in each CBO and one focal point from WC opens the boxes and check them regularly.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Accountability to Affected Population, Protection, Conflict Sensitivity, Do No Harm and GBV are mainstreamed into War Child’s project implementation through 1) accountability measures in line with War Child Integrity Framework, including community-based and child-friendly complaints and feedback mechanisms for all services, and participants’ satisfaction assessments using WCH tools and FGD with women, men, girls and boys. 2) Identifying vulnerable groups across communities to foster social stability, avoid exacerbating tensions within the community and promote the equitable distribution of services across identity groups; 3) Analyzing existing community power dynamics to ensure the needs of individuals and groups at risk of marginalization are voiced (also through the feedback mechanism); 4) Do No Harm principles included within CP and child safeguarding competency development of partners and communities; 5) commitment on child safeguarding and confidentiality; 6) Timely referral to specialized MHPSS, CP, and GBV professionals; 7) Meaningful Access through outreach to children and caregivers with disability; 8) promoting participation and empowerment of boys and girls, and men and women; 9) applying a gender age marker in the design and monitoring of activities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project enables equal and impartial access to assistance and services and targets vulnerable groups by 1) working in the locations most affected by the crisis and focusing on newly displaced; 2) including specialized services for vulnerable children and caregivers survivors of/at risk of CP or SGBV whom will be identified through Case Management; 3) ensuring meaningful access and participation of vulnerable children and caregivers (including people with disabilities) through coordination with MoSD, CBOs and outreach. Selection of participants will be based on vulnerability and following humanitarian standards (such as being acutely affected by air strikes, family or community violence/abuse/neglect, disabilities, child labor, COVID-19, child-headed households, UASC). Activities and interventions will be adapted to meet the specific needs and all team members and field facilitators will receive a refresher/ orientation on inclusion by War Child CP team.
