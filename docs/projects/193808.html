Project Summary

The proposed project contributes to HRP SO.#2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources also, FSS SO2. #2: Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs. and FSS SO #3: advocacy and awareness, are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, in fair partnerships among concerned parties.
The project aims to contribute to reduced social and economic risks and vulnerabilities among crises-affected populations of all gender, age, and abilities in the Gaza Strip.
To ensure effectiveness, inclusivity and relevance of the response, the project proposes two modalities: (1) Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA): 300 HHs (Total of 1,680 individuals: 390 M, 379 W, 395 B, 384 G, 45 elderly including 84 PwDs) will benefit from a 6-month Multi-Purpose Cash support to cover their basic needs. The transfer value will be based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket “MEB” and the transfer value of the Gaza Cash Working Group “GCWG”. To the extent possible, the assistance will be tailored to different gender and abilities of each HHs by adding a top- up for protection to meet the special needs of at-risk-people -who are PwDs, elderly, people with chronic diseases and pregnant women- (2) Cash-for-Work: 227 HHs (total 1271 individuals 280 M, 270 W, 303 B, 285 G, 33 elderly including 100 PwDs ) will benefit from
temporary employment through CfW opportunities for five months. 150 of the male and female participants will be unskilled (laborer) and 77 skilled (graduates). 100 male/female out of the 227 participants will be with different disabilities.
The project will be implemented in partnership with “Gaza Culture and Development Group”; in the meanwhile. all interventions with participants with disabilities will be coordinated with main actors such as Humanity and Inclusion and Disabilities Representative Persons Network - DRBN (Gaza).

1 - Needs

The frequent crises and escalations have exacerbated pre-existing vulnerabilities in Gaza, increasing the already high poverty, food insecurity, and unemployment rates, constraining the ability of local authorities to maintain basic service provision, and leading to concerning losses to its human capital. The losses in GDP due to consecutive crises have translated into worsening social conditions, unemployment in Gaza, which was already high prior to the conflict, is predicted to increase by around two percentage points and reach 50 percent in 2021 due to the damage and physical destruction of businesses, physical injuries and the overall decline in economic activity. In 2022, 53.4% of Gaza population are people in need (PiNs), approximately 1 out of 3 people experienced some form of food insecurity, 84% (1.54 million people) of which are expected to need humanitarian assistance. 77 % of those in need of humanitarian assistance are in the Gaza Strip (FSS_ HNO_2023 Narrative draft.Poverty and unemployment remain the main drivers of chronic food insecurity, which is largely caused by the limited economic access to food rather than availability of food in the local market.The 2022 MSNA data showed that 54.4% of households in Gaza borrowed money for food as a coping mechanism, 80.7% of families rely on less preferred/less expensive food, 44% Limit portion sizes at mealtimes and 37.4% borrow food/relying on help from relatives or friends. Moreover, the negative strategies are extended to other priorities; 52.2% of households in Gaza reduced expenses on health for the purpose of securing food. As known, the percentages food insecurity gets higher in households headed by women (61% FHH in Gaza) where it’s higher than men headed households by 3 percent (31 versus 28%) .These factors, combined with some protection concerns paint more dire picture of household food security in Gaza, for example, 23% of women and girls participated in the 2022 MSNA identified markets as one of places that they avoid, main reasons of market avoidance were their fear from verbal harassment (50%), fear from sexual harassment (35%) and fear of robbery (24%).Concerning livelihoods and employment, the 2022 MSNA shows that 60% of the HHs are unemployed and looking for a job. However, opportunities are scarce. Among younger population (ages 15-29) -who are 22% of the total population- and majority of the unemployed youth are holders of intermediate diplomas or higher), the unemployment rate stood at 44.1 %. As well as unemployment in Gaza is still much higher among women than among men, reaching 65.1% in the second quarter of 2022. 43.6% of unemployed women in Palestine had completed 13 or more years of schooling. According to the Global Protection Cluster latest assessment in March 2022; Gender division of labour and traditional social norms in Palestinian -specially Gaza- are main drivers of the low employment rate among women.Geographically, the 2022 MSNA data showed that the highest percent of unemployment in Gaza Strip is in the Middle area with 70.2% followed by Rafah with 65.5%, 63.3% in North Gaza, 56.1% in Khan Younis and 54.4% in Gaza City. The high rate of unemployment in Gaz Strip (44% (38.2% male,65.1% female)) and lack of economic opportunities appears to be linked directly and indirectly to many of the key issues faced by households as identified through the MSNA 2022 data. This appears to be increasing the dependency of Gaza households on aid/assistance and contributing to the high reliance of Gaza households on negative coping mechanisms. Whereby; 81.0% of Gaza households reported challenges to being able afford their household’s basic needs and 50.5% reported relying on aid and assistance as their primary source. In Gaza; Multi-dimensional needs have raised the HHs vulnerability. Findings of 2022 Gaza MSNA showed that 89.6% of surveyed HHs indicated that at least one of its members has a healthcare need (91.1% were FHH and 96.1% were HH includes member with disability). 59.8 %of HHs reported different type of shelter damage due to protracted escalations Besides HHs’ food insecurity, 7.1% of HH reported having school-aged children not attending school where 36.5% indicated the main reason is that they cannot afford to pay school related expenses. This in addition to inflation and significant increase in prices.Moreover, considering gender and disability special needs are genuinely increase the HH vulnerability status, IRPAL conducted a rapid assessment -August 2022- as continuous learning practices for better future cash-based interventions and ensure safety and dignity, and equitable access of participants of all gender, age, and disabilities, key findings were: - 100% of respondents who have a vocation and/or any work skills including F/M youth with disabilities (YwDs) do prefer the CFW as a modality that enhances their dignity and equitable access to satisfy their families' needs.- On the contrary, 100% of respondents of all genders, ages, and disabilities who don’t have work vocation or skills prefer Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance” MPCA” as an unconditional and unrestricted modality.- Feedback of F/M youth with disabilities (YwDs) who have abilities to work demonstrate dissatisfaction about the level of market accessibility due to a major barrier of selecting one sector (such as agriculture) is a limitation to the market accessibility as the employers hold assumptions that YwDs are not able to work in this heavy sector- The social norms and assumption that the woman has the reproductive role, and the man is the one who should gain a living and the one who has access and control over resources may cause conflict and GBV to women if women are targeted under the CfW component as a reversal to gender roles.- The social habit of undermining or considering the needs of PwD, children, women, and the elderly as a low priority might cause the protection risk of exclusion or neglect.

2 - Activities

R 1: Decreased the multi-sectoral needs of the targeted vulnerable households as a result of equitable access to multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA)Act1.1. Nomination through partner, crosscheck with clusters and main actors and selection of 300 HHs to receive MPCA. Act1.2 Conduct SADDD and AGD analysis to selected HHs and tailor the assistance (add 100 ILS top up for each at-risk-person inside HHs). Act1.3. Conduct baseline for selected HHs which assess the HH multi-dimensional needs Act.1.4 Conduct orientation and awareness sessions to participants across Gaza governorates, The orientation sessions shall include sharing information about the project details, the complaint and feedback mechanism in IRPAL and partner GCDG, raising awareness about GBV and PSEAH and promote inclusion and protection manners. Act. 1.5. Provision of MPCA (1081 ILS) plus tailored top up for protection (100 ILS per each at-risk-person in the HH) for 6 months to 300 HHs (Total of 1,680 individuals: 390 M, 379 W, 395 B, 384 G, 45 elderlies including 84 PwDs). Act.1.6. Conduct (PDM) after the third MPCA round. Act.1.7. Based on results of AGD analysis, refer the HHs with high protection risks (i.e GBV, SEAH, ..etc) to specialized organizations within IRPAL, partner referral and SEAH networks. R2.: Improved food security status of targeted households as a result of access to equal CFW opportunitiesAct. 2.1. Nomination, crosscheck and selection of 227 HHs (total 1271 individuals 280 M, 270 W, 303 B, 285 G, 33 elderlies including 100 PwDs) to receive CFW opportunities (100 participants out of 227 direct participants will be PwDs). Act.2.2 Conduct SADDD and AGD analysis to selected HHs. Act.2.3, Select the hosting institutions based on pre-set criteria. The CFW opportunities will be at community organizations and private sector companies in different sectors, this to ensure appropriateness, adaptability and protection to female participants and m/f participants with disabilities. Act 2.4. Conduct baseline with selected HHs & hosted organizations which would focus on food security status and inclusion Act.2.5 Conduct orientation and awareness sessions to participants & hosting organizations across Gaza governorates, The orientation sessions shall include sharing information about the project details, the complaint and feedback mechanism in IRPAL and partner GCDG, raising awareness about GBV and PSEAH and promote inclusion and protection manners. Act. 2.6. Provide 5 month-CFW opportunity to 227 participants from vulnerable HH (total 1271 individuals 280 M, 270 W, 303 B, 285 G, 33 elderlies including 100 PwDs). Unskilled (labours) will work in agriculture activities supporting small to medium farmers and cooperatives. Skills graduates (agronomists) will be responsible on supervising the labor and providing extension technical services to labours and small farmers. For participants with disabilities, they will be hosted in agriculture or non-agriculture hosting organizations to perform tasks suites their abilities and skills. Monthly wages will be set based on the daily rates endorsed by FSS cluster, graduates (skilled) will receive 75 ILS per day while the unskilled workers (labour) will receive 60 ILS per day for 22 working days per month. A top up for protection (100 ILS) will be added to Participants with disabilities’ monthly wages for 5 months considering their special needs. Act.2.7. Conduct (PDM) after the third CFW round. Act.2.7. Based on results of AGD analysis, refer the HHs with high protection risks to specialized organizations within IRPAL and GCDG referral networks. *Nominations will be open for be received from different sources; i.e IRPAL database. GCDG database, MOSD, selected hosting organizations., Humanity and Inclusion and Disabilities Representative Persons Network -DRBN (Gaza). *Selection criteria for project components will be determined the project team (IRPAL and GCDG) then shared with related parties, then to be disseminated with stakeholders and participants*Selection criteria and scoring of HHs to receive MPCA will be basically built on vulnerability factors i.e HHH (male or female), source of income, level of deep poverty, # of HH members, # of ARP, etc.*Selection criteria and scoring of HHs to receive CFW will be built on vulnerability factors plus availability of members who are able to work (skilled or unskilled).*The selected long list (project target & waiting list) will be crosschecked with MoSD, other related ministries and FSS cluster.*Orientation and awareness sessions will include distribution of related information, educational and communication material (IEC) i.e project brochure, CFM brochure and PSEAH leaflet – IRPAL will contact UNWOMAN to share some PSEAH material and to deliver orientation session to project team about PSEAH and detection. * IRPAL and GCDG will review their protection policies to increase the focus on PSEAH related procedures *GCDG will have a leading role facilitating the field activities starting by nominations, data collection and analysis, conducting the orientation sessions and the regular follow up with participants. on the other hand, IRPAL will ensure the project result-based monitoring, engagement and coordination with related ministries, clusters. and related actors like Humanity and Inclusion and Disabilities Representative Persons Network -DRBN (Gaza).

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Building on IRPAL, community feedback sessions, latest research in August 2022 and PDM of 2021 MPCA/CFW project ; Findings revealed that: Most households have multi-sectoral needs. that voucher modality usually doesn’t meet i.e. debt and seasonal items. Thus the Multi-purpose cash allows households to meet their priority needs in dignified manned with freedom of choice. Moreover, the (MPCA) modality is the most preferable modality to vulnerable HHs whose members don’t have certain vocation or skills. On the other hand, (CfW) modality is more preferable to HHs that includes members able to work including members with disabilities.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

As a CHS certified organization, IR ensures accountability to affected people (AAP) throughout its projects by implementing mechanisms that allow target communities to be meaningfully and continuously involved in the proposed project activities' decision-making process. The commitment to complaints and feedback handling mechanism (CFRM) constitutes an essential part of IRPAL’s overall work on accountability as per Commitment 5 of the CHS with the quality criterion: “Complaints are welcomed and addressed”. IRPAL’s CFRM is designed to provide proactive inclusive channels to all people of all ages and abilities. Complaints can be received through phone calls, SMS, WhatsApp, Complaints Box, and through IRPAL and all partner organizations. Therefore, people with different abilities have proper awareness and access to safe and secure complaints channels.An inclusive CFRM intends to include local communities in feedback and complaints processes, close the protection gap, and ensure that persons with disabilities enjoy the same standards of equality, rights and dignity. Thus, employment contracts and awareness materials are written in Braille language and a sign language interpreter will be available during awareness sessions and in digital video materials. Complaint guidance notes, procedures, and contact information will be included in all project documents, publications, and available at all project sites.Moreover, Communication tools will be inclusive by including braille and sign language. During project implementation, the project team will conduct regular inclusive consultation (face-face or through phone calls) with the communities; at least three consultations. Consultations will accommodate persons of all ages and abilities to enable full participation of the communities. By the end of the project, lessons learned will be captured through workshops organized with the target group and other project stakeholders.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

IRPAL with partners maintain inclusive and protective programming through identifying the age, gender and diversity of all people of all ages and abilities. Primary assessment up to date research on gender power relations, gender roles, gender gaps access and control over resources, identified and informed the design of the intervention in which top-up were planned to be added based on participants gender needs.Additionally, Orientation and awareness sessions are planned as mitigation strategy to maintain safety, dignity, reduce GBV and encourage reporting any SEAH cases.Using bank transfers and e-delivery mechanisms will maintain flexibility and privacy for participant to withdraw the assistance amount based on their needs in dignified manner.IRPAL and GCDG will implement the project within the protection and inclusion framework and safeguarding policies which its inclusive complaint and feedback response mechanism (CFRM) constitute key part of these policies. IRPAL safeguarding focal point shall conduct a refresher session for the project team on the overall protection principles, and coordination with UNWOMEN will take place to conduct an orientation session about PSEAH to strengthen team understanding and planning mitigation measures.All contracts with participants will include clauses related to protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH).
