Project Summary

The project targets 20,0000 individuals in Mundri East and Mundri West Counties (WES) and Terekeka, Morobo and Yei Counties (CES), using an integrated approach including nutrition, health, and GBV/protection using in-kind modalities for distribution of WASH items, coupled with a small component of Cash-For-Work for the construction of semi-permanent WASH facilities. All interventions will be carried out following Covid-19 preventive measures; social distancing, use of PPE and preventive messaging through IEC.
The response will include WASH-nutrition integrated interventions, targeting 400 couples of “couple mother/care-taker – severely malnourished child” at nutrition facilities and household level, with ditribution of standard hygiene kits aiming at breaking WASH-related diseases transmission cycle. Each hygiene kit will contain a minimum package of household water treatment products for 2 months, a bucket with lid for storage, a bucket without lid or jerrycan for transportation, soap for 3 months and a filter cloth.
Additionally, the response will improve access to safe water and promote safe hygiene practices in communities showing high prevalence of GAM, targeting 4,800 vulnerable displaced individuals with access to semi-permanent water points (construction of 7 boreholes).
The response will also reduce the prevalence of WASH-related diseases and risk of outbreak by transmission, by targeting 3,400 individuals with access to semi-permanent latrines and hand-washing facilities (construction of 5) and semi-permanent water points (construction of 3 boreholes). One Water Management Committee will be formed in each location of construction of a water point (one WMC could cover 2 water points if relatively in the same area), and its members will be trained in order to take part to the construction of the facilities, under the CFW component of this project, and will then have the responsibility of their maintenance.
Furthermore, YEDA will focus on targeting 8,000 vulnerable displaced individuals with rapid interventions aimed at catering their WASH needs, through direct distribution of WASH hygiene kits (minimum package described above). For this kind of response, beneficiary targeting will be guided by the WASH Cluster criteria of multiple vulnerabilities; PLWs, children <5, the elderly, PWN/PWDs (30%), high number of dependents, combined with multiple displacement, lack of ties with the local community/purchasing power, GBV and COVID-19 risks associated with SNFI needs.
This will be paired with hygiene promotion using the PHAST/CHAST approach to improve hygiene behaviors, to reduce diarrheal diseases and encouraging effective management of WASH services. 60 trained Community Hygiene Promoters (20 per County) will conduct house-to-house visits and hygiene awareness sessions in health and nutrition clinics, on diarrhea prevention, hand washing, MHM, infant and young child feeding (IYCF), water treatment, food storage and waste disposal. Additionally, key messaging including HIV/AIDS, COVID-19 prevention, EVD and cholera prevention will be passed on during distribution and trainings, as an EPR mainstreaming.
Finally, WASH-GBV mitigation will be prioritized in all the targeted counties, after conduction of a site level safety audit, with protection/GVB partners on ground. Under this objective, 3,400 vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age will be targeted with distribution of Menstrual Health Management kits, paired with training in menstrual health management, enabling them to maintain their personal hygiene, privacy and dignity, and improve school attendance. Additionally, the siting, construction and repairs of WASH facilities will be done through thorough GBV risk analysis (FDG with women/girls, conducted by female staff with a GBV/protection partners) to ensure gender-appropriate and need-tailored interventions, minimizing GBV associated risks.

1 - Needs

5.6 million people in South Sudan are in IPC phase 3 or worse, and lack adequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices, including IDPs, returnees and non-displaced host community members. This was caused by the effect of subnational conflict coupled by unprecedented rains resulting in floods, and to the effects of the Covid-19 pandemic and related restrictions of movements on the local economy. Internal displacement is straining the limited resources among the host communities hence limiting the coping abilities to face shocks. WASH needs are high in non-camp settings, especially among PWDs who have limited access to WASH assistance. PWDs are at least one family member for 15% of the population, according to the HNO 2020, due to the effects of the conflict on civillian population.Destruction of infrastructures, and health, education and WASH facilities are going to negatively impact the living standards and wellbeing of the population, with women and PWDs being the most affected (HNO, 2020). Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased GBV risk when collecting water far from their homes, while the high rate of open defecation exposes women, children and PWD to higher risk of violence and discrimination. Also, lack of MHM kits for women and adolescent girls of reproductive age poses sanitation and protection concerns.Access to water is critical; only 36% of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without protection concerns, while the remaining 64% rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41%), take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19%), or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4%) (HNO 2020). Particularly, in CES 41% of the population relies only on surface water, second only to Upper Nile (66%) and Jonglei (43%). The quantity of water available per day is also below standards across South Sudan; 17% of people are unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39% have insufficient access to water for handwashing, which greatly affects hygiene standards and poses greater risks of epidemics.More than 73% of the population practices open defecation due to lack of sanitation facilities or negative cultural practices. Overall, 77% of the population declares not having access to latrines, while only 17% of households reports having a latrine in their compound, 4% having access to shared ones, and 2% having access to a communal/institutional one (HNO 2020). The lack of sanitation facilities is prominant in remote areas of Morobo, Terekeka, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties, among others, where zero to 10 per cent of households reported using latrines. Limited access to appropriate and dignified sanitation and hand washing locations, in particular in urban and peri-urban settings, increases the risk of cholera and other diarrheal or vector-borne diseases. Hygiene practices, and especially hand-washing at critical moments are reportedly low across CES and WES, increasing risk of cholera and EVD outbreaks and highlights the need of spreading preventive hygiene messages and establishing hand-washing facilities. The critical health needs also related to the Covid-19 epidemic, insufficient food access, high cases of SAM and GAM, and poor WASH services may contribute high levels of morbidity and mortality if not responded rapidly. Inadequate WASH services contribute not only to disease outbreak, but also to increased SAM cases. Lack of improved sanitation and limited knowledge of hygiene practices contributes to poor feeding habits of children leading to malnutrition. Data from IOM, OCHA and partners revealed that displaced persons are settled in areas without clean water or improved sanitation, and have little opportunity to practice proper hygiene in what turns out to be flooded areas for most of the year.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Market functionality will be considered at all stages of the project cycle: preparedness, planning, design and implementation set-up, Distribution cycle and monitoring, exit and feedback.YEDA has put in place a contingency plan in order to be able to respond rapidly with targeted CVA in cases of chrisis, since this kins of response can be implemented quickly on a large scale. Strategic placement of YEDA’s field offices in Yei, Mundri and Pibor, as well as the preparation of guidelines and templates, and staff training on them, has been taken on during the preparedness phase of this project. The YEDA’s preparedness plan and guidelines are informed by its participation to the national Cash Working Group and the WASH cluster MBP/cash working group, which together with information collected from field partners, allow us to monitor market functionality in different rural areas.At the planning phase, need and gap analysis will be complemented by a market analysis in all locations, to establish the best way of implementing Cash and Voucher Assistance. The response analysis report will inform the course of action by providing information on; beneficiary needs and preferences for assistance delivery, community and political acceptance of CVA, market conditions and operational conditions, including beneficiary access to the market, and supplier’s ability to restock and willingness to take part to the project with full awareness. Design and implementation set-up will be informed by the response analysis report, which inherently consider market functionality, including all actors involved in the market system where the CVA will be implemented, obstacles to access, protection risks and risk of harm to the local economy by introducing CVA, and related mitigation measures. Community consultations, including representatives of all vulnerable groups, are key at this stage to assure the best outcome of this intervention. Also at this stage, contracted partners and suppliers, such as financial service providers, will be required to establish all relevant operational processes and support mechanisms necessary for successful cash or voucher distribution, in order to ensure timely and efficient delivery. YEDA’s prior experience has shown that it is key that vendors and supplier undergo a creful vetting process and sign a MoU to ensure ability and commitment to provide quality items in a timely manner and at an agreed maximum cost. In this view, a risk analysis must also consider factors that might affect the cash transfer, for instance insecurity on the road and inflation which can cause the cost of items to increase or the exchange rate to be unfavourable. At the stage of distribution, rapid monitoring, monitoring of market prices and the established CFRM will allow a smooth distribution of cash or voucher transfers to beneficiaries and their subsequent exchange/utilisation by beneficiaries to purchase commodities and services. Due to this monitoring mechanisms, it will also be possible to make adjustments to the programme design and implementation processes if deemed necessary and appropriate, after consultation with the WASH cluster coordinators and UN-OCHA.Finally, through the exit strategy developed during programme planning, YEDA will determine if the project will be handed over to local stakeholders, or formally closed. The exit strategy and outcome of the project will be communicated to the beneficiaries and target communities, and will involve restitution and closure of documentation and related contracts. Success stories and lessons learnt will be shared with all stakeholders, donors and cluster coordinators and will inform future programming.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In South Sudan, CVA has been proved to be an appropriate form of assistance, because the following preconditions are met: cash is a familair form of exchange for commodities and services within the community, and it is used to meet WASH needs in areas with functional markets, and when accompanied by a careful planning informed by context analysis, cash assistance does not increase the level of risk or vulnerability faced by any recipient. For this specific project, CVA is preferred to in-kind assistance in the targeted areas (Nagero, Terekeka and Mundri East County) where markets are functional, there are low risk of conflict and all target beneficiaries have access to the market. The latter includes absence of economic, social, cultural and physical barriers to access. In such areas, CVA can revitalize the local market, creating a multiplier effect that benefits the whole community, and can help restore dignity and coping abilities. It can also ensure more flexibility and rapidity in terms of aid delivery.Evidence shows that in many contexts, cash transfers are more cost-efficient to deliver than in-kind aid as they require fewer staff and lower logistical costs. Additionally, multipurpose cash will give the maximum amount of flexibility to meet the beneficiary needs, as well as choice at a low cost – ensuring value for money. This has been proven by YEDA’s experience in implementing Cash-Based Interventions in Mundri East, Mundri West, Yei and Nagero County, through a combination of Cash-for-Work for construction of communal latrines and water points rehabilitation by community members and cash vouchers for purchase of market based WASH items. All these interventions empowered people by increasing their purchasing power, increasing their resilience to shocks, rehabilitating community assets such as access roads to the market, and providing them with essential skills (e.g. training water points rehabilitation/latrine construction/hand-pump management) to be employable in the future, either by NGOs or within the community. Finally, careful project and market monitoring, also through the Complaint, Feedback and Response Mechanisms embedded in this project, will allow the YEDA team to ensure that the grants will be directed and used by the intended beneficiaries (e.g. selecting women as head of households/direct beneficiaries for cash delivery), that suppliers are able to meet the supply demands and to stock quality items in time.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Addressing inequality and protection threats is key in this project. YEDA has trained and continuously reminds its staff on PSEA, including GBV referral pathways to ensure GBV/PSEA risks are prevented and/or mitigated, and it is already in contact with the GBV subcluster FP for CES for any referrals. Under this project, any new staff, volunteers and local partners will be trained in gender integration and protection mainstreaming to better enable them apply a gender and protection lens (do no harm principle) throughout program activities. This also includes a zero tolerance to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). A criteria for any activity implementation will consider 3 key components; gender appropriateness, safety and accessibility by all groups. Appropriate, safe and accessible sites and schedules for distribution will be agreed upon in consultation with the elderly, PLW and PSN/PWDs, who normally face difficulties in accessing distribution sites due to distance and SGBV risks. The selection criteria for the beneficiaries will prioritize gender and protections concerns to ensure persons with disability, pregnant and lactating women, female heads of households, and the elderly among other groups with protection concerns receive the assistance needed. Beneficiaries, as well as the community as a whole, will be encouraged to report any suspected behavior/acts of SGBV in their community by humanitarian staff/volunteers, using the confidential CFRM that will be put in place, as well as the different channels (GBV/Protection referral pathways, or PSEA taskforce) for remedial. In order to prevent SEA, YEDA will mostly target female-headed households (widows, single mothers), providing items which are desperately needed to reduce the risk of exchanging sexual favors. During the distributions, the beneficiaries will use separate queues by gender, in order to avoid harassment towards women, while the YEDA volunteers will handpick the most vulnerable people and assist them in receiving and transporting their items. YEDA will also work to ensure that the contents of aid are sensitive to any protection risks, monitored through community assessments and PDMs. YEDA is already in contact with the GBV FP and partners in CES, and referral pathways will be used to ensure PSEA risks are prevented and mitigated, and will collaborate with GBV/protection partners (IsraAID) for joint activity implementation. A GBV/PSEA risk analysis will be undertaken on access to WASH services and WASH item distributions to make sure that no harm is done and that a minimum of WASH and Protection sphere standards are implemented. This will also allow YEDA to take into account all gender perspectives, ensuring protection of all community members and mitigating risks that the project activities might pose to specific age and gender groups. All activities will include community sensitization on GBV and creation of Community Representation Committees (CRCs), with the aim to reduce dangerous behavior and find common strategies to reduce inequalities and protection threats within the community.Finally, YEDA staff will ensure to inform beneficiaries that the assistance is free of any cost, and confidential complaints, feedback and protection mechanisms are in place to report any violation. Sensitization on GBV, in relation to creating a safe environment for women and girls, will be done with protection partners using gender-tailored messages.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community consultations will conducted at inception phase of the project, as integral part of the multi-sector analysis in the targeted project locations. They will not only include the community leaders, but also Focus Group Discussions with representatives of the vulnerable groups (women, youth, elderly and disable persons), in order to make sure that views, challenges and needs of the vulnerable groups are addressed. Also, in each distribution location YEDA will form a Community Representation Committee, including representatives of women/youth/PWD and other vulnerable groups, IDP and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs (50% female). The CRCs provide great insight for activity planning and project design, Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), as well as context-specific information, changes, and early warning in case of conflict.The CRCs will work with YEDA in order to identification the most vulnerable households to be targeted by in-kind or cash-based interventions, based on the shared vulnerability criteria. The composition of the CRCs will ensure that each vulnerable and minority group is taken under consideration. The CRC will also be responsible for ensuring that IDPs, returnees and host communities are aware of the priorities for the selection of beneficiaries for distribution, and how vulnerable households have been verified. They will also assist the YEDA team in the distribution of WASH items (including a complaint desk), they will report to the YEDA staff on a regular basis about in case of any feedback and complaint about the intervention modalities (both in-kind and CBIs), in order for the project team to be able to make due changes in the project activities, if need be. Finally, the CRCs will assist the YEDA team by mediating in case of discontent among the population in regards to the prioritization of the beneficiaries, quality of items, and any other complaint raised.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community consultations will be conducted at the inception phase of the project, including FGDs with representatives of vulnerable groups (women, youth, elderly, PWDs), in order to make sure that views, challenges and needs of the vulnerable groups are addressed. Also, in each distribution location YEDA will form a Community Representation Committee, including representatives of vulnerable groups, IDP/returnee and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs. The CRCs, together with the Water Management Committees in case of WASH facility construction, provide great insight for activity planning and project design, Complaint and Feedback Mechanism, as well as context-specific information, changes, and early warning in case of conflict. They will work with YEDA in order to identify the most vulnerable households to be targeted by in-kind or cash-based interventions, based on the shared vulnerability criteria, and will be responsible for ensuring that all stakeholders are aware of the priorities for beneficiary selection, and providing feedback to the community about the project activities. They will also assist the YEDA team in the WASH distribution and other field activities, and assist the YEDA team by mediating in case of discontent among the population in regards to the prioritization of the beneficiaries, quality of items, and any other complaint raised and be part of the project monitoring team. For quality assurance and guidelines’ adherence, a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism will be in place, in consultation with the community to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback on project activities including the conduct of staff. This will be supplemented by continuous monitoring, PDM and end line evaluation (third party monitoring) that will be conducted jointly with community members/local leaders to assess satisfaction, timeliness, quality and impact of our intervention. Data collected will inform action during the activity implementation, while lessons learnt will be shared with the different stakeholders including the WASH cluster to inform and improve its future work.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The establishment of a Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) as an essential component of this project, through the establishment of complaint desks at every distribution center and of the Community Representation Committees will allow to adjust the internventions in the course of action. Such system will see key stakeholder act as a bridge between YEDA and the beneficiaries, in order to inform the organization about the best modalities for aid delivery, as well as issues, concerns and obstacles to the current intervention. For YEDA it is paramount to ensure effectiveness and efficiency in the aid delivery, and ensure that the interventions do no cause any harm to the local economy, social relations and fabric, and beneficiary wellbeing. To this effect, rapid monitoring will also allow to determine if the beneficiaries perceive the aid receive to be adequate, catering their main needs and ensuring that all communications are done appropriately, for instance using appropriate IEC, language and communication channels to reach a given aim. Such aim could include explaining purpose of response, targetning criteria and NGO capacity in order to manage expectaion, enforce behavioural change for COVID-19 prevention, effective training in hand-pump rehabilitation, sensitization on the use of the WASH items and materials distributed, explanation on the scope of the response and GBV referral pathways among others. Continuous project monitoring through the collabotation of the CRCs will allow us to determine if the modalities used are appropriate or need to be adjusted to that specific context or situation.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To minimize the beneficiaries’ risk of being exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, all activity locations and other key activity details will be agreed upon the members of the Community Representation Committees. For instance, the distribution locations, time and days will have to be favourable for all vulnerable categories to minimize their protection risks on the way. The CRCs include representatives of vulnerable cathegories, which will be able to advise appropriately on the matter, and local authorities that will negotiate access and ensure that no armed actors are present at the distribution site. The long-standing positive relationship of YEDA with the local authorities will also be helpful to this end. Additionally, there is often a risk of interference from local leaders and sometimes authorities in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries. This will be mitigated by leveraging the long-lasting positive relationship that YEDA has maintained with the local authorities during the years, sensitizing the local population on the need to maintain certain prioritizaion criteria for assistance and on the risk of conflict over resources and negative effects on social cohesion, and by creating project ownership by involving beneficiaries and affected communities at every stage of the project implementation (as local staff, volutneers, trainees, community representatives, etc.). Additionally, data triangulation and third party monitoring, coupled with a carefully implemented CFRM, will guarantee to always target the intended beneficiaries of the project.
