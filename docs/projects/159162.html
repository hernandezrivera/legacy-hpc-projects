Project Summary

WRRS in the proposed intervention will ensure effective delivery of services in sites and settlements in Bay, Lower Juba, Gedo, sool and sanaag Regions involving equal input from women and men in the affected populations. WRRS will carry out activities in the proposed project in line with 2020 strategic objectives of the Somalia CCCM Cluster. A well coordinated strategy will done with consultations with the cluster, beneficiaries as well as the government to ensure that the outcome is of added value to the targeted beneficiaries. The project will offer supported capacity building, provision of the communication gargets, solar lights/ touches as well as the in cooperation of the IDPs to the cash for work interventions. Coordination meetings will be held on regular basis to ensure that the intervention is well coordinated and in an harmonized manner. WRRS will work closely with the clusters that are in line with the interventions within the targeted regions.

Mitigation Measures

One of the major risk of the Somali environment with a potential high impact is the worsening of the security situation due to clan conflicts, terrorism and political controversies that can limit the movements, resulting in a restriction of access to facilities, increased demand for health care services, population displacement, increased morbidity and mortality with a possibility of halting the intervention. As a mitigation measures WRRS has operational safety procedures that include clear risk assessment procedures, evacuation plans and suspension for the staff and it collaborates with local authorities and with community to guarantee acceptance, access and security of properties and staff. Regular contacts with the authorities at the regional and national levels will be maintain to ensure that valuable supporters and local authorities remains engaged and supportive of the programme. Under or non-representation of minority or marginalized communities in consultative meetings or amongst Community structures. - Likelihood: High; - Impact: high - Mitigation Measures: Hold an initial open mass gathering and introduce project objectives, selection criteria for various components, organizational procedures and make clear the entitlements for all. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms and ensure the information reaches all. Emphasize on inclusive subsequent meetings and possibly hold separate meetings among households clusters. Undertake random physical consultations in the settlements or if need be outside the settlements. Condition implementation to on equal and direct access with all communities and make this very clear to gatekeepers - Risk: Forced Evictions and Government unwillingness to provide land for sustainable infrastructure development for the displaced - Likelihood: high - Impact: High - Mitigation measures: Align project to government’s resilience recovery agenda to gain support and sustainability for project Advocate for legal rights of the IDPs in relation to the due process to be followed to relocate with dignity Sensitize and support the government on developing policies and enforcing laws on land to protect the displaced.gender-based violence and discrimination against women and girls as they access facilities Likelihood: High - Impact: Very high - Mitigation: - Involve women and girls in siting locations for communal facilities considering their security and safety . Ensure the location and access routes to facilities and distribution points are safe. Where possible avoid central distribution on NFIs, Hygiene kits to avoid long ques and exposure of items to evil doers but rather use mobile money cash transfer for beneficiary to purchase items at own convenient time. Ensure lighting in basic facilities to reduce women and girls overcrowding, waiting too long at the ques and getting caught in darkness. Avoid community tensions, by providing the host community with access to basic services where they are experiencing similar needs for services and facilities. Work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of basic services. Please provide further explanation

IDPs have limited options for durable solutions to their displacement. According to JMCNA findings indicate that the majority of IDPs prefer local integration. This however has so far remained a challenge due to lack of land availability, repeated evictions and minimal livelihoods options. Returns to the area of origin are extremely limited, as most IDPs have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian assistance. WRRS will make use of the CCCM coordination platforms to advocate Housing, Land and Property rights for the displaced. Durable solutions are one of the key pillars of WRRS Somalia programming, and continuous efforts are directed towards promoting durable solution and self-sufficiency of the displaced and vulnerable people across Somalia. However, WRRS recognizes the complexity and long milestones required to reach this vision. This project will contribute towards supporting opportunities for displaced people to achieve durable solutions given people cantered response, capacity building for both the government and IDP communities, linking community priorities with national government plans and as well as implementing community-based initiatives that can promote community recovery. WRRS with the UNHCR post distribution monitoring team will work towards ensuring accountability and proper registration of the beneficiaries verses the entitlements they have been issued with. The project will contribute to durable solutions efforts and especially in the targeted areas where the government together with UNHABITAT are working on site planning and settlement management in the existing IDP settlements. The support by the government offers an environment that if well utilized will see many durable solutions achieved. In line with the CCCM Cluster strategy, the proposed project will aim to provide specialized assistance for the most vulnerable and undeserved temporary settlements, enabling a livable environment for IDPs in which they receive timely and effective comprehensive response to their needs, in accordance with international humanitarian standards and aligned with the specific needs of women, men, girls and boys. People with disability will be factored in in our intervention so that they can be in a position to benefit with the humanitarian assistance being offered to the rest of the beneficiaries.
