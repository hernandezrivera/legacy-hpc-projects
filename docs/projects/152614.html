Project Summary

The project aims to provide Survival Kits /Emergency shelter/Non Food items to the most vulnerable
persons newly displaced/host communities and returnees. The project will focus on the life saving shelter/NFIs needs as identified through assessment to protect the affected displaced people from harsh weather conditions and protection risks targeting all states of South Sudan through mobile response as per need as well as using its static teams based in Wau, Bor, Akobo, twic and Duk. However major focus areas will be Jonglei, Unity, Northern Bahrul Ghazal, Westerna nd Eastern Equatoria states. Cash programming will also be considered in areas where markets are functional and shelter/NFI items are available and accessible to the community.
When blanket targeting is not the applicable, the action will target mainly lactating and pregnant women,
households with malnourished children, and households with members who have certain health
complications. Besides unaccompanied children, unaccompanied elderly and persons with no
community linkages will also be targeted.
The project will promote timely delivery of assistance through deployment of rapid response teams in
line with humanitarian priorities for 2019 HRP's SO 1 and SO2. NRC’s mobile response teams will be ready for deployment within 8 days to conduct assessments and within 14 days for full-fledged delivery of NFIs
and emergency shelters and other activities per location.
The project will also support returnees in settlement as a part of their local integration or returns to their areas of origin. This will be done in an integrated way with other projects being implemented by NRC so that the returnees dont only receive shelter support but also a source of Food security and Livelihood through the food security projects in the planned locations. Additionally provision for education in emergencies and Legal Counseling will be made for the returnees.
Approach:
Under the Cash Programme mainly the focus will be on construction of Shelters for returnees with active involvement of communities. Geographic preference will be in WAU, Bor and Yei areas where there are many returnees and markets are functional. Around $400,000 is estimated for the cash programme mentioned under the Supplies cost. The Cash programme will target 10,000 people covered by shelter and NFIs.
Integration: The shelter interventions will be implemented as part of the integrated Emergency Preparedness and Response (EP&R) where implementation is based on the multi-sector assessments and team involving WASH, Shelter, Food security and emergency Education with protection as a cross cutting theme. In some of the locations, the shelter projects link up with other ECHO and WFP funded emergency projects to ensure the beneficiaries receive a comprehensive response package.
The project will be implemented in coordination with other players in the sector especially IOM for information sharing and use of the cluster core pipeline supplies as well as other Shelter cluster members for joint assessments and response when appropriate. The project will also be coordinated with the Shelter cluster especially in prioritization of locations and needs assessment.

Needs

More than five years into the South Sudan conflict, the situation keeps worsening. Several ceasefire agreement were signed and violations to the agreements continue to be reported in different parts of the country between the government and various anti-government forces forcing thousands of civilians to flee within and outside the country resulting in more suffering for civilians. Inter and intra-communal violence, including cattle raiding, continues and is often interconnected with the national conflict. According to UN OCHA , there are 1.96 million internally displaced poeple in South Sudan, 198,086 living in POCs, 300,137 refugees living in South Sudan and 2.47 million South Sudanes refugees living in the neighboring countries. The 2018 HRP estimated that seven Million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. The protracted conflict has already resulted in the forced internal displacement of almost 1.9 million people with the large majority in hard to reach areas. The 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview shows that 1.53 million are in need of shelter/Nfis. In South Sudan, it has been observed that many of the IDPs have been displaced multiple times, and they need some sort of emergency shelter and/or non-food item (ES/NFI) support. This includes the majority of those who are living in PoCs, others in camp-like settings and collective centres together with those who living outside PoCs with the host communities require new shelters or reinforcement of existing infrastructure. REACH survey shows that IDPs living in the open places are estimated as 36% in Ulang, 30% in Myandit and 15% in Leer. While settlement with 50% or more shelter damaged due to conflict are estimated as 100% in Kajo Keji, 40% in Myandit, 26% in Koch and 25% in Leer County. Regarding Non Food Items (NFIs), high need was found for mosquito nets, plastic sheets and cooking pots mainly in Yei, Myandit, Magwi, Yambio, Leer and Panijar. Most of the affected population especially those in hard to reach areas have limited access to basic humanitarian assistance. The limited assistance provided initially was intended to sustain the IDPs for a short period of time, with the anticipation that the conflicts would cease and the IDPs return to their villages of origin. However, the renewed conflicts in various states points to a protracted conflict situation which will continue to displace populations. Additionally, the rainy season has strained humanitarian efforts as well as exposing the IDPs to greater vulnerability as most of the land in South Sudan is prone to flooding, leading to further displacement and also hindering accessibility to some areas. The climatic extremes experienced both the rainy and dry season create high need for NFIs to strengthen the populations copying mechanism. The emergency shelter kits provided are not adequate enough and will soon need to be replaced / changed. Most adults still sleep in the open, and occasionally congesting in one shelter to cover against the night rains, while the other available shelters are used to accommodate children, the sick and the old during the night. Destruction of shelters by burning the grass roofs and looting of the building materials as witnessed in various locations has left many returnees homeless. Lack of functional markets to procure materials for reconstruction worsens the situation thus leaving the community exposed to harsh climatic conditions.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The NRC team will engage local community leaders to help in the identification and verification of the beneficiaries. Besides, Protection officers from NRC and local authorities will orient the communities on Do No Harm, protection issues, humanitarian principles, distribution principles and complaint response mechanism.Complaint response mechanism will be handled by protection officers in the field by setting up a complaint desk in the waiting area. NRC normally does and will engage communities in the needs assessment by employing them as part of assessment team. Communities will also participate during the distributions and identification of beneficiaries. Communities also participate in focus group discussions, orientation and feed back mechanisms. Local communities are also employed during the distribution process especially in crowd control, registration, mobilization, arranging items/NFIs and offloading.Awareness and basic project information material will be translated in to local language for the better understanding of beneficiaries. A community leader will always be involved, complaints will always handled and immediate feedback given to beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will fill the complain forms and will be provided with help if required for record purpose. Feedback mechanism will also help in decisions for improvement to serve the communities in more effective manner.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

NRC conducted protection needs assessment in several locations targeted by the project, both those intended to be served by static and mobile activities. Primary and secondary data collected during the assessments informed a protection risk analysis that particularly considered the needs of women, recently displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Based on NRC’s protection needs assessment and protection risk analysis, we carefully considered a range of possible barriers to meaningful access during the design of the implementation, including security, financial, and perceived discrimination. NRC’s analysis reveals that it is likely beneficiaries will face a diversity of barriers, particularly according to AGD-related issues and local conflict dynamics. For example, a newly arrived IDP women in Wau, some single young educated young man in Akobo, and an unaccompanied elderly person Yei are all likely to face sever access constraints, if for different reasons (assumption of discrimination, fear of physical assault, mobility limitations). NRC will approach its implementation with nuance, recognising that its target groups are likely to encounter a range of access constraints that NRC teams will need to be aware of and reactive to. Teams will regularly re-evaluate access constraints throughout the intervention to ensure that beneficiaries are consistently able to reach, receive, and benefit from services.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In recognising that every individual faces a unique array of protection risks, depending upon their own vulnerabilities, capacities, and external threats, NRC conducted a gender-sensitive needs assessment, consulting more than 100 women, girls, men and boys in targeted areas, including older men and women, female-headed households, and persons with disabilities of both sexes. The assessment identified a range of individualised protection threats and potential inequalities that NRC is considering in programming interventions and approach modalities. An example of one such consideration is that that men must be explicitly targeted and significantly engaged. In all target locations, a majority of respondents reported that men enjoy sole control over assets and decision-making in households At the same time, respondents across gender and location reported high rates of GBV. A failure to proactively engage men will likely result in significant harm to women and girls.A second is the diversity and inter-related nature of protection risks facing different members of the same community. For example, in Akobo, numerous respondents noted that a lack of livelihoods opportunities for displaced men and male youth and an inability to cultivate land due to insecurity led adolescent girls tended to travel long distances to collect firewood to sell, exposing themselves to heightened risk of sexual violence. The intersection of needs in communities should be carefully considered; what might at first appear to be a livelihoods opportunity for men could in fact have the positive outcome of reducing gender-based violence and empowering women. A final potential inequality from NRC's assessment has to do with freedom of movement; older persons and those most concerned with threats of physical abuse - often young men - were the most likely the least likely to access services. Thus, special care must be taken to reach diverse and often unexpectedly vulnerable groups in NRC programming.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

NRC will evaluate three pillars of meaningful access – ability to reach, receive, and benefit from services – during assessment, throughout implementation, and during monitoring and evaluation stages. Questionnaires utilized at individual, household, community level will ask about actual and perceived barriers to access, and project teams will make all possible adjustments to intervention modalities to mitigate challenges as the arise. Especially in static or recurrent mobile locations, NRC will seek to verify access concerns with service providers and maintain and up-to-date, quality-based service maps.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

NRC prioritises the safety of beneficiaries in all activities. Thus, all assessment and monitoring tools take questions of community and individual safety as mandatory. Likewise, all M&E tools will ask about improvements in safety generally, and related to the intervention. Beneficiaries will be asked, before, during, and after an intervention what steps would contribute to feelings of safety, and if they felt that NRC was failing to account for safety in meaningful ways. Beyond perceptions of safety, protection monitors will be asked to report on specific instances of rights violations, both related to NRC interventions and those otherwise occurring in targeted communities. NRC will also include an internal incident reporting mechanism for staff to monitor our own observations regarding beneficiary safety.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

As with issues of safety, NRC prioritises the dignity of beneficiaries in all activities. Thus, all assessment and M&E tools take questions of community and individual dignity as mandatory. Beneficiaries will be asked, before, during, and after an intervention what steps would contribute to feelings dignity and self-worth, and if they felt that NRC was undercutting feelings of dignity in meaningful ways.
