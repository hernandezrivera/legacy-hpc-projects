Project Summary

QC will conduct an identification/ assessment of existing water sources through a systematic assessment/ mapping, including the functionality status, major issues (if not functional) and estimated number of populations benefitting from the water source in the targeted areas. Based on the results of the assessment, ,the project will provide safe water fitted by submersible pumps, for communities that do not have access to water stations. QC will choose to repair/reconstruct water sources over constructing new water facilities.
In order to monitor the effectiveness of this result, reports will be maintained by assistant engineers, tracking the number of IDPs/host communities who have access to safe drinking water, and the percentage of beneficiaries who have access to functional water sources. In addition to this, water quality tests will be conducted and the number of main stations and sub stations which are functional will also be monitored.
Fuel will be required for running the generators, which operate the pumps, powering the movement of water to cities, from the water stations. Engine oil, oil and diesel filters are necessary for the ongoing maintenance of these generators. The electrical panelboards are the electrical circuitry running the water station, which control the operation of the generators.
Water will be treated with chlorine, to ensure the safe consumption by beneficiaries. Additionally, dosing chlorine pumps provide the correct amount of chlorine treatment, just outside of the water well, in order to ensure that water is appropriate for human consumption. Water testing will be completed on a daily, weekly and monthly basis, in order to ensure water quality. Water samples will be taken at random locations throughout the entire water network and tested for Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) and microbiological presence, ensuring that the water is pathogen free.
QC will meet with the local council and local water authority to explain how the repair and rehabilitation of the systems will be conducted, and organize trainings for them in order to continue the water pumping after the project is completed through the collection system, which secures the sustainability of the service.
For those HHs that aren’t able to connect to the city water network, metal tanks and safe and clean water tankers will be distributed to them.
The sewage systems in the most urban areas/district capitals in northern Syria, discharge to a network of sewage ponds that provide secondary treatment for sewage. In assessments carried out by QC’s team, it was found that some of the sewage systems are provided with pumps and power supply for pumping the wastewater, through the piped network to the ponds. Some of these ponds are situated alongside an existing natural water system. As long as households have access to a sufficient and reliable water supply as well as support to the pumping system, wastewater will move through the network consistently. However, there are blockages and leakages in the sewage system, as with any network. Thus, QC will support the target communities with resources to repair blockages and leaks, so the system is in good working order.
The project team will conduct hygiene promotion sessions through a hygiene promotion team to promote diseases prevention mechanisms, focusing on the most vulnerable groups, such as children < 5 years, lactating mothers and pregnant women, the elderly and households with critically ill family members, and then hygiene kits will be distributed to the targeted groups.

Needs

High levels of violence negatively impacted the existing WASH infrastructure in Northern Syria. Damage to WASH infrastructure was particularly severe in Aleppo and Idlib governorates. According to WASH HNO 2019 and WASH Cluster’s data in Syria, 15,476,721 people are in need, 6,183,921 of them are in acute need. The breakdown of services and infrastructure disrupted the consistent provision of: clean and safe drinking water with adequate quality and quantity; sewage disposal and treatment services for communities. hospitals, health centers, schools and camps and access to basic family hygiene materials. Irregular sources of power (electricity/fuel) for generators to operate water systems is a major constraint for water supply, as is damage to water infrastructure and lack of spare parts, technical expertise and other resources for much needed repairs and maintenance.Limited maintenance resulted in sewerage spill over into the living environment and contamination of water supplies in certain areas since water pumping was erratic and the pressure in water mains was low. Furthermore, the operation of waste water facilities broke down, causing the subsequent dumping of raw sewage into bodies of fresh water, increasing the risk of surface water contamination. In addition, limited access to essential hygiene products – such as soap, sanitary towels, and diapers – combined with strained economic capacity inhibited good regular practices amongst the affected population, where conditions exacerbate the risk of diarrhea and other communicable diseases.The assessment indicated that women and men need to have equal access to the water points, toilets and bathing facilities and the WASH facilities are to be safe, especially for girls and women. The targeted beneficiaries of the project are men, women, boys and girls of all ages.

Implementation of activities

QC will directly implement project through qualified staff as the following activities: -Rehabilitation of major water networks, such as water stations and boreholes, and the construction of new water systems for areas without access to established water facilities in communities, camps, hospitals, health centers and schools.- Provision spare part contingency stocks, such as fuel stocks and filters, to local water authorities- Construction/Rehabilitation of the sewage networks for communities, camps, hospitals, health centers and schools. Capacity building to local council and water authority for operations and maintenance of water system and cost recovery system.- Hygiene promotion campaigns, especially for pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls, including the distribution of Hygiene kits.-Distribution of water tanks to the IDPs in camps and communities - Distribution of hygiene kits to the IDPs in camps and communities- Distribution of water tankers to the IDPs in camps and communities Organize single sex focus group discussions for women, girls, boys and men to choose the location and design.

Protection Risk Analysis

1- Risk of theft of NFIs and chemicals such as chlorine/Sodium Hypochlorite which may be at tractive to armed groups inside SyriaAssumption: The local councils secures that all the items will be under their control Mitigation: Agencies provide feedback and accountability; Work closely with all partners on the ground; Promote distribution stock between multiple warehouses in the target regions (for large quantities of stock) 2- Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities. Assumption: The local councils secures that all the items will be under their control Mitigation: -MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities.- Promote cost recovery systems for longer term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership. 3- Improper storage of hazardous supplies (chlorine and other) materials) - Possibility of explosion, human casualty and asset destruction. Assumption: The chlorine is water use and stored in safe warehouses.Mitigation: Ensure proper storage of supplies with clear provision of care and safety consideration. Make sure that clear instruction for use is available for chlorine products primarily for household use. 4- Children below 18 years old conduct works on behalf of humanitarian organisation, its partners or contractors. MoUs signed with all suppliers not to hire any child in any activities Mitigation: Ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ labourers; add zero tolerance disclosure on child labour in the contracts; check and register civil documentation;

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Outbreak of conflict prevents access to project locationsAssumption: The project locations are safe and the conflicts are limitedMitigation: Suspend the activities until the conflict is over, in addition to prepare a contingency plan, which includes changing the locations if necessary. QC will train field staff to improve their security management on daily basis and improve their knowledge to assist people in risk. Risk: Arrival of new IDPs to the project locationsAssumption: The political situation is settled after the agreement between Russia and TurkeyMitigation: Advocacy through the WASH cluster for coordination and cooperation to cover the new needs, in addition to prepare an updated assessment an include the new IDPs in the targeted beneficiaries. Risk: Current controlling entity or local council no longer supports QC staff and activitiesAssumption: The local councils are selected by local people and the cooperation with the humanitarian agencies is one of their priorities.Mitigation: Continuous coordination with the local councils to explain them the projects positive targets Risk: Beneficiaries may sell some items from hygiene kits to secure cash.Assumption: The beneficiaries are selected through clear criteriaMitigation: An analysis will be carried out that will advise future procurement and/or selection of beneficiaries.
