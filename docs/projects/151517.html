Project Summary

This project aims at enhancing access to protection-related information and services for at-risk women, girls, men and boys, and aid workers in order to create a more secure environment for displaced populations in Somalia. To this end, ACTED will work together and collaboratively with the civil society, local and international protection and other sector partners to ensure delivery of timely and tailored assistance to vulnerable individuals.

Needs

Recent drought and floods, protracted conflict and limited governmental authority are some of the major causes of displacement in all parts of Somalia which put civilians and IDPs lives at risk. The year 2018 has seen a substantive increase of evictions: 204,951 people flee their homes mostly by force (OCHA Humanitarian snapshot, August 2018). IDPs and vulnerable populations (such as women, children and elderly) in particular are most at risk of suffering from protection violations. GBV incidents are continuously reported, amongst which 76% were perpetuated within IDP communities according to the Protection cluster 2018 mid-year review report. The year 2018 also saw 2,877 grave violations affecting 2,316 children. These violence, abuse and exploitation are on the rise in 2018 and need to be addressed as a major protection concern (Country Taskforce on Monitoring and Reporting, June 2018). Vulnerable populations such as women, children and disabled are the most at risk and this vulnerability prevents them from having equal access to humanitarian aid, enhancing this vulnerability especially in IDP sites. Therefore, there is a strong need for protection-mainstreamed humanitarian interventions to improve information and access to tailored protection services for vulnerable populations and also among the aid workers community to enhance safe, dignified and more efficient humanitarian assistance. The dissemination of protection referral pathways and improved coordination between protection partners and the civil society are crucial to deliver the humanitarian aid in a safer environment.

Activities

Protection service mapping: In collaboration with protection partners, conduct a protection assessment to identify individuals at-risk, people with special needs and vulnerabilities, issues of access and control in order to improve service delivery and coordination of protection partners. The mapping will also support the information on protection services available.Establishment and training of youth and women committees: Conduct protection trainings and engage them in the design of protection services to improve their access to information and protection services.Child-friendly spaces and recreation activities to provide children with a safe environment and to engage care givers in livelihood activities through the DoNoHarm approach.PSEA trainings for staff, the community, camp committees and gatekeepers to improve knowledge on rights and increase their intervention capacity toward protection violations through a rights-based approach.Cash for Protection: With the motive of providing basic needs and livelihoods to GBV survivors in order to address some of the causes of GBV.Provision of in-kind protection kits: Including dignity kits to complement NFI kits in WASH, Shelter interventions.Supporting the strengthening of GBV referral pathways, and disseminating existing GBV pathways/reporting mechanisms to beneficiaries and partners in intervention areas.Collaborate with local partners and civil societies to support the revision and adoption of national, local, customary laws and policies that promote the rights of women, girls and other at-risk groups.Infrastructure adaption to the needs of people with physical impairments: which include but not limited to construction of trails, handle bars, ramps.Conduct trainings to affected populations, teachers, camp leaders, local leaders on their rights and where/how to access protection services and produce associated material in protection in forms of pamphlets, on radio, pictograms, on community boards etc.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

ACTED intervenes in areas where IDPs live within the host community or adjacent to neighborhood of host communities and targets both groups with humanitarian interventions. Through a rights-based approach, ACTED works with various stakeholders, contributing actively to durable solutions platforms such as REDSS to integrate IDPs with host communities where possible through a DoNoHarm approach. In addition, ACTED seeks to ensure sustainability of its interventions through:- Constitution of youth and women committees to empower them;- Collaborating closely with local authorities, civil society and local partners throughout the project cycle;- Establishment of an efficient and effective Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM);- Developing the community awareness as well as the aid workers knowledge and capacity to respond to protection concerns;- Through Community Participation approaches, ACTED seeks to encourage community ownership and management of projects/ assets created;- By linking/ graduating at- risk individuals to long term and resilience initiatives.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: Assistance and services do not reach the most vulnerable categories Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measures: Project aims to target IDPs settlements and host communities. ACTED strictly applies the 4 core humanitarian principles and DoNoHarm approach in all its interventions. Through regular physical presence in project areas and service delivery monitoring activities, ACTED will ensure that the principle of impartiality is respected by partners, and that beneficiaries are prioritized only on needs basis. Inclusive Community Governance structures representative of the all social groups within the affected population will help in identification of most vulnerable and most in need HHs. ACTED will make sure to intentionally involve all social groups within the affected population in identifying and responding to protection needs. Community Governance structures and communities will be empowered through capacity building activities and dissemination of information on services available as well as their rights. An integrated and multi-sector CRM will be established in the deliberate effort to give a chance to every individual to present complaints and receive feedback, including on beneficiaries’ selections and verification processes. ACTED will also ensure that affected populations, health workers, camp leaders know their rights and where/how to access protection services. Throughout the project, ACTED will communicate information through various means to ensure communication reaches the broader community and consider literacy levels.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk: Community governance structures are not representative in terms of age and gender, and cultural friction experienced in challenging negative gender normsLikelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measures: ACTED will map existing community structures in place and if community governance structures in place are not inclusive, they will be challenged in a diplomatic way in order to equally represent different social groups. Particular attention during training and capacity building will be given to empower women and youth in the community, including the possible implementation of specific skill-training activities for them by establishing participatory youth and women committees and engage them in the design of protection services. All social groups, including men, women, boys and girls will be included in trainings that aim to challenge existing detrimental cultural norms. The project will focus on establishing inclusive safety audits, encouraging active participation of women as well as mitigating some root causes of GBV risks such as unequal economic power . By considering the gender balance of protection staff, ACTED will ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point. ACTED will also ensure that all staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct, which includes a child protection policy.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Risk: Project operations are hampered by volatile security environment Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measures: ACTED closely monitors the security dynamics liaising with the local communities including traditional elders, local authorities and regional state government representatives. Over time, ACTED has developed a low profile access strategy with solid security measures. A participatory approach is used to update security risk analysis and data collected is triangulated with other sources to provide regular and comprehensive security briefings to staff. Working closely with targeted communities and local partners ACTED encourages a bottom-up design of projects ensuring community buy-in. ACTED will also identify the power dynamics in the areas of intervention in order to understand who has access to services, who manages the facilities and who is vulnerable and marginalized. This information will be used to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. Take this into consideration when determining locations for interventions.
