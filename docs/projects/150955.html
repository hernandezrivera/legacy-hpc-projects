Project Summary

The proposed project is an upscaling of earlier initiatives of Caritas Vienna and Caritas Ukraine aimed at contributing towards the improvement of well-being and strengthening of resilience of local communities affected by the conflict in eastern Ukraine.
As in the previous phase of the project, assistance will be provided by specially trained locals who live in or close by the target areas, and remain in the community after the project ends.
The planned project outcome is that Children from the 16 target settlements in the buffer zone are more resilient and able to better cope with daily life activities. The target areas are 17 schools in 16 locations within the 20 km wide buffer zone.
The project intends to build up on the positive impact of the previous one and on continuous concentration on working with children and parents as active agents to directly improve the wellbeing of children.
In order to reach this outcome the following main activities are planned:
1) After school activities for at least 2000 children in spaces equipped friendly for children, including leisure group activities, developmental group activities and team building activities, art and craft activities, individual counseling and other activities that will be directed at the provision of a supportive environment, reinforcement of children’s skills and strengths, mobilization towards active participation and interaction with others, supporting of positive relationships with peers.
2) Intense work on parental engagement, establishing weekly meetings in 17 schools with regular participation of at least 340 parents/ caregivers. Project social pedagogues prioritize on topics like how to improve own stress resilience and stress resilience of their children.
3) In cooperation with Kyiv Mohyla Academy (NaUKMA) that already partnered with Caritas Ukraine (CUA) in the previous project phase, a pilot activity will be realized in at least 3 schools: NaUKMA will train Caritas field staff for on-the-job coaching of teachers in implementing the concept of Ukrainian New School Reform

Needs

In the early years of the conflict, psychosocial support (PSS) in areas near the contact lines was heavily focussed towards immediate, emergency response. This included the provision of psychological first aid by a range of local and international actors, as well as supporting individuals and communities to deal with the acute trauma caused by the first waves of violence and conflict they encountered at the outset of hostilities. At present, there is recognition of the need for more durable PSS solutions that bridge humanitarian and developmental activity. Responses to date, which have largely relied on mobile teams visiting schools and communities, are increasingly questioned for their effectiveness and sustainability. Specifically, UNICEF’s 2017 (compare source above) report highlights that such responses lack long-term, consistent support, opportunities for individualised support, and their inability to be effectively integrated with the instructional programme of the school. The recent project evaluation proofed needs assessments and interviews conducted with key stakeholders during the field visit suggest the critical need for transitioning PSS delivery out of the hands of external, itinerant experts; and instead, support local professionals to identify and properly address children’s stress/trauma, and to improve their understanding of where and how they can refer children with more acute needs for further assistance. Most local personnel in schools (particularly school principals and teachers) do not have sufficient training on identifying psychosocial problems in children and ways of accommodating or referring them onto others for help. This point is reiterated in UNICEF’s 2017 report. For this reason, a critical aspect of ensuring more continuous, local PSS to children in the buffer zones includes building the capacity of local personnel, including those already working with them in formal schooling settings.

Activities & outcomes

The planned project outcome is that boys and girls from the 16 target settlements in the buffer zone are more resilient and able to better cope with daily life activities. In order to reach this outcome the following main activities are planned:1) After school activities for at least 700 boys 1300 girls (70 % of the age 6-12 years, 30% of the age 13-18 years) in spaces equipped friendly for boys and girls, including leisure group activities, developmental group activities and team building activities, art and craft activities, individual counseling and other activities that will be directed at the provision of a supportive environment, reinforcement of boys and girls’s skills and strengths, mobilization towards active participation and interaction with others, supporting of positive relationships with peers. 2) Intense work on parental engagement, establishing weekly meetings in 17 schools with regular participation of at least 340 (240 women and 100 men) parents/ caregivers. Project social pedagogues prioritize on topics like how to improve own stress resilience and stress resilience of their children.3) In cooperation with Kyiv Mohyla Academy (NaUKMA) that already partnered with Caritas Ukraine (CUA) in the previous project phase, a pilot activity will be realized in at least 3 schools: NaUKMA will train Caritas field staff for on-the-job coaching of teachers in implementing the concept of Ukrainian New School Reform. AAP mechanisms that will be used by Caritas Ukraine during implementation of the project are as follows:Leadership/Governance – already confirmed partnership with the main state institution as lead national organization in charge of state school psychologists and use of officially supported methodology increase the possibility for further inclusion of such activities in the region, supported at the state level.Feedback and Complaints – Active mechanism of feedback and complaints is established in each location.Participation - Beneficiaries will be involved directly as survey respondents. Feedback from beneficiaries (complaints, suggestions, etc.) will be taken into account during routine planning of the project activities and future projects of Caritas Ukraine.Design, Monitoring and Evaluation - Monitoring and evaluation will be an essential part of the proposed project as there will be a necessity to regularly check the effectiveness of the interventions, modifying (if necessary) the kinds of activities and monitor changes in behavioral patterns of both children, parents/caregivers and service providers. management of other projects of Caritas Ukraine) usage together with assessment results for planning of further activity.Working with Partners and Other Stakeholders On project level: regular coordination meetings (virtually and at locations) to streamline working approaches and exchange on project learningsOn national level: coordination with projects that work on child protection topics, PSS and youth topics.Grant learnings are shared externally on a regular base in the local and nationalCluster network. The project staff participate currently in the sectors of education, child protection and mental health/ PSS.
