Project Summary

Acute malnutrition remains a major public health emergency in Uror County, South Sudan. Malnutrition rates are high across the country; however they are particularly high in conflict affect states, particularly in Central Equatoria, Upper Nile and Jonglei, where chronic food insecurity contributes to malnutrition rates well above the emergency threshold of 15%. In addition, insecurity, displacement, poor access to services, lack of physical access, poor agricultural season, extremely poor diet (in terms of both quality and quantity), sub optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices, low coverage of water and sanitation facilities and deplorable hygiene practices are underlying the high levels of acute malnutrition. CRADA is targeted U5s and PLWs in Uror County as the area of operation to prevent and managements the malnourished children and PLWs. However, SAM targets 2671 and MAM 7212 for children U5s and MAM PLWs 3062. CRADA is now ready to open up nutrition centres in Uror County in 2019 HRP project.

Needs

As per the latest IPC analysis in May 2018, hunger and malnutrition in South Sudan have now reached unprecedented levels compared with all previous periods. An estimated 6 million people, 50% of the population, are expected to be severely food insecure in June July 2018, compared to 5.5 million people, 45% of the population, in May 2018. This is the greatest number of people ever to experience severe food insecurity (IPC Phases 3, 4 and 5) in South Sudan.According to REACH report in April this year, it shows that food security has further deteriorated in Pibor and it is possible that more than 20 percent of households are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). In an assessment conducted by REACH in March/April, 24.3 percent of households reported a Household Hunger Scale of six, indicative of Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and a significant increase from already severe hunger reported in late 2017. In October 2017, the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM (WHZ)) was 26.8 percent (22.8-31.2), somewhat below the Famine (IPC Phase 5) threshold of 30 percent. A SMART survey, which will collect representative nutrition and mortality information, is likely to be conducted. Urgent humanitarian assistance is needed in this county to save lives.In February 2017, Action against Hunger conducted a SMART survey in Fangak, Jonglei, highlighting GAM prevalence was 15.8% (13.0–19.0 95% CI) and SAM 2.9% (1.9–4.5 95% CI). In June 2017, Action against Hunger conducted a SMART survey in Maiwut, Upper Nil, and found a prevalence of GAM among children of 22.8% (18.8 27.4 95% C.I), SAM prevalence of2.8% (1.4 5.8 95% C.I). Acute malnutrition remains a major public health emergency in South Sudan. Malnutrition rates are high across the country; however they are particularly high in conflict affect states, particularly in Jonglei, in which Uror County experiences more Government and Oppositions offensive where chronic food insecurity contributes to malnutrition rates well above the emergency threshold of 15%. In addition, insecurity, displacement, poor access to services, lack of physical access, poor agricultural season, extremely poor diet (in terms of both quality and quantity), sub optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices, low coverage of water and sanitation facilities and deplorable hygiene practices are underlying the high levels of acute malnutrition. CRADA is targeted U5s and PLWs in Uror County as the areas of operations to prevent and managements the malnourished children and PLWs.Based on this, CRADA proposes to continue the delivery of nutrition services in the targeted areas during 2019 for the prevention and treatment of malnutrition in compliance with CMAM existing national guidelines. Prevention activities will include regular community based MUAC screening, case identification and referrals of malnourished children and PLW. Treatment for malnourished children and PLW will be provided at nutrition sites, with malnourished children with medical complications being referred to County hospital’s Stabilization Centers. Sensitization sessions will be delivered to caregivers of malnourished children as well as PLWs, while targeted activities will focus on empowering women within the community as lead mothers able to engage vulnerable and at risk women in Mother to Mother Support Groups. Nutrition situation monitoring will be performed through standardized nutrition surveys methodologies, such as SMART and SQUEAC. CRADA South Sudan will respond through an integrated WASH in nutrition approach to break the “diarrhea malnutrition” vicious circle, improving the effectiveness of the treatment of malnutrition and removing one of the main causes of malnutrition in South Sudan.
