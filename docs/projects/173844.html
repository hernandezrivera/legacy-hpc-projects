Project Summary

According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute Malnutrition analysis 2020 (IPC AMN), the nutrition situation in Borno and Yobe State is classified as Phase 3 (Serious) and Adamawa at Phase 2 (Alert) level, with Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels of 13.6% in Yobe, 10.7% in Borno and 7.5% in Adamawa. The nutritional situation is expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying causes including increased displacement, sub-optimal childcare practices and feeding environments, food insecurity and the socio-economic impact of COVId-19.
To overcome this challenge of malnutrition, UNICEF nutrition interventions will aim at reducing malnutrition through the provision the lifesaving treatment services for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) whilst strengthening malnutrition prevention measures amongst children aged below 5 years, adolescent girls and boys, pregnant and lactating women. Specifically, this will include;
 Direct interventions for treatment: Procuring, managing and prepositioning nutrition supplies (Ready to Use Therapeutic Food, essential routine medicines, anthropometric equipment and data tools) for effective management of SAM. A total of 295,288 cartons of RUTF will be procured to cover the sector target of 295,288 children aged 6-59 months who are envisaged to suffer from SAM in 2021. Timely detection, referral and treatment of children with SAM in Borno (116,257), Adamawa (60,209) and Yobe (118,822). This is 100% of overall sector target for SAM treatment as UNICEF will provide all the required RUTF needs for the collective nutrition response. UNICEF will provide Mid Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) tapes to all pregnant and lactating women to necessitate scale up of family MUAC approach.
 Direct preventive interventions: Promoting exclusive breast feeding among children aged below 6 months , improving the consumption of quality diets for children, adolescents and women of reproductive age, iron folic acid supplementation for adolescents and women of reproductive age group, Vitamin A supplementation and deworming among children and adolescents.
 Indirect promotional interventions: Promoting uptake of optimum maternal, child feeding and care practices among 361,470 Borno (153,213), Adamawa (106,962) and Yobe (101,295). pregnant and lactating women in the BAY states. This represents 60% of sector targets. This will be done through community-based support groups as well as facility-based approaches.
 Strengthening nutrition governance; integration of nutrition information within the health information system, supply integration with health as well as technical capacities of government, partners and community to prevent, identify and treat malnutrition from a life-cycle approach.
 Strengthening Nutrition Surveillance to determine the evolving nutrition situation that will further inform the response implementation strategy and location prioritization.
 Coordination and information management of the collective the nutrition response in BAY States.
UNICEF has planned to mobilize and generate $39.8 million funds to accomplish the planned activities for children and women in North east Nigeria.

1 - Needs

The decade of prolonged armed conflict involving non-state armed groups (NSAG) in North-eastern Nigeria has resulted in large-scale migration, disruption of agricultural activities, deterioration of nutritional status among children and women, and devastation of an already fragile public infrastructure, including health, food security and water services. With the massive destruction of towns and villages and ongoing violent attacks by the insurgents, the region has experienced increased displacement, loss of livelihoods, food insecurity, and increased morbidity and mortality as a result of malnutrition, inadequate health services and poor sanitation especially in the most affected states – Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (“the BAY states”). An estimated 10.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance across the BAY states according to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview. This represents the highest level recorded since the beginning of the coordinated humanitarian response in 2015 as the number of people in need rose from 7.1 million people in 2019 to 7.9 million people at the beginning of 2020. The figure further increased to over 10 million with the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. As of November 2020, an estimated 1.2 million people are living in areas that are inaccessible to humanitarian intervention due to ongoing conflict and lack of negotiated humanitarian access. The Cadre Harmonise (CH) analysis released jointly by the Nigerian Government authorities and humanitarian agencies in November 2020 paints a bleak picture of the food security situation in Nigeria. According to the CH analysis, there is continued deterioration in the food security situation in the BAY states, with over 3.4 million people at risk of facing critical levels of food insecurity during the post-harvest period of October to December 2020. This represents an increase of about 16 percent compared to the same period in 2019. The deterioration is directly attributed to a decrease in cereal production in 2020 due to floods as well as the socio-economic impacts of Covid-19. In the event of no sustained humanitarian assistance or scale-up in the most affected areas, these numbers are expected to increase to about 5.1 million people during the next lean season period of June to August 2021, when farmers cultivate and await their harvest. This marks an increase of about 20 percent compared to the 4.3 million food insecure people reported in the June 2020 CH analysis, which was conducted at the peak of the COVID-19 pandemic. The recent 9th round of the EFSNS conducted in October 2020 by the National Bureau of Statistics in collaboration with UNICEF and other partners reported the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe at 10.0 percent, 6.2 percent and 12.3 percent, respectively. Prevalence of GAM was found to be higher in Yobe mainly due to inadequate coverage and availability of nutrition services and inadequate funding to scale up preventive and curative nutrition services. The SAM rates at state level were estimated as 0.6% in Adamawa, 0.9% in Borno and 2.1% in Yobe. The under-five mortality rate also varied widely within the state; from as low as 0.04 deaths/10,000 under-fives/day in Southern Adamawa to as high as 0.66 deaths/10,000 under-fives/day in Jere/MMC domain. It is important to note that results of the assessment are only representative of accessible areas. GAM and mortality rates in inaccessible areas are projected to be far much worse. In absolute terms, the current rates of malnutrition translate into a total annual caseload of 295,288 children with SAM -a condition associated with a 12-fold increased risk of death. It is estimated that approximately 60,000 of these cases will die if they are not reached with treatment whilst 14,700 (5%) will default treatment if efforts to scale up community mobilization are not strengthened.

2 - Activities

 Procuring, managing and prepositioning nutrition supplies: UNICEF is the major procurement agency for Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) used by the government health facilities and those managed by NGO partners. The nutrition sector has targeted a total of 295,288 SAM children to be treated in the North eastern states of Nigeria. To meet this target, UNICEF will support in the procurement of about 295,288 cartons of RUTF and service delivery for treatment of 125,394 (42%) of SAM cases in Government run health facilities. UNICEF will also procure essential routine medication for management of co-existing illness for children having SAM, anthropometric equipment, micro-nutrient supplements and relevant data tools. UNICEF will continue to provide formal and on-the job training to partners and health workers on supply chain management to enhance their capacities on forecasting of RUTF needs, requesting procedure, storage, reporting, record keeping and utilization.  Provision of integrated preventative and curative nutrition services: To break the inter-generational cycle of malnutrition, UNICEF will support the delivery of integrated and comprehensive package of preventive and curative nutrition services. This will be done using differential programming; based on malnutrition prevalence thresholds combined with prevailing contextual factors. Direct preventive interventions will include support and promotion of exclusive breast feeding among children aged below 6 months , improving the consumption of age appropriate, adequate and dietary diversified diets for children, adolescents and women of reproductive age and iron folic acid supplementation for adolescents and women of reproductive age group. Indirect promotional interventions will include Infant and Young Child Feeding and Care practices promotional activities such as cooking and feeding demonstrations and skilled counselling at community and facility levels. Direct interventions for treatment of malnutrition among children will include early identification and active case finding for timely referral for treatment for 295,288 children with SAM. UNICEF will also support the state to implement the bi-annual Maternal, Neo-natal and Child Health week as well as to commemorate the world breastfeeding week. Alongside this, UNICEF will strengthen the capacities of the State, implementing partners and communities on provision of quality integrated nutrition services at scale as well as on data management.  Nutrition Response Coordination and Information Management: UNICEF, through the vantage point of Nutrition Sector co-lead, will work closely with the state with Government counterparts and NGO nutrition partners to conduct joint assessment, joint planning and support delivery of a coordinated multisectoral response at scale. Through co-chairing the nutrition sector, UNICEF will provide technical support to ensure a harmonized nutrition response at scale in line with global and national normative guidance. UNICEF will support Government Counterparts and implementing partners to collective, analyse and submit quality and time routine nutrition programmatic data using sector/state agreed systems and structures.  Nutrition Surveillance: UNICEF, with already received funding from DFID will continue working with National Bureau of Statistic to conduct the semi-annual Nutrition Surveillance. The surveillance exercise will indicate the evolving nutrition situation to further inform the response implementation strategy and location prioritization. UNICEF collaborates with the sector partners and the government to detect and respond to emerging/deteriorating nutrition-related crises in North East Nigeria by supporting a surveillance system which will give more frequent information at lower levels such as Local Government Areas (LGAs) and wards. Community-based sentinel site surveillance will be set up and this will be main driver of this surveillance system.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

Malnutrition was widespread in the 3 States in North-east Nigeria even before the conflict. Protracted Insecurity since 2016 as a result of the insurgency has led to some areas being prioritized as being in the emergency phase because of heightened insecurity that has led to mass population displacement and access constraints. This is in addition to the long-term structural issues that were existing before the insurgency. UNICEF in conjunction with the Sector has developed HDN strategy where UNICEF (1) is the provider of last resort for the collective nutrition response in areas under the emergency phase (2) provides complementary services as per existing gaps in nutrition services implemented by other Civil Society Organizations-aimed at ensuring provision of comprehensive and integrated nutrition services; and (3) undertakes system strengthening. System strengthening entails the integration of nutrition services in existing government health system, advocacy for state governments’ allocation and release of funds for nutrition, community sensitization for consumption of locally available nutritious foods and leverage partnership to ensure availability and consumptions of safe, adequate of nutritious foods. UNICEF is currently supporting the full operationalization of the Yobe State Committee on Food and Nutrition (SCFN) whilst supporting the revitalization of the Borno State SCFN. The SCFN is a multi-sectorial Government led platform responsible for domestication of the National Policy on Food and Nutrition’ hence an effective strategy for linking both humanitarian and developmental actors.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection Mapping Protection concerns and needs of all vulnerable groups will be identified during sectorial needs assessment-through an inclusive and consultative process- where the protection risks and threats to individuals and communities will be identified. Additionally, the communities coping mechanisms will also assessed to mitigate adverse impacts on their safety. This is in line with Core Humanitarian Standard- Identifying the most vulnerable and encompasses the ‘Do no harm’ principle. Protection Risk Analysis Feedback from the protection mapping exercise will feed into the risk analysis process. This will guide UNICEF in analyzing and identifying potential high protection risks faced by the right holders. Protection Mainstreaming Plan UNICEF protection mainstreaming plan will involve developing a solid action plan that will address the protection risks and threats identified during the risk analysis phase. Embedded in the protection mainstreaming plan is a robust monitoring and evaluation system that will track progress of intended outcomes, outputs, indicators and activities to ensure protection risks and threats are addressed inclusively and fully. UNICEF, from a vantage point of co-leading the child protection sub-sector will ensure protection concerns for children are identified and addressed in line with the strategies outlined in UNICEF’s Core Commitment for Children for humanitarian action.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNICEF has set up U-report 24x7 system in North-eastern Nigeria to ensure community feedback is collected and analysed systematically. U-Report 24x7 is a free SMS-based platform for community participation designed to collect real-time data including reports and complaints from affected citizens and to communicate life-saving information with them. All nutrition implementing partners and Government counterparts have been trained to work with respective communities to fully utilize the U-report platform. This U-reporting system will be used to get feedback from beneficiaries, communities and other stakeholders on project implementation. U-Report is easy to use and requires only a subscription on any telephone network in Nigeria to receive messages and send feedback. The U-report will complement other formal which include LGA level coordination forums, complaint handling boxes in health facilities, Focus Group Discussions and informal beneficiary feedback systems through the community volunteer network, cognizant of the fact that the platform relies on availability of telephone network to receive and send SMS freely.
