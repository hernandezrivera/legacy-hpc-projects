Project Summary

UNHCR will provide protection and seek solutions for IDPs and IDP-returnees, while working with partners at inter-agency level for provision of additional support, as follows:
- IDP response will be characterized by enhanced protection monitoring (at community and household levels) and analysis, support to community-based protection networks and community reconciliation committees, tangible support to persons with specific needs (e.g. legal aid, civil documentation, or referral to psycho-social support, or cash-based interventions), youth programming, quick impact community support projects in IDP hosting and returnee receiving areas, and will contribute to durable solutions profiling and planning.
- Coordination leadership of the Protection Sector will be reinforced, through working with partners and authorities to bolster preparedness including early warning, as well as protection assessment, analysis and response capacities.
- The Centrality of Protection will be championed within the UN/HCT, including through UNHCR serving as secretariat for the HCT Protection Task Force (HCT PTF), which oversees the implementation of the HCT Protection Strategy. UNHCR will work with the HCT PTF, and any new protection coordination forums established under UNITAMS, on advocacy related to protection of civilians, peacebuilding and durable solutions, particularly during the UNAMID drawdown and transition to an integrated mission. UNHCR will also bolster its advocacy with GoS on ensuring that laws and policies conform with international standards, and will support implementation of GoS’ National Plan on Protection of Civilians, at national and state-levels. These advocacy efforts will be buttressed by analysis of protection needs, threats and trends.
- The UNHCR/UNDP co-led Durable Solutions Working Group will prioritize the collection of relevant data to inform the inter-agency and governmental drive toward durable solutions, complemented by monitoring of returns by UNHCR to ensure they adhere to minimum standards of voluntariness, safety and dignity.
- UNHCR will strengthen the integration of PSEA actions, and maintain its active contributions to the RC/HC’s PSEA Network, and actively contribute to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) approaches through co-leadership of the newly established AAP/Community Engagement WG under the ISCG.

1 - Needs

With the change of government in 2019, and particularly with the issuance of GoS’ National Plan on Protection of Civilians (NPPOC) in June 2020, a window of opportunity has opened for UNHCR and the UNHCR-led Protection Sector (PS) to undertake household and community-level protection monitoring, and evidence-based advocacy for the achievement of protection and solutions for IDPs and IDP-returnees. IDPs and IDP-returnees in Sudan – particularly PSNs – are faced with a range of challenges, including inability to meet their basic needs, access humanitarian/protection assistance or public services, and enjoy their rights and entitlements. According to the MSNA, civil documentation continues to be a pressing need among IDPs, particularly IDP youth. In addition, IOM/DTM Mobility Tracking data published in July 2020 indicates that 90% of IDP-returnees have returned to rural areas, where Civil Registries are not always present. There are also multiple barriers faced by those seeking to access justice, including lack of courts and police provision in many areas outside urban centres, and prohibitive court fees in the formal justice sector. Traditional justice mechanisms, weakened by conflict, operate alongside the formal justice sector. Moreover, inter-communal conflict continues to remain one of the principal drivers of protection needs in Sudan. In the first half of 2020, inter-communal violence was observed in all five Darfur states, as well as South, North & West Kordofan, and Kassala. At the same time, IDP and IDP-returnee youth in Sudan continue to be subject to recruitment into armed groups. As revealed during UNHCR’s participatory assessments, there is significant interest among crisis-affected IDPs and IDP-returnees to be agents in their own protection and solutions, particularly among women and youth. IDPs and IDP-returnees have also expressed a strong preference for community-based structures over traditional structures, and the formal justice system, to resolve conflicts. Therefore, it is imperative to empower communities on protection and dispute resolution, and thereby promote self-reliance. At the same time, decades of neglect in the target localities have left communities with little communal and life-saving service facilities, which in itself can become a trigger for conflict. Moreover, IDP hosting and IDP-returnee returnee receiving communities may develop negative sentiments towards IDPs and returnees with whom they share their scarce resources, if their priority needs are not met. In the short- and medium-term quick impact projects are required to deliver immediate tangible support for priorities identified by communities. In the longer term, the GoS and the UNCT - through the Durable Solutions Working Group (DSWG), co-led by UNHCR & UNDP - recognise the need for a comprehensive strategy to address the protracted displacement situation and work towards solutions. There is a need to advocate for the GoS to convene an inter-ministerial forum on durable solutions, with representation at the national and State-levels, and for the formation of area DSWGs in affected States. Tailored support is needed for those IDPs contemplating return to their areas of origin, those facilitated to return by the GoS, and those who have already returned but have not achieved durable solutions. UNHCR will need to continue to lead the PS, which includes GBV, CP and Mine Action Sub-Sectors, and coordinate the needs assessment and strategic response planning of NGOs and UN agencies, including provision of capacity building support to partners and to relevant authorities. UNHCR also will need to continue advocate for the Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action as secretariat for the HCT Protection Task Force (HCT PTF), which oversees the implementation of the HCT Protection Strategy.

2 - Activities/Outputs

UNHCR IDP engagement in Sudan will span the full spectrum of displacement scenarios, including new displacement, protracted displacement and durable solutions. Regarding those newly displaced, UNHCR – through the Protection Sector – will participate in all OCHA-led Multi-Sector Rapid Assessments following new crises and displacement, and responds to identified needs. With respect to IDPs in protracted displacement, the following protection, assistance and durable solutions interventions will be prioritized: - Household-level protection monitoring to identify PSNs among IDPs and IDP-returnees, and provision of specialized protection/humanitarian support to identified PSNs;- Legal assistance (i.e. awareness raising, counselling/advice, representation, alternative dispute resolution) on family law (marriage/divorce, child custody), civil law (complex documentation and registration cases, HLP), and criminal law matters (arbitrary detention, SGBV survivor representation);- Regular and late birth registration, and issuance of birth certificates for IDP and IDP-returnee children;- Community-level protection monitoring missions, analytical reports and evidence-based advocacy interventions related to security from violence and exploitation targeting Protection of Civilians hotspots;- Advocacy to ensure that law and policy is consistent with international standards relating to internal displacement;- Establishment of and support to community-based protection networks (CBPNs) and community reconciliation committees (CRCs) in IDP camps/sites and return areas;- Mitigation of risks of recruitment of youth into armed groups through establishment of youth empowerment networks and adolescent community networks (10-17 years), and micro-projects for youth;- Community Support Projects (CSPs) – i.e. short- and medium-term quick impact projects are required to deliver immediate tangible support for priorities identified by communities - in IDP hosting and IDP-returnee receiving areas;- Multi-sectoral village profiling and intentions surveys to inform the development of durable solutions locality action plans in IDP hosting and IDP-returnee receiving areas. This will include advocacy on inclusion of local integration options, through regularization of IDP camp/sites, as part of urbanization plans;- Engagement on voluntary return of IDPs - i.e. facilitation of go-and-see visits, advocacy with GoS for various forms of support during facilitated return movements, return monitoring in areas of origin, preparation of reports on conditions in such areas, provision of protection and Shelter/NFI assistance, and advocacy with GoS on improvement of conditions in return areas. UNHCR will also continue to coordinate the Protection Sector – at national and State levels - with a particular focus on needs identification, delivery of effective and timely assistance, evidence-based advocacy, and transition toward solutions, through UN agency and I/NGO partners, and in coordination with authorities.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Rights and community-based approaches will be at the core of the project activities. Persons of concern will be engaged during the entire humanitarian program cycle through participatory assessment and focus groups. In this process, primary consideration will be given to the principle of accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their protection concerns. Different segments of affected people will be meaningfully engaged in all decisions and actions that have a direct impact on their well-being.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The Operation will build on its previous year’s work in enhancing a robust PSEA response across the operation. This includes strengthening the culture of accountability across the operation through the integration of PSEA actions within protection functions and broader Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) strategy. This will be achieved through regular PSEA training for UNHCR staff, partners staff, as well as contractors; regular SEA risk assessment and development and implementation of annual PSEA action plans for field offices will be implemented; collaboration with operational partners to establish inter-agency and community-based complaint mechanisms accessible to refugees in all locations with an active refugee response. The Operation will continue to lead Protection Sector activities and will maintain its active participation in the inter-agency PSEA fora, including the RC/HC’s PSEA Network. UNHCR will ensure that activities will not expose most vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up and UNHCR will ensure the inclusion of the most vulnerable groups activities related to target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. Humanitarian actors will be supported to mainstream protection throughout all activities and to coordinate specialized protection services for affected populations.
