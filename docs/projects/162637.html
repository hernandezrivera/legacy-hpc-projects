Project Summary

The objective of this project is to provide humanitarian assistance to refugees from South Sudan and the Central African Republic under all three HRP 2020 Strategic Objectives:
O1: Provide timely multi-sectoral life-saving assistance to crisis affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity;
O2: Contribute to building resilience to recurrent shocks and improving vulnerable people’s access to basic services;
O3: Enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond to protection needs through quality and principled humanitarian action. Lifesaving assistance for new arrivals/vulnerable groups is prioritized to respond to urgent humanitarian and protection needs of anticipated new arrivals.
IOM will provide Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), improved Emergency Shelters (ES), alongside Education support. WASH activities will increase access to safe drinking water, adequate quality sanitation facilities and improve personal and public hygiene practices. ES will support beneﬁciaries’ basic housing needs and offer protection from the harsh elements and access to education for children will be enhanced through the rehabilitation of classrooms and training of teachers. Finally, both refugees and hosting communities will have improved access to livelihood opportunities in an effort to both reduce protection risks (for example family splitting for employment opportunities or negative coping strategies) and improve purchasing power. IOM's project will focus on targeting both refugees and hosting communities in an effort to mitigate the risks of tensions over scarce resources, increase the capacity of host support hosting communities to absorb population inﬂuxes, and promote social cohesion.

1 - Needs

Since the outbreak of the conﬂict in South Sudan in December 2013; a total of 461,250 South Sudanese refugees have ﬂed to Sudan with 14,196 newly arrived refugees in 2019. South Sudanese refugees have crossed the border at different sites into Abyei, South and West Kordofan, White Nile and East and South Darfur due to continued instability in South Sudan. While there is a major humanitarian presence in White Nile, East and South Darfur signiﬁcant gaps remain in South and West Kordofan, where humanitarian interventions have been more limited compared to Darfur. At the same time, localities like Um Dafoug in South Darfur, have recently witnessed the inﬂux of over 6,800 refugees from the Central African Republic, and access to basic services and shelter is very limited. Multi-sector needs of the population are high both in and out of camps. Many refugees are living among host-communities that already have very limited access to basic services; for example in Keliak, West Kordofan, approximately 4 Liters of water are available per person per day (l/p/d), which is below the Sphere standard of 15 l/p/d and the inﬂux of South Sudanese continues to create tensions between the community and the refugees. A lack of proper water management, sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices have also been observed among these communities’ waste management is limited, and open defecation is widely practiced. Therefore, there is a great need to support these communities with construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of water structures and safe sanitation facilities; as well as facilitating hygiene awareness campaigns and waste management campaigns. The health and nutritional status of South Sudanese and CAR refugees entering Sudan is also dire as they were already facing serve health issues and acute malnutrition before they arrived in the country. The support provided will include the rehabilitation of education facilities to ensure access to education for young children and youth; teachers will be trained in coordination with the Ministry of Education to ensure that refugees' needs are met. Livelihood opportunities for both refugees and host communities will also be improved to increase foodsecurity and purchasing power. IOM's refugee response will focus on providing support to both refugees (46%) and host communities (54%) to promote social cohesion and decrease the likelihood of tensions or conﬂict between the two communities.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will have a neutral impact on the environment with mitigation and enhancement - IOM will employ environmentally friendly solutions for the implementation of the activities, for example the use of solar panels for the rehabilitation or construction of new water sources. New water sources will only be constructed after a geophysical study is conducted and ground water levels are monitored. IOM will also work closely with the WASH Sector and the targeted communities to ensure that Integrated Water Resource Management Systems (IWRM) are established. Hygiene and garbage collection campaigns will also be designed in a way to encourage land preservation through proper / responsible garbage disposal methods and systems. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development and population growth have made water resources extremely important to Sudan’s economic development and social cohesion; IOM will focus on ﬁnding alternatives from fuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, scalable, environmentally sustainable and climate smart. Experience has shown that solar powered systems reduce running costs by two thirds unlike motorized pumps, which are also noisy, polluting (emitting greenhouse gases which contribute to climate change) and are highly susceptible to ﬂuctuating fuel prices and availability. Solar powered water sources will be prioritized where possible. Furthermore, improved livelihood opportunities to mitigate the negative coping mechanisms of vulnerable communities (ex. cutting down of trees for ﬁrewood). Through agricultural training sessions, IOM will also aim to improve the knowledge of local farmers on how to absorb and respond to climatic shocks.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project activities will be designed in a way to support women, children and people with disabilities. Special attention will be paid to women, lactating mothers or pregnant women. The majority of the displaced returnee women have little to no assets/household goods and have no means to engage in income generating activities as they did before the displacement, therefore, there is a need for star-up kits and training especially designed to cater the speciﬁc needs of women. The project will aim to empower women through speciﬁcally designed interventions informed by a combination of targeted assessments, by enhancing their knowledge and skills on asset-based contribution and small businesses management. In addition, as described previously, women will be involved in all decision making processes when determining types of prioritized interventions. For ES, protection will cater to the needs of women, children and disabled persons. Locations for ES will be chosen through community consultations to decrease walking distances to key services to reduce associated risks of gender-based violence for women and children. IOM will also ensure to prioritize safety and security issues ensuring that shelters are built in areas where there is access to other basic services and possible land tenure / ownership issues are discussed between local authorities and among the community members before shelters are constructed. And Ensure that vulnerable women, children, persons with disabilities or other special needs are prioritized to receive transitional shelters based on needs assessments. For the WASH activities, consultative participatory approaches will also be employed to allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identiﬁcation of priority needs based on gender and vulnerability, especially for the distribution of the Menstrual Hygiene Management kits. This inclusive participatory approach assured the inclusion of ideas from vulnerable women and girls, and people with disabilities, who are often excluded from community decisions or under represented during decision making processes, especially with regard to selecting the location for the construction/ re- construction of the latrines and rehabilitation of water points with the use of the spare parts to ensure that their needs are not only addressed but protection risks are addressed and mitigated.
