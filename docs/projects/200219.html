Project Summary

[The existing water network is characterized by aging infrastructure which results in 48% loss of water due to pipe blockages, burst pipes, pressure imbalances, and defective equipment. Water Establishments (WE) no longer have the means to engage in emergency network maintenance and fix transmission pipes that disrupt supply to households. Furthermore, the water supply system operates through a network of generators that pump water from springs/wells/dams to the main reservoirs and then to the distribution reservoirs. The WEs depend on both state electricity and generators to power their pumping stations. The electricity and fuel shortages have meant that these two energy sources are limited. Therefore, in order to meet the demand of the local population, the WEs have resorted to using water trucking, an expensive, inefficient and unsustainable way to meet the demand. The availability of water is seen as crucial to avoid unsanitary conditions and the spread of disease which can limit school attendance rates and indeed the health of those in the communities.
The Action will respond to the emergency WASH needs of communities through the solarization of water pumping station in the Metn area and emergency repair for water service.
Under Outcome 1. Water service delivery is improved to crisis-affected communities in the BML area, ACTED will solarize water pumping stations in the targeted locations. Two pumping station will be selected in BMLs for the installation of the Photovoltaic (PV) system in order to provide alternative energy solutions to ensure the pump use time is maximized (Activity 1.1) and installation of PV systems to two water pumping stations (Activity 1.2).
Under Outcome 2: Capacity Building in Water service provision and emergency repair for water service delivered to Water Establishments, ACTED will improve water service provision through the provision of emergency repair services for EBML (Activity 2.1.1) and provision of technical trainings to staff members (Activity 2.1.2). ACTED will implement the EL-SICR program, which is currently being implemented as part of an ACTED led WASH consortium for an EU-Madad Trust Funded project. The approach will mean that when emergency needs of EBML are identified in relation to the operation of its water systems, ACTED will second a Senior Hydraulic Engineer to EBML to work with their staff to find a solution to the issue. The secomnded staff member will ensure Quality Assurance and Quality Control (QA/QC) of the design itself and the implementation and provide technical oversight.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

[ACTED members will ensure to target the most vulnerable women and girls, boys and men, regardless of nationality, ethnic, religious affiliation or sexual orientation who struggle the most to meet their basic needs. Under this project, members will work together with the relevant stakeholders to ensure that protection and gender is mainstreamed by following existing capacities and procedures to ensure equal access to aid for crisis affected populations. ACTED will ensure a do-no-harm approach to targeted groups throughout the design implementation of activities and interventions. This will be done through training for staff, addressing biases and stereotypes, as well as through protecting beneficiaries’ data, maintaining confidentiality of beneficiaries’ information. Protection, Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) will be taken into consideration throughout the proposed Action. Assessments and data collection will be gender and age disaggregated

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

ACTED will promote the use of best practices in accountability and transparency of its interventions through formal beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM). The partners’ CRM includes a hotline (phone and WhatsApp), email, and communicating directly with MEAL or program staff. Each partners’ CRM modalities accommodate all, including women, the elderly and people with disabilities, by providing modalities to contact the partners in-person or by phone, and confidentially. The project team and the MEAL/Accountability team will make sure that the CRM hotline is widely communicated to beneficiaries and showcased in relevant visibility material. Dedicated staff will be responsible for receiving, logging, reporting, and following up on complaints, ensuring this process occurs in an effective manner. This will ensure a direct and accessible feedback mechanism for all beneficiaries and wider stakeholders to register feedback whilst underpinning accountability to affected population
