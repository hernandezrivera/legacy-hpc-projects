Project Summary

UMCOR propose to support 15,000 households (HHs) from Rwonyi, Jansuk and Mugo Payams of Yei River State of Central Equatoria State with sustainable food security and livelihoods and cash transfer interventions. Of the total beneficiaries, 60% will be host community members while the remaining 40% will be vulnerable IDPs and Returnees. Cognizant of the plight of vulnerable women headed HHs 60% of the target beneficiaries on the proposed project will be vulnerable women headed HHs. To undertake the proposed activities, UMCOR will require US$ 500,000. To promote increased HHs production, UMCOR will distribute seeds and tools, train beneficiaries on appropriate and environmental friendly agriculture practices and increase market linkages to enable beneficiaries sell surplus produce. UMCOR will train 200 lead farmers who will act as Trainer of Trainees (ToT) and work with the remaining beneficiaries (each lead farmer will work with 50 beneficiaries) to improve their agriculture techniques and processes thereby increasing production. The support will be provided in two tranches with targeted HHs provided with fast maturing vegetable seeds during the dry planting season and with maize, groundnut and cow pea seeds during the main planting season. To promote continuous production for subsequent seasons, UMCOR will train beneficiaries on seed saving and storage to maintain their potency.
To ensure timely access to support for beneficiaries, UMCOR in collaboration with the Ministry of Agriculture will conduct 3 days refresher training for ten (10) Community Based Agriculture Extension Workers (CBEAWs). The CBEAWs will provide advisory support to the beneficiaries on crops disease and pest management, appropriate agriculture practices to increase production and protect the environment and other technical extension service whenever needed. To facilitate the movement of the CBAEWs, UMCOR will provide monthly motivational incentive amounting to US$ 50 in local currency and procure and distribute ten (10) bicycles to the CBAEWs. The bicycles will enable them reach supported beneficiaries in a timely manner. To support beneficiaries meet their immediate needs and mitigate against the potential risk of consuming donated seeds due to lack of food, UMCOR will issue cash transfers to 500 most vulnerable HHs from Rwonyi , Jansuk and Mugo Payams. UMCOR will use a third party company to distribute the cash transfers. UMCOR team in Yei will accompany the paying team to ensure beneficiaries get agreed cash transfer amounts. UMCOR will facilitate movement of the paying teams while in the field.
Through the proposed ventures, UMCOR anticipate that beneficiaries’ HHs will be empowered to support and sustain their livelihoods thereby eliminating overdependence on donor aid. With increased production at HHs level, UMCOR projects decreased malnutrition levels especially for children under the age of 5. With surplus produce sent to local markets, the beneficiaries will diversify their livelihoods approaches by establishing small businesses but will also contribute towards lowering the cost of produce within local markets making it affordable for vulnerable community members within Yei River State.

Needs

The civil strife of 2013 and 2016 resulted in devastating impacts including death and displacement of South Sudanese across the country. While the war previously cantered in Upper Nile region, in the recent past, there has been escalation of hostilities across other parts of the country forcing many to desert their homes in search for safe havens. While able groups managed to cross to neighboring countries, thousands others sought refuge in IDP camps and have been dependent on humanitarian partners for survival. Prior to the civil strife, Yei was one of the few self-sustaining states with the capacity of producing food to support its populous and export surplus produce to other parts of South Sudan. Most (HHs) engaged in crop production and business ventures within the town thrived. However, the civil war has ravaged the location previously spared the violence, with massive displacement, looting, targeted killings as well as sexual assaults and mutilations. Civilians had been indiscriminately targeted for extra judicial killings, rapes, abductions, forced recruitment, arbitrary arrests, torture, beatings, harassment, intimidation, looting as well as destruction of property and livelihoods. With peace slowly returning to Yei, some displaced community members are cautiously returning to their homes. Unfortunately, due to the war most of the HHs lost items and their sources of livelihoods were distorted. This has left them vulnerable and highly dependent on well-wishers and humanitarian agencies to sustain their livelihoods. While the Integrated Food Security Classification Phase (IPC) for the period September 2018- May 2019 reported that no data was collected from Yei due to accessibility challenges, 32% of the Central Equatoria population (165,000 people) were classified under IPC phase 3 a clear indication of increased vulnerability levels across the state. Vastly affected are women headed HHs and children some of whom lost their parents during the war and are under the care of foster families struggling to provide for them. Following the gradual return of peace, markets re opened and have remained functional, however most high vulnerability among HHs makes it hard for them to afford cost of items in the markets. Rwonyi, Jansuk and Mugo Payams like other parts of Yei were adversely affected by the war and many residents left vulnerable. A visit to Rwonyi, Jansuk and Mugo Payams provided a gloomy picture of HHs in dire need of support to restore and sustain their livelihoods. Visual evidence and discussions with affected community members pointed to groups who lost everything during the war and were exposed to grave and torturous experience. Hopelessness and desperation was evident on the faces of the residents who were unable to comprehend what the future holds for them. While the communities have land and have indicated their willingness to farm to restore and sustain their livelihoods, they lack seeds and tools to undertake crop production. As a result, most of the affected HHs remain vulnerable due to the lack of sustainable food production.As a coping mechanism, vulnerable HHs are engaged in environment hazardous income generating activities including cutting and selling logs and grass to sustain their livelihoods. Even with the sales from these environment destructive ventures, affected HHs are still unable to meet their HHs demands and remain highly vulnerable. It is critical that immediate food security and livelihoods support is provided to the most vulnerable HHs to avert the risk of hunger and possibly death resulting from lack of sustainable food supply. It is equally essential that HHs are empowered to put in place sustainable coping mechanisms to mitigate against the possible risks of malnutrition through increased food production, education on malnutrition prevention and management to promote the well-being of children under the age of 5 years.

Strategic Objective 1

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Information on project processes, proposed activities, selection criteria and beneficiaries' entitlements will be provided during the initial project meeting, during beneficiary selection process, prior to commencement of training and at the time of distribution. Beneficiaries through the all inclusive beneficiary selection committee representatives and local authorities will also be informed on what extent project objectives were realized, challenges experienced and lessons learnt to improve quality of future programs. In the event that some beneficiaries are not conversant with English language, interpreters will be used to ensure messages reach a wider audience to ensure all understand and promote uniformity of information and proposed intervention approaches and interventions.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messaging on project activities, processes and entitlement will be done in languages that target beneficiaries understand. Cognizant to the fact that UMCOR will be dealing with a diverse community ( Returnees, host communities and IDPs), UMCOR will use a translator where necessary to ensure that the target beneficiaries understand information being passed through during training, beneficiary selection and distributions. Messaging will take into consideration cultural and religious beliefs to limit the risk of conflict and feeling of disrespect towards culture and religion among target beneficiaries.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

To mitigate against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) UMCOR will use a community led approach of working with an all inclusive beneficiary selection committee who will work voluntarily during the grant period. the selection committee will have inclusive representation across the board including youth, women, persons living with disability, local authorities, chiefs, IDPs, host community and returnees representatives. The committee with support from UMCOR will develop a selection criteria for beneficiary identification. The inclusive representation will limit instances of SEA unlike in a situation where the sole decision of beneficiary identification is left to local authorities or chiefs. UMCOR has tried this approach in Northern Bahr el Ghazal States (NBeG) and found it effective in advocating for beneficiary rights and reducing SEA risks. UMCOR staff and volunteers will also sign a SEA policy document which they will be expected to abide by while interacting with beneficiaries. Any violation of the SEA policy attracts severe consequences including immediate contract termination and in some instances possible jail term.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

During the proposal development period, UMCOR not only talked to local authorities and leaders but went to HHs where and discussed the needs of communities and possible interventions with beneficiaries. While visiting Rwonyi, Jansuk and Mugo Payams, UMCOR talked to 785 HHs members who indicated their challenges and proposed interventions to improve and sustain their livelihoods. The 785 community HHs represent 5% of proposed project target population.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

UMCOR will establish a complaint redress mechanism (GRM) where project beneficiaries will channel their dissatisfaction and recommendations on the project. Two channels will be established to manage complaints. The beneficiaries will either use their representatives at within the all inclusive beneficiary selection committee or go to UMCOR office in Yei to air their concerns. All received grievances and persons presenting them will be managed with utmost confidentiality to avoid exposing the aggrieved persons to possible retribution from accused persons/entities. Upon receipt, the grievances will be investigated by UMCOR in collaboration with the all inclusive beneficiary selection committee and local authorities involved on the project and an amicable solution ensuring upholding of the beneficiary right is derived. Should the accused person be among the investigating team, the person will be requested to relieve him/herself from the process until the matter is finalized. At all times, the aggrieved person will remain anonymous unless he/she is willing to appear in person during the investigation process.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The community through the all inclusive beneficiary selection committee will be involved during all project processes. The committee will work closely with UMCOR to monitor project processes, deliverable and appraise the final project outcomes against the original project objectives. The committee will also be actively involved in identifying lessons learnt, challenges experienced and provide recommendations on how to improve future programming.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Security in Yei is still fluid. UMCOR will put necessary steps to ensure the safety of beneficiaries and staff during the project processes. All possible risks, their occurrence and potential impacts on the project will be identified, evaluated and mitigation steps put in place to limit the extent of their impact on the project. To limit exposure, UMCOR will conduct cash distributions at HHs level. This will be done discreetly to limit visibility. UMCOR will also discuss and collaborate with local authorities to ensure security is provided at all times during distribution of project assets and trainings. Distributions will be conducted within safe environs and close to HHs to ensure women can be served near their HHs to enable them continue with their HHs chores and also to prevent beneficiaries from trekking for long distances to access services and exposure to risks including mugging and gender based violence during the long walks. Where necessary, persons living with disabilities, pregnant women, the elderly and the sick identified for support and are unable to access distribution points, UMCOR in collaboration with the beneficiary selection committee will allow such beneficiaries to nominate a person to receive the items on their behalf. The nominated person will need to provide proof of relation and be vetted by the members of the selection committee. Prior to leaving for project monitoring, UMCOR will contact community leaders from target locations and also work closely with security organs to determine security status of project sites before sending the project teams out.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project has taken into account the various barriers experienced by target population and put steps to overcome their effects. For example, UMCOR has a wide berth understanding of the project context security and will make informed decisions that will limit exposure to risks for beneficiaries and project team. UMCOR is also aware of challenges experienced by different beneficiary categories including women, the elderly and the sick and will come up with a mechanism that will ensure such beneficiaries are not impeded in their pursuit to access project support. Where needed, UMCOR will conduct HHs distributions or accept representatives of primary beneficiaries to receive their entitlements upon vetting by UMCOR in collaboration with the beneficiary selection committee and local authorities.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Through field reports, UMCOR staff will indicate the number of times they will have interacted with beneficiaries, in which location, undertaking what activities and report views and contributions from the project beneficiaries. The project will report the different groups met, supported, their ages (where possible) and gender.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Prior to conducting project activities, UMCOR will consult beneficiary selection committee members and local authorities to understand security situation in target locations. This will be done to limit exposure of beneficiaries and staff to insecure environments. UMCOR will also consult beneficiaries from time to time during the project period to understand their perception on their safety and gather appropriate information that will ensure safety of beneficiaries and staff prior to conducting project activities such as distribution of cash and other project assets. To limit exposure to risks for cash transfer beneficiaries, UMCOR will also conduct HHs cash transfers to limit visibility that could attract attention. UMCOR will report any cases of perceived insecurity brought to its attention by beneficiaries, selection committee and staff to local authorities for investigation and mitigation of possible escalation of insecurity.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

All UMCOR staff and stakeholders will be expected to treat all project beneficiaries and other stakeholders with utmost respect and in a dignified way. Monitoring will be done in collaboration with local authorities and representatives from the all inclusive beneficiary selection committee on behalf of supported communities. This will play a critical role in ensuring that processes , activities and produced messaging materials do not conflict with cultures and traditions of target beneficiaries.
