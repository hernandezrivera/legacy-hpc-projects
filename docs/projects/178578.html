Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to a safe and equitable access to education and to guarantee quality and sustainable formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children in Rural Aleppo. The project targets the sub-districts of Jebel Saman (severity scale 4), Daret Azza (sc. 5) and Atareb (sc. 5) of Aleppo governorate. This intervention has been planned and it will be implemented in partnership with the Ministry of Education, already guaranteeing AVSI the access to the area. The project plans to provide BTL campaigns, catch-up courses, remedial classes, children clubs, soft rehabilitation of schools, provision of schools’ furniture, quality trainings for teachers and life skills/psycho-social support to children in three selected and assessed schools in the area and to the neighboring community. 100% of sub-districts selected have an high severity scale and the area selected, even though part of different sub-districts, are very close one each other for a real community impact of the intervention. The cost for single beneficiary activities and included rehabilitation of schools is around 300 USD. 5% of beneficiaries are disables and specific action and specific budget lines are dedicated to them. AVSI Syria reached 27000 beneficiaries in 2020 in Aleppo, Rural Aleppo, Damascus and Rural Damascus with a budget of 3.7 million Euro.

4 - Needs

The project is based on an assessment conducted with MoE in Rural Aleppo from November 2002 to February 2021 which identified three communities in particular need of educational support, NFE activities, teachers-training and soft rehabilitation of schools according to specific BoQs. The prevalent needs emerged are: training in active learning, activities in life skills, PSS and remedial classes and support to exams, rehabilitation of schools, furniture, equipment of a resources room and an early child education room.The project is in line with the three educational objectives of HRP 2021 through multiple activities that address availability, access, quality and equity in education that want to serve children with special needs according to Bruxelles V response priorities. It focuses on priority target beneficiaries (especially out-of-school children and disable pupils) and it is developed in an area with an high number of very vulnerable families (HNAP Demographic Overview 2020) and high need of schools rehabilitation and in sub-districts with educational severity scale equal to 4 and 5 (HNO 2021) and in line with local priorities (improving school conditions, providing school supplies and support teachers according to HNO 2021).

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities planned are:- Multiple BTL campaigns in line with MoE and UNICEF national campaign with outreach activities promoted by volunteers, case manager and PSS officers, referrals and follow-up, targeting 500 children 5-15 years old in the communities (sub-districts) selected.- Soft rehabilitation of three schools selected according to specific BoQs with the additional installation of disable toilets, gender sensitive toilets, water tanks, solar panels - Remedial classes for children 5-15 yrs old for children at risk of dropping out- Catch-up classes for children 5-15 yrs old temporary out of school- PSS/Life skills activities for above targeted children NFE targets 440 children in the three areas/schools selected.- Provision of school supplies for children- Provision of school furniture- Provision of recreational materials- Provision of three specific trainings to teachers and educational personnel on Active Learning, CP/PSS/Life skills, Inclusion (gender, disability, etc.) with follow-ups and mentoring among the trained teachers/education personnel with the trainers/supervisors- Special inclusion pathway support to children with special needs (like disability) with inclusion officers and case manager- Creation in three schools of pre-primary rooms for education and kindergarten activities targeting at least 100 children 3-5 yrs old- Incentives to address educational barriers like CVA and transportation for teachers and students- Parents/teachers/community leaders meetings and focus groups.All activities consider Covid19 prevention measures and PPE and hygiene materials for schools. All activities will be monitored by technical AVSI staff on the field and by M&E officer with specific tools.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

AVSI analysis starts from previous experiences and lessons learned in close coordination with the partner, the UN, the cluster Education and the other actors involved in the same field/area. One main risk is to have inappropriate and unsafe spaces for learning, and to mitigate this risks one of the first objective will be to soft rehabilitate classrooms and schools to make learning spaces safe and appropriate; moreover violence and bullying could be a threat to the correct development of FE and NFE promoted and this is why specific trainings (PSS/CP, inclusion, etc.) are planned for teachers and educational personnel, psychosocial support is provided for children and continuous monitoring and feedbacks/complaints mechanism will be in place. Child Safeguarding polices and PSEA policies (see below) will be also in place. In particular, at the beginning of the project a specific Child Safeguarding Risk Assessment Template will be developed to mitigate all the risks of adults dealing with children. The project is designed as much as possible in a participatory way informing and involving (specific campaigns, regular meetings, focus groups, parents teachers meetings, qualitative M&E, etc.) partners, all the community , the teachers, the parents of children and the children themselves about activities performed and opportunities with clear criteria, entrance and exit strategy, with a clear measures for privacy and protection of data in order to avoid any risks connected to a negative attitude and collaboration towards the activities, delays and potential threats to beneficiaries. AVSI considers limiting the amount of identifiable data. Finally therefore the program promotes no do harm and no discrimination principles.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

There are clearly risks of PSEA which have been considered. AVSI is ensuring that all staff and volunteers involved in this program are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Child Safeguarding polices, and the internal reporting mechanisms for CP and SEA cases, through the mandatory annual trainings and the signature of the code of conduct and the SG bulletin. AVSI has worked on enhancing its internal PSEA minimum standards through participation in trainings, coaching provided by UNICEF in 2020. For this project AVSI will reinforce field monitoring, outreach activities, case managers' presence and work (including reporting and referrals), awareness among personnel, beneficiaries and local populations regarding PSEA, limitations of the risks through support to transportation, specific girls and disables' toilets rehabilitation, etc.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There are many risks to operate in Syria. The greatest risk derives from access to the project sites which can be limited by government provisions related to the Covid19 pandemic or related to security concerns. To mitigate these risks, AVSI promotes collaboration with local volunteers for its educational projects and the use of temporary educational centers. Furthermore, the excellent collaboration and coordination with the partner and the relations with the local authorities allow AVSI to further mitigate this risk. The risk of Covid19 infection both for the staff and the beneficiaries, which can prevent the implementation of activities in the education centers, is mitigated by the adoption of AVSI producers related to COVID pandemic including the use of PEE, social distancing, temperature’s measurement, early warning system, limited number in classes, etc.. Other risks concern the arrival of funds in Syria and the great inflation and volatility of the exchange rate. AVSI has already set up mitigation measures such as the change of the bank at HQ country level for sending funds to Syria, accelerated procurement and payment procedures and having increased its local contingency funds both in SYP and in USD to balance any fluctuations. The risks of fraud or corruption are mitigated by internal procedures (DIGE 8/2020). The project will be well coordinated with the MoE and local education authorities and actors, within the cluster Education both at central and local level, with UNICEF, and with OCHA as main educational actors in Syria. Population have been already consulted and it will be regular consulted through regular meetings and specific M&E initiative (like focus groups).
