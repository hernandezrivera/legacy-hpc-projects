Project Summary

The recent Food Security and drought plights in Hirshabelle, Banadir and South West State of Somalia call for timely life-saving Food security interventions to enable the displaced, returnees and vulnerable host communities' men, women boys and girls in the project targeted locations that are yet to recover from impacts of past devastating drought to meet their immediate food security needs. The food security needs will be addressed through conditional and non-conditional safety nets. The communities will also earn income in form of cash for work to sustain their families’ food requirements in order to mitigate already precarious food insecurity conditions and further restore their livelihoods and strengthen their resilience. With our suitably designed food security support programme, it is expected that the communities will be able to cope with the current and future shocks in the food security supply chain.
The project targets 2400 HH which is approximately 14,400 persons (6,480 females 5,760 male, 864 boys, and 1,296 girls) with increased access to food and improved livelihood standards; and strengthened the resilience of the most vulnerable and displaced 11,520 and 2,880 host communities in Banadir and Hirshabelle regions respectively, through appropriate livelihood support initiatives during the project time.
The project objectives are to improve targeted vulnerable households’ immediate access to food security and livelihoods through the distribution of conditional and unconditional cash vouchers. The project will target the most vulnerable affected people in three selected areas by providing Food security and livelihood assistance to become resilience against shocks.
The project will support urgent life-saving access to adequate food and nutrition and support livelihood recovery for 14,400 persons who include IDPs, host communities, pastoralists and farming communities in Somalia through conditional and unconditional transfers as well as the in-kind provision of assistance.
Knowing the fact that livestock is among the main livelihood assets for pastoralists in target locations, SYPD will do restocking to the affected vulnerable households. In tandem with animal restocking, this project will also protect and promote target beneficiaries' livestock assets through training of select community animal health workers on livestock treatment and vaccination of livestock against common diseases.
The project will aim to reduce and prevent acute malnutrition to the PLWDs and children under 5 who live in the targeted areas, by providing support mechanisms as well as reduce mortality rates from crisis-driven diseases and outbreaks.
The project will provide safe, dignified and meaningful access to life-saving assistance, provided through food voucher or conditional e.g. CFW program to the target beneficiaries including persons with disability with due account to age, gender, disability, and social status.
Restocking will target pastoral households who lost most of their livestock due to drought and displacement and those who demonstrate the capacity and desire for animal husbandry.

Project Activities

NEEDSThe food Security situation in Hirshabelle, Banadir and Southwest state Regions is still fragile owing to impacts of past drought that nearly culminated in famine. In early 2019, FEWS NET and FSNAU, released analyses detailing the poor start of the April-June 2019 Gu rainfall season and expected deterioration in food security conditions. The joint report shows that over 2.2 million people are expected to experience food shortages and are in need of humanitarian food assistance and interventions to prevent high levels of acute malnutrition. The average number of beneficiaries reached with food assistance in the first half of 2019 declined 47 percent compared to those reached from August to December 2018In north-central pastoral areas of Somalia, food security outcomes are driven by stagnated or declining herd sizes. The 2019 Gu rains started late and were significantly below average in most of Somalia, causing drought conditions to persist through mid-May. The short duration for rainfall, the low amount, and erratic distribution, as well as low river levels, led to significant reductions in area planted and yield of cereal crops. In the northwest, Gu rainfall was still below average. Dwindling market stocks from previous harvests and the poor 2019 Gu harvest led to sharp increases in sorghum prices in July, and the livestock-to-cereal and wage labor-to-cereal terms of trade exhibit a downward trend.The recent market survey report by FSNAU and FEWSNET expressed the highest inflation. Compared to June and July 2019, Consumer Price Index (CPI) changed mildly (2-8%) in August 2019. CPI increased moderately (5-16%) compared to a year ago due to increases in current cereal prices.The situations call for timely Food security intervention to enable the worst affected vulnerable and drought displaced communities earn some income in form of cash for work to sustain their family food needs in order to mitigate already deteriorated food insecurity conditions and further restore their livelihoods and strengthen their resilience to cope with future shocks. PROJECT ACTIVITIES1. Rehabilitation of 10 canals in Hirshabelle and South-West State Regions on cash for work scheme to benefit 250 HH vulnerable and displaced families.2. Provision of food vouchers of $60 each per month to 700 HH most vulnerable and displaced households and those with disability and health problems 3. Distribution of agricultural inputs (tools) to the 1,000 HH most vulnerable and displaced farming households in Hirshabelle and Southwest state Region 4. Strengthen agricultural 100HH farmers through capacity building training on Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), Famer association approaches and build their capacity for Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) to mitigate, adapt and increase resilience towards the impact of disasters and other related shocks.5. Provide agricultural package support to 350 HH identified vulnerable persons. The Agricultural package will include water pump machines, land preparations, distributions of fertilizers, hand pump sprayers, startup fuel and engine oil, installation machines, and capacity building training. The unconditional cash transfers, cash for work and voucher interventions have been primarily used in the program design in order to give some degree of autonomy to the beneficiaries to decide their most pressing needs and meet them through the cash and vouchers provided. The target locations have facilities/where the requisite the beneficiaries can easily purchase whatever they need. Because of the freedom, these intervention mechanisms have on the beneficiaries and from experience working in these locations, they are likely to be welcomed by the community.The UCT and cash for work will be administered through mobile money transfers after each beneficiary sign-off of attendance sheets. Vouchers will be administered through liaison with suppliers/vendors with close proximity to the target beneficiaries.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement1. SYPD will incorporate inclusion considerations into project design by prioritizing internally displaced persons, returnees and host communities in food security interventions including establishing and strengthening community committees that are balance in terms of age, gender and clan diversity. 2. During the project startup, SYPD will organize consultative meetings with all stakeholders including minority groups to avoid social exclusion errors.3. SYPD will carry out a needs assessment to identify the needs of displaced people and other vulnerable groups. The beneficiary registration will ensure needs analysis brings to the fore all dimensions of exclusion and village relief committee will participate in beneficiary selection and registration. 4. SYPD will establish inclusive community communication mechanism by forming beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms to ensure that all vulnerable groups have a voice in the project implementation. The project implementation will mainstream capacity building on all dimensions of exclusion.5. SYPD will carry out community consultations in order to identify obstacles which may hamper the full participation of IDPs, including from marginalized communities in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects. IDPs, host communities and refugee returnees will be included to prevent aggravation of tensions between IDPs and host communities.6. SYPD with the consultation of local authorities and elders will constitute a joint village committee and train on knowledge to oversight and manage the rehabilitated community infrastructures, the committee will solve the conflicts among the community. Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age1. SYPD will carry out needs analysis during the beneficiary selection and registration to ensure that all vulnerable people have access to assistance without discrimination. 2. SYPD will conduct constant community consultations meetings to identify barriers to the participation of women, girls, marginalized and minority groups and youth to ensure that risks regarding exclusion based on gender and age are identified and mitigated in a timely manner. 3. The project will include the formation of a feedback mechanism that involves women, men, and youth to gather information from beneficiaries’ access to assistance and provide timely feedback on the project achievement. 4. SYPD will carry out a deliberate inclusion of women, girls, youth and other vulnerable groups in the project design, implementation, and monitoring. Project committees will be all-inclusive of gender and age. 5. SYPD will train gender balanced committees to strengthen the capacity of vulnerable groups in participating in project implementation. 6. SYPD will manage assistance provision proximity to the dwelling places and in daylight to allow safe travel back home. Distributions will be managed through effective crowd control mechanisms and separate lines for men, women, and people with special needs. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence1. SYPD will ensure that beneficiaries are not exposed to armed conflicts and violence by the nature of assistance provided by undertaking community consultations at all project stages. 2. SYPD will conduct pre-intervention assessment and do evaluation to identify any risks and community dynamics that may lead to violence or armed conflict. 3. SYPD will ensure the participation of all on affected communities, inclusion of all stakeholders in food security projects and coordination with government authorities.4. SYPD will also undertake pre-context analysis to identity the existing government institutions like the presence of judicial frameworks available in the area of intervention.5. SYPD will avoid overcrowding during the distributions as it may arise protection concerns

Please provide further explanation

Building of resilience of food insecure people will be a key component for the project implementation to tackle the key risks and address the underlying causes of food insecurity and vulnerability for IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in terms adequate and sustainable food security and livelihoods. SYPD will work with vulnerable people including forcibly displaced people, both refugees and IDPs and host communities to support resilience building through capacity building in agropastoral practices, income generation and asset creation. Even in the context of protracted displacements, the environment in Hiraan, Lower Shabelle, Bay region, middle Shabelle still presents opportunities for durable solutions and resilience building. SYPD will work with the community leaders, relevant ministry at the local and national level, and local government authorities to find durable solutions including secure land tenure, capacity building and income generation activities, community assets, irrigation systems and hand over mechanisms for community based projects. During the project implementation, SYPD will mainstream capacity building initiatives for community water infrastructure management systems in order to create a strong management structure and eventual hand over mechanisms.
