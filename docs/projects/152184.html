Project Summary

The Action aims to strengthen prevention of and response to gender-based violence (GBV) for 7,832
displaced vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys and vulnerable host commmunity members through
coordinated, principled humanitarian support in Bama town and Ngala LGA in Borno State, Nigeria. As
well as the 7,832 IDPs and vulnerable host community members, the Action will reach 62 CARE and
other humanitarian staff, religious leaders, police, security forces, community leaders, and volunteers
engaged in addressing GBV in the areas of intervention.

Needs

CARE's Rapid Gender and GBV Assessment in Borno (January 2018) highlighted how conflict anddisplacement affect women, men, girls, and boys differently. Men have experienced their livelihoodactivities seriously disrupted, broken or made impossible due to insecurity; they are obliged to rely onhumanitarian assistance. At the same time a significant number of women have become single heads offamily due to family separation or the result of mass killings.The conflict has also initiated a vicious cycle of shrinking livelihood options and increasing GBV andgeneral protection risks. With limited options to develop alternative livelihood strategies, some womenand girls have resorted to negative coping strategies, including sex for survival/transactional sex; youngboys in particular resort to begging and petty trade, while men in particular, under pressure frominactivity in the camps and changes in gendered roles, often resort to domestic violence, and substanceabuse.With the insurgency, women and girls have limited freedom of movement outside the home, especiallyIDPs living in camps. Charged with their traditional roles, women and girls who walk long distances tocollect firewood and water are at risk of abduction and sexual violence thereby limiting freedom ofmovement, which in turn affects their livelihoods. Safety and security challenges are higher for womenand girls living in areas where the security parameter is limited to the LGA towns. However, movementoutside communities/camp is limited not only for women but also for men and boys, who are at risk ofviolence and abduction.Furthermore, GBV has increased in severity but also new forms of GBV appear due to this conflict. According to respondents during the CARE GBV Assessment, sexual violence happens mostly at home (23%), to or from wood collection outside the camp/community (21%), and on the way to/from school (10%). Sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) is widespread but underreported due to heavy gender norms and social stigmatization combined with inadequate response mechanisms.Engaging in transactional sex or 'survival sex' also increases people's risk to exploitation and abuse -especially for women and girls. Results from CARE's Gender and GBV Assessment indicate that sex forsurvival existed before the insurgency as vulnerable women used to cross the border to engage in thepractice in communities where they are not known.Limited access to humanitarian assistance and services, food shortages and movement restrictions bypredominantly male and untrained civilian vigilantes contribute to negative coping mechanisms andsexual exploitation and abuse of women and girls (UNHCR et al, June 2016; Human Rights Watch,October 2016; and Summary HRP 2017, November 2016).With approximately 90 per cent of those affected by armed conflict in North East Nigeria also affected byfood insecurity and with regular reports emerging on the issue, it must be assumed that the extent of theissue of women and girls being forced into exchanging sex for basic needs and exposed to furtherviolence is extremely high. Vulnerability screenings found that 56 per cent of GBV cases wereassociated with survival sex (Summary HRP 2017).A key risk for affected populations, in particular most vulnerable in particular most vulnerable individuals such as newly arriving IDPs, are exacerbated by the fact that in the context, humanitarian actors have relied heavily on the Nigerian Armed Forces to access people in need, in particular in newly accessible areas. The Humanitarian Response Plan for 2018 raises concerns that "this has blurred the line between humanitarians and the Nigerian security forces, including community-based militia such as the Civilian Joint Task Force,thereby creating security threats for both civilians and aid workers, and compromising opportunities for asustained field presence in many areas."

Activities

Overall, the Action is designed to enhance the access of newly displaced, vulnerable women, men,girls, and boys to comprehensive GBV prevention activities and response services through coordinated,principled humanitarian support and community-based prevention activities.Through a first component, the project will address prevalent GBV risks that affect displaced people, inparticular those newly displaced, which include cramped and inadequate camp conditions, non-familymembers sharing shelters, women and girls' lack of safe spaces, and the erosion of social structuresand individual and community mechanisms to prevent and address GBV.Through a second component, the Action addresses the fact that in the operational context and due toextremely high needs, crisis-affected populations, and in particular those recently displaced, dependheavily on humanitarian aid and are at high risk of sexual and other forms of exploitation and abuse.The Action thus, as a second, but interlinked component, aims to enhance the awareness, knowledge,and practice around the application of humanitarian principles and standards, and measures to preventand address SEA with humanitarian workers, and police and members of the security forces working inthe camps or other affected areas. Utilizing well-developed and tested approaches and tools, such asthe IASC Guidelines on Accountability to Affected Populations / Protection from Sexual Exploitation andAbuse (AAP/PSEA) as well as CARE's regional and global expertise, CARE will support humanitarianactors to establish/strengthen mechanisms to prevent and address SEA, and internally, CARE willstrengthen its accountability to protect beneficiaries by establishing accountability mechanisms in allproject locations and by training project staff on accountability principles and processes.Implementation Approach:1. Meaningful participation of community through community based GBV prevention system throughparticipatory analysis of GBV of risks, and community GBV prevention mechanisms. CARE will drawand adapt learning from best practices, for examples the Social Analysis and Action (SAA) and theengaging men and boys approach for the identification and structured activities for critical self-reflectionon social structures, social norms, gender stereotypes, relations and rights;2. Reducing vulnerability/livelihood and socio economic empowerment: This will be done by mobilizingand strengthening marginalized and vulnerable groups to come together for building context-appropriatelivelihood skills. Targeting all vulnerable at-risk women and girls alongside GBV survivors is shown asbest practice to ensure anonymity and reduce stigmatisation of GBV survivors.3. Coordination with GBV sub working group and other actors and services providers for referral4. Synergy and complementarity: the GBV case management will be implemented closely with otherCARE key sectors including SRH, food security and livelihood.5. Technical oversight, quality insurance and alignment with standards and principles by CARE: GBVand Prptection specialist, and an Assistant Project Manager with GBV technical expertise provideoversight and technical support, monitoring and guidance to the team;7. PSEA: CARE Nigeria adapted the CARE International (zero tolerance) PSEA policy to the COcontext and develop the related accountability measures including a whistle blowing policy, training forstaff, partnersh and peer organizations, development of contextual gender sensitive security analysisand implementation etc. CARE's staff at all level and other humanitarian actors will understand andcomply with the PSEA policy and accountability measures will be put in place in order to prevent and ortake appropriate measures should a CARE staff be involved or affected by SEA.The above will be implemented in line with the survivor-centred approach and the GBV standards, andguidelines within the GBV working group.
