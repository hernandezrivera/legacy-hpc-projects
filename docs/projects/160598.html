Project Summary

This project is targeting 4 counties; Wau and Raga in Western Bhar el Ghazal, Budi and Terekeka counties from Eastern and central equatorial respectively. The project will reach total of 87,119 beneficiaries (SAM 11,079, MAM 12,648, PLW 12,648 and MIYCN 43,713) in all the four Counties with estimated cost of $1500, 000.
AFOD will adopt combination of strategies for this nutrition response such as establishment of static services, mobile OTP and TSFP site, including use of expanded criteria in case of supply shortage, capacity building, and integration of nutrition services with Health (malaria screening and assessment of immunization status using child health card), WASH (provision of safe water, soap for hand washing, education on hygiene and sanitation), FSL (vegetable gardening, cooking demonstration, and post-harvest handling that preserve food nutrients), Education (establishment of health and nutrition clubs in schools to promote nutrition education) and protection of beneficiary by community for complementarity and synergy and preposition of supplies for desired treatment outcome. Key stake holders shall be engaged in implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project including establishment of complaint feedback mechanism and complain desk, community dialogues meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback.

1 - Needs

The effect of protracted conflict, displacement and lack of basic social services continue to impact negatively on the communities with an estimated 7.2 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, about 5 million women, children (boys and girls) and men require urgent assistance and protection (OCHA July 2019). 6.35 million people face ‘crisis’ food insecurity (IPC phase 3) of whom 1.7 million people are in emergency (IPC phase 4) even in the presence of humanitarian food assistance (IPC, August 2019) In South Sudan, the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) for children 6 -59 months is 11.9% (FSNMS Round 23) which is classified as serious by WHO and South Sudan CMAM guideline (SSD CMAM Guideline 2017). This is a result of inadequate dietary intake due to food insecurity (tribal conflict, cattle rustling and armed conflict by worrying parties) which continuously affect the livelihood activities of the citizens. The unpredictable rainfall pattern and increasing poverty levels in the Country due to economic shock coupling with poor accessibility limits free movement of food commodities which could help to diversify dietary intake of the population. Terekeka, Budi, Wau and Raga Counties are regions classified with poor GAM rate by FSNM Round 23 due to conflict (tribal and civil), economic shock, poor accessibility of road and weak health system. Although the revitalization of peace agreement signed in September 2018 has registered relative improvement in security situation, the nutrition situation has not improved significantly despite ongoing humanitarian response in the County. Project objectives1) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for management of acute malnutrition in children 6-95 months.2) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition specifici preventive services for children, adolescents and PLW. 3) To promote equitable access for Nutrition sensitive intervention from WASH, Health, Protection and FSL for synergy and complementarity.4) Strengthen nutrition information system and surveillance for evidance based nutrition response. The need for management of acute malnutrition is still huge despite ongoing humanitarian assistance due recurrent armed conflict, unpredictable rainfall pattern, economic shock and poor accessibility to markets. Acute malnutrition has a direct undesirable impact on children under 5 years, women of childbearing age (15-49yrs) including Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs). Therefore, the need for management of acute malnutrition in under five, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in Terekeka, Budi, Wau and Raga Counties is critical as indicated by poor GAM levels and is likely to worsen without intervention. It is on this basis that AFOD is submitting a proposal to implement management of acute malnutrition to prevent further deterioration in GAM prevalence and averting complications related to acute malnutrition and promote health in under 5 years.

2 - Activities

• Conduct nutrition screening for target groups (6 – 59 months and PLWs)• Admit and treat SAM/MAM and PLW cases• Refer SAM/MAM cases with medical conditions for management• Conduct nutrition outreach and defaulter tracing• Train nutrition staffs on nutrition sensitive interventions (MIYCN, sanitation, malaria prevention).• Pilot Kitchen gardening demonstration sites at health facilities by Mother to mother support groups to promote food diversity • Conduct food preparation demonstration to mothers • Print and distribute CMAM, MIYCN guidelines and look up tables • Conduct mentorships for nutrition staffs and MSGs to improve their skills • Provide safe water for appetite test• Establish complaint feedback mechanism in all nutrition sites• Conduct malaria testing for SAM cases enrolled in OTP program• Submit monthly NIS report, weekly update report, quarterly report and annual report.• Conduct joint partners supportive supervision in all the states• Conduct SMART survey in Budi and Raga Counties• Provide quarterly trend analysis to the CHD, SMOH and donor to inform decision making
