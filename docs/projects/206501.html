Project Summary

According to the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 9.4 million people in South Sudan remain in need of humanitarian assistance, including an alarming figure of 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children. According to IOM’s Consolidated Appeal of 2023 as well as the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), 2.2 million people remain displaced across the country. In a number of informal and formal displacement sites and collective centers, including current and former Protection of Civilian (PoC) sites, more than 908,800 individuals continue to live in situations of protracted displacement. Displacement figures have continued to increase despite the signing of the R-ARCSS in 2018 due to an increase of displacement-inducing incidents such as sub-national conflict and unprecedented climate-related disasters. The situation is exacerbated by the impacts of the conflict in neighboring Sudan, with a projected 400,000 returnees expected to require CCCM assistance in 2024.
Out of the 1.36 million people targeted under the CCCM Cluster Response Plan, IOM CCCM intends to target 589,091 individuals, including some 300,000 returnees fleeing the conflict in Sudan. In addition to lifesaving flood response activities in IOM’s Areas of Operations (AoOs), IOM will continue to provide returnees in transit centers as well as displaced communities with sustained and emergency camp management services, ensuring that the provision of protection and cross-sectoral humanitarian assistance is equitable, coordinated, monitored and adhering to standards. This includes the Bentiu IDP Camp (Former PoC), the Malakal PoC, the Bulukat Transit Center in Malakal, the Panakuach entry point in Unity State, as well as various ports serving secondary movement operations, such as Adok and Nyaruop Ports. Of critical relevance to the current displacement environment in the country, IOM CCCM’s interventions will also integrate a robust, community-led disaster-mitigation lens in all care & maintenance interventions and capacity building exercises to enhance protective environments to increase resilience of infrastructure and communities to climate shocks. Further, fully committed to ensuring that Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is streamlined in any and all activities, and in close collaboration with technical experts and humanitarian partners, IOM will continue to develop and improve AAP mechanisms to ensure that the organization is accountable to the people that it serves and that programmes are informed by the voices of the displaced communities.
IOM intends to contribute to the sectoral objectives through the following interventions:
1) With a focus on the impacts of climate change, IOM CCCM will implement sustained flood-response activities in and around Rubkona and Bentiu Towns, as well as the Bentiu IDP Camp and the Malakal PoC Site.
2) Provide sustained, adapted and transitional (where appropriate) critical CCCM activities to IDPs living in Bentiu, Naivasha and Hai Masna IDP Sites as well as critical care & maintenance activities in Malakal PoC, as well as returnees in Transit Centers across the country.
3) Deploy emergency CCCM teams to deliver sustainable mobile responses promoting self-governance, community ownership and resilience in hard-to-reach locations following triggers at the ICCG-level.
4) Co-lead the CCCM Cluster at national level and continue acting as State Focal Point (SFP) in WBeG, Jonglei and Upper Nile States in order to support national and state-level partner agencies in accountable CCCM responses and systems.
5) Continue chairing the Communication and Community Engagement Working Group (CCEWG) in order to provide technical CCE support to partner organizations and to operationalize outputs under the 2021 South Sudan AAP Framework.

1. Needs

According to Round 14 of IOM DTM’s Mobility Tracking conducted across South Sudan, in addition to the 2,335,604 returnees present at the time of assessment, 2,027,331 people remain displaced in South Sudan, 73% of which are sheltering with already crisis-hit host communities. The remaining 27% (554,504), which include both present and past PoCs, are currently taking shelter in displacement sites spread across the 10 states and two administrative regions of the nation. There are 270,344 IDPs in total living in Rubkona County, where IOM CCCM oversees the largest and most populated IDP camp in South Sudan. Unity State has the highest number of IDPs, more than 420,000, than in any other state. Whilst being at the frontlines of the flood response in Malakal, Bentiu and Rubkona towns since the onset of the crisis, IOM continues to have serious concerns about the next rainy season and the arrival of returnees fleeing the Sudan conflict into Unity State. The new arrivals may create tensions, worsening the already precarious situation of the existing IDPs and host communities due to the limited resources for humanitarian assistance. Accessibility for humanitarian responders remains a challenge due to cut supply lines and the increased price of commodities. The Renk-Malakal corridor will remain one of the main arteries for IOM’s Onwards Transportation Assistance interventions, where CCCM operations in transit locations will continue to be crucial. Ongoing displacement in Twic, Warrap State will also continue requiring attention from life-saving mobile and roving response teams. Moreover, sub-national conflict continues to have an impact on the displacement context across the country, with a sizeable number of the displaced finding shelter in informal settlements and collective centers – located in hard-to-reach locations and cut off by the effects of climate shocks like floods. In 2023, according to the CCCM Cluster, 58% of displacement sites hosting more than 327,000 people did not receive CCCM support. The ability to quickly mobilize and respond to immediate displacement due to conflict and climate shocks is critical to reach people in need – and as such flexible and agile mobile responses, geared towards self-governance, empowerment and resilience will be essential. Displaced communities and returnees fleeing conflict from abroad are in need of urgent humanitarian support, with most of their emergency coping mechanisms exhausted due to protracted displacement and repeated shocks. Regardless of whether the affected population has settled in formal IDP sites or informal settlements, the need for appropriate monitoring and coordination of humanitarian service provision and protection remains a critical enabler in aid delivery. Furthermore, the increase in both the frequency and intensity of climate-related shocks call for vigorous flood mitigation, site development, care and maintenance interventions to be implemented by CCCM’s engineering teams to ensure and maintain a protective environment where humanitarian aid is accessible. These interventions should also be led by the community to ensure that adequate capacity is built within the community to promote community resilience and the sustainability of responses. Whilst continuing to implement sustained and emergency CCCM interventions, IOM will strive to support solutions to displacement for IDPs (especially in the Wau area). Meanwhile, IOM CCCM’s core services and responsibilities will continue to cater to the various displacement contexts under IOM’s AoR, including Bentiu, Naivasha and Hai Masna IDP Sites, Malakal PoC for Care & Maintenance, as well as targeted mobile responses in Warrap, WBeG, Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei, attempting to reach a target of 589,091 individuals.

2. Project activities

The project activities will contribute to all four objectives as stipulated under the 2024 CCCM Cluster Response Plan. In 2024, in line with the needs identified in the 2024 HNO and in coordination with the CCCM Cluster, IOM will implement the following activities: IOM will enhance the living conditions of internally displaced people (IDPs) in former and current PoCs areas under its management. This will be achieved through practical gap analysis, well-coordinated camp services, and fair access to cross-sectoral humanitarian assistance through CCCM core services, DRR-adapted site care and maintenance interventions, and significant AAP-oriented community engagement activities. IOM CCCM will provide Camp Management coordination and information-sharing to partners including the CCCM Cluster, monitoring services to ensure equitable access to aid and protection by using referral mechanisms, providing capacity development and enhancing resilience to the displaced population including Dead Body Management (DBM) and fire prevention and response mechanisms; IOM will further engage daily with representative governance and community protection structures – including women, PwD/PSN and youth; conduct daily CwC activities such as door-to-door sensitization, townhall and awareness sessions on relevant topics and camp services and improve access to information; IOM will maintain and expand the use of communication centers in order to enhance Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) and accessibility to meaningful and effective feedback mechanisms, including through planned outreach CFM; implement appropriate regular and emergency site development, care and maintenance activities geared towards improving access, protection, sanitation conditions and congestion as well as mitigating the risks and impacts of climate-induced shocks such as floods; coordinate with the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) to ensure adequate coordination of services, including in identification, mitigation and response to protection risks, in close collaboration with Protection Cluster and GBV Sub-Cluster; lead and update elaboration of contingency plans in coordination with service providers; strengthen resilience, prevention and response capacities of the affected population to climate shocks and epidemics through human-centered design (HCD) workshops, capacity building and information-sharing; collaborate with the CCCM Cluster and other CM Agencies in mapping displacement sites in IOM’s AoR; advocate for durable solutions and work towards sustainable and empowering interventions in CCCM’s programming, emphasizing AAP and the HDPN; provide CfW in planned and emergency community-led care and maintenance activities; support DTM in registration, maintenance and verification exercises as well as maintain and improve information management systems; mainstream protection and AAP through satisfaction surveys, consultations and trust-building. In coordination with CCCM Cluster and the ICCG, working in transit areas for the Sudan Response, IOM will also deploy flexible mobile response teams to respond to new displacements due to conflict and natural disasters in hard-to-reach areas, prioritizing sustainability, ownership and self-governance. IOM CCCM will lead or participate as applicable in inter-cluster needs assessments and CCCM Mobile Assessments in priority locations; produce service and site mappings, 3Ws and gap assessments in assessed locations; conduct capacity building, training and HCD workshops to representative governance structures, including women, PwD/PSN and youth, geared towards self-governance, entrepreneurship, improving life-skills, sustainable and sustainable site care and maintenance activities to prevent and mitigate risks as well as climate-induced shocks; set up CBCM committees in order to operate a meaningful and responsive CFM in hard-to-reach locations; provide CfW in planned and emergency community-led care and maintenance activities.
