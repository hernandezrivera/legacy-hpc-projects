Project Summary

This project aims at enhancing resilience through GBV intervention responses in Ayod, Duk, and, Canal Pigi Counties, Jonglei State, South Sudan
This project aims to provide a comprehensive response to 6682 vulnerable women, men, boys and, girls with and without disability in Duk, Ayod and, Canal Pigi counties that are affected by multiple humanitarian crises including conflict, displacement, flooding, food insecurity, and population return burdens.
MTA aims to provide GBV responses and services to 6682 women, men, boys, and girls including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability through targeted assistance.
Mission Trust Aid will increase efforts on protection and GBV risk mitigation and prevention outcomes through mainstreaming all humanitarian sectors to keep women and girls safe, promote their dignity, and protect their rights.
MTA will regularly report the implementation of GBV interventions on 5W's and resources mobilized on Financial Tracking Services

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to grapple with the effects of conflict, displacement, insecurity, flooding, economic difficulties, and the influx of returnees from Sudan further straining the already limited resources and deepening extreme poverty.Access to WASH basic services is limited in South Sudan. A significant portion of the population (41 percent) lacks access to improved water sources, and 37 percent need to travel over 30 minutes to reach their primary water source, posing particular hardships for persons with disabilities and making women and girls more vulnerable to GBV. Access to sanitation is even more limited with prevalent open defecation, as 88 percent of the population lacks access to improved latrines, according to the 2023 FSNMS data and 2024 HNRP posing risk for GBV. Approximately 2 million children and adolescents between 6 and 17 years old (53 percent are girls), along with 1 million children between 3 and 5 years old, encounter significant obstacles in accessing education, including early marriage and GBV, especially for girls. (HNOs)The persistent conflicts and flooding in different parts of the Jonglei state, increased risks of exploitation and sexual abuse by authorities including international and national staff, psychosocial distress, including the impact of traumatic events on children's development, learning capacity, management of violent emotions, the use of physical, verbal and sexual violence, displacement including forced secondary displacement from IDP sites and returnees, the lack of social cohesion, the difficulties of restoring the peaceful coexistence of certain communities, the risk of violation of children in at-risk communities including children without appropriate care and children with disabilities, lack of primary caregivers including foster case, gender-based violence including domestic violence, forced and child marriage and early pregnancy among others.

2. Project activities

MTA will implement the following activities• Provision comprehensive case management services to boys and girls, men and women• Cash-based initiative for case management where required• Structured and non-structured, community-based mental health and psychosocial support to boys, girls, men and women• Conducting awareness raising sessions with women, men, boys, and girls on GBV risks, available response services, and timely reporting• Establishment of GBV prevention initiatives (e.g., SASA, EMAP, community care programming)• Provision of specialized GBV response services (GBV case management, referrals, legal aid, safe houses & WGFS programming• Provision of PSS services to women, men, boys, and girls, including recreational and social activities.• Provision of dignity kits to women and adolescent girls• Cash voucher assistance where necessary• GBV risk mitigation interventions (Income generating activities, fuel-efficient stoves, etc.
