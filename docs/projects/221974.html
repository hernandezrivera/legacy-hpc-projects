Project Summary

The project has been designed to address the deteriorating food and nutrition security in the project locations. The proposed locations include the 5 States (15 Counties) Uror, Twic East, Duk, Pochalla, Boma (Jonglei State), Aweil East, Aweil South, Aweil Centre (Northern Bahr el Ghazal State), Mayendit, Rubkona (Unity State), Kajo Keji, Yei, Lainya, Morobo (Central
Equatoria State), Pibor-Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA).
According to recent food security updates for South Sudan, alarming new food security data shows that 57 per cent of the population (7.7 million up from 7.1 million) will be acutely food insecure through the 2025 lean season with returnees fleeing the war in Sudan and young children facing some of the highest levels of hunger and malnutrition (2.1 million children, up from 1.65 million) as economic pressures, climate extremes, and the effects of Sudan's war drive worsening hunger. The latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), released on 18-11-2024, shows more than 85 per cent of returnees fleeing Sudan will be acutely food insecure through the next lean season, starting April 2025. An estimated 2.71 million people (56 percent of the region’s population) are in Phase 3 or above, of which 10,000 people (0.2 percent) are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), 779,000 people (16 percent) are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and 1.92 million people (40 percent) are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). Generally, food insecurity is driven by the impact of the Sudan crisis, flooding, sub-national and localised insecurity and violence, unusually high food prices, low agricultural production, crop and livestock pests and diseases, market disruptions because of poor road networks, among others.
This project proposed intervention will achieve the HRP Strategic (SO) and Cluster objectives (CO) in line with the sector objectives: SO1: Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe, and dignified access to lifesaving
assistance to meet their needs, SO3: Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand the impact of shock is increased, and nexus opportunities are optimised. CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in IPC 4 and 5, CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock, CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes.
This will be done through the provision of lifesaving assistance to crisis affected communities by providing agricultural inputs and providing training to vulnerable households to increase food production through good agricultural and fisheries practices to improve food security. Emergency livelihood kits distribution, and increasing access to local markets through community assets/feeder road maintenance, market structure, seed and produce fairs will be organised for local farmers and the project will provide direct distribution of food rations to the most vulnerable communities. Tearfund will also look at ways of resilience-building that reduce recipient’s future risk and vulnerability to negative shocks.
The proposed project activities will enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and field crop livelihood inputs, and fishing kits support. The distribution of farming & fishing inputs will provide the households with a variety of food (veggies, vitamins & proteins) for their consumption in a short span, in addition, surplus harvest will be sold in the market for income or preserved for future consumption during the lean season. Field crop varieties suitable to local soil, climatic and environmental conditions such as rice & yams suitable for flood prone areas shall be distributed.

2. Project activities

Tearfund will focus on providing holistic programmes in an integrated approach factoring in sectors and resilience building of communities. All the proposed activities will work to establish a nexus between Humanitarian response, Peace building (conflict sensitivity), recovery and Resilience programming. The main activities will include the emergency food and cash distribution, including distribution of complementary vegetable and field crop livelihood inputs, and fishing support. Farmers groups and VSLAs scaling up and strengthening.Priority 3 counties will be supported with agricultural inputs & tools as Tearfund will work closely with FAO for some of these items and deliver to beneficiariesThe distribution of early maturing OPV vegetable seeds will provide the households with a variety of vegetable food for their consumption in a span of 3-4 months. Surplus harvest will be sold for income or preserved for future consumption during the lean season. Households will be trained on appropriate post-harvest handling and storage practices to enable them avoid losses, preserve surplus produce for future consumption and marketing.Distribution of fishing kits will be conducted across the Bomas in safe distribution sites agreed upon with local community leaders and local government actors. Project staff, community volunteers and leaders will facilitate distribution and ensure crowd control.The Fishing kits will help the households get the much-needed proteins almost immediately after they receive them and engage in fishing activities, the households will be encouraged to preserve and or sell surplus for future use or earn extra income for other household needs.PDM meetings will also be conducted in all the selected sites to identify key learnings, record statistical distribution data and confirm receipt of intended good condition items on behalf of the communities. Several other technical training on the vegetable, field crop and fishing kits shall be conducted including vegetable & field crops production, agronomy, and local or organic pesticides production; fishing and fish preservation. This is to enhance household knowledge and improve production & productivity. Training on climate smart agriculture practices will be conducted amidst the current surge of climate change effects. Training sessions will be facilitated by Tearfund’s FSL Project officers in collaboration with Government extension workers. Lead farmers will also be targeted during vegetable kit and field crop seeds training so as to disseminate key learnings to communities. The project will be implemented by a team of staff who are already established and currently implementing crop kit distribution food security programmes, supported by a technical FSL manager. Tearfund staff in Juba, will provide backup support in quality reporting, MEAL, financial, donor coordination and logistics & supply activities throughout the project. The community and other stakeholders will be sensitised on the project and informed of the beneficiary selection criteria, which will be agreed by community members. During community sensitization the importance of prioritising women and vulnerable groups will be emphasised in order to gain community acceptance of the selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs will be prioritised for assistance, including female, elderly and child headed HHs, those with malnourished children, PLW and people living with disabilities. Beneficiaries will be asked their preferences regarding seeds through FGDs, which will be held separately for men, women and specific vulnerable groups to ensure that vulnerable groups are able to fully participate in and contribute to the discussions.As part of Tearfund’s continued risk mitigation approach, a comprehensive analysis will be conducted and periodically updated in all the project sites. Tearfund will work closely with FAO and other NGOs to ensure timely detection and reporting to the appropriate focal points.
