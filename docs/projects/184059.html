Project Summary

In Sudan, following the collapse of the previous regime in 2019, peace talks between the Sudanese government and the rebel groups resumed, and the peace agreement was signed in October 2020. However, the South Kordofan-based rebel group (a faction of SPLA/M-N) did not participate in it and negotiations are still ongoing in Juba, the capital of South Sudan, to reach an agreement. Also, in May 2020, an armed group clash occurred in Kadugli, the capital of South Kordofan, and it developed into an armed attack targeting each other's ethnic groups. Houses were destroyed and looted in some of the IDP settlements, and many people flowed into other settlements in search of a safe environment. IDP children and adolescents who continue to be exposed to unstable situation do not have sufficient education and employment opportunities.
In accordance with increase of young children who move to government area from anti-government area, people are facing lack of educational facility that can cover new comers in IDPs area. Moreover the teachers in South Kordofan are facing lack of teaching skills, technics, class management , commitment to study schedule and psychological support for students.

Therefore, the project will promote a learning opportunity for the children and provide training for teachers in South Kordofan.

1 - Needs

Since the outbreak of the conflict in South Kordofan in 2011, JVC has supported people affected by the conflict, about 10 years have passed since the outbreak of the conflict, and many people began to make a living without relying on food assistance. The life has shown a certain level of stability. However, there are reality that many children, including existing IDPs and IDPs who have newly arrived, cannot access administrative services including education. Therefore, since 2016, we have been focusing on child support, providing birth registration support and school construction necessary for school attendance. School buildings are being constructed so people who have started returning after the situation has stabilized can attend school in the original village. Along with the collapse of the former government, the interim administration has shown a stance to promote peace with anti-government organizations and is expected to create a society for social stability, but children who have not received sufficient education, It is difficult to demonstrate sufficient ability to create a social infrastructure. In particular, many IDP children have lost their opportunity to enter regular elementary schools because they have reached the middle and upper grades without attending school. There are also many young people who do not have enough education and vocational skills to find jobs. Many people move to Khartoum and other cities in search of jobs, but often they have simple jobs under severe working conditions and environments. In addition, since many local NGOs that were deeply involved were ordered to suspend their activities after the collapse of the former government, there was concern about the lack of support, and the role that international NGOs will play an important role in stabilizing the community.Moreover, there are an influx of 18,528 IDPs in 2019 and 2020 (IOM, August 2021) and many young children (approximately 55% of the newly-arrived IDPs) moved to government areas from anti-government areas to meet their families and start new life. Most of them have never studied in anti-government areas, even those who have studied, the duration is only a few years in English so special care including reading and writing of Arabic, psychological support to get custom to study in school would be required for them.As increase of the number of children who move to government area, people are facing the lack of the education facility such as school building, desks, benches and water supply. Therefore the intervention for both soft (operation of ALP) and hard (school facility) remain in high needs. Regarding the people in needs (PiN), the number of IDPs in South Kordofan state is reportedly 272,781 (IOM, August 2021), and the number of the IDP children is estimated approximately 55% of them (150,000) and 30% of them (approx. 45,000) are out of school according to our assessment conducted in several communities hosting IDPs. Considering the IDP population is concentrated in Kadugli locality, we estimate that approximately 20,000 children are out of school in the locality.
