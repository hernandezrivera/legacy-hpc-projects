Project Summary

Starting from the needs of the affected people in Syria, the project will participate in providing assistance for vulnerable groups and people whom under or at risk to be under protection concerns.
Starting from the needs of children related to child labor, dropping out of schools, separation, and psychosocial needs, the project will provide child protection services as structured PSS, Awareness raising on child protection concerns and specialized child protection services including case management, and referrals based on service mapping in each targeted location, the project will support 30 informal schools by establishing 2 rooms to deliver structured PSS and case management activities, also will support 40 formal schools by providing recreational sessions along with supervision to identify children with special protection needs.
The high target of child protection component, is because of the concrete structure of Bonyan in education which clearly described through the project under code: HSYR19-EDU-155841-1
On the other hand, and according to girls and women needs in Maaret Al Numan, either women and girls under GBV risks (domestic violence, sexual harassment, and early marriage, Bonyan will establish 1 WGSS centers to provide all GBV services, PSS for women and adolescent girls, women empowerment activities (soft skills, computer skills, languages).
And after building the capacity of the field staff through conducting trainings on GBV Specialized services provision, Bonyan will implement specialized activities in GBV with close coordination with GBV sub-cluster in future during coming projects.
To expand protection services out of protection and education facilities, 12 mobile teams will provide integrated protection services (CP, GBV, RE), in child protection topic mobile teams will provide recreational PSS, awareness raising sessions on CP risks, in GBV awareness raising sessions will be delivered to Men, Women, and Girls in GBV concerns, along with providing Risk Education in camps and IDPs collective centers to mitigate the risks from mines and war remnants.
Legal awareness raising and assessments will be conducted to issue advocacy messages regarding CSD issues, and the implementation will target students at schools and caregivers since the lost documentation will cause problems regarding students’ registration at schools.
Awareness raising sessions will be conducted along with distributing brochures to deliver the message clearly.
As a previous experience in this topic, Previous project supported by IOM for 1 year, targeted 750 HHs with rehabilitation, along with 10 local councils targeted with HLP trainings to ensure the Identifications of HHs which related to properties.
Specialized protection services will be delivered to the most vulnerable BNFs at risk of CP, GBV, General Protection concerns, the services are referrals to services providers, and direct support provision, based on service mapping will be implemented in the early beginning of the project.
the needs and risks should be measured during the project.
Since the situation on the field level is rapidly changes, the needs assessment should be implemented on monthly basis, thus Bonyan is a partner in Child Protection Situation Monitoring, will implement CPSM survey in the project locations to measure the changes of CP issues severity during the project’s duration.
Regarding the capacity building component, all protection staff will be targeted with capacity building program focusing on the following topics:
CPiE 2 days, CPMS 1 day, Case management 1 day, GBV 3 days, Risk Education 1 day.
Each trainee will attend 8 days training program to ensure the knowledge and quality of service which will be delivered.
Total trainees will be (65 mobile teams’ members, 180 CFSs protection staff (30 schools will be supported to include CFSs), 80 protection staff from 40 formal schools, 12 WGSS center staff members, 3 senior field staff. In total of 340 trainees

Needs

According to the HNO 2019 assessments have indicated that gender-based violence, particularly sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, as specific needs of the planned location to establish WGSS under this project, according to PMTF the index of negative coping mechanisms in Maaret Alnuman is 0.34 with 45% of KIs reported that women are not accessing to WGSS.The length of the crisis and deep-rooted social norms, in conjunction with the mounting lawlessness in some areas, are normalizing this violence, resulting in the detrimental psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights.Additionally, various means of technology—e.g. texting and social media—are used to sexually harass and facilitate early/forced marriages. The restriction on freedom of movement of women and girls also inhibits their access to services, humanitarian aid and ultimately curtails their rights, with widows who have experienced divorce and widowhood and adolescent girls disproportionately impacted.The socioeconomic situation, lack of livelihood opportunities, and increased poverty are ultimately leading to women and girls to be at higher risks of all forms of violence including sexual exploitation and abuse. Where GBV services exist, women and girls have confirmed that they have a positive impact on their lives, with women and girls’ safe spaces serving as a place to seek safety and to receive essential life-saving services (health, psychosocial and legal support) and other needed services (e.g. vocational and skill training). Children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks.Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern. The conflict and its impact on children and families have fueled negative coping mechanisms such as child labor, early marriage and child recruitment, putting adolescent boys and girls at particular risk, while straining the capacities of families and communities to protect children.According to CPMTF reports, 83% of communities in Idleb and 90.75% in Aleppo reported that children in need of focused non-specialized services along with 48.81% in need to specialized services in Idleb and 62.45% in Aleppo, 72.3% of communities reported that boys and girls are engaging in works instead of going to school in Idleb, and 90.47% in Aleppo.Child marriage mainly affecting adolescent girls, though adolescent boys are also at risk. Girls and boys are married early as a household negative coping strategy. Recruitment of children, particularly adolescent boys, by all parties to the conflict continues to be reported with 4.59% in Aleppo, and 2.7 in Idleb.Regarding the prevalence of loss in civil documentations and according to PMTF reports, 60% of Aleppo communities reported that people losing civil documentations because of security issue which reflects on movement of people along with registering children in schools, in Idleb, 52% of KIs reported that GoS services are not available in this governorate.

Protection Risk Analysis

1.1- 1. For awareness raising: expectations of response/services in locations where services may in fact be limited/non-existent1.1-2. For PSS/MHPSS: lack of sufficient support/supervision for specialized staff; limited specialized mental health professionals inside Syria.1.1-3. For targeted assistance: risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups / individuals at the exclusion of other groups/individuals.1.2- 1. Limited judicial mechanisms for redress 1.2-2. Risk that by providing information about documentation which may not be recognized is perceived as endorsement of said documents/legal systems.2.2. – 1. There is no redresses of the violation and/or relevant service available 2.2-2. Organizations do not sufficiently staff teams with dedicated protection monitors so monthly collection varies/data is hard to compare 2.2-3. Unnecessary expectations are created among individuals and communities.3.1. – 1. Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services. 3.1-2. GBV service providers safety and security because of their role and PTS for dealing with violence. 3.1-3. Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services.3.2. – 1. Exploitation by service provider or by other actor in the community 3.2-2. Pressure on the service provider from the local authorities on who should or should not receive the service.3.3- 1. Focus on quantity of service providers trained instead of on quality of training, relevance and impact on knowledge and improved service provision5.1.- 1. Girls, boys, women and men’s physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed parties to the conflict; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receive services; lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure enroute to, during and directly after service provision. 5.1-2. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. 5.2-1 Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Bonyan will take the following steps to mitigate the risks that been listed and described previously: 1.1- 1. Train outreach workers and community center staff on how to deliver messages to communities and provide advice on how to answer questions1.1-2. Agree on minimum standards and monitoring mechanisms in collaboration with the MHPSS along with prepare service mapping before start implementing activities with good coordination between Bonyan and service providers on the field level. 1.1-3 Develop clear selection criteria in consultation with the community and other key sectors.1.2-1. Regular mapping the existing forms of violations redress available for program design.1.2-2. WoS advocacy on non-penalization and non-discrimination for alternate documents that follow the spirit of recognized legal framework.2.2. 1. Rollout of Cluster referral pathways/protection monitoring organizations sign SOPs.2.2-2. Advocate with donors to ensure dedicated funding for protection monitoring.2.2-3. Make a clear communication plan to communities on CPM.3.1 1. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centered services 3.1-2. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral 3.1-3. GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place.3.2-1. Ensure beneficiaries know services where to disclose if violence occurs 3.2-2. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report 3.3. 1.Use of SOPs and other guidelines and resources from GBV AoR in line with global standards 5.1. 1. Consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed site or mobile service provision. 5.1-2. Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (route and location) by boys, girls, women and men, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical, visual/hearing deficits, psychological issues). 5.1-3. Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community. 5.1-4. Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community (to ensure diverse perspective and perceptions are considered). 5.1-5. Ensure consultation and mobilization of key persons in the community. Provide culturally adapted information on key child protection issues and responsibilities of different people in the growth and development of children. 5.2-1. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules.
