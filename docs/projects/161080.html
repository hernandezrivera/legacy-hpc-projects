Project Summary

The project will be aligned to Health Cluster objectives, namely 1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities; 2. Improve and scale up response through integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations; 3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, people with disabilities and patients presenting mental health disorders.
The project will complement the ongoing intervention in selected priority Counties, narrowing the health care provision gaps in the most remote sites and for the most neglected groups and ensuring outbreaks and other emergencies impacting on health to be timely and effectively prevented, addressed and contained. The project will therefore contribute enhancing the resilience and the capacity to cope with current and future crises of the targeted communities, including those people whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) makes them particularly vulnerable and their access to services more challenging, besides requiring specific response routine interventions are not used to provide. Such response will be strengthen by integrating the health related activities with those pertaining to the protection sector, through the opening and sustain of reciprocal referral channels for people to be offered both clinical care and psychosocial support. In general, project impact will be maximized through a multisectoral approach. Nutrition and WASH will be given high attention, with the result of services being highly comprehensive, as addressing both health and nutrition conditions and the inter- related complications, and offering quality care, being informed by hygiene promotion and IPC principles, becoming the first setting for an effective prevention of the main infectious diseases.
The project will target both displaced and host population, proposing a range of different services provision modalities (static and mobile), expected to be able to overcome the accessibility issues affecting current health care coverage at all level of the health system. The proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities will facilitate the selection of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable option, while orientating the intervention on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

Targeted Counties’ increasing needs and challenging response are due to: internal conflicts, affecting services provision/access; IDPs burden over hosting families, whose resources are limited; inadequate HFs number, distribution, conditions (staff number and skills, equipment, supplies); failure of funding mechanism in sustaining routine services. Former Lakes MCH indicators are low: ANC4 30% (ANC1 75%), DPT3 54% (34% drop out), institutional delivery 27% (47% of which SBA), attended MDOC 6.5%. Curative consultation rate is 0.88 (1.47 in U5). Malaria/Diarrhea/Pneumonia are U5 main reported diseases (62%, 20%, 18%). Malaria prevention and diagnosis are poor: 48% cases confirmed, 65% women accessing ANC receiving IPT2 and LLTN. HFs based LLTN distribution reaches 9% U5. Water borne diseases are common (79%,household self-diagnoses in 2018), favored by poor WASH: 95% households practice open defecation, 66% do not have soap. While 82% would have access to protect water sources, 63% do not feel safe when accessing them. Insecurity is hampering access to resources for 46% of households, matter of further concern when considering 62% of households are headed by a woman, more exposed to violence when dealing with domestic tasks. Female headed households are more likely to be food insecure. In Lakes, 89% of households are in such situation, with 57.9% of population in crises/emergency/catastrophe status (particularly in Yirol East, Yirol West and Awerial). Acute Malnutrition is critical, 15-30% incidence. Nutrition services integration at HFs is not complete yet, especially in case of different implementing partners; ie. growth monitoring is not part of routine OPD package yet (36%), resulting in missed opportunities for SAM/MAM identification. Prevention and referral at village level are limited and defaulter rate still high (11%). Positive health/nutrition practices are poorly adopted (exclusive breastfeeding 56%). Greater Mundri (former Western Equatoria) HFs have been reactivated after long internal conflict, but their full functionality is penalized by the impact looting and damages had on their infrastructures and staff reluctance to resume duty. Mobile services resumption is affected by insecurity. Services demand is increasing (91% ANC1, 65% DPT1, 2.5 U5 OPD UR), but utilization is still low (53% ANC4, 54% DPT3), as well as institutional deliveries coverage (33%), with 70% of them concentrated in Hospital/PHCCs (little assistance in the periphery). Food security is at crisis stage. Fragmented vertical nutrition interventions hamper malnutrition response (alert level). In Maridi County, health facilities are facing the same problems, being not effective in preventing/containing outbreaks. This is concerning, as Maridi is bordering with other Countries where epidemic diseases are endemic. EVD preparedness and response measures are being put in place but without the due continuity and support. Unity State is one of conflict cores. Panyijar County is safer, therefore affected by IDPs influx. IDPs look for care, but health system is not adequate to cope with increasing demand. 76% IDPs are women and children and 42% of coming households are including one PLW, while County has only 1 CEmONC center and PHCCs capacities of dealing with health and nutrition complications are poor. IDPs impact on the limited food availability (food security at emergency level). In all sites, crises lead new needs health system has to deal with as mental disorders and disabilities. The HMIS does not capture their data, therefore the size of the problem site by site is still unknown, they represent 5% and 11% of South Sudanese population. Few information on SGBV, mainly affecting women; besides the lack of dedicated data collection tools, victims do not speak up. OCHA estimated 50% women in each household undergo a violence episode within a year. [2018DHIS, IPCSouthSudanAugust2019, FSNMRRound22, ReachFactsheetPortMonitoringNyal August2019]

2 - Activities

Project aims ensuring essential lifesaving services to the most vulnerable groups. PHCU/PHCC/County Hospital Emergency Clinical Services Packages, as defined by Health Cluster, will be provided in targeted Counties’ selected PHC Facilities/Hospital. The selection will be done in agreement with Health Cluster and after needs assessment and discussion with the beneficiary communities and representative authorities. The intervention will prioritize sites not covered by health system or where existing services are not sufficient to cope with the actual demand for care, as neglected by common funding mechanisms or overloaded because of IDPs influx, outbreak, violence wave. Mobile and ICCM/CHP Emergency Clinical Services Packages, as defined by Health Cluster, will complement/replace static care, increasing preventive services coverage, offering healthcare when HFs functioning/accessibility will be compromised by insecurity/adverse environment, responding to outbreak/displacements. In Panyijar County, these Packages will be preferred. All this will be realized by: hiring/rewarding/training/supervising local health staff (profiles listed in the Emergency Clinical Services Package) and CBDs/CHWs; procuring and distributing essential drugs and supplies buffer stock, complementing what provided in kind by other actors; replacing old/ looted equipment and repairing damaged infrastructures to restore enabling conditions for services provision; ensuring transport means availability and running, to support movements to the field. All sites will be provided the due supplies and staff trained/supervised for preventing, diagnosing, treating the most common infectious diseases (Malaria, Diarrhea, Pneumonia) as well as TB and HIV, which management will be guarantee at frontline level as well. County wide free of charge ambulance service will be guarantee, prioritizing obstetric and pediatric emergencies. PHCCs will be upgraded to BEmONC centers, County Hospitals to CEmONC. Nutrition services will be integrated, with HFs/Mobile Team/CHW implementing the functions included in the above mentioned package and referring/treating SAM related complications. EPI coverage will be boosted, with routine immunization both at HFs and in the villages, through outreaches conducted by health staff and field volunteers, using motorbikes. In case of outbreak, mass campaigns will be organized. Supervision/coordination for outbreaks prevention, timely identification, integrated response through multi-disciplinary approach will be supported, especially at the border. Linkage with WASH Cluster and partners will be established for hygiene and IPC basic conditions and principle promotion at community and facility level. Local authorities’ surveillance activities will be technically and materially sustained, to ensure their continuity and consistency. Coordination mechanisms will be put in place, involving all stakeholders and defining roles and responsibilities for concerted actions. In specific hot spot and in case of particular circumstances, as EVD alert, specific measures for suspected cases identification and first line management will be applied, as per National Task Force orientations. Protection approach will be streamed, ensuring due attention to particularly vulnerable and neglected groups, as SGBV victims and people with disabilities or mental disorders. They will be promptly identified and carefully handled, being given adequate clinical attendance and then referred to specialized centers for psychosocial and legal support and follow up. Network with these facilities will be strengthen to ensure adequate attendance to concerned people. HWs will be trained and sensitized to respect the dignity and to address the specific needs of these groups of patients. Specific data collection tools will be introduced to track their access to services and to provide health authorities with an updated overview, advocating for adequate response to be put in place.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project aims to benefit both host and displaced population. Actually, one of the problems it will address is the inadequacy of routine health services to respond to an increasing population, under IDPs influx, especially when IDPs prefer to locate in remote sites, not served by any health facilities. The adoption of mobile service delivery modalities is functional to bring the needed assistance at the closest possible level to beneficiaries, reducing both the distance they would have covered to reach static providers and the risk they would have been exposed to while moving, usually higher for displaced population.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Project targeted Counties are the same CUAMM is present as local health authorities main Implementing Partner for the provision of routine health services, role it is playing since many years, in deep cooperation with State Ministry of Health, County Health Departments and local communities. The project complement this long term intervention. It is intended to guarantee prompt response to any emergency occurring in the targeted sites, which routine services would not be in conditions to handle, and/or to complement routine services coverage, sustaining those sites still missing health care provision or reaching those groups not benefiting of any assistance but presenting specific vulnerabilities

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to Health Cluster objectives, which are reflecting Ministry of Health priorities

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Former Lakes Counties are affected by internal clashes producing IDPs movements within their own borders, merged to those coming from other States. Greater Mundri situation is normalizing after years of conflict, but local militia are still maintaining some instability pockets in some areas. Panyijar County is the safest within Unity State, but because of this destination of all IDPs escaping from conflict main spots. In spite of this complex and uncertain context, CUAMM yearly presence in the area, widely recognized and appreciated by both communities and authorities, facilitates the implementation of the proposed activities, which aim is to help local population to overcome all obstacles (including those related to insecurity) hampering access to and utilization of services

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

CUAMM added value is mobilizing different donors on the same comprehensive objective, within a broader intervention, aligned to local priorities. This is functional to the effective integration between different sector and between humanitarian and development component, as in this specific case, with the present project being part of a wider program CUAMM is implementing in the same area, benefiting of various contributions. All these constitute a solid ground, assuming Health System running costs, and their longer term perspective allows targeting local authorities and staff with capacity building, aimed to create the conditions for the taking over of health services management. In 2018, CUAMM supported services reported 1.202.040 access. Health services economical sustainability is unlikely in the short/medium term. In the meanwhile, local authorities start resuming health system ownership responsibility, developing a sense of accountability. CUAMM signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the SMoH in the targeted areas, which specifies each part’s duties and task. CUAMM provides technical and material support for health services delivery and management, but the decision making process and responsibility is held by the SMoH, familiarizing with planning, prioritizing, allocating resources...acquiring the capacities it will need once NGOs and donors will step back. The present project is aimed to address issues related general context and donors geographical and technical scope change, providing additional resources to narrow a specific gap, while already available funds will keep sustaining the overall system and the above mentioned process of capacity building. During project implementation, CUAMM will collect all elements needed to discuss with local authorities and other donors an eventual priorities redefinition and consequent reallocation of existing funds to ensure current proposal activities continuity and/or to raise new resources for this purpose

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.1Marginalized are usually living isolated, fearing being harrassed while moving, with limited resources and therefore with few possibilities of getting assistance. Mobile- ICCM/CHP Emergency Clinical Services Package will enable them to access health care, close to their living places, free of charge. Community representatives/BHWs will provide Health authorities with information on these groups locations and status and for promoting their acceptance whitin local population. Right based approach will informcapacity building/training activities for health staff and authorities, for all patients being attended, indipendently on their belonging. 8.2Mechanisms for community involvement in health system are in place but members are mainly men, as per traditional habits. CUAMM and CHD will promote gender balanced composition of HF/Boma health committees through community wide sensitization and by fixing a mandatory female quote at County Quarterly Review Meetings. Women inclusion will create a virtuous circle of increasing awareness on the importance of all groups contributing to health system running. Health services enhanced accessibility will make women healthier and released from their caretaker role, enabling them to engage in public functions and tasks.8.3Health services will be victims entry point to specific assistance (clinical, psychosocial, legal). CUAMM will sensitize and train health staff on how to identify and handle this kind of patients, offering first line care while encouraging their adherence to the following therapeutic steps. Health authorities will be updated on protection actors and supported in linking with them, establishing effective referral pathway for victims comprehensive attendance. CUAMM will advocate for Health authorities and staff to promote county wide sensitization and education on this matter, for violence episodes drop and victims stigma fear reduction, encouraging them to speak up and to look for proper assistance

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CUAMM is used to maintain a constant dialogue with communities representatives. These latest belong to the Health Facilities related Health Committees but also to other community based associations, which are invited to take part to the Quarterly Review Meetings held at County level and which ensure a wider perspective on community needs and expectations and can report on services responsiveness and acceptability. CUAMM and CHD will ensure HCs gender balanced and inclusive composition and attendance, confirming them as participatory spaces women and other vulnerable individuals can be part of, an empowerment driver, offering public engagement opportunity, usually very limited for certain groups, and making these latter aware of their own value, to be enhanced through a virtuous circle of increasing participation. The recently introduced Client Satisfaction Survey offered the opportunity to get feedback on provided services by the direct beneficiaries. The questionnaire is filled by literate volunteers so that it can be administered to any patient and it has been piloted to confirm all questions being easy to understand and to be answered by people with different education background.
