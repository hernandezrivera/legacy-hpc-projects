Project Summary

Concern proposes to target both Banadir IDP & Urban, Lower Shabelle, Gedo and Bay regions for this project. 102622 targeted for Nutrition services while 163753 for Health. Most of the health and nutrition indicators for these regions proposed by Concern for this project far exceed the recommended SPHERE standards. In order to promote access to health and nutrition services, Concern proposes to conduct mobile clinics in Gedo and Lower Shebelle regions particularly in order to promote access, protect beneficiaries from harm associated with walking long distances and proposes to expand this to other regions of operation. Concern proposes to target IDP camps to ensure the most vulnerable in the community receive the health services that are much needed. The catchment population in the Concern supported districts of Gedo and and L. Shabelle are very far from available fixed sites (mostly district hospitals) necessitating a 4 day walk at a minimum, which presents a significant barrier to access for people living with disabilities and a protection risk for men, women, boys and girls. It is against this background that mobile H&N response intervention is deemed viable to meet the critical need. Within this project, Concern proposes to continue with mobile response in the selected districts working closely with stakeholders to ensure beneficiary access to quality, timely and non-interrupted critical health service delivery. All fixed health facilities will provide skilled delivery services as well as GBV awareness and referral including CMR in the selected centres with capacity in terms of infrastructure and HR for health. In all the facilities, priority will be given to people with disabilities and service provision areas (including toilets) will be adapted and rehabilitated to be disability friendly. To promote accountability to affected population, all the facility catchment areas will have community health committees which will be responsible for the linkage between the community and the healthcare facility, enhancing the Complaints Response Mechanism. Toll free numbers will be placed at strategic locations within the facility for beneficiaries to express any concerns, and the hotline will be monitored by the M&E team. All Concern staff are inducted and trained on PSEA. Additionally, Concern has safeguarding focal points in place with clear appropriate channels for reporting. All necessary COVID-19 safety procedures will be observed. Concern has directly run Health and Nutrition programmes in multiple regions in Banadir (Mogadishu) and Afgoye communities, Lower Shebelle and Gedo since 2008 and developed long-standing, trusting relationships with key actors including communities served, MoH authorities, peer Non-Government Organizations and the UN. In 2021 Concern undertook direct service provision in 10 integrated health and nutrition facilities in Banadir and 5 in Lower Shebelle regions, including over 100,000 Out Patient Department (OPD) consultations for children and adults, serving a total catchment population of 400,000. Additionally, since late 2017 Concern has managed the stabilization center at the National Referral hospital in Mogadishu, which provides treatment for children with life-threatening complications of severe acute malnutrition Concern is proposing to support and increase access to health and nutrition services, particularly maternal & child health services at ten sites in Banadir –Banadir Region, Wadajir District 3 Health centers and 1 SC namely Halane HC, Obosibo Halane HC, Wadajir HC and Banadir Hospital – SC (stabilization Center). Daynile District: Sinkadheer HC, Weydow HC, Ceeldacas HC and Bondhere District, Wilwal HC and Bondhere HC. Shibis District: Shibis HC Lower Shabelle: Afgoye District; Gargar HC; Gurdaan Mobile Clinic. Wanlaweyn: Warmahan HC, Tabellaha Mobile Clinic and Hero Mobile clinic. 1. - Projects activities

The basic service provision will support communities through the prevention and treatment of malnutrition (SAM and MAM using the CMAM Surge approach, MCHN etc.), immunizations, ANC, PNC, and outpatient treatment for diseases as per the IMNCI approach for < 5 children and vaccinations as per the Somalia EPI schedule. OPD consultation services will be offered to all members of the community in addition to PLWDs. These will be Primary Health Care services as per the EPHS. The project will support the work of Community Health Workers, M2M/F2FSGs in sensitizing and mobilizing communities in accessing health and nutrition services by focusing on IYCF messaging at both community and facility levels for better detection of malnutrition. Concern and our partner’s health staff will be trained on the provision of EPI vaccines as well as IMNCI. In all of these facilities Concern will adapt COVID-19 preparedness, prevention and response activities in reference to the MOH COVID-19 response and preparedness plan as well as the Somalia Inter-Agency Contingency plan for COVID-19. Proposed include activities: Infection Prevention and Control measures, recruitment and training of health workers and Community Health workers, planning, coordination, implementation and monitoring to enhance EWARS, PPE provision, Case referral, Risk Communication and Community Engagement, and Establishment of extra triage areas and handwashing facilities as well as psychosocial care. Considerable efforts will be made to ensure mainstreaming of GBV interventions i.e. provision of CMR services, GBV awareness, sensitization as well as referral linkages, a separate room will be made available in the ANC sections to ensure confidentiality access to services especially for GBV cases as well as promote adolescent friendly services. To ensure WASH integration, all facilities will be incorporated with WASH elements i.e. setting up of hand-washing stations. LLG, SHACDO and GREDO are Concern’s local implementing partners in Gedo, Lower Shabelle and Bay regions respectively and help Concern to reach underserved and vulnerable populations. Concern is dedicated to the capacity building of the local partners technically and administratively, enhancing the localization agenda and ensuing local ownership and sustainability in the long run. Concern incorporates a District Centred approach in implementation of H&N activities and thus will coordinate with other stakeholders in the supported districts to ensure complementarity and referral pathways. Secondary Health care beneficiaries will be referred to referral hospitals as required as well as beneficiaries seeking health care who would also be in need of other services i.e. protection, Education, GBV etc. A referral mapping will be conducted at the onset of the project and necessary sensitization on the same carried out. Mobile clinic health service delivery is a key component of the EPHS especially in hard to reach areas and more importantly in areas where fixed health service delivery is minimal as in the case of L. Shabelle and Gedo where this implementation modality is proposed. In compliance with MOH/WHO SARA (Service Availability and Readiness Assessment) and to enhance regulation and standardization of health facility set ups, partner stand-alone setting up of health centres are greatly discouraged as there is a potential risk of mushrooming unregulated non-reporting health centres. The catchment population in the Concern supported districts of Gedo and and L. Shabelle are very far from available fixed sites (mostly district hospitals) necessitating a 4 day walk at a minimum, which presents a significant barrier to access for people living with disabilities and a protection risk for men, women, boys and girls. All fixed health facilities will provide skilled delivery services as well as GBV awareness and referral including CMR in the selected centres with capacity in terms of infrastructure and HR for health.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Project implementation staff consult the IOM displacement registers for each camp to ensure they are aware of potential new arrivals that may not be aware of the services and have CHWs conduct sensitization at field level to ensure maximum awareness. Maximum visibility of facilities (were security permits) with both graphics and signage to increase awareness in communities; work with community groups for inputs and ensure they have a diverse demographic of the community. Concern will work with DAC forums and other community groups to ensure the displaced person are being included to decision making processes and meetings (men and women). Accurate assessments, both individual and joint, will be conducted throughout the implementation period to determine caseloads and levels of need taking into consideration extremely vulnerable persons. Project activities are designed with the specific and additional needs faced by displaced communities in consideration and registrations at health facilities are aware and sensitive to communities dynamics: clans, host vs displaced etc. so as not to increase possible conflict triggers. Facilities are, as much as possible, located centrally to affected populations so as not to cause undue protection risks on patients trying to attend and clinic hours take into consideration the daily responsibilities of patients, particularly women who culturally have responsibility for domestic duties. Concern’s Code of Conduct and patients right information is displayed clearly within facilities, patients and attendees of the clinic are made aware of CRM mechanisms.
