Project Summary

CARE AID’s proposed 12-month intervention aims to deliver life-saving Mobile Camp Coordination and
Camp Management (CCCM) as well as Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Item (S/NFI) assistance,
directly benefitting 25,000 displaced and conflict-affected persons in out-of-camp settings.
The areas of intervention are Madagali, Maiha, Michika, Girei, and Lamurde LGAs.
CARE AID will utilize a Mobile CCCM response to respond to critical needs and the provision of life-saving support in the targeted LGAs. This will build on CARE AID’s previous experience in providing CCCM and Shelter/NFI assistance in the Borno particularly in Mafa and Konduga LGAs, where it is the CCCM Camp Manager of 3 IDP Camps, offering critical assistance to IDPs through CCCM as well
as urgently needed S/NFI provisions. To address the critical needs of IDP’s, CARE AID’s mobile CCCM response will aim to increase access to CCCM services amongst IDP populations residing in these sites through enhancing multi-sectoral coordination of humanitarian response, monitoring of protection and assistance, promotion of participatory approach, community organizing, promoting, and strengthening of accountability to the affected population, ensuring infrastructure improvement and to mainstream protection, Child Protection, GBV, Prevention from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and other cross-cutting issues like Early Recovery into CCCM for improved living conditions, and durable solutions of the affected populations. Below are some of the proposed activities that Care Aid will implement.
• Site facilitation: -Support the authorities to effectively coordinate multi-sectoral response in project locations and report gaps to respective partners and sector leads through the Shelter/NFI/CCCM sector.
• Site Improvement: - preparation and upgrade of identified areas, including communal infrastructure
within the site/area.
• Strengthening of community governance Structures: strengthening the existing site management
committees in each of the sites and the identified areas through capacity building and establishing such
structures where none exists.
• Capacity building: - staff, partners, and authority’s capacity building for improved accountable and
effective delivery of CCCM activities.
• Establishment of Information Centers (out of camp): - outreach to IDPs, returnees who live within the
host community in Madagali, Maiha, Michika, Girei, and Lamurde.
• Community mobilization & engagement: - reinforcement of community mobilization and information dissemination through the existing Complaints and Feedback through the use of loudspeakers and IEC materials
In summary, the Action will build on extensive coordination and collaborative efforts implemented by
CARE AID and Sector partners in order to provide comprehensive S/NFI and CCCM assistance modalities, which address critical risks, supports flood readiness and responds directly to the needs of vulnerable IDP populations in out-of-camp settlements as well as within camp settings in Adamawa State.

1 - Needs

The current security situation in the BAY States continues to deteriorate with some of the worst attacks taing place in the first and last quarter of 2022, when NGOS and IDP populations were directly targeted. This continuing conflict severely affects millions of people, subjecting them to massive displacement. What started as a localized insurgency more than twelve years ago has turned into aregional armed conflict with no clear end in sight. With 8.7 million people in need and 1,700,000 million internally displaced (HNO 2022), the humanitarian crisis in the northeast of Nigeria remains one of the largest and most severe in the world today. Eleven years on, there is no sign of the conflict abating. On the contrary, the protracted character of the crisis has had a devastating impact on the region and is adding to a long history of marginalisation, underdevelopment and poverty. The conflict has resulted in a decimation of social and economic infrastructure, and grave large-scale humanitarian needs continue to persist, requiring a strong coordinated humanitarian response capable of tackling multi-sectoral gaps.In relation to specific needs in Adamawa State and looking to the suggested targeted LGAs (Madagali, Michika, Maiha, Girei and Lamurde), the results of Round 42 of the IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) prove instructive in relation to the proposed areas of Intervention: Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) and Shelter/NFI.The number of returnees varies from state to state, with the majority in Adamawa State (82,000), most of which are fleeing the conflict in Cameroon. In relation to informal out-of-camp sites in host communities, there was before now no Site Management Actors or CCCM support in the targeted locations, the situation has changed somewhat, where organisations such as INTERSOS have moved into providing some mobile CCCM support. This however is not sufficient to respond to the needs of the population.In response to multiple critical needs of IDP’s in informal out-of-camp sites in Adamawa State, Care Aid proposes the above mentioned CCCM activities to respond to the absence of CCCM actors in the targeted locations. Care Aid’s CCCM programming will include a strong focus on accountability, community participation, mobilization and engagement. Participation will be fully mainstreamed in the response from the very beginning and a participatory, community-based approach will be applied to all activities and services taking place in sites.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1.1: Site Facilitation (management of out of camp sites, site improvement and repairs, community leadership strengthening, site planning, complaints and feedback mechanisms, FGD, sensitization and awareness, camp/community coordination and information sharing (out of camp sites)Activity 1.1.2: Capacity building: - staff, partners and authority’s capacity building for improved accountable and effective delivery of CCCM activities.Activity 1.1.3: Infrastructure maintenance/rehabilitation, Site planning, Land advocacy, mapping of existing suitable lands, relocation, fire response and prevention, flood mitigation for Rainy season response through provision of mitigation tools and formation of water channels in all camp/camp-like sites and prioritized host community locations based on needs, and with involvement of the affected population (women, girls, boys, men of all ages and diverse background, including People Living with Disability (PLWD) at camp/camp-like sites and within the host community under the Out of Camp.Activity 1.2.1: Establishment and maintenance of an all inclusive community committee in camps/camp-like and in Out of Camp settings with membership of minority groups, youth, PLWD in full respect of AGDM approach.Activity 1.2.2: Community mobilization through sensitization and awareness creation on leadership and selection of leaders, joint community mobilization events that leads to peaceful coexistence and cohesion among leaders in camps/camp-like structures including leadership in Out of Camp settings.Activity 1.2.3: Training for community leaders, volunteers, religious leaders, youth and women leaders on how to discharge their responsibilities well, the understanding on camp management, protection, GBV, trafficking, PSEA, referral system, community participation among others, and in full respect of AGDM approach, and the integration of livelihood activities and vocational training to youths and vulnerable women in the communities.Activity 1.3.1: Construction and management of information & Coordination centers in Maiha, Girei and Lamurde – out-of-camp to ensure continuity. This will include procurement of sporting and recreational activities equipment to promote community coexistence and cohesion.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

CARE AID will ensure accountability to the affected population throughout all stages of the project life cycle. This action is centered on 4 pillars of participation and inclusion, communication and transparency, feedback mechanisms, and organizational learning. To ensure participation/engagements, CARE AID will employ participatory methodologies at each stage of the operations management cycle to incorporate capacities and priorities of people with disability into protection and assistance activities of this action by engaging with organizations of people with disabilities. CARE AID will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons with Specific Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision-making process. CARE AID has already established complaints and feedback response mechanisms (CRM ) in Mafa which has been functioning well as two-way communication between the community and the agency. These are also shared with other agencies accountable for their respective sectors.
