1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

In response to the urgent educational needs arising from emergencies in Mozambique, Street Child aims to implement a comprehensive Education in Emergency (EiE) program. Recognizing the devastating impact of natural disasters, conflict, and other crises on the educational infrastructure in Mozambique, our initiative focuses on providing immediate and sustained support to affected communities. The program encompasses the establishment of learning spaces equipped with essential educational materials, ensuring access to quality education for children. Additionally, we prioritize the training of local teachers and facilitators to deliver trauma-informed and culturally sensitive education, addressing the emotional and psychological needs of students. To enhance the resilience of the education system, our proposal includes the development of community-based disaster preparedness and response plans, fostering a proactive approach to safeguarding education during emergencies. By collaborating with local stakeholders and leveraging community engagement, Street child seeks to empower Mozambique's affected populations, particularly children, through the restoration and enhancement of their educational opportunities in times of crisis.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

We propose to provide the following categories of activities in Nampula and Cabo Delgado:1) To provide equitable access to safe and protective education services to girls, boys, and CWD of 6-17 years old in emergency through formal, non-formal and ALP by 2023 in 3, 4 and 52) To improve the quality of education through providing emergency training to teachers and education personnel 3) To strengthen and improve learning environment by ensuring access to improved drinking water sources and access to hygienic latrines

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Key risks and mitigation methods:• Security – security SOPs will be adopted when starting field activities in the target districts, and coordination will be sought with INGO security structures. Security of the staff is a consideration given they will be engaging with sensitive topics and working directly with affected population. As such, community engagement and sensitsation will be prioritized throughout the project timeline, particularly during sensitive activities around MHM training and VAC campaign. • Community acceptance is a high priority for the project. This is in particular due to engagement with child protection issues and girls education, and with regards to messaging on GBV, MHPSS, and VAC. Community acceptance is crucial to the overall buy in and success of the project, specially during the back to school and VAC campaigns. In order to mitigate the risk that the project is not accepted, and therefore does not have the desired impact, all initial engagement will be done with key stakeholders from all relevant beneficiary groups, as well as leadership. MINEDH/DPEDH and SDEJT will be involved in design and roll out of the modality of the implementation, and feedback of the affected population, particularly children, will be gathered and reviewed on a monthly basis. • Creating tension between peoples – to avoid the risk of creating tension between host and IDP communities, beneficiary criteria will be vulnerability, not status, based, activities will target those from host and IDP communities, and selection criteria and rationale will be explained by field staff every step of the way

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Equitable access to all services and activities will be sought through 1) overtly targeting marginalised girls (60% target for students/teachers), 2) conducting specific gender and age-appropriate activities. This programme responds to gender-based risks. Girls are at higher risk of early marriage and teen pregnancy, greater risk of Sexual Gender Based Violence due the recent Gombe cyclone and lack of shelter, frequent movement, domestic labour, and deprioritisation for education. Boys are exposed to risks such as recruitment into armed groups and subsequent social stigma, becoming unaccompanied or separated, and child labour. Gender will also be a consideration when training teachers, care givers, and community members. As climate crises threatens to exacerbate existing inequalities, these strategies are essential to ensure alertness to gendered needs. SC will provide teenage girls with dignity kits that include MHM material to enhance school retention and uphold their dignity.All staff will be trained in Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) and Child Protection principles. Community mapping of perceived risks and activity planning will take age and gender into account. To ensure equity, programme activities will be assessed on a GESI continuum, ensuring they are either GESI accommodating or GESI transformative; gender- age- and disability disaggregated data collection ensures equitable approaches to monitoring, assessment and accountability.Sustainability will come from capacity building of national actors, allowing them to form new partnerships and making them centre to humanitarian response in Nampula. Frequent training on child rights and child protection for teachers and caregivers, Community Based Child Protection Committees (CBCPC) members and local authorities, coupled with awareness raising and sensitisation around MHPSS and VAC, ensure increased community’s understanding and ownership of Child Protection and gender related issues and solutions.
