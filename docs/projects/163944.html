Project Summary

The project aims to achieve the main goal :
Vulnerabilities of IDPs and recently war-affected population in the Al-Bab and Jarablus district of Aleppo are reduced through improved access to safe water and equitable access to wash facilities with provision of personal hygiene items in emergency settings to the targeted population/IDPs, conforming to the sphere standards and local customs, taking into consideration the special needs of men, women, boys, and girls with special attention to the needs of the disabled persons in the camps and informal settlements. QRC will mainstream protection to all aspects of this intervention during the project cycle.
The proposed intervention will provide life-saving integrated WASH services (safe and adequate water supply, provision and maintenance of gender-appropriate water distribution points, water quality monitoring, solid waste collection and management, as well as the periodic distribution of hygiene items accompanied with hygiene promotion based on training for community hygiene promoters) in camps and informal settlements for IDPs displaced in the first quarter of 2019 from Idleb, in addition to long-term IDPs living in settlements. This intervention supports the main strategic priority and matchs withthe WASH sector objective 2 "Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services to the most vulnerable people." This intervention is directly supporting the WASH cluster strategic objective 1 "Provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people, with emphasis on areas with high severity of needs, including those in UN-declared besieged and hard-to-reach areas."
QRC’s WASH field team intervention will provide basic WASH support to internally displaced populations who have settled in 55 camps and settlements in the Al-Bab and Jarablus district of Aleppo. QRC’s framework for response builds on 12 months of intervention and seeks to ensure that the households residing in these settlements continue to survive, and in a way that respects their right to water and safe. Humanitarian actors in Syria have struggled to secure funding for informal camps and self-settlements, where conditions have continued to deteriorate as influxes continue to arrive in what remains of a haven in Syria. QRC will provide chlorinated water to 40,600 individuals in 55 camps and settlements in Al-Bab and Jarablus district. The trucks used to transfer this water will be cleaned and chlorinated by QRC staff to prevent waterborne diseases. The water will be delivered to steel tanks (QRC will provide 1587 tanks during this intervention), which will provide a ‘water point’ in the settlement where beneficiaries will be able to access water at their convenience, decided on through discussions with the community. Each water tank can provide water to four people simultaneously, and the placement of the water tanks will reduce the waiting time and distance that people have to travel to access water. QRC will test this water (at the source, tankers and in tanks) on an ad hoc basis to ensure it is potable.
QRC will remove solid waste from 55 camps and remove the waste to landfills; this is a crucial step since much of the waste is currently dumped within proximity to the camps. QRC will distribute rubbish bins to prevent littering. QRC will distribute 9,022 Hygiene Kits in 55 camps. Important hygiene promotion will take place in gender-segregated groups, accompanied by the distribution of hygiene kits. This component contributes to the mobilization of the community, as the hygiene promoters will come from the communities themselves.

4 - Needs

Comprehensive WASH assessments were conducted in June-August 2019 to inform the 2020 HNO. Based on WASH assessments, there are an estimated 10.7 million people who require WASH assistance, 3 million of whom are considered in acute need (people with elevated risk of unsafe consumption water, facing issues with water sufficiency or affordability, issues with sanitation or garbage removal; people unable to access hygiene items; people living in areas with high ratio of water-borne diseases, high intensity of hostilities, overburdened communities; and IDPs living in formal and informal sites). IDPs in formal and informal settlements are highly dependent on humanitarian aid. Nevertheless, access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations and IDPs living in sites, in particular those in informal or overcrowded sites. Newly displaced populations require a timely and quality response based on sufficient preparedness. IDPs in formal sites and informal settlements are highly dependent on continued humanitarian assistance. Disruptions of services in IDP sites and their quality have direct negative physical and mental well-being and protection consequences, in particular of women and girls. ** 200126_HRP 2020 - WASH_v4The project targets the IDPs in north Aleppo (Euphrates shield), where there are 55 camps; most are informal and self-settlements (9022 HHs). 40,600 individuals are in dire need of WASH intervention. The area is considered a safe resort for the people because this area is under the control of the Turkish government.Turkish authority and AFAD requested QRC to support the vulnerable IDPs with adequate aid to improve their living conditions. Furthermore, there is no WASH response from any NGO in these camps. QRC WASH team had access to the area through its ongoing projects.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities are divided into two categories of intervention. The first is the contractual services, which include water trucking and solid waste management. The contractual services will be implemented through local contractors. The second is supply aid, hygiene kits, and liquid chlorine. The supplies will be purchased according to QRC procurement procedures and later will be distributed to the affected population by QRC’s field team. QRC will hire two experienced hygiene promoters to act as trainers to conduct TOT for the volunteer’s hygiene promoters. QRC will work with the affected population to choose 20 male and female promoters from the targeted location; those will respond to meet men/women and boys/girls of the targeted beneficiaries to increase their hygiene knowledge.QRC will hire a project field coordinator responsible for the coordination of the project activities and the high quality of the implementation on time. Three site engineers will support the field coordinator; the site engineers will be responsible for worksite according to the geographical location of the camps and self-settlements. Also, six supervisors will work on the site of work locations daily, where the activities are taking place. The implementation staff will collect the needed information to measure the progress of the project indicators and deviation from the original plan to make the correct decisions.M&E team will also the post-intervention surveys to measure the project outcomes and the contribution to the overall goal of the project. All the distribution and water trucking, Local contractors, and skilled labor will be engaged. Local councils will be involved in all the stages of the project cycle. This project is designed by consulting the IDPs and camp managers directly as well as collecting information from the NGOs working in the area. Key stakeholders in the sector with whom QRC is coordinating now are all WASH partners.
