Project Summary

A decade of conflict has trapped civilians in a pervasive protection crisis where people’s rights to safety, security and well-being are regularly violated. The overall protection situation defined by the protracted crisis and characterized by complex and interconnected protection concerns remains dire.
Protection risks, especially GBV and CP continue their disproportionate impact on women and children. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern. In whole of Syria, 5.9 M children are in need of protection, of whom 60% are in severity category 4 and 5.Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 23 per cent of surveyed households demonstrating the cumulative toll on mental well-being with immediate and if not addressed, lifelong consequences.
The goal of this project is to improve the psychological, mental, and educational well-being of children (1137) of kindergarten and primary school age residing in non-formal camps in Idlib and Afrin region of NWS, meeting the HRP 2021 objective. The project will target 4 camps, AlMadina, AlManhal in Idlib and Tranda and Jisr Afrin in Afrin region. The 4 camps in total has 6,448 individuals in 1352 HHS. Outcomes through this intervention can be summarized as follows:
• The mental and psychological wellbeing of the children improved and reflected in their education performance.
• Children and caregivers (12 in total) are more aware of Covid-19, its danger, psychological effects, ways to avoid and prevent it.
• Parents (379 in total) and caregivers are more understanding and convinced of the child's needs and the importance of the surrounding environment and its effect on the child’s mind and thinking, and the necessity of joining children in formal or informal education and keeping them out of child labor.
• More children feel mentally and psychologically stronger and able to cope with the trauma of conflict. And equipped with the knowledge to merge in formal education.
• The region (the community) is equipped with trained cadres qualified to work in the psychological, mental, and educational fields
The project CP activities include Children’s Activites, Parental Sessions and Caregivers Training. Covid Awareness will also be conducted. Activity 1: Children's activities will be conducted in 2 different modalities, Online platform and Olive Caravan bus. In the non-formal camps in NWS, families will be relied upon to provide smart devices or computers to their children as activities will be implemented online through the Olive Branch platform and include Puppet theater, Painting/Drawing, Educational Cartoons, Sports sessions, Recreational sessions, UCMAS-Abacus learning, and Digital Library. Activity 2: Parents sessions will be conducted (2 sessions at each camp site) to raise adequate awareness among the parents with respect to child’s mental and psychological health. Activity 3: Training of aid workers/cadres to make sure they are sufficiently trained in supporting the mental health and wellbeing of the children. Activity 4: Covid awareness campaign will be conducted for the camp residents. In Afrin, activities will be conducted through the Olive caravan equipped to receive children and well prepared to maintain safety standards and distancing considering the Covid risks. Olive Branch plans to implement this project over 12 months period with the help of 12 specialist caregivers and 5 field support staff backed up with management by HQ in Gaziatep. Cost per child is at $136.35.
Project will ensure that disability is taken into consideration by ensuring that activities are design to enable children with disability to participate. For example, materials will be provided for those who cannot attend the online. There will be a ramp in the bus for wheelchairs. The aid workers and trainers are sufficiently trained to work with children with disability.

4 - Needs

According to Needs & Response Summary (Feb 2021), with the crisis in its tenth year, staggering levels of need persist for people in Syria. Whilst there has been a reduction in violence in many parts of the country over the past year, an estimated 11.7 M people require multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance. Within the affected population, protection issues disproportionately affect women and children. The COVID-19 pandemic brought new protection risks and consequences for the affected population while compounding existing vulnerabilities. Children comprise almost half of the affected population. Children are at risk of being killed or injured, recruited and used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted and sexually abused. (MRM for Syria 2020). Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 23 per cent of surveyed households. NWS has 2,7 M displaced people, 1302 IDP camps (1017 formal +285 non formal), 300,000 displaced families with 391,000 school aged children. Afrin region has 39,000 displaced people. To address the psychosocial distress of the children, Olive Branch decides to conduct protection intervention in IDP camps, namely, Al-Madina Al-Munawara and Al-Manhal in Idlib province, Trandah, and Jisr Afrin camps in Afrin. In terms of HHs, Al-Madina camp has 579, Al-Manhal 271, Tranda 355, and Jisr Afrin 147.The project also acknowledges that parents of the children need their awareness raised in children rights, their protection and violations. The project will begin with a reassessment activity to confirm the needs. Covid SOPs will be observed while interacting with children and other beneficiaries. OB will include female workers in field works.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

By following the cluster guidelines, the project will address potential protection risks with mitigating measures appropriate to the intervention modality of this project. Oliver Branch assesses the risks to be in following areas. A: Incorrect perception on locations – Med/Med. Choice of location is based on the severity of need and non-formal camps are often overlooked by aid agencies.B: Reluctance to acceptance of project by community – Med/Low. Direct engagement with girls/women can be incorrectly construed by the community. Involve female caregivers in the service delivery.C: Risk to humanitarian nature of the intervention by armed actors – Low/Med Strong coordination with local civil authorities, camp management, and community actors to prevent interference by armed actors. D: PSEA risk – Low/High. OB has a strict PSEA and CP Code of Conduct and complaint mechanism open to beneficiaries. All staff are trained on PSEA.M&E department will be responsible to monitor and document at all stages of the operation to avoid risk of corruption. OB also have a strict PSEA and CP code of conduct for their staff to adhere to. As part of accountability to the affected population, beneficiaries will have access to hotlines to report any incidents ofmisconduct or feedback in general.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Olive Branch have assessed the risks to the proposed intervention and can besummarized in terms of Operation, Financial, Security in addition to Protection discussedin the previous section. In terms of security risks, we estimate this to be low in that thedistricts chosen are relatively safe with active armed action and close to Turkish border. OB will also coordinate with the local civilian authorities and the camp management to avoid any kind of interference by any armed groups in our operation. Financial risks are low as we have a well-established finanancial control,procurement, and anti-fraud policy to ensure the duties are sufficiently segregated for thebenefit of transparency, control, and avoidance of any frauds. In terms of disruption to our operationdue to lack of internet service, we estimate the risk to be low in that the project will ensure that all live sessions are recorded for use by beneficiaries. Camps often lack stable internet. Provision of internet cards by project is an option to improve access. Cost of mobile device is also a factor for many parents. They will be encouraged to share their devices with the children.
