Project Summary

This project will provide Integrated, life-saving, emergency health interventions for populations affected by flooding, conflict, disease outbreak and displacement. The project will focus on women, girls, boys and men among the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), Returnees and host populations with limited or no access to health services. The project will ensure improved access to integrated emergency lifesaving healthcare services focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality for conflict-affected and vulnerable IDP, host community and returnee households in the two states of Western Bahr El Ghazal and Unity and the two administrative areas of Greater Pibor (GPAA) and Ruweng (RAA).
The proposed interventions under this project will include the following:
1. Provision of essential primary health care services targeting the most vulnerable populations through various clinical packages (Hospital, PHCCs, PHCUs, mobile clinics and ICCM)
2. Implementation of outbreak investigation and response for epidemic prone diseases in locations where CARE works.
3. Support to static Health facilities in Western Bahr el Ghazal and Unity States particularly on essential medical supplies and capacity building in line with the strategies and priorities of the health cluster
4. Mainstream accountability to the affected populations

1 - Needs

Frequent disease outbreaks such as cholera, measles and malaria have exposed the fragile health system existent in South Sudan. With only 28.6% of the population living within 5 km of a health facility (Macharia et al, 2017), there are continuing efforts from both the Government, the UN, Donors and NGOs to improve access to health services. Drivers of the low access to health services include limited resources especially in rural areas, inadequately staffed facilities, low level of education and lack of a reliable public transport system. These factors contribute to delays in accessing lifesaving health interventions especially for women and children. Access to health services in parts of Unity and Jonglei is severely affected by conflict and flooding as has been the case in the past several years. During such events, displacement of the population including health workers, destruction of health facilities, loss of water storage containers and mosquito nets and destruction of latrines leads to widespread contamination of water sources. Increased malaria, watery diarrhea and respiratory tract infection morbidity and mortality has been a consequence of the flooding. In addition, outbreaks of vaccine preventable diseases (especially measles) have caused a huge burden on the health system. Wau has also witnessed significant conflict which has resulted in displacement of the population around Wau town. The rest of the population is not currently reached by Humanitarian actors, including health services providers. Indeed, of the 36 health facilities in Wau County, only 10 are currently functional, which leaves a big proportion of the population out of the mainstream health services. Maternal mortality ratio in South Sudan continues to be very high at 800 deaths per 100,000 live births with an equally high neonatal mortality ratio at 37 per 1,000 live births. In addition, only 25% of mothers had given birth at a health facility or received care from a skilled birth attendant at the time of delivery. Because of the elevated insecurity and displacement, incidences of gender based violence have also been reported in Jonglei and Unity. Access to mental health services has also not been guaranteed at the health facilities because of the undue focus on communicable diseases. The impact of COVID-19 continues to be felt within the health sector: increased workload at the health facilities, infections among health care workers and the need for isolation and quarantine has disrupted delivery of health services departments; COVID-19 has also disrupted training programs; mobility restrictions have affected shipment of medical supplies and movement of staff. According to the recently released IPC report , between December 2020 and March 2021, close to 6.35 million people (55.3% of the population) will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Between April 2021 and July 2021, the food security situation will deteriorate with an estimated 7.24 million people (68.7% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. This unprecedented food insecurity is expected to have a huge impact on the malnutrition situation and therefore morbidity and mortality especially among children under the age of 5 and pregnant and lactating women in areas with a high burden of communicable diseases. The high burden of infectious diseases such as malaria, acute respiratory infections and diarrhea, which contribute up to 77% of all the OPD consultations (HMIS, 2012) will also have an impact on the health status of the South Sudan population. CARE proposes to implement a project that will reduce morbidity and mortality among the vulnerable populations, improve access to life saving health services and increase access to health services for the SGBV survivors, the disabled, those with mental health disorders and the elderly. this will be done by integrating with other sectors: protection, nutrition and FSL.

2 - Project activities

in 2021, CARE will support 9 static health facilities and 2 mobile clinics in Wau, 7 static health facilities and 2 mobile clinics in Pariang, 4 static health facilities in Abiemnhom, 4 static health facilities and 1 mobile clinic in Boma and 4 mobile clinics in Akobo respectively. CARE will carry out the following activities in response to the 2021 humanitarian response plan (HRP)- Ensuring epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response across all its health projects. - Providing immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities for the health programmes. - Provision of BeMONC/ CeMONC services including procurement of essential RH commodities and building the capacity of skilled birth attendants- Provision of life-saving essential health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers.- Integrating MHPSS and SGBV services in health facilities and provision of supplies for MHPSS services. - IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities- Views of the communities are taken into consideration in the provision of health services - Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services - Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives - Provision of rehabilitation services and assertive devices for the for disabled.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARE has ensured that the PSEA Focal Person is known to all staff and beneficiaries and that contacts are made widely shared. Additionally, CARE provides awareness-raising sessions on sexual exploitation and abuse for all staff and beneficiaries and other personnel in CARE on a regular basis. The Human Resource department makes appropriate recommendations to management on enhancing PSEA prevention efforts.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CARE will use their community engagement strategies to ensure that the vulnerable members of the community (people with disability, women and girls) and ensure that their voices are heard. This will be done by taking advantage of the existing feedback and complaints mechanisms structures that are in existent at the community level (complaint boxes, M&E staff, focal persons, community leaders etc). The above groups will be informed of these options and will be encouraged to provide feedback through any of them.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The opportunity for communities (both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) to provide CARE with their feedback and/or complaints, and in exchange to receive feedback or response from CARE, is an important part of being accountable. To this end, CARE will train all staff implementing the project to ensure that they understand and have the capacity to implement the Accountability to Affected Populations Mechanism

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CARE will use the beneficiary feedback to adapt the project and ensure that it is addressing the needs of the community. To this end, CARE will involve the community in all the implementation of the project including the target criteria for beneficiaries and mechanisms for referral. Regular project reviews will be conducted together with the affected population to ensure that the project is on track and is meting the expectations of the community.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CARE is actively working with communities to ensure that they are aware of the existing protection related services, including on GBV and Child protection. In addition, referral mechanisms at the community level have been enhanced, by working with partners that provide protection and Gender Based Violence Services in the locations where CARE works. All CARE staff have also been trained on protection mainstreaming. In addition, GBV audits have been carried out to ensure that beneficiaries and staff are aware of what if expected of them in the access and provision of health services.
