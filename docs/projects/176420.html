Project Summary

The impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic, particularly the increased economic instability and school closures, along with expected high levels of food insecurity in 2021, flooding, and conflict-related displacements, continue to exacerbate the already existing high levels of gender-based violence (GBV). IOM's programmatic assessments indicate that the COVID-19 prevention measures and economic contraction have had a significant impact, particularly on adolescent girls and young women, especially related to increased use of harmful coping mechanisms, such as sexual exploitation and forced marriage, resulting in increased adolescent pregnancies.
The current humanitarian context is exacerbated by a high prevalence of violence against women and girls, particularly conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV), intimate partner violence (IPV), and forced marriage, including child marriage. This violence is structurally underpinned by a patriarchal social system, connected to the political economy of bridewealth (Luedke, 2019), with harmful gender norms underpinning cycles of violence within and between communities, particularly affecting the rights of women and girls.
Furthermore, the needs for GBV services are high, yet the availability of multi-sectoral GBV services across the country is low, and access remains a challenge for women, especially in rural communities. Key barriers for survivors include distance to services, availability of qualified service providers, social restrictions over the women and girls movement, and high levels of social stigma and possible harmful repercussions for disclosure (including the threat of divorce and marriage to perpetrators).
IOM aims to contribute to strengthening the humanitarian response to GBV contributing to HRP objective 2.4. IOM’s approach is to work closely to build national capacities to implement GBV prevention and response programming based on the Interagency GBV minimum standards. This includes strengthening community-based referral mechanisms to improve access for GBV survivors, establishing women and girl friendly spaces with tailored and age-appropriate activities for awareness, skills-building and psychosocial support, provision of GBV case management, psychosocial and legal counselling. For outreach and GBV prevention, IOM utilizes messaging developed with communities based on key issues identified and primary prevention approaches including Engaging Men through Accountable Practices and with school-age children, with significant capacity building and supervision requirement to ensure real behaviour change is created on these issues. Finally, IOM builds capacity among IOM outreach teams and partners on PSEA, community-based complaints and feedback mechanisms as well as working with organisations of persons with disabilities and elderly persons to improve inclusivity of GBV programming.

1 - Needs

The impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic, particularly the increased economic instability and school closures, along with the expected high levels of food insecurity, flooding, and conflict-related displacements, continue to exacerbate the existing high levels of gender-based violence (GBV). IOM's programmatic assessments indicate that the COVID-19 prevention measures and economic contraction have had a significant impact, particularly on adolescent girls and young women, especially related to increased use of harmful coping mechanisms. The current humanitarian context is exacerbated by a high prevalence of violence against women and girls, particularly conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV), intimate partner violence (IPV), and forced marriage, including child marriage. This violence is structurally underpinned by a patriarchal social system, connected to the political economy of bridewealth (Luedke, 2019). Furthermore, the needs for GBV services are high, yet the availability of multi-sectoral GBV services across the country is low, and access remains a challenge for women, especially in rural communities. Key barriers for survivors include distance to services, availability of qualified service providers, social restrictions over the women and girls movement, high levels of social stigma, and possible harmful repercussions for disclosure. In Tonj South and Jur River, IOM and partner The Organisation for Child Harmony (TOCH) conducted GBV assessments in December 2020. The main protection risks affecting women and girls are forced marriage, IPV, and CRSV. These forms of violence are also highly normalized; for example, when asked if women should accept partner violence to keep the family together, 83% agreed. Qualitative findings show that boys and young men are socialized to be brave and defend their community; the show of masculine strength contributed to both intra and inter-communal violence. In addition, there is limited accountability for perpetrators of violence at either the household or community level, and all GBV cases are handled by older male community leaders who tend to reinforce norms around the use of violence against women and girls. There are significant gaps in GBV response services in both locations, with a lack of trained medical personnel to provide clinical management of rape (CMR), psychosocial support (PSS), case management, and legal assistance. Due to an absence of trained service providers in both locations, these harmful gender norms are held by those providing any available services, which is a barrier for women to access GBV services. If cases are taken seriously, there is limited capacity to provide care, with acute cases being referred to the Wau one-stop centre (OSC), which is several hours by road. In Wau, IOM works in partnership with the Strategic Initiative for Women in the Horn of Africa (SIHA) on GBV prevention and response programming, integrated with youth peacebuilding programming. In December 2020, a participatory action research was conducted with young men and women at-risk or engaged in criminal activities in and around Wau town. The findings show that the criminalized groups provide youth, especially young men, with a sense of belonging and peer support; however, several forms of GBV are affecting and perpetrated and experienced by members of these groups. These include forced marriage and victim-blaming for survivors of sexual violence. There was generally more acceptance or tolerance for IPV from female participants for the sake of keeping their families together. Gang-related violence and GBV perpetrated by young men in the town is accompanied by significant alcohol and drug abuse. While the one-stop centre (OSC) in Wau town has significantly improved access to services for GBV survivors, there is an average of 50 GBV survivors per month, which is over-stretching current capacity for both case management and PSS, including increased demand for access to justice.

2 - Project activities

IOM works to ensure a consistent and robust approach to addressing GBV, utilizing IOM's institutional framework on GBV in crisis action plan (2019 – 2021), contributing to HRP objective 2.4, GBV sub-cluster activities and indicators, and strengthening application of the Interagency GBV minimum standards:1. Provision of survivor-centered psychosocial support – IOM will build national partners' capacity and strengthen referrals to and from IOM MHPSS services. This includes providing technical capacity building on GBV case management, counselling for GBV survivors, caring for child survivors, including boys, and communication methods for people with disabilities.2. Provision of GBV case management services, including coordinated care and support - IOM will build national partners' capacity to provide quality case management, ensuring on-going mentoring of case workers, strong supervision, and staff support and care. 3. Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and link GBV survivors to available response services and support – working with TOCH in Tonj South and Jur River and SIHA in Wau, IOM will support strengthening community-level referral mechanisms to address barriers identified during recent assessments. 4. Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information, and activities. IOM will work with TOCH in Tonj South and Jur River and Active Youth Agency (AYA) in Juba to improve access to services and women and girls empowerment through WGFSs recently constructed in these locations. Group psychosocial support activities will be based on women and girls preferences and targeted at age-appropriate activities for adolescent girls (13- 17 years), young women (18 – 29 years old), and older women (30 – 55 years and 55 plus). 5. Provision of legal services that protect GBV survivors' rights and promote their access to justice – To address the gap in demand in Wau, IOM will support the strengthening of access to legal counselling with SIHA at the Wau OSC. 6. Provision of livelihood and economic support, including through Cash and Voucher Assistance to vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors as part of a multi-sectoral GBV response – Through direct implementation and with national partners, IOM will ensure that GBV survivors and other women and girls at-risk are engaged in income generation activities. 7. Implement community engagement and awareness-raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures, and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the "do no harm" principle – IOM will build the capacity of national partners and IOM MHPSS and health teams to raise awareness on GBV services. Furthermore, IOM will train and conduct GBV safety audits with IOM humanitarian multi-sectoral programming and with NGO partners. 8. Engage men and boys for accountable practices to transform harmful social norms. In all operational locations, IOM will expand GBV primary prevention programming utilizing EMAP and activities with school-age children (10-15 years old) within integrated WASH programming, as well as developing new gender transformative approaches within the youth and peacebuilding programming. This includes capacity building of national partners, and orientation of community workers and activists.9. Provide capacity building training on various topics of GBV to frontline service providers (health, psychosocial, and security actors) to improve the quality of GBV response services for male and female survivors based on survivor-centered principles and support GBV survivors of trafficking. This includes providing technical capacity building for partner CSOs/NNGOs on GBV primary prevention and GBV response based on Interagency GBV minimum standards and national guidelines. This will also include building capacity among men and women WASH, MHPSS, and peacebuilding actors at the programme and community level.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IOM has designed pictorial IEC material depicting the 6 principles and is using the simplified 6 principles posters translated into different South Sudanese languages. IOM trains staff and partners on IOM/IP code of conduct and the reporting mechanisms on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. IOM ensures that community outreach personnel are trained on using the IEC materials, including understand their rights to access humanitarian services and are trained on accessible communication methods for people with different impairments for inclusivity. IOM applies COVID-19 prevention measures when conducting community outreach and displays posters at WGFSs. IOM will emphasize on the necessity to report any case, and will provide the UN in South Sudan hotline where complainants are able to report the cases and by training all staff and volunteers on the reporting mechanisms.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IOM uses pictorial information, education and communication (IEC) materials for low literacy populations and local staff/volunteers who speak native languages. IOM applies COVID-19 prevention measures when conducting community outreach., but ensures specific consultations and information sessions are held which engage people with disabilities, elderly and persons from minority ethnic groups. Furthermore, IOM trains project staff and utilizes an “Inclusive communications checklist” to ensure IEC materials and communications are accessible for people with different impairments and staff are trained to adapt their communication methods for inclusivity.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IOM and partners conduct community consultations with different age and gender groups; with separate discussions with adult women and men (ages 25 – 59, 60+), girls/young women and boys/young men (ages 15-24) and ensuring specific consultations and information sessions are held which engage people with disabilities, elderly and persons from minority ethnic groups. IOM assessments gather information of preferred communication channels for complaints and feedback, which supports tailoring of communication and feedback channels. Information on rights and entitlements related to project activities, along with expected standards of behaviour of staff and complaints and feedback communication avenues is providing during community entry and establishing activities at WGFSs. IOM trains staff and partners on inclusive communication.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IOM utilizes a system to capture community complaints and feedback to ensure that responses are provided to complainants, and so that analysis of feedback and complaints can be conducted. Information related to key issues coming up is passed to project teams and partners to address. Furthermore, client satisafaction surveys are analysed to identify and address gaps in application of IA GBV minimum standards.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

IOM works with national partners, many of whom have limited internal capacities on PSEA. When IOM initiates a partnership a PSEA capacity assessment is conducted, and where partners agencies have capacity gaps, IOM supports by providing training. To mitigate risks of exclusion of persons with disabilities, older persons and young persons especially girls, IOM ensures that consultation and assessment process specify a requirement to consult with marginalized group, staff are trained on inclusive communication methods. This also includes providing accessible information to marginalized and different age and gender groups to ensure they can access CFMs, as well as conducting monitoring and providing technical guidance to partners on ensuring functional CFMs. IOM implements an institutional data protection policy and follows GBV case management data protection protocols, including capacity building and monitoring implementing partners on their application. IOM works with the GBV sub-cluster to ensure GBV response partners are trained on use of GBIMS and information sharing protocols, as well as ensuring the data collected from beneficiaries access project activities (including their image) is based on informed consent.
