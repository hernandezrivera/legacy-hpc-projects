Project Summary

The overall intervention aims to support populations affected by disasters in target areas
to meet their basic food needs during and after crises. Food-insecure people affected by
long-term conflict and/or displacement are able to meet their basic food needs and
increase their self-reliance throughout the year.

1 - Needs

According to Sudan’s 2020 HNO, it is estimated about 9.3M will need humanitarian assistance in 2020. This has been exacerbated by the current macroeconomic situation which has negatively impacting the food and nutrition security of a large share of the population including IDPs, refugees, and resident population. Despite a good harvest mid-year in 2019, the economic crisis has kept food costs high. Inflation, combined with uneven and unpredictable rain, drives food insecurity. In some Darfur areas, prices in July this year were 15 to 25 per cent higher than same time one year ago. Households are unable to purchase a basic daily basket of food. In July, the national average price of a local food basket reached SDG31.5, which is 13.7 per cent higher than the previous month and 40 per cent higher than in December 2018. East Darfur, followed by South Darfur and White Nile recorded the highest prices, with SDG37.5, 36.2, and 33.4 respectively. This is manifested by households already experiencing food insecurity prior to the onset of the crisis sinking to even deeper levels of food insecurity and previously marginally food secure people becoming food insecure.Under normal circumstances, IDP and refugees are already the most vulnerable, given their limited livelihood opportunities and these are most affected by the economic crisis. There are 1,87M IDPs and 1.1M refugee and asylum who seekers continue to need humanitarian assistance and protection in both in camps and communities. As of June-August 2019, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), estimated that 5.8 million people (14 per cent of the total population) are experiencing crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above). Around 1 million people are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) and around 4.8 million people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). The three states with highest number of people IPC 3 and above are Khartoum (793,000), South Darfur (763,000 people), and South Kordofan (607,000 people), followed by White Nile, Kassala, Red Sea, and North Darfur, with more than 400,000 people in each state in at least IPC 3 levels. Extreme and severe levels of intersectoral needs (severity 3 and 4) add to the already severe situation in Blue Nile, Central Darfur, Kassala, Nile state, North Darfur, South Kordofan, and West Kordofan, with a lesser extent in Al Gezira, Sennar, and White Nile. Women-headed households are more food insecure. 41 per cent of households headed by women were food insecure (moderate and severe). Following the similar trend, the purchasing power of the households headed by women was lower compared to households headed by men. With a commitment to zero hunger by 2030, there is a need to strengthen and continue the humanitarian response within the food security cluster on access and coverage of internal displaced people and residents through a diverse set ofactivities for them to effectively meet their food security needs.

2 - Activities / Outputs

- Provide unconditional general food assistance to people affected by shocks;- Provide integrated conditional and unconditional food assistance packages to vulnerable households. The introduction of different modalities of cash-based transfers (cash or voucher) is based on a multi-sectoral capacity assessment (MSCA) which looks into the capacity of: cooperating partners, supply chain at the local markets, financial institutions, information and communication technologies, gender and protection aspects that verify acceptance of cash transfers by intended beneficiaries, and security and safety of staffand WFP assets. Transfer values are established based on a standard local food basketand this in turn is regularly monitored through our market monitoring tools and adjustedon regular basis – at the moment on quarterly basis. Given WFP’s unique capacity to undertake price monitoring on weekly basis across the country, food price inflation and other economic constraints are closely monitored and reflected in our internal transfer value review exercise. As for the duration of the assistance, cash or voucher as a modality of food assistance is implemented in the same manner as the in-kind food assistance – for those profiled and found as severely food insecure, assistance in calibrated against their daily need and cash or voucher provided on a monthly basis.For cash transfers related to asset creation activities, the overall project duration is around six months as it is contingent on the type of the asset being created, availability of the targeted population to engage in manual activities, accessibility for partners to guide and monitor project implementation and others.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Accountability is one of core values that helps WFP to provide the best possible service to the people it assists. AAP is defined by WFP as an active commitment to give account to, take account of, and held to account by the people it assists. During the preliminary stages, WFP provides accurate, timely and accessible information to affected people about its assistance. The information provided must be clearly understandable by everyone, irrespective of their age, gender or other characteristics. Upholding the ‘centrality of protection’, the design and planning phase also considers food and nutrition assistance to be provided in a safe and dignified manner to the targeted population.During implementation, regular meetings and beneficiary reference sessions are hold with community leaders and representatives of different community groups to ensure that they understand the project objectives and intended outcomes, and to solicit their feedback about any necessary changes or adaptations needed to make the programme a success.
