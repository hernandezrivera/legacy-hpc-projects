Project Summary

Six years after the start of the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine, Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts remain effectively divided along an almost 500 km long “contact line”, separating the oblasts between areas controlled by the Ukrainian government (GCAs) and non-government-controlled areas (NGCAs).
The COVID-19 pandemic and the related lockdowns, which started in Ukraine in March 2020, is further challenging these oblasts’ populations in unprecedented ways. The two conflict-affected oblasts face particularly high vulnerabilities to the risk of COVID-19 transmission due to: (i) the high proportion of the elderly population in the region, (ii) the deterioration of the healthcare system as a result of the cumulative impact of the armed conflict and (iii) pre-existing access constraints to the area outside Government’s control. Women are particularly vulnerable to the many challenges that surround them, in particular:
- female heads of families and single mothers
- women caring for elderly people, children and members of family with disabilities
- elderly women living alone
- women with disabilities
With the lockdown restrictions and measures put in place to fight the COVID-19 pandemic, the population of these two regions is facing not only a reduction of disposable incomes and consumer power, but also key constraints in accessing social, psychological, legal and administrative services.
Since the country’s independence, public services in Ukraine have been affected by a number of inherited inefficiencies resulting in low quality of service provision. In the country’s conflict and pandemic-affected areas, inefficiencies in public services provision are significant and compromise the human rights of the population. They include:
- difficult (physical) access to services;
- un-systematized information on services;
- protracted service delivery in particular (especially for people living next to or crossing the contact line).
People most affected by these inefficiencies are those living in small settlements:
- close (0 to 5 km) to the contact line (GCA); this represents 266 000 people (out of which 70% women)
- in more remote rural areas (5 to 20 km from the contact line) (GCA); this represents 207 000 (60% are women)
These small communities are the most hit because public service facilities are very few if non-existent. The population of these communities has to travel long distances, often in perilous conditions, to reach services located in urban centres. This represents a particular challenge for vulnerable groups such as elderly women and men, women with children, people with disabilities, who, either cannot afford to undertake such long and risky travels or are not physically able to do so; this challenge is now further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic and the infection risks that such travels may engender.
The Project’s objective is to support 14 communities in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts in ensuring a facilitated access to administrative, social, legal, psychological support services by this target population. The Project will promote the cooperation between humanitarian and development actors and the Humanitarian Development Nexus approach. It will also ensure synergies and a further transfer of service provision and duty of care to local authorities, with a view towards the sustainability of service provision infrastructure.
The action will be implemented as part of the UN Recovery and Peacebuilding Programme. The design of the service package and other actions will be coordinated with humanitarian actors and will contribute to the sustainability of legal and PSS assistance in the region. As a result of project activities, partners will be able to prioritize other locations for the subsequent support, as well as to benefit from a new infrastructure that will be delivered by UNDP.

1 - Needs

Taking into account the complex context described above, and against the backdrop of the decentralisation process, the proposed Project embraces the key principles of the Humanitarian Development Nexus (HDN) for recovery and development efforts and results to be scaled up and fully owned by regional and local authorities.The key needs that the Project seeks to address are those relating to the inability of certain population groups, mainly women, in 14 conflict- and COVID-19 affected communities and settlements of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts (GCA) to have access to/be provided with:• Administrative, social and legal services. Population living in isolated settlements along the contact line (0 to 5 km) and remote areas further away from the line (5 to 20 km) experience significant difficulties in obtaining the payment of their social benefits, subsidies and pensions, and in getting key documents such as (among other things) IDP certificate, birth and death certificates, passport and ID cards. They also need legal advice to protect their housing, land and other property rights.• Psychological support services. People living along the “contact line”(mostly elderly people) have been experiencing a permanent state of psychological distress during the armed conflict,as their sense of safety and security is negatively affected. This chronic distress is exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. People suffer from solitude and cannot take care of themselves.• Online services. In the context of the quarantine restrictions associated with the COVID-19 pandemic, a growing number of services at the national and local levels can be processed through online services using digital tools. The Pension Fund, the Ministry of Social Policy, the State Employment Service, and utility service providers offer electronic services for processing (among other things) pensions, subsidies, benefits, and payments of utilities online.• The overall level of e-participation among communities is low, due to insufficient competencies and skills to use digital technologies. According to AGORA findings, only 15% of senior residents (60+years) are comfortable with using a computer or smartphone. In small communities of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, the public library and the day-care centres are among the few public premises visited by the local population not only for borrowing books, but also for public events, trainings and service delivery. Based on the UN RPP experience, these public facilities can become drivers for the development of community members competencies and skills, community engagement, and promoting social cohesion.Citizens living in semi-rural or rural communities along the contact line in GCAs have the rights to receive, in a timely manner and without excessive hardships, the above-mentioned services as much as citizens living in larger urban communities.These rights must be maintained and protected, particularly in the context created by the COVID-19 pandemic and related limitations and lockdowns.Such services, for these target groups, must be:• safe and easy to access,• effective and efficient,• reliable and timely,• responsive to the differentiated needs of women and men of various ages.The Project will therefore intervene to ensure that the above service requirements are met by:• developing accessible outreach facilities (mobile service units and remote workstations) for administrative, social, legal, and psychological support services);• ensuring that an effective information system is in place for easy localisation of the mobile services,and general and specific information provision aimed at the target population;• ensuring awareness of online services and promoting computer literacy among the population, including the elderly and vulnerable groups;• mobilising volunteers from the civil society to support the operation of the mobile service units and workstations (with a focus on access facilitation and information provision).
