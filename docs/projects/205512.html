Project Summary

The WHO’s commitments during the year 2024 is outlined in this HRP project “Emergency life-saving health response to Crisis-affected and displaced population in South Sudan” that will significantly contribute to the reduction of excess morbidity and mortality due to health emergencies orchestrated by the ongoing effects of acute food insecurity, localized violence within communities, flooding, and the consequences of the Sudan crisis along with related population displacement into South Sudan which together exacerbates the public health situation in the country.
WHO envisions an integrated and seamless health response where all sources of health insecurities are addressed for maximum impact. As such, the project will support timely, independent, and rigorous risk assessment and situation analysis to provide health partners with information to facilitate prompt response decision making. In addition, WHO will deploy technical officers to support partners and improve coordination of health responses.
In addition to the above, the project will provide Inter-agency Emergency Health Kits (IHEK) to responding partners, capacitate the implementing partners and counties to provide quality essential health services through training of frontline health workers and provision of guidelines and SoPs on case management of common communicable and non- communicable diseases, and provision of safe maternal and child services, Mental and Psychosocial care. Moreover, WHO will deploy a multidisciplinary teams; the emergency Mobile Medical Teams (eMMTs), to provide integrated health services in deep field locations through mobile outreaches to bridge service delivery gaps in areas with critical unmet health needs.
The project will also strengthen the country’s capacities to prevent, prepare for, detect, and respond to emergencies at all levels through investments in the public health surveillance, and reporting system (IDSR/EWARN) while facilitating integration into DHIS2; the mobile phone based EWARS reporting by the static and mobile clinics will be supported through capacities to implementing partners in counties. This will enhance weekly reporting and ensure early detection of and response to disease outbreaks. Besides, the national and state Rapid Response Teams (RRTs) and the Emergency Mobile Medical Teams will be deployed to conduct risk assessments, alert, and outbreak investigation, and to provide initial public health response during emergencies to reduce excess morbidity and mortality. As part of outbreak prevention and response, WHO will lead the implementation of preventive and reactive vaccination campaigns for vaccine-preventable diseases such as measles and cholera.
Further, WHO will conduct regular water quality testing and surveillance, IPC/WASH assessments in health facilities and implement IPC measures as a component of risk identification and mitigation efforts to healthcare-related infections. Lastly, the project will contribute to enhancing the capacities for diagnosis and management of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and associated medical complications with a view of reducing avoidable mortality in collaboration with the Nutrition Cluster.
The project will also support prevention and response to GBV, MHPSS delivery to affected people by mainstreaming the needs of affected people into ongoing programming, and work with organizations of persons living with disability to assess and address their needs. Finally, interventions will prioritize reproductive health providing EmONC (emergency newborn and obstetric care) and Clinical Management of Rape.
Overall, the above strategy and interventions will contribute to the three health cluster objectives of (1) Improving equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services, (2) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases and (3) Enhancing resilience and promoting humanitarian-development linkages

1. Needs

In 2023, South Sudan continued to experience protracted humanitarian crisis driven by food insecurity, conflict, economic downturn, displacement, climatic shocks including floods, drought, and disease outbreaks. An estimated 8.9 million people will require humanitarian assistance in 2024, among them 2million Internally Displaced Persons and over 328,000 refugees and asylum seekers. Sub-national violence occurred in parts of Abyei, Pibor, Malakal Protection of Civilian Camps (POC), Twic East, Nimule, Mangala and other parts of the country in 2023, during which several lives were lost with many more civilians injured. Between April and June 2023, the UNMISS documented 222 incidents of violence against civilians (including 128 children) during which 395 civilians were killed, 281 injured, 166 abducted, and 29 were subjected to conflict-related sexual violence. Moreover, since the outbreak of war in the Sudan’s capital Khartoum between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in April 2023, the country has witnessed a steady influx of returnees and refugees. As of 6 October, 302,838, people have been recorded mostly through the Joda/Renk border crossing points. Average daily new arrivals since the start of the conflict stood at 1,741 people per day and it is projected that the crisis could precipitate the influx of more than 520,000 South Sudanese returnees, 70,000 Sudanese refugees, and 10,000 third-country nationals by the end of 2023 (South Sudan ERP, August 2023). In 2023, an estimated 7.7 million people are reportedly facing severe acute food insecurity across 78 counties, among them are 43,000 people in catastrophe IPC 5, 2.9 million in IPC 4, and 4.8 million in IPC 3. The most affected locations are Fangak, Canal/Pigi, Akobo, Pibor and Kapoeta east counties where food insecurity levels are more critical. A March 2023 assessment in Kapoeta East reported that 59% of assessed households did not have access to adequate food. Additionally, over 1 million people in 37 counties were affected by floods in 2022, causing human death with over 62 people reportedly killed due to drowning, trauma, or snake bites (OCHA 2022). As of February 2023, a total of 7,021 people (1170 h/hs) were still displaced in Rubkona (IRNA 24th Feb 2023), yet in September, fresh floods in Renk and Mundri West Counties displaced over 6,000 people (OCHA September 2023).A combination of acute food insecurity, conflict, floods, internal displacement, and the associated overcrowding in the context of an underperforming health system characterized by low health workforce availability, a weak disease surveillance system, and low vaccine coverage for most vaccine-preventable diseases will increase health risk for the population. The functionality and capacity of the country’s health facilities are limited and inadequate. As of September 2023, only 12% facilities were fully functional and able to provide full Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNS); there is high disease burden and malaria remains endemic and the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, contributing to 50.2% of cases, and 39.4% death. Since 2022, the country is facing recurrent measles outbreak that has been reported in 68 counties with 6,389 suspected cases, 524 laboratory confirmed cases 63% positivity rate ,149 death and CFR of 2.33%. Other outbreaks include Hepatitis E outbreaks in Bentiu IDP Camp reported since 2018 and in Fangak, cholera outbreak in Malakal County of upper Nile state where 1,471 cases and 2 deaths were reported.The burden caused by multiple disease outbreaks is responsible the high morbidity and mortality rates among the population for instance, South Sudan has one of the highest maternal mortality rates in the world, with 1,223 deaths per 100,000 live births with infant mortality rate high at 84 deaths per 1000 live births, and the under-five mortality rate even higher at 98.7 deaths per 1000 live births.

2. Project activities

WHO’s HRP project will prioritize high impact interventions tailored to support Ministry of Health and emergency health partners to provide the much-needed lifesaving health services. The broad and specific activates will include; - Strengthening the country’s surveillance systems for timely detection and response to disease outbreaks through support to national and subnational RRTs structures, CSOs,SSOs, health partners and community based health structures through capacity building ,provision of guidelines and reporting tools to ensure a seamless surveillances system that picks information from the community ,facility , counties ,states, Pont of Entries (PoEs) and National levels for effective preparedness and response.- Reduce excess morbidity and mortality from disease outbreaks by strengthening capacity for outbreak prevention and response through timely, independent, and rigorous risk assessment, situation analysis whilst deployment of Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), Public Health experts and Emergency Mobile Medical Teams (eMMTs) to conduct alert verification, investigation, and response as well as provision of technical support and guidance to MoH and health partners during all emerging public health events.- Strengthen laboratory and molecular capacities in the country to facilitate timely testing and confirmation of suspected disease outbreaks by prepositioning adequate equipment, reagents, consumables, sample collection and management for priority highly pathogenic diseases. - Supporting the MoH and partners to establish emergency response coordination platforms (within the PHEOC) in line with the principles of the incident management system. - Overall coordination of the health cluster response to ensure appropriate coverage and quality of essential health services and effective outbreak response. - Support the country and health partners with health information systems management including capacities in EWARS, DHIS2 integration and health information products for informed health response decision making. - Facilitate health and WASH partners on health facilities IPC/WASH assessment and capacity-building of health care workers on Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) from selected health facilities in targeted locations.- Water quality testing and Nutrition Surveillance as part of outbreak monitoring, outbreak detection and control.- Capacitate health partners with training, guidelines and tools to facilitate delivery of front-line health and nutrition services ranging from curative consultations, reactive and preventive vaccinations as well as strengthening routine immunization against vaccine preventable diseases, MHPSS (Mental Health and Psychosocial support) and GBV (Gender Based Violence), Psychosocial care, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and delivery of Sexual reproductive health services/EmONC (emergency newborn and obstetric care), family planning, PMTCT (prevention of mother to child transmission)
